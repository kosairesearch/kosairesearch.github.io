02

Business structure

Stechm is a special-purpose machinery manufacturer founded in 2020 and headquartered in Hwaseong, Gyeonggi Province. Its core products are secondary-battery equipment, and it also produces semiconductor and display equipment.

Its integrated production system covers the full process from design, procurement, assembly and quality control to setup, mass production and customer service. Based on this system, it supplies customized automation equipment to customers across several industries in Korea and abroad.

The business base is weighted toward battery production equipment, so it is sensitive to the investment cycle of its end markets. IBK Investment & Securities, the listing sponsor, identified earnings volatility tied to the investment cycle in the secondary-battery and electric-vehicle markets as a key risk.

Competitively, it vies with domestic and foreign automation equipment makers for customer orders, and its revenue of roughly KRW 10 billion is small next to large equipment makers. Diversification is also under way.

At a recent extraordinary shareholders' meeting, the company added renewable energy product manufacturing, solar plant development and operation, and electricity sales to its business purpose.

On October 27, 2026, it signed a memorandum of understanding with China's state-owned Shiji Xinsheng Group to pursue entry into domestic solar power projects. The plan is to link its existing automation manufacturing experience with the partner's industrial and investment network.

03

Recent trends

Revenue for 2025 was KRW 10.003 billion, down 2.1% from the prior year. Operating profit was KRW 854 million, up 44.8%, and net profit was KRW 661 million, up 183.3%. Profitability improved even as revenue declined.

According to NICE D&B audit data, revenue fell from KRW 13.56 billion in 2023 to KRW 10.21 billion in 2024 and held near KRW 10 billion in 2025. Operating cash flow in 2025 was a net outflow of KRW 1.25 billion, narrower than the KRW 1.67 billion outflow in 2024.

Receivables and inventory rose KRW 2.73 billion, exceeding the KRW 950 million increase in payables. Raw-material inventory surged from KRW 420 million to KRW 3.04 billion.

Financing activities included KRW 400 million raised through a rights issue and KRW 340 million of debt repaid, following KRW 8.81 billion of borrowings in 2024. Short- and long-term borrowings at the end of 2025 stood at KRW 8.69 billion, slightly above equity of KRW 8.06 billion.

The shares hit the daily upper limit on their first trading day on April 27, 2026, closing at KRW 5,730 against a base price of KRW 4,990, and hit the limit again the next day at KRW 6,580. The current price of KRW 11,100 (+8.50% from the prior close) is more than twice the first-day base price.

04

Outlook

Future results are likely to depend on the investment cycle in the secondary-battery and electric-vehicle markets and on customers' equipment order flow. The sharp rise in inventory at the end of 2025 is read as early procurement of raw materials and higher process volume ahead of expected production growth.

Still, working-capital strain only eases if inventory converts into sales and collected cash, so receivables collection and inventory turnover are the key checkpoints. Whether borrowings continue to exceed equity is another core financial indicator.

On the business side, the question is whether the solar entry produces actual orders and revenue. In the KONEX market, the number of listed companies fell from 14 in 2023 to 6 in 2024 and 4 in 2025, and Stechm became the first new listing of 2026.

Trading liquidity and investor attention in a shrinking market will also matter. In sum, profitability gains and business expansion coexist with the constraints of working capital and borrowing, and the balance between these two sides will shape the next phase.

05

Bull factors

Earnings strength improved in 2025

Revenue fell 2.1% in 2025, yet operating profit rose 44.8% and net profit rose 183.3%. Higher profits without major revenue growth point to a better earnings structure. Operating and net profit rising together suggests the cost base has partly stabilized.

End-to-end production and business expansion

Its in-house process from design to mass production and customer service underpins customized equipment orders. It also has experience beyond batteries, in semiconductor and display equipment. Adding solar-related business purposes and signing an MOU with Shiji Xinsheng Group on October 27, 2026 widens its scope.

Market attention since listing

After its April 27, 2026 KONEX listing, the shares hit the daily upper limit on the first two trading days. The current price of KRW 11,100 is more than twice the first-day base price. With about 1.51 million listed shares, the small-cap structure of KONEX is read as having amplified the move.

06

Bear factors

Rising working-capital burden

In 2025, the KRW 2.73 billion rise in receivables and inventory far exceeded the KRW 950 million rise in payables. Raw-material inventory jumped from KRW 420 million to KRW 3.04 billion, adding to cash outflow.

Operating cash flow remaining in net outflow of KRW 1.25 billion highlights a gap between reported profit and cash generation.

Borrowing reliance and equity quality

Short- and long-term borrowings at the end of 2025 of KRW 8.69 billion slightly exceeded equity of KRW 8.06 billion. Equity includes KRW 5.26 billion of revaluation surplus from land and buildings, so book equity may overstate cash-generating capacity.

After borrowing KRW 8.81 billion in 2024, the company added KRW 400 million of equity through a rights issue in 2025, underscoring limited financial room.

Revenue decline and cycle dependence

Revenue dropped from KRW 13.56 billion in 2023 to KRW 10.21 billion in 2024 and fell another 2.1% in 2025. Revenue has declined for two consecutive years since 2024. The listing sponsor flagged the investment cycle in secondary-battery and electric-vehicle markets as a main source of earnings volatility.

07

Risk factors

Macro (end-market cycle)

If the investment cycle in secondary-battery and electric-vehicle markets turns, equipment orders may shrink and results could weaken. As an equipment supplier, revenue is directly tied to customers' capacity expansion decisions. An order gap could raise the inventory burden further.

Market and liquidity

Trading volume in the early sessions was 1,461 shares, with turnover under KRW 10 million. KONEX has a small free float, so even small trades can move prices sharply. In a market where listings fell to four in 2025, improvement in liquidity is uncertain.

New-business execution

Solar power and electricity sales are still at the purpose-amendment and MOU stage. Revenue contribution and investment scale for these new businesses are difficult to gauge from current disclosures. If borrowing rises to fund expansion, financial strain could deepen.

08

Overall view

Stechm saw revenue edge down in 2025 while operating and net profit rose sharply, marking a clear improvement in profitability. Operating cash flow remained in net outflow, however, and inventory and borrowings sit close to equity, leaving balance-sheet strain.

Since its KONEX listing the shares have moved sharply, but limited market liquidity means price swings can grow regardless of results. The demand cycle for battery equipment is the main revenue variable, while new ventures such as solar are still too early to gauge in terms of results.

Strengths include better earnings, an end-to-end production system and expansion efforts; weaknesses include working-capital strain, reliance on borrowing and revenue decline.

Because these two sides are intertwined, inventory turnover, cash collection and changes in borrowing levels remain the key financial indicators for reading this company.
