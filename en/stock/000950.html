02

Business structure

Chonbang is a textile manufacturer producing yarn and woven fabric. As of December 2024, yarn accounted for 96.87 percent of sales, while fabric, the second product line, made up just 1.48 percent.

In 2025, yarn sales were split 60.94 percent domestic and 39.06 percent export, and fabric was sold entirely in the domestic market.

The consolidated subsidiaries are Chonbang Auto, which trades used cars, Qingdao Condenser, a wholesaler, and Taotour, a travel company that is currently dormant, and most revenue comes from textile manufacturing with only a portion from used car sales.

The production base was reshaped around domestic plants after the Yeongam dyeing and fabric plant stopped in April 2023 and the Iksan yarn plant stopped in December 2023. The company attributed the 2025 sales decline to the halt of existing plants.

Listed peers in the same textile sector include Kyungbang, Ilshin Spinning and Pangrim, and trade press has described Chonbang as a company that has kept production in Korea rather than moving it overseas.

The cost of sales ratio improved from 123.27 percent in 2022 to 107.56 percent in 2025, while the selling and administrative expense ratio rose from 4.77 percent to 10.34 percent over the same period. R&D spending in 2025 was KRW 84 million, equal to 0.21 percent of sales.

04

Earnings analysis

Based on disclosed results, Chonbang's sales fell from KRW 124.9 billion in 2022 to KRW 94.8 billion in 2023, KRW 57.2 billion in 2024 and KRW 40.8 billion in 2025, a decline for three consecutive years from 2023 through 2025.

Sales in 2025 dropped 28.8 percent from the prior year, making it the smallest year of the 2022 to 2025 period.

The operating loss was KRW 35 billion in 2022, KRW 32.5 billion in 2023, KRW 8.48 billion in 2024 and KRW 7.31 billion in 2025, which means the company has posted operating losses for four consecutive years from 2022 through 2025.

The loss shrank sharply in 2024 and 2025, and the operating margin was -28.1 percent in 2022, -34.3 percent in 2023, -14.8 percent in 2024 and -17.9 percent in 2025.

Net income attributable to controlling shareholders followed a different path, with KRW 67.5 billion in 2022, a net loss of KRW 19.2 billion in 2023 after the swing to deficit, and KRW 8.91 billion in 2024 after the return to profit.

The KRW 29.8 billion in 2025 rose 234.4 percent from the prior year, and the company attributed this to a reversal of bad debt allowances and lower foreign exchange losses. Because the operating loss persisted while net income rose, earnings quality depends heavily on non-operating items.

Quarterly sales were KRW 6.87 billion in the third quarter of 2025 and KRW 9.37 billion in the fourth quarter, up 36.4 percent sequentially, followed by KRW 7.9 billion in the first quarter of 2026, KRW 6.91 billion in the second and KRW 8.12 billion in the third.

Operating losses were KRW 1.72 billion, KRW 1.5 billion, KRW 919 million and KRW 141 million across the third and fourth quarters of 2025 and the first two quarters of 2026, before widening to KRW 1.6 billion in the third quarter of 2026.

A company overview source described the first quarter of 2026 sales decline as stemming from weaker domestic demand, a strong exchange rate and halted export orders tied to the Middle East conflict, and it cited the consolidation of production and maintenance lines and workforce reductions as factors narrowing the operating loss. The net loss attributable to controlling shareholders over the last four quarters totaled KRW 199 million.

05

Industry analysis

The spinning industry to which Chonbang belongs is a cyclical business whose results swing sharply with cotton yarn prices, domestic consumption and export demand.

In an article on first-half 2026 results, the Korean textile trade paper Textile Plus reported that Kyungbang and Ilshin Spinning, both listed spinning peers, saw operating profit rise from a year earlier, with higher yarn prices cited as a driver of the industry-wide improvement.

The same report noted that the upturn was concentrated in the first half. Chonbang, by contrast, has been described as a company that kept production in Korea rather than moving it overseas, so its exposure to the industry upturn may differ from peers.

In the first quarter of 2026, weak domestic demand, a strong exchange rate and halted export orders linked to the Middle East conflict together pushed sales down 38.4 percent year on year according to a company overview source.

Third-quarter 2026 sales of KRW 8.12 billion rose 18.2 percent year on year, showing sales stabilizing from a low base.

Compared with apparel-focused peers such as Youngone and F&F, Chonbang's yarn and fabric business has a different demand structure, with more direct exposure to raw material prices and changes in orders from large customers.

Its market capitalization is small within the sector, so shifts in industry conditions tend to appear in reported results before they are reflected elsewhere.

Outlook

Sales were KRW 7.9 billion in the first quarter of 2026, KRW 6.91 billion in the second and KRW 8.12 billion in the third, rising 17.6 percent sequentially and 18.2 percent year on year in the third quarter.

In the same quarter the operating loss widened to KRW 1.6 billion, and the net result attributable to controlling shareholders turned to a loss of KRW 1.42 billion. Whether the sales recovery translates into a narrower operating loss is therefore the central point to watch.

A company overview source cited the consolidation of production and maintenance lines and workforce reductions as factors narrowing the first-quarter 2026 loss, and whether the headcount effect holds while sales are still declining needs to be verified in quarterly figures.

External variables include export order flows linked to the Middle East conflict and the exchange rate, both of which affect the yarn export share of 39.06 percent in 2025.

On the financial side, total equity of KRW 227.5 billion and total liabilities of KRW 9.87 billion at the end of 2025 provide capacity, so the pace of operating improvement is the key question. Cash dividends continued in the 2025 fiscal year, and disclosures around the next record date are the next point to confirm.

Bull factors

Financial capacity

Total equity at the end of 2025 was KRW 227.5 billion and total liabilities were KRW 9.87 billion, a debt ratio of 4.3 percent. The ratio fell sharply from 49.8 percent in 2022, so borrowing pressure remains small even while operating losses continue. Financial capacity does not, however, guarantee an improvement in profit.

Narrowing operating loss

The operating loss narrowed from KRW 35 billion in 2022 to KRW 7.31 billion in 2025, and the operating margin improved from -34.3 percent in 2023 to -17.9 percent in 2025.

A company overview source said consolidating production and maintenance lines and reducing outsourced staff narrowed the operating loss in the first quarter of 2026. Cost restructuring is cited as a possible source of operating improvement.

Partial industry recovery and continued dividends

Textile Plus reported that operating profit at spinning peers Kyungbang and Ilshin Spinning rose year on year in the first half of 2026. Chonbang also continued paying cash dividends for the 2025 fiscal year. Whether the industry upturn is temporary or sustained will need to be confirmed from further quarterly figures.

Bear factors

Shrinking sales base

Sales fell from KRW 124.9 billion in 2022 to KRW 40.8 billion in 2025. The halt of two plants in 2023 was a major driver of the decline, and the company attributed the 2025 drop to the stoppage of existing plants. Whether sales can recover from a reduced production base is the core of the bearish case.

Renewed quarterly deterioration

The operating loss widened to KRW 1.6 billion in the third quarter of 2026 from KRW 141 million in the second quarter, and the company posted a net loss attributable to controlling shareholders of KRW 1.42 billion. Sales rose 18.2 percent year on year but did not translate into profit. Operating losses have continued for five consecutive quarters since the third quarter of 2025.

Reliance on non-operating gains

The KRW 29.8 billion net income attributable to controlling shareholders in 2025 reflected a reversal of bad debt allowances and lower foreign exchange losses, according to the company. The operating loss that year was KRW 7.31 billion, so core operating profit remained negative. The cost of sales ratio was 107.56 percent in 2025, meaning costs exceeded revenue.

Risk factors

Exchange rate and external demand

A company overview source cited high exchange rates and halted export orders linked to the Middle East conflict as drivers of the first-quarter 2026 sales decline. The export share of yarn sales was 39.06 percent in 2025, so results depend on external demand and currency moves. If exchange rates shift or export orders do not recover, a sales rebound may be delayed.

Production base and cost structure

Production at the Yeongam and Iksan plants stopped in 2023, and the cost of sales ratio was 107.56 percent in 2025. When utilization is low, fixed costs remain in the cost ratio. Further facility or workforce adjustments could appear as one-off charges in the income statement.

Volatility of net income

Net income attributable to controlling shareholders swung widely, from a net loss of KRW 19.2 billion in 2023 to KRW 8.91 billion in 2024 and KRW 29.8 billion in 2025. The 2025 result leaned on non-operating items such as bad debt allowance reversals and lower foreign exchange losses. Because such items do not recur at the same scale every year, net income volatility is high.
