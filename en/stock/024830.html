02

Business structure

Sewon was founded in 1985 and makes and sells automotive parts, with core products including reinforcement panels, brackets and body components. Its headquarters and plant are in Yeongcheon, North Gyeongsang Province.

Most products are supplied to Hyundai and Kia, with volume production for multiple models made by Hyundai Motor and its affiliates. On the production side, the company has introduced automated press lines and TWB laser welding equipment.

The company states that it has achieved a defect rate of 100 PPM and is a Level-1 plant designated by Hyundai Motor. Product mix is shown as MBR ASS'Y 94.30%, by-products 3.00% and others 2.70%, per a stock page as of October 8, 2026.

Lee Jong-tae was appointed CEO on March 31, 2026, after the term of Kim Bong-su ended. Headcount was 543 as of June 30, 2026. Listed peers in the same industry include Hyundai WIA, Sungwoo Hitech, Seoyon E-Hwa, Myoung Shin Industry and Hallacast, and comparisons should consider customer mix and product scope together. Because sales depend heavily on Hyundai and Kia, revenue tracks production volumes for those models.

04

Earnings analysis

Annual revenue rose from KRW 171.1 billion in 2022 to KRW 193 billion in 2023 (up 12.8%) and KRW 230.9 billion in 2024 (up 19.6%), then fell to KRW 211.6 billion in 2025 (down 8.4%). Of 2022 to 2025, 2024 was the largest year for revenue.

Operating profit fell from KRW 15 billion in 2022 to KRW 5.91 billion in 2023 (down 60.6%) and KRW 1.34 billion in 2024 (down 77.3%), then recovered to KRW 3.06 billion in 2025 (up 127.8%).

The operating margin declined from 8.8% in 2022 to 3.1% in 2023, 0.6% in 2024 and 1.4% in 2025, so the margin was lowest in 2024, the year of peak revenue. Net profit attributable to controlling shareholders peaked at KRW 22 billion in 2024 (up 72.6%) even though operating profit that year was only KRW 1.34 billion.

The gap appears to have been filled by non-operating items; Job Korea financial data shows non-operating income of KRW 24.8 billion in 2024 and KRW 19.3 billion in 2025.

In 2025, controlling-shareholder net profit of KRW 18.6 billion (down 15.3%) was far larger than operating profit of KRW 3.06 billion, indicating a large reliance on non-operating items.

Operating cash flow was KRW 35.4 billion in 2022, KRW 40.2 billion in 2023 and KRW 50.2 billion in 2024, before falling to KRW 19.1 billion in 2025.

On a quarterly basis, operating profit of KRW 1.67 billion in the third quarter of 2025 and KRW 1.09 billion in the fourth quarter (down 34.4% from the prior quarter) gave way to an operating loss of KRW 1.96 billion in the first quarter of 2026.

The loss narrowed to KRW 1.73 billion in the second quarter but widened to KRW 3.12 billion in the third quarter. Revenue peaked at KRW 54.3 billion in the first quarter of 2026 and then fell to KRW 41.6 billion and KRW 38.1 billion.

Controlling-shareholder net profit over the latest four quarters totaled KRW 5.74 billion, a sharp drop against the KRW 22 billion reported for 2024.

Industry analysis

Sewon's demand is tied directly to production volumes at automakers, so its industry picture should be read through vehicle production and sales trends. The company itself presents Hyundai-centered body parts manufacturing as its main business.

Strengthening the competitiveness of Korea's auto industry and restructuring among parts suppliers are also raised as major challenges. For suppliers, restructuring phases tend to show up as customer volume adjustments and changes in plant utilization.

Whether the sales decline since the second quarter of 2026 reflects these forces cannot be separated from the disclosures alone. Body parts involve heavy investment in press and welding equipment and dies, which tends to raise fixed-cost burdens.

Among listed peers in the same industry, Hyundai WIA, Sungwoo Hitech, Seoyon E-Hwa, Myoung Shin Industry, Hallacast, Inhwa Precision and SL are natural comparison points. Sewon's market value is small relative to large parts makers, and its heavy reliance on the Hyundai group means customer diversification is limited.

On the other side, its Level-1 plant designation from Hyundai Motor and its 100 PPM quality level support its position as a supplier to that customer. Its credit rating was described as sound as of April 2026.

Where the industry stands in its cycle should be judged against vehicle production and sales indicators, which need to be checked separately.

Risk factors

Customer concentration risk

Most products go to Hyundai and Kia, so changes in automaker production plans or model transitions feed directly into revenue. Production cuts or model discontinuations at the customer can quickly reduce sales of specific items. Limited customer diversification also increases volatility tied to negotiating leverage and volume allocation.

Deteriorating profitability and cash flow

Operating losses have run for three consecutive quarters through the third quarter of 2026, and the 2025 operating margin was low at 1.4%. Operating cash flow fell to KRW 19.1 billion in 2025, less than half the 2024 level. If revenue keeps declining in a production structure with high fixed costs, losses could accumulate.

Management change and trading liquidity
