Report overview

Youngpoong: smelter recovery and governance dispute intertwined

Youngpoong returned to an operating loss of KRW 66.8 billion in the third quarter of 2026, while court rulings on its Korea Zinc stake and control dispute added to the uncertainty.

  1. 1

    Youngpoong posted an operating loss in each of 2023, 2024 and 2025, with the 2025 loss of KRW 259.7 billion the largest of the period.

  2. 2

    Operating profit of KRW 43.3 billion in the first quarter of 2026 was followed by KRW 1.51 billion in the second quarter and an operating loss of KRW 66.8 billion in the third quarter.

  3. 3

    Net income attributable to controlling shareholders over the last four quarters totals KRW 264.2 billion, driven mainly by KRW 377 billion in the second quarter of 2026, which diverges from operating results.

  4. 4

    On November 12, 2026 a first-instance court ruled that Korea Zinc's restriction of Youngpoong's voting rights was unlawful, and on November 13 an appellate court upheld Korea Zinc's share issuance, a loss for Youngpoong.

  5. 5

02

Business structure

Youngpoong's core business is zinc smelting, with zinc ingots produced at its Seokpo smelter in Bonghwa, Gyeongbuk.

According to press reports, zinc ingot product and merchandise sales accounted for about 81 percent of KRW 732.7 billion in smelting segment sales for January to September 2025, so zinc prices and smelting fees feed directly into core profitability.

Electronics and semiconductor affiliates are the main driver of group revenue. Press reports put third-quarter 2025 electronics and semiconductor segment sales at KRW 922.8 billion before consolidation adjustments, or 69.6 percent of total sales.

The FPCB affiliate Interflex reportedly posted third-quarter 2025 sales of KRW 102.0 billion and operating profit of KRW 5.9 billion. Youngpoong also holds a stake in Korea Zinc, and voting and disposal terms for that stake have become the central governance issue.

The closest domestic comparable in zinc smelting is Korea Zinc, and Youngpoong is distinguished more by its affiliate portfolio and shareholding structure than by smelting scale.

The company said an annual environmental investment of roughly KRW 100 billion, running since 2019, would widen earnings recovery once completed.

04

Earnings analysis

Annual sales fell from KRW 4.43 trillion in 2022 to KRW 3.76 trillion in 2023, a 15.1 percent decline, and then to KRW 2.79 trillion in 2024, a 25.9 percent decline, before rebounding to KRW 2.91 trillion in 2025, up 4.4 percent. Among 2022 to 2025, 2022 was the largest year by sales and 2024 the smallest.

Operating profit of KRW 68.9 billion in 2022 turned into an operating loss of KRW 169.8 billion in 2023, followed by a loss of KRW 160.7 billion in 2024, a narrower loss, and KRW 259.7 billion in 2025, a wider loss, making 2023 through 2025 three consecutive years of losses.

Operating margin moved from 1.6 percent in 2022 to -4.5 percent in 2023, -5.8 percent in 2024 and -8.9 percent in 2025. Margin fell in 2025 even as sales rose 4.4 percent, which fits with the Seokpo smelter utilization drop to 34.9 percent during the first half of 2025 after a suspension order.

Net income attributable to controlling shareholders went from KRW 367.2 billion in 2022 to a loss of KRW 60.9 billion in 2023 and KRW 252.1 billion in 2024, before narrowing to a loss of KRW 8.3 billion in 2025.

Net income including non-controlling interests was KRW 30.9 billion in 2025, a profit that differs in sign from the controlling-shareholder loss. Operating cash flow went from KRW 326.3 billion in 2022 and KRW 313.1 billion in 2023 to -KRW 2.84 billion in 2024, then recovered to KRW 47.1 billion in 2025.

In the quarters, sales reached KRW 987.7 billion in the fourth quarter of 2025, up 31.8 percent from the prior quarter, yet the operating loss widened to KRW 100.5 billion.

The first quarter of 2026 returned to operating profit of KRW 43.3 billion, the second quarter posted KRW 1.51 billion, and the third quarter swung back to an operating loss of KRW 66.8 billion.

Net income attributable to controlling shareholders reached KRW 377 billion in the second quarter of 2026, a gap wide enough versus operating results that the non-operating share should be examined in the next annual report.

05

Industry analysis

Zinc smelting profitability is shaped jointly by feedstock supply, smelting fees (TC) and zinc prices. A press analysis of the third quarter of 2025 said falling smelting fees and weaker zinc prices combined to weigh on earnings.

Over the same period, utilization at the Seokpo smelter rose from 34.9 percent in the first half to 51.9 percent in the third quarter, and zinc ingot output increased from 31,303 tonnes in the first quarter and 38,577 tonnes in the second to 52,108 tonnes in the third.

Because smelters carry high fixed costs, analysts said recovering utilization feeds quickly into profit. Korea Zinc, the closest domestic comparable, announced in December 2025 plans for a large US smelter and a share issuance of nearly KRW 3 trillion to a joint venture involving US government participants.

Because that plan is tied to the control fight between the two companies, the market focused on the change in shareholding structure as much as on the investment itself.

Youngpoong competes with Korea Zinc on smelting, and a higher share of electronics affiliates in group revenue is cited as a buffer against swings in the smelting cycle.

As of November 2026, press reports put the friendly shareholding of Choi Yun-beom's side at about 38 percent of Korea Zinc and the Youngpoong-MBK group at about 41 percent.

06

Outlook

The near-term variables are smelter utilization and zinc prices. Having swung back to an operating loss of KRW 66.8 billion in the third quarter of 2026, Youngpoong's next-quarter results will depend largely on Seokpo's operating rate and the zinc price trend.

At its third-quarter 2025 results announcement, the company said earnings recovery would widen once the environmental investment program is completed. On the governance side, the first-instance ruling by the Seoul Central District Court on November 12, 2026 is the key variable.

The court held that Korea Zinc could not use cross-shareholding provisions to restrict existing shareholders' voting rights in defense of its management control, and Korea Zinc said it intends to appeal.

On November 13 the Seoul High Court found Korea Zinc's share issuance lawful as a measure within corporate purposes, reversing the first-instance judgment on that point.

If the first-instance ruling stands, the board size cap in Korea Zinc's articles and the validity of board appointments could be affected, but with appellate and Supreme Court rulings still to come, the short-term impact is seen as limited.

Korea Zinc's next regular shareholders' meeting is scheduled for March 2027, and press analysis said five board seats expiring or vacant include three held by the Korea Zinc side and two by the Youngpoong side. For Youngpoong's side to gain a board majority it would need all five seats.

Under the cooperation contract, MBK holds a call option on 2,570,910 Korea Zinc shares held by Youngpoong at KRW 700,000 per share plus a 5 percent annual premium, and Youngpoong holds a put option on the same terms.

The parties said the call has not been exercised and that MBK does not plan to exercise it before recovering its investment.

08

Bull factors

Operating leverage from smelter recovery

Seokpo utilization rose from 34.9 percent in the first half of 2025 to 51.9 percent in the third quarter, and zinc ingot output increased over the same period. Because smelting carries high fixed costs, analysts said recovering utilization passes quickly into profit.

The first quarter of 2026 returned to operating profit of KRW 43.3 billion. The third quarter of 2026 swung back to an operating loss of KRW 66.8 billion, so whether the recovery holds is the next point to watch.

Court rulings reshaping the governance question

On November 12, 2026 a first-instance court ruled that the Korea Zinc shareholders' meeting resolutions restricting Youngpoong's voting rights should be annulled. If the ruling stands, the board size cap in Korea Zinc's articles would lose effect, which could favour the Youngpoong and MBK side in board composition.

Korea Zinc has said it will appeal, and appellate and Supreme Court decisions remain. The ruling is seen as strengthening Youngpoong's legal position.

Portfolio diversification and quarterly profit swings

Press reports put third-quarter 2025 electronics and semiconductor segment sales at KRW 922.8 billion before consolidation adjustments, or 69.6 percent of total sales. Affiliates are described as supporting group profitability when smelting is weak.

Net income attributable to controlling shareholders reached KRW 377 billion in the second quarter of 2026, so group earnings have shown large swings. The nature of that quarter's income is a point to confirm in the next annual report.

09

Bear factors

Three straight years of operating losses and margin erosion

Operating losses were KRW 169.8 billion in 2023, KRW 160.7 billion in 2024 and KRW 259.7 billion in 2025, three years in a row. Operating margin fell from 1.6 percent in 2022 to -8.9 percent in 2025. Margin declined in 2025 even though sales rose 4.4 percent.

The third quarter of 2026 again showed an operating loss of KRW 66.8 billion, so whether revenue recovery has turned into profit recovery has yet to be shown.

Option terms and voting constraints in the contract

Reports say the cooperation contract requires voting on the option shares to follow MBK's instructions even before the option is settled. The contract is also reported to give MBK greater influence over CEO nomination and board composition.

Press reports say both representative directors were in custody when the agreement was signed and the board approved it with only three independent directors present. These points are at issue in a derivative suit brought by a Youngpoong minority shareholder.

Seokpo disruption and weaker zinc prices

During the first half of 2025, Seokpo utilization fell to 34.9 percent after an administrative suspension order. A third-quarter 2025 analysis pointed to falling smelting fees and weaker zinc prices as added pressure. A worker death at the Seokpo smelter also led to the arrest of representative directors. If utilization and cost structure weaken again, the pace of core profit recovery could slow.

Risk factors

Governance and litigation

The first-instance ruling took about 18 months, so litigation could run for years through appeal and the Supreme Court. On November 13, 2026 the appellate court upheld Korea Zinc's share issuance, a loss for Youngpoong. Board composition could shift depending on how the voting-rights ruling is ultimately decided.

The joint voting provisions in the contract could also become a source of friction among shareholders as the dispute lengthens.

Core operations and commodity prices

Core profit is sensitive to Seokpo utilization, zinc prices and smelting fees. The third-quarter 2026 operating loss of KRW 66.8 billion and the second-quarter operating profit of KRW 1.51 billion show wide quarterly swings.

Environmental investment of roughly KRW 100 billion a year has been running since 2019, so cash availability may be absorbed by that spending.

Balance sheet and cash flow

The debt-to-equity ratio rose from 33.4 percent in 2022 to 49.4 percent in 2025. Total liabilities at the end of 2025 were KRW 1.98 trillion. Operating cash flow turned negative at -KRW 2.84 billion in 2024 before recovering to KRW 47.1 billion in 2025. If operating losses persist, borrowing capacity and cash generation could both be tested.

Overall view

Youngpoong is a group whose core business is zinc smelting, alongside electronics and semiconductor affiliates and a stake in Korea Zinc. On reported results, operating losses continued for three consecutive years from 2023 through 2025, with operating margin at -8.9 percent in 2025.

The first quarter of 2026 returned to operating profit of KRW 43.3 billion, but the third quarter swung back to an operating loss of KRW 66.8 billion.

Net income attributable to controlling shareholders over the last four quarters totals KRW 264.2 billion, driven heavily by KRW 377 billion in the second quarter of 2026, which points in a different direction from operating results.

Bullish factors include operating leverage from smelter recovery, the first-instance finding that restricting Youngpoong's votes via cross-shareholding was unlawful, and the affiliate portfolio.

Bearish factors include three years of operating losses, the option and voting terms in the cooperation contract, weaker zinc prices and smelting fees, and a rising debt-to-equity ratio.

Valuation reads differently depending on whether the focus is earnings or asset value, with the share price well below book value per share. The next checkpoints are the 2026 annual report, whether Korea Zinc files its appeal, and Korea Zinc's March 2027 shareholders' meeting.
