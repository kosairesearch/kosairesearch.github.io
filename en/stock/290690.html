02

Business structure

Solux traces its roots to an LED lighting specialist founded in 1996 that focused on developing design-driven, high-value-added lighting products.

It listed on KOSDAQ in November 2020, and in July 2026 changed its corporate name to ARIBIO Holdings, beginning a transition toward a holding-company structure spanning both lighting/electrical operations and biotech investment.

The company has stated a strategy of using the lighting and electrical business as a revenue base while nurturing new drug development and DC-based smart lighting technology as its core growth pillars.

The existing lighting and electrical business serves as a stable cash-generation source for the group, while biotech investment is pursued in parallel as a separate growth driver.

Solux is the largest shareholder of unlisted drug developer AriBio, and has funded a portion of the global Phase 3 trial costs for AriBio's oral Alzheimer's drug candidate AR1001. In 2026 it also acquired KOSDAQ-listed affiliate Aribiolab, broadening the group's business base and adding a further growth engine.

Under the KOSDAQ "electrical equipment" sector classification, Solux is grouped alongside heavy-electrical-equipment majors such as LS Electric, HD Hyundai Electric, Hyosung Heavy Industries, and Gaon Cable, which center on transformers and cables, but Solux's core products of lighting fixtures and electrical construction work make its business character quite different.

As a result, a simple comparison against sector averages is less useful than separately assessing the core lighting/electrical business and the value of the biotech stake.

04

Earnings analysis

Revenue rose 7.0% from KRW 55.9 billion in 2022 to KRW 59.8 billion in 2023, but after peaking in 2023 it declined for two straight years to KRW 50.7 billion in 2024 (-15.3%) and KRW 38.3 billion in 2025 (-24.5%).

Operating profit swung from an operating loss of KRW 4.32 billion in 2022 to an operating profit of KRW 516 million in 2023, before turning into an operating loss of KRW 6.62 billion in 2024 and continuing with an operating loss of KRW 6.72 billion in 2025, marking two consecutive years of operating losses.

The operating margin improved from -7.7% in 2022 to 0.9% in 2023 before deteriorating again to -13.1% in 2024 and -17.6% in 2025.

Net income attributable to controlling shareholders posted losses that widened from KRW 4.33 billion in 2022 to KRW 6.7 billion in 2023 and KRW 41 billion in 2024, before the loss narrowed to KRW 7.72 billion in 2025.

The outsized 2024 net loss of KRW 41 billion, far larger than that year's operating loss of KRW 6.62 billion, indicates a significant impact from non-operating items.

On a quarterly basis, revenue fell from KRW 9.77 billion in Q2 2025 to KRW 7.9 billion in Q3 2025 (-19.1%), then rose to KRW 15.5 billion in Q4 2025 (+95.7%), fell again to KRW 7.36 billion in Q1 2026 (-52.4%), and rebounded to KRW 10.7 billion in Q2 2026 (+44.7% quarter-on-quarter, +9.1% year-on-year), showing substantial quarter-to-quarter swings.

Operating profit briefly turned positive at KRW 342 million in Q4 2025 before reverting to an operating loss of KRW 3.06 billion in Q1 2026 and continuing with an operating loss of KRW 2.75 billion in Q2 2026, marking two consecutive quarters of operating losses.

In contrast, net income attributable to controlling shareholders moved from a profit of KRW 17.9 billion in Q4 2025 to a loss of KRW 8.53 billion in Q1 2026 and back to a profit of KRW 11.2 billion in Q2 2026, diverging from the operating-profit trend, with the sum across the last four quarters (Q3 2025 through Q2 2026) reaching a net profit of KRW 13.4 billion.

Operating cash flow swung from an inflow of KRW 15.7 billion in 2022 to outflows of KRW 3.66 billion in 2023 and KRW 6.45 billion in 2024, before the outflow narrowed to KRW 2.7 billion in 2025.

05

Industry analysis

The lighting industry is a mature market where price competition has intensified as LED technology became commoditized; although classified under KOSDAQ's 'electrical equipment' sector, it differs fundamentally in product mix from heavy-electrical-equipment majors such as LS Electric, HD Hyundai Electric, Hyosung Heavy Industries, and Gaon Cable, which center on transformers, circuit breakers, and cables.

Heavy-electrical-equipment makers are positioned to benefit from expanding data center, renewable energy, and power-grid investment, whereas lighting makers are more sensitive to construction-cycle conditions and public-procurement order flow.

The two consecutive years of declining core revenue at Solux in 2024-2025 can be interpreted as partly reflecting this industry sensitivity.

In the biotech segment, the Alzheimer's disease treatment market is viewed as a growth area, with competition centered against an already-approved market dominated by anti-amyloid antibody injectables.

AriBio's AR1001 is an oral small-molecule compound rather than an antibody, crossing the blood-brain barrier to act intracellularly, giving it a different mechanism and dosing format compared with existing antibody injectables.

The company describes POLARIS-AD as the largest clinical trial among oral treatments currently in development, with 1,535 patients enrolled across roughly 240 institutions in 13 countries.

However, with major global pharmaceutical companies already established in the market with anti-amyloid antibody therapies, AR1001's commercial positioning will likely hinge on the final Phase 3 data and regulatory approval outcome.

06

Outlook

In the POLARIS-AD Phase 3 trial, AriBio completed the final safety follow-up visit for the last dosed patient in July 2026, after which the process moved into data cleaning, database lock, and statistical analysis.

The company plans to first release topline results as soon as the 52-week efficacy and safety data are available, with detailed results scheduled for the CTAD 2026 opening session in Boston on November 16, 2026.

Solux and AriBio terminated the absorption-merger agreement they had pursued since August 2024, via board resolutions on September 29, 2026.

The stated reasons for termination included Solux's share price falling below the appraisal price for dissenting shareholders' buyback requests as of the day before the termination agreement, raising the risk of large-scale buyback-right exercises, as well as a change in the reference value caused by AriBio's additional exclusive licensing agreements.

The related securities registration statement underwent 14 amendments between September 2024 and June 2026, and received a further amendment request in July 2026.

The day after the merger termination, on October 1, 2026, Solux, AriBio, and Aribiolab held a group vision-declaration event at which each company presented its plan for independent management alongside continued group-level cooperation, and Solux indicated it would pursue AriBio's capital-market entry, including a possible direct IPO.

AriBio has already signed exclusive regional licensing deals with China's Fosun Pharma, UAE-based Arcera, and Korea's Samjin Pharm, using upfront payments of more than KRW 150 billion toward clinical trial costs.

Bull factors

Completion of AR1001 Phase 3 and Upcoming Data Release

AriBio has completed dosing in the POLARIS-AD main trial and the final safety follow-up for the last patient. Detailed results are scheduled to be presented at CTAD 2026 in Boston on November 16, 2026.

With 87.8% of trial participants completing the full treatment period, expectations have built around the completeness of the dataset.

Ownership Commitment Signals from Major Shareholders

Chairman Jeong Jae-joon's side raised its holding ratio to 9.10% through open-market purchases in September 2026. Vice Chairman Seong Soo-hyun also purchased roughly KRW 500 million worth of shares in the open market.

Both purchases occurred immediately after the merger termination and have been cited as a sign of management's commitment.

Upfront-Funded Clinical Financing and Licensing Deals

AriBio has signed exclusive regional licensing agreements with China's Fosun Pharma, UAE-based Arcera, and Korea's Samjin Pharm. Through these deals it secured upfront payments of more than KRW 150 billion for use toward clinical trial costs. This can be read as partial confirmation of external commercial demand for the drug candidate.

Bear factors

Core Revenue Declining for Two Straight Years

Core lighting and electrical revenue peaked at KRW 59.8 billion in 2023 and declined for two consecutive years to KRW 50.7 billion in 2024 and KRW 38.3 billion in 2025. Operating losses also continued for two straight years over 2024-2025, meaning a recovery in core profitability has not yet shown up in the numbers.

Merger Termination Prolongs Uncertainty

The absorption merger between Solux and AriBio, pursued since August 2024, dragged on for over two years before being terminated on September 29, 2026. The related securities registration statement went through 14 amendments, reflecting prolonged procedural difficulty.

AriBio's path to capital markets remains without a confirmed concrete timeline even after the vision-declaration event.

Reliance on Convertible-Bond Financing and Dilution Structure

Both Solux and AriBio have relied on issuing multiple convertible bonds to fund clinical trials and operations. Some of these bonds include terms that lower the conversion price as the share price declines.

If these bonds are converted into shares, the resulting increase in share count could affect existing shareholders' ownership ratios.

Risk factors

Clinical and Regulatory Risk

Until final clinical data are released, AR1001's efficacy and safety outcomes remain unconfirmed. Whether an FDA new drug application will be filed and approved has not yet been determined. If topline results fall short of expectations, it could affect the assessed value of the AriBio stake.

Governance and Disclosure Risk

The merger process involved 14 rounds of amendments to the securities registration statement before ultimately being terminated after more than two years. The repeated schedule changes communicated to investors during this process are a factor worth monitoring for disclosure reliability going forward.

Financial and Dilution Risk

Multiple convertible bonds are outstanding at both Solux and AriBio, with some including terms that lower the conversion price as the share price falls. Early redemptions and refinancing of these bonds are ongoing, making it important to track changes in interest expense and repayment burden.

12

Overall view

Solux is in the process of being reshaped into a holding company with two distinct sources of value: the core lighting and electrical business, and its stake in unlisted biotech affiliate AriBio.

The core business has seen revenue decline for two straight years since peaking at KRW 59.8 billion in 2023, with operating losses also continuing for two straight years, meaning a recovery in profitability has not yet been confirmed by the numbers when looking at lighting and electrical construction alone.

By contrast, net income attributable to controlling shareholders has swung sharply between profit and loss on a quarterly basis, suggesting a greater influence from gains and losses tied to the AriBio stake than from core operating performance.

The absorption merger with AriBio, pursued since August 2024, was terminated in September 2026 after the share price fell below the appraisal price for dissenting shareholders, meaning the path for the unlisted biotech assets to be directly folded into the listed platform is not open for now.

The company held a vision-declaration event the day after the termination, outlining a plan for independent management alongside continued group-level cooperation, and raised the possibility of a separate capital-market entry for AriBio.

In the near term, the detailed AR1001 clinical data to be presented at CTAD on November 16, 2026, followed by Q3 disclosure figures, are likely to provide the next clues for gauging both the core business and the value of the biotech stake.

Ahead of any investment decision, the repeated history of merger-related amendments and the dilution variables embedded in the convertible-bond-based financing structure also warrant close attention.
