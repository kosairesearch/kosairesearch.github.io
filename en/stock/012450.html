02

Business structure

Hanwha Aerospace is a defense and aerospace company whose main segments are ground defense and aerospace.

Its ground defense business centers on the K9 self-propelled howitzer and the Chunmoo multiple rocket launcher, and according to preliminary Q3 2026 results released on October 29, 2026, ground defense revenue was KRW 1.2455 trillion, down 41% year on year.

At the end of Q3 2026, the ground defense backlog stood at about KRW 35 trillion, with exports accounting for 74%.

The aerospace segment posted preliminary revenue of KRW 659.9 billion, up 9% year on year, but an operating loss of KRW 2.4 billion, as losses from the GTF engine risk-sharing partnership (RSP) weighed on profitability despite higher military volumes.

Affiliates Hanwha Systems, which supplies multifunction radars, and Hanwha Ocean, which builds higher-margin commercial vessels, are separately listed companies.

Customers include the Korean military and foreign governments and defense firms in Poland, the United States, and the Middle East, and export destinations have recently widened to Croatia and Spain. In the United States, the K9 wheeled howitzer received the U.S.

Army designation XM1776 and completed a first prototype delivery, while a U.S. Navy landing ship construction contract was also secured.

In the Middle East, a report said UAE defense firm EDGE completed an inspection of the Changwon plant, and export working-level talks on the L-SAM long-range air defense missile have advanced.

In ground systems the company competes with Hyundai-Rotem for orders, and in aerospace it overlaps with Korea Aerospace Industries.

04

Earnings analysis

Annual results show revenue growth alongside a mixed profit trend.

Revenue rose from KRW 6.54 trillion in 2022 to KRW 9.36 trillion in 2023 (up 43.1% year on year), KRW 11.2 trillion in 2024 (up 20.1%), and KRW 26.7 trillion in 2025 (up 137.6%), and it has increased for three consecutive years from 2023 to 2025, with 2025 the largest year in the 2022–2025 span.

Operating profit went from KRW 377.2 billion in 2022 to KRW 691.1 billion in 2023 (up 83.2%), KRW 1.73 trillion in 2024 (up 150.6%), and KRW 3.09 trillion in 2025 (up 78.4%), also rising for three consecutive years. Operating margin was 5.8% in 2022, 7.4% in 2023, and 15.4% in 2024, before easing to 11.6% in 2025.

Net profit attributable to controlling shareholders grew from KRW 200.8 billion in 2022 to KRW 817.5 billion in 2023 (up 307.1%) and KRW 2.3 trillion in 2024 (up 181.2%), then fell to KRW 1.4 trillion in 2025 (down 38.9%).

Net income including non-controlling interests was KRW 2.54 trillion in 2024 and KRW 2.2 trillion in 2025, so the gap between the two measures widened, and in 2025 the decline in controlling-shareholder profit was far steeper than the decline in total net income.

The debt-to-equity ratio fell from 286.3% in 2022 and 317.2% in 2023 to 281.3% in 2024 and 221.4% in 2025. At the end of 2025, total equity was KRW 16.8 trillion, of which controlling-shareholder equity was KRW 9.68 trillion, and total liabilities were KRW 37.2 trillion.

Operating cash flow was KRW 1.52 trillion in 2022, KRW 1.39 trillion in 2023, KRW 1.39 trillion in 2024, and KRW 4.05 trillion in 2025.

Quarterly figures show Q3 2025 revenue of KRW 6.49 trillion and operating profit of KRW 856.4 billion, followed by Q4 2025 revenue of KRW 8.42 trillion (up 29.8% quarter on quarter) and operating profit of KRW 807.6 billion (down 5.7%).

Q1 2026 revenue was KRW 5.75 trillion (down 31.7% quarter on quarter) with operating profit of KRW 638.9 billion (down 20.9%), and Q2 2026 recovered to revenue of KRW 9.29 trillion (up 61.6%) and operating profit of KRW 1.37 trillion (up 113.7%).

Q3 2026 revenue was KRW 6.05 trillion (down 34.9% quarter on quarter and down 6.7% year on year), operating profit was KRW 452 billion (down 66.9% quarter on quarter and down 47.2% year on year), and the company swung to a net loss attributable to controlling shareholders of KRW 137.1 billion.

In its preliminary release, the company attributed the Q3 drop to the concentration of major deliveries in Q4, with some volumes pulled into Q2, along with a production halt at the Daejeon plant and one-off costs of KRW 30 billion.

Over the last four quarters (Q4 2025 to Q3 2026), net profit attributable to controlling shareholders totaled KRW 1.39 trillion, a span with large quarter-to-quarter swings.

Industry analysis

Defense order businesses show quarterly revenue that swings sharply with delivery timing. Hanwha Aerospace's quarterly revenue moved between KRW 5.75 trillion in Q1 2026 and KRW 9.29 trillion in Q2 2026.

With Polish orders declining, Korean defense makers have voiced concern about an order gap, and Middle East air defense exports have been cited as a possible breakthrough.

Within the same sector, a report said Korea Aerospace Industries' cumulative new orders through Q3 2026 reached KRW 2.2093 trillion, about 21.2% of its annual target. A report also said Hyundai-Rotem had not signed a major finished-product export contract through Q3 2026.

By contrast, Hanwha Aerospace has widened its export destinations to Croatia and Spain. In the United States, the Army's adoption of the howitzer and the Navy landing ship award show that the localization strategy is producing results.

In early November 2026, a securities-market report said a rapidly strengthening won and delayed revenue recognition on export shipments had raised earnings concerns, while the same report cited a view that the impact on domestic defense makers' results would be limited.

A report said the credit rating was raised to AA (stable) in November 2025. On the cycle, the backlog has grown, but revenue recognition is tied to delivery schedules, so larger orders do not translate directly into quarterly results.

06

Outlook

In its Q3 2026 results announcement on October 29, 2026, the company said earnings would improve meaningfully from Q4. Major ground defense deliveries are concentrated in Q4, including the second batch of K9 units for Poland. For the full year, the company expects volume growth to continue.

The company is pursuing normal operation of its Daejeon plant in the first half of 2027. Cumulative investment in the plant's capacity expansion rose from KRW 32.9 billion to KRW 47.7 billion, and the completion date was extended from November 2027 to September 2028.

Cumulative production in the first three quarters of 2026 reached KRW 2.3821 trillion, up 14.6% year on year. On exports, the U.S.

Army assigned the K9 wheeled howitzer the designation XM1776, and working-level talks with the UAE on L-SAM have advanced, although the contract size and delivery schedule have not been disclosed.

On the domestic side, the company was selected as preferred negotiator for a 10,000-pound-class aircraft engine development program announced on November 15, 2026, with a total project size of KRW 177.3 billion and development running through October 2031.

A report also said the company is pursuing a controlling stake in Philly Shipyard. Whether these schedules translate into revenue and profitability is the next thing to watch.

Bull factors

Large backlog with high export share

At the end of Q3 2026, the ground defense backlog was about KRW 35 trillion, with exports at 74%. In a structure with many long-term contracts, revenue can continue as long as delivery schedules hold. Total equity at the end of 2025 was KRW 16.8 trillion, providing a financial base for order expansion.

Q4 delivery concentration and management's outlook

In its October 29, 2026 announcement, the company said earnings would improve meaningfully from Q4. Major deliveries, including the second batch of K9 units for Poland, are concentrated in that quarter.

Operating profit reached KRW 1.37 trillion in Q2 2026, so whether Q4 captures the deliveries will be the key test for the next result.

Diversifying overseas business in the U.S. and Middle East

In the U.S., the K9 wheeled howitzer received the XM1776 designation, and the landing ship award opened entry into the naval shipbuilding market. Reports say working-level talks with the UAE on the L-SAM system have advanced. Widening export destinations to Croatia and Spain also adds geographic diversification.

09

Bear factors

Large swings in quarterly revenue and profit

Q1 2026 revenue fell 31.7% quarter on quarter, rose 61.6% in Q2, then fell 34.9% in Q3. Operating profit dropped from KRW 1.37 trillion in Q2 to KRW 452 billion in Q3. The results confirm a structure in which quarterly earnings swing sharply with delivery timing.

One-off costs and aerospace losses

In the preliminary Q3 release, one-off costs of KRW 30 billion related to the Daejeon plant accident were reflected. The aerospace segment swung to an operating loss as the GTF engine RSP business posted a KRW 22.7 billion operating loss. Analysts also described domestic business profitability as near break-even.

Profit decline and debt burden

Net profit attributable to controlling shareholders fell 38.9% to KRW 1.4 trillion in 2025. Total liabilities at the end of 2025 were KRW 37.2 trillion, well above equity attributable to the owners of the parent.

Operating margin eased from 15.4% in 2024 to 11.6% in 2025, so revenue growth did not translate into margin gains.

Risk factors

Production disruption and safety

A Daejeon plant accident halted production and triggered one-off costs in Q3. The company said it would prioritize safety and minimize the impact on output. Because normal operation is targeted for the first half of 2027, delivery schedules could still be disrupted in the interim.

Currency and revenue recognition timing

A report in early November 2026 said a strengthening won and delayed revenue recognition on export shipments raised earnings concerns. The company has indicated export volumes carry higher profitability than domestic volumes, so the timing of export deliveries directly affects quarterly profit. Currency translation also remains a variable given the high export share.

Uncertainty in export contracts

Reports say a decline in Polish orders has raised concerns about an order gap for Korean defense makers. The UAE L-SAM export is at the working-level negotiation stage, and its contract size and delivery schedule have not been disclosed.

Until the timing and scale of large export contracts are confirmed, the pace of backlog growth remains uncertain.

Overall view

Hanwha Aerospace held a ground defense backlog of about KRW 35 trillion at the end of Q3 2026, with exports at 74%. Revenue and operating profit rose for three consecutive years from 2023 to 2025, and 2025 was the largest year in the 2022–2025 span at KRW 26.7 trillion.

However, operating margin eased from 15.4% in 2024 to 11.6% in 2025, and Q3 2026 showed sharp quarterly swings, with operating profit of KRW 452 billion and a net loss attributable to controlling shareholders of KRW 137.1 billion.

The company attributed these to the concentration of Q4 deliveries, the Daejeon plant production halt, and RSP losses on GTF engines. Management has said earnings would improve from Q4, and export and U.S. localization efforts are advancing.

Still unconfirmed are Q4 delivery results, the timing of the Daejeon plant's normal operation, and the size and timing of the L-SAM contract. The next checkpoints are the preliminary Q4 2026 results and the operating margin in the 2026 annual report.
