Choil Aluminum: Q3 Loss Amid a Value-Up Plan

Choil Aluminum swung to an operating loss of KRW 604 million in the third quarter of 2026, while a value-up plan announced in late July 2026 covering share cancellation and the resumption of cash dividends sits alongside a first-quarter 2026 operating profit of KRW 13.1 billion.

02

Business structure

Choil Aluminum is a specialist aluminum rolling company that melts and processes aluminum ingots into sheet and coil products. The company's corporate information lists its founding date as September 19, 1985, and its KOSPI listing dates to November 1988.

According to company disclosures, the aluminum rolling business accounts for 99.9% of sales. Core products are aluminum foil stock, battery cathode-foil materials, and industrial aluminum sheet for building interiors and exteriors, while the remaining 0.1% comes from processing aluminum plate.

Press reports highlight the company's combination of continuous casting, slab casting and hot rolling lines as rare among domestic producers. In the first half of 2026, industrial materials sales were 77.5% domestic and 22.5% export, and most exports are local exports routed through domestic customers.

Major customers are domestic aluminum foil manufacturers, including Lotte Infracell and Dongwon Systems, which together account for 44% of sales. The company has disclosed a long-term supply contract with Lotte Aluminum covering battery cathode foil and general-purpose strip.

Selling prices are reported to be set with reference to LME aluminum prices, so raw-material market moves drive both sales scale and margins. The largest shareholder group, led by Lee Young-ho, held 41.98% as of December 2025.

04

Earnings analysis

On an annual basis, revenue was KRW 562.4 billion in 2022, fell 17.2% year on year to KRW 465.8 billion in 2023, which is the smallest revenue year in the 2022 to 2025 period.

Operating profit that year dropped 46.5% to KRW 8.47 billion, with an operating margin of 1.8%, and the company moved to a net loss attributable to controlling shareholders of KRW 134 million.

In 2024, revenue recovered to KRW 498.4 billion, up 7.0%, operating profit rose 137.3% to KRW 20.1 billion with a 4.0% operating margin, and net income attributable to controlling shareholders turned positive at KRW 11 billion.

In 2025, revenue was KRW 486.7 billion, down 2.3%, operating profit fell 31.4% to KRW 13.8 billion, and the operating margin slipped to 2.8%. The company attributed the decline mainly to lower sales volume and the resulting pressure on profitability.

Net income attributable to controlling shareholders was KRW 7.98 billion in 2025, down 27.4%. The debt-to-equity ratio moved from 96.7% in 2022 to 104.2% in 2023 and 111.2% in 2024 before falling to 96.2% in 2025. Operating cash flow went from negative KRW 3.75 billion in 2023 to KRW 17.7 billion in 2025.

Quarterly, operating profit was KRW 1 billion in the third quarter of 2025 and KRW 1.15 billion in the fourth quarter, then jumped to KRW 13.1 billion in the first quarter of 2026.

Second-quarter 2026 operating profit was KRW 10 billion, down 23.5% from the prior quarter, and the third quarter turned into an operating loss of KRW 604 million.

Third-quarter revenue of KRW 129.1 billion rose 13.7% year on year, while profit moved the other way, and the net loss attributable to controlling shareholders widened to KRW 2.16 billion from KRW 417 million a year earlier.

A securities analyst's mid-2026 report expected average selling prices to peak in the second quarter and cost of sales to rise in the second half.

05

Industry analysis

Aluminum rolling conditions are being driven sharply by raw-material prices and tariff policy. Press reports said LME aluminum prices approached USD 3,500 per tonne, close to their highest level in more than four years, with a 50% US import tariff and the Strait of Hormuz disruption adding supply anxiety.

The same reports noted that spot price moves pass into supply prices with a lag of several months, so price spikes did not translate directly into profit.

Market-share estimates for domestic aluminum rolling differ by source: one report cited 11.4%, while a securities analyst quoted by Daily Invest estimated about 10.0%.

The domestic rolling market faces ongoing competition from imported material, and the company points to delivery responsiveness and quality control as its advantages.

On the demand side, wider electric-vehicle battery use is raising quality standards for cathode-foil materials, particularly thickness uniformity and surface finish.

The same analyst estimated that sales to a major foil customer rose from KRW 82.8 billion in 2022 to KRW 129.1 billion in 2025 and would reach KRW 153.0 billion in 2026, up 18.5%.

That estimate predates the third-quarter results, and the actual third-quarter operating loss of KRW 604 million was larger than the analyst's expected loss of about KRW 200 million. How quickly the company can close the lag between cost increases and selling-price adjustments remains the key industry variable.

06

Outlook

The value-up plan disclosed on July 28, 2026 is the most concrete forward schedule currently available. Its first pillar is improving the price-to-book ratio, with targets of 0.7 to 0.8 times in 2027 and at least 1.0 times by 2029.

To get there, the company plans to raise core profitability step by step, citing production efficiency and a higher share of value-added products as means. The shareholder return ratio is targeted at 10% in 2027 and at least 15% by 2029.

As part of the plan, the company signed a KRW 10 billion share buyback trust agreement with NH Investment & Securities covering about 8.6% of total issued shares, and it intends to cancel all repurchased shares.

Cash dividends were suspended after the 2018 year-end dividend and are scheduled to resume with the 2026 year-end dividend. At the same time, the company decided on a 5-for-1 share consolidation, which would reduce issued shares from 126,631,721 to 25,326,344 once completed.

The company itself has described earnings volatility as high because of the timing gap between aluminum raw-material costs and selling prices. On the equipment side, a 60-tonne annealing furnace was installed in October 2024, and expanding battery cathode-foil supply is presented as a medium-term growth axis.

Full-year 2026 results, which will be the first benchmark for resuming dividends, will be settled by cumulative results through the third quarter and selling-price trends in the fourth quarter.

Raw-material prices and tariff exposure

Press reports said LME aluminum prices rose close to four-year highs, yet the company's annual profit remained constrained. A 50% US import tariff and the Strait of Hormuz disruption added cost pressure. Operating profit fell 31.4% in 2025, showing how raw-material swings feed directly into reported results.

Customer concentration and demand swings

Sales to major customers account for 44% of the total. A company official attributed the 2025 volume decline partly to a broader slowdown in the battery materials industry. Competition from imported material in the domestic rolling market may also limit pricing power.

Risk factors

Raw-material price pass-through lag

Aluminum spot price moves reach supply prices with a lag of several months, which amplifies quarterly profit swings. Results diverged sharply within 2026, from KRW 13.1 billion of operating profit in the first quarter to a KRW 604 million operating loss in the third. The company itself describes earnings volatility as high, so the speed of selling-price adjustments is central.

Customer concentration and battery demand

With 44% of sales tied to major customers, order changes from a single customer can move results quickly. The company attributed lower 2025 volumes partly to a slowdown in battery materials demand. Equipment and process upgrades to meet rising quality standards for cathode foil will remain necessary.

Shareholder-return execution and balance sheet

Dividends have been suspended since the 2018 year-end dividend, so whether the 2026 year-end dividend is actually paid is the next test. Total liabilities at the end of 2025 were KRW 201.5 billion against total equity of KRW 209.6 billion, and the debt-to-equity ratio fell from 111.2% in 2024 to 96.2% in 2025. Running buybacks, cancellations and dividends together will require earnings to hold up.

Overall view

Choil Aluminum's revenue peaked at KRW 562.4 billion in 2022, then went through a loss in 2023 before its operating margin recovered to 4.0% in 2024, and operating profit fell again to KRW 13.8 billion in 2025.

Profit concentrated in the first and second quarters of 2026, at KRW 13.1 billion and KRW 10 billion of operating profit, but the third quarter swung to a KRW 604 million operating loss. The core driver of earnings volatility is the lag between aluminum raw-material prices and selling prices of several months.
