02

Business structure

Koas is an office furniture manufacturer founded in 1992 and listed on the KOSPI market in 2005. Office furniture products made up 70.45% of 2025 sales, office furniture merchandise 26.58%, and construction work 2.97%.

The company offers customer space solutions under the WITH brand and is expanding into AI and IoT-based smart offices. Public procurement is a major channel, with Public Procurement Service sales of KRW 16.06 billion in the first half of 2026, or 43.6% of revenue.

Direct sales reached KRW 10.57 billion and dealer sales KRW 10.2 billion in the same period. By product, other items led standalone sales at KRW 9.335 billion, followed by chairs at KRW 9.065 billion and desks at KRW 5.713 billion.

Domestic dependence is high, as 97.60% of 2024 office furniture product sales were domestic. In an August 2026 release, the company said it would broaden growth with ESG furniture certified for carbon storage in the second half.

With procurement above 40% of sales, public tender outcomes and private office demand both move quarterly revenue.

04

Earnings analysis

Annual revenue went from KRW 97.9 billion in 2022 to KRW 73.7 billion in 2023 (down 24.7%), KRW 78 billion in 2024 (up 5.7%) and KRW 62.2 billion in 2025 (down 20.2%). 2022 was the largest year in the period and 2025 the smallest.

Operating losses were KRW 705 million in 2022, KRW 3.03 billion in 2023, KRW 7.24 billion in 2024 and KRW 5.68 billion in 2025, a four-year run of losses from 2022 to 2025. The operating margin moved from -0.7% in 2022 to -4.1%, -9.3% and -9.1% in 2025.

The net loss attributable to controlling shareholders widened from KRW 1.91 billion in 2022 to KRW 6.87 billion, KRW 8.87 billion and KRW 31.3 billion in 2025, while the net loss including non-controlling interests was KRW 31.4 billion in 2025.

The debt ratio fell from 3,300.6% in 2024 to 134.5% in 2025, with total equity of KRW 34.7 billion (KRW 34.5 billion attributable to controlling shareholders) and total liabilities of KRW 46.7 billion at year-end 2025.

Operating cash flow was -KRW 4.5 billion in 2022, KRW 789 million in 2023, -KRW 1.63 billion in 2024 and -KRW 5.38 billion in 2025.

Quarterly revenue rose from KRW 12 billion in Q3 2025 to KRW 19 billion in Q4 2025 (up 57.6% quarter on quarter), peaked at KRW 21.4 billion in Q1 2026, then fell to KRW 15.8 billion in Q2 (down 26.0%) and KRW 14.7 billion in Q3 (down 6.7%), a two-quarter decline; Q3 revenue was still up 22.3% year on year.

Operating profit of KRW 1.9 billion in Q1 2026 was followed by an operating loss of KRW 771 million in Q2 and KRW 4.05 billion in Q3, and the Q3 loss was larger than a year earlier.

A company release for the first half of 2026 reported that gross profit rose 38.9% while SG&A fell 5.0%, but reporting indicated that investment asset valuation losses and net financing costs outweighed the core improvement, keeping net results negative.

Net income attributable to controlling shareholders turned positive at KRW 123 million in Q1 2026, then recorded losses of KRW 3.11 billion in Q2 and KRW 1.97 billion in Q3, with the Q3 loss narrower than the KRW 21.4 billion loss a year earlier. The last four quarters total a net loss attributable to controlling shareholders of KRW 5.75 billion.

05

Industry analysis

Office furniture demand has been held back by a global economic slowdown and weak domestic demand, and the company attributed a narrower operating loss partly to government economic recovery policies. Reporting on first-half 2026 results described private office furniture demand as still uncertain.

Within that backdrop, the company grew sales by expanding public procurement. With procurement at 43.6% of sales, tender outcomes can amplify quarterly revenue swings.

The cycle position can be gauged by whether private office investment and public orders move in the same direction, and the two straight quarterly revenue declines after the Q1 2026 peak point to a weaker demand signal.

The company runs direct, dealer and procurement channels side by side, so channel-level demand can be tracked separately. Overseas sales are a small share, which keeps the company sensitive to domestic demand.

Plant consolidation and production efficiency helped reduce cost pressure, a factor that contributed to margin improvement independent of the industry backdrop.

Listed companies in the same electronics and components classification have different business structures, which limits direct comparison of profitability metrics.

The 2026 annual report, the next periodic filing, will disclose the full-year operating margin and capital structure. Compared with equity of about KRW 2.4 billion at end-2024, the capital base has changed, so the trend in the debt ratio and total equity will be the benchmark for financial stability.

Bull factors

First-Half 2026 Core Turnaround

Per a company release in August 2026, first-half consolidated revenue reached KRW 37.152 billion, up 19.1% year on year, and operating profit of KRW 1.127 billion marked a turn from loss.

The operating margin improved from -6.9% to 3.0%, which the company attributed to plant consolidation, cost reduction and lower SG&A. This compares with four straight years of operating losses from 2022 to 2025.

Public Procurement Channel

Public Procurement Service sales reached KRW 16.06 billion in the first half of 2026, or 43.6% of total revenue. In a period of uncertain private demand, public orders help underpin the revenue base. Alongside direct sales of KRW 10.57 billion and dealer sales of KRW 10.2 billion, the company runs three sales channels. Reporting also said Q2 2026 revenue rose 45.4% year on year.

Capital Structure Repair and Price Below Book Value

The debt ratio fell from 3,300.6% in 2024 to 134.5% in 2025, with total equity of KRW 34.7 billion at year-end 2025. Compared with equity of about KRW 2.4 billion at end-2024, the capital base has changed significantly. The price-to-book ratio sits well below 1x, so the share price is low relative to book value. Equity recovery is cited as a factor that reduces financial risk.

Financial Asset Losses and Funding Burden

Risk factors

Demand Risk

Reporting describes private office furniture demand as still uncertain. Procurement accounts for 43.6% of sales, so cuts in public orders or tender outcomes could swing quarterly revenue sharply. High domestic dependence means the company is directly exposed to domestic economic conditions and corporate investment. Revenue declines in Q2 and Q3 2026 show how this risk can show up in results.

Investment Asset Valuation Risk

First-half 2026 reporting argued that price swings and recoverability of investment assets have become key variables for corporate value. When investment asset valuation losses exceed core improvements, net income stays negative. Net financing costs add another source of volatility. Net results can swing independently of operating performance.

Governance Risk

Reporting said the company declared a hostile M&A bid for a delisted firm, intensifying a management dispute. Concerns were also raised about repeated investments in businesses unrelated to its core operations. Without clear investment purpose and recovery plans, financial burden could grow. Management-related issues could also affect capital raising and decision-making.

Overall view

Koas is an office furniture manufacturer that posted four consecutive years of losses from 2022 to 2025, with the 2025 net loss attributable to controlling shareholders widening to KRW 31.3 billion.

It swung to operating profit of KRW 1.9 billion in Q1 2026, and the first-half turnaround was supported by higher public procurement sales and lower SG&A. However, operating losses of KRW 771 million in Q2 and KRW 4.05 billion in Q3 mean profitability has not held on a quarterly basis.

The Q3 net loss attributable to controlling shareholders narrowed to KRW 1.97 billion from a year earlier, and the last four quarters total KRW 5.75 billion.

On the balance sheet, the debt ratio fell to 134.5% at year-end 2025 from 3,300.6% in 2024, but total equity of KRW 34.7 billion and negative operating cash flow of -KRW 5.38 billion remain burdens.

Strengths include the turnaround, equity recovery and procurement channel, while weaknesses include consecutive losses, investment asset valuation losses and uncertain private demand.

The next checkpoints are the full-year operating margin in the 2026 annual report and whether operating profit holds in the Q1 2027 quarterly report. This summary provides information only and does not contain an investment view.
