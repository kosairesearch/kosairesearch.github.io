02

Business structure

Chinyang Poly Urethane produces flexible polyurethane slabstock foam (SLAB FOAM) and supplies it as an intermediate material to industrial users in automotive interiors, furniture, mattresses, footwear, electronics and apparel.

According to company disclosures and industry reports, polyurethane foam accounts for the whole of its revenue, so the business is a single-product structure.

Its main product is foam used in beds and sofas; demand rose during the work-from-home period in 2021, lifting revenue by 43.0% to KRW 530.7 million-equivalent levels reported in a 2023 technical analysis, after which revenue stagnated in 2022.

The domestic polyurethane foam market is described as highly competitive with many manufacturers, and a public disclosure summary cites the company's domestic market share at 16%.

Raw materials centre on chemical inputs such as polyols, and margins move with the spread between raw material prices and selling prices as well as plant utilisation. A 2023 report described plans to build a new plant in the Eumseong Yongsan industrial complex in Chungbuk.

At the March 2026 shareholder meeting, recycled polyol and polyurethane manufacturing, processing and sales were added to the articles of incorporation, creating room to widen the business toward raw materials.

Customers span automotive parts, furniture and bedding, and footwear materials, so demand shifts in end markets pass directly into revenue.

04

Earnings analysis

On disclosed annual figures, revenue went from KRW 52.7 billion in 2022 to KRW 54.5 billion in 2023 (up 3.4% year on year), then KRW 54 billion in 2024 (down 0.9%) and KRW 46.6 billion in 2025 (down 13.7%).

Revenue has declined for two consecutive years, from 2024 through 2025. 2023 was the largest year for revenue in the 2022–2025 span and 2025 the smallest.

Operating profit rose from KRW 3.29 billion in 2022 to KRW 3.96 billion in 2023 (up 20.4%) and KRW 3.97 billion in 2024 (up 0.3%), then fell to KRW 1.83 billion in 2025 (down 54.0%). 2024 was the largest year for operating profit and 2025 the smallest.

The operating margin went from 6.2% in 2022 and 7.3% in both 2023 and 2024 to 3.9% in 2025. In 2025 operating profit fell far more than revenue (54.0% versus 13.7%), which suggests cost and selling expense pressure pulled the margin down.

Profit attributable to controlling shareholders was KRW 3.49 billion in 2022, KRW 3.66 billion in 2023 (up 4.9%), KRW 3.16 billion in 2024 (down 13.6%) and KRW 1.62 billion in 2025 (down 48.7%), declining for two consecutive years from 2024 through 2025.

Net income including non-controlling interests matched the controlling-shareholder figure in each disclosed year. Operating cash flow was KRW 4.94 billion in 2022, KRW 5.3 billion in 2023 and KRW 5.17 billion in 2024, then fell to KRW 3.08 billion in 2025, so cash generation weakened along with earnings.

Quarterly revenue was KRW 11.8 billion in Q3 2025, KRW 11.9 billion in Q4 2025, KRW 12.4 billion in Q1 2026, KRW 14.9 billion in Q2 2026 and KRW 15 billion in Q3 2026, rising for four consecutive quarters from Q4 2025 through Q3 2026.

Quarterly operating profit was KRW 617 million in Q3 2025, KRW 437 million in Q4 2025 and KRW 290 million in Q1 2026, then KRW 1.77 billion in Q2 2026 (up 512.6% quarter on quarter) and KRW 1.46 billion in Q3 2026 (down 17.7% quarter on quarter, up 137.0% year on year).

Profit attributable to controlling shareholders summed to KRW 3.36 billion over the last four quarters, from Q4 2025 through Q3 2026. Reporting on the Q3 2026 quarterly report puts January–September revenue at KRW 42.3 billion (up 22.0%) and cumulative operating profit at KRW 3.5 billion (up 153.9%).

Whether the Q2 2026 jump came from one-off items needs to be checked against the line-item breakdown in the filings, and whether profit at this level persists in the following quarters is the key test of the earnings base.

Industry analysis

End markets for Chinyang Poly Urethane include automotive interiors, furniture and bedding, footwear and electronics, and when construction, automotive, electrical and electronics activity slows, polyurethane foam demand falls.

According to an fnguide company summary, cumulative standalone revenue for January–September 2025 fell 17.4% year on year and operating profit fell 61.7%, a decline the summary attributes to weaker end markets.

Foam demand for beds and sofas surged during the work-from-home period in 2021, and revenue stagnated in 2022 as that boost faded. Quarterly revenue has risen for four straight quarters into 2026, but whether this reflects an end-market recovery or company-specific factors cannot be separated from the filings alone.

On raw materials, polyol price swings relative to selling prices are cited as a key driver of margin volatility.

On competition, the domestic polyurethane foam market has several manufacturers, and the company's market share is cited at 16%, though the date of that figure is not clear, so it should be read as a reference point.

Compared with large petrochemical affiliates, its product focus is narrower, which makes earnings more sensitive to raw material costs and end-market swings. The March 2026 addition of recycled polyol to the business purpose points to a possible expansion on the raw material side.

The pace of any industry recovery is best judged by combining end-market indicators with the company's quarterly results.

Bear factors

Sharp 2025 annual decline

Revenue fell 13.7% to KRW 46.6 billion in 2025, and operating profit fell 54.0% to KRW 1.83 billion. Profit attributable to controlling shareholders fell 48.7% to KRW 1.62 billion, declining for two consecutive years from 2024 through 2025.

The operating margin of 3.9% is the lowest in the disclosed 2022–2025 span, which is the bearish point on annual earnings strength.

Rising debt and weaker cash flow

Operating cash flow fell from KRW 5.17 billion in 2024 to KRW 3.08 billion in 2025. Combined with the facility investment plan, the financial burden could grow, which is the bearish point.

Rights offering dilution and earnings volatility

The first tranche of the rights offering was indicated at KRW 2,080 per new share, and final issue terms and payment results will come through follow-up disclosures. If the new shares are issued, existing shareholders' stakes could be diluted.

Quarterly operating profit swung from KRW 290 million in Q1 2026 to KRW 1.77 billion in Q2 2026, and this large volatility is a bearish point.

Risk factors

Raw material prices and margins

Swings in raw material costs such as polyols can shift the spread against selling prices and move margins. If the Q2 2026 jump in operating profit does not persist, quarterly earnings volatility will widen. How quickly raw material costs can be passed through to selling prices is the central profitability risk.

Recycled polyol execution

Whether recycled polyol manufacturing and sales, added to the business purpose in March 2026, translate into actual revenue has not yet been confirmed in disclosed results.

Widening the scope into raw materials requires securing equipment and developing customers together, and this may add investment and working capital needs. If execution is delayed, the raw material expansion room is unlikely to be reflected beyond the wording of the business purpose.

End-market demand and fundraising

If end markets such as automotive, furniture, footwear and electronics weaken, polyurethane foam demand falls. Cumulative revenue for January–September 2025 had fallen 17.4% year on year (fnguide company summary).

Depending on the size and execution of the rights offering, funding conditions and the shareholder structure could change.

Overall view

Quarterly results have clearly recovered in 2026. Revenue has risen for four straight quarters, Q3 2026 operating profit was up 137.0% year on year, and cumulative January–September revenue rose 22.0%.

On disclosed figures, however, 2025 annual revenue, operating profit and profit attributable to controlling shareholders all fell, and the operating margin dropped to 3.9%. The debt ratio also rose from 50.1% in 2022 to 139.3% in 2025.

Bullish factors include the quarterly recovery, the capacity expansion plan and room to expand into raw materials through recycled polyol, while bearish factors include the annual decline, the financial burden, rights offering dilution and raw material margin swings.

With both trends present, the next checkpoints are the full-year results in the 2026 annual report, execution of the facility investment, and the final terms of the rights offering.
