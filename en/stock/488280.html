Overseas Growth Continues as Losses Persist at AI Security Firm

02

Business structure

S2W is a data intelligence company founded in 2018 that develops security and decision-support solutions based on technology linking and analyzing data from multiple sources.

Its main business areas are dark web intelligence, cyber threat intelligence (CTI) and digital forensics, delivered through the QUAXAR and XARVIS platforms.

XARVIS is an AI security platform for government and public agencies, QUAXAR is a security AI solution for enterprises and institutions, and SAIP is an ontology-based decision operating system.

XARVIS includes DRI, an investigation-focused AI agent that answers natural-language questions by combining multiple analysis tools. QUAXAR is presented as a solution that analyzes dispersed threat information to indicate attack likelihood and response priorities.

On the customer side, the company is the only Korean partner in the Interpol Gateway Initiative and has been reported to provide data to Microsoft Security Copilot.

International organizations, friendly-nation special agencies in Asia, the Middle East and Europe, and global large enterprises form its main customer base. QUAXAR was supplied to a Greek government agency in 2025, which serves as a public-sector reference in Europe.

Overseas revenue exceeded 20% of total in each of the last two years. A growth pillar is AI service vulnerability diagnosis, and in late July 2026 the company said it had won such a project from a large domestic financial company.

It also plans to use investment proceeds for a synthetic data platform for industrial AI and to expand into global manufacturing and robotics markets. The relative weight of the public and enterprise lines is a point to confirm in the filed report.

04

Earnings analysis

On a filed basis, 2025 revenue rose 5.0% year on year to KRW 10.1 billion, while the operating loss widened from KRW 4.5 billion in 2024 to KRW 5.64 billion in 2025. The operating margin fell from -46.8% in 2024 to -55.9% in 2025, meaning costs grew faster than revenue.

The net loss attributable to controlling shareholders narrowed from KRW 14.1 billion in 2024 to KRW 5.29 billion in 2025, even as the operating loss widened, so non-operating items and cost-structure changes need to be checked in the business report's income discussion.

Total net loss including non-controlling interests moved in the same direction, from KRW 14.1 billion to KRW 5.29 billion.

The balance sheet improved: the debt-to-equity ratio fell from 230.2% in 2024 to 31.9% in 2025, with total liabilities of KRW 7.39 billion and total equity of KRW 23.1 billion at year-end 2025, all of which is attributable to controlling shareholders.

Operating cash flow worsened from -KRW 3.27 billion in 2024 to -KRW 4.28 billion in 2025. Quarterly, the fourth quarter of 2025 showed revenue of KRW 3.7 billion, an operating loss of KRW 482 million and a net loss attributable to controlling shareholders of KRW 311 million.

First-quarter 2026 revenue fell 41.2% quarter on quarter to KRW 2.18 billion, and the operating loss widened to KRW 2.25 billion. Second-quarter revenue was KRW 2.09 billion, down 4.2%, with the operating loss widening to KRW 3.16 billion.

Third-quarter revenue was KRW 2.13 billion, up 1.9%, and the operating loss narrowed to KRW 2.93 billion. Over the last four quarters, the net loss attributable to controlling shareholders totaled KRW 8.39 billion, and all four quarters were loss-making.

With revenue flat in the KRW 2 billion range, the narrowing of the operating loss in the third quarter is the main observation point in this period.

05

Industry analysis

The market S2W operates in covers cyber threat intelligence and AI security. Unlike conventional security equipment that blocks attacks after they occur, the company emphasizes a preventive approach that detects signs of attack in advance.

The company's CEO described Europe as a strategically important market where security regulation is tightening and investment continues.

As enterprises adopt generative AI and agents, demand is emerging to test the vulnerabilities of AI services themselves, and the company is expanding its AI service vulnerability diagnosis business in line with this trend.

It says it applies real attack techniques collected from dark web, Telegram and hacking forums directly in its testing. On technical credentials, its papers have been accepted at ACL, NAACL and EMNLP natural language processing conferences for four consecutive years since 2022.

Among listed peers in the same sector, Ahnlab is the closest comparison for security software, while Samsung SDS, LG CNS and Hyundai Autoever in public IT, and NAVER and Kakao in AI platforms, have different business structures.

Because the business depends on public and government procurement, quarterly revenue can fluctuate with project timing, a factor to weigh when reading the industry. Its 2026 Korea AI Industry Award, a Deputy Prime Minister-level prize in the industry category, is cited as external recognition of its AI technology. The link between procurement timing and quarterly revenue is the key item to track.

Outlook

At the June 2026 award ceremony, the company said it would accelerate growth in overseas business and aim to be recognized as an AI company in global markets. At it-sa 2026 in Nuremberg, held October 27 to 29, 2026, it showcased XARVIS and QUAXAR and discussed cooperation with potential customers and partners.

The company introduced an investigation-focused AI agent built into XARVIS and said it is widening its reach into European public and enterprise markets, building on its supply reference in Greece.

In September 2026, it announced participation in a specialized AI foundation model project for cybersecurity led by the Ministry of Science and ICT and NIPA, where it plans to apply its threat intelligence and security-focused AI capabilities.

In late July 2026 it announced an expansion of its AI service vulnerability diagnosis business, citing a large financial company contract as an initial reference. It said investment proceeds will go toward a synthetic data platform for industrial AI and upgrades to its data infrastructure.

The item that links most directly to results is when won contracts are recognized as revenue. The next checkpoint is whether fourth-quarter and full-year 2026 revenue move beyond the KRW 2 billion range seen through 2026.

First-quarter 2027 results will be compared with the first quarter of 2026, when revenue was KRW 2.18 billion and the operating loss was KRW 2.25 billion.

Whether the foundation model project and the financial sector contract begin to appear in quarterly revenue is a question for the revenue breakdown in the annual business report.

08

Bull factors

Sustained overseas revenue share

The company has run its business with international organizations, overseas government agencies and large enterprises, and its overseas revenue share has exceeded 20% for two consecutive years. With domestic revenue still small, keeping that overseas share supports diversification.

Its role as the only Korean partner in the Interpol Gateway Initiative is cited as a basis for international cooperation.

Expanding public and global references

QUAXAR was supplied to a Greek government agency in 2025, giving the company public-sector experience in Europe. At the October 2026 it-sa exhibition, it used that reference to discuss cooperation with prospective customers and partners. Accumulated references like this provide a basis for judging future public procurement in Europe.

Contract base in new AI businesses

The company announced an expansion of its AI service vulnerability diagnosis business. It also said that, alongside the launch, it had won such a project from a large domestic financial company. A separate business line for testing AI services alongside its existing security products could broaden its revenue sources.

09

Bear factors

Four straight quarters of operating losses

The company has posted operating losses in each of the four quarters from the fourth quarter of 2025 through the third quarter of 2026. The second-quarter 2026 operating loss of KRW 3.16 billion was the largest in that span.

The third-quarter operating loss narrowed to KRW 2.93 billion but still far exceeds quarterly revenue of KRW 2.13 billion, so continued losses drain financial resources.

Stalled quarterly revenue

Revenue fell 41.2% quarter on quarter, from KRW 3.7 billion in the fourth quarter of 2025 to KRW 2.18 billion in the first quarter of 2026. It then moved between KRW 2.09 billion and KRW 2.13 billion in the second and third quarters, without a clear recovery trend.

Annual revenue rose 5.0% to KRW 10.1 billion in 2025, but that growth has not carried into a steady quarterly uptrend.

Accumulated losses and cash outflows

The net loss attributable to controlling shareholders totaled KRW 8.39 billion over the last four quarters. Operating cash flow worsened to -KRW 4.28 billion in 2025 from -KRW 3.27 billion in 2024. Whether the narrowing of losses translates into better operating cash flow remains an open question.

10

Risk factors

Public procurement volatility

A substantial part of the business relies on public and government procurement, so project timing feeds directly into quarterly revenue. As the 41.2% quarterly revenue decline in the first quarter of 2026 shows, gaps in procurement can sharply change the quarterly trend.

The gap between contract announcements and revenue recognition also makes quarterly results harder to interpret.

Financial capacity and sustained losses

Total liabilities were KRW 7.39 billion and total equity KRW 23.1 billion at year-end 2025, but operating losses have continued for two consecutive years. Larger operating cash outflows could create a need for additional funding. Whether cost-structure improvements that reduce the loss actually materialize is the central question.

Technology competition and overseas execution

The AI security market includes large platform companies alongside specialized security firms, so the durability of the company's technological edge is a key question. Overseas expansion depends heavily on local regulation and partners, so the pace may differ from plans.

Entry into European public markets will be tested in practice when additional references appear beyond the Greek case.

12

Overall view

S2W operates the XARVIS platform for government and public agencies and the QUAXAR platform for enterprises, built on data intelligence technology, and has kept its overseas revenue share above 20% for two consecutive years.

Revenue rose 5.0% to KRW 10.1 billion in 2025, but the operating loss widened to KRW 5.64 billion, and operating losses have continued for four straight quarters through the third quarter of 2026.

First-quarter 2026 revenue fell 41.2% quarter on quarter to KRW 2.18 billion, and revenue has since moved within the KRW 2 billion range. On the other hand, the third-quarter 2026 operating loss narrowed to KRW 2.93 billion from the second quarter, an early sign of loss reduction.

The debt-to-equity ratio fell from 230.2% in 2024 to 31.9% in 2025, so the balance sheet has moved in a better direction. When new businesses such as AI service vulnerability diagnosis and the foundation model project begin to show up in revenue is the next point to watch.

The next checkpoints are the annual operating margin in the 2026 business report and first-quarter 2027 results.
