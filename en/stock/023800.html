02

Business structure

Inzi Controls manufactures and sells automotive cooling and engine-management components, and its disclosed business segment is automotive alone.

Key products include plastic air intake manifolds, water temperature control (WTC) parts, thermostats, fuel pressure regulators, engineering plastic parts, sensors and switches, and valves.

As of December 2025, plastic air intake manifolds accounted for 24.33% of sales, WTC parts for 10.43%, thermostats for 2.70%, and fuel pressure regulators for 0.79%, while other products made up 61.70%. Domestic sales were 70.90% of the total in the same period and exports 29.10%.

As of June 2026, the company's share of the domestic TMS parts market stood at 85.00%, and its share of engineering plastic parts at 40.00%.

Overseas, subsidiaries in Tianjin and Suzhou in China, Malaysia, India, Alabama and Detroit in the United States, and Hungary handle production and sales, and Inzi Controls Europe GmbH in Germany was established in May 2024.

The major customers listed in the 2023 quarterly report include Hyundai Motor, KIA, Hyundai Mobis and GM Global, with GM Korea also named. An analyst note on 2021 results said more than 80% of that year's sales came from engine-related parts. Other products exceed 60% of sales, so the product range beyond the main categories is also sizable.

04

Earnings analysis

Annual results show revenue and operating profit rising together from 2022 through 2025. Revenue went from KRW 570 billion in 2022 to KRW 634 billion in 2023 (up 11.2% year on year), KRW 649.3 billion in 2024 (up 2.4%) and KRW 717.9 billion in 2025 (up 10.6%), making 2025 the largest year in the 2022 to 2025 span.

Operating profit went from KRW 17.1 billion in 2022 to KRW 20.9 billion in 2023 (up 22.7%), KRW 26.4 billion in 2024 (up 26.1%) and KRW 39.6 billion in 2025 (up 50.2%), rising for three consecutive years from 2023 to 2025.

The operating margin widened from 3.0% in 2022 to 5.5% in 2025, and it improved in 2024 even though revenue growth slowed to 2.4%. Net profit attributable to controlling shareholders was KRW 13.9 billion in 2022, KRW 14 billion in 2023, KRW 10.2 billion in 2024 (down 27.3%) and KRW 16.1 billion in 2025 (up 58.4%).

In 2024 operating profit grew while this figure fell, so the two measures moved in opposite directions. Net profit including non-controlling interests was KRW 9.13 billion in 2024, below KRW 13 billion in 2023, and recovered to KRW 17.7 billion in 2025.

Operating cash flow was KRW 13.9 billion in 2022, KRW 83.5 billion in 2023, KRW 30 billion in 2024 and KRW 75.9 billion in 2025. The debt ratio fell from 197.8% in 2024 to 169.3% in 2025, and total liabilities at the end of 2025 were KRW 408.8 billion. The quarterly picture moved in a different direction.

Revenue was KRW 182 billion in Q3 2025, KRW 178.9 billion in Q4 2025 (down 1.7% from the prior quarter), KRW 178.5 billion in Q1 2026 (down 0.3%), KRW 183.7 billion in Q2 2026 (up 2.9%) and KRW 175.7 billion in Q3 2026 (down 4.3% quarter on quarter and down 3.4% year on year).

Operating profit fell from KRW 12.4 billion in Q3 2025 to KRW 5.41 billion in Q4 2025 (down 56.5%), then rebounded to KRW 9.79 billion in Q1 2026 (up 81.0%), which marked the peak.

It then declined for two consecutive quarters, to KRW 9.29 billion in Q2 2026 and KRW 2.66 billion in Q3 2026, and the Q3 figure was down 78.6% year on year. Net profit attributable to controlling shareholders was KRW 11.7 billion in Q3 2025, then a net loss of KRW 9.11 billion in Q4 2025 after a swing into loss.

It turned positive at KRW 5.95 billion in Q1 2026, reached KRW 15.5 billion in Q2 2026 (up 159.5%), and swung back into a net loss of KRW 14.1 billion in Q3 2026. The four quarters from Q4 2025 to Q3 2026 total a KRW 1.8 billion net loss attributable to controlling shareholders.

In Q4 2025 and Q3 2026 operating profit was positive while the controlling-shareholder result was a loss, so items below operating profit weighed heavily on the bottom line in those quarters.

Industry analysis

Inzi Controls' end market moves with domestic and overseas vehicle production and demand for engine and cooling components.

A corporate monitor comment on Q1 2026 results, dated June 2026, described the global auto market as having entered a mature phase, with EV slowdown and currency and raw material swings as added external factors.

The same comment said customer cooperation and cost reduction led to a modest improvement in operating profit. That suggests cost management, rather than volume growth, has been supporting profit.

On competitive position, the company held an 85.00% share of the domestic TMS parts market as of June 2026 and 40.00% in engineering plastic parts.

Among listed automotive peers, Hyundai Mobis and Hanon Systems have larger businesses in electrical and thermal systems, whereas Inzi Controls concentrates on engine cooling and intake components.

On EV demand, press reports in August 2026 cited a KRW 475.7 billion supply contract with Hyundai Mobis for six types of EV battery sensing assemblies.

Overseas, the company decided in August 2026 to acquire full ownership of the Slovak battery parts affiliate Nexplus SK s.r.o., and said the aim was to secure a European production base for its eco-friendly vehicle business. With domestic sales at 70.90% as of December 2025, dependence on domestic vehicle volumes remains high.

On governance, one inside director was newly appointed at the March 2026 general meeting, and a change in the largest shareholder was disclosed in the same month.
