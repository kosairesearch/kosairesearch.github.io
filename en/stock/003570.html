02

Business structure

SNT Dynamics makes automatic transmissions and axles for vehicles, along with defense powerpacks and gun systems. Of first-quarter 2026 revenue of KRW 179.9 billion, the transportation equipment segment accounted for KRW 179.0 billion, or 99.5%, while the machinery segment contributed KRW 0.9 billion, or 0.5%.

The transportation equipment segment includes both defense products and vehicle parts. The core of the defense business is automatic transmissions for the K9 self-propelled howitzer and the K2 tank.

Media reports say the company exclusively supplies defense transmissions to domestic customers including Hanwha Aerospace, and supply volumes increased after Hanwha Aerospace won K9 orders from Romania and Egypt in 2024.

A January 2026 report said the transmission would also be applied to the fourth production batch of the K2 tank.

Overseas, a January 2023 supply contract for transmissions for the Altay main battle tank for Turkey's BMC is cited as an export case, and reports say exports grew at an average annual rate of 124.7% over 2023–2025.

On the technology side, the company set up a V-CS development team at its technical research center at the end of 2025 to extend electric drive and control technology into defense. R&D spending rose from KRW 4.3 billion in 2023 to KRW 15.9 billion in 2024. The largest shareholder is the holding company SNT Holdings, which held 42.27% as of March 31, 2026.

04

Earnings analysis

Annual revenue rose from KRW 408 billion in 2022 to KRW 486 billion in 2023 (up 19.1%), KRW 614.5 billion in 2024 (up 26.4%), and KRW 712 billion in 2025 (up 15.9%), growing three years in a row from 2023 through 2025.

Operating profit climbed from KRW 25.8 billion in 2022 to KRW 41.6 billion in 2023 (up 61.4%) and KRW 110.5 billion in 2024 (up 165.7%), then fell to KRW 85.5 billion in 2025 (down 22.7%). The largest operating profit year in 2022–2025 was 2024.

Operating margin moved from 6.3% in 2022 to 8.6% in 2023, 18.0% in 2024 and 12.0% in 2025. Brokers cited a rising share of higher-margin defense business as a driver of the 2024 improvement.

Profit attributable to controlling shareholders peaked at KRW 96.1 billion in 2024 before falling to KRW 61.6 billion in 2025 (down 35.9%). In 2025 revenue grew while operating profit and controlling-shareholder profit declined, so margins moved in opposite directions.

Cumulative R&D for the first three quarters of 2025 reached KRW 15.8 billion, close to the full-year 2024 figure of KRW 15.9 billion.

In 2026, first-quarter revenue was KRW 179.9 billion (down 19.5% from the prior quarter), operating profit KRW 21.3 billion (up 16.5%), and profit attributable to controlling shareholders KRW 20.2 billion, a swing back to profit.

In the second quarter, revenue was KRW 196.3 billion (up 9.1%), operating profit KRW 17.2 billion (down 19.3%), and controlling-shareholder profit KRW 13.1 billion (down 35.2%).

In the third quarter, revenue fell to KRW 143.6 billion (down 26.9%), operating profit to KRW 6.06 billion (down 64.7%), and controlling-shareholder profit to KRW 3.34 billion (down 74.6%).

Fourth-quarter 2025 revenue of KRW 223.6 billion was the largest of the last five quarters, but that quarter posted a controlling-shareholder net loss of KRW 1.37 billion, a swing to loss.

Media reported first-quarter 2026 revenue as a record quarterly high, but on a disclosed basis the fourth quarter of 2025 was larger.

05

Industry analysis

In Korea's ground-weapons defense market, SNT Dynamics has established itself as a supplier of transmissions for tanks and self-propelled howitzers. Rising demand for ground weapons, centered on the Middle East and Europe, is cited as a common backdrop for the sector.

K2 tank transmissions have used products from Germany's RENK, and German export approval restrictions were cited as an obstacle to Middle East sales. An April 2026 report explained that the company's domestic transmission localization removed that constraint.

In the same report, an SK Securities analyst said confirmed adoption of domestic transmissions could benefit Korean defense exports. Competitively, the company has built a track record supplying domestic customers, and overseas export results complement that base.

However, heavy reliance on domestic prime-contractor volumes means that changes in order flow feed quickly into results. In commercial vehicles, exports of European electrified-model parts were reported to have supported first-quarter 2026 results.

In the United States, a report in early November 2026 said the company became the first Korean defense firm to obtain CMMC Level 2 certification.

06

Outlook

In its corporate value-up plan disclosed on March 3, 2026, the company set a target of expanding overseas revenue in defense and vehicle parts and said it would diversify its overseas sales customers.

The KRW 510.4 billion transmission supply contract with Hanwha Aerospace signed March 23, 2026, and the KRW 126.5 billion K21 infantry fighting vehicle fourth-batch production order signed April 29, 2026, indicate the scale of future defense revenue.

One explanation for the K21 volume is that part of Hanwha Aerospace's original contract supply flowed to this company. For R&D, the company named a 120mm self-propelled mortar, a mortar system mounted on light tactical vehicles, electric drive units and hybrid drive units as key projects.

In the United States, the company plans to pursue U.S. military MRO and production supply chains and NATO markets based on its CMMC Level 2 certification. The chairman of the SNT group laid out a plan to become a Tier-1 partner in production and MRO of core components for U.S. weapon systems.

On shareholder returns, a KRW 9.55 billion quarterly dividend for the first quarter of 2026 was approved by the board on May 12, 2026. An industry official said the company is likely to expand dividends and strengthen treasury share policy if earnings keep growing.

Conversely, the company said in its 2025 annual report that it had no short-term plan to cancel treasury shares. The next checkpoint is the annual operating margin and overseas revenue share in the 2026 annual report.

Bull factors

Large defense contracts keep coming

In March 2026 the company signed a KRW 510.4 billion transmission supply contract with Hanwha Aerospace, followed in April by a KRW 126.5 billion order for the fourth K21 production batch. Analysts read the two contracts as widening visibility for future defense revenue.

Transportation equipment, which includes defense products, generated KRW 179.0 billion, or 99.5%, of first-quarter 2026 revenue.

Localization and export expansion

Reports say localizing K2 tank transmissions removed German export approval constraints. Exports are reported to have grown at an average annual rate of 124.7% over 2023–2025, and the company received a USD 100 million export award in 2026.

Overseas customer results are building, which is shifting the business away from reliance on domestic volumes alone.

Shareholder returns and capital structure

Total dividends for 2025 were KRW 41.3 billion, a payout ratio of 67.1%. A KRW 9.55 billion quarterly dividend for the first quarter of 2026 was approved.

After the full exchange of convertible bonds in April 2026, treasury shares fell to 25.1% of the total, and reports note that market attention has turned to how the remaining treasury shares will be handled.

Bear factors

Quarterly profit declining for two quarters

Operating profit and controlling-shareholder profit have each fallen for two straight quarters since the second quarter of 2026. Third-quarter operating profit was KRW 6.06 billion, down 69.5% year on year, and controlling-shareholder profit was KRW 3.34 billion, down 80.6%. Cumulative operating profit for January–September 2026 fell 33.7% to KRW 44.54 billion.

Rising R&D burden

Media reports say the R&D burden in the first quarter of 2026 grew sharply even after KRW 3.396 billion in government subsidies. R&D rose from KRW 4.3 billion in 2023 to KRW 15.9 billion in 2024, and that trend has continued, creating tension between near-term profitability and longer-term investment.

Export decline and labor tensions

First-quarter 2026 exports were KRW 77.13 billion, down 7.4% year on year, while domestic sales rose 53.8% to KRW 101.87 billion. The Gyeongnam branch of the Korean Metal Workers' Union criticized a voluntary retirement program as an attempt to weaken the union, according to a January 2026 MBC report. The company said in its 2025 annual report that it had no short-term plan to cancel treasury shares.

10

Risk factors

Concentration of order timing

Defense volumes swing sharply quarter to quarter with contract and delivery timing. The consecutive declines in the second and third quarters after the first-quarter 2026 peak show this pattern. Because revenue depends on a few large contracts, any change in a prime contractor's order flow would have a large effect.

Export markets and approvals

Exports to the Middle East, Europe and Turkey depend on local demand and each country's approval procedures. K2 tank transmissions were previously constrained by German export approvals. Because first-quarter 2026 exports fell 7.4% year on year, overseas revenue growth may not be consistent.

Shareholder policy and labor relations

Amendments to the Commercial Act raised the question of mandatory cancellation of held treasury shares, but the company said it has no short-term cancellation plan.

Industry sources have mentioned possible dividend increases and stronger treasury share policies if earnings continue, but these are not official company plans. The union's criticism of voluntary retirement and subcontracting leaves labor relations as an open variable.

Overall view

SNT Dynamics' results sit within a pattern of revenue growth for three consecutive years from 2023 through 2025, an operating profit peak in 2024, and lower profit in 2025.

In 2026 revenue held at KRW 179.9 billion in the first quarter and KRW 196.3 billion in the second, but operating profit and controlling-shareholder profit have each fallen for two straight quarters since the second quarter.

Third-quarter controlling-shareholder profit of KRW 3.34 billion is the smallest since the net loss of the fourth quarter of 2025. Cited strengths include large defense contracts, improved export conditions after K2 transmission localization, dividends and a lower treasury share ratio.

Cited weaknesses include declining quarterly profit, rising R&D spending, lower first-quarter exports, labor tensions and the absence of a short-term treasury cancellation plan.

The price-to-book ratio is below 1x, but it should be read against the KRW 35.3 billion total of controlling-shareholder profit over the last four quarters.

The next checkpoints are preliminary fourth-quarter 2026 results in mid-February 2027, the 2026 annual report around March 31, 2027, and first-quarter 2027 results with the dividend decision in mid-May 2027.
