Report overview

OLED shift keeps profits positive, but the earnings base is still thin

LG Display returned to annual profit in 2025 and lifted OLED to 67% of revenue in the third quarter of 2026, yet third-quarter operating profit still fell 41.9% from a year earlier.

02

Business structure

LG Display makes display panels for smartphones, IT devices, TVs and monitors, and automotive displays. Its product lines are grouped into mobile, IT, large-area (TV and monitor) and auto.

In the third quarter of 2026, according to the preliminary release, mobile accounted for 42% of revenue and IT for 29%, with mobile up 10 percentage points from the prior quarter. OLED made up 67% of total revenue in the same quarter, the highest quarterly share on record.

IT fell about 7 percentage points from the prior quarter, and the company attributed the year-on-year revenue decline to lower shipments of mid-sized LCD products. A 2025 report said LG Display cut low-margin TV LCD business and sharply expanded small and mid-sized OLED shipments aimed at Apple.

The large-area business is expanding premium TV OLED, and the company plans to raise monitor OLED from roughly 10% of shipments to about 20% in 2026. Auto accounted for about 10% of revenue in the first quarter of 2026.

In competition, Samsung Display is reported to start 8.6-generation OLED mass production in 2026, while LCD oversupply linked to China has lasted longer and kept pressure on LCD-heavy suppliers. Against this backdrop, LG Display is reducing LCD volume and relying on higher-value OLED products to defend profitability.

04

Earnings analysis

On an annual basis, revenue fell from KRW 26.2 trillion in 2022 to KRW 21.3 trillion in 2023, a decline of 18.4%, then rose 24.8% to KRW 26.6 trillion in 2024, the largest year in the 2022–2025 window. Revenue eased 3.0% to KRW 25.8 trillion in 2025.

Operating losses widened from KRW 2.09 trillion in 2022 to KRW 2.51 trillion in 2023, then narrowed to KRW 560.6 billion in 2024. The company returned to operating profit of KRW 517 billion in 2025. Operating margin moved from -8.0% in 2022 to -11.8% in 2023, -2.1% in 2024 and 2.0% in 2025.

Net loss attributable to controlling shareholders narrowed from KRW 3.07 trillion in 2022 and KRW 2.73 trillion in 2023 to KRW 2.56 trillion in 2024, and turned into a net profit of KRW 226.3 billion in 2025. Net income including non-controlling interests, which is a different figure, was KRW 303.8 billion in 2025.

The debt ratio fell from 307.0% in 2024 to 243.4% in 2025. At the end of 2025, total liabilities were KRW 19.1 trillion and total equity was KRW 7.84 trillion, of which equity attributable to controlling shareholders was KRW 6.6 trillion. Operating cash flow was KRW 2.41 trillion in 2024 and KRW 2.35 trillion in 2025.

Quarterly operating profit fell from KRW 431 billion in the third quarter of 2025 to KRW 168.5 billion in the fourth quarter, a 60.9% drop from the prior quarter, and to KRW 146.7 billion in the first quarter of 2026. The second quarter swung to an operating loss of KRW 107.7 billion.

The third quarter returned to KRW 250.6 billion, a swing from loss, but it was down 41.9% year on year. Revenue bottomed at KRW 5.53 trillion in the first quarter of 2026 and rose to KRW 5.61 trillion and KRW 6.28 trillion in the second and third quarters, the second consecutive quarter of growth.

The preliminary release cited a weaker won, chipflation and softer premium demand as reasons the year-on-year decline persisted.

Net profit attributable to controlling shareholders reached KRW 746.7 billion in the third quarter, and the company attributed the improvement partly to a gain from translating foreign-currency balances at a lower year-end exchange rate.

That item differs in nature from operating results and should be read alongside operating profit of KRW 250.6 billion. Over the last four quarters, from the fourth quarter of 2025 to the third quarter of 2026, the net loss attributable to controlling shareholders totals KRW 584.7 billion.

05

Industry analysis

The display industry is moving toward OLED as LCD oversupply persists. A late-2025 report said oversupply from China has lasted longer, and that Samsung and LG are raising the share of higher-value OLED to defend profitability.

In February 2026, Omdia projected monitor OLED shipments would rise from 3.4 million units in 2025 to 5.4 million in 2026, while LCD monitors were expected to stagnate.

The large-area OLED shipment target was in the low 7 million units in February 2026, and the company raised its outlook to the high 7 million range at its October 2026 briefing.

In small and mid-sized OLED, an iM Securities analyst expected a major customer's first foldable smartphone to lift LG Display's share in bar-type phones. The TV market is considered mature in 2026, which makes premium large-screen strategy central to competition.

Samsung Display's planned 8.6-generation OLED mass production is seen as a variable that could shift competition in both large and small OLED. Whether supply growth from Chinese panel makers turns into price pressure is a point the industry continues to watch.

06

Outlook

For the fourth quarter of 2026, the company expects shipment area to rise about 10% from the prior quarter and selling price per area to rise in the low single digits, according to its October 22, 2026 preliminary release.

In the same briefing, the company projected large-area OLED shipments in the high 7 million range in 2026 and plans to lift monitor OLED to about 20% of shipments. In April 2026, the company said capital expenditure for 2026 was expected to be around KRW 2 trillion.

Idle capacity will be restarted gradually as demand rises, but the company gave no timing. The company described FLiPP, a next-generation RGB patterning technology that can use the full 8th-generation glass sheet to cut costs, but has not decided on the timing or scale of investment.

Automotive is expected to stabilize gradually as projects already won move into mass production. The company also flagged uncertainty from advance panel purchases by customers in the first half and from weaker finished-product demand caused by chipflation.

Analysts in early 2026 expected operating profit to exceed KRW 1 trillion for the year, but the sum of operating profit from the first through third quarters of 2026 falls well short of that level. The next checkpoints are the fourth-quarter 2026 preliminary results and the 2026 annual report.

08

Bull factors

Higher OLED mix and product mix

OLED reached 67% of third-quarter 2026 revenue, the highest quarterly level on the company's preliminary figures. Mobile rose to 42% of revenue, strengthening the high-value product mix. The company said average selling price per area rose 16% from the prior quarter to USD 1,250.

Cost reduction and cash generation

The company positions the FLiPP patterning technology as a means of cost innovation. Operating cash flow stayed in the KRW 2 trillion range in both 2024 and 2025. The company said operating efficiency activities contributed to the third-quarter improvement.

Room to expand monitor OLED

In February 2026, Omdia projected monitor OLED shipments would rise from 3.4 million units in 2025 to 5.4 million in 2026. The company plans to raise monitor OLED to about 20% of shipments in 2026. It also plans to strengthen its gaming OLED lineup built on its own technology.

09

Bear factors

Operating profit base is still thin

Third-quarter 2026 operating profit of KRW 250.6 billion fell 41.9% from a year earlier. Summed across the first three quarters of 2026, operating profit falls well short of KRW 1 trillion. The net loss attributable to controlling shareholders over the last four quarters totals KRW 584.7 billion.

Currency and chipflation cost pressure

In the third quarter, the average won-dollar rate fell about 6% from the prior quarter, which weighed on profitability. The company said chipflation, a rise in component prices such as semiconductors, made price management harder. Weaker demand for premium products limited the effect of its high-end lineup.

LCD oversupply and competitive shifts

Reports say oversupply of LCD panels linked to China has lasted longer and keeps pressure on LCD-heavy suppliers. Samsung Display is also reported to begin 8.6-generation OLED mass production in 2026. A shift in large-area OLED competition could affect the company's shipments and pricing.

10

Risk factors

Currency and cost

The weaker won and chipflation offset the effect of higher selling prices. If currency swings persist, quarterly operating profit could fluctuate sharply. If component cost increases are passed on to finished products, set demand could weaken.

Demand and customer inventory

The company noted that customers made advance panel purchases in the first half, which could reduce later orders. Slower demand for premium products also weighs on its premium-focused strategy. The pace of foldable market growth and divergence in tablet demand could affect shipment plans.

Investment and restructuring costs

Capital expenditure for 2026 is expected at around KRW 2 trillion, and the company said in its first-quarter 2026 briefing that workforce restructuring continues. The timing and scale of FLiPP investment have not been set.

Because the restart timing for idle capacity has not been given, changes in utilization and fixed-cost burden need to be tracked.

12

Overall view

LG Display moved out of the operating-loss years of 2022 through 2024 with KRW 517 billion of operating profit in 2025, and it stayed in the black in the third quarter of 2026 with KRW 250.6 billion.

Still, that quarter's operating profit fell 41.9% from a year earlier, and the net loss attributable to controlling shareholders over the last four quarters still totals KRW 584.7 billion.

The company attributed its third-quarter net profit of KRW 746.7 billion partly to a gain from translating foreign-currency balances, so it should be read apart from operating strength. Strengths include OLED at 67% of revenue, a better mobile mix and higher selling price per area.

Weaknesses include the weaker won, chipflation, softer premium demand, and competitive shifts from LCD oversupply and Samsung Display's 8.6-generation OLED mass production. The key next check is whether the company's outlook for higher fourth-quarter shipment area and selling prices shows up in actual figures.

The 2026 annual report, due by March 31, 2027, will also update the annual operating margin and debt ratio. The price-to-book comparison is a factual point, but it does not substitute for an earnings outlook.
