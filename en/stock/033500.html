02

Business structure

Dongsung Finetec is a KOSDAQ-listed company that mainly makes cryogenic cargo containment insulation for LNG carrier tanks. In 2025 the insulation business accounted for KRW 714.0 billion, or 96.2%, of segment revenue, while the gas business contributed KRW 28.2 billion (3.8%).

A press tally puts the PU insulation business at about 96% of first-half 2026 revenue, or KRW 376.6 billion. Domestic sales were KRW 738.9 billion in 2025, or 99.6% of the total, reflecting a concentration on Korean shipbuilders.

The main customers are the three largest Korean shipbuilders, HD Hyundai Heavy Industries, Hanwha Ocean and Samsung Heavy Industries, and the top three customers account for 84.2% of revenue.

The company produces its own membrane, the primary barrier in contact with LNG, along with R-PUF reinforced urethane foam for insulation, which lowers costs and allows supply to outside customers. Dongsung Finetec and Hankuk Carbon are widely described as sharing the Korean LNG carrier insulation market.

Beyond LNG cargo insulation, the product range is expanding into fuel tanks for dual-fuel LNG-powered vessels, although these still make up less than 10% of revenue.

In September 2026 the company completed product verification and certification from GTT and DNV for a low-carbon insulation product, HFO R-PUF, which improves insulation performance by up to 6% in the cryogenic range.

Because revenue depends on one product and a few customers, shipbuilding order cycles pass through to results quickly.

04

Earnings analysis

Annual results show revenue rising from KRW 434.2 billion in 2022 to KRW 531.4 billion in 2023 (up 22.4% year over year), KRW 597.4 billion in 2024 (up 12.4%) and KRW 742.2 billion in 2025 (up 24.2%), an increase for three straight years from 2023 through 2025.

Operating profit went from KRW 15.3 billion in 2022 to KRW 37.3 billion in 2023 (up 144.4%) and then KRW 72.6 billion in 2025. Operating margin climbed from 3.5% in 2022 to 7.0% in 2023, 9.0% in 2024 and 9.8% in 2025, so profit grew faster than sales in each year after 2022.

Profit attributable to controlling shareholders rose from KRW 8.42 billion in 2022 to KRW 28.7 billion in 2023 (up 241.3%) and KRW 55.9 billion in 2025. Net profit including non-controlling interests is disclosed at the same figures for each year.

The debt-to-equity ratio fell from 131.5% in 2022 to 93.7% in 2025, when total equity was KRW 258.9 billion and total liabilities were KRW 242.7 billion. Operating cash flow grew from KRW 9.73 billion in 2022 to KRW 84.7 billion in 2025.

Quarterly revenue went from KRW 201.1 billion in the third quarter of 2025 to KRW 175.4 billion in the fourth quarter (down 12.8% quarter on quarter), then KRW 169.1 billion in the first quarter of 2026 (down 3.6%).

It jumped to KRW 224.5 billion in the second quarter (up 32.8%) and eased to KRW 205.5 billion in the third quarter (down 8.5% quarter on quarter, up 2.2% year on year).

Operating profit was KRW 23.5 billion in the third quarter of 2025, KRW 19.6 billion in the fourth quarter, KRW 19.9 billion in the first quarter of 2026, KRW 35.9 billion in the second quarter (up 81.0% quarter on quarter) and KRW 24.3 billion in the third quarter (down 32.4% quarter on quarter, up 3.3% year on year).

Profit attributable to controlling shareholders over the last four quarters totaled KRW 77.8 billion. NH Investment & Securities said the third quarter of 2025 included a one-off gain of KRW 2.0 billion from pipe tank project settlement.

A third-party analysis put the cost of sales ratio at 86.1% in the first quarter of 2025 and 78.7% in the second quarter of 2026.

05

Industry analysis

The downstream market is tied to the LNG carrier ordering cycle. According to one press report, Chinese shipyards have more than tenfold their LNG carrier orders this year, and in October 2026 China won five of seven orders, overtaking Korea, which won two.

In the same tally Korea still leads on a cumulative basis with 44 vessels, or 57.9%.

The China Classification Society granted factory certification in 2023 to a local maker of LNG carrier cargo insulation, and Hudong-Zhonghua Shipbuilding is expanding domestic sourcing of core LNG carrier equipment including insulation.

Dongsung Finetec is diversifying its Chinese customers and already supplies some membranes to a Chinese shipyard.

Dongsung Finetec and Hankuk Carbon are widely described as sharing the Korean LNG carrier insulation market, and a May 2026 report estimated LNG insulation at about 90% of Dongsung Finetec's total revenue and about 70% of Hankuk Carbon's.

A market research firm projects the global insulation market to grow at an average 5.6% a year through 2035. Stricter environmental rules from the International Maritime Organization are raising demand for low-carbon, high-efficiency cargo containment, which both the company and industry commentary cite.

The company's position rests on in-house membrane production and long-term supply relationships with large shipbuilders. On the other side, fewer domestic shipbuilder orders would reduce volume, and the company would face price competition from local suppliers in China.

06

Outlook

The most recent order the company has disclosed is the KRW 192.3 billion cryogenic insulation supply contract with HD Hyundai Heavy Industries, signed on November 5, 2026.

The contract covers large LNG carriers used under long-term charters for LNG shipments from the United States, and some 200,000-ton vessels will use GTT's Mark III Flex+ system.

The company said the deal secured 29 of the 31 confirmed Mark III Flex+ vessels, lifting its share to about 94%, and that it includes the first insulation volume for a major Japanese shipowner adopting the system.

On April 8, 2026, the company disclosed a KRW 87.82 billion LNG carrier insulation contract with Samsung Heavy Industries, running through May 9, 2028. Securities analysts said the backlog of KRW 2.1 trillion at the end of 2025 gives three to four years of growth visibility.

On the product side, the company announced in June 2026 an LNG carrier insulation with DNV carbon footprint verification, and said that applying it to a 174K-class vessel could cut carbon emissions by about 54,500 tons versus the previous product.

New businesses being discussed include tanks for LCO2 carriers and cargo containment for ammonia, hydrogen and liquefied CO2. Analysts expect earnings growth from higher delivery volumes after 2027.

On the other side, IBK Investment & Securities said in an April 2026 report that a 34% rise in MDI raw material prices since the US-Iran conflict would weigh on second-half profitability.

The Alaska LNG project was reported in March 2025, when a company official said order value could exceed expectations, and the scale of any orders will become clear through later disclosures.

Bull factors

Order visibility and a larger share

The backlog was KRW 2.1 trillion at the end of 2025, which analysts described as three to four years of growth visibility. The November 2026 KRW 192.3 billion contract with HD Hyundai Heavy Industries lifted the Mark III Flex+ share to about 94%.

Whether US-linked long-term charter volumes turn into additional contracts is the next checkpoint for this strength.

A shift in profitability

Operating margin rose from 3.5% in 2022 to 9.8% in 2025. According to one analysis, the quarterly cost of sales ratio fell from 86.1% in the first quarter of 2025 to 78.7% in the second quarter of 2026. Second-quarter 2026 operating profit reached KRW 35.9 billion, up 81.0% quarter on quarter.

Balance sheet strength and a low-carbon product

The debt-to-equity ratio fell from 131.5% in 2022 to 93.7% in 2025, and operating cash flow was KRW 84.7 billion in 2025. The low-carbon insulation HFO R-PUF has completed GTT and DNV verification, with cryogenic insulation performance improved by up to 6%. The company is using it to pursue wider global orders.

Bear factors

Concentration in one product and few customers

About 96% of first-half 2026 revenue came from the PU insulation business, and the top three customers account for 84.2% of revenue. Because the main customers are the three large Korean shipbuilders, lower orders from them would reduce supply volume. Dependence on one product and a few customers feeds into quarterly volatility.

Chinese competition and price pressure

Chinese shipyards have more than tenfold their LNG carrier orders, and the China Classification Society certified a local insulation maker in 2023. Reports say the company will face price-led local competitors in China.

The company emphasizes insulation performance, quality stability and long-term operating efficiency over unit price alone.

Cost pressure and a weaker third quarter

Third-quarter 2026 revenue of KRW 205.5 billion fell 8.5% quarter on quarter, and operating profit of KRW 24.3 billion fell 32.4%. IBK Investment & Securities said in an April 2026 report that higher MDI prices would weigh on second-half profitability. One analysis noted that finished goods reached 45.3% of inventory, so inventory trends also need monitoring.

Risk factors

Customer and order concentration

With the top three customers at 84.2% of revenue, a delay or term change at one shipbuilder would flow directly into sales. If US-linked LNG carrier orders do not rise as expected, the pace of backlog conversion could slow.

One analysis notes related-party purchases from Dongsung Chemical of KRW 14.1 billion, which exceed related-party sales, so raw material sourcing is also worth tracking.

Chinese competition and localization

As Chinese shipyards expand LNG carrier orders, the use of locally made insulation is also growing. If Korean orders fall while Chinese market share rises, volume declines and price pressure could arrive together. The company is responding through Chinese customer diversification and membrane supply.

Raw materials, FX and inventory

IBK Investment & Securities noted that MDI prices rose 34% after the US-Iran conflict. One analysis reports that a 5% rise in the exchange rate would affect profit by +KRW 2.0 billion. Finished goods reached 45.3% of inventory, and an inventory valuation loss of KRW 2.2 billion was recognized.

Overall view

Dongsung Finetec's disclosed results for 2022 to 2025 show revenue, operating profit and profit attributable to controlling shareholders rising for three straight years from 2023. Operating margin climbed from 3.5% to 9.8%, and the debt-to-equity ratio fell from 131.5% to 93.7%.

In the third quarter of 2026, revenue and operating profit both declined from the prior quarter, and profit attributable to controlling shareholders over the last four quarters totaled KRW 77.8 billion.

The business is concentrated in one LNG carrier insulation product and three large Korean shipbuilders, so ordering trends pass directly into results. Supporting factors include a Mark III Flex+ share of about 94%, the KRW 192.3 billion contract signed in November 2026, and certification of a low-carbon product.

Offsetting factors include expanding LNG carrier orders in China, localization of insulation there, higher MDI raw material prices in the second half, and customer concentration.

The next checkpoints are the preliminary fourth-quarter 2026 results around late January 2027 and the 2026 annual report due at the end of March 2027.
