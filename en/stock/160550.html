Annual Turnaround Meets Four Straight Loss Quarters at NEW

02

Business structure

NEW (Next Entertainment World) is a content company whose core business is film investment and distribution. It invests in film production and secures distribution rights for theatrical and overseas release, so the box office performance of individual titles strongly drives revenue and profit.

In 2025 the company was reported to have ranked first among domestic film distributors on the strength of the film Zombie Daughter, a title whose English name is not confirmed here.

Its production arm is widening supply to domestic broadcasters and global OTT platforms, with the SBS series Good Partner 2 and a Disney+ series cited as representative examples. The distribution arm seeks additional returns from existing intellectual property through overseas remakes and rights sales.

Affiliate New ID handles content distribution in global markets, and the company reported that viewing time in India rose eightfold year on year; this is a figure compiled by the affiliate and does not map directly onto NEW's consolidated results.

In September 2026 the company officially announced a contract to distribute Makoto Shinkai's next animated feature in South Korea.

Competition splits between film distributors vying for box office success and the production market for broadcast and OTT content, where NEW overlaps with media groups such as CJ ENM and SBS.

04

Earnings analysis

Annual revenue went from KRW 155.6 billion in 2022 to KRW 129.1 billion in 2023, down 17.0% year on year, and to KRW 113.2 billion in 2024, down 12.3%, before rising to KRW 142.7 billion in 2025, up 26.0%. Among 2022 to 2025, 2022 was the largest year and 2024 the smallest.

Operating profit of KRW 7.22 billion in 2022 turned into an operating loss of KRW 5.58 billion in 2023, and the loss widened to KRW 19 billion in 2024. In 2025 operating profit returned to KRW 2.65 billion, an operating margin of 1.9%, compared with 4.6% in 2022, -4.3% in 2023 and -16.7% in 2024.

Net loss attributable to controlling shareholders was KRW 12.3 billion in 2022, KRW 11.4 billion in 2023 and KRW 20.1 billion in 2024, before a return to attributable net profit of KRW 785 million in 2025. Net profit including non-controlling interests, a different figure, was KRW 524 million in 2025.

The debt ratio, total liabilities divided by total equity, was 104.6% in 2022, 116.0% in 2023 and 114.6% in 2024, and fell to 95.2% in 2025. Total equity at the end of 2025 was KRW 122.8 billion, of which KRW 91.1 billion belonged to controlling shareholders, and total liabilities were KRW 116.8 billion.

Operating cash flow was -KRW 9.08 billion in 2022, -KRW 4.12 billion in 2023 and -KRW 19 billion in 2024, then turned to KRW 35.4 billion in 2025.

On a quarterly basis, revenue of KRW 54.5 billion and operating profit of KRW 3.94 billion in the third quarter of 2025 gave way to revenue of KRW 25.3 billion in the fourth quarter, down 53.6% from the prior quarter, and an operating loss of KRW 2.44 billion.

Revenue then came in at KRW 25.6 billion in the first quarter of 2026, KRW 27 billion in the second quarter and KRW 15.8 billion in the third quarter. Third-quarter 2026 revenue was down 71.0% year on year, and the operating loss of KRW 2.81 billion marked a swing from profit a year earlier.

Operating losses have run for four consecutive quarters from the fourth quarter of 2025 through the third quarter of 2026, and attributable net losses have appeared in two consecutive quarters, the second and third quarters of 2026. Attributable net losses over the last four quarters totaled KRW 5.03 billion. Film results that swing sharply with individual title performance appear to be behind the quarterly volatility.

05

Industry analysis

The film investment and distribution business is highly volatile, because a single hit title can dictate quarterly or annual results. For this reason NEW says it is raising investment efficiency around titles with strong box office potential and expanding income from non-film businesses to secure earnings stability.

Global content distribution differs from film releases in that existing content can be reused across multiple platforms and generate advertising revenue, rather than earning mostly at one theatrical release.

Securities analysts see the company's medium- to long-term growth as dependent on box office results in film and on monetization in global distribution.

In the film segment, stable title supply and hit-title acquisition matter most, while at New ID the ability to convert rising viewing volume into advertising revenue will be key.

In broadcast and OTT production, NEW competes in the same arena as media groups such as SBS, CJ ENM and Studio Dragon, but its core film investment and distribution business gives it a different position.

Individual title performance, rather than industry cycle indicators, more directly determines the direction of NEW's earnings.

06

Outlook

In a shareholder letter the company said it will focus on building a sustainable earnings base rather than short-term top-line expansion. It plans to widen monetization opportunities by strengthening links across businesses and to ease earnings swings from film box office results.

In its core film investment and distribution business it has presented a main lineup for 2027 and 2028. The production arm plans to expand supply to domestic broadcasters and global OTT platforms, centered on Good Partner 2 and a Disney+ series.

The distribution arm aims to widen additional returns from existing IP through overseas remakes and rights sales. It has also secured domestic distribution rights to Makoto Shinkai's next film, which becomes one pillar of its release slate.

A listing of affiliate New ID has drawn market attention, but a subsidiary listing does not automatically lift the parent's share price, and the effect on enterprise value depends on New ID's listed valuation, NEW's retained stake and any dilution during the listing process, according to one analysis.

Chairman Kim Woo-taek, the largest shareholder, reported on November 10, 2026 a plan to buy shares on the open market during a window from December 10, 2026 to January 8, 2027.

The planned purchase is about KRW 1 billion, equal to 3.55% of total issued shares, and if completed his stake would rise from 36.50% to 40.05%. The next checkpoint is the operating margin and operating cash flow in the 2026 annual report.

Bull factors

Improving balance sheet trend

The debt ratio fell to 95.2% at the end of 2025 from 116.0% in 2023. Operating cash flow swung from -KRW 19 billion in 2024 to KRW 35.4 billion in 2025. On an annual basis, both operating profit and attributable net profit returned to profit.

Share purchases by insiders

Chairman Kim Woo-taek reported a plan to buy about KRW 1 billion of shares on the open market. The window runs from December 10, 2026 to January 8, 2027, and would lift his stake to 40.05% if completed. Director Han Tae-moon also bought 4,377 common shares on the open market on November 10, 2026.

Growth in global viewing

Affiliate New ID reported that viewing time in India rose eightfold, in Australia threefold and in Europe and Canada twofold year on year. These are company-reported figures, presented as a sign of growing room to monetize overseas distribution. Securing domestic rights to Makoto Shinkai's next film also broadens the content lineup.

Bear factors

Persistent quarterly operating losses

The company recorded operating losses for four consecutive quarters from the fourth quarter of 2025 through the third quarter of 2026. Third-quarter 2026 revenue of KRW 15.8 billion was down 71.0% year on year, with an operating loss of KRW 2.81 billion. Attributable net losses over the last four quarters totaled KRW 5.03 billion.

Earnings swings tied to box office

Because each title's box-office result flows directly into earnings, results swing widely. Revenue fell from KRW 155.6 billion in 2022 to KRW 113.2 billion in 2024 before recovering to KRW 142.7 billion in 2025. If a title falls short of expectations, earnings could deteriorate quickly.

Uncertain effect of subsidiary listing

Affiliate New ID's planned listing has drawn market attention, but one analysis notes that a subsidiary listing does not automatically lift the parent's share price. The impact depends on New ID's listed valuation, NEW's retained stake and any dilution in the listing process. How much the market credits New ID's profitability and growth is also a key question.

10

Risk factors

Box office variability risk

Film distribution revenue swings sharply each quarter with the box office results of individual titles. Third-quarter 2026 revenue falling 71.0% year on year reflects this structure. If the company cannot secure hit titles consistently, the loss-making period could extend.

Subsidiary listing and dilution risk

If the New ID listing causes dilution, the parent's share of value would shrink. The effect on company value depends on New ID's listed valuation and NEW's retained stake. Listing timing and terms have not been fixed in the public materials.

Overseas monetization risk

Growth in global distribution depends on converting higher viewing volume into actual advertising revenue. Rising viewing time does not guarantee immediate income. The gap between company-compiled metrics and consolidated results also needs to be monitored.

Overall view

On an annual basis NEW returned to operating profit and attributable net profit in 2025, and operating cash flow turned positive at KRW 35.4 billion. Over the same period the debt ratio fell to 95.2%.

On the other hand, operating losses have run for four consecutive quarters from the fourth quarter of 2025 through the third quarter of 2026, and third-quarter 2026 revenue of KRW 15.8 billion was down 71.0% year on year.

The largest shareholder's plan to buy about KRW 1 billion of shares, insider purchases and the review of listing affiliate New ID are presented as signs of the company's push to raise enterprise value.

However, a subsidiary listing is not directly tied to parent earnings, and box office variability still drives quarterly results. Securities analysts see medium- to long-term growth as dependent on film box office results and on monetization of global distribution.

The next checkpoints are the end of the purchase window on January 8, 2027, the 2026 annual report expected in late March 2027, and the operating results in the first-quarter 2027 report. This summary is provided for information only and does not include a buy or sell opinion or a target price.
