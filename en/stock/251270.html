Business structure

Netmarble develops and publishes mainly mobile games and expands globally through overseas subsidiaries, including its North American unit. By genre, the 2026 third-quarter mix was casual 37%, role-playing games 32%, MMORPGs 25% and other genres 6%.

By region, North America accounted for 40%, Korea 25%, Europe 12%, Southeast Asia 10%, Japan 8% and other areas 5%, with overseas sales making up 75% of total sales. The top titles were SOL: enchant, Marvel Contest of Champions and Rat Chaslot, each at 8%, while three social casino titles together made up 22% of sales.

Commission and fee expenses rose to 33.0% of sales in the third quarter from 31.4% in the second, which the company attributed to a larger share of games built on outside IP. Fees had fallen to 32% by the third quarter of 2025 after in-house payment systems were introduced, according to press reports.

Marketing spending fell 22.2% from the prior quarter as launches slipped. Netmarble competes with listed peers such as Krafton, NC, Pearl Abyss, Shift UP and Kakao Games in both new releases and overseas markets.

Earnings analysis

Annual filings show sales of KRW 2.67 trillion in 2022, falling to KRW 2.5 trillion in 2023 (down 6.4% year on year) before rising to KRW 2.66 trillion in 2024 (up 6.5%) and KRW 2.84 trillion in 2025 (up 6.4%), so sales grew in both 2024 and 2025.

The 2025 figure is the largest of the 2022–2025 years, and one press report described that year's results as record-level.

Operating loss of KRW 108.7 billion in 2022 and KRW 68.5 billion in 2023 narrowed, then operating profit of KRW 215.6 billion in 2024 marked a turn to profit, and KRW 352.5 billion in 2025 was up 63.5%. The operating margin moved from -4.1% in 2022 and -2.7% in 2023 to 8.1% in 2024 and 12.4% in 2025.

Parent net loss of KRW 886.4 billion in 2022 and KRW 255.7 billion in 2023 gave way to parent net income of KRW 25.6 billion in 2024 and KRW 225 billion in 2025, up 777.7%. Net income including non-controlling interests is reported separately, at KRW 3.22 billion in 2024 and KRW 230.8 billion in 2025.

Operating cash flow shifted from KRW -408.4 billion in 2022 and -KRW 98.1 billion in 2023 to KRW 287.7 billion in 2024 and KRW 337.8 billion in 2025.

Quarterly operating profit peaked at KRW 110.8 billion in the fourth quarter of 2025, fell to KRW 53.1 billion in the first quarter of 2026 (down 52.1% quarter on quarter), recovered to KRW 80.2 billion in the second quarter, and dropped to KRW 50.3 billion in the third quarter (down 44.6% year on year).

Parent net income of KRW 209.7 billion in the first quarter of 2026 and KRW 197.6 billion in the second quarter was followed by KRW 20.1 billion in the third quarter, a decline that press reports tied to the absence of the Gtower disposal gain. The company attributed the slowdown to launch delays that left existing titles to decline naturally.

Industry analysis

Korean game makers' earnings are highly sensitive to the hit rate of new releases, so annual results often hinge on a few titles. Netmarble highlighted a pipeline of eight titles for 2026 at the start of the year, a slate that the press described as among the deepest among domestic game companies.

However, its key title The Seven Deadly Sins: Origin moved from January to March after global closed-beta feedback, and several second-half 2026 titles slipped into 2027, exposing execution risk in the high-volume strategy.

Genres are widening into idle RPGs, four-player co-op action and roguelite action RPGs, while platforms are expanding from mobile to PC and console. Netmarble's peers in the listed game sector, including Krafton, NC, Pearl Abyss, Shift UP and Kakao Games, compete for the same players and launch windows.

With overseas sales at 75% of revenue, results are closely tied to global performance, including North America. When new releases are delayed, existing titles decline, and this pattern showed up directly in the 2026 third quarter.

Outlook

At its 2026 third-quarter results briefing, Netmarble said it plans to launch 13 or more titles in 2027, with 10 published by its domestic unit and 3 by overseas subsidiaries, including in China.

CFO Do Ki-wook expected fourth-quarter sales to stay broadly in line with the third quarter, and said profitability could improve from the third quarter on cost discipline. The company plans to lift profitability in the fourth quarter through major game updates and efficient cost management.

Of the seven titles revealed for the first time, press reports indicate three are slated for the first half of 2027 and four for the second half. Two existing titles, Solo Leveling: Arise and The King of Fighters All-Star, are being prepared for China.

CEO Kim Byung-kyu said the delay of some second-half releases reflected lessons from the launches of Monster Strike-style and Seven Deadly Sins titles, which were applied to follow-up games. Some press reports said securities analysts did not expect a clear rebound in the fourth quarter.

Whether these release schedules convert into actual launches and hit performance is the central variable for the outlook.
