02

Business structure

Namsun Aluminium started in Daegu in 1947 as an aluminum products maker, listed on KOSPI in 1978, and joined the SM Group in 2007. According to its 2026 quarterly report, the company runs four business divisions: aluminum, automotive, construction and other.

The aluminum division produces AL sashes and assemblies, with windows, doors, curtain walls, industrial structural frames and explosion-proof windows as its main product lines.

The automotive division manufactures, assembles and sells auto parts domestically and exports them, and bumper supply for the Trax Crossover and Trailblazer has been confirmed.

The construction division was added after the company acquired 100% of a construction firm from the SM Group holding company in June 2025 and absorbed it through a merger in September 2025.

According to a Dealsite report, only about 20 days of post-merger results were reflected, and the division posted revenue of KRW 1.2 billion and an operating loss of KRW 1.3 billion in Q3 2025. The other division covers domestic and overseas investment securities and equity holdings.

The company completed its technical research institute in 1992. The aluminum and automotive divisions form the two main pillars, and construction is a new pillar added to results from the second half of 2025.

04

Earnings analysis

Based on reported annual results, revenue rose from KRW 255.7 billion in 2022 to KRW 310.5 billion in 2023, a 21.4% increase, then fell 6.8% to KRW 289.5 billion in 2024 and 10.6% to KRW 258.9 billion in 2025.

Operating profit swung from an operating loss of KRW 2.49 billion in 2022 to operating profit of KRW 6.45 billion in 2023, fell 25.2% to KRW 4.82 billion in 2024, and turned into an operating loss of KRW 3.27 billion in 2025. The operating margin was -1.0% in 2022, 2.1% in 2023, 1.7% in 2024 and -1.3% in 2025.

Net profit attributable to owners of the parent was KRW 27.9 billion in 2022, then a net loss of KRW 264 million in 2023 and a net loss of KRW 26.7 billion in 2024, before returning to a profit of KRW 3.63 billion in 2025.

In 2024, operating profit of KRW 4.82 billion coexisted with a net loss of KRW 26.7 billion, pointing to a large non-operating impact. In 2025, an operating loss coincided with a turn to profit at the net level, so the two measures moved in opposite directions.

Quarterly, the operating loss of KRW 4.31 billion in Q3 2025 on revenue of KRW 53.6 billion was the largest among the last five quarters. It narrowed to KRW 684 million in Q4 2025, widened to KRW 1.72 billion in Q1 2026, then came to KRW 930 million in Q2 and KRW 1 billion in Q3 2026.

Q3 2026 revenue of KRW 63.6 billion rose 18.8% from a year earlier. A wisereport commentary on Q1 2026 cited aluminum price increases, a weak construction market and costs from newly consolidated businesses as profitability drags, while bumper supply for automakers was described as stable. The net profit attributable to owners of the parent over the last four quarters totaled KRW 313 million.

05

Industry analysis

Aluminum supply concerns came to the fore in 2026. In an April 2026 report, Etoday described a supply disruption driven by a halted smelter in the Middle East and fears of a renewed closure of the Strait of Hormuz, noting that the region accounts for about 9% of global output.

When raw material prices rise, processors face higher cost burdens, and the company has said it plans to pass increases on to product prices to protect profitability. Whether that pass-through has reached quarterly margins is not yet clear, given the operating losses recorded through 2026.

On the construction side, a weaker construction market was cited as a factor pressuring profitability, and demand for windows and curtain walls is highly sensitive to the building cycle. The automotive division's bumper supply is described as stable.

Sama Aluminium, a listed peer in the same sector, is the most direct comparison, while large steel and non-ferrous players such as Hyundai Steel Company and Korea Zinc differ in scale and raw material structure.

In cycle terms, rising raw material prices and weaker construction demand are occurring at the same time, so the speed of product price increases is the central variable for judging the sector.

Outlook

The response the company has outlined is to improve profitability through price increases on aluminum products. In its Q3 2025 results coverage, a company official said raw material cost increases would be reflected in product prices, and how that plan shows up in 2026 quarterly operating results is the item to verify.

Q2 2026 revenue rose to KRW 82.8 billion, yet an operating loss of KRW 930 million remained, so higher sales have not translated directly into profit.

As of the disclosures for Q1 2026, the construction division had several ongoing aluminum window projects, including a residential and commercial complex project in Daegu commissioned by POSCO E&C with a contract value of KRW 3.5 billion and an end date of December 31, 2026.

Because the construction division has posted losses since consolidation, project progress and cost ratios are variables for consolidated results. The automotive division's bumper supply for the Trax Crossover and Trailblazer is viewed as stable, so the key question is how much volume there offsets weakness in aluminum.

Quarter to quarter the picture is uneven, with the loss widening to KRW 1.72 billion in Q1 2026 before narrowing to KRW 930 million in Q2.

Q3 2026 net profit turns positive

Net profit attributable to owners of the parent reached KRW 10.2 billion in Q3 2026, breaking a run of net losses in the four preceding quarters. The four-quarter total through Q3 2026 is also positive at KRW 3.13 billion.

Cost pass-through plan and equity base

In its Q3 2025 results coverage, the company said it would pass higher aluminum raw material costs on to product prices to improve profitability. Total equity at end-2025 was KRW 284.2 billion against total liabilities of KRW 153.1 billion, so the equity base is larger than the liability base.

Risk factors

Raw material supply and pricing

Supply disruptions in aluminum from the Middle East add to price volatility. If the pass-through of higher costs into product prices is delayed, cost pressure feeds directly into quarterly results, which is consistent with the operating losses seen through 2026.

Construction integration and cost ratios

The construction division has posted losses since its absorption merger in September 2025. If the construction downturn persists, the impact may extend to contract wins and cost ratios, and completion and settlement of ongoing aluminum window projects are variables for consolidated results.

Volatility in non-operating results

Operating results and net profit attributable to owners of the parent moved in opposite directions in 2024, in 2025 and in Q3 2026. When net profit turns positive without an operating recovery, the annual business report notes are needed to determine whether the gain depends on one-time items.

Overall view

Namsun Aluminium combines aluminum windows, automotive bumpers and construction, and revenue has declined for two consecutive years since the 2023 peak. Operating results turned to a loss in 2025 and have stayed negative for five consecutive quarters from Q3 2025 through Q3 2026.

On the other side, net profit attributable to owners of the parent turned positive at KRW 10.2 billion in Q3 2026, breaking the run of net losses in the four preceding quarters, and the operating loss narrowed from Q3 2025.

Strengths include the narrower operating loss, Q2 2026 revenue of KRW 82.8 billion and an equity base of KRW 284.2 billion. Weaknesses include persistent operating losses, aluminum cost and construction market pressure, and a rising debt ratio.

Because non-operating items have a large influence, whether the net profit reflects operating improvement or one-time factors should be read from the annual report notes.

The next checkpoints are the Q4 2026 provisional results and the 2026 annual business report, and reading operating trends and segment results together will widen the basis for judgment. This report is for information only and does not include an investment opinion or target price.
