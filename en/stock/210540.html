02

Business structure

DY Power was founded in December 2014 and listed on KOSPI in January 2015. It manufactures and sells hydraulic cylinders and hydraulic and pneumatic equipment for construction and industrial machinery.

Corporate information sites report that hydraulic products account for most of revenue, so the business is close to a single product family. Its main customers are equipment makers, so demand in the equipment market flows directly into component orders.

A 2024 brokerage report described the customer base as diversified across customers and sales destinations. A 2024 news report said the company plans capacity expansion in line with orders to localize production for major U.S. customers, and that it secured a hydraulic cylinder factory site in Mexico in 2023.

On digital transformation, an industry analysis describes MES deployment as the first step, followed by an AI-based intelligent root-process system. The same material says the company won a national project from the Ministry of Trade, Industry and Energy for the intelligent root-process system.

In June 2025 it was selected for a SMEs and Startups Administration support program for autonomous smart factories, in July 2026 it obtained AEO certification, and in August 2026 it was selected for an AI model development project run by the Ministry of Trade, Industry and Energy, according to a corporate information site.

Among peers, the company shares demand cycles with listed construction equipment makers such as Doosan Bobcat and HD Construction Equipment, but it operates as a component supplier whose volumes follow the production plans of finished-equipment makers.

04

Earnings analysis

Annual revenue rose from KRW 419 billion in 2022 to KRW 430 billion in 2023, up 2.6%, which is the largest year in the 2022–2025 period. It then fell to KRW 384.6 billion in 2024, down 10.6%, and to KRW 382.8 billion in 2025, down 0.5%, and 2025 is the smallest revenue year in the period.

Operating profit was KRW 27 billion in 2022 and KRW 36.3 billion in 2023, up 34.7%, making 2023 the largest year, before falling to KRW 25.5 billion in 2024, down 29.7% and the smallest year. The KRW 30.4 billion in 2025 was up 19.2% from the prior year.

The operating margin was 6.4% in 2022, 8.4% in 2023, 6.6% in 2024 and 8.0% in 2025, so margins recovered in 2025 even as revenue declined.

Net profit attributable to controlling shareholders rose from KRW 17.9 billion in 2022 to KRW 27.5 billion in 2023, up 54.0%, which is the largest year, then fell to KRW 21.9 billion in 2024, down 20.2%, and recovered to KRW 25.1 billion in 2025, up 14.7%.

Net profit including non-controlling interests was also reported at KRW 25.1 billion for 2025.

Operating cash flow fell from KRW 34.3 billion in 2022 and KRW 49.9 billion in 2023 to KRW 39.9 billion in 2024 and KRW 23.1 billion in 2025, so cash generation weakened in 2025 even as operating profit rose; the drivers, such as working capital, should be checked in the detailed cash flow statement.

On a quarterly basis, revenue fell from KRW 95.5 billion in Q3 2025 to KRW 86.3 billion in Q4 2025, down 9.6% from the prior quarter, then rose to KRW 102.9 billion in Q1 2026 and KRW 115.9 billion in Q2 2026 before reaching KRW 104.3 billion in Q3 2026, up 9.2% year on year.

Operating profit went from KRW 8.02 billion in Q3 2025 and KRW 4.81 billion in Q4 2025 to a peak of KRW 11.8 billion in Q1 2026, then fell to KRW 7.2 billion in Q3 2026, down 10.1% from a year earlier.

Net profit attributable to controlling shareholders dropped to KRW 424 million in Q3 2026, down 95.9% from the prior quarter, a decline far steeper than the 31.6% quarterly drop in operating profit, so non-operating items and taxes should be checked in the quarterly report. Over the last four quarters, net profit attributable to controlling shareholders totaled KRW 23.6 billion.

05

Industry analysis

DY Power's industry exposure runs through construction equipment demand. Hydraulic cylinders are core components for construction and industrial machinery, so they are sensitive to global construction investment, mining development and infrastructure orders.

A 2024 brokerage report cited large U.S. infrastructure investment, a global construction recovery, mining growth in emerging markets, reconstruction projects and NEOM as momentum factors for the company. Around the same time, some brokers viewed expanded U.S. reshoring of manufacturing as a second-half driver.

These outlooks were issued in 2024, however, and should not be read as a direct map onto 2026 conditions. A 2024 report attributed the 2023 profitability to raw material cost increases being passed through to selling prices.

On the margin trend, the operating margin fell from 8.4% in 2023 to 6.6% in 2024 and recovered to 8.0% in 2025. Revenue has declined in two consecutive years since its 2023 peak, so the sales trend has been in a post-peak downturn for two years.

Among peers, Doosan Bobcat and HD Construction Equipment are finished-equipment makers, while DY Power is a component supplier whose volumes follow their production plans. The utilization and order flow of finished-equipment makers therefore largely drive the company's quarterly sales.

The decline from KRW 115.9 billion in Q2 2026 to KRW 104.3 billion in Q3 2026 should be read alongside this cycle.

08

Bull factors

Operating profit recovery

Operating profit rose 19.2% to KRW 30.4 billion in 2025, and the margin of 8.0% recovered from 6.6% in 2024 toward the 8.4% of 2023. Net profit attributable to controlling shareholders also grew 14.7% to KRW 25.1 billion.

Lower financial leverage

The debt ratio fell from 36.3% in 2022 to 20.3% in 2025. Total liabilities at the end of 2025 were KRW 63.3 billion against total equity of KRW 312.5 billion, all attributable to controlling shareholders.

Growth investments and expansion

A capacity expansion to localize production for major U.S. customers is planned in line with orders. The customer and sales base is viewed as diversified. In 2026 the company also advanced projects in AI model development and obtained AEO certification to strengthen process and logistics systems.

09

Bear factors

Sharp Q3 2026 earnings drop

Q3 2026 operating profit was KRW 7.2 billion, down 10.1% from a year earlier. Net profit attributable to controlling shareholders was KRW 424 million, down 95.2% year on year. Operating profit has declined for two consecutive quarters, reversing the direction seen in Q1 2026.

Weaker sales and cash generation

Revenue has declined for two consecutive years since its 2023 peak of KRW 430 billion. Operating cash flow fell from KRW 49.9 billion in 2023 to KRW 23.1 billion in 2025, a weaker cash generation trend even in the year operating profit rose.

Volatile quarterly earnings

Net profit attributable to controlling shareholders fell to KRW 3.1 billion in Q4 2025, recovered to KRW 9.81 billion in Q1 2026, and dropped again to KRW 424 million in Q3 2026. Quarterly swings of this size make the annual trend harder to read.

10

Risk factors

Dependence on construction equipment cycle

Revenue depends on the production and orders of finished-equipment makers. A slowdown in global construction investment or mining development could reduce orders. Two consecutive years of revenue decline since 2024 illustrate this exposure.

Raw material costs and price pass-through

A 2024 brokerage analysis attributed 2023 profitability to raw material cost increases being passed through to selling prices. If raw material costs are not reflected in prices in time, the operating margin can be affected, as the drop to 6.6% in 2024 showed.

Overseas expansion tied to orders

The U.S. capacity expansion is a conditional plan tied to orders. If orders are not secured, the cost of the investment could weigh on earnings before the volume benefit arrives. Utilization of overseas capacity and customer concentration are points to monitor.

Overall view

DY Power is a component supplier focused on hydraulic cylinders for construction equipment. In 2025 operating profit rose 19.2% to KRW 30.4 billion and the operating margin recovered to 8.0%. The debt ratio also fell from 36.3% in 2022 to 20.3% in 2025, so the financial structure moved toward stability.

On the other hand, revenue has declined for two years since its 2023 peak, and operating cash flow fell to KRW 23.1 billion in 2025. In 2026, operating profit peaked at KRW 11.8 billion in Q1 and net profit attributable to controlling shareholders fell to KRW 424 million in Q3, reversing the quarterly direction.

Strengths include the profit recovery, financial capacity, the planned U.S. expansion and digitalization projects, while weaknesses include dependence on the equipment cycle, quarterly earnings volatility and weaker cash generation.

Valuation can only be described as a comparison of the share price with earnings and net assets, and it does not by itself settle a directional view.

The next checkpoints are the 2026 annual report and the Q1 2027 quarterly report, and the key question is whether the quarterly recovery in earnings carries into the annual trend.
