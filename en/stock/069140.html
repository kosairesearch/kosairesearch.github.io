02

Business structure

Nuriplan was founded in 1994 and listed on KOSDAQ in 2010 as a specialist in urban environment improvement. Its construction and plant segment produces bridge bearings, hanger supports and expansion joints, while its urban construction segment produces pedestrian overpasses and noise barriers.

A 2024 press report said that product sales such as bridge bearings and expansion joints accounted for more than 50% of revenue in the first nine months of that year.

The landscape lighting business uses LED lighting fixtures for night-time scenic displays and has grown with construction companies and local governments as its main clients.

The core product of the air-environment segment is a white-smoke reduction device, which the company describes as reducing white smoke from industrial processes without separate hot or cold heat sources. Its smart street light uses heat sensing to switch on only when a vehicle passes.

Subsidiaries cover smart street lighting, landscape lighting, LED manufacturing and construction. The company said its seismic isolation bearing structures have been applied to earthquake-vulnerable facilities such as domestic data centers and security facilities.

Demand rests on expanding social overhead capital investment and urban regeneration programs, so public investment flows feed directly into results. 04

Earnings analysis

Based on annual results disclosed in regulatory filings, revenue fell from KRW 131.2 billion in 2022 to KRW 122.2 billion in 2023 (down 6.8% year on year) and to KRW 116.1 billion in 2024 (down 5.0%), before rising 1.4% to KRW 117.6 billion in 2025.

Among the years from 2022 to 2025, 2022 was the largest year and 2024 was the smallest. Operating loss was KRW 6.06 billion in 2022 and KRW 24.6 billion in 2023, with losses widening in 2023, before operating profit of KRW 4.13 billion in 2024 marked a turn to profit.

Operating profit rose 16.7% to KRW 4.82 billion in 2025, with an operating margin of 4.1%. Attributable net profit turned to KRW 1.76 billion in 2024 but fell 88.0% to KRW 211 million in 2025.

Net profit including non-controlling interests was KRW 2.03 billion in 2024 and KRW 1.37 billion in 2025, a different figure from attributable net profit. The filings do not explain why operating profit rose in 2025 while attributable net profit fell sharply, and that reason could not be confirmed.

Quarterly, the operating loss of KRW 4.64 billion and attributable net loss of KRW 6.19 billion in the fourth quarter of 2025 can be seen as factors that lowered annual earnings.

Operating profit ran at KRW 1.72 billion in the first quarter of 2026, KRW 3.75 billion in the second and KRW 371 million in the third, a wide quarterly swing.

Attributable net profit was KRW 732 million in the first quarter and KRW 3.26 billion in the second, then turned to an attributable net loss of KRW 207 million in the third. The debt-to-equity ratio fell from 359.3% in 2022 to 203.3% in 2025.

At the end of 2025, total equity was KRW 46.3 billion, of which KRW 39.5 billion belonged to controlling shareholders, and total liabilities were KRW 94 billion. Operating cash flow moved from negative KRW 13.1 billion in 2023 to positive in 2024 and 2025, at KRW 5.01 billion and KRW 9.23 billion.

A wisereport company comment on the first half of 2026 said consolidated revenue rose 10.2% year on year while operating profit fell 10.7%, and that cost items such as performance bonus provisions limited profitability.

The same report expected carbon-neutrality and smart-city policies to widen the company's business scope. In its 2024 results announcement, the company said it was counting on entry into the small modular reactor market and overseas regions with high earthquake frequency.
