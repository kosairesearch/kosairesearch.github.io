Report overview

Expanding into Energy Infrastructure; Profit Recovery Still Pending

Nuriplan is shifting its weight from urban environment works toward bridge bearings, plant parts and energy infrastructure, but its operating profit fell sharply in the third quarter of 2026, so whether the profit recovery holds is the next test.

02

Business structure

Nuriplan was founded in 1994 and listed on KOSDAQ in 2010 as a specialist in urban environment improvement.

Its core business is urban construction such as pedestrian overpasses and noise barriers, alongside construction and plant works covering bridge bearings and expansion joints, urban landscape lighting, defense and IT, and air environment products.

In a WiseReport breakdown, product sales accounted for 66.48%, construction sales for 26.42% and merchandise sales for 5.59%.

A JobKorea business classification puts bridge bearings and expansion joint products at 58%, pedestrian overpass installation at 14%, lighting installation at 7%, and noise barriers, fences and railings at 7%.

Subsidiaries include Unison HKR for construction, Nurion for LED-based smart streetlights, Nurworks for landscape lighting and Power Glass Global for LED manufacturing. A FnGuide business summary says demand for landscape lighting has grown mainly from construction firms and local governments.

The flagship white smoke reduction device cuts white smoke from industrial processes without a separate heat source, according to a 2024 press report. In March 2026, shareholders approved adding steel-pipe pedestrian bridge and building steel structure businesses to the articles of incorporation. Compared with the KOSDAQ metal sector, its 2024 annual revenue was small relative to the whole industry group.

04

Earnings analysis

Annual revenue went from KRW 131.2 billion in 2022 to KRW 122.2 billion in 2023 (down 6.8% year on year) and KRW 116.1 billion in 2024 (down 5.0%), before rising 1.4% to KRW 117.6 billion in 2025. 2022 was the largest year and 2024 the smallest within 2022 to 2025.

Operating results moved from a loss of KRW 6.06 billion in 2022 to a loss of KRW 24.6 billion in 2023, when the loss widened, and then to an operating profit of KRW 4.13 billion in 2024. Operating profit rose 16.7% to KRW 4.82 billion in 2025.

The operating margin went from -20.2% in 2023 to 3.6% in 2024 and 4.1% in 2025. Net income attributable to controlling shareholders swung from a loss of KRW 28.4 billion in 2023 to a profit of KRW 1.76 billion in 2024, then fell 88.0% to KRW 211 million in 2025.

Net income including non-controlling interests was KRW 1.37 billion in 2025, a different figure from the controlling-shareholder number. The debt ratio fell from 359.3% in 2022 to 203.3% in 2025.

At the end of 2025, total equity was KRW 46.3 billion, of which KRW 39.5 billion belonged to controlling shareholders, and total liabilities were KRW 94 billion. Operating cash flow went from -KRW 13.1 billion in 2023 to KRW 5.01 billion in 2024 and KRW 9.23 billion in 2025.

Quarterly, the third quarter of 2025 showed revenue of KRW 31.1 billion, operating profit of KRW 3.34 billion and controlling-shareholder profit of KRW 2.74 billion. The fourth quarter of 2025 swung to an operating loss of KRW 4.64 billion and a controlling-shareholder net loss of KRW 6.19 billion.

The first quarter of 2026 recovered to revenue of KRW 27.2 billion, operating profit of KRW 1.72 billion and controlling-shareholder profit of KRW 732 million.

The second quarter of 2026 posted revenue of KRW 35 billion, up 28.5% quarter on quarter, operating profit of KRW 3.75 billion and controlling-shareholder profit of KRW 3.26 billion.

The third quarter of 2026 returned to a controlling-shareholder net loss of KRW 207 million, with revenue of KRW 21.8 billion, down 30.1% year on year, and operating profit of KRW 371 million, down 88.9%.

The four quarters from the fourth quarter of 2025 to the third quarter of 2026 total a controlling-shareholder net loss of KRW 2.41 billion.

A WiseReport summary dated 7 September 2026 said first-half 2026 revenue rose 10.2% year on year while operating profit fell 10.7%, with profitability limited by items such as performance-bonus provisions.

05

Industry analysis

Urban environment works such as pedestrian overpasses, noise barriers and landscape lighting depend on public facility orders, so they track SOC investment and urban regeneration budgets.

A 2025 press report attributed the earnings improvement partly to expanded government and local government investment in urban regeneration and environmental projects. Carbon neutrality and smart city policies were also cited as factors that could widen the company's scope.

In construction and plant works, a WiseReport summary said demand for bridge bearings and plant parts rose in the first half of 2026.

In a June 2026 interview, the chairman said the company would treat industrial infrastructure such as nuclear, LNG and plant equipment as new growth drivers, and would target LNG, SMR and data center work through its subsidiary Unison HKR.

A 2024 press report described how seismic isolation bearing structures were applied at data centers and security facilities in earthquake-vulnerable areas, and noted expectations for the small modular reactor market.

Compared with Korea Electric Power or KEPCO Plant Service & Engineering, which center on power infrastructure operation and maintenance, Nuriplan focuses on fabricating and installing facilities, so its business profile is different. On the strength side, its expansion ambitions are clear. On the weakness side, dependence on public orders and earnings volatility coexist.

06

Outlook

The company has set a 2030 target of KRW 500 billion in revenue and a double-digit operating margin, according to a June 2026 interview with its chairman. That target sits well above the KRW 117.6 billion of revenue disclosed for 2025, so the route to achieving it is the key item to track.

The chairman cited the earnings improvement at its subsidiary Unison HKR and the financial restructuring that followed the large losses of 2023 as grounds for the target.

At the regular shareholders meeting in March 2026, building steel structure construction was added to the company's business purposes in the articles of incorporation. In July 2026 the company held an executive workshop to strengthen its order-winning capacity, according to its website.

In the first half of 2026, higher demand for bridge bearings and plant parts fed into revenue, while profitability was affected by items such as performance-bonus provisions.

Third-quarter 2026 revenue fell to KRW 21.8 billion and operating profit to KRW 371 million, so whether order growth carries through to quarterly revenue and operating profit is the next checkpoint. The timing of revenue from the new businesses has not been disclosed.

Annual business reports will show the revenue mix and operating margin as interim markers of progress toward the target.

08

Bull factors

Energy infrastructure expansion target

The company has set a 2030 target of KRW 500 billion in revenue and a double-digit operating margin, and named nuclear, LNG, SMR and data centers as focus areas. Seismic isolation bearing structures have been applied at data centers and security facilities in earthquake-vulnerable areas. If the target is reached, the revenue mix could move beyond urban environment works.

Balance sheet repair and cash flow

The debt ratio fell from 359.3% in 2022 to 203.3% in 2025. Operating cash flow was KRW 5.01 billion in 2024 and KRW 9.23 billion in 2025, both positive. The chairman has said the balance sheet improved after the large losses of 2023 were cleared.

Rebound in the second quarter of 2026

Second-quarter 2026 revenue was KRW 35 billion, up 28.5% quarter on quarter, with operating profit of KRW 3.75 billion and controlling-shareholder profit of KRW 3.26 billion. WiseReport recorded first-half 2026 revenue growth of 10.2% year on year, attributed to higher demand for bridge bearings and plant parts. Demand in construction and plant works has supported results.

09

Bear factors

Sharp drop in profitability in the third quarter of 2026

Revenue fell 30.1% year on year to KRW 21.8 billion in the third quarter of 2026. The decline in operating profit was larger than the decline in revenue, and the company moved to a controlling-shareholder net loss.

Thin earnings cushion

Controlling-shareholder net income fell 88.0% in 2025 to KRW 211 million. The operating margin of 4.1% in 2025 remains low. WiseReport said profitability in the first half of 2026 was still limited by items such as performance-bonus provisions. There have been periods in which higher revenue did not translate into higher profit.

Dilution from convertible bonds

In February 2026, conversion of the 8th private convertible bond led to 693,240 new shares being listed, equal to 4.81% of total shares issued. Convertible bonds were also issued several times in 2024, so financing could keep adding to the share count.

10

Risk factors

Business concentration

Bridge bearings and expansion joint products account for 58% of the business according to a JobKorea classification, so dependence on one product group is high. Urban construction and landscape lighting demand relies on public orders and local government budgets. If orders are delayed, quarterly revenue can swing. Whether the third-quarter 2026 revenue decline was linked to this pattern needs to be checked.

Subsidiary and new business execution

Results at subsidiaries such as the construction unit Unison HKR weigh heavily on consolidated revenue and profit. In the March 2026 shareholder meeting materials, 2025 revenue on a standalone basis was about 37.5 billion won, well below the consolidated KRW 117.6 billion.

Whether new businesses in nuclear, LNG and data centers will convert into orders is not yet confirmed by disclosures.

Ownership structure and share count

Shares held by the largest shareholder and related parties stood at 26.64%, as of the last change recorded on 20 March 2025. Conversions of convertible bonds could still add shares. Free float and trading flow may affect share price volatility.

12

Overall view

Nuriplan is a KOSDAQ-listed company that grew from urban environment improvement works and is now shifting its weight toward bridge bearings, plant parts and energy infrastructure.

Annual results for 2022 to 2025 moved from a large loss in 2023 to a profit in 2024 and higher operating profit in 2025, and the debt ratio fell from 359.3% to 203.3%.

However, controlling-shareholder net income fell to KRW 211 million in 2025, and the recovery seen in the first and second quarters of 2026 reversed to operating profit of KRW 371 million in the third quarter.

Bullish factors include the 2030 revenue target and rising demand for bridge bearings and plant parts, while bearish factors include earnings volatility, convertible bond dilution and concentration in bridge bearing products.

On valuation, the price-to-earnings ratio cannot be calculated given the latest four quarters of net losses, and the price-to-book ratio sits below 1x. The next key point is whether annual earnings strength is confirmed in the 2026 annual business report.
