02

Business structure

Yoosung T&S is an inland logistics company that grew out of steel product transport.

It signed an inland transport contract with POSCO in 1986 and developed into a comprehensive logistics provider specializing in steel products, and it still maintains logistics contracts with major domestic steelmakers including Hyundai Steel.

The business has been organized into three areas: logistics, steel, and distribution. As of December 2024, the revenue mix was 55.03% distribution, 43.08% logistics, and 1.11% steel, compared with 46.06%, 28.42%, and 24.91% respectively in December 2023.

The drop in the steel share to the 1% range within a single year is the core of the structural shift.

The center of the distribution segment was rest-area facilities the company operated under a lease from the Korea Expressway Corporation, and after that lease ended in February 2025, distribution revenue became the main reason for the 2025 decline.

The center of gravity now lies in heavy steel transport and third-party logistics (3PL) services, supplemented by steel trading and logistics consulting built on a proprietary IT solution.

Its main customers are domestic steelmakers and steel product shippers, and its heavy cargo transport capacity and inland and rail infrastructure are presented as its strengths.

In real estate, a condominium sales project in Michuhol-gu, Incheon, and development of the Dohwa-dong and Pyeongtaek sites are mentioned in company profile materials. Segment operating profit and the latest revenue mix should be cross-checked against the periodic filings.

04

Earnings analysis

On an annual basis, revenue was KRW 276.8 billion in 2022, KRW 237.3 billion in 2023 (down 14.3% year on year), KRW 152.9 billion in 2024 (down 35.6%), and KRW 67 billion in 2025 (down 56.1%), declining for three consecutive years from 2023 through 2025. 2022 was the largest year and 2025 the smallest.

Operating profit was KRW 2.69 billion in 2022, KRW 2.1 billion in 2023 (down 22.2%), KRW 4.93 billion in 2024 (up 135.1%), and KRW 1.23 billion in 2025 (down 75.1%), making 2024 the largest year and 2025 the smallest. The operating margin ran at 1.0%, 0.9%, 3.2%, and 1.8% over the same years.

Margin improved in 2024 even as revenue fell, while in 2025 operating profit declined again, which the company attributed to the end of the distribution business and fixed transport costs that were held flat.

Net income attributable to controlling shareholders was KRW 55.5 billion in 2022, KRW 69.5 billion in 2023, KRW 70.9 billion in 2024, and KRW 54.8 billion in 2025 (down 22.6%).

Because operating profit is only in the single- or low-double-digit billions of won while attributable net income runs into hundreds of billions, most of the bottom line appears to come from non-operating items.

Total net income including non-controlling interests was KRW 57.6 billion in 2025, a different figure from attributable net income, and the company cited lower equity-method income after the disposal of subsidiary shares as a drag.

The balance sheet has strengthened, with the debt ratio falling from 45.2% in 2022 to 17.4% in 2025. At year-end 2025, total equity was KRW 553.5 billion, of which equity attributable to controlling shareholders was KRW 530.5 billion, and total liabilities were KRW 96.2 billion.

Operating cash flow was KRW 3.19 billion in 2022, KRW -3.51 billion in 2023, KRW -22.6 billion in 2024, and KRW 4.39 billion in 2025, changing direction almost every year.

On a quarterly basis, revenue moved from KRW 15 billion in Q3 2025 to KRW 13.3 billion in Q4 2025 (down 11.3% quarter on quarter), then held at KRW 13.2 billion, KRW 13.6 billion, and KRW 13.5 billion in Q1 to Q3 2026, roughly flat in the low-to-mid KRW 130 billion range.

Operating profit went from a KRW 181 million operating loss in Q4 2025 (a swing back into loss) to KRW 928 million in Q1 2026 (a swing back into profit), then KRW 860 million in Q2 and KRW 577 million in Q3 (down 32.9% quarter on quarter, but up 122.7% year on year).

Attributable net income was KRW 18 billion in Q4 2025, KRW 7.14 billion in Q1 2026 (down 60.3%), KRW 56.7 billion in Q2, and a net loss of KRW 44.4 billion in Q3 2026, with the last four quarters summing to KRW 37.4 billion.

Operating results stayed in the black, but attributable bottom-line results swung sharply from quarter to quarter.

05

Industry analysis

The core end market is the steel industry. Because the volumes the company hauls are tied to domestic steelmakers' production and shipments, weaker steel demand feeds directly into lower transport revenue.

The company's January to September 2025 cumulative results cited the end of its rest-area operating rights together with softer steel demand as the drivers of weaker performance.

On costs, a company comment says that higher fuel and toll charges were not fully passed through into transport costs, which hurt profitability. The same material notes a shift toward third-party and comprehensive logistics models in response to restructuring and cost-cutting demands.

Within the sector, the company is small in revenue compared with large comprehensive logistics groups such as Hyundai Glovis, CJ Logistics, and HANJIN Logistics, and its structure is specialized in heavy steel cargo transport. Its model also differs from shipping-centered companies such as HMM and Pan Ocean.

On the steel side, the company's steel manufacturing plant in Gwangyang stopped production, after which its business shifted toward distribution, according to a 2021 report. A company profile notes that its steel transport subcontract with POSCO continues during a restricted period. The first indicator of where the cycle stands is whether operating profit stays positive after Q1 2026.

06

Outlook

The confirmed near-term event is the value-up plan. The company has set a target of paying out 40% of standalone net income each year for fiscal 2026 to 2028, and it plans to cancel all remaining directly held treasury shares, totaling 1,826,442 shares, by February 2027.

The plan was approved by the board on October 30, 2026 and disclosed in early November. The payout and cancellation are targets rather than confirmed amounts, and both must go through procedures under laws and the articles of incorporation.

The payout size will therefore depend on standalone net income for each year and on shareholder meeting resolutions. On the business side, the basic premise is that the POSCO steel transport subcontract continues through its restricted period.

In real estate, a condominium sales project in Michuhol-gu, Incheon is proceeding while the company monitors market conditions, and development profits from the Dohwa-dong and Pyeongtaek sites are described as company plans.

Following the disposal of subsidiary shares that reduced equity-method income in 2025, how the earnings structure changes is another item to track. Once the 2026 annual report is filed, the gap between operating profit and attributable net income can be reviewed on a full-year basis.

08

Bull factors

Operating profit stays positive

Operating profit was positive in all three quarters of 2026, at KRW 928 million in Q1, KRW 860 million in Q2, and KRW 577 million in Q3.

The swing from an operating loss in Q4 2025 back to profit in Q1 2026 suggests that the transport-centered structure after the distribution exit is generating a certain level of earnings. Because the profit amounts are small, whether this positive run continues is the next item to check.

Balance sheet headroom

The debt ratio fell from 45.2% in 2022 to 17.4% in 2025, and at year-end 2025 total liabilities were KRW 96.2 billion against total equity of KRW 553.5 billion. Equity attributable to controlling shareholders, at KRW 530.5 billion, makes up most of that equity.

A balance sheet with low borrowing burden leaves more room to pursue a business transition and shareholder returns at the same time.

Shareholder return plan disclosed

A target to pay out 40% of standalone net income for each fiscal year from 2026 to 2028 has been disclosed as part of a value-up plan. The company plans to cancel its remaining 1,826,442 treasury shares by February 2027.

Both the dividends and the cancellation will be finalized through procedures set by law and the articles of incorporation.

Bear factors

Revenue contraction continues

Revenue fell for three straight years, from KRW 237.3 billion in 2023 to KRW 152.9 billion in 2024 and KRW 67 billion in 2025. Quarterly revenue held between KRW 13.2 billion and KRW 13.6 billion through 2026, and Q3 2026 revenue fell 9.7% year on year.

A new revenue stream large enough to replace the distribution revenue lost to the business exit is not yet visible in the reported results.

Earnings quality and volatility

Q3 2026 operating profit was KRW 577 million, yet net income attributable to controlling shareholders was a net loss of KRW 44.4 billion. In 2025, the disposal of subsidiary shares reduced equity-method income and pulled down net income. With small operating earning power, the bottom line depends heavily on non-operating items.

Cash flow and sector pressure

Operating cash flow was negative at KRW -3.51 billion in 2023 and KRW -22.6 billion in 2024 before turning to KRW 4.39 billion in 2025. Weaker steel demand and fuel and toll increases that are not reflected in transport costs are cited as profitability pressures. Cash generation that is not steady from year to year is a weakness.

Risk factors

Earnings volatility and non-operating items

Attributable net income swung sharply between quarters, from KRW 56.7 billion in Q2 2026 to a net loss of KRW 44.4 billion in Q3 2026. The composition of non-operating items must be verified in the notes to the periodic filings. If subsidiary share disposals occur again, equity-method income will move as well.

Business transition and property development

After the distribution business and rest-area operations ended, the business base narrowed to steel transport and third-party logistics. The Incheon condominium project depends on market trends for its timing, and development profits from the Dohwa-dong and Pyeongtaek sites remain at the planning stage.

Steel sector and contract structure

The steel transport subcontract runs for a restricted period, leaving the company exposed to contract termination or volume declines. Weaker steel demand reduces transport revenue directly, and if fuel and toll increases are not passed through into freight rates, profitability pressure rises. Whether the shareholder return targets are carried out also depends on the procedures still ahead.

Overall view

Yoosung T&S is an inland logistics company centered on heavy steel transport and third-party logistics. Revenue fell from KRW 276.8 billion in 2022 to KRW 67 billion in 2025 as the distribution business and rest-area operations ended.

Operating profit in 2025 declined to KRW 1.23 billion, and net income attributable to controlling shareholders fell to KRW 54.8 billion.

Operating profit remained positive in all three quarters of 2026, but attributable net income in Q3 2026 was a net loss of KRW 44.4 billion, moving in the opposite direction from operating profit.

Strengths include a balance sheet whose debt ratio fell to 17.4% and a plan to pay out 40% of standalone net income for 2026 to 2028 alongside treasury share cancellation.

Weaknesses include three consecutive years of revenue decline, weaker steel demand, and large swings in non-operating items relative to the size of operating profit. The shareholder return plan is still a target, and actual dividend amounts will be determined through legal and charter procedures.

The next checkpoints are the non-operating breakdown in the 2026 annual report and the operating profit trend in Q1 2027. This report is for information only and does not include a buy or sell opinion.
