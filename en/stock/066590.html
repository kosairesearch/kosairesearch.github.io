02

Business structure

Smotronic is a Korean automotive parts maker that has been listed on KOSDAQ since February 2003 and describes itself as a specialist in e-mobility solutions. Its former name was Woosu AMS. The center of gravity points toward EV parts.

A wholly owned subsidiary based in Ulsan makes and sells electric vehicles and EV components. Group affiliates, including an Indian unit, receive products and support from the parent.

A supplier partner that was to produce a spider parts program in India reportedly saw its volume cut to 40% of the original plan and has filed a damages suit. Shareholders approved adding solar and renewable-energy businesses to the articles of incorporation in October 2026, citing ESG and carbon-neutral policy. Listed peers in automotive parts include Hyundai Mobis, Hanon Systems, HL Mando and SL.

04

Earnings analysis

Annual revenue went from KRW 356.9 billion in 2022 to KRW 348 billion in 2023 (down 2.5%), then KRW 354.2 billion in 2024 (up 1.8%) and KRW 368.6 billion in 2025 (up 4.1%), rising for two consecutive years from 2024 through 2025. 2025 was the largest revenue year in 2022 to 2025, and 2023 the smallest.

Profit moved in the opposite direction. Operating profit rose from KRW 1.68 billion in 2022 to KRW 8.38 billion in 2023 (up 398.9%) and KRW 9.05 billion in 2024, the largest year of the four, before falling to KRW 451 million in 2025 (down 95.0%), the smallest.

The operating margin slipped from 2.6% in 2024 to 0.1% in 2025, so higher sales did not offset weaker profitability.

Net income attributable to controlling shareholders went from a loss of KRW 17.5 billion in 2022 to a profit of KRW 10.2 billion in 2023, KRW 7.34 billion in 2024 (down 28.1%), and a loss of KRW 10.6 billion in 2025.

Net income including non-controlling interests was a loss of KRW 11.3 billion in 2025, a different figure. Operating cash flow recovered to KRW 10.2 billion in 2025 from KRW 1.68 billion in 2024, a gap versus the profit trend. In the quarters, Q3 2025 operating profit was KRW 497 million on revenue of KRW 94.4 billion.

Q4 2025 posted an operating loss of KRW 1.52 billion, then Q1 2026 returned to operating profit of KRW 3.38 billion. Q2 2026 revenue was KRW 105.2 billion (up 10.8% quarter on quarter) with operating profit of KRW 3.04 billion.

Q3 2026 revenue was KRW 102.8 billion (up 8.9% year on year) with operating profit of KRW 3.09 billion (up 521.6% year on year). Net income attributable to controlling shareholders over the last four quarters totaled KRW 452 million.

The debt ratio went from 280.7% in 2022 to 240.8% in 2025, with year-end 2025 total equity of KRW 78.7 billion and total liabilities of KRW 189.5 billion.

05

Industry analysis

The domestic auto parts sector faces demand that splits according to vehicle output and the pace of the shift to electric vehicles. Analysis reported on the core auto parts business describes a weakening profitability pattern, consistent with the operating margin falling to 0.1% in 2025.

The company promotes its e-mobility focus and runs an EV parts subsidiary, which points to a larger electrified portfolio. Public disclosures give limited detail on the revenue share and margin contribution of electrified parts.

At group level, renewable energy was added to the articles of incorporation as a step into new businesses beyond the core. Against larger automotive parts groups, the company is smaller by capital base and market value of KRW 83.5 billion.

Hyundai Mobis and Hanon Systems have wider customer and geographic reach and serve as benchmarks. Customer concentration and overseas production share are not clearly disclosed, which limits a numerical comparison of its position.

The fall in 2025 operating profit to KRW 451 million suggests cost and selling expenses grew faster than revenue.

Outlook

The biggest change is at the shareholder level. The change of largest shareholder was completed on 4 November 2026, with Otronic acquiring 12.92 million shares, or 33.40%. The purchase price of KRW 28.2 billion was paid entirely from its own funds, and the stated purpose is participation in management.

No changes to board appointments or dismissals were planned after the acquisition. Shareholders approved the articles amendment at the extraordinary meeting on 7 October 2026. Affiliate funding continues: a KRW 6.23 billion loan to a subsidiary extends an existing loan and runs from 4 November 2026 to 4 November 2027.

Operating profit stayed positive from Q1 through Q3 2026, but Q2 fell 10.3% quarter on quarter and Q3 rose only 1.8%, so the path is not a straight line. Specific orders or capacity expansion plans for renewable energy and EV parts were not confirmed.

The supplier volume dispute has moved to litigation, and no outcome has been reported. The first confirmation point is the 2026 annual report, where the full-year operating margin can be compared with 0.1% in 2025.

08

Bull factors

Quarterly operating profit stays positive

Operating profit was KRW 3.38 billion in Q1 2026, KRW 3.04 billion in Q2 and KRW 3.09 billion in Q3, positive in all three quarters. This follows the operating loss of KRW 1.52 billion in Q4 2025. The initial signal is a profit recovery after the 2025 margin low of 0.1%.

Operating cash flow recovers

Operating cash flow rose from KRW 1.68 billion in 2024 to KRW 10.2 billion in 2025, even as operating profit fell to KRW 451 million. This gap between profit and cash is noted in press analysis, so the durability of this cash generation remains a point to track. It serves as a financial cushion within a KRW 78.7 billion equity base.

Ownership change and expansion intent

Otronic completed its acquisition of the largest stake on 4 November 2026, with the stated purpose of participating in management. The articles now cover solar and renewable-energy businesses, creating a legal basis for broader scope.

An EV parts subsidiary and the e-mobility focus give room to widen the portfolio, though concrete orders or investment plans have not been disclosed.

09

Bear factors

Sharp profit decline in 2025

Revenue rose 4.1% in 2025 but operating profit fell 95.0% to KRW 451 million, and the operating margin dropped to 0.1%. Net income attributable to controlling shareholders swung to a loss of KRW 10.6 billion. If sales growth keeps failing to reach profit, the next annual report may show the same pattern.

High debt and a negative-equity subsidiary

The debt ratio rose again to 240.8% in 2025 from 217.1% in 2024, with total liabilities of KRW 189.5 billion against KRW 78.7 billion of equity.

A subsidiary, the EV parts maker, reported negative equity of KRW 7.01 billion at the end of 2025 and a net loss of KRW 5.06 billion, so funding pressure from the group can fall on the parent.

Repeated guarantees and supplier dispute

Several loan-guarantee disclosures for third parties were filed in September 2026, along with corrections. A damages suit followed a supplier dispute over an Indian production project in which the company reportedly committed to only 40% of the volume.

Departures of key staff at the Indian unit were also reported, which could weigh on execution and business credibility.

Risk factors

Ownership change

The largest shareholder changed on 4 November 2026 after share-transfer agreements were disclosed in July and November 2026. Frequent officer and major-shareholder disclosures around the change reduce the predictability of management direction. The stated purpose is management participation, but concrete plans are not yet disclosed.

Affiliate support and guarantees

A KRW 6.23 billion loan to a subsidiary at 4.5% was approved on 4 November 2026, equal to 7.92% of equity. Loan-guarantee decisions for third parties were also disclosed in September 2026. Growing affiliate support can raise contingent liabilities and funding burden on a consolidated basis.

Supplier dispute and concentration

In the India spider parts project, the supplier reportedly invested about KRW 730 million in equipment and labor before the volume was cut. The supplier has filed a damages suit, and the company reportedly attributed the matter to its Indian unit.

Such disputes can affect other customer relationships, and dependence on group affiliates and specific projects adds to the risk.

Overall view

Smotronic's 2025 revenue rose to KRW 368.6 billion, but operating profit fell to KRW 451 million and the net result turned to a loss of KRW 10.6 billion attributable to controlling shareholders.

Operating profit stayed positive in each quarter from Q1 through Q3 2026, and Q3 operating profit was reported up 521.6% year on year. The largest shareholder changed to Otronic on 4 November 2026, and renewable energy was added to the articles.

Risk factors remain, including a debt ratio of 240.8%, a subsidiary with negative equity, affiliate loans and guarantee disclosures, and a supplier lawsuit. The strengths are the run of quarterly profits and the cash flow recovery, and the weaknesses are the annual profit collapse and high leverage.

The next confirmation points are the operating margin in the 2026 annual report and how the loan maturing in November 2027 is handled.
