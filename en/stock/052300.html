02

Business structure

Ocean In W previously held media and restaurant affiliates centered on its former media subsidiary, but after the transfer of its entire stake in that media company was completed in late November 2024, it classified those businesses as discontinued operations and removed them from consolidation.

Its continuing operations were then organized around glass wholesale and retail and real estate leasing. At the end of 2025, its valve business subsidiary, Jo Kwang IL&I, was consolidated, and an equity research site noted that this business was not included in 2025 results.

A third-party data aggregator, Butler, put the 2026 Q2 revenue mix at 74% valves, 18% glass and 8% real estate, a figure that should be checked against the original disclosures.

In its 2025 results explanation, the company cited higher rental income in real estate leasing and an improved rental cost structure as reasons for the smaller operating loss.

In December 2024 the company acquired 99% of a venture investment partnership for KRW 25.5 billion and later added capital, bringing its total VC commitment to KRW 64.0 billion, a step media reports linked to the need for replacement businesses after the exit from legacy operations.

In March 2026 the company decided on a 10-to-1 free capital reduction at the valve subsidiary, citing balance sheet improvement and a more appropriate capital base. The company had 14 employees as of August 2026.

04

Earnings analysis

On a disclosed basis, revenue fell from KRW 214.7 billion in 2022 to KRW 11.9 billion in 2023 (down 94.5% year on year) and KRW 6.8 billion in 2024 (down 42.7%), then rose to KRW 7.57 billion in 2025 (up 11.3%). The largest year in 2022–2025 was 2022, and the smallest was 2024.

The 2022 figure appears to include legacy businesses before they were reclassified as discontinued, so direct comparison with later years is limited.

Operating profit was KRW 5.12 billion in 2022, then turned to an operating loss of KRW 683 million in 2023, and the company has posted losses for three consecutive years from 2023 to 2025.

The operating loss narrowed from KRW 3.27 billion in 2024 to KRW 2.45 billion in 2025, and the operating margin improved from -48.0% to -32.4%. The company attributed the 2025 revenue increase and smaller operating loss to higher rental income and an improved rental cost structure in real estate leasing.

Net income attributable to controlling shareholders turned to a profit of KRW 167.9 billion in 2024, a period that overlaps with the timing of the media stake disposal, so disposal-related gains may have contributed.

In 2025 it swung back to a loss of KRW 19.1 billion, and the net loss including non-controlling interests was also KRW 19.1 billion. The company said financial asset valuation losses, equity-method losses from associates and valuation losses on short-term loans were reflected in that year's bottom line.

Operating cash flow was KRW 6.33 billion in 2022, KRW 47.7 billion in 2023 and KRW 43.8 billion in 2024, then turned negative at -KRW 1.59 billion in 2025.

On a quarterly basis, revenue bottomed at KRW 1.84 billion in 2025 Q4, after KRW 2.14 billion in 2025 Q3, and then rose to KRW 6.87 billion in 2026 Q1, KRW 15 billion in Q2 and KRW 15.3 billion in Q3, three consecutive quarterly increases after 2025 Q4. Q3 2026 revenue was up 613.8% year on year.

Operating results moved from an operating loss of KRW 1.3 billion in 2026 Q1 to an operating profit of KRW 1.95 billion in Q2, and Q3 operating profit of KRW 1.74 billion was down 10.7% from Q2.

Net income attributable to controlling shareholders swung from a KRW 67.5 billion loss in 2025 Q4 to a KRW 18.5 billion profit in 2026 Q1, a KRW 6.57 billion loss in Q2 and an KRW 18 billion profit in Q3.

Because the swings in controlling-shareholder results are far larger than operating profit in several quarters, non-operating valuation gains and losses appear to carry significant weight.

05

Industry analysis

Valve demand is often tied to plant and industrial facility investment and maintenance needs, so it moves with the capital spending cycle. Glass wholesale and retail tracks construction and interior demand, while real estate leasing depends on rental income and the cost structure of the properties.

On an aggregator basis, valves were the largest share of revenue in 2026 Q2, suggesting the center of gravity has shifted from glass and real estate toward valves.

An equity research site, however, noted that the lack of backlog, product-level revenue and cost-ratio data makes it hard to verify how quickly valve revenue is converting.

Larger listed consumer and distribution companies such as Shinsegae, E-Mart and POSCO International operate retail-channel or trading-centered models, so their structure and scale differ widely from Ocean In W, which limits direct comparison with peer averages.

The key variables are whether the valve business can generate recurring revenue and whether operating cash flow supports it. The negative operating cash flow in 2025 should be read in the same context.

06

Outlook

Quarterly revenue has held in the KRW 15 billion range through 2026 Q3, so whether the valve business consolidation effect continues into the next quarters is the first item to watch.

Operating profit was positive in 2026 Q2 and Q3, while net income attributable to controlling shareholders diverged, with a loss in Q2 and a profit in Q3. Whether operating profit carries through to net income and cash flow is therefore the next point to confirm in filings.

As of the October 15, 2026 disclosure, loans were KRW 21.3 billion to one holding company and KRW 20.4 billion to another at 6.0% interest, both for purchases of shares in other companies, with terms ending December 31, 2026.

These equal 7.37% and 7.06% of equity respectively, and the KRW 20.4 billion loan has been extended four times since its first disclosure in May 2025. Whether these are repaid or extended again at maturity is a key item for cash flow and governance.

On March 19, 2026 the company entered a KRW 2.0 billion treasury share trust contract for share price stabilization and shareholder value, with a term ending September 21, 2026. The 10-to-1 capital reduction at the valve subsidiary set a record date of April 29, 2026.

The next periodic report is the 2026 annual report, where full-year operating cash flow and operating margin will show how much the valve consolidation contributes.

08

Bull factors

Revenue scale expanded after valve consolidation

Quarterly revenue fell to KRW 1.84 billion in 2025 Q4 and then rose to KRW 6.87 billion in 2026 Q1, KRW 15 billion in Q2 and KRW 15.3 billion in Q3, three consecutive increases. Q3 revenue was up 613.8% year on year.

The timing of the valve consolidation and the jump in quarterly revenue coincide, which points to a broader revenue base. If recurring sales take hold, that would strengthen the case for the business.

Operating profit turned positive in recent quarters

Operating results moved from a KRW 1.3 billion operating loss in 2026 Q1 to a KRW 1.95 billion operating profit in Q2, and Q3 operating profit was KRW 1.74 billion. Q3 operating profit also turned positive from a year earlier.

For 2025, the company attributed the smaller operating loss to higher rental income and a better rental cost structure. Continued operating profit at this level could be read as evidence of recovering earnings power.

Balance sheet buffer

Total equity at the end of 2025 was KRW 288.9 billion, of which KRW 263.9 billion belongs to controlling shareholders, and total liabilities were KRW 86.2 billion. The debt ratio declined from 55.8% in 2022 to 29.8% in 2025.

The price-to-book ratio sits at a low level relative to net assets, so the asset base acts as a financial cushion. Lower debt also adds to financing capacity.

Bear factors

The company posted operating losses for three consecutive years from 2023 to 2025, with operating margins of -48.0% in 2024 and -32.4% in 2025. Operating cash flow fell from KRW 43.8 billion in 2024 to -KRW 1.59 billion in 2025.

If cash outflows from operations persist, it remains unclear whether higher valve revenue will translate into cash generation, a verification task that an equity research site also flagged.

Large swings in quarterly net income

Net income attributable to controlling shareholders went from a KRW 67.5 billion loss in 2025 Q4 to a KRW 18.5 billion profit in 2026 Q1, a KRW 6.57 billion loss in Q2 and an KRW 18 billion profit in Q3. The four-quarter total through 2026 Q3 is a loss of KRW 37.5 billion.

For 2025, the company cited financial asset valuation losses, equity-method losses and valuation losses on short-term loans. Results driven by valuation items need to be read separately from operating performance.

Loans to a counterparty led by the same CEO and shareholder disputes

Loans disclosed on October 15, 2026 total KRW 21.3 billion and KRW 20.4 billion at 6.0% interest, with terms ending December 31, 2026. The same executive is listed as representative of both the company and the counterparty, and the counterparty reported negative total equity.

Media reports from 2025 described shareholder lawsuits over the 2024 stake sale. Uncertainty around governance and loan recovery is a weakness.

Risk factors

Business verification risk

An equity research site has noted that backlog, product-level revenue and cost ratios for the valve business are not sufficiently disclosed, which makes the speed of revenue conversion hard to verify.

Quarterly revenue has risen to the KRW 15 billion range, but whether that level holds over several quarters is still unproven. The operating margin moved from -32.4% in 2025 to positive quarterly results in 2026, but the annual track record is short.

Loan and governance risk

The KRW 21.3 billion and KRW 20.4 billion loans equal 7.37% and 7.06% of equity respectively. The KRW 20.4 billion loan has been extended four times since its first disclosure in May 2025.

The proceeds fund a share purchase by the counterparty, and the outcome at the December 31, 2026 maturity is an important checkpoint. The shared executive structure and the counterparty's negative equity are factors to weigh when judging recoverability.

Valuation-driven earnings and dispute risk

The 2025 net loss was affected by valuation items such as financial asset valuation losses and equity-method losses. Net loss attributable to controlling shareholders widened to KRW 67.5 billion in 2025 Q4, and quarterly results have changed direction often since.

Shareholder lawsuits over the media stake sale and VC investments were described in 2025 reports as showing signs of resolution.

Overall view

Ocean In W's business structure has changed substantially since 2022. After exiting its media and restaurant affiliates and keeping glass wholesale and retail and real estate leasing, it consolidated its valve business subsidiary at the end of 2025, lifting quarterly revenue to the KRW 15 billion range in 2026.

Operating profit was positive in 2026 Q2 and Q3, but the company posted operating losses for three consecutive years from 2023 to 2025 and operating cash flow was negative in 2025.

Net income attributable to controlling shareholders swung widely across quarters, with losses in 2025 Q4 and 2026 Q2 and profits in 2026 Q1 and Q3.

Strengths include the expansion of valve revenue, the appearance of profitable quarters and a financial buffer of KRW 288.9 billion in total equity and a 29.8% debt ratio at the end of 2025.

Weaknesses include negative operating cash flow, loans maturing on December 31, 2026 to a company led by the same executive, earnings driven by valuation items and shareholder disputes. One analysis notes that limited disclosure of backlog and cost ratios makes the durability of revenue hard to verify.

The next checkpoints are the treatment of the loans at their December 2026 maturity, the full-year operating cash flow and operating margin in the 2026 annual report, and the revenue mix in the 2027 Q1 quarterly report.
