02

Business structure

Daesung Hi-Tech makes precision parts using ultra-precision machining, and it is moving from its legacy machine-tool business toward a parts platform serving defense, robotics and medical industries. It is applying the high-mix, low-volume production capability it built in machine tools.

The company attributed the 2025 profitability improvement mainly to higher defense sales within its precision-parts business. In December 2025 it signed a contract worth about KRW 22.7 billion to supply next-generation missile parts to a European defense company.

In February 2026 it secured another supply deal of roughly KRW 10 billion for key parts used in weapon systems linked to Elbit Systems. To serve European defense demand it built a local production base in Romania, and it has subsidiaries in Japan and Vietnam.

In robotics it is pursuing contract development and manufacturing (CDMO) using precision machining, with discussions under way with domestic robot companies on autonomous mobile robot, reducer and joint structural parts.

Its Vietnam unit directly mass-produces liquid-cooling components for AI data-center systems and also supplies manufacturing equipment. Its medical device business is building an AI production line, with a prototype supply targeted for October 2026 and full delivery in December 2026. Backlog stood at KRW 76.5 billion at end-June 2026, up 144% year on year.

04

Earnings analysis

On a full-year basis from the regular reports, revenue fell from KRW 128.4 billion in 2022 to KRW 92.8 billion in 2023, a 27.7% decline. It was flat at KRW 92.8 billion in 2024 and rose to KRW 95.2 billion in 2025, up 2.6%. The 2022 figure is the largest of the four years and 2023 the smallest.

Operating profit of KRW 11.5 billion in 2022 (operating margin 8.9%) turned into an operating loss of KRW 6.58 billion in 2023.

The loss widened to KRW 13.1 billion in 2024 (operating margin -14.2%) and narrowed to KRW 2.3 billion in 2025 (operating margin -2.4%), extending a three-year run of operating losses from 2023 to 2025.

Profit attributable to controlling shareholders moved from KRW 2.19 billion in 2022 to net losses of KRW 7.23 billion in 2023, KRW 12.4 billion in 2024 and KRW 5.58 billion in 2025. Net income including non-controlling interests, a different figure, was a net loss of KRW 6.17 billion in 2025.

The company attributed the 2025 narrowing to business diversification and new defense sales.

On a separate-entity basis the company returned to operating profit in 2025, but on a consolidated basis it still recorded an operating loss, which it linked to one-off items including investment to strengthen its Japanese and Vietnamese subsidiaries and development costs for AI data-center cooling parts.

Quarterly revenue was KRW 23.1 billion in Q3 2025 with an operating loss of KRW 436 million. It fell to KRW 17.6 billion in Q1 2026, the low point, before rising to KRW 23.1 billion in Q2 and KRW 32.9 billion in Q3 2026, a rise over two consecutive quarters.

Q2 2026 returned to an operating loss of KRW 2.19 billion, while Q3 2026 operating profit was KRW 18.48 million, a turn to profit. Net losses attributable to controlling shareholders were KRW 16 billion in Q1 2026 and KRW 8.7 billion in Q3 2026, a wide gap from the operating results.

The four quarters from Q4 2025 through Q3 2026 total a net loss of KRW 23.8 billion attributable to controlling shareholders.

05

Industry analysis

Daesung Hi-Tech's revenue mix is shifting from machine-tool-related sales toward newer demand from defense, robotics and AI data centers.

In defense, the company built a production base in Romania as European defense supply chains expand, and in August 2026 it said shipments of parts to Europe would begin in the second half.

Defense parts generally go through supplier qualification and long procurement cycles, so once a supplier is added, volumes tend to continue. In robotics, the company is pursuing supply of precision parts to domestic robot specialists, focused on industrial robots and humanoids.

Among listed peers in the same machinery and equipment sector, Robotis and Rainbow Robotics are the natural reference points for gauging the growth pace of robot-parts businesses.

In AI data centers, the Vietnam unit already mass-produces liquid-cooling components, and the company is building this area as a core growth pillar. Demand for machine tools and manufacturing equipment, however, depends on the global capex cycle, so a pullback in customer investment could weigh on existing sales.

The first-half 2026 result, which was temporarily affected by customer shipment schedule changes, shows how sensitive earnings are to timing at a few customers. The key variable for reading results is therefore how fast new-business orders convert into revenue, more than the direction of the broader market.

Outlook

Based on backlog of KRW 76.5 billion at end-June 2026, the company expects its earnings recovery to take hold from the second half of 2026.

Because there is a lag of about three to twelve months between an order and revenue recognition, the orders it has secured are expected to feed into results in sequence from the second half.

The company said orders began converting into sales from July 2026, and third-quarter 2026 revenue of KRW 32.9 billion was up 42.5% from the second quarter. A company official said the defense, robotics, medical device and AIDC businesses would keep growing through 2027, which is the company's own view.

In defense, shipments of parts to Europe are under way in the second half, alongside wider global supply-chain access. The medical device business targets full delivery in December 2026 after completing its AI production line.

In robotics, the key question is whether discussions with domestic robot companies turn into supply contracts. Shipment deferrals at customers earlier in 2026 show that the pace of second-half conversion can depend on customer schedules.

The items to confirm are the fourth-quarter 2026 result and whether full-year 2026 operating profit is positive in the annual report, as well as whether the controlling-shareholder net result follows the operating trend. The first-quarter 2027 quarterly report will also show how quickly backlog turned into sales.

08

Bull factors

Backlog expansion and conversion into sales

Backlog stood at KRW 76.5 billion at end-June 2026, up 144% year on year. The company says revenue recognition follows orders with a lag of about three to twelve months, so the backlog should feed results from the second half.

Third-quarter 2026 revenue of KRW 32.9 billion fits that pattern, and bulls argue that a growing base of long-term supply volumes improves revenue visibility.

Defense supply-chain entry and overseas production

In December 2025 the company signed a contract worth about KRW 22.7 billion to supply next-generation missile parts to a European defense company. In February 2026 it added a deal of roughly KRW 10 billion for key parts in weapon systems linked to Elbit Systems.

The company says its Romanian production base could bring it into European defense value chains once mass production is established, and shipments to Europe starting in the second half would diversify its customer base.

Diversification into new businesses

The Vietnam unit directly mass-produces liquid-cooling components for AI data-center systems and also supplies manufacturing equipment. In robotics, the company is discussing supply of autonomous mobile robot, reducer and joint structural parts with domestic robot companies.

Its AI production line for medical devices is targeted for full delivery in December 2026. Bulls argue that a wider set of end markets beyond defense could reduce exposure to any single sector's cycle.

Bear factors

Persistent net losses for controlling shareholders

Net losses attributable to controlling shareholders over the last four quarters total KRW 23.8 billion. The Q1 2026 loss was KRW 16 billion and the Q3 2026 loss was KRW 8.7 billion, a wide gap from positive operating profit in the same periods. Q3 2026 also turned to a loss compared with the prior-year quarter. If operating profit does not carry through to net profit, the substance of the margin improvement is limited.

Risk of shipment deferrals

The company said first-half 2026 results were temporarily affected because some equipment shipments moved into the second half on customers' schedules. The second quarter of 2026 returned to an operating loss of KRW 2.19 billion.

Because the timing of order-to-sales conversion depends on customer schedules, repeated deferrals could delay the point at which the business returns to sustained profit.

Balance-sheet burden and cash flow

Total liabilities at end-2025 were KRW 123.4 billion, above total equity of KRW 62.6 billion, and the debt-to-equity ratio was 197.3%. Operating cash flow was KRW -18.3 billion in 2023 and KRW -4.79 billion in 2025, and 2024 at KRW 4.15 billion was the only positive year in the table.

Continued investment in new businesses and overseas subsidiaries could constrain the pace of improvement given funding needs.

Risk factors

Business and order concentration

Defense orders can shift in timing with geopolitics and customer procurement. The robot CDMO and medical device lines are still at the discussion and target stage, so their revenue scale is not yet set.

The structure means a delay in a single project can push back revenue conversion, as the first-half 2026 shipment deferrals showed.

Overseas subsidiaries and investment costs

Investment to strengthen the Japanese and Vietnamese subsidiaries raised consolidated costs, and AI data-center cooling part development costs were reflected in 2025 consolidated results. If overseas production investment takes time to yield profit, the burden on earnings persists. Exchange-rate movements can also affect the results of overseas units.

Capital structure and funding

Total liabilities were KRW 123.4 billion at end-2025, and the debt-to-equity ratio was 197.3%. Operating cash flow was negative at KRW -4.79 billion in 2025, so operations did not generate enough cash.

While operating losses continue, handling debt repayment and new investment at the same time could be difficult, and if new equity is needed, existing shareholders' stake could be diluted.

Overall view

Daesung Hi-Tech is widening its parts business into defense, robotics and AI data-center components, and revenue has recovered to KRW 32.9 billion in the third quarter of 2026, up 42.6% year on year. Backlog of KRW 76.5 billion at end-June 2026 rose 144% year on year.

Operating profit turned positive in the first and third quarters of 2026, yet net losses attributable to controlling shareholders over the last four quarters total KRW 23.8 billion. The full-year 2025 operating loss narrowed to KRW 2.3 billion, but the company has not yet exited a three-year run of operating losses.

A debt-to-equity ratio of 197.3% and total liabilities of KRW 123.4 billion keep the balance sheet a point to monitor. Bullish factors include the backlog, the defense supply-chain entry and new business lines. Bearish factors include persistent net losses, possible shipment deferrals and the financing burden.

The next checkpoints are whether the 2026 annual report shows full-year operating profit, whether the gap between operating and net results narrows, and how quickly the backlog converts into sales in the first-quarter 2027 report.
