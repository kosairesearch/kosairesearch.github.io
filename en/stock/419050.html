02

Business structure

Samkee Energy Solutions was spun off in October 2020 from the EV battery division of its parent Samkee and listed on KOSDAQ in 2023. Its established core product is battery end plates, aluminum die-cast mechanical parts that, according to the company, are applied to overseas EV platforms including VW MEB.

The growth engine is the FF-PCB-based sensing connector used in battery management systems, alongside hybrid and internal-combustion powertrain parts made by the North American production subsidiary.

FF-PCB sales rose from about KRW 30 million in 2024 to KRW 5.4 billion in 2025, and reached KRW 17.6 billion in the first half of 2026, lifting its share of standalone sales to 22.02% (MoneyToday, reported October 8, 2026).

The mechanical parts business posted cumulative January-September 2026 sales of KRW 60.5 billion, up 40% year on year, according to the company's November 16, 2026 release (preliminary).

The North American subsidiary's sales rose from about KRW 17.7 billion in 2024 to KRW 60.4 billion in 2025 and reached KRW 33.4 billion in the first half of 2026. Its customers are centered on global battery makers, and the FF-PCB technology has completed US patent registration.

In the domestic secondary-battery parts theme, Sungwoo Hitech is among the names grouped together. Its revenue is small compared with large listed auto parts companies, and it is distinguished as a specialist supplier focused on battery components.

04

Earnings analysis

On the filed annual figures, sales went from KRW 107 billion in 2022 to KRW 90.9 billion in 2023 (down 15.1% year on year), then to KRW 98.7 billion in 2024 (up 8.6%) and KRW 144.2 billion in 2025 (up 46.1%).

Sales rose for two consecutive years from 2024 through 2025, and 2025 was the largest year of the 2022-2025 period. Operating profit fell from KRW 8.59 billion in 2022 to KRW 745 million in 2023 (down 91.3%), then turned into an operating loss of KRW 6.26 billion in 2024.

It returned to operating profit of KRW 5.43 billion in 2025. The operating margin was 8.0% in 2022, 0.8% in 2023, -6.3% in 2024 and 3.8% in 2025.

Net profit attributable to controlling shareholders was KRW 7.98 billion in 2022 and KRW 4.75 billion in 2023 (down 40.5%), then turned into a net loss of KRW 4.81 billion in 2024 and widened to a net loss of KRW 11.1 billion in 2025.

Net profit including non-controlling interests followed the same direction, with net losses of KRW 4.81 billion in 2024 and KRW 11.1 billion in 2025. The fact that the operating margin stayed at 3.8% in 2025, even as sales grew 46.1%, is a defining feature of this earnings structure.

The debt-to-equity ratio rose from 110.7% in 2022 to 214.3% in 2025, when total equity was KRW 91.7 billion, of which KRW 64.2 billion belonged to controlling shareholders.

Operating cash flow was KRW 3.96 billion in 2022, KRW 8.84 billion in 2023, KRW 4.06 billion in 2024 and KRW 17 billion in 2025, the largest of the four years.

The quarterly path began with the fourth quarter of 2025, when sales of KRW 39.6 billion (up 0.9% quarter on quarter) came with an operating loss of KRW 3.09 billion and a net loss attributable to controlling shareholders of KRW 12.3 billion.

In the first quarter of 2026, sales were KRW 48.9 billion (up 23.6% quarter on quarter), operating profit KRW 1.14 billion and net profit attributable to controlling shareholders KRW 710 million, turning profitable.

In the second quarter, sales reached KRW 64.3 billion (up 31.4% quarter on quarter), operating profit KRW 3.81 billion (up 233.6%) and net profit attributable to controlling shareholders KRW 4.34 billion.

In the third quarter, sales were KRW 62.6 billion (down 2.7% quarter on quarter, up 59.5% year on year), operating profit KRW 2.72 billion (down 28.6% quarter on quarter, down 5.5% year on year), and a net loss attributable to controlling shareholders of KRW 3.63 billion, a return to loss.

The net loss attributable to controlling shareholders over the last four quarters totals KRW 10.9 billion. Some quarters show operating profit alongside a net loss for controlling shareholders, so the items below operating profit are the key point for reading the earnings structure.

05

Industry analysis

Demand from downstream markets runs along two paths. One is the existing EV battery parts market, which a MoneyToday analysis reported on October 8, 2026 as recovering. The other is energy storage system (ESS) demand driven by AI data centers.

Research summaries describe the global BESS market as growing on expanding renewable energy and rising power demand from AI data centers. The company is building FF-PCB for ESS into a new growth axis, and it has completed US patent registration for the FF-PCB technology.

Viewed by cycle position, EV parts are in a recovery phase while ESS parts appear to be at an early stage of expansion. The same analysis said FF-PCB has begun to establish itself as a new growth axis while the end-plate business recovers.

On competition, the company is a specialist supplier combining aluminum die-casting with battery electrical parts. Its revenue is small compared with large listed auto parts makers, so results are sensitive to changes in customer volumes.

In North America, competition for hybrid vehicle component orders is also under way, and its position there will be confirmed through new order results. Movements in downstream industries and the degree of customer concentration are the main variables shaping earnings volatility.

06

Outlook

The company's stated target is annual FF-PCB sales of at least KRW 44 billion for 2026 (MoneyToday, reported October 8, 2026). Given cumulative January-September FF-PCB sales of KRW 30.9 billion (preliminary), whether the target is met will be confirmed in fourth-quarter 2026 results and the 2026 annual report.

Production capacity has also expanded, with the Suwon electrical parts plant rising from 1.84 million units to 4.27 million units in the first half of 2026.

In North America, the company secured two hybrid components, including lower crank cases, in orders totaling USD 211.56 million, with supply running from October 2026 through 2033.

The same analysis expected two additional hybrid orders to begin generating new sales from the fourth quarter and to be fully reflected in annual results in 2027. The company said it would strengthen its North American production base to secure a stable growth foundation (Newspim, reported November 16, 2026).

In defense, it is developing explosive detectors and tracked-vehicle battery packs at a Gwangju defense base, and it is also pursuing battery parts for robots. The company plans to broaden its supply scope into ESS and hybrid components to build a medium- to long-term growth base.

The confirmed facts are order sizes and supply periods, while the timing of actual revenue recognition will depend on how quickly production ramps up.

08

Bull factors

Expansion of ESS and FF-PCB sales

Cumulative January-September 2026 FF-PCB sales reached KRW 30.9 billion (preliminary), well above full-year 2025 sales of KRW 5.4 billion. The company set a 2026 annual target of at least KRW 44 billion.

The company attributes this growth to rising ESS demand from AI data center expansion and wider supply to global battery makers.

Larger North American order backlog

The North American subsidiary secured two hybrid components in orders totaling USD 211.56 million, with supply running from October 2026 through 2033. The company said major North American projects over the last two years total about KRW 600 billion.

North American sales rose to KRW 60.4 billion in 2025 from about KRW 17.7 billion in 2024, more than tripling. An analysis expects this trend to be fully reflected in 2027 annual results.

Operating profit positive in each 2026 quarter

Operating profit was KRW 1.14 billion in the first quarter of 2026, KRW 3.81 billion in the second and KRW 2.72 billion in the third, so each of those quarters was profitable at the operating line.

This follows an operating loss of KRW 3.09 billion in the fourth quarter of 2025, showing a recovery at the operating level. Cumulative January-September operating profit of KRW 7.7 billion (preliminary) exceeded full-year 2025 operating profit of KRW 5.43 billion.

09

Bear factors

Gap between operating profit and net results

Third-quarter 2026 operating profit was KRW 2.72 billion, but the loss attributable to controlling shareholders was KRW 3.63 billion, a return to loss. The 2025 loss attributable to controlling shareholders was KRW 11.1 billion, a wider loss than the prior year.

The composition of items below operating profit must be checked in the filing text to read the earnings structure.

Cost burden and margin pressure

Consolidated first-quarter 2026 sales rose 65.6% year on year, but operating profit fell 45.2%, according to a WiseReport company comment dated June 8, 2026. The same comment attributes the decline to higher cost burden reducing gross profit and operating profit.

Third-quarter operating profit fell 28.6% quarter on quarter. The bear case is that cost pressure from stabilizing North American production lines could keep margins under pressure.

Rising debt burden

The debt-to-equity ratio rose from 110.7% in 2022 to 154.7% in 2023, 176.4% in 2024 and 214.3% in 2025. Total liabilities at end-2025 were KRW 196.4 billion, well above total equity of KRW 91.7 billion. Equity attributable to controlling shareholders is KRW 64.2 billion, so debt is large relative to the equity base.

The bear case is that funding needs for North American expansion and new orders could keep financing costs and funding pressure elevated.

10

Risk factors

Downstream demand swings

Volumes for the core EV battery parts move with EV demand. A recovery appeared in the first half of 2026, but if EV demand slows again, end-plate sales could be affected.

The company is broadening supply into ESS and hybrid components, yet customer concentration should be checked against the customer disclosure in the annual report.

Financial leverage

The debt-to-equity ratio rose to 214.3% in 2025 from 110.7% in 2022. Operating cash flow reached KRW 17 billion in 2025, up from KRW 4.06 billion in 2024, but borrowing levels and interest costs are sensitive to earnings swings. Financial capacity should be read together with the trend in operating cash flow.

North American ramp-up and FX

The U.S. production subsidiary is pursuing a profitability recovery through stabilizing local production lines and improving efficiency (Newspim, reported November 16, 2026). If mass production of new projects is delayed, the timing of revenue recognition could shift between quarters. Converting dollar-denominated orders into won can also affect reported results through exchange rate movements.

12

Overall view

Samkee Energy Solutions has seen sales rise sharply in 2026, driven by recovery in its established end-plate business and expansion of ESS FF-PCB.

Cumulative January-September 2026 consolidated sales reached KRW 175.8 billion, up 68.1% year on year, and operating profit of KRW 7.7 billion (all preliminary) exceeded full-year 2025 operating profit of KRW 5.43 billion.

However, the net result attributable to controlling shareholders posted losses in 2024 and 2025, turned profitable in the first quarter of 2026, and returned to a loss in the third quarter. The debt-to-equity ratio rose to 214.3% in 2025.

On the strength side, the growth pace of FF-PCB and the North American hybrid order backlog are taking shape. On the weakness side, the gap between operating profit and net results, cost pressure from North American ramp-up, and leverage are cited. Which of these trends persists will become clear in fourth-quarter 2026 results and the 2026 annual report.
