Construction slump pushes Hyundai Livart into the red; cost gains are the test

02

Business structure

Hyundai Livart Furniture Company is a furniture and interior company affiliated with a major department store group, operating consumer-facing furniture and interior business (B2C) alongside business-to-business work (B2B) such as built-in furniture, office furniture and overseas temporary-facility projects.

Of Q3 2026 consolidated revenue of KRW 333.6 billion, the furniture business accounted for KRW 183.8 billion, the B2B business for KRW 140.2 billion and overseas subsidiaries for KRW 9.6 billion, according to a November 2, 2026 DigitalToday report.

The same report put furniture revenue down 9.1% year on year, B2B revenue up 11.6% and overseas subsidiary revenue down 25.7%. Another outlet, Joonggi Economy, reported that B2B furniture revenue fell 12% in the same quarter, so the direction of B2B change differs by source.

According to the company, household furniture sales fell 15.6%, interior renovation sales fell 26% and built-in furniture sales fell 18.2%. Built-in furniture moves with new housing supply and move-in volumes, which makes it directly exposed to the construction slowdown.

Overseas, the company is expanding temporary-facility work and FF&E supply, with the Iraq CSSP project contracted in early 2026 (about KRW 121.5 billion) as a representative case.

Competitive positions differ by segment: office furniture overlaps with Fursys and beds with AceBed, while built-in and contract markets are won through project bidding. The company said it is avoiding low-price bids and expanding premium items to improve order-level margins.

04

Earnings analysis

Annual revenue rose from KRW 1.5 trillion in 2022 to KRW 1.59 trillion in 2023 (up 6.0%) and KRW 1.87 trillion in 2024 (up 18.0%), then fell to KRW 1.55 trillion in 2025 (down 17.3%). Among 2022 to 2025, 2024 was the largest year and 2022 the smallest.

Operating losses of KRW 27.9 billion in 2022 and KRW 19.9 billion in 2023 narrowed over the period, and operating profit of KRW 24 billion in 2024 marked a turn to profit. Operating profit fell 34.6% to KRW 15.7 billion in 2025, with operating margins of -1.9% in 2022, -1.3% in 2023, 1.3% in 2024 and 1.0% in 2025.

Net income attributable to controlling shareholders turned positive at KRW 15.2 billion in 2024 before falling 51.2% to KRW 7.41 billion in 2025, and total net income including non-controlling interests was also KRW 7.41 billion in 2025.

At year-end 2025, total equity was KRW 418.1 billion, all of it attributable to controlling shareholders, and total liabilities were KRW 355.2 billion. The debt ratio moved from 94.8% in 2022 and 123.5% in 2023 to 108.5% in 2024 and 85.0% in 2025.

Operating cash flow went from negative KRW 42.7 billion in 2022 to KRW 2.75 billion in 2023, KRW 40.6 billion in 2024 and KRW 38 billion in 2025.

Quarterly, operating profit of KRW 3.74 billion in Q3 2025 became an operating loss of KRW 2.61 billion in Q4 2025, then operating profit of KRW 1.05 billion in Q1 2026 and KRW 5.56 billion in Q2 2026, up 428.1% quarter on quarter.

Q3 2026 revenue was KRW 333.6 billion, down 10.6% from the prior quarter and down 2.1% year on year, and the operating loss of KRW 851 million reflected lower built-in supply alongside weaker consumer demand.

The company cited a prolonged construction slump and said selling and administrative expenses fell 10.6% year on year, which was not enough to offset the revenue decline. The pretax loss also reflected foreign-exchange valuation losses tied to a weaker exchange rate.

Preliminary nine-month 2026 figures released on November 2, 2026 showed revenue of KRW 1.06 trillion (down 10.6%) and operating profit of KRW 5.76 billion (down 68.6%). Over the last four quarters from Q4 2025 to Q3 2026, net loss attributable to controlling shareholders totaled KRW 2.46 billion.

05

Industry analysis

Furniture demand depends heavily on new housing sales and move-in volumes. Mirae Asset Securities expects the recovery in B2B furniture revenue to be delayed by new-sale and move-in declines that began in 2023, according to a November 3, 2026 report reported by Joonggi Economy.

Heungkuk Securities said in a May 2026 report that revenue has kept declining since the first quarter of 2024 amid construction and housing slowdowns. The company itself cited a prolonged construction slump as the reason built-in supply shrank.

On the consumer side, the company attributed weaker B2C furniture sales to fewer move-ins and softer consumer sentiment. Views on the cycle bottom are divided. Mirae Asset Securities said slower construction recovery makes a meaningful share-price rebound point hard to expect.

B2B accounts for more than 40% of revenue, which raises sensitivity to the construction cycle. On costs, Heungkuk Securities reflected segment-level cost gains and said gross margins are improving.

Among listed furniture makers, Fursys focuses on office furniture, AceBed on beds and Zinus on mattresses, so direct comparison depends on product scope. Mirae Asset Securities also views overseas temporary-facility orders as having low near-term visibility.

The timing of demand recovery and the speed of cost improvement are the core variables for the industry outlook.

Mirae Asset Securities projected 2027 operating profit would rise 94.2% to KRW 14 billion. In overseas temporary-facility work, the Iraq CSSP project contracted in early 2026 is gradually contributing more revenue.

Share count reduction from treasury-share cancellation is another variable for per-share metrics, which Heungkuk Securities said it reflected in a May 2026 report. Bull factors

Expanding overseas temporary-facility orders

The Iraq CSSP project contracted in early 2026 (about KRW 121.5 billion) is gradually adding to overseas B2B revenue. The company is expanding temporary-facility and FF&E work to add new projects.

Overseas subsidiary revenue fell to KRW 9.6 billion in Q3, but overseas temporary-facility work could partly offset weaker domestic built-in demand.

Quarterly profit rebound on cost gains

Q2 2026 operating profit was KRW 5.56 billion, up 428.1% from the prior quarter. Q3 selling and administrative expenses of KRW 59.1 billion fell 10.6% year on year, and Heungkuk Securities said in a May 2026 report that it reflected segment-level cost gains. The company is tightening order cost control by avoiding low-price bids.

Easing balance sheet pressure

The debt ratio fell from 123.5% in 2023 to 85.0% in 2025, and total equity at the end of 2025 was KRW 418.1 billion. Operating cash flow stayed positive at KRW 40.6 billion in 2024 and KRW 38 billion in 2025. Cancellation of treasury shares reduced the share count, which affects per-share metrics.

Risk factors

Prolonged construction and housing slowdown

The construction and housing slowdown has lasted since the first quarter of 2024, so built-in supply could recover later than expected. Mirae Asset Securities expects B2B furniture recovery to lag because of new-sale and move-in declines that began in 2023. With B2B above 40% of revenue, changes in construction activity pass through directly.

Visibility of overseas temporary-facility work

Mirae Asset Securities said overseas temporary-facility orders are unlikely to show visible results in the short term. Overseas subsidiary revenue fell 25.7% in Q3, which shows the volatility of this business.

Overseas sites are exposed to local execution schedules and currency movements, so the pace at which orders become revenue can vary.

Exchange rate and non-operating swings

In Q3 2026, foreign-exchange valuation losses from a weaker exchange rate widened the pretax loss. Even with operating profit, net income can swing with currency moves. The company's policy of avoiding low-price bids may coincide with fewer orders, making the balance between revenue and margin another variable.

Overall view

Hyundai Livart Furniture Company swung to an operating loss of KRW 851 million in Q3 2026 as construction weakness and softer B2C demand overlapped.

Operating profit turned positive in 2024 but fell to KRW 15.7 billion in 2025, and preliminary nine-month 2026 operating profit was KRW 5.76 billion, down 68.6% (released November 2, 2026). By contrast, Q2 2026 operating profit recovered to KRW 5.56 billion, so quarterly profitability is volatile.

The debt ratio fell to 85.0% in 2025 from 94.8% in 2022, and total equity was KRW 418.1 billion. Overseas temporary-facility work and cost gains are the improving factors, while delayed recovery in built-in and consumer demand and limited near-term visibility on overseas orders are the weakening factors.

Mirae Asset Securities estimates 2026 operating profit at KRW 7 billion, cut its target price to 6,300 won, and kept a Neutral rating. The next checkpoints are the Q4 2026 preliminary operating result, full-year figures in the 2026 annual report, and the Q1 2027 built-in revenue trend.
