Photodiode growth brings profitable quarters, distribution mix remains the challenge

02

Business structure

Wooriro was founded in 1998 and listed on KOSDAQ in 2012 as an optical communication component maker. Its core products are optical distribution units (PLC) and photodiodes (PD), and it also runs IT storage device sales (SI) and audio equipment distribution.

OpenBizData, an external aggregator, put distribution (SI and audio) at about 68% and in-house optical components (PD and PLC) at about 32% for 2025.

The same source said photodiode revenue rose about 77% from KRW 5.5 billion in 2024 to KRW 9.8 billion in 2025, linked to recovering demand from AI data centers and 5G systems. The investment media outlet Investing.com reported that the SI division generated KRW 8.095 billion in Q1 2026, or 55.7% of total revenue.

Butler's aggregation of Q2 2026 mix instead shows CJS at 46%, SI at 38% and optical communications at 16%, so the figures depend on how segments are classified. Photodiode module output in Q1 2026 was 71,236 units, with utilization at 64.53% of capacity.

Exports rose to KRW 9 billion in 2025 from KRW 6.7 billion in 2024 on the same aggregator's data, and dollar settlement makes results exposed to currency moves. Among listed peers in optical components, TAIHAN Fiberoptics operates in the same sector.

R&D spending was KRW 1.838 billion in 2024, or 3.88% of revenue, and the company is pursuing single-photon detection technology for quantum encryption communication and lidar markets.

04

Earnings analysis

Based on reported results, annual revenue fell from KRW 99.0 billion in 2022 to KRW 54.6 billion in 2023 (down 44.8% year on year), KRW 47.4 billion in 2024 (down 13.3%), and KRW 42.5 billion in 2025 (down 10.3%), a decline for three consecutive years from 2023 through 2025. 2022 was the largest year and 2025 the smallest.

Operating profit swung from a profit of KRW 1.35 billion in 2022 to an operating loss of KRW 3.45 billion in 2023, followed by operating losses of KRW 3.0 billion in 2024 and KRW 680 million in 2025, so the losses continued but narrowed.

The operating margin moved from 1.4% in 2022 to -6.3% in 2023, -6.3% in 2024, and -1.6% in 2025. An external data aggregator attributed the improvement in earnings to the gross margin rising from 10.9% to 18.8%, and also noted that the consolidated cost-of-sales ratio fell from 89% the previous year to 81%.

Net income attributable to controlling shareholders went from a net loss of KRW 3.45 billion in 2022 to net losses of KRW 4.79 billion in 2023 and KRW 5.11 billion in 2024, so the losses widened, before shrinking to a net loss of KRW 790 million in 2025.

The company has posted losses for four consecutive years, from 2022 through 2025. On a net income basis including non-controlling interests, the 2025 net loss is also KRW 790 million.

Cash flow from operating activities was KRW 1.29 billion in 2022, then negative at KRW -1.76 billion in 2023 and KRW -3.19 billion in 2024, before turning positive at KRW 1.16 billion in 2025.

At the end of 2025, total equity was KRW 39.2 billion, of which KRW 38.8 billion was attributable to controlling shareholders, and total liabilities were KRW 17.3 billion. The debt ratio was 61.9% in 2022, 33.5% in 2023, 50.8% in 2024, and 44.1% in 2025.

In quarterly results, revenue bottomed at KRW 11.4 billion in the fourth quarter of 2025 (down 5.1% from the previous quarter) and then rose for three consecutive quarters to KRW 14.5 billion in the first quarter of 2026, KRW 14.7 billion in the second quarter, and KRW 20.4 billion in the third quarter, with third-quarter revenue up 68.8% from the same period a year earlier.

Operating profit grew from KRW 53.92 million in the fourth quarter of 2025 to KRW 1.47 billion in the first quarter of 2026 and KRW 2.55 billion in the second quarter, but fell 22.5% from the previous quarter to KRW 1.98 billion in the third quarter.

Because operating profit declined in the third quarter even as revenue rose 38.2%, the operating margin works out to be lower than in the second quarter. The specific cause of the third-quarter decline in operating profit is hard to pinpoint from the reported figures alone.

Net income attributable to controlling shareholders turned from a net loss of KRW 420 million in the fourth quarter of 2025 to a profit of KRW 1.53 billion in the first quarter of 2026, followed by KRW 1.27 billion in the second quarter and KRW 1.67 billion in the third quarter, and the total for the most recent four quarters is KRW 4.05 billion.

According to external data, the improvement in the first quarter of 2026 was driven jointly by expanded storage device sales in the SI division and higher photodiode revenue in the optical communications division.

05

Industry analysis

Demand for Wooriro's optical components is tied to investment in AI data centers and 5G and 6G infrastructure. An external aggregator attributed the 2025 photodiode revenue growth to recovering demand for 10G, 25G, 50G and 100G-class photodiodes used in data centers and 5G.

In the same source, optical distribution unit (PLC) revenue fell from KRW 5.9 billion to KRW 3.9 billion and then KRW 3.6 billion, which was described as a structural weakness. Demand direction is therefore split between high-speed photodiodes and lower-speed PLC products.

Cycle position can be gauged from utilization, and the 64.53% photodiode module utilization in Q1 2026 indicates spare production capacity. Whether photodiode demand lasts depends on the AI investment cycle, so a firm call is difficult.

Wooriro is developing high-capacity 400G and 800G photodiodes, so product mix across speed generations is a key measure of competitiveness. TAIHAN Fiberoptics is also listed in the same sector for optical components, and Wooriro's revenue scale is small compared with large electronics and components names.

In March 2026, media coverage linked the stock's sharp rise to the optical semiconductor theme. On currency, rising exports and dollar settlement mean exchange rate moves feed into both won-denominated revenue and profit.

06

Outlook

Wooriro's stated direction centers on high-capacity photodiodes and new optical sensor markets. The company is pursuing technology development and early mass production of 400G and 800G high-capacity photodiodes.

It has also set out a direction to enter quantum encryption communication and lidar markets using single-photon detection technology. Q3 2026 revenue rose to KRW 20.4 billion while operating profit fell to KRW 1.98 billion, so the next check is whether higher sales translate into a better margin.

Q1 2026 utilization of 64.53% suggests room to raise output without new capacity, so revenue could scale quickly if demand holds. With exports growing as a share of sales, the effect of currency moves on results also needs watching.

R&D spending was KRW 1.838 billion in 2024, or 3.88% of revenue, so new business preparation continues to carry cost. The scheduled date for lifting venture company designation is August 30, 2027, so business and financial changes around that date are also a point to monitor.

The first check in the next periodic report, the 2026 annual report, is whether the operating margin moved from -1.6% in 2025.

Bull factors

Photodiode demand recovery and spare utilization

Q1 2026 photodiode module output was 71,236 units at 64.53% utilization, leaving production room that could convert added demand into sales. Demand for high-speed products used in data centers and 5G was cited as the driver.

Sustained quarterly revenue growth and profitability

Revenue bottomed at KRW 11.4 billion in Q4 2025 and then rose to KRW 14.5 billion in Q1 2026, KRW 14.7 billion in Q2 and KRW 20.4 billion in Q3, three straight quarters of growth. Q3 revenue was up 68.8% year on year. Operating profit and parent net income were both positive in each quarter from Q1 to Q3 2026.

High-speed optical component technology pipeline

The company is developing 400G and 800G high-capacity photodiodes and pushing for early mass production. It is pursuing quantum encryption communication and lidar markets with single-photon detection technology. R&D spending in 2024 was KRW 1.838 billion, or 3.88% of revenue.

Whether the product line keeps pace with the shift to faster generations will determine how durable this advantage is.

Distribution mix and the Q3 profit decline

An external aggregator put distribution (SI and audio) at about 68% of the mix and described the margin as thin. Q3 2026 revenue rose to KRW 20.4 billion, but operating profit fell to KRW 1.98 billion, down 22.5% from Q2. The cause of the Q3 decline cannot be pinned down from the disclosed figures alone.

Currency exposure and cash flow swings

Exports rose to KRW 9 billion in 2025 from KRW 6.7 billion in 2024 on an external aggregator's figures, and dollar settlement leaves results exposed to exchange rates. Operating cash flow was negative at KRW 1.76 billion in 2023 and KRW 3.19 billion in 2024 before turning positive at KRW 1.16 billion in 2025. Because the positive period is short, the durability of cash generation needs more years of data to confirm.

Risk factors

Business mix and customer concentration

Storage device distribution supports the top line, but an external analysis describes its margins as thin, so revenue gains may not reach profit. Photodiode demand is tied to data center and 5G investment, so a slowdown in that cycle could swing sales sharply.

Segment classifications differ across sources, which makes division-level profitability hard to gauge precisely.

Exchange rate and export exposure

As exports grew as a share of sales, dollar settlement also increased. Currency moves can shift won-denominated revenue and profit together. Operating cash flow has been positive only in 2025, so the cash generation needed to absorb currency swings has a short track record.

Theme-driven share price swings and new business uncertainty

In March 2026, media coverage tied Wooriro's share price surge to the optical semiconductor theme. Price moves driven by a theme can diverge from the earnings trend. Quantum encryption and lidar businesses cannot be separated out in the disclosed earnings figures, which makes their performance hard to gauge.
