02

Business structure

Gaonchips, founded in 2012, is a system semiconductor design company that serves as an official Design Solution Partner for Samsung Foundry and as an Arm AADP partner. It sits between fabless chip designers and foundries, supporting customers' custom ASIC development from design through mass production.

Its revenue mix has shifted from design services toward product sales: the service share fell from 60% in 2020 to 36% in 2023 and 11% in 2025, while product sales rose to 88%. By application, AI/HPC and automotive semiconductors each account for more than 40% of revenue and make up most of sales.

Demand for high-performance AI chips has grown with the spread of generative AI and expanding data center investment.

A key customer project is an ASIC design contract with a Japanese corporate customer, originally worth KRW 57.7 billion and due for completion by 2025; it was adjusted to KRW 55.7 billion and its period was extended to August 2026.

The company is expanding its global customer base, centered on its US and Japanese subsidiaries.

In competition, Gaonchips is most often compared with other domestic design house (DSP) companies that vie for references on Samsung Foundry processes, and KB Securities said in 2024 that it was the domestic DSP best optimized for the highest-end process nodes.

04

Earnings analysis

Annual revenue rose from KRW 43.3 billion in 2022 to KRW 63.6 billion in 2023, up 46.8% year on year, and to KRW 96.5 billion in 2024, up 51.7%, making 2024 the largest year in the 2022 to 2025 period. Revenue fell to KRW 68.5 billion in 2025, down 29.0%.

Operating profit was KRW 3.9 billion in 2022, KRW 4.35 billion in 2023 (up 11.6%) and KRW 3.52 billion in 2024 (down 19.0%), before the company swung to an operating loss of KRW 16.7 billion in 2025. The operating margin declined from 9.0% in 2022 to 6.8% in 2023 and 3.7% in 2024, and stood at -24.4% in 2025.

Margins fell even as revenue grew in 2023 and 2024; IB Tomato reported that operating costs such as labor and recurring R&D expenses rose, and the cost of sales ratio climbed from 73.16% in 2022 to 85.24% in 2024. R&D spending rose from KRW 2.3 billion in 2023 to KRW 3.2 billion in 2024 and KRW 3.6 billion in 2025.

For 2025, the company cited delayed development projects as the main cause of lower revenue, and also pointed to higher fixed costs from facility investment and engineer hiring, plus convertible bond interest and derivative valuation effects.

The net loss attributable to controlling shareholders was KRW 14.4 billion in 2025, compared with a net profit of KRW 7.53 billion in 2024. By quarter, revenue was KRW 11.1 billion in the third quarter of 2025 with an operating loss of KRW 7.28 billion.

In the fourth quarter revenue reached KRW 33.1 billion, up 199.3% from the prior quarter, and operating profit of KRW 1.71 billion turned positive.

Korea Investment & Securities had projected in a November 2025 report that delayed project progress recognition would concentrate in the fourth quarter and drive a return to profit, and the fourth-quarter result moved in that direction.

Revenue then fell to KRW 20.6 billion in the first quarter of 2026, with an operating loss of KRW 3.91 billion. In the second quarter revenue was KRW 21.3 billion with operating profit of KRW 328 million, and in the third quarter revenue reached KRW 26.6 billion with operating profit of KRW 2.99 billion.

05

Industry analysis

End-market demand centers on high-performance AI chips, driven by the spread of generative AI and expanding data center investment. In 2024, KB Securities analyzed that as global big tech companies expanded in-house chip development, new contracts leveraging existing references would increase.

Cho Su-heon of Korea Investment & Securities said in a November 2025 report that new orders were steadily rising across domestic DSP companies, raising visibility on an industry recovery.

Analysis of the 2025 slump attributed it to a combination of business structure and new development projects rather than weaker demand. The same report noted that a majority of the company's roughly 240 engineers were assigned to a very large 2nm AI chip development project, which raised short-term cost burdens.

In cycle terms, orders have been building while revenue recognition lags behind development progress. Against peers, the company's Samsung Foundry DSP position and its automotive and AI design portfolio have been cited as strengths.

However, because the pace of a few large projects heavily influences quarterly results, delays in any single project can slow the visible recovery. The order backlog at the end of 2025 was KRW 8.2 billion, down from KRW 25.0 billion at the end of the third quarter of 2025.

06

Outlook

Consolidated revenue in the first half of 2026 rose 72.1% year on year, while the operating loss narrowed by 67.9% and the net loss narrowed by 91.7%, according to the wisereport compilation.

Operating profit of KRW 2.99 billion turned positive from a year earlier in the third quarter, and revenue rose for two consecutive quarters starting from the second quarter. On orders, three new ASIC development contracts worth KRW 59.4 billion were secured in the second half of 2025.

However, the order backlog fell to KRW 8.2 billion at the end of 2025, so whether future order disclosures and revenue recognition can support results is the next checkpoint.

For the 2nm project, a November 2025 report presented a schedule of tape-out within the year and delivery of prototypes to a customer in early 2026. Whether that schedule led to mass-production revenue will be visible in full-year 2026 results.

On funding, of the KRW 34.5 billion convertible bond issued in April 2025, KRW 14.5 billion was earmarked for facilities and KRW 20.0 billion for operations.

Tangible asset acquisitions rose from KRW 6.1 billion in 2022 to KRW 6.8 billion in 2023 and KRW 9.5 billion in 2024, and the company said it would keep investing until operations stabilize.

Whether the expansion of the Japanese subsidiary contract period and the global customer push, centered on the US and Japanese subsidiaries, become the core of revenue after 2026 is also an item to confirm.

08

Bull factors

Quarterly profit return and revenue rebound

Operating profit turned positive in the fourth quarter of 2025 at KRW 1.71 billion, and the second and third quarters of 2026 also posted operating profits. Third-quarter 2026 revenue was KRW 26.6 billion, up 140.8% year on year.

Revenue rising for two straight quarters from the second quarter of 2026 is the factual basis for reading that project revenue recognition has normalized.

Samsung Foundry DSP position and new contracts

The company remains an official Design Solution Partner for Samsung Foundry, handling advanced-node design for customers. Three new ASIC development contracts worth KRW 59.4 billion were secured in the second half of 2025. Korea Investment & Securities said new orders have been steadily rising across domestic DSP companies.

AI and automotive focused business mix

AI/HPC and automotive semiconductors each account for more than 40% of revenue, and together they make up over 80% of sales. Product sales rising to 88% marks a shift away from a design-service-centered model.

Generative AI adoption and expanding data center investment are cited as the structural background for demand for high-performance AI chips.

Bear factors

Annual loss and eroded profitability

The operating loss in 2025 was KRW 16.7 billion, and the operating margin was -24.4%. The first quarter of 2026 also posted an operating loss of KRW 3.91 billion.

The four-quarter total net loss attributable to controlling shareholders is KRW 83.67 million, so the return to profit so far is limited to individual quarters.

Financial strain and higher fixed costs

The debt-to-equity ratio rose to 191.3% at the end of 2025 from 62.5% in 2024, with total liabilities of KRW 104.6 billion. Convertible bond interest and derivative valuation effects were reflected in 2025 results.

Higher fixed costs from facility investment and engineer hiring mean costs do not fall quickly when revenue declines.

Shrinking backlog and reliance on large projects

The order backlog fell to KRW 8.2 billion at the end of 2025 from KRW 25.0 billion at the end of the third quarter of 2025. The Japanese corporate ASIC contract was adjusted from KRW 57.7 billion to KRW 55.7 billion, and its period was extended to August 2026.

Because the 2025 slump centered on delays in a major project, the company's results depend heavily on individual projects.

10

Risk factors

Revenue recognition timing risk

Revenue from development projects is recognized based on progress, which creates large quarter-to-quarter swings. Revenue jumped to KRW 33.1 billion in the fourth quarter of 2025 and then fell 37.9% to KRW 20.6 billion in the first quarter of 2026. If progress on a specific project slows, quarterly results could swing back into losses.

Customer and order concentration risk

Adjustments and extensions to the Japanese corporate ASIC contract reflect how concentrated the order book is. Because the backlog fell from KRW 25.0 billion at the end of the third quarter of 2025 to KRW 8.2 billion at year-end, any gap in follow-on orders could translate into a revenue gap.

Results from the customer expansion centered on the US and Japanese subsidiaries are not yet fully visible in reported figures.

Financing and balance sheet risk

The debt-to-equity ratio stood at 191.3% at the end of 2025, with total liabilities of KRW 104.6 billion. The KRW 34.5 billion convertible bond issued in April 2025 was earmarked for KRW 14.5 billion in facilities and KRW 20.0 billion in operations. If profitability does not hold with high fixed costs, the burden of additional financing could increase.

12

Overall view

Gaonchips has a business built on AI and automotive design demand, but its results were hit hard in 2025, with revenue of KRW 68.5 billion (down 29.0%) and an operating loss of KRW 16.7 billion.

Quarterly operating profit has been positive since the fourth quarter of 2025, and third-quarter 2026 revenue of KRW 26.6 billion was up 140.8% year on year.

On the other hand, the first quarter of 2026 posted an operating loss of KRW 3.91 billion, and the four-quarter total net loss attributable to controlling shareholders is KRW 83.67 million.

On the balance sheet, the debt-to-equity ratio stands at 191.3%, and the burden of fixed costs from convertible bond funding and facility and staff investment remains.

On orders, KRW 59.4 billion of new contracts were secured in the second half of 2025, but the backlog fell to KRW 8.2 billion at year-end, so the base of orders feeding future revenue has narrowed.

Because strengths and weaknesses coexist, the next checkpoints are full-year 2026 operating results and first-quarter 2027 results. This report does not present an investment opinion, rating, or target price, and is provided for information only.
