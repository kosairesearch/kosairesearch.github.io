02

Business structure

JNB is a maker of vacuum-pump-related equipment and ultra-precision machined parts for semiconductor processes. Its main product is the stacker, which supports installation and operation of vacuum pumps, and the company sells its self-developed equipment through overseas companies to major domestic chipmakers.

Press reports say it has supplied Samsung Electronics' production lines as standard equipment for more than 20 years. The second pillar, ultra-precision machining, supplies core materials to overseas vacuum-pump makers.

According to Butler's aggregation, the 2026 Q2 revenue mix was 54% contract processing, 26% stackers, 10% on-tool kits, 7% other items such as modules, and 4% rental income. Contract processing accounts for more than half, so revenue is tied closely to customer order volumes.

In September 2025 the company said it would expand into multilayer ceramic capacitor (MLCC) blade parts and was reported to target entry into the Samsung Electro-Mechanics supply chain. Its subsidiary NextHigh is still in the new-business preparation stage, and related costs are reflected in consolidated results.

The largest shareholder, Lee Jeong-beom and related parties, held 71.12%, according to a 2025 press report. The industry classification is automotive, but the actual business is closer to semiconductor equipment and precision parts.

Because customers are concentrated among chipmakers, the company is directly exposed to the capital-spending cycle.

04

Earnings analysis

On a disclosed annual basis, revenue went from KRW 16.6 billion in 2022 to KRW 15.3 billion in 2023 (down 7.5% year on year), KRW 16 billion in 2024 (up 4.7%), and KRW 14.9 billion in 2025 (down 7.1%). 2022 was the largest year in the period and 2025 the smallest.

Operating profit moved from KRW 5.03 billion in 2022 to KRW 3.29 billion in 2023 (down 34.6%) and KRW 3.36 billion in 2024 (up 2.3%), then fell to KRW 175 million in 2025 (down 94.8%). The operating margin went from 30.4% in 2022 to 21.5% in 2023 and 21.0% in 2024, and reached 1.2% in 2025.

In its 2025 disclosure the company said weak recovery in the semiconductor industry reduced revenue, and that preparation costs for new businesses at a consolidated subsidiary cut operating profit.

Net profit attributable to controlling shareholders was KRW 5.22 billion in 2022, swung to a KRW 2.54 billion loss in 2023, returned to a KRW 1.99 billion profit in 2024, and turned to a KRW 348 million loss in 2025.

Net profit including non-controlling interests was a KRW 448 million loss in 2025, a different figure from the controlling-shareholder loss. Operating cash flow was KRW 4.09 billion in 2022, KRW 4.14 billion in 2023, KRW 4.67 billion in 2024, and KRW 2.52 billion in 2025.

The debt ratio rose from 126.6% in 2022 to 72.9% in 2023, 71.8% in 2024 and 80.1% in 2025. At the end of 2025, total equity was KRW 40.1 billion, of which KRW 39.3 billion belonged to controlling shareholders, and total liabilities were KRW 32.1 billion.

On a quarterly basis, operating losses narrowed from KRW 653 million in Q3 2025 to KRW 473 million in Q4 2025 and KRW 369 million in Q1 2026, and Q2 2026 turned to operating profit of KRW 265 million.

Q3 2026 revenue was KRW 5.59 billion, up 13.4% from the prior quarter and 48.4% year on year, with operating profit of KRW 585 million. Revenue has risen for three consecutive quarters from Q1 2026 through Q3 2026, after the low point in Q4 2025. The company said the Q3 2026 separate-basis operating margin was 18%.

However, the four quarters from Q4 2025 through Q3 2026 still add up to a controlling-shareholder net loss of KRW 1.47 billion.

05

Industry analysis

The downstream market for JNB is semiconductor capital spending. The company said that investment in new production lines by domestic and overseas chipmakers, driven by rising demand for AI and high-bandwidth memory (HBM), lifted core business revenue.

A 2025 press report said Samsung Electronics would resume investment in its Taylor fab in the United States, and named JNB's stacker systems as a likely beneficiary. However, the company's own 2025 explanation described the semiconductor recovery as weak, so downstream demand has not moved evenly from year to year.

In cycle terms, 2025 looks like the trough and revenue has recovered through Q3 2026. Three consecutive quarters of growth is an early signal of recovery, not confirmation of a cycle turn.

Both of the company's business lines depend on customer capital spending: stackers on new fab investment, and ultra-precision machining on production expansion at overseas vacuum-pump makers. The company and press reports describe the same logic, namely that more vacuum-pump production raises demand for core materials.

Because the company's profit structure differs from other automotive-classified listed companies, a direct comparison with sector averages does not fit the earnings profile well.

Its position within the semiconductor equipment and precision parts supply chain depends on how closely it is linked to specific customer lines.

Outlook

A company official said that as global chipmakers expand capital spending, the stacker and ultra-precision machining segments are entering full-scale joint growth.

In the same remarks, the official said the company would continue its results into next year by responding to rising customer demand and would also accelerate new-business results. This is a stated direction, and no numerical guidance was confirmed.

The next confirmable scheduled item is the Q4 and full-year 2026 result. The 2025 full-year results were disclosed on February 9, 2026, so the Q4 and full-year 2026 results are likely around early February 2027.

The 2026 annual report must be filed by March 31, 2027, and this will fix the full-year operating margin and controlling-shareholder net profit as disclosed figures.

On the new-business side, the key variables are whether the MLCC blade business enters the Samsung Electro-Mechanics supply chain and whether preparation costs at the subsidiary NextHigh decline.

If those costs do not fall, the link between core-business profit and consolidated profit, as seen in Q3 2026, could be tested.

Positive factors include three consecutive quarters of revenue growth and the return to operating profit, while negative factors include operating cash flow falling to KRW 2.52 billion in 2025 and the debt ratio rising to 80.1%. The biggest external variable is whether customer capital spending continues into 2027.

The operating schedule of Samsung Electronics' Taylor fab remains an external variable directly tied to the company's outlook.

Bull factors

Revenue recovery tied to capital spending

Q3 2026 revenue was KRW 5.59 billion, up 48.4% from a year earlier. Revenue rose for three consecutive quarters from Q1 2026 through Q3 2026, after the low in Q4 2025. The company attributes the increase to new production-line investment by chipmakers driven by AI and HBM demand.

Return to operating profit and margin improvement

Operating profit was KRW 265 million in Q2 2026 and KRW 585 million in Q3 2026. It also said operating profit improved for four consecutive quarters, as its own account.

Ultra-precision machining and diversification

Company-reported sales in the ultra-precision machining segment rose 67% year on year in Q3. Because it supplies core materials to overseas vacuum-pump makers, it can grow alongside demand for semiconductor materials. In September 2025 the company announced an expansion into MLCC blade parts.

09

Bear factors

Sharp earnings decline in 2025

Operating profit fell 94.8% in 2025 to KRW 175 million. The operating margin dropped from 30.4% in 2022 to 1.2% in 2025. The company cited weak semiconductor recovery and preparation costs for new businesses as the causes.

Cumulative net losses over the last four quarters

Controlling-shareholder net losses over the last four quarters total KRW 1.47 billion. A KRW 106 million loss remained in Q2 2026, and the first profit came in Q3 2026. Whether this profit persists beyond one quarter is not yet established.

Dependence on the cycle and customer concentration

Contract processing made up 54% of 2026 Q2 revenue, more than half. Revenue is directly tied to the semiconductor capital-spending cycle. As in 2025, when the chip recovery was weak, a downturn in downstream demand could quickly affect earnings.

10

Risk factors

Cycle risk

Most revenue is linked to chipmakers' capital spending. If customer investment schedules slip or shrink, stacker and ultra-precision machining volumes could fall together. The sharp drop in 2025 results illustrates this structure.

New-business execution risk

The MLCC blade business and the new businesses at subsidiary NextHigh are still in preparation. Related costs are reflected in consolidated results and can offset core-business profit. Whether the company enters the Samsung Electro-Mechanics supply chain must be confirmed through disclosures and company statements.

Cash generation and financial capacity

Operating cash flow fell from KRW 4.67 billion in 2024 to KRW 2.52 billion in 2025. The debt ratio rose from 71.8% in 2024 to 80.1% in 2025. If cash generation does not follow the return to profit, financial flexibility could narrow.

Overall view

JNB is a maker of equipment and precision parts whose revenue is tied to semiconductor capital spending. In 2025, weak chip recovery and new-business preparation costs cut operating profit to KRW 175 million.

In 2026, revenue has risen for three consecutive quarters from Q1 through Q3, and operating profit turned positive in Q2 and Q3. However, the four quarters through Q3 2026 still show a controlling-shareholder net loss of KRW 1.47 billion, so earnings durability is not yet established.

The share price sits at a discount to book equity, and no earnings-based multiple is calculated. Positive factors are revenue growth from the investment recovery and improving profitability. Negative factors are cycle dependence, new-business costs, lower operating cash flow and a higher debt ratio.

The next checkpoints are the Q4 and full-year 2026 results, the full-year operating margin and controlling-shareholder net profit in the 2026 annual report, and the trend in MLCC and new-business costs.
