02

Business structure

Shinil Pharmaceutical sells medicines in Korea and overseas, built on plaster and patch manufacturing know-how, and runs contract manufacturing for other pharmaceutical companies alongside. Its contract manufacturing work is described as covering products for about 80 companies.

By business segment, pharmaceuticals made up 96% of Q2 2026 revenue, cosmetics 2%, real estate 1%, and quasi-drugs 1%.

A product-level breakdown dated March 2026 puts general and prescription drugs, veterinary drugs and OEM work in an 'other' group at 72%, with Defenplasta at 9%, Harupenplasta at 7%, Lipicanjeong at 3%, and other items at 8%.

The two classifications differ and cannot be compared directly, but pharmaceuticals dominate under either view. New ventures include the Pharmtree functional cosmetics brand, with health functional foods, veterinary drugs and quasi-drugs also under way.

A 2024 first-half report put cosmetics and health functional foods together at 3.1% of revenue. In earlier reporting, the company said it planned to expand prescription drug sales and consider a larger CSO network. The largest shareholder group, led by Hong Jae-hyun with 22 others, holds 32.67%.

04

Earnings analysis

On a disclosed basis, revenue rose from KRW 80.0 billion in 2022 to KRW 89.1 billion in 2023, up 11.4%, and 2024 at KRW 89.4 billion, up 0.3%, was the largest year in 2022-2025. Revenue fell 9.9% to KRW 80.6 billion in 2025. The company attributed the fall in operating profit and net profit to lower revenue.

Operating profit rose from KRW 13.7 billion in 2022 to KRW 15.2 billion in 2024, then fell 51.5% to KRW 7.39 billion in 2025, the smallest year in the period. Operating margin went from 17.1% in 2022 and 17.0% in 2024 to 9.2% in 2025.

Profit attributable to controlling shareholders fell 47.3% to KRW 7.53 billion in 2025, and net profit including non-controlling interests was also KRW 7.53 billion. Operating cash flow dropped from KRW 22.7 billion in 2024 to KRW 11.6 billion in 2025.

Quarterly results moved from an operating profit of KRW 2.67 billion in Q3 2025 to an operating loss of KRW 424 million in Q4 2025, then back to an operating profit of KRW 1.33 billion in Q1 2026. Q2 2026 operating profit was KRW 2.26 billion on revenue of KRW 24.2 billion.

Q3 2026 revenue of KRW 23.8 billion rose 15.5% year on year, while operating profit of KRW 1.39 billion fell 48.0%. Profit attributable to controlling shareholders over the last four quarters totaled KRW 516 million. Revenue is recovering, but the margin pressure is reaching profit first. The cause of the Q4 2025 loss is a point to confirm in the annual report.

05

Industry analysis

Shinil Pharmaceutical operates in a market directly exposed to drug pricing policy. A securities data provider attributed the 2026 profitability decline to drug price cut policies and tighter national health insurance finances.

The same kind of source linked the weak first half of 2025 to government policy and rising competition. The company itself said consumer spending on over-the-counter products weakened with the economic slowdown. Its differentiation rests on specialized plaster and patch manufacturing.

The positioning is best read through its business structure and policy sensitivity. On the bullish side, contract manufacturing and patch technology support the revenue base despite policy risk. On the bearish side, policy costs have hit profit faster than revenue recovery has lifted it. The balance between these two forces is visible in future quarterly operating margins.

06

Outlook

The disclosed materials do not show a company-issued annual revenue or operating profit target. What is confirmed is that quarterly revenue has stayed between KRW 20.9 billion and KRW 24.2 billion since the start of 2026.

A securities data provider credited hospital distribution and stable CDMO orders for the revenue gains. New business lines are still small in revenue terms, and cosmetics and health functional foods would need sales channels and marketing spend to scale.

In earlier reporting the company said it was considering a larger CSO network to support prescription drug sales. The next regular filing is the 2026 annual report, where whether the operating margin recovers from 9.2% in 2025 is the key question. After that, the 2027 Q1 quarterly report will show whether CDMO revenue and the cost structure are changing.

Bear factors

Falling operating profit

Operating profit fell 51.5% to KRW 7.39 billion in 2025, and operating margin dropped to 9.2% from 17.0% in 2024. Q4 2025 turned to an operating loss of KRW 424 million. Q3 2026 operating profit of KRW 1.39 billion fell 48.0% year on year. Profit attributable to controlling shareholders over the last four quarters totaled KRW 516 million.

Policy cost pressure

A securities data provider said rising costs from drug price cuts and tighter health insurance finances weighed on profitability. The weak first half of 2025 was also attributed to government policy and competition. The bear case rests on policy changes reaching costs faster than revenue.

Limited diversification

In a 2024 first-half report, cosmetics and health functional foods together accounted for only 3.1% of revenue. Pharmaceuticals made up 96% of Q2 2026 revenue, so the business remains concentrated. Until new ventures become meaningful earnings sources, diversifying policy risk will be hard.

Drug price policy changes and tighter health insurance finances are cost factors the company cannot easily control. Both the 2025 profit decline and the first-half 2026 operating profit decline are linked to this trend. How policy changes flow into future results is the next point to track.

Earnings volatility

Business concentration and cash flow

Pharmaceuticals account for 96% of revenue, and the largest shareholder group holds 32.67%. How lower cash generation affects new business investment and dividend capacity is a point to track through disclosures.
