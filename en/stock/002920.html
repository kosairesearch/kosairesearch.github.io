02

Business structure

Yoosung Enterprise, founded in 1959, is a specialist maker of automotive engine components.

It produces core internal-combustion parts such as piston rings, cylinder liners, camshafts, valve guides and air compressors, supplying them to domestic automakers and construction and agricultural machinery makers for both assembly and aftermarket use.

Its overseas aftermarket sales reach roughly 40 countries across Southeast Asia, the Middle East, South America and the EU. Its main customers are vehicle makers such as Hyundai Motor and KIA, and a corporate monitoring service describes its supply to Hyundai and KIA and construction-equipment makers.

Affiliates include a joint venture with Japan's TPR, a wholly owned subsidiary, a China-based unit, and an electric-vehicle powertrain company. Because engine components remain the core, output at vehicle makers and demand for engine parts directly shape the revenue trend.

The company emphasizes its technology in piston rings and cylinder liners and cites surface-treatment capabilities such as composite plating and gas nitriding.

At the annual general meeting held on March 26, 2026, the re-election of inside directors Ryu Hyun-seok and Choi Jong-il and the new appointment of outside director Ham Yun-young were all approved.

04

Earnings analysis

On an annual basis, revenue rose from KRW 296.8 billion in 2022 to KRW 324.6 billion in 2024, which was the largest year in the 2022–2025 span. Revenue in 2025 was KRW 304.6 billion, down 6.2% year on year.

Operating profit was KRW 6.73 billion in 2022 and KRW 8.02 billion in 2023 (up 19.2% year on year), before swinging to an operating loss of KRW 474 million in 2024 and widening to an operating loss of KRW 30 billion in 2025. The company has posted operating losses for two consecutive years, 2024 and 2025.

The operating margin fell from 2.3% in 2022 and 2.6% in 2023 to -0.1% in 2024 and -9.9% in 2025.

Net income attributable to controlling shareholders dropped from KRW 19 billion in 2022 to KRW 9.76 billion in 2023 (down 48.7%), turned to a net loss of KRW 11.1 billion in 2024, and widened to a net loss of KRW 28.2 billion in 2025.

Net income including non-controlling interests was KRW 22.1 billion in 2022, KRW 13.8 billion in 2023, a net loss of KRW 6.81 billion in 2024 and a net loss of KRW 23.3 billion in 2025, which follows a different path from the controlling-shareholder figures.

Quarterly, third-quarter 2025 revenue was KRW 74.9 billion with an operating loss of KRW 3.55 billion. In the fourth quarter revenue fell 7.6% quarter on quarter to KRW 69.2 billion and the operating loss widened to KRW 24.4 billion.

The first quarter of 2026 narrowed the operating loss to KRW 3.43 billion, but in the second quarter revenue rose 3.6% to KRW 69.8 billion while the operating loss widened again to KRW 7.53 billion. The third quarter of 2026 recorded revenue of KRW 67.9 billion and an operating loss of KRW 8.13 billion.

The company has posted operating losses for five consecutive quarters from the third quarter of 2025 through the third quarter of 2026. The consolidated financial statements for the third quarter of 2026 were submitted without an external auditor review.

Operating cash flow was KRW 25.3 billion in 2022, KRW 21.3 billion in 2023, KRW 37.2 billion in 2024 and KRW 19.8 billion in 2025, and it stayed positive throughout.

At the end of 2025, total equity was KRW 299.6 billion, of which KRW 257.1 billion belonged to controlling shareholders, and total liabilities were KRW 134.3 billion.

05

Industry analysis

The automotive parts industry moves closely with global vehicle sales, and product competitiveness, business development, productivity and cost competitiveness are cited as the key competitive factors.

Engine components, the core of Yoosung Enterprise's business, link directly to internal-combustion vehicle production, so a decline in vehicle output or a shift in engine configurations feeds straight into revenue.

A corporate monitoring service attributes the first-half 2026 decline in engine-part sales and the wider operating loss to weaker global demand and supply disruptions.

The same data show consolidated first-half 2026 revenue down 14.5% year on year and the operating loss up 432.4% (corporate monitoring service, as of August 31, 2026). How quickly demand for engine parts declines during electrification is the largest variable for the company's direction.

Its electric-vehicle powertrain affiliate can be read as one part of its response to electrification. Engine and powertrain component makers such as Hyundai WIA and SNT Motiv are the natural peer comparison group.

Because the company exports aftermarket parts to roughly 40 countries, aftermarket demand can follow a different pattern from new-vehicle production.

Bull factors

Operating Cash Flow Stayed Positive

Operating cash flow was KRW 25.3 billion in 2022, KRW 21.3 billion in 2023, KRW 37.2 billion in 2024 and KRW 19.8 billion in 2025, positive in all four years. Bulls cite 2025 as evidence of cash generation even in a year with a KRW 30 billion operating loss.

Total equity of KRW 299.6 billion and liabilities of KRW 134.3 billion at the end of 2025 are also cited as capacity to absorb losses.

Electrification Affiliate and Overseas Reach

The affiliate Electrification-focused powertrain company gives the group a route into electric-vehicle components beyond combustion-engine parts. Its aftermarket export network covering roughly 40 countries provides demand that does not depend only on new-vehicle output. Bulls argue these two factors could partly offset the decline in engine-part demand.

Bear factors

The operating margin fell from -0.1% in 2024 to -9.9% in 2025, and the 2025 operating loss was KRW 30 billion. The third-quarter 2026 operating loss of KRW 8.13 billion was wider than KRW 3.55 billion a year earlier. Bears point to the continuation of losses on a quarterly basis as the key evidence.

Shrinking Revenue and Weak Demand

Third-quarter 2026 revenue was KRW 67.9 billion, down 9.4% from a year earlier. A corporate monitoring service attributes the decline in engine-part sales to weaker global demand and supply disruptions. Bears argue that this decline reflects structural demand change rather than a temporary factor.

Rising Leverage and Cumulative Net Losses

The debt-to-equity ratio rose from 30.5% in 2022 and 31.2% in 2023 to 33.4% in 2024 and 44.8% in 2025. Net losses attributable to controlling shareholders were KRW 11.1 billion in 2024 and KRW 28.2 billion in 2025. The controlling-shareholder net loss over the last four quarters totals KRW 41.3 billion. Bears argue that cumulative losses could reduce financial flexibility over time.

Risk factors

Demand Shift from Electrification

Because the core business is internal-combustion engine parts, faster electrification could reduce demand for the main products. How far the electric-vehicle powertrain affiliate offsets that decline is the central variable. Pace of product transition and the burden of capital spending could both weigh on results.

Concentration on Vehicle-Maker Customers

Key customers are concentrated among vehicle makers such as Hyundai Motor and KIA and construction-equipment makers. Production plans or model changes at those customers can move revenue directly. Because pricing pressure from customers flows into margins, it could constrain any recovery in the operating margin.

Possible Revision of Unaudited Quarterly Figures

The fact that the quarterly figures predate an external auditor review is the source of this uncertainty. Figures could still be adjusted during the audit. Until the 2026 annual report is filed after the full-year audit, the finality of the quarterly numbers is limited.
