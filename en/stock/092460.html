Report overview

Ship MRO expansion, order backlog and China sales in focus

Hanla IMS posted KRW 133.5 billion in revenue and KRW 22.3 billion in operating profit in 2025, the largest year in the 2022-2025 span, and is now extending into ship repair (MRO) after acquiring a shipyard in Yeongdo in 2026.

02

Business structure

Hanla IMS makes measurement, control equipment and components for ships. It is classified under the automotive sector, but its actual business is marine equipment, so shipbuilding equipment makers are the more direct comparison group.

By business unit in 2025, the Control system accounted for 45.9% of revenue, the GAUGING system for 27.3%, the Gas and Safety system for 9.8%, and the Eco system for 5.9%, according to a Korean IR research association.

Based on second-quarter 2026 revenue, shipbuilding equipment and industrial plant made up 88% and ship service repair and facility leasing made up 12%, according to a data aggregator.

The valve remote control system (VRCS) and ship automation systems are sold under the integrated HX SOLUTION brand to global shipbuilding and offshore markets.

The ship service unit posted 2025 revenue of KRW 12.9 billion, up 70.1%, with operating profit of KRW 4.8 billion and a 36.9% operating margin, per the same analysis. Exports are a large share of sales, with KRW 72.8 billion of KRW 104.2 billion in 2024 revenue coming from overseas.

Shipyards are the main customers, and the company has cooperative ties with HD Hyundai, Hanwha Ocean and HJ Shipbuilding in naval ship MRO. Sales to China are built on the Zhangjiagang plant, rising from KRW 9.7 billion in 2021 to KRW 67.8 billion in 2025.

04

Earnings analysis

Based on annual figures in the official filings, revenue fell from KRW 98.6 billion in 2022 to KRW 78.9 billion in 2023 (down 20.0%), then rose to KRW 104.2 billion in 2024 (up 32.0%) and KRW 133.5 billion in 2025 (up 28.1%). Of the 2022-2025 years, 2025 was the largest and 2023 the smallest.

Operating profit went from KRW 8.82 billion in 2022 to KRW 5.37 billion in 2023 (down 39.1%), KRW 16 billion in 2024 (up 197.5%) and KRW 22.3 billion in 2025 (up 39.5%). Operating margin moved from 8.9% in 2022 and 6.8% in 2023 to 15.3% in 2024 and 16.7% in 2025, the highest of the four years.

A 2024 report said the company attributed a sharp first-quarter operating profit rise to a reversal of bad-debt provisions on prior-year deliveries, a one-off factor.

Net profit attributable to controlling shareholders jumped from KRW 8.07 billion in 2022 to KRW 71.6 billion in 2023, far above that year's operating profit of KRW 5.37 billion, which suggests non-operating items may have weighed heavily.

It then fell to KRW 13.7 billion in 2024 (down 80.8%) before rising to KRW 25.5 billion in 2025 (up 85.8%). Operating cash flow went from KRW 7.77 billion in 2022 and KRW 3.2 billion in 2023 to negative KRW 187 million in 2024 before recovering to KRW 22.1 billion in 2025.

Quarterly revenue rose from KRW 39.6 billion in the fourth quarter of 2025 (up 25.0% quarter on quarter) to KRW 42.8 billion in the second quarter of 2026 (up 8.8%), and the third quarter of 2026 reached KRW 41.7 billion (up 31.7% year on year).

Operating profit peaked at KRW 9.19 billion in the second quarter of 2026 (up 66.6% quarter on quarter). The third quarter's KRW 6.12 billion was up 12.5% year on year, while net profit attributable to controlling shareholders of KRW 6.04 billion fell 22.8% year on year. Over the last four quarters, that net profit totaled KRW 26.6 billion.

05

Industry analysis

Hanla IMS revenue is tied to the global new-shipbuilding ordering cycle. In an April 2026 report, a Korean IR research association said rising orders for container ships, tankers and gas carriers are lifting utilization at major Korean shipyards.

Shipbuilding equipment is recognized as revenue within 6 to 18 months of an order, so the order backlog gauges near-term revenue visibility. The company was once heavily dependent on a single tanker type, and domestic production of gas carrier measurement systems has helped it win gas carrier orders.

Demand for ship MRO follows inspection cycles set by the International Maritime Organization and classification societies, so it recurs regardless of newbuild orders. This reduces reliance on the newbuild cycle, but the same analysis notes that early-stage costs at the Yeongdo site limit near-term profit contribution.

In naval ship MRO, cooperation with leading Korean shipbuilders and defense firms is seen as a foothold for entry. For valuation comparisons, shipbuilding equipment peers are the more relevant group than listed automotive companies. The same report said the stock trades at a lower multiple than shipbuilding equipment peers.

06

Outlook

According to the company's 2025 annual report, it plans to expand production volume at its China plant by more than 50% in 2026 compared with 2025. A Korean IR research association said the order backlog at the end of 2025 exceeded KRW 200 billion.

In January 2026, the company acquired a Yeongdo shipyard site, land and buildings, for about KRW 107.1 billion from a Busan shipbuilder. That widens its vessel coverage from mid-sized ship repair at Gwangyang to large merchant ships, government vessels and special-purpose ships, according to the same analysis.

The analysis also notes early-stage costs limit near-term profit at Yeongdo, while the Gwangyang precedent suggests a shift to a high-margin structure after stabilization.

The association estimated 2026 consolidated revenue of KRW 168.9 billion, up 26.5%, and operating profit of KRW 28.7 billion, up 28.8%, with China-bound sales of KRW 101 billion, or 59.8% of the total. These are third-party estimates, not company guidance.

Expansion into naval ship MRO is also the direction the company has announced for 2026. The next checkpoints are how closely the fourth-quarter and full-year 2026 results track these estimates and whether Yeongdo follows Gwangyang's utilization curve.

08

Bull factors

Order backlog supports revenue visibility

A Korean IR research association said the order backlog at the end of 2025 exceeded KRW 200 billion. Because shipbuilding equipment is recognized as revenue within 6 to 18 months of an order, the analysis argues the backlog secures much of 2026 growth visibility. Second-quarter 2026 revenue reached KRW 42.8 billion, up 8.8% quarter on quarter.

Growth and high-margin potential of ship MRO

A Korean IR research association said the ship service unit posted 2025 revenue of KRW 12.9 billion, up 70.1%, with a 36.9% operating margin. The January 2026 Yeongdo site acquisition extends coverage to larger vessels. The same analysis cited the Gwangyang site's high margins after stabilization as a precedent for Yeongdo.

Improving quarterly profitability

Operating profit in the second quarter of 2026 rose 66.6% quarter on quarter to KRW 9.19 billion. Think Pool reported that the quarter's revenue and operating profit were the highest in three years. Its operating margin for the quarter was 21%, above the 16.7% full-year margin for 2025.

09

Bear factors

Large year-to-year earnings swings

Net profit attributable to controlling shareholders fell from KRW 71.6 billion in 2023 to KRW 13.7 billion in 2024 (down 80.8%) before recovering to KRW 25.5 billion in 2025. In the third quarter of 2026, that profit was KRW 6.04 billion, down 22.8% year on year, even though operating profit rose 12.5%. Earnings volatility remains a key point to monitor.

Concentration in China-bound sales

China-bound sales expanded from KRW 9.7 billion in 2021 to KRW 67.8 billion in 2025, and a Korean IR research association estimated their share would reach 59.8% in 2026. With production centered on the Zhangjiagang plant, changes in local business conditions and trade rules could have a larger effect. Execution of the plan to expand China production volume by more than 50% will also determine that share.

Early-stage cost burden at the Yeongdo site

A Korean IR research association said the Yeongdo site's early-stage costs limit near-term profit contribution. Operating profit in the third quarter of 2026 fell 33.4% quarter on quarter to KRW 6.12 billion.

Operating cash flow was negative KRW 187 million in 2024, so cash generation after a large acquisition needs to be checked alongside profit.

10

Risk factors

Shipbuilding ordering cycle

Revenue is highly linked to global new-shipbuilding orders. If orders slow, revenue gaps may appear once the backlog is worked off. The 6-to-18-month recognition lag also means order declines take time to show up in results.

China revenue share and external conditions

The rising China share raises exposure to trade conditions in one market. If the plan to expand China production does not proceed as scheduled, the cost and delivery advantages could weaken. With a large overseas sales share, exchange rate moves could also affect profitability.

Returns on Yeongdo investment and MRO utilization

About KRW 107.1 billion went into the Yeongdo site, raising the asset burden. If utilization does not rise as expected, depreciation and early-stage costs could keep weighing on profit.

The debt ratio was 21.7% at the end of 2025, a low level, but changes to the capital structure after the acquisition will need to be checked in the next periodic report.

Overall view

Hanla IMS recorded its largest year in the 2022-2025 span in 2025, with revenue of KRW 133.5 billion, operating profit of KRW 22.3 billion and a 16.7% operating margin. After the 2023 low, revenue and operating profit rose in both 2024 and 2025, and operating cash flow recovered to KRW 22.1 billion in 2025.

Net profit attributable to controlling shareholders has been volatile, moving from KRW 71.6 billion in 2023 to KRW 13.7 billion in 2024 and KRW 25.5 billion in 2025. Quarterly operating profit peaked at KRW 9.19 billion in the second quarter of 2026 before falling to KRW 6.12 billion in the third quarter.

Bullish factors include an order backlog reported above KRW 200 billion, growth in ship service, and expansion in MRO after the Yeongdo acquisition. Bearish factors include a rising share of China-bound sales, early-stage costs at Yeongdo, and earnings volatility.

The next checkpoints are the fourth-quarter and full-year 2026 provisional results and how the Yeongdo site's contribution appears in the 2026 annual report.
