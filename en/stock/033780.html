Overseas cigarettes now drive KT&G's earnings

02

Business structure

KT&G runs two main businesses: tobacco and health functional foods. Tobacco comprises domestic cigarettes, overseas cigarettes and next-generation tobacco (NGP).

The tobacco segment posted Q3 2026 revenue of KRW 1.31 trillion and operating profit of KRW 425.5 billion, up 6.0% and 14.4% year on year, on a preliminary basis announced November 5, 2026. Cigarettes and NGP account for 66.37% of the company's main product revenue mix, according to company overview data.

Domestic cigarette market volume in 2025 was about 56.15 billion sticks, down roughly 5.1% from 2024, while KT&G's share rose from 66.7% to 67.3% and reached 67.9% in the first half of 2026.

Overseas cigarette revenue in 2025 was about KRW 1.88 trillion, or 28.5% of consolidated revenue, by DS Investment & Securities' estimate.

By volume in 2025, overseas cigarettes split 42% Asia-Pacific, 36% Middle East and Africa, and 22% Americas, Europe and CIS, and the Middle East and Africa share of overseas cigarette revenue reached 50.3% in Q3 2026. Production sites are in Korea, Indonesia, Russia, Türkiye and Kazakhstan.

NGP revenue reached KRW 241.0 billion in Q1 2026, up 51.5%. Competition is domestic share-led in cigarettes and vs. next-generation devices, and overseas against Philip Morris International, BAT and JTI. Health functional food operations run through a subsidiary, and profitability recovery was reported in Q3 2026.

04

Earnings analysis

On a disclosed basis, annual revenue rose from KRW 5.85 trillion in 2022 to KRW 5.86 trillion in 2023 (up 0.2%), KRW 5.91 trillion in 2024 (up 0.8%) and KRW 6.58 trillion in 2025 (up 11.4%), with three consecutive years of growth from 2023 through 2025 and 2025 the largest year in the 2022-2025 range.

Operating profit fell to KRW 1.17 trillion in 2023 (down 7.9%), the smallest year, then rose to KRW 1.19 trillion in 2024 (up 1.8%) and KRW 1.34 trillion in 2025 (up 13.0%), two consecutive years of growth.

Operating margin dipped from 21.7% in 2022 to 19.9% in 2023, then recovered to 20.1% in 2024 and 20.4% in 2025, still below 2022. In 2025 operating profit grew faster than revenue, indicating margin improvement.

Profit attributable to controlling shareholders was KRW 1.02 trillion in 2022, KRW 902.7 billion in 2023 (down 11.1%), KRW 1.17 trillion in 2024 (up 29.1%, the largest year) and KRW 1.09 trillion in 2025 (down 6.5%), so it fell in 2025 even as operating profit rose.

Net profit including non-controlling interests was KRW 1.1 trillion in 2025, above the attributable figure, and the gap reflects non-controlling interests. The breakdown of non-operating items should be checked in the annual report notes.

The debt ratio rose from 31.4% in 2022 to 52.0% in 2025, with year-end 2025 total equity of KRW 9.34 trillion and total liabilities of KRW 4.85 trillion. Operating cash flow was KRW 887.9 billion in 2022, KRW 1.27 trillion in 2023, KRW 822.3 billion in 2024 and KRW 582.9 billion in 2025, the lowest of the four years.

Quarterly operating profit was KRW 465.3 billion in Q3 2025, fell to KRW 242.9 billion in Q4 2025 (down 47.8% quarter on quarter), then rose to KRW 364.5 billion in Q1 2026 (up 50.1%), KRW 414.5 billion in Q2 2026 (up 13.7%) and KRW 530.1 billion in Q3 2026 (up 27.9%), three consecutive quarters of growth.

Hana Securities attributed the Q1 2026 gain to favorable currency and a low base from one-off costs such as voluntary retirement in the prior year.

By contrast, Q3 2026 profit attributable to controlling shareholders was KRW 106.1 billion (down 70.9% quarter on quarter), and reports cited non-operating losses tied to currency movements. Attributable profit for the last four quarters totals KRW 1.12 trillion.

05

Industry analysis

Domestic cigarette demand is structurally shrinking because of population decline and a shift to alternatives, and KT&G puts the annual decline at about 4%. Total domestic cigarette demand fell 1.2% in Q2 2026 from a year earlier.

By estimate, total domestic cigarette demand fell 5.4% in Q3 2026 while the NGP market grew about 5%. Tobacco taxes and levies have been frozen since their 2015 increase, which limits external room for price adjustments. As a result, domestic earnings depend on offsetting volume declines with cost reductions.

Overseas markets are seeing both volume and unit price rise together. DS Investment & Securities noted that Philip Morris International, BAT and JTI on average keep payout ratios near 70% with dividend yields around 3-4%.

The company's target is to raise the overseas share of global cigarette production from 30% in 2025 to more than 60% by 2028. Its domestic cigarette market share was 67.9% in the first half of 2026. The sector picture combines domestic contraction with overseas expansion.

06

Outlook

According to DS Investment & Securities, KT&G raised its annual guidance at its Q2 2026 results, lifting revenue growth from 3-5% to 5-7% and operating profit growth from 6-8% to 10-13%. The same report projected 2026 consolidated revenue of KRW 6.9 trillion and operating profit of KRW 1.54 trillion, a 22.1% margin.

On November 5, 2026, the company projected group revenue of KRW 7 trillion and operating profit of KRW 1.5 trillion for 2026. Its 2030 targets are overseas share above 70% of cigarette revenue and above 30% for both NGP and health functional food overseas revenue.

To reach them, it plans price increases on core brands, a richer premium mix and entry into about 30 markets it has not yet reached. The Indonesia plant began full operation in 2026, widening supply from Southeast Asia to the Middle East.

The Kazakhstan plant has design capacity of 6 billion sticks a year but can produce 4 billion sticks a year on current staffing, and DS Investment & Securities expects capacity could expand more than threefold. The Kazakhstan sales unit plans to start sales in November 2026.

Shareholder returns from 2027 include buybacks and cancellation of KRW 1.2 trillion over four years. Hana Securities cited the NGP base effect, short-term swings in cigarette exports and a property-related base effect as second-half burdens. The next check is the overseas cigarette revenue share and operating margin in the 2026 annual report.

08

Bull factors

Sustained overseas cigarette growth

Overseas cigarettes posted growth in volume, revenue and profit for 10 consecutive quarters, and Q3 2026 revenue rose 10.5% to KRW 579.0 billion. First-half 2026 overseas revenue reached KRW 1.12 trillion, up 21.8%, lifting its share of tobacco revenue to 47%. With a 2030 target of more than 70% overseas, supporters see a clear growth path.

Expanded shareholder returns

On November 5, 2026, the company announced buybacks and cancellation of KRW 1.2 trillion over four years from 2027 and total shareholder returns above KRW 5 trillion. Treasury shares held in April 2026 were fully cancelled. Annual dividend per share was also raised, and brokers see room relative to global tobacco peers.

Local production expected to lower costs

The company targets raising the overseas share of cigarette production from 30% in 2025 to above 60% by 2028. DS Investment & Securities expects logistics savings and higher local sourcing to lower unit manufacturing costs. The Indonesia plant began full operation in 2026, broadening supply.

09

Bear factors

Structural decline in domestic cigarettes

KT&G says domestic cigarette demand is falling about 4% a year. Domestic market volume fell about 5.1% in 2025 from 2024, and total demand declined 1.2% in Q2 2026. Market share reached 67.9%, but gains may not offset a shrinking market, a view some analysts hold.

Earnings quality and non-operating volatility

Profit attributable to controlling shareholders fell 6.5% in 2025 even as operating profit rose. In Q3 2026 it fell 74.7% year on year, with non-operating losses tied to currency movements reported as the cause. Operating cash flow of KRW 582.9 billion in 2025 was the lowest of the four years.

Limited pricing room and regional concentration

Tobacco taxes and levies have been frozen since their 2015 increase, limiting external room for domestic price adjustments. Middle East and Africa rose to 50.3% of overseas cigarette revenue in Q3 2026. Concentration in one region raises exposure to local demand and currency moves.

10

Risk factors

Currency and non-operating items

Q3 2026 operating profit was a record, yet attributable profit fell sharply, with currency-linked non-operating losses reported to offset operating gains. As overseas revenue grows, foreign exchange risk management increasingly determines earnings stability.

Tax and pricing constraints

Tobacco taxes and levies have remained frozen since 2015. Domestic price increases are constrained by the tax structure, which makes it hard to offset volume losses through price. Any change in tax rules would directly affect domestic cigarette economics.

Overseas concentration and local factors

Overseas cigarette volume is split across Asia-Pacific, the Middle East and Africa, and the Americas, Europe and CIS, with the Middle East and Africa share rising. Plant output at sites such as Kazakhstan depends on utilization and staffing. Growth pace can shift with local market conditions and subsidiary operations.

Overall view

Disclosed results for 2022-2025 show revenue rising three consecutive years to KRW 6.58 trillion in 2025, and operating profit rising two consecutive years after the 2023 low to KRW 1.34 trillion. Q3 2026 operating profit of KRW 530.1 billion was announced as a single-quarter record on a preliminary basis.

Growth has shifted from domestic to overseas cigarettes, with first-half 2026 overseas revenue up 21.8%. Domestic cigarette demand is falling about 4% a year, and profit attributable to controlling shareholders declined in 2025 and in Q3 2026, so earnings quality remains a question.

The debt ratio rose from 31.4% in 2022 to 52.0% in 2025, and operating cash flow was lowest in 2025. Shareholder returns from 2027 include KRW 1.2 trillion of buybacks and cancellations within a package above KRW 5 trillion.

Broker target prices published since mid-2026 differ across brokers, and these are broker views. The next checks are the Q4 2026 preliminary results, non-operating items in the 2026 annual report and the overseas share of cigarette revenue.
