Samji Electronics: Revenue Growth Meets Volatile Quarterly Profit

02

Business structure

Samji Electronics runs a telecom equipment business that makes wireless relay devices, an electronic component distribution business, a construction business and real estate leasing.

In the first-quarter 2026 report, the consolidated revenue mix was 94.26% for component distribution (through Samt), 4.92% for construction, 0.80% for telecom equipment and 0.02% for other businesses. In the first half of 2023 the split was 83.9%, 14.8%, 1.3% and 0.1%, so revenue has tilted further toward distribution.

Some press coverage put the distribution share at 84–87%, so the period and the method of calculation matter when comparing figures.

The distribution unit supplies memory chips, system chips and multilayer ceramic capacitors (MLCCs) to smartphone, TV and automotive component makers, and it holds supply contracts with Samsung Electronics, Samsung Electro-Mechanics and Samsung SDI.

The construction unit mainly handles installation and maintenance work for semiconductor facilities, while the telecom unit's main product is wireless relays.

Its R&D spending was about 0.05% of sales in the first half of 2023, so the business rests more on sourcing and distribution capability than on product technology. Profit swings are likely to track the volume of distribution deals and demand from Samsung affiliates.

04

Earnings analysis

On a disclosed annual basis, revenue fell from KRW 2.92 trillion in 2022 to KRW 2.53 trillion in 2023, a 13.5% decline, before rising to KRW 3.36 trillion in 2024, up 33.0%, and KRW 4.29 trillion in 2025, up 27.7%.

Revenue has grown for two consecutive years from 2024 through 2025, and 2025 was the largest year of the 2022–2025 period. Operating profit went from KRW 88.5 billion in 2022 to KRW 78.3 billion in 2023, down 11.5%, then to KRW 115.6 billion in 2024, up 47.6%, and KRW 162.4 billion in 2025, up 40.5%.

The operating margin edged up from 3.0% in 2022 to 3.1%, 3.4% and 3.8% in 2025, so profit has grown more slowly than revenue, which reflects the thin margins of a distribution-led business.

Net profit attributable to controlling shareholders was KRW 48.1 billion in 2022, KRW 31.6 billion in 2023 (down 34.2%), KRW 61.5 billion in 2024 (up 94.4%) and KRW 75.7 billion in 2025 (up 23.0%).

Total net income, which includes non-controlling interests, was KRW 118.3 billion in 2025 and should be read separately from the controlling-shareholder figure. Quarterly results in 2026 were volatile.

First-quarter revenue reached KRW 1.9 trillion, up 60.9% from the prior quarter, and operating profit rose to KRW 334.2 billion, up 420.7%, marking the quarterly peak. Second-quarter revenue reached KRW 2.69 trillion, up 41.3%, but operating profit fell 52.6% to KRW 158.4 billion.

Third-quarter revenue reached KRW 2.88 trillion, up 148.3% year on year, while operating profit fell 42.9% to KRW 90.5 billion. Whether the first-quarter spike was one-off in nature should be checked against the item-level notes in the quarterly report.

Net profit attributable to controlling shareholders for the latest four quarters totals KRW 262 billion. Operating cash flow went from KRW 29.5 billion in 2022 and KRW 184.5 billion in 2023 to -KRW 110 billion in 2024 and -KRW 140.8 billion in 2025.

Over the same period the debt ratio rose from 87.7% in 2024 to 145.2% in 2025, and total liabilities of KRW 1.08 trillion at year-end 2025 exceeded total equity of KRW 744.7 billion.

05

Industry analysis

Samji Electronics' results are tied to component and memory demand in the Samsung-affiliated electronics industry. Because distribution accounts for most revenue, the volume of memory and MLCC transactions feeds directly into the top line.

Samsung Electronics reported operating profit of KRW 107.4 trillion for the third quarter of 2026 in its preliminary results, saying it was the first time a quarter had passed KRW 100 trillion. In the same release, Samsung Electronics said memory supply constraints could last until 2028.

That can be read as a supportive signal for Samji's distribution environment, but how much distribution revenue rose, and in which product lines, has to be checked through the segment trend.

Samsung Electronics also said currency movements weighed heavily on its third-quarter results, so the won exchange rate is another variable for distribution earnings.

Within the electronics and components sector, Samsung Electro-Mechanics is named as one of the counterparties in Samji's supply contracts, placing Samji within the Samsung-affiliated component value chain.

Unlike manufacturing-focused component makers, Samji depends on distribution margins, which is why its operating margin of 3.8% in 2025 sits at the low end.

The telecom equipment unit accounts for only 0.80% of revenue in the first-quarter 2026 report, so industry swings in that business have a limited effect on total results.

Outlook

The starting point for the outlook is the quarterly trend through the third quarter of 2026. Revenue rose from KRW 1.9 trillion in the first quarter to KRW 2.69 trillion in the second and KRW 2.88 trillion in the third, while operating profit fell from KRW 334.2 billion to KRW 158.4 billion and KRW 90.5 billion.

Revenue is growing while profit shrinks, so the key variable for the coming quarters is whether the operating margin can climb back above the 2025 annual level of 3.8% while revenue holds its scale.

The 2026 annual report is expected in mid-March 2027 if the prior year's timing holds, and it will confirm the full-year operating margin and cash flow. The first-quarter 2027 report is expected around mid-May 2027, following the same pattern as the first-quarter 2026 report filed on May 15, 2026.

The biggest driver of the industry is Samsung-affiliated memory demand, and Samsung Electronics said its 2027 HBM supply volume has already been sold. How closely distribution revenue tracks that demand will show up in the segment trend.

The telecom equipment unit is too small to move the outlook, so the weight of the outlook rests on distribution. The checkpoints that will shape the outlook are whether quarterly profit holds at current levels in the next periodic reports and whether operating cash flow turns positive.

Bull factors

Continued revenue growth

The operating margin also edged up from 3.0% in 2022 to 3.8% in 2025. If Samsung-affiliated memory demand continues, distribution transaction volumes have room to be supported.

Scale of the profit recovery

First-quarter 2026 operating profit of KRW 334.2 billion was more than double the 2025 annual operating profit of KRW 162.4 billion. Net profit attributable to controlling shareholders for the latest four quarters totals KRW 262 billion, more than three times the KRW 75.7 billion recorded for 2025. The sharp increase in quarterly earnings power is the core argument on the bullish side.

Share price low relative to net assets

The price-to-book ratio sits below 1x, so the shares trade at a low price relative to net asset value. Total equity reached KRW 744.7 billion at the end of 2025, which can be read as growth in the asset base. The valuation position is cited on the bullish side as a possible cushion on the downside.

Bear factors

Reversal in quarterly profit

Operating profit fell 52.6% to KRW 158.4 billion in the second quarter of 2026 and then 42.9% to KRW 90.5 billion in the third. Profit has declined for two consecutive quarters after the first-quarter peak, so the earnings trend points down. The fact that profit is falling even as revenue rises is the starting point of the bearish case.

Cash flow and financial burden

Operating cash flow was -KRW 110 billion in 2024 and -KRW 140.8 billion in 2025. The debt ratio rose to 145.2% at the end of 2025 from 87.7% in 2024, and total liabilities stood at KRW 1.08 trillion. Profit has grown while cash has not accumulated, which is a key bearish point.

Thin operating margin structure

The operating margin was only 3.8% in 2025, thin relative to the company's revenue scale. R&D spending in the telecom unit was about 0.05% of sales in the first half of 2023, so room for margin gains through technology differentiation looks limited. Unless the distribution-centered structure changes, the operating margin may rise only modestly.

Risk factors

Customer concentration

Most revenue comes from the component distribution unit, which relies on supply contracts with Samsung-affiliated companies including Samsung Electronics, Samsung Electro-Mechanics and Samsung SDI. Changes in demand or procurement policy within the Samsung group would directly affect the revenue base. Dependence on one customer group is both a source of stability and a concentration risk.

Earnings volatility

Quarterly operating profit swung sharply in 2026, at KRW 334.2 billion, KRW 158.4 billion and KRW 90.5 billion across the first three quarters. Whether the first-quarter spike was one-off should be determined from the item-level notes in the quarterly report. Such swings also reduce the predictability of annual results.

Balance sheet and cash flow

At the end of 2025 total liabilities of KRW 1.08 trillion exceeded total equity of KRW 744.7 billion. Operating cash flow was negative in both 2024 and 2025. If the business absorbs working capital, profit may not turn into cash quickly, so the room for financial flexibility needs continued monitoring.

Overall view

Samji Electronics' revenue rose for two consecutive years in 2024 and 2025, and quarterly revenue has continued to grow for four consecutive quarters into 2026.

Operating profit recovered in 2024 and 2025 after the 2023 low, but it has fallen from the first-quarter 2026 peak of KRW 334.2 billion to KRW 158.4 billion in the second quarter and KRW 90.5 billion in the third.

The bullish factors are revenue growth, the sharp first-quarter profit spike and a price-to-book ratio below 1x. The bearish factors are negative operating cash flow in 2024 and 2025, a debt ratio of 145.2% at the end of 2025 and a thin operating margin of 3.8% in 2025 from a distribution-led model.

Most revenue comes from Samsung-affiliated distribution, which is both a source of transaction flow and a concentration risk. The next checkpoints are the operating margin and cash flow in the 2026 annual report, and whether quarterly profit keeps its direction after the peak in the first-quarter 2027 report.
