02

Business structure

Hanwha Ocean is a shipbuilding and defense company that changed its name after Daewoo Shipbuilding & Marine Engineering joined the Hanwha Group in 2023. Its business is split into three divisions: commercial vessels, naval vessels, and energy plants.

The commercial vessel division builds LNG carriers, very large crude carriers (VLCCs), and container ships, and accounted for most of consolidated revenue in the third quarter of 2026.

According to preliminary divisional figures reported by the press, commercial vessel revenue was KRW 2.72 trillion and operating profit was KRW 471.8 billion, with an operating margin of 17.4% versus 12.5% a year earlier.

In the same reporting, the naval vessel division, which builds submarines and surface warships, posted revenue of KRW 363 billion and an operating loss of KRW 70.6 billion. Some articles reported the naval loss as KRW 7.6 billion, so the figures differ across outlets.

The energy plant division, which covers floating production units such as FPSOs, reported revenue of KRW 527.5 billion and operating profit of KRW 91.4 billion.

Cumulative orders at the end of September 2026 totaled USD 7.72 billion and included eight LNG carriers, 18 VLCCs, six container ships, three very large ammonia carriers, three very large LPG carriers, and one offshore wind installation vessel.

Within commercial vessels, a shift in mix toward container ships and VLCCs and away from high-margin LNG carriers has been cited as a factor in margins.

The company competes with HD Hyundai Heavy Industries and Samsung Heavy Industries for high-value commercial orders, while the naval division faces rising fixed costs from hiring and capacity expansion.

04

Earnings analysis

On a DART-reported basis, revenue grew from KRW 4.86 trillion in 2022 to KRW 7.41 trillion in 2023 (up 52.4% year on year), KRW 10.8 trillion in 2024 (up 45.5%), and KRW 12.8 trillion in 2025 (up 18.6%), rising for three consecutive years from 2023 through 2025.

Of the 2022 to 2025 period, 2025 was the largest year for revenue. Operating results moved from an operating loss of KRW 1.61 trillion in 2022 to an operating loss of KRW 196.5 billion in 2023, which means the loss continued but narrowed.

The company then swung to operating profit of KRW 237.9 billion in 2024 and reached KRW 1.17 trillion in 2025, up 390.8% year on year. The operating margin improved from -33.2% in 2022 to -2.7% in 2023, 2.2% in 2024, and 9.1% in 2025.

Net income attributable to controlling shareholders moved from a net loss of KRW 1.74 trillion in 2022 to net profit of KRW 159.9 billion in 2023, a turn to profit, and then rose to KRW 528.1 billion in 2024 (up 230.3%) and KRW 1.25 trillion in 2025 (up 135.9%), increasing for two consecutive years.

Net income including non-controlling interests, a separate measure, was KRW 1.25 trillion in 2025. Operating cash flow shifted from an outflow of KRW 2.9 trillion in 2024 to an inflow of KRW 1.31 trillion in 2025. The debt ratio fell from 1,542.4% in 2022 to 226.2% in 2025.

At the end of 2025, total equity was KRW 6.18 trillion, equity attributable to controlling shareholders was KRW 6.17 trillion, and total liabilities were KRW 14 trillion. Quarterly results were more volatile.

Revenue was KRW 3.02 trillion in the third quarter of 2025, KRW 3.32 trillion in the fourth quarter, and KRW 3.21 trillion in the first quarter of 2026, then jumped to KRW 5.44 trillion in the second quarter before reaching KRW 3.41 trillion in the third quarter.

Operating profit was KRW 247.5 billion in the fourth quarter of 2025, rose to KRW 441.1 billion in the first quarter of 2026 and KRW 736.1 billion in the second quarter, then fell 34.0% from the prior quarter to KRW 485.7 billion in the third quarter, still up 67.6% year on year.

Net income attributable to controlling shareholders was KRW 145.1 billion in the third quarter, down 79.0% from the prior quarter, and the sum over the last four quarters was KRW 1.95 trillion.

The company stated that one-off costs such as the settlement of labor negotiations and annual performance bonuses could lower profitability in the fourth quarter.

05

Industry analysis

The most important variable in the shipbuilding cycle is when high-priced vessels ordered around 2024 are recognized as revenue. An April 2026 report from NH Investment & Securities noted that vessels ordered in 2024 and 2025 account for 70% of revenue and that commercial vessel profitability exceeded 15%.

The same report credited productivity gains from repeat builds and early deliveries. This reflects a phase in which high-priced contracts are converting into profit. The price level of future orders, however, must be confirmed through disclosures.

Within commercial vessels, the share of LNG carriers has declined while container ships and VLCCs have grown, making mix management a key task. The naval vessel division's submarine and surface warship work advanced and lifted revenue, but rising fixed costs widened its loss.

Hanwha Ocean has set up a dedicated team for nuclear-powered submarine projects and is strengthening its local base at the Philly Shipyard in the United States. In competition, it contends with large peers such as HD Hyundai Heavy Industries and Samsung Heavy Industries for high-value commercial and offshore orders.

The company has expanded currency hedging since June 2026 to reduce the effect of exchange rates on profit and loss. Labor negotiations and workforce supply remain shared challenges across Korean shipbuilders.

06

Outlook

The company has said that one-off costs such as labor settlement payments and annual performance bonuses could lower profitability in the fourth quarter. It has expanded currency hedging and expects exchange-rate effects on profit and loss to shrink gradually.

The commercial vessel division plans to keep profitability stable by raising the share of high-margin projects while VLCC and container ship construction grows.

The naval vessel division is working to accelerate construction schedules for the Jang Bogo-III Batch-II No. 2 and Ulsan-class Batch-III Nos. 5 and 6, and expects revenue to rise year on year as a result.

The energy plant division expects revenue to increase gradually as delayed projects return to normal progress and new projects enter production.

A brokerage analysis published in the first half of 2026 said that if two or more offshore projects are won within the year, a turnaround in the offshore segment could be expected from the fourth quarter of 2026.

In April 2026, NH Investment & Securities said that outcomes for large orders within the year, including the Canadian submarine program, the Venus FPSO, and KDDX, would matter most. The company plans to secure work centered on vessels that can deliver profitability rather than expanding order volume.

The order backlog on a delivery basis at the end of the third quarter of 2026 was reported at USD 26.83 billion.

08

Bull factors

Improved profitability in high-priced commercial ships

The operating margin rose to 9.1% in 2025 from -33.2% in 2022. Press-reported preliminary figures show the commercial vessel margin at 17.4% in the third quarter of 2026, above 12.5% a year earlier. Analysts attribute the gain largely to revenue recognition from vessels ordered in 2024 and 2025.

Revenue visibility from the order backlog

Cumulative orders at the end of September 2026 were USD 7.72 billion, centered on LNG carriers and VLCCs. The delivery-basis order backlog provides the basis for revenue recognition over several years.

Pipeline of naval and offshore orders

The company created a dedicated team for nuclear-powered submarine projects and is building its US position around the Philly Shipyard. Some analysts see a possible offshore turnaround if orders continue within the year.

09

Bear factors

Quarter-on-quarter slowdown in the third quarter

Third-quarter revenue fell 37.3% and operating profit fell 34.0% from the prior quarter. Press reports said operating profit missed the market estimate of KRW 541.5 billion compiled by FnGuide by 10.3%. The drop reflected a combination of the second-quarter base effect, fewer working days, and currency effects.

Naval losses and fixed-cost pressure

Press reports said the naval vessel division returned to an operating loss of KRW 70.6 billion in the third quarter. Analysts have said fixed-cost pressure from hiring and capacity expansion could delay near-term margin recovery. Commentary also notes that insufficient construction volume would add to fixed-cost burdens.

Financial burden and cash decline

Total liabilities were KRW 14 trillion at the end of 2025, with a debt ratio of 226.2%. Borrowings at the end of the third quarter were reported at KRW 5.74 trillion, down from the prior quarter, while cash fell by roughly KRW 800 billion over the same period. Large investment and debt repayment at the same time could reduce liquidity headroom.

10

Risk factors

Currency and one-off fourth-quarter costs

The company said that the recognition of one-off labor settlement payments and annual bonuses could lower fourth-quarter profitability. The exchange-rate decline was cited as a burden on third-quarter margins. Expanded hedging reduces but does not eliminate the effect of currency moves on profit and loss.

Order gaps and vessel mix

The company said it will focus on vessels that secure profitability rather than expanding order volume. Reported order totals differ by outlet for the same period, which makes the trend hard to pin down. A smaller share of high-margin LNG carriers could weigh on commercial vessel margins.

Naval execution and financial leverage

If progress and cost control in the naval division lag, losses could persist. The debt ratio was 226.2% at the end of 2025, meaning liabilities are large relative to equity. Borrowing burdens and cash declines together make the balance between investment and repayment more important.

12

Overall view

Hanwha Ocean's results shifted from operating losses in 2023 to KRW 1.17 trillion in operating profit in 2025, a structural improvement. In the third quarter of 2026, high-priced commercial vessel profits still supported results, with the commercial margin at 17.4% according to press-reported preliminary figures.

In the same quarter, the naval vessel division swung to an operating loss of KRW 70.6 billion, and revenue and operating profit both fell from the prior quarter partly because of the second-quarter base effect. One-time labor settlement payments and bonuses are flagged as fourth-quarter variables.

The order backlog of USD 26.83 billion improves revenue visibility, but a cash decline of roughly KRW 800 billion in the third quarter and a debt ratio of 226.2% constrain financial flexibility.

The next checkpoints are the outcomes of large orders within the year, the preliminary fourth-quarter 2026 results, and the 2026 annual report. Both strengths and weaknesses are present, so tracking the trend against disclosed figures is the more reliable approach.
