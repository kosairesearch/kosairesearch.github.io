After the Turnaround: Boeing Volumes and Defense Entry in Focus

02

Business structure

AeroSpace Technology of Korea manufactures aircraft fuselage structures and components, with commercial aircraft production at its core. It mainly supplies rear fuselage sections for the Boeing B737 MAX family and parts for Embraer models.

In 2024 it merged with a subsidiary, securing aircraft component production capacity. From June 2025 it began delivering new products for IAI's B777 passenger-to-freighter (P2F) conversion program.

Its defense business started with C-390 military transport parts won together with Embraer, and in a May 2026 report the company said it had completed initial deliveries for that program.

In the same period, reports said it signed a C-390 component supply contract with Czech aircraft maker Aero Vodochody and a B737 parts supply contract with a Malaysian state-owned aircraft maker.

Because customers are concentrated among whole-aircraft makers such as Boeing and Embraer, production rates and certification timing feed directly into sales.

In defense, the company tends to expand at the component supply stage rather than compete head-on with large system makers such as Hanwha Aerospace or Korea Aerospace Industries. It entered a workout in July 2023, and after UAMCO took part in management, its financial structure improved. A November 2026 report said that operating stability after the end of the workout is now the company's task.

04

Earnings analysis

On a disclosed basis, annual sales rose from KRW 162.1 billion in 2022 to KRW 168.5 billion in 2023 (up 3.9% year on year) and KRW 171.4 billion in 2024 (up 1.7%), then jumped to KRW 254.4 billion in 2025 (up 48.4%).

Sales have risen for three consecutive years from 2023 to 2025, and 2025 was the largest year in the 2022 to 2025 period. The company attributed the 2025 sales increase to orders received, and said higher selling prices and cost reductions contributed to the swing to operating profit.

Operating results went from an operating loss of KRW 9.97 billion in 2022 and an operating loss of KRW 36.4 billion in 2023 (loss widened) to an operating loss of KRW 6.4 billion in 2024 (loss narrowed) and operating profit of KRW 7.36 billion in 2025.

The operating margin moved from -6.1% and -21.6% to -3.7% and then 2.9% in 2025. Net income attributable to controlling shareholders was a loss in each year from 2022 to 2025, and the 2025 loss of KRW 8.84 billion was smaller than the KRW 17 billion loss in 2024.

Operating cash flow shifted from outflows of -KRW 146.2 billion in 2023 and -KRW 66.5 billion in 2024 to an inflow of KRW 18.8 billion in 2025, and the debt ratio fell from 2,222.6% in 2022 to 82.7% in 2025.

Quarterly, sales fell to KRW 65 billion in Q4 2025 (down 5.0% from the prior quarter) with an operating loss of KRW 806 million, but rebounded to KRW 71.4 billion in Q1 2026 (up 9.7%) with operating profit of KRW 7.05 billion.

Sales then fell to KRW 57 billion in Q2 2026 (down 20.1%) and KRW 48.1 billion in Q3 2026 (down 15.6% from the prior quarter and down 29.7% from a year earlier), with an operating loss of KRW 4.01 billion.

The company said shipments and revenue recognition for some products were delayed in Q3 by a drop in the exchange rate and direct quality verification by Boeing. The combined net loss attributable to controlling shareholders over the last four quarters is KRW 12.5 billion.

05

Industry analysis

On the demand side, Boeing and Airbus are expanding production, which is lifting demand for commercial aircraft components. A May 2026 report said a continuing shortage of production capacity in the global aerospace parts market could move some volumes previously handled by existing suppliers to the company.

The Boeing 737 MAX10 faced production disruption from delayed approval by the US Federal Aviation Administration, but its approval has become more likely, and the model has an order backlog of more than 4,800 aircraft. The company said it plans to handle a large share of rear fuselage production for this model.

After Boeing acquired Spirit AeroSystems and restructured the supply chain, the company said some bulkhead and deck items moved to effectively single-source supply, having previously been split between the two companies.

In defense, DB Financial Investment analyst Seo Jae-ho projected that 2026 defense export orders would reach about 3.7 times the 2025 level, a report from early 2026.

That outlook centers on Hanwha Aerospace, Hyundai-Rotem and Korea Aerospace Industries, while the company's C-390 volumes are limited, with around three aircraft in domestic deployment.

Commercial aircraft component cycles depend heavily on how quickly Boeing normalizes production, so Boeing's output rate and certification schedules are the key variables for the industry view.

06

Outlook

A company official said the firm intends to improve operating stability in its existing business after the end of the workout, respond to rising deliveries of B737 MAX-family parts, and win additional items. The company also plans to pursue entry into defense and space.

Centered on a new plant under construction at Sacheon by an affiliate, it is exploring technical cooperation with companies in aluminum materials and aerospace parts. The company expects its supplier status could be upgraded to Tier 1 as Boeing restructures its supply chain.

However, the size and timing of additional orders have not been fixed, and talks with Boeing are continuing. The company's view is that shipments of volumes delayed in Q3 by quality verification can resume, and sales can recover, once that verification is completed.

On the military side, the company completed initial deliveries of the C-390 and finished related R&D work. Reports said construction of a new plant began in early April 2026 to enter space-related industries.

Whether the Boeing 737 MAX10 receives approval within 2026 will determine when rear fuselage volumes can expand. The most direct next checkpoints are the Q4 2026 results and whether the delayed Q3 shipments resume.

08

Bull factors

Expected benefit from Boeing's supply chain restructuring

As Boeing restructures its supply chain after acquiring Spirit AeroSystems, the company expects a possible upgrade to Tier 1 status. A company official said some bulkhead and deck items have moved to effectively single-source supply, which has raised its pricing leverage.

Reports also said volumes previously handled by existing suppliers could move to the company as production capacity remains tight.

Q1 2026 profit and cumulative profitability maintained

Q1 2026 sales reached KRW 71.4 billion, the peak quarter in the disclosed series, with operating profit of KRW 7.05 billion. A May 2026 report described the quarter as a record result. The company said cumulative operating profit stayed in the black through Q3 even though some Q3 shipments were delayed.

Defense supply references accumulating

The company completed initial C-390 military transport deliveries and finished related R&D work. Reports cite a C-390 component contract with Aero Vodochody in the Czech Republic, alongside a B737 parts contract with a Malaysian customer.

The company views accumulating defense supply records as a starting point for diversifying beyond commercial aircraft.

Bear factors

Quarterly sales down for two straight quarters

Q2 2026 sales of KRW 57 billion fell 20.1% from the prior quarter, and Q3 sales of KRW 48.1 billion fell a further 15.6%. The company swung to an operating loss of KRW 4.01 billion in Q3. If shipment delays persist, the timing of a sales recovery in Q4 could also slip.

Long-running net losses and limited earnings scale

Net income attributable to controlling shareholders was negative every year from 2022 through 2025, and the combined net loss over the last four quarters is KRW 12.5 billion. The 2025 swing to operating profit of KRW 7.36 billion remains small in scale. The 2025 net loss attributable to controlling shareholders of KRW 8.84 billion also remains in the red.

Uncertainty over additional orders and defense revenue

The size and timing of additional orders have not been fixed, and discussions with Boeing are ongoing. The defense business has limited revenue scale, with domestic C-390 deployment around three aircraft. Whether defense operations translate into meaningful revenue will depend on the results of future orders.

Risk factors

Customer concentration and aircraft certification risk

Sales are concentrated among whole-aircraft makers such as Boeing and Embraer, so certification delays or production adjustments for a single model feed directly into revenue. If approval of the B737 MAX10 is delayed, the timing of expanded rear fuselage volumes will also slip. Shipments were already delayed in Q3 by Boeing's quality verification.

Currency and cost pressure

The company said a drop in the exchange rate weighed on Q3 results. Reports also noted that higher raw material prices pushed down the gross margin. If the cost structure does not change, profitability gains could remain limited even if sales recover.

Balance sheet and cash flow volatility

Operating cash flow recorded large outflows of -KRW 146.2 billion in 2023 and -KRW 66.5 billion in 2024 before turning to an inflow of KRW 18.8 billion in 2025. Even after the workout ends, the stability of cash generation needs to be confirmed if quarterly sales keep falling.

Overall view

AeroSpace Technology of Korea achieved both an annual operating profit swing in 2025 and a lower debt ratio, but sales have declined since Q2 2026 and Q3 turned to an operating loss.

In commercial aircraft, Boeing's production normalization, expanding B737 MAX-family volumes and a possible Tier 1 upgrade are cited as positives. On the other hand, the size and timing of additional orders are not fixed, and Q3 sales were affected by the exchange rate and delayed quality verification.

The defense business is at the stage of building references through initial C-390 deliveries and contracts in the Czech Republic and Malaysia, and its revenue scale remains limited.

On the financial side, operating cash flow turned positive in 2025, but large outflows in 2023 and 2024 and a combined net loss of KRW 12.5 billion over the last four quarters coexist with it. The key issue is whether operating stability after the workout and additional orders translate into results.

The next checkpoints are whether the Boeing 737 MAX10 is approved by year-end 2026, the Q4 2026 preliminary results and recovery of delayed shipments, and the confirmed financial figures in the 2026 annual business report.
