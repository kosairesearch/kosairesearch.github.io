02

Business structure

E-Starco was founded in 1980 and listed on the KOSPI market in 1988. It operates four business segments: the construction and sale of residential and commercial properties, operation of the Walnut foreign-language academy, leasing of owned real estate, and operation of the Seosan Benikia Hotel.

According to an AlphaSquare tally, property sales account for 42.05% of revenue, leasing of officetels and commercial space for 33.14%, education for 23.84%, and leisure, mainly restaurants, for 0.97%. Property sales are the largest revenue item, but quarterly revenue moves sharply with the timing of sales contracts.

The leasing segment seeks to secure creditworthy tenants when unsold units arise, which the company says helps stabilize its rental base. The education segment's revenue declined on fewer students and tougher competition. The Seosan Benikia Hotel runs ancillary facilities but posts an operating loss.

Customers differ by segment: buyers, tenants, and academy students, each with a different profit structure. In its sector of 27 listed real estate companies, E-Starco ranked 26th by market capitalization and 18th by revenue on an annual 2025 basis.

Unlike leasing-focused REITs, its higher share of sales revenue makes earnings more volatile. The largest shareholder group holds 23.87% and treasury shares total 6.91%, as of October 1, 2026.

04

Earnings analysis

Revenue on a DART-filed basis rose from KRW 7.63 billion in 2022 to KRW 13.3 billion in 2023 (up 74.0%), fell to KRW 11.5 billion in 2024 (down 13.4%), and reached KRW 16.4 billion in 2025 (up 43.0%), the largest year in the 2022 to 2025 window.

Operating losses were KRW 3.17 billion in 2022, KRW 1.66 billion in 2023, KRW 3.29 billion in 2024, and KRW 407 million in 2025, a loss in each of the four years. The operating margin moved from -41.6% in 2022 to -12.5% in 2023, widened to -28.6% in 2024, and narrowed to -2.5% in 2025.

In 2024 the operating loss widened as revenue fell, while in 2025 it narrowed as revenue rose 43.0%. The company attributed the smaller 2025 loss to revenue recognized from its sales business.

Net losses attributable to controlling shareholders were KRW 5.02 billion, KRW 2.92 billion, KRW 4.95 billion, and KRW 1.38 billion for 2022 to 2025, and net loss including non-controlling interests was reported at the same amounts.

Operating cash flow was -KRW 1.12 billion in 2022, KRW 3.29 billion in 2023, -KRW 6.96 billion in 2024, and KRW 578 million in 2025, changing direction each year.

The debt ratio rose from 76.1% in 2022 to 136.8% in 2025, and at year-end 2025 total equity was KRW 37.5 billion against total liabilities of KRW 51.4 billion.

On a quarterly basis, revenue was KRW 3.53 billion in the third quarter of 2025 and KRW 3.93 billion in the fourth quarter (up 11.3% quarter on quarter), fell to KRW 3.4 billion in the first quarter of 2026 (down 13.6%), then rose to KRW 3.54 billion in the second quarter and KRW 3.58 billion in the third, two consecutive quarters of growth.

The operating loss widened from KRW 733 million in the third quarter of 2025 to KRW 873 million in the fourth, narrowed to KRW 302 million in the first quarter of 2026 and KRW 147 million in the second, then widened again to KRW 273 million in the third.

The net loss attributable to controlling shareholders over the last four quarters totaled KRW 3.7 billion. First-half 2026 standalone revenue fell 22.6% year on year and the company moved to operating and net losses, a picture consistent with the quarterly trend.

05

Industry analysis

E-Starco is classified under real estate, but its business model differs from that of REITs. REITs pass rental income to shareholders mainly as distributions, while E-Starco generates revenue from property sales and runs leasing, education, and hotel operations alongside.

As a result, revenue varies widely between quarters with and without sales. Within its sector, E-Starco ranked near the bottom quartile for operating profit on an annual 2025 basis, while its debt ratio sat below the sector average.

Demand for property sales is sensitive to interest rates and housing transactions, so external conditions weigh on results. Because sales contributed directly to the smaller loss in 2025, the timing of this segment drives annual profit and loss.

The education segment faces structural pressure from fewer students and more intense competition among academies. The hotel relies on regional lodging demand but continues to post operating losses. The plan to sell the hotel points toward restructuring the asset base.

Compared with peers, its smaller scale may limit its funding capacity and business diversification relative to larger companies.

06

Outlook

Three company plans are confirmed. First, the company plans to open additional facilities, including a swimming pool, at the Seosan Benikia Hotel. Second, it plans to pursue a sale of the same hotel.

Third, shareholders approved by special resolution at the 46th annual general meeting held on March 27, 2026 an amendment adding business purposes, and also approved a change in par value tied to a share consolidation. At the same meeting, the director compensation limit was rejected by ordinary resolution.

The added business purposes read as a step that keeps the door open to new business lines. The 2026 earnings trend shows revenue falling in the first half and recovering slightly in the third quarter. Third-quarter revenue rose for two consecutive quarters, but the year-on-year gain was only 1.3%.

The third-quarter operating loss of KRW 273 million was smaller than the KRW 733 million a year earlier but larger than the KRW 147 million in the second quarter. The next checkpoints are the operating margin in the 2026 annual report and the 2026 audit opinion.

Whether sales recognized in 2026 lift annual results is also a point to track. The timing and price of the hotel sale, and how sale proceeds are used, are variables that could affect the capital structure.

Risk factors

Expansion into new businesses

The articles of incorporation were expanded when the added business purposes passed by special resolution at the March 2026 annual general meeting, opening room for entry into new business lines. The specific business the company will pursue has not yet been determined.

Running a new business would require different capabilities and funding from the existing property sales, leasing, education, and leisure operations, and could add management load. With the financial flexibility of a small company limited, sustained losses in a new venture could further strain the balance sheet.

Hotel facility expansion

The company plans to open additional facilities, including a swimming pool, at the Seosan Benikia Hotel. Even after completion, the hotel's earnings may improve only modestly if regional lodging and ancillary facility demand does not support the added space.

The opening date has not been fixed, so how the investment is executed and how it performs remain to be confirmed. If the added facilities raise operating costs, losses in the hotel segment could widen.

Business concentration and execution

Results depend on when sales revenue is recognized, which makes quarterly earnings volatile. The education and hotel segments continue to face weaker demand and operating losses. If the hotel sale is delayed or priced below expectations, the restructuring benefit could be limited.

Overall view

E-Starco's revenue rose 43.0% in 2025 and its operating loss narrowed to KRW 407 million, and revenue grew for two consecutive quarters through the third quarter of 2026.

However, the third-quarter 2026 operating loss of KRW 273 million and net loss attributable to controlling shareholders of KRW 1.22 billion both widened from the prior quarter, and the four-quarter total of such losses is KRW 3.7 billion.

First-half 2026 standalone revenue fell 22.6% year on year and turned into a loss. The debt ratio rose from 76.1% in 2022 to 136.8% in 2025. The disclaimer of opinion in the 2025 standalone audit carries more weight than the other items.

Strengths include the smaller loss tied to sales revenue, the quarterly revenue recovery, and a price-to-book ratio below the sector average. Weaknesses include five straight quarters of operating losses, weaker education and hotel segments, and the audit opinion alongside financial leverage.

The next checkpoints are the audit opinion and annual operating margin in the 2026 annual report, and progress on the hotel sale.
