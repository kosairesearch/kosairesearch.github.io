02

Business structure

Zaram Technology is a fabless system-on-chip (SoC) design company founded in 2000 and listed on KOSDAQ in 2023. Its core products are XGSPON chips for telecom equipment and XGSPON sticks that pair those chips with optical components, and it also supplies optical transceivers and GigaWire products.

Its optical transceiver line spans 1 to 100Gbps and has been supplied to Korean telecom operators and overseas global companies.

Revenue splits into development revenue, recognized when work starts at a customer's request, and mass-production revenue, which follows the customer's volume orders after development is complete.

Because ASIC projects generate meaningful profit only once they reach mass production, development revenue tends to come first and mass-production revenue follows.

The customer base has widened from domestic telecom operators to a European global telecom equipment maker; the company has not named the customer, but media have reported it as Nokia.

Following a KRW 16.5 billion first ASIC contract in 2023, the company signed a second contract worth KRW 23.05 billion in December 2025. It is also developing RISC-V-based telecom chips, mobility network processors and neuromorphic chips.

Unlike large diversified chipmakers, it is a telecom-focused fabless firm whose revenue is concentrated in a small number of customer projects. A 2025 media report described optical transceivers and GigaWire as a revenue base of roughly KRW 5 billion to KRW 6 billion a year.

04

Earnings analysis

On a filed basis, annual revenue fell from KRW 16.1 billion in 2022 to KRW 11.6 billion in 2023, a 27.9% decline, then rose to KRW 22.2 billion in 2024, up 90.7%, making 2024 the largest year of 2022 to 2025. Revenue dropped 52.0% to KRW 10.6 billion in 2025, the smallest year in that span.

Operating income moved from KRW 203 million in 2022 to an operating loss of KRW 2.14 billion in 2023, back to operating income of KRW 365 million in 2024, and then to an operating loss of KRW 7.06 billion in 2025.

Net income attributable to controlling shareholders went from KRW 617 million in 2022 to a net loss of KRW 1.27 billion in 2023, a profit of KRW 1.98 billion in 2024 and a net loss of KRW 6.84 billion in 2025.

The operating margin fell from 1.6% in 2024 to -66.4% in 2025, and the company attributed the deterioration to lower optical transceiver sales and higher R&D spending on XGSPON chip development.

Quarterly revenue went from KRW 2.09 billion in Q3 2025 with an operating loss of KRW 1.96 billion, to KRW 2.75 billion in Q4 2025 (up 31.3% quarter on quarter), and then to KRW 5.18 billion in Q1 2026, KRW 6.1 billion in Q2 2026 and KRW 7.57 billion in Q3 2026, which is four consecutive quarters of growth from Q4 2025 through Q3 2026.

The operating loss narrowed from KRW 2.16 billion in Q4 2025 to KRW 314 million in Q2 2026, and Q3 2026 posted operating income of KRW 292 million. Net loss attributable to controlling shareholders in Q3 2026 was still KRW 185 million, after a net profit of KRW 146 million in Q2 2026.

The sum of the last four quarters' net losses attributable to controlling shareholders is KRW 2.18 billion. At the end of 2025, total equity was KRW 35.7 billion and total liabilities KRW 41.3 billion, with the debt ratio rising from 84.5% in 2024 to 115.8% in 2025. Operating cash flow was KRW 612 million in 2025, below KRW 4.37 billion in 2024.

05

Industry analysis

XGSPON is a PON-family communications chip used in high-speed internet equipment, and the shift to 10Gbps products is cited as the basis for the company's mass-production revenue.

A 2025 NewsQuest report estimated that 10Gbps PON penetration, around 25% at the time, would rise to about 45% in 2025, a projection made when the report was published.

The 25Gbps product is likely to enter development after 10Gbps moves into mass production, and the same report estimated mass production in 2027 to 2028 after sample supply in 2026.

The 25Gbps product was originally planned for Samsung Electronics' 14nm foundry process, but analysts said it is likely to move to a more advanced node at the customer's request. On competition, the company cites its 1 to 100Gbps product range and its references with Korean telecom operators and overseas customers.

On the other hand, the 2025 operating margin of -66.4% ranked 143rd among 153 companies in the semiconductor and semiconductor equipment sector, according to Alphasquare. Telecom equipment investment cycles can also shift the timing of customer orders.

06

Outlook

The most important variable the company has cited is the second XGSPON ASIC contract signed in December 2025. The contract runs from December 8, 2025 to January 7, 2028, and the company expected mass-production revenue from the first contract to become meaningful starting in 2026.

The company said that once the first product enters mass production, it could generate annual revenue of about KRW 50 billion for more than ten years, but this is the company's own description and not a formal guidance figure.

In its August 2026 announcement, the company said it would expand mass-production revenue from its first XGS-PON ASIC while pursuing a second ASIC, 25GS-PON and mobility semiconductors.

The company has said that mass-production volumes could vary with telecom operators' investment plans, so customers' capital spending schedules could determine the timing of revenue.

The January to September 2026 quarterly figures in the filings are consistent with the preliminary first-half 2026 results the company announced in August. The 2026 annual business report, the next periodic filing, will be the first to disclose full-year 2026 revenue and fourth-quarter profit.

Whether operating profit stays positive in Q4 2026 and when second-contract development shifts into mass production are the next points to watch.

08

Bull factors

Large orders lay the groundwork for mass-production revenue

The second XGSPON ASIC contract announced in December 2025 is worth KRW 23.05 billion, equal to 104% of 2024 revenue. It follows a first contract with the same customer in 2023 worth KRW 16.5 billion, and media read the award as validation of the first development project.

The company has said that mass production of the first product could generate annual revenue of about KRW 50 billion for more than ten years.

Quarterly revenue growth and a Q3 2026 operating profit

Revenue rose for four consecutive quarters from Q4 2025 through Q3 2026, and Q3 2026 revenue of KRW 7.57 billion was up 261.2% year on year. Operating profit of KRW 292 million in the same quarter marked a turn to profitability.

The sum of net losses attributable to controlling shareholders over the last four quarters is KRW 2.18 billion, and quarterly losses in 2026 have been smaller than in Q4 2025.

Convertible bond clean-up eases balance-sheet pressure

In its announcement of August 14, 2026 (preliminary), the company said KRW 26.4 billion of the KRW 33 billion face value of its first convertible bonds had converted into common shares.

It said the remaining KRW 6.6 billion was acquired through a call right before maturity and retired, leaving no outstanding convertible bonds. The same announcement put the mid-year debt ratio at 23.2%.

09

Bear factors

A large 2025 loss and continuing losses

The 2025 operating loss was KRW 7.06 billion and the net loss attributable to controlling shareholders was KRW 6.84 billion, with an operating margin of -66.4%. The operating loss in Q4 2025 was KRW 2.16 billion.

Operating profit turned positive in Q3 2026, but the net loss attributable to controlling shareholders in that quarter was still KRW 185 million.

Revenue volatility and customer concentration

Annual revenue swung sharply, at KRW 16.1 billion in 2022, KRW 11.6 billion in 2023, KRW 22.2 billion in 2024 and KRW 10.6 billion in 2025. The company cited lower optical transceiver sales as a main cause of the 2025 decline.

Because the large ASIC orders are concentrated in one customer, changes to that customer's capital spending or project schedule could shift revenue timing substantially.

Debt burden and weak cash generation

Total liabilities at the end of 2025 were KRW 41.3 billion, above total equity of KRW 35.7 billion, and the debt ratio was 115.8%. Operating cash flow was only KRW 612 million in 2025 and was negative at KRW -6.79 billion in 2023.

The lower debt ratio after the convertible bond conversion comes from the company's preliminary announcement, and rising development spending could weigh on cash flow again.

10

Risk factors

Customer and project concentration

A large share of revenue is tied to ASIC projects with a European global telecom equipment maker. If that customer delays capital spending or changes product specifications, both the timing and volume of mass production could shift. The company has said mass-production revenue depends on telecom operators' investment plans.

Technology transition and process risk

The move from 10Gbps to 25Gbps products could lengthen development time and raise costs. Media reports say the 25Gbps product is likely to shift to a more advanced process node than originally planned.

Delays in sample and mass-production schedules for next-generation products would push back the growth scenario for 2027 to 2028.

Dilution and financial variables

Most of the first convertible bonds converted into common shares, which increased the number of shares outstanding. The conversion reduced debt but diluted existing shareholders.

If development spending and working capital needs rise together, operating cash flow could turn negative again, so quarterly cash-flow trends matter.

12

Overall view

Zaram Technology posted an operating loss of KRW 7.06 billion and a net loss attributable to controlling shareholders of KRW 6.84 billion for 2025, but revenue rose for four consecutive quarters from Q4 2025 through Q3 2026 and operating profit turned positive in Q3 2026.

The second ASIC contract signed in December 2025, worth KRW 23.05 billion, follows the first contract and is presented as the foundation for mass-production revenue.

On the balance sheet, most convertible bonds converted into common shares and no bonds remain outstanding, according to the company's preliminary announcement of August 14, 2026.

On the other side, the last four quarters still total a net loss attributable to controlling shareholders of KRW 2.18 billion, and Q3 2026 still showed a net loss. Revenue depends heavily on a few customer projects and has swung widely from year to year, and the debt ratio was 115.8% at the end of 2025.

Profitability ranks near the bottom of its sector, so the pace of earnings recovery matters. In short, the bullish factors are the large orders and the quarterly improvement, while the bearish factors are the remaining net losses and customer concentration.

The next points to watch are preliminary Q4 2026 results and the full-year profit and loss in the 2026 annual business report.
