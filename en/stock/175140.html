AGOS: Anti-drone pivot, profitability is still the test

AGOS is reorganizing from AI speakers and mobile routers toward anti-drone and defense businesses, but its operating loss widened to KRW 6.13 billion in the third quarter of 2026, so the timing of a profit recovery remains unclear.

02

Business structure

AGOS (formerly Human Technology, 175140) is a telecom device company founded in 2002 and listed on KOSDAQ in 2015. It started with mobile routers and wearable devices and from 2017 supplied AI speakers through a major portal, widening its customers to telecom carriers and education companies.

Through a media company acquisition at the end of 2022 and a merger in 2023 it added home shopping and e-commerce distribution as well as video content, and the 2025 acquisition of Human AGOS brought it into integrated anti-drone solutions and defense.

In a breakdown by the aggregator Butler based on second-quarter 2026 revenue, the segment mix was radio measurement and surveillance equipment 46%, AI speakers 27%, mobile routers 9%, distribution 8%, and other 8%.

The core of the anti-drone business is an integrated detection, tracking and response system built on in-house RF scanners, smart jammers and spoofers, and the company says it holds the number one share of Korea's anti-drone market.

Key customers include Korea Gas Corporation, Korea Airports Corporation, Korea Southern Power, Korea Hydro & Nuclear Power and Korea East-West Power, all operators of national core energy and transport infrastructure.

The company changed its name to AGOS after a shareholder meeting on June 30, 2026, and restructured around anti-drone and defense while planning to wind down non-core operations.

The AI speaker business faces steady smart-home demand, but competition has intensified due to low-priced offerings from Chinese manufacturers. The company says it has secured competitiveness in public-sector procurement, and because orders depend on government agency timing, quarterly revenue can vary widely.

04

Earnings analysis

Annual revenue rose from KRW 35.5 billion in 2022 to KRW 38.5 billion in 2023 (up 8.3% year on year), KRW 42.7 billion in 2024 (up 10.9%) and KRW 55.5 billion in 2025 (up 30.0%). Revenue has increased for three consecutive years from 2023 through 2025, and 2025 was the largest year in the 2022 to 2025 span.

The operating loss was KRW 6.62 billion in 2022, KRW 7.99 billion in 2023, KRW 6.59 billion in 2024 and KRW 11.6 billion in 2025, a loss in each of the four years. The operating margin was -18.6% in 2022, -20.8% in 2023, -15.4% in 2024 and -21.0% in 2025, so the loss margin widened again in 2025.

In 2025 revenue grew 30.0% while the operating loss widened, showing that top-line growth did not translate into better profitability.

Net loss attributable to controlling shareholders was KRW 10.3 billion in 2022, KRW 666 million in 2023, KRW 16 billion in 2024 and KRW 18.3 billion in 2025, and the 2023 gap between that figure and the consolidated net loss of KRW 14.8 billion was large, meaning non-controlling interests carried a sizable share of losses that year.

At the end of 2025 total equity was KRW 41.3 billion, of which KRW 40.9 billion belonged to controlling shareholders, total liabilities were KRW 20.7 billion, and the debt-to-equity ratio was 50.1%.

Operating cash flow was negative in every year, at -KRW 17.7 billion in 2022, -KRW 15.2 billion in 2023, -KRW 1.62 billion in 2024 and -KRW 7.15 billion in 2025.

On a quarterly basis, revenue was KRW 14.4 billion in the third quarter of 2025, KRW 17 billion in the fourth quarter (up 18.0% from the prior quarter), KRW 9.16 billion in the first quarter of 2026 (down 46.0%), KRW 9.62 billion in the second quarter (up 5.0%) and KRW 5.4 billion in the third quarter (down 43.9% quarter on quarter and down 62.5% year on year).

The operating loss was KRW 3.05 billion, KRW 437 million, KRW 2.96 billion, KRW 2.54 billion and KRW 6.13 billion across the same five quarters, an operating loss in each of the five quarters.

Net income attributable to controlling shareholders was positive at KRW 2.4 billion in the second quarter of 2026 even though that quarter still posted an operating loss, so the size of non-operating items is the next point to verify.

Net losses attributable to controlling shareholders were KRW 6.61 billion in the fourth quarter of 2025, KRW 3.71 billion in the first quarter of 2026 and KRW 6.08 billion in the third quarter of 2026, totaling KRW 14 billion over the last four quarters.

The third-quarter 2026 revenue decline was driven largely by the shrinking e-commerce business, and in its suspension filing the company said the impact of the discontinued business on total revenue would be limited.

05

Industry analysis

Demand for anti-drone systems is rising as drone threats against national core facilities and energy infrastructure grow. The company says it has established itself as a leading supplier in the public procurement market, including airports, power plants and government buildings.

Because orders depend on the budgets and timing of public agencies, they tend to cluster in certain quarters, which shows up as large swings in quarterly revenue.

Overseas, reports cite a K-defense consortium of five companies entering the European market and ongoing talks with Middle Eastern and Southeast Asian countries, but the scale and timing of such contracts have not yet been confirmed through disclosure.

In defense, competition involves large defense groups and specialized equipment makers, and AGOS positions itself on RF detection and jamming technology. The AI speaker market is expected to see steady demand from smart-home adoption, but low-priced offerings from Chinese manufacturers are the main pressure on pricing.

As the AI speaker business shrinks in relative weight, defense is becoming a larger share of the mix. The company argues that its telecom engineering history and public-sector product track record create barriers to entry in the new business.

Compared with listed electronics peers such as Samsung Electro-Mechanics, LG Innotek and Hanwha Vision, the scale and profit structure differ widely, and AGOS remains in an operating-loss position.

The cycle can be described as a transition period in which public defense demand expands while price competition persists in AI speakers.

06

Outlook

The company has stated that it aims to achieve a full-scale revenue recovery and profitability improvement in 2026 and reach a turn to profit.

Its growth strategy has three stages: strengthen domestic market leadership in the short term, become a leading global anti-drone player in the medium term, and evolve into a comprehensive defense solutions provider over the long term.

On orders, the cumulative backlog stood at KRW 11.0 billion at the end of April 2026, and a KRW 6.0 billion package, including KRW 3.7 billion from Korea Gas Corporation and KRW 2.3 billion from Korea Airports Corporation, was announced on July 1, 2026.

Talks with Middle Eastern and Southeast Asian countries are ongoing, which the company presents as the basis for second-half orders. On organization, the absorption merger with Human AGOS was completed on September 1, 2026, and Jeong Cheol-gon, a defense industry veteran, was appointed CEO.

Press coverage viewed the merger as a driver of second-half growth because anti-drone earnings would be fully reflected in the parent's standalone results, but the two entities were already consolidated, so the merger by itself does not directly raise consolidated revenue.

The e-commerce suspension decided on October 28, 2026 is aimed at focusing on core businesses and improving the financial structure, and the filing states the suspension date may change depending on the closing of counterparties and after-sales progress.

The next point to confirm is how the revenue mix shifts toward anti-drone and AI speakers after the e-commerce exit. Once the 2026 annual report shows full-year revenue and operating margin without e-commerce, the progress toward the turn-to-profit target can be measured.

Bull factors

Anti-drone order flow

Contracts announced in July 2026 with Korea Gas Corporation and Korea Airports Corporation total KRW 6.0 billion, and the cumulative backlog stood at KRW 11.0 billion at the end of April 2026. Ongoing talks with Middle Eastern and Southeast Asian countries are a further order channel the company has cited.

Public demand for national infrastructure protection is expanding alongside drone threats, which supports room for top-line growth.

Restructuring and merger effects

The absorption merger with Human AGOS was completed on September 1, 2026, and Jeong Cheol-gon, a defense industry veteran, was appointed CEO. The company says it is winding down non-core operations and focusing capital and staff on advancing its anti-drone solutions. Consolidating R&D and production in one structure is presented by the company as a source of efficiency gains.

E-commerce exit and financial structure

The e-commerce suspension decided on October 28, 2026 closes a business that had been shrinking. The company says concentrating resources on its core businesses could improve the financial structure.

Net income attributable to controlling shareholders turned positive at KRW 2.4 billion in the second quarter of 2026, which some may read as a sign of a possible earnings trough.

09

Bear factors

Persistent and widening operating losses

The operating loss widened to KRW 11.6 billion in 2025, and the company has reported an operating loss in each of the five quarters from the third quarter of 2025 through the third quarter of 2026. The third-quarter 2026 operating loss of KRW 6.13 billion was larger than in the prior quarter.

The operating margin stayed between -15.4% and -21.0% from 2022 through 2025, so higher revenue has not changed the loss structure.

Sharp revenue decline and e-commerce shrinkage

Third-quarter 2026 revenue of KRW 5.4 billion fell 62.5% year on year and 43.9% from the prior quarter. The e-commerce business subject to suspension totaled KRW 7.8 billion, equal to 14.09% of 2025 revenue.

The company says the impact on total revenue is limited, but the size of the remaining core revenue will determine how quickly results can recover.

Price competition in AI speakers

AI speakers still account for about 27% of revenue in the second quarter of 2026. Steady smart-home demand is expected, but low-priced offerings from Chinese manufacturers are intensifying competition.

Greater price pressure could make it harder to defend profitability in this segment and slow the recovery of overall margins.

Risk factors

Procurement timing and order volatility

Revenue in the anti-drone business depends heavily on the budgets and procurement schedules of national agencies. A KRW 11.0 billion cumulative backlog suggests revenue may rise and fall sharply as large contracts cluster. If overseas talks do not turn into contracts, the second-half growth assumption could be delayed.

Cash flow and financial capacity

Operating cash flow was negative in every year from 2022 through 2025, at -KRW 7.15 billion in 2025. Total liabilities at the end of 2025 were KRW 20.7 billion, with a debt-to-equity ratio of 50.1%. If the operating losses continue and working capital needs grow, additional funding may become necessary.

Execution risk in the business transition

The name change, merger, CEO appointment and e-commerce suspension are overlapping within a short period, reshaping the organization and counterparties together. The suspension date may change depending on the closing of counterparties and after-sales progress, according to the filing.

If the restructuring creates revenue gaps or higher costs, the timing of the targeted turn to profit could slip.

12

Overall view

AGOS is shifting from a telecom device company built on AI speakers and mobile routers toward a business centered on anti-drone and defense.

Revenue rose 30.0% to KRW 55.5 billion in 2025, but the operating loss widened to KRW 11.6 billion, and the third quarter of 2026 still showed an operating loss of KRW 6.13 billion.

The turn to positive net income attributable to controlling shareholders in the second quarter of 2026 is a positive signal, but the operating loss in the same quarter means the weight of non-operating items should be examined as well.

Anti-drone orders, including a KRW 6.0 billion package and an KRW 11.0 billion backlog, plus talks in the Middle East and Southeast Asia, underpin the growth case.

On the other side, third-quarter 2026 revenue fell to KRW 5.4 billion, the e-commerce business was suspended, and AI speakers face price competition from Chinese manufacturers. Valuation rests more on assets and turnaround expectations than on earnings, with the price-to-book ratio above 1x.

Strengths and weaknesses are both present, so the next checkpoints are the operating margin without e-commerce in the 2026 annual report and whether overseas contracts materialize.
