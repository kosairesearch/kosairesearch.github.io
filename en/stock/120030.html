Business structure

Chosun Welding Pohang is a welding consumables maker headquartered in Pohang, Gyeongbuk, classified under coated arc welding electrodes and filler products. The company's own profile lists coated arc welding electrodes, flux-cored wire, submerged arc flux, and MIG and TIG wire as its main products.

The Onsan-based subsidiary was spun off in January 2010 under a holding-company restructuring and became an independent entity. The Japanese local subsidiary CHOSUNWELDING JAPAN was established in 2009 to supply products to Japanese customers. As of September 2025 the company had 109 employees.

Demand tracks shipbuilding, automotive and construction activity. In 2023 sales dipped slightly as construction stayed depressed even though auto output rose and shipbuilding was strong. Through the first nine months of 2025, separate-basis sales rose but cost pressure limited profitability.

Earlier in 2025, stabilized supply of base materials improved results. The steel and metals sector mixes large listed groups with small manufacturers, so direct comparison is limited by differences in business structure and scale.

Earnings analysis

On a disclosed annual basis, revenue went from KRW 67 billion in 2022 to KRW 66.6 billion in 2023 (down 0.5% year on year) and KRW 50.9 billion in 2024 (down 23.6%), before recovering slightly to KRW 51.8 billion in 2025 (up 1.7%). The largest year in 2022 to 2025 was 2022, and the smallest was 2024.

Operating profit rose from KRW 9.44 billion in 2022 to KRW 10.6 billion in 2023 (up 12.1%), then fell to KRW 8.68 billion in 2024 (down 17.9%) and KRW 7.79 billion in 2025 (down 10.3%), two straight years of decline.

The 2023 gain came from lower cost of sales and selling and administrative expenses even as construction weakness trimmed sales. The operating margin went from 14.1% in 2022 to 15.9% in 2023 and 17.1% in 2024, then eased to 15.0% in 2025.

Net income attributable to controlling shareholders was KRW 10.5 billion in 2022, KRW 13 billion in 2023 (up 23.6%), and KRW 16.3 billion in 2024 (up 25.1%), the peak, before dropping to KRW 9.38 billion in 2025 (down 42.4%).

In 2024 operating profit fell while net income rose, so the two moved in opposite directions, which suggests non-operating items had a large effect.

Quarterly revenue was KRW 12.6 billion in the third quarter of 2025, KRW 12.9 billion in the fourth, KRW 12.3 billion in the first quarter of 2026, KRW 16.4 billion in the second, and KRW 10.8 billion in the third, a 34.6% quarter-on-quarter drop.

Operating profit was KRW 3.96 billion in the second quarter of 2026, up 165.6% from the prior quarter, and KRW 1.3 billion in the third quarter, down 67.0%. The company posted operating profit in the third quarter of 2026 but a net loss, which turned to a loss from a profit a year earlier. Earlier in 2025, stabilized base-material supply lifted results.

Risk factors

Raw-material supply disruption

Raw-material conditions have driven quarterly swings in results. If base-material supply tightens again, procurement costs and production schedules could come under pressure and raise cost of sales. This means Chosun Welding's output relies on a steady supply of raw materials. If supply disruption delays production, the timing of quarterly revenue recognition could also shift.

Japanese subsidiary customer demand

The Japanese local subsidiary, CHOSUNWELDING JAPAN, serves as the channel for supplying products to Japanese customers. If the timing or volume of orders from Japanese customers changes, sales through this subsidiary could be affected.

Japanese demand can move apart from the shipbuilding, construction and auto cycles, and may diverge from domestic demand signals. Demand shifts in the overseas sales channel could add another variable to total company revenue.

Automotive-sector demand

Chosun Welding's demand also spans automotive production, so order flow from the auto sector affects the revenue mix. If auto output moves in a different direction from shipbuilding or construction, the sales mix by product could shift.

Whether other industries offset weakness in one sector will determine how stable quarterly results remain. Changes in auto-sector demand bear directly on the mix of welding consumables orders.
