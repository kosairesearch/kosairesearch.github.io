02

Business structure

Cellbion is a biotech company developing radiopharmaceutical therapies (RPT), headquartered inside the Cancer Research Institute of Seoul National University. The company listed on KOSDAQ in October 2024 under the technology growth special listing track.

Its core pipeline is Lu-177-DGUL, a PSMA-targeting radiopharmaceutical for metastatic castration-resistant prostate cancer. The domestic Phase 2 study reported an objective response rate of 35.9%, or 47.4% in patients with measurable lesions.

The conditional marketing authorization application was filed on December 30, 2025, and the Phase 3 trial plan (IND) was approved in September 2026. The company has also signed a clinical trial collaboration and supply agreement with Merck (MSD) to evaluate Lu-177-DGUL with Keytruda (pembrolizumab) in a Phase 1 study.

Its stated strategy centers on indication expansion and out-licensing, so licensing deals are the core route to monetization. Novartis's Pluvicto is the reference product in the competitive discussion. Annual revenue of KRW 1.95 billion in 2025 reflects a business still before product approval.

Potential partners are global pharma and biotech companies, and the company said it planned one-on-one partnering meetings with about 19 firms at BIO Japan 2026. A company official said several global pharma firms had reached out first.

04

Earnings analysis

Annual revenue on a disclosed basis rose from KRW 1.47 billion in 2023 to KRW 2.29 billion in 2024 (up 55.6% year on year) and then fell to KRW 1.95 billion in 2025 (down 15.1% year on year), with 2024 the largest year of the three.

Operating losses over the same period were KRW 5.04 billion in 2023, KRW 7.14 billion in 2024 (losses widened), and KRW 8.24 billion in 2025 (losses widened).

In 2024 operating losses grew even as revenue rose, and in 2025 falling revenue and wider losses coincided, which points to revenue not covering the fixed cost base. The operating margin was -342.8% in 2023, -311.7% in 2024, and -423.9% in 2025.

The net loss attributable to controlling shareholders was KRW 3.63 billion in 2023, KRW 7.27 billion in 2024, and KRW 7.57 billion in 2025, and the net loss including non-controlling interests was reported at the same amounts.

At year-end 2025, total equity was KRW 22.8 billion, all attributable to controlling shareholders, total liabilities were KRW 12.4 billion, and the debt ratio was 51.3% in 2023, 16.3% in 2024, and 54.5% in 2025.

Operating cash flow was -KRW 3.94 billion in 2023, -KRW 4.78 billion in 2024, and -KRW 5.55 billion in 2025, so the outflow widened each year.

Quarterly revenue fell from KRW 679 million in Q3 2025 to KRW 382 million in Q4 2025 (down 43.8% quarter on quarter), then rose to KRW 613 million in Q1 2026 (up 60.4%), eased to KRW 472 million in Q2 2026 (down 22.9%), and reached KRW 487 million in Q3 2026 (up 3.1% quarter on quarter, down 28.3% year on year).

Quarterly operating losses ran from KRW 2 billion in Q3 2025 to KRW 2.59 billion in Q3 2026, five consecutive quarters of losses, and the Q3 2026 loss was larger than a year earlier.

Net results attributable to controlling shareholders swung from a loss of KRW 1.83 billion in Q3 2025 to a profit of KRW 14.4 billion in Q2 2026, which turned to a loss of KRW 290 million in Q3 2026.

Because the Q2 2026 profit was far larger than the KRW 2.64 billion operating loss, non-operating items likely drove the gap, so the profit should be read apart from operating results. Net profit attributable to controlling shareholders over the last four quarters (Q4 2025 to Q3 2026) totals KRW 9.29 billion.

05

Industry analysis

Radiopharmaceutical therapies attach radioactive isotopes to antibodies or peptides to deliver radiation to tumors. In prostate cancer, an established competitor already leads the global market, so a later entrant must prove differentiation through clinical data.

Media coverage framed Cellbion's Phase 2 results against Novartis's Pluvicto as the reference product. In licensing talks, clinical stage is a key benchmark for technology value, and early-stage talks center on mechanism, early safety, and efficacy potential. Phase 3 candidates carry higher trial and approval costs.

Phase 3 is the stage that confirms real therapeutic value and risk, so Cellbion's negotiating position depends on how quickly Phase 3 data accumulate. The Keytruda combination follows the trend of pairing immunotherapy with radiopharmaceuticals, and its Phase 1 study is ongoing.

A company official cited an industry estimate of more than KRW 1 trillion for the technology value, which is a company-side statement rather than an independent valuation. Media reports suggest that conditional approval could enable an early commercial launch.

Compared with listed Korean biotech peers that rely on licensing and clinical progress, Cellbion has a small revenue base and depends on the value of its development-stage assets. The domestic approval timeline and whether licensing deals close remain the main variables for commercialization.

The Phase 3 trial plan was approved by the Ministry of Food and Drug Safety in September 2026, and subsequent clinical development steps are underway. The Phase 1 study with Merck (MSD) combining Keytruda is also ongoing. On licensing, the company is continuing follow-up talks with some firms it met at BIO Japan 2026.

It also ran a standalone booth at BIO-Europe 2026 to present its pipeline clinical results. Of the April 2026 rights issue proceeds, KRW 17 billion was earmarked for facility investment from 2026 through 2029.

The operating funds include R&D spending such as clinical trial costs, so Phase 3 costs are a major use of the raised capital. Operating losses continued through Q3 2026 on a disclosed basis.

The next checkpoints are the Ministry of Food and Drug Safety's conditional approval decision and whether licensing agreements are disclosed, followed by Phase 3 patient enrollment and operating results in the 2026 annual report.
