02

Business structure

Sbsungbo is a KOSPI-listed chemical company whose main business is manufacturing and selling crop protection products, or agrochemicals. As of the third quarter of 2025, crop protection products accounted for 95.42% of total sales, with rental income and cafe sales making up the rest.

Its main product lines are insecticides, fungicides and herbicides, and the company says it aims to strengthen external competitiveness through quality improvements and new product development.

Sales run through a network of regional sales branches, and its main customers are wholesalers and retailers that serve farmers. Revenue is concentrated in the spring and summer seasons, so quarterly results can swing widely.

On the competitive front, intensifying market competition driven by agricultural import liberalization and the opening of imports of crop protection active ingredients is cited as a key pressure.

In 2025 the company said a sharp rise in the exchange rate lifted manufacturing costs, which suggests its cost structure is sensitive to currency moves.

On the production side, the company has pursued the prevention of serious industrial accidents and stronger safety in chemical handling as core tasks since 2022, and it ranked first in a 2025 chemical safety best-practice contest.

Rental and cafe operations provide revenue beyond crop protection, but their scale is small relative to crop protection. The largest shareholder and related parties hold 48.62%, a figure last changed on December 20, 2022, so the ownership structure is concentrated.

Compared with large chemical companies such as LG Chem and Hanwha Solutions, the company's portfolio is concentrated in a single crop-protection line.

04

Earnings analysis

On the DART-filed annual figures, sales fell from KRW 66.4 billion in 2022 to KRW 62.6 billion in 2023, a 5.7% decline, and then to KRW 59.8 billion in 2024, a 4.4% decline, before rising 7.5% to KRW 64.3 billion in 2025. 2022 was the largest year and 2024 the smallest within the 2022 to 2025 window.

Operating profit dropped from KRW 1.9 billion in 2022 to KRW 201 million in 2023, an 89.4% decline, and then turned into an operating loss of KRW 2.93 billion in 2024. The 2025 operating loss widened to KRW 8.14 billion, which makes 2024 and 2025 two consecutive loss years.

Operating margin moved from 2.9% in 2022 to 0.3% in 2023, -4.9% in 2024 and -12.7% in 2025. The company attributed the 2025 widening to higher manufacturing costs from a sharp rise in the exchange rate and to one-time costs tied to the headquarters relocation.

Controlling-shareholder net profit, by contrast, swung from a KRW 3.44 billion net loss in 2024 to a KRW 34.5 billion profit in 2025, which the company linked to a larger gain on the sale of the headquarters building.

Net income including non-controlling interests was KRW 34.1 billion, a different figure from the controlling-shareholder number, and the combination explains why an operating loss and a net profit appeared in the same year.

On a quarterly basis, the company moved from an operating loss of KRW 7.41 billion in Q4 2025 to operating profit of KRW 7.05 billion in Q1 2026, but operating losses of KRW 3.67 billion in Q2 2026 and KRW 3.92 billion in Q3 2026 followed.

Q3 2026 sales of KRW 7.57 billion rose 12.6% from a year earlier, and the operating loss narrowed from the year-earlier quarter. Net losses attributable to controlling shareholders over the last four quarters total KRW 12.8 billion.

Operating cash flow was negative in every year, at -KRW 3.66 billion in 2022, -KRW 1.78 billion in 2023, -KRW 3.26 billion in 2024 and -KRW 6.05 billion in 2025.

Industry analysis

Demand for crop protection products follows the farming calendar, so Sbsungbo's sales are concentrated in the spring and summer seasons. On the market side, the expansion of agricultural import liberalization has put pressure on domestic selling prices and volumes, a point the company's own materials repeatedly raise.

The import liberalization of crop protection active ingredients is also cited as widening raw material sourcing routes and intensifying competition. Because raw material and manufacturing costs are exposed to exchange rates, the company said a sharp rise in the exchange rate lifted manufacturing costs in 2025.

The company presents its network of regional sales branches as a competitive tool and positions quality improvements and new product development as ways to differentiate.

Large companies in the same chemical sector run several businesses, including petrochemicals, battery materials and high-value materials, so their business mix differs sharply from Sbsungbo's crop-protection focus.

The company attributes the 2025 earnings swing to a combination of the currency rise and one-time items tied to the headquarters relocation and sale. Within the competitive landscape, Sbsungbo sits as a small specialist operator that relies on its branch sales network and product quality.

Market competition and import rules

Agricultural import liberalization and the opening of active ingredient imports are structural factors that can pressure domestic selling prices and market share. The company said it is responding through quality improvements and new product development.

As competition intensifies, the efficiency of the regional sales branch network becomes more important. Crop protection makes up more than 95% of sales, so changes in competition for this one business flow directly into total results.

Exchange rate and cost

In 2025 the company said a sharp rise in the exchange rate lifted manufacturing costs. If cost increases cannot be passed on to selling prices, the operating margin falls. The 2025 operating margin was -12.7%. How currency moves are reflected in the cost ratio after 2026 is the next key question.

Reliance on one-off gains

One-time costs from the headquarters relocation also widened the operating loss in the same year. When one-off items drive results, quarterly volatility increases.
