02

Business structure

LOTTE rental operates four businesses: long-term auto rental, short-term auto rental including car sharing, Biz rental for corporate customers, and used-car sales.

In the third quarter of 2026, on a preliminary basis announced November 5, 2026, auto long-term revenue reached KRW 430.5 billion, up 6.2% year on year, with operating profit of KRW 51.8 billion and a 12.0% operating margin.

The long-term rental fleet grew 6.5%, and used cars in long-term rental, which carry higher margins, rose to 21.2% of the total from 15.1% in the third quarter of 2024.

Auto short-term revenue reached KRW 93.1 billion with operating profit of KRW 12.9 billion and a 13.9% margin, supported by a 19.3% rise in daily rentals tied to foreign customers. Revenue from the monthly short-term product grew 63.0%.

Biz rental revenue rose 12.4% as the company refocused on core items such as office automation equipment, measuring instruments and forklifts, with operating profit of KRW 4.2 billion and a 9.2% margin.

Used-car sales posted revenue of KRW 206.4 billion and operating profit of KRW 24 billion, keeping a double-digit margin. Among the rental units, auto long-term generates the largest operating profit.

On competitive positioning, the company said its Lotte Rent-a-car brand ranked first in the rental category of the 2026 NBCI evaluation by the Korea Productivity Center, with a score of 78 and 15 consecutive years at the top.

Overseas, the company began a short-term rental service for Korean travelers in Okinawa, Japan in July 2026 and formed a strategic alliance with Hertz in August 2026, expanding overseas booking to 63 cities in 8 countries.

04

Earnings analysis

On the basis of filed results, revenue rose from KRW 2.74 trillion in 2022 to KRW 2.92 trillion in 2025, and grew for three consecutive years from 2023 to 2025. Revenue in 2025 was up 4.5% from the prior year. Operating profit was KRW 305.2 billion in 2023, KRW 284.8 billion in 2024 and KRW 312.5 billion in 2025.

After a 6.7% decline in 2024, operating profit rose 9.7% in 2025, making 2025 the largest year among 2023 to 2025. The operating margin moved between 10% and 11%, at 11.1% in 2023, 10.2% in 2024 and 10.7% in 2025.

Net income attributable to owners of the parent was KRW 120 billion in 2023 (up 27.1%), KRW 106.8 billion in 2024 (down 11.0%) and KRW 127.6 billion in 2025 (up 19.5%). Net income including non-controlling interests rose from KRW 102.7 billion in 2024 to KRW 126.7 billion in 2025.

The debt ratio at the end of 2025 was 372.3%, down from 434.4% in 2022. However, operating cash flow went from KRW 498.3 billion in 2023 and KRW 325.5 billion in 2024 to -KRW 512.6 billion in 2025.

On a quarterly basis, revenue bottomed at KRW 726.4 billion in the fourth quarter of 2025, down 4.2% from the prior quarter, and then rose for three consecutive quarters to KRW 775.4 billion in the third quarter of 2026.

Operating profit also rose for three consecutive quarters, from KRW 79.3 billion in the fourth quarter of 2025 to KRW 92.9 billion in the third quarter of 2026.

Net income attributable to owners of the parent fell to KRW 19.7 billion in the second quarter of 2026 before recovering to KRW 30.5 billion in the third quarter, but was still down 22.0% year on year.

The company reported adjusted operating profit of KRW 97.2 billion for the third quarter, up 6.6% year on year, on a preliminary basis. Press reports attribute the third-quarter net income decline to a KRW 16.1 billion one-off loss tied to litigation-related refunds.

Cumulative operating profit for January to September 2026 was KRW 261.16 billion, up 12.0%, on a preliminary basis announced November 5, 2026.

05

Industry analysis

Long-term car rental involves multi-year contracts with customers, which gives the revenue base comparatively stable characteristics. An industry official has said that fleet size, utilization rates and used-car disposal prices determine profitability in car rental.

When used-car market volatility rose in July and August 2026, the company shifted good-quality vehicles into long-term rentals rather than selling them quickly. This choice delays exposure to disposal prices, which is how the shift can be read. The trade-off was a 27.7% decline in used-car sales operating profit.

From September, the company shifted to wholesale-focused sales to speed up inventory turnover, according to its statement. In short-term rental, growth has been driven by foreign customer demand. A 6.5% increase in the long-term fleet points to a larger rental base.

On competitive positioning, the company said Lotte Rent-a-car ranked first in the car rental category of the 2026 NBCI evaluation and has held the top spot for 15 years. The company itself describes its position as the number one operator in the mobility industry.

06

Outlook

The company said it will continue to expand high-margin products and pursue cost innovation to sustain stable growth. Overseas, it started a short-term rental service in Okinawa in July 2026 and formed an alliance with Hertz in August 2026, expanding overseas booking to 63 cities in 8 countries.

The company has also introduced an AI reservation service. Securities analysts expect the rise in long-term rental fleet size and growth in foreign short-term rental demand to continue into 2027. Hana Securities projected 2027 operating profit of KRW 375 billion, according to a report published in November 2026.

That projection is above the filed 2025 operating profit of KRW 312.5 billion. It is a brokerage estimate, and its realization will be confirmed through future filings.

Press reports say the largest shareholder has changed to the global private equity firm TPG, so the effect of the ownership change on management direction is worth watching. How the wholesale-focused used-car sales introduced in September 2026 affect fourth-quarter sales profit is another point to check. The next checkpoints are the preliminary fourth-quarter 2026 results and the 2026 annual report.

08

Bull factors

Rental core profit growth

Auto long-term operating profit reached KRW 51.8 billion in the third quarter of 2026 on a preliminary basis, up 20.2% year on year. The long-term fleet grew 6.5%, and accident costs per vehicle declined. Operating profit also rose in auto short-term and Biz rental, so the earnings gain did not rely on a single unit. Operating profit has risen for three consecutive quarters since the fourth quarter of 2025.

Expansion of higher-margin used-car long-term rentals

The share of used-car long-term rentals rose steadily from 15.1% in the third quarter of 2024 to 18.8% in the third quarter of 2025 and 21.2% in the third quarter of 2026. The company describes this product as higher-margin.

When the used-car sales market weakened, vehicles were redirected into rental assets to secure income over the contract period. The company presents this as evidence of structural improvement.

Overseas expansion and brand ranking

The company launched a short-term rental service in Okinawa in July 2026 and expanded overseas booking to 63 cities in 8 countries through the Hertz alliance in August. The company said its brand has ranked first in the car rental category of the NBCI evaluation for 15 consecutive years.

Bear factors

Weaker used-car sales

Used-car sales revenue fell 6.6% to KRW 206.4 billion in the third quarter of 2026 on a preliminary basis, and operating profit dropped 27.7% to KRW 24 billion. Because good-quality vehicles were moved into long-term rentals during the market volatility of July and August 2026, short-term sales gains declined.

The company said it shifted to wholesale-focused sales from September to speed up inventory turnover. If the used-car market recovers slowly, profit recovery in this unit could also be slower.

Weak net income

Net income attributable to owners of the parent was KRW 30.5 billion in the third quarter of 2026, down 22.0% year on year. It had fallen to KRW 19.7 billion in the second quarter of 2026 before recovering. Press reports say the third quarter included a KRW 16.1 billion one-off loss. Operating profit gains have not translated directly into net income gains.

Cash flow and financial burden

At the end of 2025, total liabilities of KRW 5.79 trillion were well above total equity of KRW 1.56 trillion. The debt ratio fell from 434.4% in 2022 to 372.3% in 2025 but remains high.

10

Risk factors

Used-car price volatility

Used-car disposal prices and timing shape both sales-unit profit and the residual value of long-term rental vehicles. Because vehicles were held back during the July and August 2026 volatility, the outcome of this approach depends on market recovery. The 27.7% drop in used-car sales operating profit reflects this risk in its current form.

Financial structure and cash flow

The debt ratio was 372.3% at the end of 2025, a high level, and operating cash flow turned negative in 2025. If borrowing conditions worsen, interest costs and vehicle financing costs could rise. Because rental operations require funding for vehicle purchases, financing conditions directly affect profitability.

One-off losses and ownership change

Press reports say one-off losses, including litigation-related refunds, pulled down third-quarter net income. Reports also say the largest shareholder has changed to the global private equity firm TPG. The effect of the ownership change on investment and dividend policy is a matter to confirm through future filings.

Overall view

LOTTE rental's operating profit for the third quarter of 2026 was KRW 92.9 billion, up 4.4% year on year, and operating profit has risen for three consecutive quarters.

The growth is centered in auto long-term rentals, and the used-car long-term share rising to 21.2% shows the margin-improvement structure in operating indicators.

On the other side, used-car sales operating profit fell 27.7%, and net income attributable to owners of the parent fell 22.0%, so operating profit gains have not carried through to net income. A report said Hana Securities projects 2027 operating profit of KRW 375 billion, but this is a brokerage estimate whose realization will be confirmed through future filings. The strengths of the rental core and the weaknesses in used-car sales and cash flow warrant equal weight in review.
