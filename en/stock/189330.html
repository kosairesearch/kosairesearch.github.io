Report overview

Sales up two years running, but losses persist

XIIlab's revenue has grown for two straight years since its 2023 low, yet it has posted operating losses in each of the last five quarters, so sales growth has not yet translated into profit.

02

Business structure

XIIlab was founded in 2010 as an AI solutions company and listed on KOSDAQ in 2021. The business runs in three segments. AI Infrastructure builds GPU infrastructure on its AstraGo orchestration software and supplies NVIDIA DGX systems, and the company reported 11 new customers in this segment in Q1 2026.

Vision AI, anchored by the real-time video analytics solution XAIVA and the AI video-reading service VidiGo, is expanding into defense and public-sector clients, and the company said segment revenue rose 73% in 2025. Digital Twin serves large global corporations on NVIDIA Omniverse.

In Q1 2026 segment growth was 60.7% for AI Infrastructure, 55.6% for Vision AI and 18.7% for Digital Twin. A March 2025 report put the 2024 mix at 71.4% for the appliance business and 21.5% for the AI business.

AI video-analytics revenue was reported at KRW 1.5 billion in 2022, KRW 600 million in 2023 and KRW 1.0 billion in 2024. Gross profit in 2025 was KRW 2.44 billion, up 87% year on year, which the company attributed to a larger share of its own software and licensing sales.

In April 2026 the company was elevated to Elite partner in NVIDIA's Partner Network software category. Against large IT services and platform companies in the same sector, its revenue scale is small, so widening its customer references remains a task.

04

Earnings analysis

On the DART-filed annual figures, revenue fell from KRW 9.9 billion in 2022 to KRW 3.66 billion in 2023, a 63.0% decline, then recovered to KRW 9.09 billion in 2024 (up 148.2%) and KRW 10.3 billion in 2025 (up 13.0%). Revenue grew in 2024 and 2025 after the 2023 low, and 2025 was the largest year of 2022–2025.

Operating losses were KRW 4.93 billion in 2022, KRW 6.91 billion in 2023 (wider), KRW 5.28 billion in 2024 (narrower) and KRW 3.23 billion in 2025 (narrower), a four-year run of losses from 2022 through 2025. The operating margin was -49.8% in 2022, -188.6% in 2023, -58.1% in 2024 and -31.4% in 2025.

Net loss attributable to controlling shareholders was KRW 4.54 billion in 2022, KRW 6.46 billion in 2023, KRW 5.19 billion in 2024 and KRW 3.12 billion in 2025, and the net loss including non-controlling interests was reported at the same amounts.

A 2025 report said gross profit reached KRW 2.44 billion, which it linked to a shift away from low-margin hardware distribution. Operating cash flow was negative in every year, at -KRW 4.94 billion in 2022, -KRW 5.18 billion in 2023, -KRW 3.62 billion in 2024 and -KRW 712 million in 2025.

The debt-to-equity ratio was 17.2% at end-2025, down from 38.9% in 2022, and total equity of KRW 25.1 billion is entirely attributable to controlling shareholders.

Quarterly revenue went from KRW 3.84 billion in Q3 2025 to KRW 4.93 billion in Q4 2025, fell to KRW 1.11 billion in Q1 2026, then rose to KRW 2.01 billion in Q2 and KRW 5.63 billion in Q3 2026.

Operating losses widened to KRW 1.93 billion in Q1 2026 and KRW 2.72 billion in Q2 before narrowing to KRW 1.31 billion in Q3, still wider than a year earlier. The company attributed the Q1 2026 cost increase to hiring, infrastructure investment and one-off costs including a certification fee for XAIVA. Net losses attributable to controlling shareholders over the last four quarters total KRW 6 billion.

05

Industry analysis

The company's case rests on rising domestic AI computing infrastructure investment, which it says is lifting demand for GPU operating software. In an August 2026 investor briefing, XIIlab said that as AI data center build-outs expand, demand grows for software that allocates and operates large GPU pools.

One report described enterprise infrastructure shifting from general-purpose servers toward high-density GPU data centers. The same report flagged a 'resource silo' problem, in which GPUs bought separately by departments cannot be managed centrally, so idle capacity and shortages appear at once.

Government-led national and regional AI computing centers were also cited as demand sources. XIIlab targets this gap with its AstraMon GPU diagnostics tool and the AstraGo orchestration platform. In physical AI, manufacturing, safety and defense were named as application areas.

On technology, the company presented research on lightweight vision AI at the ICML 2026 workshop in July 2026 to show on-device capability. Competition in infrastructure construction itself appears concentrated among large players, so XIIlab has narrowed its role toward operating software and services. In Vision AI, defense and public-sector demand sits alongside competition in real-time video analytics.

06

Outlook

In the second half of 2026 the company set two focus areas, AI data centers and physical AI, and said it links infrastructure construction, operating software and physical AI development environments.

Its chief executive said the company would concentrate on turning these into contracts and revenue during the second half. A large-scale project using Vera Rubin professional services capabilities was also planned for the second half of 2026.

The company said it is strengthening a dedicated AI data center sales unit and expanding external partnerships. In August 2026 it unveiled the AstraMon GPU diagnostics tool at a reseller partner summit and said it would spread the sales model through its partner network.

On funding, the KRW 10 billion convertible bond announced on November 6, 2026 is intended to secure working capital for GPU infrastructure construction and operations, with payment scheduled for November 16.

In physical AI it plans to supply solutions for model training and field validation and expand into manufacturing, safety and defense.

Product certifications were also cited as sales support, including an intelligent CCTV performance certification from KISA in February 2026 and GS Grade 1 software quality certification in April 2026. Whether these plans appear in quarterly revenue and the size of operating losses is the next checkpoint.

08

Bull factors

Revenue growth restored

Revenue rose in both 2024 and 2025, and 2025's KRW 10.3 billion was the largest year of 2022–2025. Q3 2026 revenue of KRW 5.63 billion was up 46.5% year on year. The company said Vision AI revenue grew 73% in 2025 as defense and public-sector supply expanded.

Software-weighted earnings mix

A 2025 report said gross profit rose 87% to KRW 2.44 billion. The company attributed this to a larger share of higher-margin software and licensing sales. The operating loss narrowed from KRW 5.28 billion in 2024 to KRW 3.23 billion in 2025, moving the operating margin from -58.1% to -31.4%.

AI data center demand and channels

The company's strategy rests on rising domestic AI infrastructure spending and demand for GPU operating software. AI Infrastructure revenue grew 60.7% year on year in Q1 2026, with 11 new customers added. Its April 2026 elevation to NVIDIA's Elite software partner is cited as a sales channel.

09

Bear factors

Five straight quarters of operating losses

Operating losses have continued for five consecutive quarters, from Q3 2025 through Q3 2026. The largest of these losses was KRW 2.72 billion in Q2 2026. Even with Q3 2026 revenue at KRW 5.63 billion, the operating loss of KRW 1.31 billion was wider than the KRW 662 million a year earlier.

Large swings in quarterly revenue

Q1 2026 revenue of KRW 1.11 billion fell 77.5% from the prior quarter before recovering in Q2 and Q3. A 2023 Korean securities research report described Q1 as the seasonal low point, with sales rising toward year-end as customers add servers. Such wide quarterly swings make the trend hard to read from one quarter to the next.

Convertible bond dilution and cash outflows

The KRW 10 billion convertible bond has a conversion price of KRW 8,106 per share and zero coupon and maturity rates. Conversion would add new shares and could dilute existing shareholders. Operating cash flow was still negative at -KRW 712 million in 2025.

10

Risk factors

Financing and dilution risk

The company holds a call option on up to 50% of the bond amount, and bondholders can exercise a put option from 30 months after issuance. Conversion would raise the share count and reduce existing stakes.

The proceeds fund GPU infrastructure working capital, and delayed recovery of that spending could extend operating losses.

Concentration risk in the business mix

A report put the appliance business at 71.4% of 2024 revenue, so dependence on the hardware supply cycle remains high. The timing of revenue recognition on large projects can swing quarterly results sharply. The company describes a shift toward software, but the change in mix will take time to show up in the numbers.

Policy and demand timing risk

Construction of national and regional AI computing centers depends on government project schedules. The pace at which policy funds such as the National Growth Fund are deployed is another variable. In Vision AI, public and defense contract timing and intensifying competition in video analytics could affect revenue.

Overall view

XIIlab's revenue has grown in 2024 and 2025 after the 2023 low, and its operating loss narrowed from KRW 6.91 billion in 2023 to KRW 3.23 billion in 2025.

However, operating losses have persisted for five consecutive quarters from Q3 2025 through Q3 2026, and even with Q3 2026 revenue at KRW 5.63 billion, the operating loss of KRW 1.31 billion was wider than a year earlier. The business spans AI Infrastructure, Vision AI and Digital Twin, and all three grew in Q1 2026.

The KRW 10 billion convertible bond announced on November 6, 2026 funds GPU infrastructure working capital but leaves dilution exposure at a KRW 8,106 conversion price. The shares trade in a premium range to book value, and the P/E cannot be calculated.

The next points to check are Q4 2026 results, the 2026 annual report, and whether large second-half projects convert into contracts.
