Seohee Construction: Core Business Shrinks as Financial Gains Carry Profit

Seohee Construction is rebuilding its order backlog and expanding shareholder returns, but it posted a 2026 third-quarter loss and relies heavily on financial asset gains.

  4. 4

02

Business structure

Seohee Construction builds apartment complexes mainly under regional housing cooperative (jiyeok jutaekjohap) projects.

KIS Ratings, in a report published in June 2026, described private residential construction contracts, led by cooperative projects, as the core business, and said the largest shareholder and related parties held 59.8% as of end-March 2026.

Because payment depends on each cooperative's funding, collection of construction receivables can slip, and member recruitment and construction starts drive earnings swings.

The business spans building and civil engineering; KIS attributed the 2025 profit decline to fewer building starts and a revised estimated cost in civil works. In 2026, large projects such as the Yongin Yangji 2-block complex (total construction value KRW 332.2 billion) and Hwaseong Bibong began construction.

The Busan Seomyeon cooperative contract signed in September 2026 is worth KRW 318.27 billion. Beyond cooperative projects, the company said it is expanding into public housing and Seoul redevelopment, and press reports said it was selected as builder for Mokdong and Siheung-dong redevelopment projects.

The Ministry of Land, Infrastructure and Transport ranked it 16th in construction capability for two consecutive years, the company said in August 2026. Cooperative-led order intake is sensitive to policy changes and member recruitment conditions.

As of end-March 2026, unstarted order backlog stood at KRW 1.9 trillion and signed project sites at KRW 3.1 trillion.

04

Earnings analysis

Annual revenue rose from KRW 1.44 trillion in 2022 to KRW 1.44 trillion in 2023 (up 0.3% year on year) and KRW 1.47 trillion in 2024 (up 2.2%), then fell 25.3% to KRW 1.1 trillion in 2025. Of 2022 to 2025, 2024 was the largest year and 2025 the smallest.

Operating profit fell 38.8% from KRW 235.7 billion in 2024 to KRW 144.4 billion in 2025, and the operating margin dropped from 16.0% to 13.1%. The company attributed the 2025 decline to lower construction revenue, while KIS Ratings cited fewer new cooperative project starts and a revised estimated cost in civil works.

Net profit attributable to controlling shareholders rose from KRW 98.1 billion in 2022 to KRW 160.4 billion in 2024, then fell 24.0% to KRW 121.9 billion in 2025. Net profit including non-controlling interests was KRW 120.6 billion in 2025, a different figure.

Net income running ahead of operating profit was reported to reflect financial asset valuation and disposal gains. First-half 2026 financial income was reported at KRW 172.6 billion, up 535.7% from a year earlier.

By quarter, the third quarter of 2025 posted revenue of KRW 236.9 billion and operating profit of KRW 28.3 billion, and the fourth quarter revenue of KRW 273.4 billion and operating profit of KRW 26.1 billion.

Revenue fell to KRW 191.7 billion in the first quarter of 2026 with operating profit of KRW 10.5 billion, then recovered to KRW 200.5 billion and KRW 36.4 billion in the second quarter.

In the third quarter of 2026 revenue was KRW 151.7 billion, with an operating loss of KRW 32.6 billion and a net loss attributable to controlling shareholders of KRW 120.6 billion, both turning to losses.

Net profit attributable to controlling shareholders over the last four quarters totaled KRW 90.2 billion, and quarterly swings are large. Operating cash flow moved from KRW 242.8 billion in 2024 to -KRW 3.75 billion in 2025, and was reported at -KRW 7.3 billion in the first half of 2026.

Rising construction receivables mean booked profit is not converting into cash, which is flagged as a financial challenge. Other bad-debt expenses were KRW 88.4 billion in 2024 and KRW 42.7 billion in 2025, according to KIS Ratings.

05

Industry analysis

The key variable for Seohee Construction is the housing market in provincial areas and the outskirts of the Seoul metropolitan area. KIS Ratings pointed to oversupply and weak housing demand in those regions, which has delayed securing new construction volume and advancing projects.

According to the same assessment, major cooperative projects were completed after 2024 and new construction starts declined, so revenue turned down from 2025.

KIS Ratings also said the government is pursuing reforms to regional cooperative housing rules, and their effect on new projects and bidding competitiveness should be monitored.

On the Yamok Station Seohee Stahills sale, a April 2026 press report cited industry views that its non-transit-adjacent location and high sale price make a strong take-up difficult.

Meanwhile, the average sales rate of ongoing housing projects was 93.9% at end-March 2026, and the average member recruitment rate for unstarted cooperative sites was 88.5%. The company said its construction order backlog is recovering to past levels.

On competitive position, the company held 16th place in construction capability rankings and is expanding from cooperative projects into Seoul redevelopment and public housing. A high share of cooperative-client orders means the company is directly exposed to policy changes, which is a risk specific to this business.

06

Outlook

The company disclosed its 2026–2028 corporate value enhancement plan in early November 2026. According to press reports, the plan includes paying out 25% of net profit as dividends, additional share cancellation, and starting construction at four pending projects by 2027.

The same reports said total dividends would rise 10% a year. On construction starts, three sites began in the first half of 2026: Hwaseong Bibong, Yongin Yangji 2-block, and Namyangju Onam 1st complex.

The company said revenue from about KRW 1 trillion of new sites started in the first half will be recognized in sequence from 2027. The order backlog rose 49.0% to KRW 1.86 trillion at end-June 2026 from KRW 1.25 trillion at year-end 2025.

The Busan Seomyeon cooperative contract signed in September 2026 runs from June 30, 2027 to February 29, 2032. KIS Ratings expects revenue to stay weak for now but said consolidated annual revenue of roughly KRW 1 trillion can be supported by the backlog and new projects.

The same assessment expects the operating margin to stay above 10% a year. It also flagged that slow sales in provincial and outer-metropolitan areas could delay some projects. In a first-half 2026 press report, a company official said about KRW 330 billion of unstarted backlog would be recognized within the year.

The next checkpoints are how much construction-start revenue shows up in 2027 results and whether the plan's construction targets are met.

08

Bull factors

Order backlog recovery and more construction starts

The order backlog rose 49.0% to KRW 1.86 trillion at end-June 2026 from KRW 1.25 trillion at year-end 2025, as large cooperative sites such as Hwaseong Bibong and Yongin Yangji 2-block started construction.

The company said revenue from about KRW 1 trillion of sites started in the first half will be recognized from 2027. Unstarted backlog of KRW 1.9 trillion and signed project sites of KRW 3.1 trillion are cited as a future revenue base.

A long-standing net cash position

KIS Ratings noted the company has maintained a net cash position for an extended period. Consolidated debt-to-equity was 51.4% at end-March 2026 and was reported at 47.3% at end-June 2026. Cash and cash equivalents were reported to have risen to KRW 218.5 billion at end-2025. Contingent liabilities are a burden, but the borrowing structure is described as comparatively comfortable.

Expanded shareholder return plan

Press reports said the 2026–2028 corporate value enhancement plan includes paying 25% of net profit as dividends and additional share cancellation. The plan was also reported to target total dividends rising 10% a year.

The return plan shows the company's intent to maintain shareholder returns even while core earnings are weak. How much the dividend funding depends on financial asset gains is a point to verify in periodic reports.

Bear factors

Shrinking core revenue and profit

Revenue fell 25.3% to KRW 1.1 trillion in 2025, and first-half 2026 revenue was reported at KRW 392.2 billion, down 33.5%. Operating profit over the same period was reported at KRW 46.9 billion, down 47.9%.

The operating margin fell to 13.1% in 2025, and the third quarter of 2026 turned to an operating loss of KRW 32.6 billion. The speed at which the backlog converts into revenue is the key to earnings recovery.

Reliance on financial income and quarterly volatility

First-half 2026 net income of KRW 169.5 billion was reported to be supported by financial income of KRW 172.6 billion. Of that financial income, valuation gains on financial assets such as listed shares were KRW 137.8 billion.

In the third quarter of 2026 the company swung to a net loss attributable to controlling shareholders of KRW 120.6 billion, widening quarterly swings. Earnings that move separately from construction operations are cited as a weakness.

Cash flow strain and contingent liabilities

Operating cash flow turned to -KRW 3.75 billion in 2025 and was reported at -KRW 7.3 billion in the first half of 2026. Analysts say delayed collection of construction payments has increased receivables and short-term borrowings.

Actual guarantee payments were reported at about KRW 1.61 trillion, against a guarantee limit of about KRW 2.58 trillion. Other bad-debt expenses of KRW 88.4 billion in 2024 and KRW 42.7 billion in 2025 added to the burden.

10

Risk factors

Cooperative housing rules and sales market

The government is pursuing reforms to cooperative housing projects, and the effect on new projects and bidding competitiveness is not yet defined. Oversupply and weak sales in provincial and outer-metropolitan areas can delay projects.

An April 2026 press report cited views that Yamok Station Seohee Stahills faces a tough take-up given its non-transit location and high price. Weak member recruitment would delay construction starts and revenue recognition.

Governance and internal controls

A former vice president was involved in an embezzlement case in 2025, and the KRW 1.375 billion taken was reported as fully recovered in July 2025. The individual was sentenced to two years in prison at first instance in January 2026, and an appeal was reported to be under way.

An April 2026 press report covered the company being named in a special prosecutor probe and the start of a delisting review. The largest shareholder and related parties hold 59.8%, so governance effects on management remain a matter to track.

Contingent liabilities and construction costs

Maximum credit exposure from financial guarantees was KRW 1.76 trillion at end-March 2026. The guarantees include interim payments for cooperative members, bridge loans, and securitization-related guarantees.

In 2025 an estimated-cost revision was booked in civil works, and bad-debt charges on loans are recorded in KIS Ratings' data. Whether cost increases continue to be passed through in contract prices is the point to check.

12

Overall view

Seohee Construction grew revenue and operating profit in 2023 and 2024 on the back of cooperative-led construction, but in 2025 revenue fell to KRW 1.1 trillion and operating profit to KRW 144.4 billion.

In 2026 the order backlog recovered to KRW 1.86 trillion at end-June and large sites started construction, but revenue from them is scheduled to be recognized from 2027.

The third quarter of 2026 turned to an operating loss of KRW 32.6 billion and a net loss attributable to controlling shareholders of KRW 120.6 billion. Press reports said first-half 2026 net income was supported by financial asset valuation gains, so the link between core operations and profit is weak.

On the financial side, debt-to-equity has declined and a net cash structure has been maintained, supporting stability, but negative operating cash flow, rising receivables and guarantee exposure are burdens to watch. Shareholder returns are set to expand under the 2026–2028 plan.

Strengths include the backlog recovery and higher returns, while weaknesses include shrinking core operations, volatile financial gains and cash flow strain.

The next checkpoints are the operating margin and operating cash flow in the 2026 annual report and whether construction-start revenue appears in the first quarter of 2027.
