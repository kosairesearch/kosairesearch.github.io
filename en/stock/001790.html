02

Business structure

TS, founded in 1956, is a sugar company whose main segments are food, livestock distribution and feed, and it also runs golf course and restaurant operations through its subsidiaries.

Based on the 2025 annual business report, segment revenue was KRW 695.1 billion in food (51.2%), KRW 374.1 billion in livestock distribution (27.6%), KRW 235.2 billion in feed (17.4%) and KRW 51.9 billion in other businesses (3.8%).

Sugar is the core of the food segment, and in 2022 TS ranked first in the industry by sugar export value at KRW 110.4 billion, ahead of Samyang at KRW 108.3 billion and CJ Cheiljedang at KRW 68.3 billion.

In the same year, domestic sugar sales were led by CJ Cheiljedang at KRW 569.1 billion and Samyang at KRW 456.9 billion, with TS third at roughly KRW 286 billion. The livestock distribution segment focuses on imported meat and seafood distribution, with 2025 revenue of KRW 374.1 billion.

The feed business is run by a subsidiary that manufactures and sells compound feed. Overseas revenue was KRW 293 billion in 2025, roughly one-fifth of the total, and the remaining KRW 1.06 trillion came from domestic sales. Domestic utilization at the group's plants in 2024 was 94.0% for food and 89.3% for feed.

Past press reports noted that TS imports nearly all of its raw materials, including raw sugar, soybean meal and corn, which leaves it exposed to exchange rates and grain prices.

In domestic sugar, CJ Cheiljedang and Samyang are reported to hold higher market shares, and TS's mix of feed and livestock distribution alongside sugar is the defining feature of its business.

04

Earnings analysis

Consolidated revenue was KRW 1.38 trillion in 2022, the largest year in the period, and KRW 1.33 trillion in 2023, the smallest. Revenue then reached KRW 1.37 trillion in 2024, up 3.1% year on year, before falling 1.3% to KRW 1.36 trillion in 2025, so the top line has been flat.

Operating profit rose to KRW 47.2 billion in 2023, up 24.1%, fell to KRW 37 billion in 2024, down 21.6%, and then reached KRW 56.6 billion in 2025, up 52.9%, the largest year in the period.

The operating margin was 2.8% in 2022, 3.5% in 2023, 2.7% in 2024 and 4.2% in 2025, so the profitability measure was highest in 2025 even while sales stayed flat.

Net income attributable to controlling shareholders was KRW 25.5 billion in 2022, KRW 32.6 billion in 2023, up 27.5%, and KRW 20.5 billion in 2024, down 37.1%, before swinging to a net loss of KRW 60.4 billion in 2025.

Total net income, including non-controlling interests, was a net loss of KRW 60.3 billion in 2025, in the same direction. A net loss despite positive operating profit means that items below operating profit, such as non-operating gains and losses and income tax, weighed heavily.

Operating cash flow was KRW 11.4 billion in 2022, KRW 40 billion in 2023 and KRW 50.4 billion in 2024, then turned to -KRW 8.6 billion in 2025.

Quarterly operating profit was KRW 18.6 billion in Q3 2025 and KRW 15 billion in Q4 2025, down 19.7% from the prior quarter, when controlling-shareholder results swung to a net loss of KRW 118.6 billion.

Q1 2026 operating profit was KRW 5.49 billion, down 63.3% from the prior quarter, marking the low point, and controlling-shareholder net income of KRW 9.44 billion returned the company to profit.

Operating profit then rose to KRW 13.4 billion in Q2 2026, up 143.5%, and KRW 29.3 billion in Q3 2026, up 118.8% from the prior quarter and up 57.1% year on year, a two-quarter rise after the Q1 trough.

Q3 2026 revenue was KRW 348.1 billion, up only 0.6% year on year, so the profit recovery came mainly from profitability factors rather than sales growth.

Still, cumulative controlling-shareholder net losses over the last four quarters, from Q4 2025 to Q3 2026, total KRW 73 billion, with the large Q4 2025 loss pulling the total down.

05

Industry analysis

In domestic sugar, CJ Cheiljedang and Samyang sit at the top, and TS was third by sugar sales value in 2022. Past press coverage noted that raw materials account for roughly 90% of TS's manufacturing costs, so raw sugar and other input prices largely determine profitability.

Commentary on the 2025 improvement cites stable international grain prices, a lower cost ratio and cost reduction. In Q3 2026, CJ Cheiljedang said it cut sugar and flour prices in line with government price stabilization efforts, which reduced gross profit in its core ingredients segment by 9.0%.

In the same quarter, CJ Cheiljedang also cited higher raw material and logistics costs linked to high oil prices. Because sugar prices can be affected by policy, the same pressure could also appear in TS's sugar business.

Feed profitability depends on grain prices and compound feed volumes, and the segment ran at 89.3% utilization in 2024. Livestock distribution depends on imported meat prices and domestic livestock supply, and it accounted for 27.6% of 2025 revenue.

Against peers, TS ranks third by domestic sugar sales but ranked first by sugar export value in 2022, and that overseas sales position is cited as a strength. CJ Cheiljedang guided to an operating margin in the 5% range for Q4 2026, excluding Korean Air's logistics affiliate, which is a useful benchmark for the sector.

Outlook

In its 2024 annual business report, the company set out a direction of raising revenue and improving profitability through new customer acquisition, new product development and the search for new growth engines.

Revenue has been stuck in the KRW 1.3 trillion range since 2022, so whether new customers and new products translate into sales growth is the item to track. Operating profit rose for two consecutive quarters after the return to profit in Q1 2026, and whether the trend continues in Q4 2026 is the next checkpoint.

The absence of a repeat of the KRW 118.6 billion Q4 2025 net loss is indirectly supported by controlling-shareholder net income being positive in each quarter from Q1 to Q3 2026.

TS's Q4 2026 preliminary results are scheduled for release within Q1 2027, and the 2026 annual business report must be filed by March 31, 2027. That report will finalize full-year earnings, the dividend decision and the Q4 profit structure.

In food, whether sugar prices are adjusted under government price stabilization efforts is a variable that will shape gross profit. In feed, grain prices and compound feed volumes set the direction, and in livestock distribution, imported meat volumes and prices play that role.

With the debt-to-equity ratio up to 159.4%, the scale of borrowings and whether operating cash flow recovers are also items to monitor.

Bull factors

Operating profit recovery

Food segment and overseas sales base

The food segment is the largest business, with 2025 revenue of KRW 695.1 billion, or 51.2% of the total. TS ranked first by sugar export value in 2022 at KRW 110.4 billion. Overseas revenue in 2025 was KRW 293 billion, roughly one-fifth of the total.

Asset base and dividend profile

Total equity at the end of 2025 was KRW 483.9 billion, with KRW 482.4 billion attributable to controlling shareholders. The price-to-book ratio sits in a low range, and in past sector comparisons the dividend yield has tended to exceed the sector average. Operating cash flow reached KRW 50.4 billion in 2024, showing that the company has produced cash before.

Bear factors

Cumulative net losses

Controlling-shareholder results turned to a net loss of KRW 60.4 billion in 2025. The Q4 2025 controlling-shareholder net loss was KRW 118.6 billion, and cumulative net losses over the last four quarters total KRW 73 billion.

Even as operating profit rose, the bottom line has stayed negative, which points to a heavy burden below the operating line.

Weaker balance sheet and cash flow

The debt-to-equity ratio rose from 126.8% in 2022 to 159.4% in 2025, with total liabilities of KRW 771.3 billion. Operating cash flow fell from KRW 50.4 billion in 2024 to -KRW 8.6 billion in 2025. As borrowing grows, less of any earnings recovery may remain available to shareholders.

Flat revenue and quarterly volatility

Revenue has been essentially flat, moving from KRW 1.38 trillion in 2022 to KRW 1.36 trillion in 2025. Operating profit fell 63.3% from the prior quarter to KRW 5.49 billion in Q1 2026, showing large quarterly swings. Q3 2026 revenue rose only 0.6% year on year.

Risk factors

Raw materials and FX

TS imports nearly all of its raw materials, including raw sugar, soybean meal and corn, so grain prices and exchange rates feed directly into costs. Past press reports noted that export sales of around 20% leave the company exposed to a weaker won.

In Q3 2026, CJ Cheiljedang also cited raw material and logistics costs tied to high oil prices, which shows cost pressure across the sector.

Price policy

Under government price stabilization efforts, CJ Cheiljedang cut sugar and flour prices, and gross profit in its core ingredients segment fell 9.0%. If sugar prices are affected by policy, TS's food margins could face similar pressure. The food segment accounted for 51.2% of 2025 revenue, so the scope of exposure is not small.

Imported meat and livestock

The livestock distribution segment depends on imported meat prices, import volumes and domestic livestock supply, and its 2025 revenue of KRW 374.1 billion accounted for 27.6% of the total. If imported meat prices rise or domestic livestock supply shifts, distribution margins and trading volumes could both be affected.

The segment affects earnings through a different channel from sugar price policy. Swings in imported meat markets could therefore add another variable to the overall revenue trend.
