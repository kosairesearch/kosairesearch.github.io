02

Business structure

Baiksan (035150) is a synthetic leather manufacturer listed on the KOSPI market on August 11, 1999, and had 215 employees as of June 30, 2026. Its core product is synthetic leather for sports footwear, supplied to global brands including NIKE and ADIDAS.

FnGuide's business summary states that the company holds a 25% share of the market for global brands such as NIKE, ADIDAS and REEBOK and supplies more than 80% of its revenue to them. By segment, based on 2026 Q2 revenue, synthetic leather accounted for 77%, apparel 21% and investment 2%.

Other company information sites use a different breakdown, showing product sales at 78% and merchandise sales at 15%, so segment shares vary by source.

Automotive interior materials are presented as a growing market, and the 2026 value-up plan also names expanded automotive interior sales as a driver of top-line growth.

On the production side, the company said a new plant in Indonesia would expand capacity, while it plans to raise the share of high value-added products and cut costs through operating efficiency.

Unlike petrochemical base-material producers such as Lotte Chemical and Kumho Petro Chemical, Baiksan supplies materials built around footwear and automotive supply chains, where brand qualification and long-term supply relationships are central to winning orders.

04

Earnings analysis

On a disclosed basis, annual revenue fell from KRW 475.9 billion in 2022 to KRW 417.7 billion in 2023 (down 12.2% year on year), then rose to KRW 497 billion in 2024 (up 19.0%) and KRW 504.9 billion in 2025 (up 1.6%), increasing for two consecutive years from 2024 to 2025. 2025 was the largest revenue year in 2022 to 2025, and 2023 was the smallest.

Operating profit rose from KRW 50.3 billion in 2022 to KRW 53.1 billion in 2023 (up 5.7%) and KRW 76.5 billion in 2024 (up 44.0%), then fell to KRW 53.6 billion in 2025 (down 30.0%). 2024 was the largest operating profit year in the period.

The operating margin climbed from 10.6% in 2022 to 15.4% in 2024 before returning to 10.6% in 2025, so the revenue peak in 2025 did not coincide with the profit peak in 2024.

Net income attributable to controlling shareholders was KRW 45.3 billion in 2022, KRW 41.3 billion in 2023 (down 8.9%), KRW 60.7 billion in 2024 (up 47.1%) and KRW 33.5 billion in 2025 (down 44.8%).

Net income including non-controlling interests was KRW 33.2 billion in 2025, a different figure that should be read separately from net income attributable to controlling shareholders. Operating cash flow was KRW 51.6 billion in 2022, KRW 46.3 billion in 2023, KRW 56.2 billion in 2024 and KRW 38.4 billion in 2025.

The debt-to-equity ratio, calculated as total liabilities divided by total equity, was 88.1% in 2022, 68.8% in 2023, 82.9% in 2024 and 71.4% in 2025. At year-end 2025, total equity was KRW 260.6 billion, of which KRW 258.6 billion was attributable to controlling shareholders, and total liabilities were KRW 186 billion.

Quarterly revenue fell from KRW 134.4 billion in 2025 Q3 to KRW 114.2 billion in Q4 (down 15.0% quarter on quarter), then peaked at KRW 130.1 billion in 2026 Q1 (up 13.9%).

It then declined to KRW 126.5 billion in 2026 Q2 (down 2.8%) and KRW 122.5 billion in 2026 Q3 (down 3.2% quarter on quarter and down 8.9% year on year), two consecutive quarterly declines.

Quarterly operating profit was KRW 12.9 billion in 2025 Q3, KRW 7.43 billion in Q4 (down 42.4%), KRW 12.7 billion in 2026 Q1 (up 70.5%), KRW 16.3 billion in Q2 (up 29.0%) and KRW 9.04 billion in Q3 (down 44.6% quarter on quarter and down 29.8% year on year).

The 2026 Q2 operating profit was the highest of the five quarters. Net income attributable to controlling shareholders rebounded to KRW 13 billion in 2026 Q1, then fell to KRW 9.07 billion in Q2 and turned to a net loss of KRW 2.2 billion in Q3.

The four quarters from 2025 Q4 to 2026 Q3 total KRW 24.4 billion in net income attributable to controlling shareholders. A securities data service summarized the 2026 Q1 results as declines in revenue and profitability due to weaker demand for sports footwear synthetic leather.

Quarterly cost ratios and the composition of non-operating items are matters to confirm in the periodic report text.

05

Industry analysis

Baiksan's core demand comes from the sports footwear materials market, which moves with the footwear volumes of global brands. A securities data service summarizing the 2026 Q1 results pointed to weaker demand for sports footwear synthetic leather as the cause of lower revenue and profitability.

The pattern of revenue peaking in 2025 and then declining in 2026 Q2 and Q3 suggests that the demand slowdown is showing up in quarterly results. By contrast, FnGuide's business summary describes the automotive interior market as expanding.

Whether automotive interior growth can offset weaker footwear demand is the key test of the company's position within the sector.

Among chemical companies, Hanwha Solutions, SKC and LG Chem serve different end markets such as solar, films and batteries, while Baiksan is tied to a narrower set of footwear and automotive applications. That concentration helps when end-demand is strong and becomes a risk when it weakens.

Raw material prices and exchange rates are general variables that affect synthetic leather costs. If the global share figure holds, Baiksan is also a supplier that feels changes in brand order volumes quickly.

The timing of any industry recovery should be checked against brand order flows and the quarterly revenue trend, since the available information does not establish the pace of recovery.

06

Outlook

In its value-up plan disclosed on March 27, 2026, the company said it would pursue top-line growth through higher sales of synthetic leather for automotive interiors and the start-up of new production sites.

Under the plan, the company will step up sales efforts to win new automotive interior customers and expand capacity through the new Indonesian plant. It also plans to grow high value-added product lines and cut costs through operating efficiency.

On shareholder returns, the annual general meeting approved a cash dividend proposal.

The company acquired 281,312 treasury shares during the acquisition period from January 26 to April 24, 2026, for a total acquisition cost of KRW 36.8 billion, and it later decided to cancel the same number of shares, reducing the number of shares issued.

Because revenue declined in both 2026 Q2 and Q3, the key checkpoint is whether automotive interiors and the new plant can offset weaker footwear volumes. With operating profit falling to KRW 9.04 billion in 2026 Q3, the utilization rate of the new plant and its start-up costs also bear watching for margins.

The plan indicates direction, but no specific revenue targets or guidance figures appear in the material reviewed. The operating margin reported in the next periodic report will show how far profitability has recovered from the 2025 level.

Shareholder returns and a stronger balance sheet

Bear factors

Quarterly earnings slowdown and a return to loss

Revenue declined in both 2026 Q2 and Q3, two consecutive quarters. Operating profit fell to KRW 9.04 billion in 2026 Q3, down 44.6% quarter on quarter, and the company posted a net loss attributable to controlling shareholders of KRW 2.2 billion.

The four quarters from 2025 Q4 to 2026 Q3 total KRW 24.4 billion in net income attributable to controlling shareholders, compared with KRW 60.7 billion in 2024.

Weaker sports footwear demand and customer concentration

A securities data service summarizing the 2026 Q1 results attributed the decline in revenue and profitability to weaker demand for sports footwear synthetic leather. Key customers are global brands such as NIKE and ADIDAS, so changes in brand orders flow directly into revenue. If footwear demand does not recover, growth in automotive interiors may struggle to offset the decline.

The 2025 profit drop and slower cash generation

Operating profit fell 30.0% to KRW 53.6 billion in 2025, and net income attributable to controlling shareholders fell 44.8% to KRW 33.5 billion. Operating cash flow fell to KRW 38.4 billion in 2025 from KRW 56.2 billion in 2024.

Profit fell even though revenue reached its highest level in 2025, which makes the cost structure and cost burden points to examine.

Risk factors

Customer and demand concentration risk

A large part of revenue depends on demand for footwear materials from global sports brands such as NIKE and ADIDAS. Reduced brand orders or shifts in production locations could hit quarterly revenue quickly. Whether the two consecutive quarterly declines in 2026 reflect this risk materializing needs to be confirmed.

Start-up risk at the new overseas plant

The plan to expand capacity through the new Indonesian plant depends on utilization and start-up costs for margins. Delays or low utilization could raise depreciation and fixed-cost burdens and pull down margins. New customer wins also need to materialize for the plant investment to pay off.

Cost, currency and financial risk

Synthetic leather costs are exposed to raw material prices and exchange rates. Because the company posted a net loss attributable to controlling shareholders in 2026 Q3, non-operating costs and detailed cost drivers should be confirmed in the periodic report.

The debt-to-equity ratio fell to 71.4% at year-end 2025, but total liabilities of KRW 186 billion remain a item to monitor against equity.

Overall view

Baiksan recorded its largest revenue year in 2022 to 2025 at KRW 504.9 billion in 2025, but operating profit fell from its 2024 peak of KRW 76.5 billion to KRW 53.6 billion in 2025.

Quarterly revenue peaked at KRW 130.1 billion in 2026 Q1 and then declined for two consecutive quarters, and 2026 Q3 operating profit of KRW 9.04 billion and a net loss attributable to controlling shareholders of KRW 2.2 billion marked a swing to loss.

Bullish factors include the revenue base, growth plans centered on automotive interiors and the new Indonesian plant, shareholder returns through treasury share activity and cash dividends, and an improved debt-to-equity ratio.

Bearish factors include weaker sports footwear demand, customer concentration among global brands, the quarterly earnings slowdown and loss, and start-up risk at the new plant. These factors should be weighed equally, and the available information does not settle the direction.

The next checkpoints are the operating margin in the 2026 annual business report and whether revenue declines stop in the 2027 Q1 results. The valuation reflects a price-to-book ratio below 1x alongside four-quarter earnings below the 2024 level, and its interpretation depends on whether earnings recover.
