02

Business structure

Sambu Construction was founded in 1948 and holds the first-grade license for civil engineering and building construction. The business mix in a Saramin company profile lists civil engineering at 56.4%, building at 20.2% and other at 23%, so public civil works form the core.

Its clients are mainly public agencies such as the Korea Rail Network Authority, the Korea Expressway Corporation and the Iksan Regional Land Management Office.

The Q3 2025 quarterly report listed the Honam High-Speed Railway Phase 2 Section 7 subgrade project at 53.50% progress and a Muju road widening project at 48.95% progress.

Following a fatal-accident-related business suspension, new orders in the civil engineering and construction license category were restricted until November 9, 2026, and that business accounted for 97.8% of 2025 revenue, according to press reports.

The order backlog stood at KRW 430.2 billion, but about 60% of it was reported as halted or not yet started. The company plans to resume bidding for public contracts after the rehabilitation proceedings end and its credit rating recovers.

The structural strength is its public civil works base, while the main weakness is that this base has currently stalled in terms of new orders.

04

Earnings analysis

Annual revenue peaked at KRW 575 billion in 2023, up 31.8% from the prior year, and then fell 19.6% to KRW 462.4 billion in 2024 and 76.0% to KRW 111 billion in 2025, a decline in two straight years.

Operating losses were KRW 81.9 billion in 2022, KRW 78.2 billion in 2023, KRW 112.6 billion in 2024 and KRW 26.6 billion in 2025, which means the company stayed in the red for four consecutive years, with the loss widening in 2024 and narrowing in 2025.

The operating margin moved from -13.6% in 2023 to -24.3% in 2024 and -24.0% in 2025, so the loss rate stayed high while revenue shrank. Net loss attributable to owners was KRW 90.4 billion in 2022, KRW 110.5 billion in 2023, KRW 127.7 billion in 2024 and KRW 76.7 billion in 2025.

Net loss including non-controlling interests was KRW 127.8 billion in 2024 and KRW 76.7 billion in 2025, and it should be read separately from the owner-attributable figure.

The debt-to-equity ratio climbed to 3,817.1% in 2024, and total equity at the end of 2025 was negative KRW 142.1 billion, meaning the company was fully capital-impaired.

Operating cash flow was -KRW 61.5 billion in 2022 and -KRW 107.1 billion in 2023, then turned positive at KRW 93.2 billion in 2024 and KRW 21.9 billion in 2025. Because this runs against the operating losses, the reason cannot be explained from the disclosed results alone.

On a quarterly basis, Q3 2025 revenue of KRW 39.1 billion and operating profit of KRW 14.9 billion gave way to Q4 2025 revenue of KRW 25.3 billion, down 35.3% quarter on quarter, and operating profit of KRW 18.2 billion, up 22.3%.

Q1 2026 revenue was KRW 15.7 billion, down 37.8% quarter on quarter, with an operating loss of KRW 1.15 billion, a swing back into the red. Q2 2026 revenue rose 39.6% to KRW 22 billion, but the operating loss widened to KRW 6.95 billion.

Q3 2026 revenue was KRW 19.4 billion, and the operating loss narrowed to KRW 2 billion. Q2 2026 net profit attributable to owners of KRW 176.4 billion reflects, according to press reports, a debt-forgiveness gain of KRW 206.5 billion recognized under the court-approved plan.

That item differs in nature from the operating result, and the four-quarter net profit total of KRW 155.8 billion also leans heavily on this Q2 gain.

05

Industry analysis

The market backdrop is dominated by a real estate downturn and a contraction in project financing (PF), according to press reports. The company said PF contingent liabilities materialized and liquidity deteriorated sharply.

In February 2025, rising raw material prices and a construction slowdown pushed it into a liquidity crisis that led to its rehabilitation filing. Its concentration in public civil works may make it less exposed to the housing and PF segments, but bidding eligibility for public contracts has to be restored first.

Its competitive position has also weakened, with its construction capability ranking falling from 78th in 2025 to outside the top 100 in 2026. On cost control, the company plans to tighten cost management on new orders, monitor projected completion costs for each site monthly and strengthen pre-screening of new orders.

PF-linked completion guarantees and liquidity support pledges will go through prior review and board approval. The current setup is one where the company's own funding capacity and order eligibility matter more than the direction of end-market demand in deciding how quickly operations can normalize.

06

Outlook

The company has outlined three routes to normalization. First, most of the KRW 33 billion investment proceeds are used to repay rehabilitation debt, which is meant to end the capital impairment and improve the capital structure.

Second, press reports said the largest shareholder, Rebuild Sambu Holdings, will support working capital and unpaid wages through rights offerings or loans.

Third, in August 2026 the company received a financial support letter capped at KRW 14 billion from the acquirer side, but because the providers are the acquirer and its funding sources, actual cash injections are seen as the key issue.

The auditor said support to the company could change depending on whether the maturity of the acquirer-side borrowings is extended and whether the additional funding providers follow through.

On the business side, the company aims to secure a stable public civil works base and expand new orders with an eye to profitability.

On governance, it is dissolving the PEF and bringing in a strategic investor (SI) to change the largest shareholder, with plans to win new projects through business cooperation with the SI.

On listing, the company was referred to the KRX corporate review committee in October 2026, and the central question was whether it can continue as a going concern. Its next periodic report is the 2026 annual report, which will show whether the operating loss trend continues and how far total equity recovers.

08

Bull factors

Capital restructuring under the approved plan

The Seoul Rehabilitation Court approved the rehabilitation plan in June 2026, and most of the KRW 33 billion investment is reported to repay rehabilitation debt. Press reports said a debt-forgiveness gain of KRW 206.5 billion under the approved plan lifted total equity to KRW 76 billion by the end of June 2026.

The exchange's move to a substantive review after deeming the capital impairment cause resolved also reads as progress in the capital restructuring.

Narrowing operating losses

The operating loss shrank from KRW 112.6 billion in 2024 to KRW 26.6 billion in 2025. On a quarterly basis, the operating loss narrowed from KRW 6.95 billion in Q2 2026 to KRW 2 billion in Q3 2026.

Press reports put first-half 2026 revenue at KRW 37.7 billion and an operating loss of KRW 8.1 billion, and the narrower Q3 loss could be read as an early improvement signal.

Plan to rebuild public civil works orders

The company said it aims to secure a stable public civil works base and expand new orders with an eye to profitability. It also proposed forming a cost innovation team, monitoring projected completion costs for each site monthly and tightening pre-screening of new orders.

Its contract history with public clients such as the Korea Rail Network Authority and the Korea Expressway Corporation is cited as a business foundation.

09

Bear factors

Shrinking revenue and consecutive operating losses

Revenue fell 76.0% to KRW 111 billion in 2025, and Q3 2026 revenue of KRW 19.4 billion was down 50.4% from the year-earlier quarter. The company posted operating losses in each of the three quarters from Q1 to Q3 2026.

The operating margin stood at -24.0% in 2025, so the business remains in loss territory, and the pace of revenue recovery is the open question.

Order gap and business suspension

A business suspension restricted new orders until November 9, 2026, and that business accounted for 97.8% of 2025 revenue. About 60% of the KRW 430.2 billion backlog was reported as halted or not yet started.

The construction capability ranking fell from 78th to outside the top 100, which puts the company at a disadvantage in bidding.

Listing status and going-concern uncertainty

The auditor said material uncertainty exists that could raise significant doubt about the company's ability to continue as a going concern. The main issue before the KRX committee was also whether the company can continue as a going concern.

Whether the new largest shareholder has the funds and whether borrowings are extended remain variables for financial support.

10

Risk factors

New order cost risk

The company plans to expand new orders with an eye to profitability. However, if the monthly monitoring of projected completion costs by site and the pre-screening of new orders do not work effectively, it could take on construction projects expected to incur losses.

The cost innovation team also has yet to prove its effect in practice. If construction losses are recognized late, after work starts, the recovery in core earnings could slow.

Liquidity and funding risk

At the end of Q1 2026, consolidated current liabilities were KRW 291.5 billion, more than five times current assets of KRW 51 billion. Borrowings matured on September 30, 2026, and whether the KRW 14 billion financial support commitment from the acquirer side is actually drawn remains the key question.

Because the new largest shareholder is the main source of working capital and unpaid wage support, cash flow uncertainty persists.

Legal and governance risk

Former executives are on trial on allegations that they inflated publicity around a 2023 Ukraine reconstruction MOU and then sold shares for alleged illicit gains of KRW 36.9 billion, according to press reports. A first-instance verdict had not been issued as of the reports.

The outcome could affect assessments of management transparency, and PF-linked completion guarantees remain a burden on operations.

Overall view

Sambu Construction posted operating losses from 2022 through 2025, and revenue peaked at KRW 575 billion in 2023 before falling to KRW 111 billion in 2025. Operating losses continued in each quarter from Q1 to Q3 2026, but the Q3 loss of KRW 2 billion was smaller than the Q2 loss of KRW 6.95 billion.

Press reports said the court-approved plan, the KRW 33 billion investment and the debt-forgiveness gain began reversing the capital impairment. The order gap, however, remains clear, with about 60% of the backlog reported as halted or not yet started and the business suspension constraining the revenue base.

Listing status and trading resumption depend, according to press reports, on the KRX review and on whether the new largest shareholder delivers funding. Earnings-based multiples are hard to compare because the debt-forgiveness gain dominates the recent four-quarter profit.

The 2026 annual report, the next periodic report, will show the operating loss trend and whether total equity holds its recovery.
