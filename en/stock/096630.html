02

Business structure

S Connect is a metal parts manufacturer founded in 2000 and listed on KOSDAQ in 2007. According to a financial information portal, the wireless business segment, which covers metal parts for mobile phones, accounts for most of its revenue.

Its core technology is press processing of metal sheets with molds to produce exterior and interior parts. Production sites are in Hwaseong, Eumseong and Ansan in Korea, plus Dongguan in China and Bac Ninh in Vietnam. One new business is battery components.

Using its metal exterior parts capability, the company supplies battery current interrupt devices (CID) to Samsung SDI, a key battery component, as reported by the press. Another is hydrogen.

Through an equity stake and manufacturing exclusivity tied to a Silicon Valley company, it is pursuing plasma carbon conversion equipment that cuts greenhouse gases and produces clean hydrogen.

Its lithium primary battery parts remain in the portfolio, and the company profile links demand for these parts to the trend toward portable electronics. Samsung Electronics is a long-standing metal parts partner. Because results track smartphone sales by its major customers, customer concentration is high.

In 2024 the company experienced a lithium primary battery fire, and it has reported process improvements and safety reinforcement to restore trust.

Among industry peers such as Samsung Electro-Mechanics and LG Innotek, which focus on different product lines, S Connect is closer to a small and mid-sized parts maker centered on metal processing and battery metal parts.

04

Earnings analysis

On an annual basis, sales rose from KRW 200.4 billion in 2022 to KRW 275.3 billion in 2023 (up 37.4% year on year) and KRW 291.9 billion in 2024 (up 6.0%), then fell to KRW 278.7 billion in 2025 (down 4.5%). Among 2022-2025, 2024 was the largest sales year and 2022 the smallest.

Operating profit was KRW 5.82 billion in 2022, KRW 7.97 billion in 2023 (up 36.8%), KRW 4.09 billion in 2024 (down 48.7%) and KRW 13.3 billion in 2025 (up 224.6%), making 2025 the largest operating profit year in the period. The operating margin rose from 1.4% in 2024 to 4.8% in 2025.

The company said that sales fell slightly due to changes in sales mix and the exclusion of a subsidiary from consolidation, but operating profit and net profit grew sharply on a restructuring focused on profitability and cost reduction.

Net profit attributable to controlling shareholders moved from a KRW 4.13 billion profit in 2022 to a net loss of KRW 1.04 billion in 2023, widened to a net loss of KRW 25.7 billion in 2024, and returned to a profit of KRW 35.8 billion in 2025.

Net profit including non-controlling interests was KRW 35.7 billion in 2025, which is a different figure. Operating cash flow grew from KRW 9.12 billion in 2022 to KRW 18.5 billion in 2025. The quarterly picture moved in the other direction.

From KRW 66.8 billion in sales, KRW 3.29 billion in operating profit and KRW 3.63 billion in net profit attributable to controlling shareholders in the third quarter of 2025, the fourth quarter of 2025 posted sales of KRW 64.9 billion (down 2.9% quarter on quarter), operating profit of KRW 4.15 billion (up 26.1%) and net profit attributable to controlling shareholders of KRW 21.9 billion (up 504.2%).

First-quarter 2026 sales were KRW 58.1 billion, with operating profit of KRW 339 million (down 91.8% quarter on quarter). Second-quarter 2026 sales were KRW 38.1 billion (down 34.5%) and the company recorded an operating loss of KRW 2.57 billion.

Third-quarter 2026 sales were KRW 36.7 billion (down 3.6% quarter on quarter and down 45.1% year on year) with an operating loss of KRW 3.6 billion.

Sales have declined for four consecutive quarters from the fourth quarter of 2025 through the third quarter of 2026, operating losses have continued for two consecutive quarters from the second quarter of 2026, and net losses attributable to controlling shareholders have continued for three consecutive quarters from the first quarter of 2026.

The four-quarter total of net profit attributable to controlling shareholders is KRW 11.6 billion, and the fourth quarter of 2025 accounts for most of it. That quarter's net profit far exceeded its operating profit, which points to a large non-operating contribution.

05

Industry analysis

Demand for smartphone metal parts is shifting toward premium products such as hinge components for foldable smartphones. In its explanation for the first half of 2025, the company said that foldable innovation products were raising demand for high-end metal parts.

The company profile also notes that demand for lithium primary batteries is rising as portable electronics spread. On the battery side, the company profile cites a forecast that the lithium-ion battery market will grow as electric vehicles expand.

The company says it is pursuing battery production line investment and process optimization based on precision mold technology. Where the sector sits in its cycle depends heavily on smartphone sales.

When major customers' sales were strong, results were supported, so finished-product shipments largely determine the direction of revenue. The quarterly decline through 2026 should be read against that shipment trend. Competitive positioning falls into three parts.

First, a long partnership with Samsung Electronics has built a record of supplying smartphone metal exterior parts. Second, supplying Samsung SDI has brought entry into battery components.

Third, large peers in the same electronics and components sector, such as Samsung Electro-Mechanics and LG Innotek, have different core products and customer bases, which limits direct comparison. The battery component market is affected by supply chain restructuring among vehicle makers and battery cell companies.

Operating production sites in Dongguan, China and Bac Ninh, Vietnam gives it a footprint that can respond to shifts in customers' production locations.

Bull factors

Profit Recovery and Stronger Cash Generation in 2025

Operating profit in 2025 rose 224.6% year on year to KRW 13.3 billion, lifting the operating margin to 4.8%. Operating cash flow also grew to KRW 18.5 billion from KRW 10.9 billion in 2024. The company attributed the gain to a profitability-focused restructuring and cost reduction. Whether the swing to profit holds beyond 2025 can be judged from later annual reports.

Diversification Beyond Smartphones

The company supplies battery current interrupt devices to Samsung SDI, entering the battery component space. It is also pursuing clean hydrogen production using plasma carbon conversion equipment.

Because these businesses build on its metal processing capability, they could reduce reliance on a single smartphone product line. However, their revenue scale is not separately disclosed in the filings.

Balance Sheet Improvement and Price Relative to Net Assets

The debt ratio fell from 263.3% in 2024 to 92.4% in 2025. Total equity at the end of 2025 was KRW 88.7 billion against total liabilities of KRW 82 billion. Market capitalization is below net assets, placing the price-to-book ratio under 1 times. With more financial room, the base for business investment and process improvement is wider.

Bear factors

Four Consecutive Quarters of Sales Decline

Sales have declined for four consecutive quarters from the fourth quarter of 2025 through the third quarter of 2026. Third-quarter 2026 sales of KRW 36.7 billion were down 45.1% year on year.

The drop from KRW 58.1 billion in the first quarter to KRW 38.1 billion in the second, a 34.5% quarterly decline, was particularly steep. Whether this decline persists within a revenue structure tied to major customers' smartphone sales is the main bearish concern.

Operating Losses and Three Quarters of Net Losses

Operating losses were KRW 2.57 billion in the second quarter of 2026 and KRW 3.6 billion in the third quarter, so operating profit has been negative for two consecutive quarters. The third quarter moved from a profit in the same quarter of 2025 to a loss.

Net losses attributable to controlling shareholders have continued for three consecutive quarters from the first quarter of 2026, with the third-quarter loss at KRW 4.26 billion. The trend raises questions about whether the 2025 swing to profit reflects a lasting earnings base.

Reliance on One-Off Gains and Unclear New Business Profitability

The four-quarter total of KRW 11.6 billion depends heavily on the KRW 21.9 billion recorded in the fourth quarter of 2025. That quarter's net profit far exceeded operating profit, meaning non-operating items played a large role.

Revenue and profitability of the hydrogen and battery businesses are not separately disclosed in the filings. Whether these new businesses have grown large enough to offset declines in the core business cannot yet be seen in the numbers.

Risk factors

Customer Concentration and Smartphone Demand

Revenue from smartphone metal parts moves with the sales of major customers including Samsung Electronics. When finished-product shipments fall, volumes and capacity utilization can both be affected. The four consecutive quarterly sales declines through 2026 are consistent with this structural sensitivity. The pace at which the customer base diversifies will determine how large this risk becomes.

New Business Execution and Safety Record

The company experienced a lithium primary battery fire in 2024 and is carrying out process improvements and safety reinforcement. Revenue contribution from battery current interrupt devices and the plasma hydrogen business is not yet visible in the filings.

Delays in commercialization or a heavier facility investment burden could push back the timing of a profitability recovery.

Financial Capacity and Continued Losses

The debt ratio fell to 92.4% at the end of 2025, but operating and net losses attributable to controlling shareholders have continued through the third quarter of 2026. If losses persist, financial capacity against KRW 88.7 billion in equity could gradually narrow.

With operating cash flow at KRW 18.5 billion in 2025, the cash generation after the swing to losses is an important point to track.
