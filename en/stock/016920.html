02

Business structure

Cas (016920) was founded in 1983 and listed on KOSDAQ in 1989 as a maker of electronic scales and load cells. Its core business covers the manufacture and sale of electronic scales and load cells, with commercial electronic scales, load cells and label paper presented as its business divisions.

A company profile source states that Cas maintains a share of about 70% of the total domestic electronic scale market.

As computers and scales become networked in a digitally converged environment, the industrial application market is growing, and demand for factory automation products such as checkweighers and automatic packaging machines is widening.

In 2024 the company added a medical device sales business to diversify, and it positions itself as a measurement specialist offering total solutions.

In its Q3 2024 results commentary, revenue fell in the commercial scale, load cell and label paper divisions, but operating profit rose because raw material prices declined, which shows cost structure driving profit more than sales volume.

Consolidated revenue fell from KRW 168.4 billion in 2022 to KRW 156 billion in 2023 and 2024, then recovered to KRW 161.1 billion in 2025, up 3.2% year on year. The business has settled in a range of roughly KRW 156 billion to KRW 161.1 billion over the last three years.

04

Earnings analysis

Cas's annual earnings peaked in 2023 and have declined since. Consolidated revenue fell from KRW 168.4 billion in 2022 to KRW 156 billion in 2023, a 7.3% decrease, held at KRW 156 billion in 2024 with no change, and rose to KRW 161.1 billion in 2025, up 3.2%.

Operating profit went from KRW 4.39 billion in 2022 to KRW 4.96 billion in 2023, a 13.0% increase, then fell to KRW 3.61 billion in 2024, down 27.2%, and to KRW 2.01 billion in 2025, down 44.2%, so it declined for two consecutive years from 2024 through 2025.

The operating margin was 2.6% in 2022, 3.2% in 2023, 2.3% in 2024 and 1.3% in 2025, the lowest of the four years.

The company attributed the 2025 profit decline, despite higher revenue, to rising prices and labor costs and higher bad debt write-offs that lifted selling and administrative expenses, along with lower foreign currency translation gains that reduced non-operating income.

Net income attributable to controlling shareholders went from KRW 2.74 billion in 2022 to KRW 1.73 billion in 2023, down 36.7%, then to KRW 1.31 billion in 2024, down 24.4%, and turned into a net loss of KRW 1.66 billion in 2025. Net loss for 2025 was also KRW 1.66 billion.

Total liabilities at the end of 2025 were KRW 97.5 billion and total equity was KRW 68.1 billion, and the debt-to-equity ratio fell from 184.3% in 2022 to 143.2% in 2025. Operating cash flow was KRW 5.71 billion in 2025, up from KRW 3.47 billion in 2024.

On a quarterly basis, Cas posted an operating loss of KRW 1.03 billion in Q3 2025, returned to operating profit of KRW 1.81 billion in Q4 2025, saw operating profit fall 97.7% to KRW 40.91 million in Q1 2026, rose to KRW 2.31 billion in Q2 2026, and returned to an operating loss of KRW 40.58 million in Q3 2026. Quarterly revenue over the last five quarters ranged from KRW 36.3 billion to KRW 47 billion.

05

Industry analysis

Demand from downstream markets is tilting toward factory automation and industrial weighing equipment.

As a digitally converged environment spreads and computers and scales are networked, the industrial application market is growing, and demand for equipment such as checkweighers and automatic packaging machines is increasing. This trend may move on a different axis from the cyclicality of commercial weighing equipment.

Data that would pin down where the industry sits in its cycle is not available within the filed results. Cas is described as holding about 70% of the domestic electronic scale market, a strength of its domestic-centered structure, though limited growth in the overall market could constrain expansion.

On costs, Q3 2024 showed operating profit rising as raw material prices fell, while in 2025 higher prices and labor costs raised selling expenses. Thus the direction of raw material and labor costs shapes the margin. Foreign currency translation gains and losses have also moved non-operating results.

The fall in the operating margin from 2.6% in 2022 to 1.3% in 2025 reflects both industry conditions and cost factors working together.

Outlook

The most recent capital action Cas has disclosed is a treasury share purchase. A November 2026 filing shows the company will directly buy 493,583 common shares on the open market for about KRW 500 million, with an expected acquisition period from November 12, 2026 to February 11, 2027.

The brokerage commissioned is Shinhan Investment Corp., and the stated purpose is stabilizing the share price and raising shareholder value. The filing notes the amount acquired may vary with share price movements.

The acquisition limit was calculated within the distributable profit ceiling under the Commercial Act as of the end of the prior fiscal year. On the business side, growth in industrial application demand is the main growth axis.

On earnings, operating profit alternated between a profit in Q2 2026 and a loss in Q3 2026, so the earnings base is not yet stable. The next confirmation point is the 2026 annual report, which will include Q4 and full-year results, with a statutory filing deadline of March 31, 2027.

Because the 2025 full-year preliminary results were disclosed on February 27, 2026, a similar preliminary disclosure for 2026 could come around late February 2027. Q1 2027 results may come in mid-May, as Q1 2026 results did. The key indicator to track is whether the operating margin improves from 1.3% in 2025.

08

Bull factors

Demonstrated quarterly profit rebound

Cas posted operating profit of KRW 2.31 billion in Q2 2026, up sharply from KRW 40.91 million in Q1 2026, and returned to net income attributable to controlling shareholders of KRW 1.24 billion. It also turned to operating profit of KRW 1.81 billion in Q4 2025.

These quarters show that earnings can recover on a quarterly basis. Because Q3 2026 returned to a loss, whether the recovery holds must be confirmed in the next quarterly results.

Growth in industrial application demand

As a digitally converged environment spreads and computers are networked with scales, the industrial application market is growing. Demand for factory automation products such as checkweighers and automatic packaging machines is rising.

A reported share of about 70% in the domestic electronic scale market gives Cas a base for this growth. Whether industrial demand can offset slowing commercial equipment sales is the point to watch.

Improving balance sheet

The debt-to-equity ratio fell from 184.3% in 2022 to 143.2% in 2025. Total equity at the end of 2025 was KRW 68.1 billion. Cash generation held up during a period of falling profit, which matters for financial stability.

Bear factors

Shrinking operating margin

The operating margin fell from 2.6% in 2022 and 3.2% in 2023 to 2.3% in 2024 and 1.3% in 2025. Operating profit declined for two consecutive years in 2024 and 2025. In 2025, operating profit fell 44.2% even though revenue rose 3.2%, so revenue growth did not reach the bottom line.

Continued losses in recent quarters

Cas posted an operating loss of KRW 40.58 million in Q3 2026, and its net loss attributable to controlling shareholders widened to KRW 2.14 billion year on year. The company swung to a full-year net loss of KRW 1.66 billion in 2025. Even with profitable quarters, a sustained recovery in earnings has not yet been shown.

Flat revenue

Q3 2026 revenue of KRW 36.3 billion fell 7.8% from the prior quarter and 0.8% from a year earlier. Annual revenue rose modestly from KRW 156 billion in 2023 and 2024 to KRW 161.1 billion in 2025. Growth has been limited, which makes it harder to absorb cost pressure through sales. Quarterly revenue has stayed below KRW 40 billion in recent quarters.

10

Risk factors

Cost structure risk

In 2025, selling and administrative expenses rose on higher prices, labor costs and bad debt write-offs. The Q3 2024 results also showed that profit moves with raw material prices. If labor and raw material costs move against the company at the same time, the operating margin could fall further.

Currency and non-operating swings

In 2025, lower foreign currency translation gains cut non-operating income and helped push pre-tax income into a loss. Non-operating items can move results sharply depending on the direction of exchange rates. Even with operating profit, unfavorable non-operating items can produce a net loss.

Receivables and small-cap volatility

Higher bad debt write-offs were cited as one reason for the 2025 cost increase. If receivables collection worsens, both profit and cash flow face pressure. As a small-cap KOSDAQ stock, Cas can also see large swings in trading volume and share price.

Overall view

Cas's operating profit has declined since peaking in 2023, and in 2025 it swung to a net loss attributable to controlling shareholders of KRW 1.66 billion. The 1.3% operating margin in 2025 was the lowest in the reported period.

Q2 2026 returned to operating profit of KRW 2.31 billion and net income attributable to controlling shareholders of KRW 1.24 billion, but Q3 2026 returned to an operating loss of KRW 40.58 million. The net loss attributable to controlling shareholders over the last four quarters totals KRW 1.38 billion.

On the positive side are the improved balance sheet, with the debt-to-equity ratio falling from 184.3% to 143.2%, growth in industrial application demand, and the treasury share purchase decision.

On the negative side are the shrinking operating margin, flat revenue, cost pressure from prices, labor and bad debts, and swings in foreign currency translation.

The next checkpoints are the close of the treasury share purchase period on February 11, 2027, the preliminary Q4 and full-year 2026 results, and the 2026 annual report due by March 31, 2027.
