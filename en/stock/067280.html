02

Business structure

Multicampus was founded in 2000 and listed on KOSDAQ in 2006 as a corporate education specialist.

The business is split into an Education Services segment that handles corporate job training, a Foreign Language Services segment that runs foreign language assessment and customized corporate training, and a Knowledge Services segment built on knowledge platforms.

The Education Services segment centers on IT job training and human resource development services, delivered through learning platforms and in-person group training.

In Foreign Language Services, its subsidiary Language Testing International (LTI) carries out and scores OPIc, a global foreign language speaking assessment. Knowledge Services runs knowledge content such as SERICEO.

As of Q3 2024, sales were made up of KRW 199.4 billion from Education Services (76%), KRW 46.1 billion from Foreign Language Services (18%), and KRW 16.3 billion from Knowledge Services (6%). The Education Services share has drifted lower, from 80% in 2022 to 78% in 2023 and 76% in Q3 2024.

Samsung Group affiliates are the core of the customer base. The company took over the education business unit of Samsung SDS in 2015, and in 2025 Samsung affiliates accounted for KRW 161.7 billion of total sales of KRW 330.8 billion. As of Q3 2024, a single customer, Company A, represented 26.35% of sales.

Competitive dynamics differ by segment: corporate training tracks HR training budgets, while OPIc volumes track corporate hiring.

04

Earnings analysis

Annual results show sales and profit both sliding. Sales rose from KRW 357.5 billion in 2022 to KRW 358.6 billion in 2023 (up 0.3% year on year), which marked the peak, and then fell 1.6% to KRW 352.7 billion in 2024 and 6.2% to KRW 330.8 billion in 2025, a two-year decline.

Operating profit fell for three consecutive years, from KRW 41 billion in 2022 to KRW 31.9 billion in 2025, and the operating margin narrowed from 11.5% to 9.6% over the same period.

Net profit attributable to controlling shareholders peaked at KRW 31.5 billion in 2023 (up 4.1%), then declined to KRW 31.1 billion in 2024 and KRW 26.2 billion in 2025. Net income including non-controlling interests was KRW 26.4 billion in 2025.

The 2025 profit decline came alongside the sales decline, and Samsung affiliate sales fell by KRW 20.8 billion from KRW 182.5 billion in 2024 to KRW 161.7 billion in 2025, accounting for most of the total sales drop. The balance sheet moved in the opposite direction.

The debt ratio fell from 69.3% in 2022 to 36.8% in 2025, and at year-end 2025 total liabilities were KRW 86.7 billion against total equity of KRW 235.3 billion, of which KRW 230.5 billion belonged to controlling shareholders.

Operating cash flow peaked at KRW 59.4 billion in 2023 and fell to KRW 43.6 billion in 2025, but it stayed positive every year. The quarterly trend turned at Q1 2026.

Sales were KRW 80.7 billion, down 5.3% from the prior quarter, and operating profit was KRW 638 million, down 93.9%, reflecting a roughly KRW 4 billion retirement benefit provision booked at once after a change in severance calculation rules.

Q2 2026 sales rose 8.6% quarter on quarter to KRW 87.6 billion, and operating profit recovered to KRW 8.81 billion. Q3 2026 sales were KRW 90.8 billion, up 7.0% year on year, and operating profit was KRW 9.12 billion, up 14.1%.

Net profit attributable to controlling shareholders reached KRW 8.15 billion in Q3 2026, up 19.8% year on year, and the last four quarters total KRW 24.3 billion.

However, preliminary cumulative operating profit for January to September 2026 was KRW 18.565 billion, down 13.6% from a year earlier, as announced on October 22, 2026.

05

Industry analysis

Multicampus sits in the education services industry. In a 21-company comparison of annual results for 2025, it ranked fourth in sales and third in operating profit within the group.

Looking at the end market, a 2023 survey of Korea's e-learning industry put domestic e-learning demand at KRW 5.7078 trillion, and the corporate and government/public institution markets that Multicampus focuses on at KRW 2.3995 trillion.

In the same survey, the corporate market grew 4.3% and the government and public market grew 4.9% year on year. Company sales, however, have moved in the opposite direction since 2023, which points to a share-related challenge. Competitive dynamics vary by segment.

Corporate job training tracks HR training budgets, while OPIc tracks corporate hiring and test-taker demand.

In April 2026, an industry analyst said that the stable demand from affiliates could support solid results, while growth would require the company to showcase its technology, and suggested that broader AI adoption could be a growth path.

The ratio of R&D spending to sales rose to 9.50% in Q3 2024 from a year earlier, and commentary has noted that R&D investment will shape competitiveness as education platforms using AI and metaverse technologies expand.

Within the IT and software sector, Samsung SDS is the group affiliate whose business boundary touches Multicampus, since Multicampus took over its education business unit in 2015.

06

Outlook

Sales for the first half of 2026 rose 4.7% year on year, while operating profit fell 30.0% and net profit attributable to controlling shareholders fell 29.1%, according to a Wisereport company summary dated September 3, 2026.

The company's growth in sales was attributed to service diversification, including learning-platform-based specialized training and in-person group training, while cost burdens have limited profitability. Management has also changed.

At the annual general meeting on March 31, 2026, Ahn Jae-woo was elected as an inside director, and the board changed the CEO from Jeong Seok-mok, whose term had expired, to Ahn.

Ahn spent 32 years at Samsung Electronics and Samsung SDI in HR, and the board cited his global business management and talent development experience. On May 29, 2026, the CEO reported buying 2,000 common shares on the open market for KRW 50.717 million.

On September 22, 2026, the company decided to acquire treasury shares, stating the aim of stabilizing the share price and enhancing shareholder value. The task of reducing reliance on affiliates has been framed as the new management's main challenge for restoring growth.

The company's published annual sales target is not covered here because no such guidance appears in the disclosures reviewed.

The next checkpoints are the 2026 fourth-quarter and full-year preliminary results, the progress of the treasury share acquisition, and the affiliate sales breakdown that will appear in the 2026 annual report.

08

Bull factors

Turn in quarterly results

Q2 2026 sales of KRW 87.6 billion rose 8.6% from the prior quarter, and Q3 sales of KRW 90.8 billion rose 7.0% year on year. Q3 operating profit of KRW 9.12 billion grew 14.1% year on year, extending the increase that began in Q2.

The Q1 trough was driven largely by a one-time retirement benefit provision, so it is distinct from a change in the underlying earnings base.

Added shareholder return tools

On September 22, 2026, the company decided to acquire treasury shares, citing share price stability and shareholder value. On May 29, 2026, CEO Ahn Jae-woo reported buying 2,000 common shares on the open market for KRW 50.717 million. Both disclosures show management involvement in shareholder returns.

Balance sheet and cash generation

The debt ratio fell to 36.8% at year-end 2025 from 69.3% in 2022. Total liabilities were KRW 86.7 billion and total equity was KRW 235.3 billion at year-end 2025.

Operating cash flow stayed positive every year, reaching KRW 43.6 billion in 2025, and an investment banking analyst pointed to affiliate-based demand as a basis for sustained results.

09

Bear factors

Accumulated annual profit decline

Operating profit fell from KRW 40.3 billion in 2023 to KRW 38.9 billion in 2024 and KRW 31.9 billion in 2025, and net profit attributable to controlling shareholders in 2025 was below its 2023 peak. The operating margin dropped from 11.5% in 2022 to 9.6% in 2025.

Preliminary cumulative operating profit for January to September 2026 was KRW 18.565 billion, down 13.6% year on year.

Shrinking demand from Samsung affiliates

Sales from Samsung affiliates fell from KRW 182.5 billion in 2024 to KRW 161.7 billion in 2025, a decline of KRW 20.8 billion or 11.4%. Samsung Electronics alone dropped from KRW 83.6 billion to KRW 78.3 billion over the same period.

As of Q3 2024, a single customer, Company A, accounted for 26.35% of sales, so customer concentration remains high.

Slow recovery in profitability

First-half 2026 sales rose 4.7%, but operating profit fell 30.0% (Wisereport, as of September 3, 2026). Service diversification lifted sales, but cost burdens were cited as limiting profitability. The 2025 operating margin of 9.6% has not returned to the 11.5% level of 2022.

10

Risk factors

Customer concentration and affiliate dependence

Samsung affiliate sales form a large part of total revenue, so any cut in affiliate training budgets flows directly into company sales. In 2025, the drop in affiliate sales explained most of the total sales decline. The 26.35% share held by one customer as of Q3 2024 reflects the same concentration risk.

Technology response and business structure

If training demand shifts toward AI-based learning platforms, the competitiveness of traditional in-person group training could weaken. The R&D-to-sales ratio rose to 9.50% in Q3 2024, yet Education Services still made up 76% of sales in that quarter. The industry commentary points to broader AI adoption as a growth path.

One-time costs and cost structure

In Q1 2026, a change in severance calculation rules led to a roughly KRW 4 billion retirement benefit provision being recognized at once. As of Q1 2023, higher personnel costs and commission fees had pushed up selling and administrative expenses. If costs grow faster than sales, the recovery in operating margin could be delayed.

12

Overall view

Multicampus posted sequential gains in sales and operating profit in Q2 and Q3 2026 after the Q1 trough, but on an annual basis sales fell for two straight years through 2025 and operating profit fell for three consecutive years from 2023.

In 2025 the company reported sales of KRW 330.8 billion, operating profit of KRW 31.9 billion, and net profit attributable to controlling shareholders of KRW 26.2 billion, with an operating margin of 9.6%.

Much of the decline came from lower sales at Samsung affiliates, and a single customer accounted for 26.35% of sales as of Q3 2024. On the other side, the debt ratio fell to 36.8%, operating cash flow stayed positive every year, and a treasury share acquisition was decided in September 2026.

Preliminary cumulative operating profit for January to September 2026 was down 13.6% year on year, so it is too early to tell whether the quarterly rebound will offset the annual decline.

The next checkpoints are the preliminary Q4 and full-year 2026 results, the affiliate sales breakdown in the 2026 annual report, and the progress of the treasury share acquisition. This report is for information only and does not include a buy or sell opinion.
