Philoptics: Glass-Substrate Hopes vs. Five Straight Quarters of Operating Losses

02

Business structure

Philoptics builds display, semiconductor and secondary-battery equipment on laser processing technology.

The company cites the world's first mass production of an OLED laser processing standard tool, mass production of laser notching and stacking tools for batteries, and the industry's first supply of a laser TGV tool as its key track record.

Since its founding, display has accounted for the largest share of revenue, and as the glass substrate industry has gained attention, semiconductor-segment sales have been expanding, according to its IR representative. A slump in display equipment was cited as a background to the sales decline in 2025.

In semiconductors, the company supplies TGV (through glass via) tools and singulation tools that cut glass with lasers. The 2.0mm glass substrate segment is seeing intensifying development competition as interest from big tech firms grows.

In solar, it has commercialized thin-film, perovskite and tandem laser equipment. Samsung Display has been cited as a key display customer.

In September 2026 the company said it had won an order for TGV equipment for 2.0mm-thick glass substrates and can now supply automated optical inspection (AOI) equipment alongside it. The company has said it is pursuing a restructuring that would lift the semiconductor share of its business to 70% over the long term.

Analysts note that several companies are entering the glass substrate equipment market, so technical differentiation and mass production yield are seen as key.

04

Earnings analysis

On a disclosed annual basis, revenue was KRW 304 billion in 2022, KRW 300 billion in 2023 (down 1.3% year on year), KRW 410.9 billion in 2024 (up 37.0%) and KRW 103.4 billion in 2025 (down 74.8%). 2024 was the largest year and 2025 the smallest among 2022 to 2025.

Operating profit fell from KRW 18.1 billion in 2022 to KRW 10.3 billion in 2023 (down 43.2%), recovered to KRW 13.8 billion in 2024 (up 34.4%), and in 2025 turned into an operating loss of KRW 34.9 billion. The operating margin was 5.9% in 2022, 3.4% in both 2023 and 2024, and -33.7% in 2025.

Net income attributable to controlling shareholders went from a loss of KRW 5.9 billion in 2022 to a loss of KRW 43.2 billion in 2023, a profit of KRW 21.5 billion in 2024 (a turn to profit), and a loss of KRW 17 billion in 2025 (a return to loss).

Net income including non-controlling interests followed a different path: a loss of KRW 5.15 billion in 2022, a loss of KRW 43.4 billion in 2023, a profit of KRW 28.8 billion in 2024 and a loss of KRW 26.4 billion in 2025.

On the 2025 profitability decline, the IR representative pointed to higher costs from added headcount at the company and at an affiliate.

Quarterly revenue was KRW 10.5 billion in Q3 2025, KRW 26.4 billion in Q4 2025 (up 151.4% quarter on quarter), KRW 11 billion in Q1 2026, KRW 8.2 billion in Q2 2026 and KRW 63.1 billion in Q3 2026 (up 669.6% quarter on quarter and 499.7% year on year).

The operating loss in Q3 2026 was KRW 5.19 billion, narrower than the KRW 15.2 billion loss in Q2 2026. Operating cash flow turned positive at KRW 9.52 billion in 2025 after -KRW 39.4 billion in 2024.

05

Industry analysis

Glass substrates are drawing attention as a next-generation material that could replace existing substrates in AI semiconductor packages. Samsung Electro-Mechanics is developing glass substrates at its Sejong plant and targets mass production as early as the second half of 2026.

Samsung Electronics is reported to be developing glass interposers to replace silicon interposers, targeting commercialization in 2028. As a result, demand is expected to rise for glass-processing equipment such as TGV and singulation tools.

One analyst noted that several companies are entering the TGV business, intensifying competition. In display equipment, sales have declined amid a weak industry cycle, and the company is shifting its business weight toward semiconductor equipment.

Against peers, Philoptics cites its supply of the industry's first mass-production TGV tool and its shipment record for singulation tools as strengths. Some analysts, however, argue that collaboration with glass material suppliers is needed, which limits what an equipment maker can achieve alone.

The timing of downstream demand depends on customers' mass production schedules, making customer investment announcements the key variable.

Bull factors

TGV equipment order and technology validation

On September 29, 2026, the company said it had won an order for TGV equipment for 2.0mm-thick glass substrates. It claims a 0 ppm defect rate in high-difficulty processing tests on mass-production panels. It also says potential customers keep requesting sample tests. Its record as the first supplier of a mass-production TGV tool is cited as a validation case.

Large customer capex plans

In a March 2026 report, iM Securities pointed out that the largest customer's decision to raise KRW 1 trillion could bring a full-scale capex cycle. The report cited TGV orders for a production line in Georgia, USA. It estimated that actual equipment shipments would begin in the second half of 2027. The report did not present a separate target price or investment opinion.

Shift toward a semiconductor-centered business

A 70% semiconductor share is the long-term target the company has set for its business restructuring. The weight is moving from display equipment toward semiconductor equipment such as glass-substrate tools.

Samsung Electro-Mechanics and Samsung Electronics setting mass-production and commercialization targets for glass substrates is cited as a demand-side backdrop. Whether this shift shows up in reported results can be checked in future disclosures.

Bear factors

Gap in revenue and earnings

Revenue in 2025 was KRW 103.4 billion, down 74.8% from the prior year, and the operating loss was KRW 34.9 billion. Net loss attributable to controlling shareholders was KRW 17 billion in 2025. A news report said first-half 2026 revenue fell 71.2% year on year. Operating losses have run for five consecutive quarters from Q3 2025 through Q3 2026.

Delayed recovery in the core business and cost burden

Weakness in the display equipment cycle reduced the core business's sales. The IR representative said costs rose from added headcount at the company and at an affiliate. The operating margin fell to -33.7% in 2025. Although Q3 2026 revenue rose sharply, an operating loss of KRW 5.19 billion remained.

Expectations priced in and share price volatility

The sharp share price rise after the limit-up move in early September 2026 was reported as expectations for glass-substrate orders being priced in quickly. One outlet noted that short-term volatility could rise if follow-on orders are not confirmed.

Short interest stood at 2.78% of market capitalization as of October 29, 2026. Some observers note that without a recovery in results, expectations could become a burden on the share price.

Risk factors

Customer concentration and delayed order confirmation

Revenue in display equipment depends on the investment plans of Samsung Display, a key customer. Glass-substrate orders remain in the expectation stage until contracts are confirmed by disclosure. The timing forecast in a March 2026 report may differ from when contracts are actually finalized. Whether contract size and shipment schedules are disclosed is the next checkpoint.

Mass-production technology and yield

The company has identified voids during copper filling and substrate breakage during CMP (chemical mechanical polishing) as challenges in the TGV process. If customers do not secure mass-production yields, follow-on orders and production tool shipments could be delayed.

Technical validation results matter more as potential customers multiply. Entry by competitors also raises pressure for technical differentiation.

Financial structure and cash flow volatility

Total liabilities at end-2025 were KRW 191.8 billion, nearly equal to total equity of KRW 191.7 billion, giving a debt ratio of 100.1%. Operating cash flow swung from KRW 111.1 billion in 2023 to -KRW 39.4 billion in 2024 and KRW 9.52 billion in 2025.

Equity attributable to controlling shareholders was KRW 116.3 billion. If losses persist, funding conditions could become a burden.

Overall view

Philoptics' disclosed results show operating profit of KRW 18.1 billion in 2022 and KRW 13.8 billion in 2024, but in 2025 revenue fell to KRW 103.4 billion and the company posted an operating loss of KRW 34.9 billion.

Q3 2026 revenue of KRW 63.1 billion signaled a rebound, yet an operating loss of KRW 5.19 billion and controlling-shareholder net losses of KRW 21.5 billion over the last four quarters remain.

On the business side, the September 2026 TGV order for 2.0mm glass substrates and the 70% semiconductor-share target stand out as strengths. On the other side, order expectations have been priced into the share price quickly, and the size and timing of confirmed orders still need to be verified through disclosure.

On valuation, a PER cannot be calculated on earnings, and the price-to-book ratio sits in a range with a large premium over net asset value. The next checkpoints are the preliminary 2026 annual results in early 2027, the operating margin in the 2026 annual report, and whether large TGV orders are disclosed.
