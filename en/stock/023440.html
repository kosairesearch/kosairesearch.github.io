02

Business structure

J Steel Company Holdings was founded in 1964 and listed on KOSDAQ in 1994 as a steel-focused company. It buys slabs and billets from domestic and overseas suppliers and processes them through rolling and other steps into hot-rolled and extruded products.

Its main products are mild steel wire rod, BIC (coiled rebar) and deformed rebar, and company information states that it keeps the only domestic production setup for mild steel wire rod. Its main plant is in Ansan, Gyeonggi Province, supplying construction sites and auto parts makers.

The company attributed the 2025 revenue increase to higher steel sales, a stronger near-term demand base, diversified sales channels and a product portfolio adjustment. Its Philippine subsidiary JSCO PH, established in 2023, holds exclusive sales rights to nickel ore from a 2,700 ha concession.

The subsidiary works with the state-owned miner PMDC and local partner EVMDC in a structure covering extraction through sales. The company said its EVMDC stake is expected to bring dividend income and equity-method gains. Its largest shareholder held 5.04% as of August 2026. The company plans to lift nickel sales once shipments begin and the volume and number of shipments increase.

04

Earnings analysis

Annual revenue went from KRW 84.1 billion in 2022 to KRW 55.3 billion in 2023 (down 34.2% year on year) and KRW 28.4 billion in 2024 (down 48.7%), before rebounding to KRW 41.1 billion in 2025 (up 44.7%). Among 2022 to 2025, 2022 was the largest year and 2024 the smallest.

The operating loss was KRW 6.19 billion in 2022, KRW 8.36 billion in 2023 (loss continued and widened), KRW 14.7 billion in 2024 (loss continued and widened) and KRW 8.48 billion in 2025 (loss continued and narrowed).

The operating margin was -7.4% in 2022, -15.1% in 2023, -51.8% in 2024 and -20.6% in 2025, with the deepest margin decline in 2024, when revenue was smallest. The company has reported operating losses four years in a row from 2022 through 2025.

The net loss attributable to controlling shareholders was KRW 27.2 billion in 2022, KRW 24.4 billion in 2023, KRW 26.7 billion in 2024 and KRW 32 billion in 2025, the largest of the four years. The net loss including non-controlling interests was reported at the same amounts.

Operating cash flow moved from KRW 13.5 billion in 2022 to KRW -6.41 billion in 2023, KRW -6.81 billion in 2024 and KRW -11.5 billion in 2025.

At the end of 2025, total equity was KRW 54.8 billion, of which KRW 54.6 billion belonged to controlling shareholders, and total liabilities were KRW 61.2 billion, a debt ratio of 111.6%.

Quarterly revenue went from KRW 10.4 billion in the third quarter of 2025 and KRW 12.1 billion in the fourth quarter to a peak of KRW 15 billion in the first quarter of 2026, then fell to KRW 11.5 billion in the second quarter and KRW 9.96 billion in the third quarter, down 4.6% year on year.

The operating loss was KRW 1.9 billion in the third quarter of 2025, KRW 921 million in the fourth quarter, KRW 1.82 billion in the first quarter of 2026, KRW 748 million in the second quarter and KRW 1.8 billion in the third quarter, five straight quarters of operating losses.

Net income attributable to controlling shareholders turned positive at KRW 460 million in the second quarter of 2026, then a net loss of KRW 4.19 billion in the third quarter returned the company to a loss. The net loss attributable to controlling shareholders over the last four quarters totals KRW 23.9 billion.

05

Industry analysis

Profitability in the core steel business depends on construction and industrial demand and the price trend of imported steel. In its 2025 results commentary, the company cited a recovery in downstream industries and diversified sales channels as drivers of higher revenue.

A December 2025 news report said the government is expanding responses such as anti-dumping investigations to protect a steel industry hit by Chinese dumping, high tariffs and weak domestic demand.

Such policy is read as a factor that could stabilize prices and improve supply and demand in the mild steel wire rod market. A March 2026 report said pressure from imports had partly eased as anti-dumping measures were strengthened.

The domestic-only production setup for mild steel wire rod is cited in company information as reducing direct price-competition pressure. Still, steel prices and demand are sensitive to construction activity and Chinese supply conditions, so the durability of the policy effect is an open variable.

Compared with POSCO Holdings, a large steel holding company with a full-process value chain, J Steel focuses on specific products such as wire rod and rebar and is much smaller in scale. Its nickel business links to demand for battery core minerals.

In March 2026, an independent research house viewed the government's emphasis on Philippine critical-mineral cooperation as a positive momentum, but it did not present an investment rating or target price.

06

Outlook

According to the company, it obtained the Mining ECC, a key environmental approval for developing the Dinagat nickel mine, from the Environmental Management Bureau of the Philippine Department of Environment and Natural Resources on June 23, 2026.

On September 29, 2026, it said it had secured final permits for nickel and chromite production and export from the Mines and Geosciences Bureau. The company said it has completed on-site infrastructure for production, transport and shipment, along with an overseas sales network.

It plans to push forward with the first shipment quickly, then gradually raise output and the number of shipments to generate continuing sales and cash flow.

A March 2026 report said preparations, including barge contracts, were understood to be complete, but as of the September 2026 disclosure the first shipment was still in the preparation stage.

A December 2025 report said a national state of emergency declared after typhoon damage in the Philippines had delayed some administrative procedures. The company plans to keep strengthening steel profitability and cost efficiency, while focusing resources on commercializing the Philippine mine.

In July 2026 it resolved to issue a KRW 2.5 billion fifth-series convertible bond to raise operating funds, with a conversion price of KRW 506 and a conversion period from July 14, 2027 to June 14, 2029.

The timing and scale of nickel revenue becoming meaningful will be confirmed through disclosures after the first shipment.

08

Bull factors

Recovery in steel revenue

Consolidated revenue in 2025 rose 44.7% year on year to KRW 41.1 billion. The company attributed this mainly to higher steel sales and diversified sales channels. First-quarter 2026 revenue of KRW 15 billion was the highest of the last five quarters.

Progress on nickel permits

The company said it obtained the Mining ECC in June 2026, followed by final production and export permits in late September. Its subsidiary JSCO PH holds exclusive sales rights to nickel ore from a 2,700 ha concession. The company described a supply contract of about 8 million tonnes with a Chinese counterparty.

Easing pressure from imports

Expanded anti-dumping investigations are expected to help stabilize prices and improve supply and demand in the mild steel wire rod market. Its domestic-only production of wire rod is cited as a differentiating factor.

The company said it is pursuing cost structure efficiency and product portfolio adjustments to strengthen steel profitability.

Bear factors

Long-running operating losses and cash outflow

Operating losses have continued from 2022 through 2025, and operating cash flow has been negative since 2023. The 2025 operating cash outflow of KRW 11.5 billion was the largest of the four years.

The operating loss widened again to KRW 1.8 billion in the third quarter of 2026 from KRW 748 million in the second quarter.

Audit opinion and listing requirements

Trading was halted on March 23, 2026 for a reason related to rumors or media reports. With an improvement period granted, the next point to check is whether the going-concern uncertainty and the disclaimer of opinion due to an audit scope limitation are resolved. Whether the company is delisted will be decided after the improvement period ends.

Funding needs and dilution

In July 2026 the company resolved to issue a KRW 2.5 billion fifth-series convertible bond to raise operating funds. The conversion price is KRW 506, and conversion would issue 4,950,711 common shares.

Concern that the largest shareholder's stake was diluted by bond conversion and a rights offering was addressed by the company, which described the financing as planned and said control was unaffected.

Risk factors

Listing status

The most significant risk is that listing status depends on the audit result for the annual report. With an improvement period in place, failure to resolve the audit opinion could increase constraints on trading. Going-concern uncertainty remains an issue as long as operating losses and cash outflows continue.

Execution of the nickel business

Even after permits, first shipments, the performance of sales contracts and logistics reliability are the key tests. A December 2025 report said a national state of emergency after typhoon damage in the Philippines delayed some administrative procedures. The size and timing of nickel revenue remain undetermined until confirmed by disclosures.

Funding and dilution

With operating losses and operating cash outflows continuing, the company relies heavily on external funding such as convertible bonds. At the KRW 506 conversion price, conversion would add 4,950,711 shares to existing shares outstanding. Because the conversion period opens on July 14, 2027, it could weigh on the supply of shares thereafter.

12

Overall view

J Steel's reported results show a steel revenue recovery and persistent operating losses at the same time. Revenue rose to KRW 41.1 billion in 2025, yet the operating loss of KRW 8.48 billion and the net loss attributable to controlling shareholders of KRW 32 billion continued.

In the 2026 quarterly sequence, revenue fell after the first-quarter peak and the operating loss widened again in the third quarter, while the swing to profit in the second quarter appeared only at the controlling-shareholder net income level.

The nickel business moved into commercialization with the June 2026 environmental approval and final permits in late September, but first shipments and revenue contribution are not yet reflected in reported results.

The audit opinion and delisting procedure are the matters most likely to produce a result first after the 2026 annual report is filed. The convertible bond issuance and the debate over dilution of the largest shareholder's stake show that the funding structure still depends on external capital.

The steel core has a policy backdrop that some view as favorable, but prices and demand remain volatile. Bullish and bearish factors coexist with comparable weight.
