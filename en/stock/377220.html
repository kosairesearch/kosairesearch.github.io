Business structure

From Bio's core business is developing, manufacturing and distributing health functional foods, alongside a cosmetics subsidiary and cell-therapy R&D. Its flagship products are 'Boswellia for Joint Cartilage' and 'Mastic for Stomach Health', and revenue is concentrated in a small number of product lines.

In a 2025 report, health functional foods accounted for more than 80% of revenue, and the two product lines of Boswellia and Mastic made up more than 60% of that segment's sales. The company is shifting away from reliance on home shopping, which fell to 34% of sales in 2024 from 79% in 2019.

It is widening its channels through OEM contract manufacturing, online platforms and overseas sales. Its joint-health solution was recognized with an award at the 2026 National Health Awards.

Overseas, it plans to use a distribution partner's network to expand 35 product lines into North America, Europe and Southeast Asia. Its cosmetics subsidiary has run at a loss since its founding in 2022, according to a 2025 report.

In R&D, the stem-cell-based hair-loss drug candidate has completed nonclinical work, with a target of entering clinical trials in Korea in 2027. In April 2026 the company was selected for a cell-culture materials commercialization program in Gyeongsangbuk-do and began a cell-therapy process standardization project.

Earnings analysis

Annual revenue fell from KRW 99.3 billion in 2022 to KRW 66.7 billion in 2023, a 32.8% decline, then rose to KRW 67.2 billion in 2024 (up 0.7%) and KRW 72.2 billion in 2025 (up 7.4%), with growth in 2024 and 2025 marking two consecutive years of increases.

Among 2022 to 2025, 2022 was the largest year and 2023 the smallest. A 2026 press report of the company's earlier history cited 2021 revenue of KRW 135.1 billion as the peak.

Operating profit of KRW 897 million in 2022 turned into an operating loss of KRW 15.4 billion in 2023, followed by losses of KRW 24.3 billion in 2024 and KRW 18.6 billion in 2025, which means three consecutive loss years. The operating margin moved from 0.9% in 2022 to -23.1% in 2023, -36.1% in 2024 and -25.8% in 2025.

In 2024 the company wrote off impaired assets in one lump, a big-bath charge that management presented as a step toward a turnaround. Press coverage linked the continuing losses after 2023 to higher advertising and selling, general and administrative expenses.

Net profit attributable to controlling shareholders went from KRW 3.24 billion in 2022 to net losses of KRW 11.1 billion in 2023, KRW 25.3 billion in 2024 and KRW 22.5 billion in 2025. Net income including non-controlling interests was KRW 1.32 billion in 2022 and a net loss of KRW 23.2 billion in 2025.

Total liabilities stood at KRW 39.1 billion against total equity of KRW 26.1 billion at year-end 2025, and the debt ratio rose from 7.8% in 2022 to 149.6% in 2025. Operating cash flow was negative KRW 10.1 billion in 2025.

Quarterly, revenue was KRW 18.3 billion in Q3 2025 with an operating loss of KRW 4.44 billion, then KRW 19.5 billion in Q4 2025 (up 6.6% quarter on quarter) with an operating loss of KRW 8 billion, as losses widened.

Q1 2026 revenue was KRW 19.2 billion (down 1.7%) with an operating loss of KRW 3.28 billion, and Q2 2026 revenue fell to KRW 14.7 billion (down 23.5%) with the smallest operating loss of KRW 553 million.

Q3 2026 revenue rebounded to KRW 21.7 billion (up 18.8% year on year), but the operating loss widened to KRW 1.72 billion and the net loss attributable to controlling shareholders was KRW 2.53 billion.

Over the last four quarters, the net loss attributable to controlling shareholders totaled KRW 17.9 billion, and the company has posted losses in five consecutive quarters. Because quarterly revenue swings are large, the segment mix should be checked in the periodic report.

Industry analysis

The domestic health functional food market has lower entry barriers, intensifying competition and exposure to consumer spending, a company official said in a 2025 report. The company is restructuring its product strategy and distribution channels.

A 2025 report noted that its risk diversification lagged competitors with broader product portfolios. Overseas, the company said it began exporting in 2024 but faced difficulty expanding because many countries are unfamiliar with its ingredients.

It was selected as a participant in the 2026 food company overseas certification support program. It is pursuing U.S. FDA New Dietary Ingredient certification for its proprietary Mastic gum complex (FHH-MG), which has been filed in Korea as an individually recognized functional ingredient for hair health.

The biotech segment is still before clinical entry, so its revenue contribution is a medium- to long-term task. The company holds about a 10% stake in a brain-barrier-penetration drug developer.

Within the core supplement business, revenue fell by about half from its 2021 peak in 2023 and 2024 before a modest recovery in 2025.
