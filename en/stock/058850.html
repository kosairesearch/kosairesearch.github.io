KTcs: Profit Recovery and a First Shareholder Return Policy

KTcs's operating profit rebounded to KRW 29.8 billion in 2025 and kept rising year over year in the third quarter of 2026, while the company adopted its first mid-term shareholder return policy.

02

Business structure

KTcs is a contact center specialist that took over customer-service work from KT and was founded in 2001 when KT spun off its 114 directory assistance business.

The company operates KT's wired and wireless customer centers as well as contact centers for public agencies and corporations, delivering customer support services. Its business areas are divided into KT customer centers, open-market contact centers, 114 directory assistance, and distribution.

According to its semiannual report for 2023, the company is the only operator in the domestic voice directory assistance market and runs large-scale contact center infrastructure with more than 1,200 seats in Seoul alone.

Revenue relies heavily on customer-service volume from the KT group, a structure that offers stable volumes but concentrates dependence on a single client.

In recent years the company has expanded its AI contact center (AICC) business, which pairs generative and conversational AI with in-depth consultation and 24-hour unmanned service, and has commercialized its own AICC solution, 'HiQri'.

To broaden its external contract base, shareholders approved adding an event execution business to the articles of incorporation at the March 2026 general meeting, which the company linked to expanding its education business and winning more contracts.

Within the KT group, KTis is also building its position in AICC, so how the two companies divide roles is a variable for KTcs's structure. In short, the model rests on two pillars: stability from group volume and external client acquisition through AICC.

04

Earnings analysis

On a reported basis, annual revenue rose from KRW 1.03 trillion in 2022 to KRW 1.04 trillion in 2023 and KRW 1.12 trillion in 2024, then fell 7.0% to KRW 1.04 trillion in 2025. Among 2022 to 2025, 2024 was the largest year for revenue and 2022 the smallest.

Operating profit went from KRW 24.3 billion in 2022 to KRW 18.7 billion in 2023 (down 23.0%) and KRW 6.97 billion in 2024 (down 62.8%), then rebounded to KRW 29.8 billion in 2025 (up 327.6%). Operating margin declined from 2.4% in 2022 to 1.8% in 2023 and 0.6% in 2024 before recovering to 2.9% in 2025.

Profit rose even as revenue fell, which points to operating efficiency and cost reduction rather than sales volume, a factor the company also cited as the driver of growth.

Parent-attributable net income moved from KRW 17.1 billion in 2022 to KRW 14.6 billion in 2023 (down 14.3%), KRW 5.27 billion in 2024 (down 64.0%), and KRW 23.4 billion in 2025 (up 343.6%). Net income including non-controlling interests was KRW 25.2 billion in 2025, above the parent-attributable figure.

Operating cash flow was KRW 19.7 billion in 2022, KRW 55.1 billion in 2023, KRW 44.6 billion in 2024, and KRW 53.4 billion in 2025, staying robust even in the weakest profit year.

Quarterly, operating profit of KRW 9.18 billion in the third quarter of 2025 fell to KRW 1.18 billion in the fourth quarter (down 87.2% quarter on quarter), then jumped to KRW 10.5 billion in the first quarter of 2026 (up 793.5%) despite an 18.9% revenue decline.

It eased to KRW 9.66 billion in the second quarter (down 8.0%) and reached KRW 12.4 billion in the third quarter (up 28.9%). The swings show that quarterly profit remains volatile even as the annual trend improved.

At year-end 2025, the debt-to-equity ratio was 109.1%, with total equity of KRW 225.3 billion, parent-attributable equity of KRW 212.6 billion, and total liabilities of KRW 245.8 billion.

05

Industry analysis

The contact center industry is moving from labor-intensive, agent-based operations toward a transition in which automation technologies such as generative AI and voice bots are combined with human service.

The KT group has set out a direction of growing its AICC business, which combines AI technology with operating consulting, and of building AICC BPO capabilities.

If AI automation reduces customer-contact volume, agent-based revenue could come under pressure, but rising demand for AICC construction and operation could create new revenue streams, so both forces coexist.

The voice-based directory assistance business is tied to voice service demand, so the pace of the shift to AI voice bots could affect its value. KTis, another KT group company, is also focused on widening its market position through AICC, so which customer segments KTcs takes within the group shapes its position.

Because KTcs depends largely on KT group volume, how its exposure to industry changes evolves will depend on how much external client business grows. Demand from the front end is shaped by how the KT group runs its customer centers and by outsourcing demand from public and financial institutions.

Overall, the industry sits in a phase where defense and growth diverge depending on how fast AI adoption proceeds.

06

Outlook

The forward-looking items the company has disclosed center on shareholder returns and capital structure.

The mid-term shareholder return policy for 2026 to 2028 sets aside 40% of adjusted standalone net income, excluding one-off non-recurring items, and is executed through cash dividends and treasury share acquisition and retirement.

The board resolved on November 6, 2026 to retire all 3,465,721 treasury shares, with a scheduled retirement date of December 21, 2026. The estimated retirement amount is KRW 8.66 billion, equal to 8.37% of total issued shares, and the date may change depending on consultations with relevant authorities.

Retiring the shares reduces the total number of issued shares, while share capital stays unchanged. Dividends are planned to move to two payments a year from 2027.

On the business side, expanding the company's own AICC solution and adding the event execution business are presented as steps toward winning more external contracts.

In preliminary results released on November 6, 2026, cumulative operating profit for the first three quarters of 2026 was about KRW 32.6 billion, up 13.8%, while cumulative revenue was about KRW 724.5 billion, down 3.7%. These cumulative figures are preliminary and could change during the external audit review. The outlook carries more weight on policy and timing than on quantitative targets.

08

Bull factors

Operating profit recovery

Operating profit in 2025 was KRW 29.8 billion, up 327.6% from the 2024 low. Third-quarter 2026 operating profit was KRW 12.4 billion, up 35.5% year over year, and revenue rose for two consecutive quarters in the second and third quarters of 2026. Operating margin in 2025 was 2.9%, above 2.4% in 2022.

Shareholder return framework

The mid-term policy for 2026 to 2028 sets aside 40% of adjusted standalone net income as the funding base. It is the company's first formal mid-term policy, with the minimum dividend set at the highest level in its history.

Dividends move to two payments a year from 2027, and retiring all treasury shares reduces the total number of issued shares.

Cost efficiency and AICC shift

The company attributes its operating profit growth mainly to operating efficiency and cost reduction in its contact center business. Third-quarter 2026 operating profit rose even as revenue declined year over year, which shows the effect of a leaner cost structure.

Amid the KT group's push to build AICC BPO capabilities, the commercialization of its own AICC solution, 'HiQri', serves as a response to the era of automated customer service.

Bear factors

Continued revenue decline

Revenue in 2025 was KRW 1.04 trillion, down 7.0% from the prior year. Third-quarter 2026 revenue was KRW 250.3 billion, down 6.9% year over year, and preliminary cumulative revenue for the first three quarters of 2026, announced November 6, 2026, fell 3.7%. Profit grew while sales shrank, so the timing of a revenue recovery is not yet clear.

Thin margins and quarterly volatility

Operating margin fell to 0.6% in 2024 and reached only 2.9% in 2025. Fourth-quarter 2025 operating profit of KRW 1.18 billion fell 87.2% from the prior quarter, and second-quarter 2026 operating profit declined 8.0% quarter on quarter. Quarterly profit swings are large, so the annual recovery cannot be assumed to hold every quarter.

Group dependence and balance sheet load

Because revenue leans heavily on KT group volume, a cut in group orders would flow straight through to results. At year-end 2025, total liabilities of KRW 245.8 billion exceeded total equity of KRW 225.3 billion, and the debt-to-equity ratio was 109.1%. With the ratio above 100%, financial flexibility should be read alongside borrowing conditions.

Risk factors

Client concentration risk

High reliance on KT group volume means any change in the group's customer-center policy or outsourcing structure would hit revenue directly. Whether external contract wins have grown large enough to change the revenue mix has to be gauged through quarterly figures. If customer segments overlap with KTis in AICC within the group, volume allocation could shift.

AI automation and agent-based revenue

Wider adoption of AI and unmanned consultation could pressure per-agent pricing and volume in agent-based revenue. The company aims to turn this shift into a business opportunity through AICC, but whether that transition outpaces revenue erosion has to be tested in quarterly results. The voice-based directory assistance business is also an area that automation could affect.

Variability of shareholder return policy and timing

The funding for mid-term returns is set at 40% of adjusted standalone net income, so lower profit would shrink the dividend pool as well. The policy runs three years through 2028, and the direction after that is not set in current disclosures. The scheduled treasury share retirement date may change depending on consultations with relevant authorities.

Overall view

KTcs shows a pattern in which operating profit and parent-attributable net income recover while revenue declines. Annual operating profit for 2025 was KRW 29.8 billion, a sharp rebound from the 2024 low, and third-quarter 2026 operating profit of KRW 12.4 billion was also higher than a year earlier.

On the other hand, revenue fell in both 2025 and the third quarter of 2026, and operating margin of 2.9% in 2025 remains low. Quarterly results are volatile, as the fourth-quarter 2025 drop and first-quarter 2026 jump show.

On shareholder returns, the 2026 to 2028 mid-term policy, the full retirement of treasury shares, and two dividend payments a year from 2027 now form the framework. Structurally, the company must balance the stability of KT group volume against expanding external contracts through AICC.

Valuation sits in a different position from the past on both earnings and net asset bases, and interpretation depends on whether the profit recovery is sustained.

The next points to confirm are the full-year 2026 operating margin in the annual business report, execution of the December 2026 treasury share retirement, and the start of two dividend payments a year in 2027.
