Report overview

Incon: A rail contract and a KRW 41.5 billion property purchase on a loss-making core

Incon's video security equipment business has posted operating losses for three consecutive quarters since the first quarter of 2026, while a rail network contract and a KRW 41.5 billion property purchase have arrived in the same period.

  1. 1

    On a filed basis, 2025 revenue rose 3.5% year on year to KRW 50.8 billion, and operating profit increased 5.3% to KRW 1.7 billion.

  2. 2

    The net loss attributable to controlling shareholders narrowed from KRW 31.6 billion in 2023 to KRW 1.26 billion in 2025, but the company has posted losses for three straight years.

  3. 3

    Third-quarter 2026 revenue fell 35.0% year on year to KRW 5.33 billion, and the operating loss widened to KRW 775 million.

  4. 4

    The LTE-R upgrade contract with Korea Rail Network Authority, signed on November 6, 2026, carries an Incon share of about KRW 5.46 billion, equal to 10.74% of 2025 revenue.

  5. 5

02

Business structure

Incon's core business is shifting from equipment manufacturing toward supplying video security solutions. Its product line ranges from large-scale integrated control solutions to GIS map systems, smart police infrastructure and safe-return services.

Video security equipment accounted for 84.9% of revenue on a first-nine-months 2025 basis, according to FnGuide's company summary. Security and safety products have shown relatively stable demand, which was cited as a backdrop to recent earnings improvement.

Overseas, the company operates a branch in the United States and sells through regional hubs. Around June 2020, Incon completed a restructuring that exited some lower-margin business units.

The rail contract signed in November 2026 has the National Rail Network Authority as its client, and its value equals 10.74% of 2025 revenue.

Under the KRX classification the company sits in Kosdaq distribution and consumer goods, but under WI26 it falls into IT hardware, so its business structure differs from listed peers in consumer goods distribution.

Because it pairs equipment manufacturing with integrated control software, a single large order can move quarterly revenue considerably. The Samseong-dong land and building purchase, agreed in May 2026 for KRW 41.5 billion, is intended for an R&D center and a new headquarters.

04

Earnings analysis

On a filed basis, revenue rose from KRW 55.3 billion in 2022 to KRW 63.4 billion in 2023, up 14.7% year on year, the largest year in the 2022 to 2025 window. It then fell to KRW 49.1 billion in 2024, down 22.6%, the smallest year in the window, before recovering to KRW 50.8 billion in 2025, up 3.5%.

Operating profit increased from KRW 1.06 billion in 2022 to KRW 2.56 billion in 2023, up 141.0%, then fell 37.0% to KRW 1.61 billion in 2024 and rose 5.3% to KRW 1.7 billion in 2025.

The operating margin was 1.9% in 2022, 4.0% in 2023, 3.3% in 2024 and 3.3% in 2025, so it held in the low-3% range even in the year revenue fell.

Net income attributable to controlling shareholders moved from a KRW 3.55 billion profit in 2022 to a KRW 31.6 billion loss in 2023, then narrowed to a KRW 2.9 billion loss in 2024 and a KRW 1.26 billion loss in 2025. Net income including non-controlling interests was filed at the same amounts in each year.

In 2023 the company recorded KRW 2.56 billion of operating profit yet a KRW 31.6 billion net loss, which means a large loss arose outside operating profit; the cause should be checked in the line items of the 2023 periodic report.

Operating cash flow was KRW 4.84 billion in 2022, KRW 2.96 billion in 2023 and KRW 1.01 billion in 2024, before rising to KRW 4.51 billion in 2025.

At the end of 2025, total liabilities were KRW 7.2 billion and total equity was KRW 74.6 billion, all of it attributable to controlling shareholders, and the debt ratio moved from 19.4% in 2022 to 9.7% in 2025.

Quarterly, revenue of KRW 18.3 billion in the fourth quarter of 2025 was the largest of the last five quarters on a filed basis, and operating profit of KRW 1.2 billion marked a swing from an operating loss of KRW 185 million in the third quarter.

In the first quarter of 2026 revenue was KRW 6.36 billion with an operating loss of KRW 612 million, yet net income attributable to controlling shareholders was a KRW 2.56 billion profit, meaning non-operating items played a large role.

Third-quarter 2026 revenue of KRW 5.33 billion fell 35.0% year on year, and the company swung to a KRW 2.16 billion net loss attributable to controlling shareholders from a year earlier. The total net loss attributable to controlling shareholders over the last four quarters was KRW 1.41 billion.

05

Industry analysis

Demand for video security equipment and solutions tends to move with public procurement and investment in social infrastructure such as transport and rail.

Incon's earnings pattern fits this structure, since quarterly revenue swings widely depending on whether large orders land in a given period, as the gap between KRW 18.3 billion in the fourth quarter of 2025 and KRW 6.36 billion in the first quarter of 2026 shows.

The LTE-R upgrade is a public works project ordered by the Korea Rail Network Authority with a contract term running to February 2030, which adds medium-term revenue visibility. In competition, Incon's distinguishing feature is that it combines equipment manufacturing capability with integrated control solutions.

By market capitalization it has a small presence even within Kosdaq, and its financial capacity is far smaller than that of large security and IT companies. Because the company is classified under KRX distribution but under WI26 IT hardware, the choice of benchmark group changes the industry comparison.

Based on the disclosures available now, it is hard to say whether overall market demand is expanding or contracting, while project-by-project orders clearly drive results. The stated purpose of the KRW 41.5 billion property purchase, an R&D center, points to a willingness to expand technology investment.

06

Outlook

The main contract for the LTE-R upgrade, announced on November 9, 2026, is a confirmed contract worth about KRW 5.46 billion. The total project value is about KRW 10.7 billion, of which Incon's share is 51%. The contract period runs from November 6, 2026 to February 2, 2030, with payments tied to construction progress.

The company has stated that contract terms in the filing may change as the project proceeds.

For the Samseong-dong property, the KRW 4 billion down payment was due May 6, 2026, the KRW 8 billion interim payment July 1, 2026, and the KRW 29.5 billion balance December 30, 2026, which is also the scheduled registration date.

The company plans to fund the purchase with equity and borrowings from financial institutions. The next checkpoint is the fourth-quarter and full-year 2026 preliminary results.

Operating losses have continued through the third quarter, so whether the fourth quarter returns to operating profit will shape the annual picture.

The 2026 annual report is due by the end of March 2027, when full-year operating margin and the composition of the net loss attributable to controlling shareholders will be confirmed in the periodic report.

Because the rail contract equals 10.74% of 2025 revenue, its share of annual revenue should be tracked as construction progresses. After the balance is paid, the borrowing amount and its effect on total liabilities and the debt ratio are also items to confirm.

Bull factors

Long-term rail infrastructure contract secured

The LTE-R upgrade contract from the Korea Rail Network Authority gives Incon a share of about KRW 5.46 billion, with a term running to February 2030. The contract value equals 10.74% of 2025 revenue. It was disclosed as a fixed contract amount, which is cited as a basis for public-sector revenue.

Narrowing losses and recovering operating cash flow

The net loss attributable to controlling shareholders narrowed from KRW 31.6 billion in 2023 to KRW 1.26 billion in 2025. Operating profit for 2025 rose 5.3% to KRW 1.7 billion. Operating cash flow recovered from KRW 1.01 billion in 2024 to KRW 4.51 billion in 2025.

Low debt burden and an asset base

At the end of 2025, total liabilities were KRW 7.2 billion and total equity was KRW 74.6 billion. The debt ratio of 9.7% is at a low level. The price-to-book ratio sits well below 1x, so on a net asset basis the asset base provides some support.

Bear factors

Three consecutive quarters of operating losses

Operating losses have continued from the first quarter through the third quarter of 2026. The third-quarter operating loss widened to KRW 775 million. Revenue of KRW 5.33 billion in the same quarter fell 35.0% year on year.

Large swings in quarterly revenue

Revenue fell 22.6% to KRW 49.1 billion in 2024. Quarterly revenue of KRW 18.3 billion in the fourth quarter of 2025 and KRW 6.36 billion in the first quarter of 2026 shows a wide gap between quarters.

Financial burden from a large property purchase

The purchase price of KRW 41.5 billion equals 50.76% of total assets. The KRW 29.5 billion balance is due December 30, 2026 and will be funded with equity and borrowings. If borrowings rise, the debt structure changes, and total liabilities at the end of 2025 were only KRW 7.2 billion.

10

Risk factors

Concentration risk

Video security equipment accounts for 84.9% of revenue, so order flow and pricing in this segment drive overall results. The overseas footprint centers on a US branch, which makes the overseas revenue base narrow. In a structure dependent on one segment, a gap in orders can quickly translate into a quarterly loss.

Contract terms risk

The company has said the disclosed terms of the rail contract may change as the project proceeds. Payments depend on construction progress, so delays could postpone both revenue recognition and cash inflows. With the contract running to February 2030, progress should be checked each quarter.

Funding and earnings volatility risk

The balance of the property purchase will be funded with equity and bank or financial borrowings. In the first quarter of 2026 net income attributable to controlling shareholders was positive despite an operating loss, which points to large swings in non-operating items. If both factors coincide, financial indicators could shift sharply over a short period.

  2. Early 2027 (timing of fourth-quarter and full-year 2026 preliminary results)

    Whether fourth-quarter 2026 operating profit returns to positive territory. Whether the operating losses from the first through third quarters can be broken is the key variable for the annual trend.

  3. End of March 2027 (deadline for the 2026 annual report)

    The point at which full-year operating margin and the composition of the net loss attributable to controlling shareholders can be confirmed in periodic report figures. The non-operating items behind the first-quarter 2026 net profit can also be reviewed then.

  4. February 2, 2030 (end of the LTE-R contract term)

    The period in which collection of payments tied to construction progress and any disclosed changes to contract amounts can be tracked. The key is how Incon's 51% share of the KRW 10.7 billion project is recognized as revenue over time.

12

Overall view

Incon's video security equipment business, which accounts for 84.9% of revenue, has narrowed its net loss attributable to controlling shareholders from KRW 31.6 billion in 2023 to KRW 1.26 billion in 2025, but operating losses have continued quarter after quarter in 2026.

The LTE-R upgrade contract signed in November 2026, with Incon's share of about KRW 5.46 billion, is a public-sector project that could become a long-term revenue source.

In the same period, the KRW 41.5 billion property purchase has a KRW 29.5 billion balance due on December 30, 2026, making the size of the financial burden a key variable.

Factors supporting the company include the narrower loss, a low debt ratio of 9.7%, a price-to-book ratio well below 1x and the long-term public contract. Factors weighing against it include three consecutive quarters of operating losses, large quarterly revenue swings and the burden of the large property investment.

Which side carries more weight will depend on the fourth-quarter 2026 preliminary results, the operating margin in the 2026 annual report and whether the balance is funded with borrowings.

This report does not present an investment opinion or a target price, and tracking the checkpoints above as facts is the starting point for further assessment.
