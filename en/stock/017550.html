Soosan Cebotics: Profit Recovery, Then a Q3 2026 Loss

Soosan Cebotics raised both sales and operating profit in 2025, but swung to a net loss attributable to controlling shareholders in Q3 2026. The next checkpoints are the 2026 annual report and the progress of its Vietnam capacity expansion.

  1. 1

    Full-year 2025 results recovered. Sales reached KRW 229.4 billion (up 15.9% year on year) and operating profit reached KRW 19 billion (up 57.1%), lifting the operating margin to 8.3%, the highest of 2022–2025.

  2. 2

    Q3 2026 turned to a loss. Net loss attributable to controlling shareholders was KRW 972 million, and operating profit of KRW 2.39 billion fell 41.8% from a year earlier. Net profit attributable to controlling shareholders over the latest four quarters totaled KRW 13.6 billion.

  3. 3

    The business has two pillars: special equipment and attachments. According to an external aggregator, special equipment made up about 83% of Q2 2026 sales and attachments about 17%. An April 2025 press report put overseas exports above 60% of total sales.

  4. 4

    Overseas capacity expansion has moved into formal disclosures. A Vietnam land acquisition by subsidiary SOOSAN VINA MOTOR, approved by the board on December 30, 2025, had its expected acquisition date corrected in a March 31, 2026 filing. A loan to the same subsidiary was decided on May 7, 2026.

  5. 5

    Management and board composition changed. After co-CEO Yang Hee-jun's term expired, Kim Byung-hyun became sole CEO on March 31, 2026, and the outside director lineup was reshuffled.

02

Business structure

Soosan Cebotics is a construction equipment maker founded in 1984 to localize construction machinery and special-purpose vehicles, and it has been listed on KOSPI since 1991.

Its main products are hydraulic breakers, truck cranes and hydraulic drills, and the business is split into an attachment segment centered on hydraulic breakers and a special equipment segment. According to an external aggregator, special equipment made up about 83% of Q2 2026 sales and attachments about 17%.

The company's hydraulic breaker, the first localized in Korea, was reported in April 2025 to hold the sixth spot in global market share, and the same report called Soosan Cebotics the top construction equipment maker in Korea. Overseas exports were reported in the same coverage as exceeding 60% of total sales.

Overseas bases include a production subsidiary in Vietnam and a US subsidiary, and supply is being widened to regions with strong infrastructure spending such as the Middle East, Southeast Asia and North America. Expanding large hydraulic breakers and new equipment lines is part of its global competitiveness strategy.

The 2025 renaming from Soosan Jungkongup to Soosan Cebotics signals a shift toward combining construction machinery with robotics, with 'Cebotics' a blend of construction machinery and robotics.

In competition, Doosan Bobcat and HD Construction Equipment operate in the same field, while Soosan Cebotics is a mid-sized maker focused on attachments and special equipment. Its sales scale is smaller than that of the large equipment makers.

04

Earnings analysis

Annual sales moved from KRW 286.3 billion in 2022 to KRW 226.5 billion in 2023 (down 20.9% year on year) and KRW 197.9 billion in 2024 (down 12.6%), before rebounding to KRW 229.4 billion in 2025 (up 15.9%). The largest sales year in 2022–2025 was 2022, and the smallest was 2024.

Operating profit went from KRW 22.6 billion in 2022 to KRW 16.4 billion in 2023 (down 27.4%) and KRW 12.1 billion in 2024 (down 26.4%), declining in each of those years before recovering to KRW 19 billion in 2025 (up 57.1%).

The operating margin fell from 7.9% in 2022 to 7.2% in 2023 and 6.1% in 2024, then rose to 8.3% in 2025, the highest of the four years.

The margin recovered alongside the sales rebound from the 2024 low, but whether the 2025 gain came from sales recovery or cost structure improvement needs confirmation from the full filing.

Net profit attributable to controlling shareholders was KRW 15.7 billion in 2022, KRW 16.9 billion in 2023 (up 7.1%), KRW 9.23 billion in 2024 (down 45.3%) and KRW 15.8 billion in 2025 (up 71.7%).

Net profit including non-controlling interests was reported as KRW 15.7 billion, KRW 16.9 billion, KRW 9.23 billion and KRW 15.8 billion for the same years. Operating cash flow rose from KRW 1.58 billion in 2022 to KRW 21.4 billion in 2023 and KRW 23.2 billion in 2024, then fell to KRW 8.65 billion in 2025.

Because operating cash flow declined in 2025 even as operating profit grew, working capital movements are worth examining as the source of the gap between profit and cash. The debt ratio fell from 88.9% in 2022 to 49.9% in 2023 and 27.4% in 2024, then edged up to 34.3% in 2025.

At the end of 2025, total equity was KRW 199.6 billion, equity attributable to controlling shareholders was KRW 199.6 billion, and total liabilities were KRW 68.4 billion.

Quarterly sales were KRW 52.6 billion in Q3 2025, KRW 71.9 billion in Q4 2025 (up 36.7% quarter on quarter), KRW 47.3 billion in Q1 2026 (down 34.2%), KRW 73 billion in Q2 2026 (up 54.3%) and KRW 47.6 billion in Q3 2026 (down 34.8% quarter on quarter and down 9.6% year on year), with swings of more than 30% repeating.

Operating profit was KRW 4.11 billion in Q3 2025, KRW 6.17 billion in Q4 2025, KRW 2.81 billion in Q1 2026 and KRW 6.44 billion in Q2 2026, peaking in Q2 before falling to KRW 2.39 billion in Q3 2026 (down 41.8% year on year).

Net profit attributable to controlling shareholders was KRW 3.92 billion in Q3 2025, KRW 6.38 billion in Q4 2025, KRW 2.93 billion in Q1 2026 and KRW 5.31 billion in Q2 2026, and then a net loss of KRW 972 million in Q3 2026, a swing into the red.

Net profit attributable to controlling shareholders over the latest four quarters totaled KRW 13.6 billion. The sharp quarterly swings may reflect delivery timing, but the filings do not state a cause.

05

Industry analysis

Demand for construction machinery moves with domestic and overseas construction activity and infrastructure spending.

In the special equipment segment, oversupply has persisted after the government-backed old equipment replacement program ended, and the company is responding by widening its line-up and developing new products.

The hydraulic drill market has shrunk, and the company is pursuing new products and entry into large mining markets. On demand, the Middle East, Southeast Asia and North America are the key target regions with strong infrastructure spending.

Market position is summarized by a sixth-place global share for its hydraulic breaker, as reported in April 2025, and a domestic top ranking among construction equipment makers.

Listed peers in the same machinery and equipment sector include Doosan Bobcat, HD Construction Equipment, Rainbow Robotics and Doosan Robotics. Sharing a sector classification does not make the businesses alike, so comparisons with robotics companies remain at the level of the direction signaled by the company name.

Overseas orders and export margins can also be affected by macro variables such as exchange rates and raw material prices. Oversupply in special equipment and a shrinking hydraulic drill market at the same time form the biggest constraint on the industry outlook.

06

Outlook

The most concrete overseas plan confirmed in filings is the expansion of production capacity in Vietnam. A land preliminary contract was signed on February 6, 2026, and the main contract will proceed after Vietnam IRC approval, according to a corrected filing. The land use period is recorded through May 26, 2060.

The acquisition amount is KRW 1.93 billion, or 8.2% of total assets. On May 7, 2026, the company decided on a loan to its Vietnam subsidiary, a transaction above 5% of equity that required disclosure.

At the March 31, 2026 general meeting, electricity generation and sales from new and renewable energy was added to the business purpose. How the new business direction connects with overseas production expansion will be clarified in future annual reports and filings.

On exports, the company has outlined expanding supply to the Middle East, Southeast Asia and North America while strengthening its large hydraulic breaker line-up. In hydraulic drills, new product development and entry into large mining projects remain open tasks.

The Q4 2026 results have not yet appeared in a periodic report, so full-year 2026 results will be fixed in the annual report due around late March 2027. Until then, whether the large quarterly swings and the Q3 2026 loss continue is the nearest checkpoint.

08

Bull factors

Annual earnings recovery and a higher margin

Sales in 2025 reached KRW 229.4 billion, up 15.9%, and operating profit reached KRW 19 billion, up 57.1%. The 8.3% operating margin is the highest of 2022–2025. Net profit attributable to controlling shareholders rose 71.7% to KRW 15.8 billion, which supports the case that earnings recovered from the 2024 low.

Overseas production base and export mix

Building on its Vietnam production subsidiary and US subsidiary, the company is widening overseas reach, and the Vietnam land acquisition and the loan to its Vietnam subsidiary are confirmed in filings. A high export share can be read as partly offsetting a slowdown in domestic construction. Strengthening the large hydraulic breaker line-up targets demand in the Middle East and North America.

A low-leverage capital structure

The debt ratio fell from 88.9% in 2022 to 27.4% in 2024 and stood at 34.3% in 2025. At the end of 2025, total liabilities were KRW 68.4 billion against total equity of KRW 199.6 billion. A low debt burden gives room for overseas facility investment and business transition.

09

Bear factors

Q3 2026 swing to a loss and quarterly volatility

Q3 2026 swung to a net loss attributable to controlling shareholders of KRW 972 million, and operating profit of KRW 2.39 billion fell 41.8% year on year. Sales of KRW 47.6 billion were down 9.6% from a year earlier.

Net profit attributable to controlling shareholders totaled KRW 13.6 billion over the latest four quarters, but the quarter-to-quarter spread is wide, leaving earnings stability as an open issue.

Slower operating cash flow

Operating cash flow fell from KRW 23.2 billion in 2024 to KRW 8.65 billion in 2025, even as operating profit grew 57.1% to KRW 19 billion. How much of the profit converted into cash is the bearish question. If working capital build-up caused the gap, the pace of collections becomes important.

Oversupply in special equipment and a shrinking drill market

The special equipment segment has remained oversupplied since the government-backed old equipment replacement program ended. The hydraulic drill market has shrunk, and although the company is pursuing large mining entry and new products, results have not yet shown up in filings. If demand in these two segments does not recover, top-line growth could stay limited.

Risk factors

Policy and demand risk

With the government-backed replacement program over, oversupply in special equipment persists, and delayed domestic demand recovery could weigh on segment sales.

Because construction activity and infrastructure spending also drive overseas demand, project delays in the Middle East, Southeast Asia and North America are a variable. If the shift to larger equipment lines does not match demand, it could build inventory burdens.

Overseas subsidiary and funding risk

The main contract for the Vietnam land is set to proceed after IRC approval. The loan to subsidiary Soosan Vina Motor was above 5% of equity and required disclosure.

Pace of overseas funding and collection, along with exchange rate moves, could flow through to consolidated results, which makes the subsidiary's disclosures more important.

Share price volatility and float

According to the Company Monitor compilation, a related-party group led by the largest shareholder holds a majority stake, which limits the share of tradable stock. Shares closed up more than 9% on April 9, 2026 and reached the daily upper limit intraday on May 7, 2026, showing large swings. Short-term moves could widen depending on trading volume and the float structure.

Overall view

Soosan Cebotics recovered in 2025, with sales of KRW 229.4 billion and operating profit of KRW 19 billion, and its 8.3% operating margin was the highest of 2022–2025.

Q3 2026, however, swung to a net loss attributable to controlling shareholders of KRW 972 million, leaving the stability of quarterly earnings as an open issue.

Quarterly sales swung by more than 30% repeatedly from Q1 to Q3 2026, and net profit attributable to controlling shareholders totaled KRW 13.6 billion over the latest four quarters. The business is split between special equipment and attachments, with a high export share.

The Vietnam land acquisition and the loan to the Vietnam subsidiary confirm an expanding overseas production base. The special equipment segment faces oversupply after the government-backed replacement program ended, and the hydraulic drill market has shrunk.

On governance, the sole-CEO transition and board reshuffle took place on March 31, 2026. Strengths such as the earnings recovery and a low debt ratio coexist with weaknesses such as the Q3 2026 loss and slower operating cash flow.

The next checkpoints are the Q4 2026 results in the 2026 annual report and the progress of the Vietnam main contract.
