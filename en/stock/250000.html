02

Business structure

Boratr imports food ingredients and distributes them through wholesale and retail channels. Its largest revenue source is dairy product sales, followed by pickled products and pasta.

The company says it finds promising new items and quickly takes market share through its existing distribution network, signing exclusive contracts at an early stage to keep its market position against large distributors.

Its customers include luxury hotels, well-known restaurants and large retailers; a corporate information site counts more than 500 clients, while a 2023 press report cited more than 1,900 clients, so the counting basis differs.

The company attributed 2022 growth to wider distribution channels, including business-to-consumer sales, military supply and catering deliveries, along with price increases in the second half.

In manufacturing, it develops home meal replacement (HMR) products and supplies customers through a subsidiary, and it plans to expand online channels and fresh food for single-person households. Internally, it introduced ERP and RPA systems and said in November 2023 that it had automated 34 internal tasks.

Through the third quarter of 2025, revenue rose 17.8% while operating profit fell 20.9%, and rising raw material prices and logistics costs were reported to have limited profitability. Because its cost base depends on imported ingredients, costs are exposed to exchange rates, raw material prices and logistics expenses. Its differentiation rests on early exclusive contracts for new items and channel diversification.

04

Earnings analysis

On a disclosed basis, revenue rose from KRW 69.5 billion in 2022 to KRW 76.9 billion in 2023 (up 10.7% year on year), KRW 91.4 billion in 2024 (up 18.8%) and KRW 102.3 billion in 2025 (up 11.9%), with 2025 the largest year of the 2022 to 2025 period.

Operating profit peaked at KRW 13.1 billion in 2023 (up 23.3%) and then declined for two consecutive years to KRW 12.8 billion in 2024 (down 2.1%) and KRW 9.15 billion in 2025 (down 28.6%).

The operating margin moved from 15.3% in 2022 and 17.0% in 2023 to 14.0% in 2024 and 8.9% in 2025, so revenue growth did not offset margin compression, and through the third quarter of 2025 rising raw material prices and logistics costs were reported to have limited profitability.

Net profit attributable to controlling shareholders was KRW 17.3 billion in 2022, KRW 9.46 billion in 2023 (down 45.4%), KRW 9.49 billion in 2024 (up 0.4%) and KRW 230 million in 2025 (down 97.6%).

In 2022, that net profit exceeded operating profit of KRW 10.6 billion, which suggests non-operating items were reflected, and the breakdown should be checked in the annual report notes. Net profit including non-controlling interests was reported at the same amounts for 2022 to 2025.

Operating cash flow was KRW 2.61 billion in 2022, KRW 8.12 billion in 2023, KRW 427 million in 2024 and KRW 8.05 billion in 2025, and in 2025 it was well above net profit of KRW 230 million.

The debt ratio rose from 24.1% in 2023 to 81.6% in 2024 and 154.2% in 2025, when total liabilities were KRW 137.1 billion against total equity of KRW 89 billion.

Quarterly operating profit bottomed at KRW 1.48 billion in the fourth quarter of 2025, down 42.9% from the prior quarter, and then rose to KRW 1.51 billion, KRW 1.91 billion and KRW 2.04 billion across the first three quarters of 2026.

Third-quarter 2026 revenue of KRW 27.8 billion was up 4.4% from the prior quarter, but operating profit was down 21.1% year on year and still below the KRW 2.59 billion of the third quarter of 2025.

Net profit attributable to controlling shareholders turned positive at KRW 14.8 billion in the third quarter of 2026, a large gap from operating profit of KRW 2.04 billion, so non-operating and other one-off items should be checked in the annual report.

Industry analysis

The food sector is affected together by raw material price and logistics cost swings and by changes in distribution channels. According to a Stock Square tally of 41 food-sector companies on 2025 full-year results, the average operating margin was 5.58%, and Boratr's 8.9% sits above that average.

In the same tally, the sector average debt ratio was 110.58%, while Boratr's 154.2% is higher. Its net margin ranked in the lower group of the sector, reflecting net profit attributable to controlling shareholders of only KRW 230 million in 2025. Revenue scale is well below the sector average for listed companies.

The company attributes its growth to wider distribution channels, including business-to-consumer sales, military supply and catering deliveries. Within the sector, CJ Freshway and Hyundai Green Food also run catering and food ingredient distribution, which makes them natural benchmarks on scale.

Against large distributors, the company's company profile says it responds by taking early positions in new items and signing exclusive contracts. The sector average dividend yield in the same tally was 2.92%, so the dividend level should be read alongside earnings swings.

Whether the planned expansion into fresh food and online channels can shift the product mix away from processed foods should be checked in future segment disclosures.
