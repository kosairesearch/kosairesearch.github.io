Operating profit rose to KRW 4.15 trillion in 2025, yet fell to KRW 284.9 billion in Q3 2026, so quarterly earnings are swinging sharply.

02

Business structure

Hanwha runs a group of businesses spanning industrial explosives and defense, chemicals, construction, shipbuilding, solar, financial services and wholesale and retail trade.

The business segment table in the 2026 first-quarter periodic report lists explosives manufacturing, wholesale and retail, chemical manufacturing, shipbuilding, construction, solar and financial services.

Chemical manufacturing generated KRW 1.74 trillion in Q1 2026, or 8.12% of total sales, with petrochemical products such as LDPE from the Hanwha Solutions chemicals business as the core.

The defense line in the same report covers self-propelled howitzers, armored vehicles, precision guided weapons, conventional ammunition and radars, and recorded domestic sales of KRW 878.3 billion (56%) and export sales of KRW 682.4 billion (44%) at the end of Q1 2026.

The defense line is attributed to Hanwha Aerospace and Hanwha Systems. Construction covers building, civil engineering, plant work and real estate development and sales.

After the August 1, 2026 spin-off, Hanwha reported its Q3 segment results centered on construction and the global segment, which handles petrochemical trading and products such as nitric acid, primers and explosives.

Hanwha Aerospace and Hanwha Systems each publish their own disclosures as separately listed companies, and the earnings figures in this report refer to Hanwha itself.

04

Earnings analysis

Annual sales moved from KRW 62.3 trillion in 2022 to KRW 53.1 trillion in 2023 (down 14.7% year on year), then to KRW 55.6 trillion in 2024 (up 4.7%) and KRW 74.8 trillion in 2025 (up 34.4%).

After the 2023 low, sales rose in 2024 and 2025, and 2025 was the largest year while 2023 was the smallest in the 2022–2025 period. Operating profit went from KRW 2.52 trillion in 2022 to KRW 2.41 trillion in 2023 (down 4.1%), KRW 2.42 trillion in 2024 (up 0.2%) and KRW 4.15 trillion in 2025 (up 71.6%).

Because operating profit grew faster than sales, the operating margin rose from 4.0% in 2022 and 4.5% in 2023 to 4.3% in 2024 and 5.5% in 2025.

Net income attributable to controlling shareholders fell from KRW 1.31 trillion in 2022 to KRW 380.5 billion in 2023 (down 71.0%), recovered to KRW 773 billion in 2024 (up 103.2%) and dropped to KRW 372.5 billion in 2025 (down 51.8%).

Total net income including non-controlling interests was KRW 1.99 trillion in 2025, well above the controlling-shareholder figure, and the difference belongs to non-controlling interests.

Quarterly, a KRW 365.4 billion controlling-shareholder profit in Q3 2025 was followed by a net loss of KRW 153.2 billion in Q4 2025, a return to profit at KRW 144.9 billion in Q1 2026, KRW 440.7 billion in Q2 2026 (up 204.1% from the prior quarter) and KRW 51.5 billion in Q3 2026 (down 85.9% year on year).

The sum over the most recent four quarters was KRW 483.9 billion. Quarterly sales rose from KRW 17.8 trillion in Q3 2025 to KRW 28.6 trillion in Q2 2026 (up 33.5% quarter on quarter), then fell to KRW 13.6 trillion in Q3 2026 (down 52.5% quarter on quarter and 23.5% year on year).

Operating profit went from KRW 2.41 trillion in Q2 2026 to KRW 284.9 billion in Q3 2026 (down 88.2% quarter on quarter and 78.8% year on year). The Q3 provisional standalone figures include the business transfer gain from the spin-off, so pre- and post-split profits need to be compared on the same basis.

In the standalone construction segment, Q3 2026 sales were KRW 403.3 billion, down 43%, and operating profit was KRW 3.5 billion, down 81%.

05

Industry analysis

Construction faced clear headwinds from Q2 2026.

Standalone construction sales were KRW 57.7 billion in Q2 2026 and operating profit was KRW 5.8 billion, down 22% and 30% year on year, which the company attributed to completed large building and development projects and the base effect of a large project settlement in the prior year.

The company also cited the construction downturn and raw material costs. In Q3, construction sales fell 43% to KRW 403.3 billion and operating profit fell 81% to KRW 3.5 billion. The global segment's Q2 2026 sales rose 7% to KRW 353.5 billion, but operating profit fell 29% to KRW 4.9 billion.

Geopolitical tension in the Middle East lifted petrochemical prices, while higher ammonia prices raised production costs for nitric acid, primers and explosives. In Q3, global segment sales rose 15% to KRW 360.5 billion, helped by the currency tailwind for exporters and expanded petrochemical trading.

Among defense affiliates, Hanwha Systems reported provisional consolidated Q3 2026 operating profit of KRW 99.2 billion, up 60.5% year on year, with a backlog of KRW 8.87 trillion at the end of the quarter, of which KRW 8.40 trillion was defense.

Over the same period, Hanwha Aerospace's Q3 operating profit fell 46% to KRW 452.0 billion, as ground defense deliveries clustered in Q4 and a Daejeon plant production halt weighed on results. Defense affiliate earnings are structured so that quarterly swings are large, depending on delivery timing.

06

Outlook

Following the August 1, 2026 spin-off, Hanwha's business structure changed, and quarterly results are first disclosed on a standalone provisional basis.

In construction, the decline in sales from completed large projects and the base effect of last year's settlement are already in results, so the size of new orders and construction starts will determine the path to a sales recovery.

The global segment faces higher petrochemical prices and higher ammonia costs at the same time, so the direction of these two variables will shape margins. The currency tailwind helped export sales in Q3, so the effect of the won's movement on export margins needs to be checked quarter by quarter.

In defense, Hanwha Aerospace has said it is pursuing normal operation of its Daejeon plant in the first half of 2027 and described main deliveries as concentrated in Q4. Hanwha Systems ended Q3 with a backlog of KRW 8.87 trillion, which adds visibility to affiliate defense sales.

Operating profit rose to KRW 4.15 trillion in 2025 and peaked at KRW 2.41 trillion in Q2 2026 before falling to KRW 284.9 billion in Q3, so whether operating profit recovers after Q4 will set the next direction.

The 2026 annual report, the next periodic report, will be the first to finalize full-year segment results on a post-spin basis.

Segment Disclosure Reshaped After the Split

Bear factors

Sharp Drop in Q3 Profit

Operating profit fell 78.8% year on year to KRW 284.9 billion in Q3 2026, and controlling-shareholder net income fell 85.9% to KRW 51.5 billion. Sales fell 23.5% to KRW 13.6 trillion over the same period, so the profit decline was far steeper than the sales decline. Profitability weakened alongside lower sales in that quarter.

Construction Weakness

Standalone construction sales fell 43% to KRW 403.3 billion in Q3 2026, and operating profit fell 81% to KRW 3.5 billion. The loss of sales from completed large projects overlapped with the fading of last year's settlement effect. Construction operating profit had also fallen 30% in Q2, so the weakness was not confined to a single quarter.

High Leverage

Total liabilities were KRW 240.9 trillion at the end of 2025 against total equity of KRW 48.4 trillion. The debt ratio fell from 911.4% in 2022 to 497.4% in 2025 but remains high. Controlling-shareholder net income fell 51.8% to KRW 372.5 billion in 2025.

10

Risk factors

Raw materials, FX and geopolitics

Geopolitical risk in the Middle East moved petrochemical prices, and higher ammonia prices pushed up production costs for nitric acid, primers and explosives. The currency tailwind helped exports in Q3, but a reversal would work in the opposite direction. When raw material costs and exchange rates move together, margin swings in the global segment widen.

Construction cycle

Construction sales fell after large building and development projects were completed, and operating profit dropped as last year's settlement base effect disappeared. If new orders and construction starts do not follow, the sales gap could persist. The company cited the construction downturn and raw material costs together.

Comparability across the spin-off

Standalone provisional net income for Q3 looked much larger because it included the business transfer gain from the spin-off. One-off items of this kind can distort the apparent earnings level after the split.

Reading consolidated quarterly figures and standalone provisional figures in the same frame could lead to misreading.

Overall view

Hanwha posted 2025 sales of KRW 74.8 trillion and operating profit of KRW 4.15 trillion, showing an earnings recovery at the operating level since the 2023 low. Controlling-shareholder net income, however, fell to KRW 372.5 billion in 2025, and Q4 2025 swung to a net loss of KRW 153.2 billion.

Operating profit rose to KRW 2.41 trillion in Q2 2026 and then fell to KRW 284.9 billion in Q3, so quarter-to-quarter variation is large.

Provisional standalone net income for Q3 rose sharply because of the business transfer gain from the August 1, 2026 spin-off, so pre- and post-split profits need to be compared on the same basis.

Strengths cited include the improved operating margin, the defense affiliates' backlog, and the positive sum of controlling-shareholder net income over the latest four quarters. Weaknesses cited include the Q3 construction slump, cost pressure in the global segment, and a high debt ratio.

Valuation sits at a discount to asset value while earnings have narrowed, so how it is read depends on whether earnings recover. The next checkpoints are the Q4 2026 provisional results and full-year segment results in the 2026 annual report.
