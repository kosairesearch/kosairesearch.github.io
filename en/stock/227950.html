02

Business structure

N2TECH produces vacuum chambers and specialty vacuum valves that supply semiconductor manufacturing processes, making it a semiconductor equipment component maker.

Japanese-style business mix data from the aggregator JobKorea, as of end-December 2025, shows semiconductor equipment parts at 92 percent, investment and operations at 6 percent, and real estate leasing at 2 percent.

Because this is an external aggregation, it should be checked against the segment notes in the company's business report. Parts of this kind follow equipment makers' orders, so shifts in equipment investment tend to show up in sales relatively quickly.

The company previously ran an OEM business in LED fan lights and merchandise, while JobKorea records that the mask business unit at its Paju branch ceased operations. In April 2025 it set up a US subsidiary in Austin, Texas (N2TECH USA CORP.), and in September 2025 it opened an Incheon branch for real estate leasing.

After the change in largest shareholder in 2025, biotech and virtual-asset businesses were added to the corporate purpose clause, but according to press reports those businesses have not yet begun.

At end-2025, total liabilities were KRW 21.3 billion and total equity KRW 127.7 billion, and the business base remains concentrated in semiconductor equipment parts.

04

Earnings analysis

Annual sales rose from KRW 21.4 billion in 2022 to KRW 34.3 billion in 2023, up 60.3 percent, which was the largest year of 2022 to 2025. Sales fell 60.3 percent to KRW 13.6 billion in 2024, the smallest year, and recovered 111.3 percent to KRW 28.8 billion in 2025.

Operating losses were KRW 1.93 billion in 2022 and KRW 1.92 billion in 2023, a continued loss that narrowed, then widened to KRW 3.52 billion in 2024 before turning into an operating profit of KRW 9.44 billion in 2025.

The operating margin moved from -9.0 percent in 2022 and -5.6 percent in 2023 to -25.9 percent in 2024 and 32.8 percent in 2025.

Net income attributable to controlling shareholders was KRW 10.7 billion in 2023, up 1,301.3 percent, then a net loss of KRW 25.6 billion in 2024 that turned into a profit of KRW 19 billion in 2025.

In 2024 the operating loss was only KRW 3.52 billion while the net loss reached KRW 25.3 billion, so items outside operations weighed far more on consolidated results than operating performance did.

In 2025 the gap between operating profit of KRW 9.44 billion and net income of KRW 19 billion is similarly large, so non-operating items should be read separately from core improvement.

The company attributed the 2025 improvement to higher sales, a gain on disposal of affiliate shares, and the consolidation of a new subsidiary.

Quarterly, an operating loss of KRW 530 million in 3Q 2025 was followed by an operating profit of KRW 2.33 billion in 4Q 2025, and 1Q 2026 returned to an operating loss of KRW 1.79 billion despite sales of KRW 7.83 billion. Operating profit jumped to KRW 12.9 billion in 2Q 2026 and reached KRW 13.3 billion in 3Q 2026.

Sales rose for four consecutive quarters from 4Q 2025 through 3Q 2026. Net income attributable to controlling shareholders over the last four quarters totaled KRW 23.9 billion. Operating cash flow was KRW -5.58 billion in 2024 and KRW 4.38 billion in 2025.

05

Industry analysis

Demand for semiconductor equipment parts follows investment flows in chip manufacturing equipment. N2TECH's sales moved from KRW 13.6 billion in 2024, down 60.3 percent, to KRW 28.8 billion in 2025, up 111.3 percent, and quarterly sales kept rising through 2026.

A company summary of the first nine months of 2025 cited the benefit of domestic equipment localization as a major reason for the large improvement in results.

The localization trend can be read as an opportunity for domestic component suppliers, but its share of N2TECH's sales has to be checked against the detailed disclosures. Because quarterly sales swing sharply, the company appears exposed to changes in the industry cycle.

Listed companies in the same machinery and equipment sector include Sunic System, People & Technology and Protec. Competition within the sector depends on equipment makers' ordering competition and their willingness to adopt domestic parts.

At end-2025 equity stood at KRW 127.7 billion, which places N2TECH among the smaller companies in its sector, and its position in the cycle is best read through the quarterly trend.

Bear factors

Cash outflows to affiliates and lower cash balance

Investment-operations losses and quarterly volatility

In 1Q 2026 sales rose to KRW 7.83 billion, but the company posted an operating loss of KRW 1.79 billion. Press coverage attributed the loss mainly to large operating losses in the investment-operations segment.

Sales fell 60.3 percent to KRW 13.6 billion in 2024, and large swings in quarterly sales and profit make earnings harder to project.

Unrelated business purposes added to the articles

Right after the change in largest shareholder, a large number of biotech-related business purposes were added to the articles, but press reports say none of them has been started. Reporting notes the stated purpose was support for businesses run by the largest shareholder.

If capital is directed to affiliates' businesses rather than to growing core operations, investment in the core could be constrained.

Risk factors

Governance and related-party transactions

After the change in largest shareholder, N2TECH was brought into an affiliate group, and purchases of affiliate convertible bonds and loans followed. In May 2026 it directly acquired KRW 10 billion of an affiliate's 31st convertible bond. As related-party transactions grow, decision-making governance becomes an area for close review.

Earnings volatility and business concentration

Annual sales swung sharply, from KRW 34.3 billion in 2023 to KRW 13.6 billion in 2024 and KRW 28.8 billion in 2025. The company cited a gain on disposal of affiliate shares alongside higher sales as a driver of the 2025 improvement.

With business concentrated in semiconductor equipment parts, a shortfall in quarterly sales can move profit sharply.

Convertible bonds and changes in share count

A KRW 10 billion convertible bond the company issued in 2025 has been convertible since July 25, 2026, and its conversion price can be adjusted if the share price falls. In May 2026 a share consolidation changed the par value from KRW 100 to KRW 500, leaving 34,508,209 common shares outstanding. Changes in share count affect per-share comparisons and how price moves are read.
