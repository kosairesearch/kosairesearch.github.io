Cheryong Electric: Confirming an Earnings Recovery Is the Task

Cheryong Electric's sales and operating profit fell in 2025, and its first-quarter 2026 operating profit was the lowest in three years, while third-quarter 2026 operating profit rose sharply from the prior quarter, so the recovery now has to be confirmed against U.S. transformer contracts and tariff and competition pressure.

02

Business structure

Cheryong Electric is a power equipment maker founded in 1986 that took over the heavy electrical division of Cheryong Industrial in 2011 and has since centered on transformers. Its core product is the distribution transformer, and it also makes and sells switchgear and gas-insulated switchgear (GIS).

The company has widened a distribution-class lineup that includes buried-type transformers, cutoff switches for distribution circuits, lightning arresters, and seismic-resistant transformers.

A company official said in a June 2025 press report that it has no plans to enter businesses such as extra-high-voltage transformers. Exports accounted for 93.02% of sales in the first quarter of 2025, and the core overseas market is the U.S. distribution transformer market.

Domestically, demand comes mainly from public institutions, and press reports say public-sector sales account for more than half of total revenue.

The supply contract with the U.S. utility PSE&G was initially disclosed at KRW 44.12 billion, signed on December 22, 2025 and running through December 31, 2027, and a March 2026 press report described it as about KRW 53.1 billion.

In competition, HD Hyundai Electric mainly exports extra-high-voltage transformers, while Cheryong Electric focuses on distribution transformers. Sanil Electric, a peer, targets the private market with special transformers, whereas Cheryong Electric has a relatively higher share of public-sector orders.

04

Earnings analysis

Annual sales rose from KRW 86.1 billion in 2022 to KRW 183.9 billion in 2023 (up 113.7% year on year) and KRW 262.7 billion in 2024 (up 42.8%), then fell 14.7% to KRW 224 billion in 2025. Within 2022 to 2025, 2024 was the largest year and 2022 the smallest.

Operating profit rose from KRW 16 billion in 2022 to KRW 70.2 billion in 2023 (up 338.9%) and KRW 97.8 billion in 2024 (up 39.5%), then fell 31.5% to KRW 67 billion in 2025. The operating margin climbed from 18.6% in 2022 to 38.1% in 2023 and 37.2% in 2024, then eased to 29.9% in 2025.

Controlling-shareholder net profit was KRW 12.5 billion in 2022, KRW 56.4 billion in 2023 (up 351.8%), KRW 79.9 billion in 2024 (up 41.7%), and KRW 58.7 billion in 2025 (down 26.5%). Net profit including non-controlling interests was reported at the same amounts in each year, so for 2025 it is also KRW 58.7 billion.

The company attributed the 2025 decline to intensified competition from the stabilization of the North American distribution transformer supply chain and U.S. government tariffs, as stated in its February 9, 2026 disclosure.

First-quarter 2026 sales were KRW 34.7 billion (down 20.7% from the prior quarter), operating profit was KRW 5.89 billion (down 26.6%), and controlling-shareholder net profit was KRW 8.06 billion (down 9.3%), and a May 2026 press report said this operating profit was the lowest in three years.

Second-quarter 2026 sales rose 2.8% to KRW 35.6 billion, but operating profit fell 11.1% to KRW 5.23 billion, while controlling-shareholder net profit of KRW 18 billion (up 123.7% from the prior quarter) far exceeded operating profit.

That pattern suggests items below operating profit lifted net profit in that quarter, and whether they were one-off cannot be judged from the disclosed figures alone.

Third-quarter 2026 sales were KRW 38 billion (up 6.6% from the prior quarter and down 45.5% year on year), operating profit was KRW 23.3 billion (up 345.8% from the prior quarter and up 3.6% year on year), and controlling-shareholder net profit was KRW 12.1 billion (down 33.0% from the prior quarter and down 39.6% year on year).

Operating profit held near its year-earlier level while controlling-shareholder net profit fell sharply, so the two measures moved in opposite directions. Operating profit is large relative to that quarter's sales, so the consistency of quarterly figures is worth rechecking against the notes in the regular report.

Operating cash flow moved from negative KRW 17 billion in 2022 to KRW 39.5 billion in 2023 and KRW 95.7 billion in 2024, then fell to KRW 67.2 billion in 2025.

Industry analysis

Power equipment makers have generally been described as benefiting from AI data center investment and U.S. power infrastructure spending.

A March 2026 press report noted that companies in the peer group posted record profits, while Cheryong Electric's 2025 sales and operating profit declined, which it described as a contrast.

Industry observers have pointed to a product mix concentrated in distribution transformers and a reference base built mainly on public projects as factors that weighed on results.

In the U.S. distribution transformer market, an early-2025 securities-firm analysis said the supply shortage for distribution transformers had largely eased. The same analysis said large-project orders were declining while smaller spot orders were rising.

Domestic competitors cutting prices to enter the U.S. market was also cited as a burden. For the first quarter of 2026, the company's performance commentary cited the combined effects of global supply stabilization, intensifying competition, higher fixed costs from preemptive investment, and tariffs.

On the cycle, the market is generally read as having shifted from a supply-shortage phase to a competition phase. HD Hyundai Electric exports mainly extra-high-voltage transformers, while Cheryong Electric focuses on distribution-class transformers, so the two occupy different product positions.

Outlook

The 2026 corporate value enhancement plan that the company disclosed on March 17, 2026 sets out a profitability-focused management system, sustainable dividends, and development of new growth businesses.

Its implementation measures include strengthening cost competitiveness, improving production efficiency, and expanding R&D capacity.

Company profile material describes a focus on entering the medium and large transformer market and widening its product range, with capacity expansion pursued through facility investment and workforce development. The PSE&G contract runs through December 31, 2027 and sets the volume baseline for that period.

The most recent backlog figure reported is KRW 219.5 billion for the first quarter of 2025, down from KRW 305.6 billion a year earlier.

The company received a public-works bidding restriction, and a KRW 39.1 billion fine was reported alongside it, so the effect on orders from public buyers is a point to check in future order disclosures. If U.S. tariff burdens and competition persist, the impact on volumes and prices may continue.

Sales rose in two consecutive quarters from the second to the third quarter of 2026, and third-quarter operating profit was reported at KRW 23.3 billion. Whether the operating margin holds and sales recover in a sustained way are points to confirm in the 2026 annual report.

Bull factors

Rebound in Third-Quarter Operating Profit

Operating profit in the third quarter of 2026 rose 345.8% from the prior quarter to KRW 23.3 billion, and sales grew for two consecutive quarters starting in the second quarter of 2026. That sequence is consistent with the first quarter of 2026 being the low point. Year-on-year operating profit growth, however, was only 3.6%.

Long-Term U.S. Distribution Transformer Contract

The distribution transformer supply contract with PSE&G was first disclosed at KRW 44.12 billion and was described in a March 2026 press report as about KRW 53.1 billion, with a term through December 31, 2027. Replacement of aging U.S. grid assets and data center power demand are cited as longer-term demand drivers.

The contract adds to revenue predictability, although unit prices and tariff terms can differ from contract to contract.

Low Leverage and Shareholder Return Policy

The debt-to-equity ratio was 11.5% at the end of 2025, and press reports put the only borrowing-type liability at KRW 400 million of lease liabilities. Cash and cash equivalents reached KRW 50.6 billion at the end of 2025, up 116% from KRW 23.4 billion a year earlier.

The company stated in its 2026 corporate value enhancement plan that it will keep sustainable dividends and a stable capital structure.

09

Bear factors

Sharp Profit Drop in First Quarter 2026

First-quarter 2026 operating profit fell 26.6% from the prior quarter to KRW 5.89 billion, and a May 2026 press report said it was down 63% year on year and the lowest in three years.

A company performance commentary attributed the drop to the combined effects of global supply stabilization, intensified competition, higher fixed costs from preemptive investment, and tariffs. The 2025 operating margin of 29.9% was also below 37.2% in 2024.

Tariffs and Price Competition

An early-2025 press report cited domestic rivals lowering prices to expand into the U.S. distribution transformer market. Intensified competition was again named as a factor in the first quarter of 2026 performance commentary. U.S. government tariffs were also cited in the company's own disclosure as a cause of the 2025 decline.

Public-Sector Dependence and Cash Flow Pressure

Press reports say public-sector customers account for more than half of sales, and the company was reported to have received a six-month public-works bidding restriction and a KRW 39.1 billion fine.

Operating cash flow fell from KRW 95.7 billion in 2024 to KRW 67.2 billion in 2025, and trade payables declined 42.7% from the prior year. Third-quarter 2026 controlling-shareholder net profit fell 39.6% year on year to KRW 12.1 billion.

Risk factors

Tariffs and Trade Policy

The company's own disclosure cited U.S. government tariffs as a main cause of the 2025 earnings decline. Because export exposure to North America is high, changes in tariff policy could move both volumes and costs. The timing and size of any tariff change could affect quarterly results differently.

Customer and Order Concentration

Press reports put public-sector demand at more than half of sales, and the business is described as concentrated in a small number of product lines. The public-works bidding restriction could affect orders from public buyers.

The initially disclosed KRW 44.12 billion PSE&G contract equaled 16.8% of 2024 sales, so dependence on a single contract and customer also needs watching.

Cost Burden and Cash Flow

A first-quarter 2026 press report cited higher fixed costs from preemptive investment as a factor in the profit decline. Operating cash flow fell to KRW 67.2 billion in 2025 from KRW 95.7 billion in 2024.

Because the company is pursuing capacity expansion, the fixed-cost burden and the payback period on investment remain risks.

Overall view

Cheryong Electric is a power equipment maker focused on distribution transformers, and its 2025 results declined after the 2024 peak. Operating profit in the first quarter of 2026 was the lowest in three years, but third-quarter 2026 operating profit rose sharply from the prior quarter to KRW 23.3 billion.

Strengths include the U.S. distribution transformer contract, power infrastructure demand, a debt-to-equity ratio of 11.5%, and a dividend policy. Weaknesses include tariffs and intensifying competition, public-sector dependence and bidding restrictions, and lower operating cash flow.

Because both sets of factors coexist, the direction of earnings is hard to call in one direction. The next checkpoints are the 2026 annual report filing in late March 2027 and the first-quarter 2027 results around mid-May 2027.

The key questions at those points are whether the operating margin moves back above 29.9% from 2025 and whether sales hold near the KRW 38 billion level of the third quarter of 2026.
