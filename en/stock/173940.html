02

Business structure

FNC Entertainment was founded in 2006, listed on KOSDAQ in 2014, and runs a full-scale entertainment business centered on artist management and music and content production.

According to a Kiwoom Securities report dated May 19, 2026, the entertainment business accounted for 97.21% of 2026 revenue and the media content production business for 2.79%. The entertainment segment combines music revenue from albums, concerts and merchandise with management of actors and TV personalities.

Music revenue relies heavily on the global fandom of P1Harmony, which has grown mainly in the Americas. An independent research firm said the group's mini album No. 8, released in 2025, sold about 460,000 copies, up 35% from the previous release.

The portfolio pairs senior acts such as FT Island, CNBLUE and SF9, which support the earnings floor, with junior acts such as AMPERS&ONE, AxMxP and FLARE U, which drive growth. Actors including Jung Hae-in and Lowoon have been reported as part of the agency roster.

On the media side, subsidiary FNC Story handles drama production, and the 2025 drama Season of Spring was co-produced with SBS subsidiary Studio S and KBS subsidiary Monster Union. The Korea IR Council said junior artists secure content exposure before debut, for example through drama appearances.

In competition, idol-focused agencies such as HYBE, SM Entertainment, JYP Ent. and YG Entertainment are the usual comparisons, and Kiwoom described the company as shifting from a band-centered model toward an idol agency model. In that transition, how fast newer artists build fandom is the main variable for the earnings trajectory.

04

Earnings analysis

On a disclosed basis, revenue went from KRW 65.8 billion in 2022 to KRW 92.2 billion in 2023 (up 40.1% year on year), then KRW 86.2 billion in 2024 (down 6.4%) and KRW 102.4 billion in 2025 (up 18.7%), making 2025 the largest year of 2022 to 2025.

The operating loss was KRW 13.2 billion in 2022, KRW 7.57 billion in 2023, KRW 4.18 billion in 2024 and KRW 614 million in 2025, narrowing each year while remaining a loss. The operating margin moved from -20.0% to -8.2%, -4.8% and -0.6%.

In its 2025 results announcement, the company attributed the progress to selection-and-focus and business restructuring that began in 2023, which it said brought the business to around break-even.

The attributable net loss was KRW 17.6 billion in 2022, KRW 8.27 billion in 2023 and KRW 6.63 billion in 2024, then widened to KRW 11.8 billion in 2025. The 2025 net loss including non-controlling interests was also KRW 11.8 billion.

The company said the wider 2025 net loss reflected a valuation loss on convertible bond derivatives linked to a higher share price, which it described as a non-cash bookkeeping loss. Quarterly results show the same gap between operating and net results.

In the third quarter of 2025, operating profit was KRW 1.78 billion and attributable net profit KRW 740 million; in the fourth quarter, operating profit was KRW 428 million while the attributable net loss was KRW 8.21 billion.

In the first quarter of 2026, revenue was KRW 27.2 billion, operating profit KRW 803 million and attributable net profit KRW 1.66 billion.

Revenue of KRW 22.1 billion in the second quarter of 2026 was the lowest of the last five disclosed quarters, and that quarter recorded an operating loss of KRW 3.39 billion and an attributable net loss of KRW 4.4 billion.

Third-quarter 2026 revenue of KRW 29 billion rose 31.5% from the prior quarter but fell 1.7% from a year earlier, with an operating loss of KRW 615 million and an attributable net loss of KRW 3.7 billion.

Operating cash flow was -KRW 16.1 billion in 2022, KRW 14.8 billion in 2023, -KRW 12.2 billion in 2024 and -KRW 6.9 billion in 2025, so 2023 was the only positive year.

The debt ratio climbed from 115.3% in 2022 to 210.4% in 2023, 284.2% in 2024 and 464.4% in 2025, when total liabilities were KRW 90.7 billion against total equity of KRW 19.5 billion.

05

Industry analysis

Since the mid-2010s, the entertainment industry has reorganized around idol groups, and album sales and concert scale have become the main drivers of earnings.

Kiwoom Securities argued that the band-centered model was at a relative disadvantage because bands tended to have lower album sales and more limited venue scale. To respond, FNC Entertainment is widening its fandom through junior artists such as P1Harmony, AMPERS&ONE and AxMxP.

An independent research firm said overseas fandom centered on the Americas and Europe is lifting album, concert and merchandise revenue together. Album revenue swings sharply by quarter depending on release timing.

The Korea IR Council said weak first-quarter 2025 results reflected artist activity concentrated after the second quarter. The same report highlighted the company's strategy of introducing new artists through drama appearances and linking music with content production.

Kiwoom said faster fandom growth among junior IP could lift annual estimates if larger venue tours materialize. Compared with large agencies, the company's results depend on a smaller number of artists, so whether a single act releases an album can move quarterly results considerably.

Because album sales and concert demand rely more on overseas fans, conditions in overseas consumer markets are a major variable for the sector's performance.

06

Outlook

The company said in its 2025 results announcement that its business had reached roughly break-even and would now enter a full earnings phase. In music, it plans to continue expanding domestic and overseas fandom, growing junior artists and strengthening album, concert and merchandise activity.

An independent research firm said in a March 2026 report that two album releases were planned for 2026, with sales of 600,000 to 700,000 copies per album expected. The mini album No. 9, scheduled for release on March 12, 2026, was presented as part of that plan.

Kiwoom Securities estimated 2026 revenue at KRW 121.5 billion and operating profit at KRW 2.7 billion in a May 19, 2026 report, without giving an investment rating. However, operating losses in the second and third quarters of 2026 have left the actual trend further from that estimate.

Kiwoom added that if faster fandom growth among junior IP leads to larger venue tours, the annual estimate could rise.

On financing, the company issued a KRW 12 billion convertible bond in April 2026 and disclosed plans to spend KRW 4 billion in 2026 and KRW 8 billion in 2027 on artist investment and album, concert and content production. Spending on artist investment and production is therefore set to continue into 2027.

The next checkpoints are the full-year operating result and debt ratio in the 2026 annual report, followed by whether the first-quarter 2027 report shows continued quarterly profitability.

08

Bull factors

Fandom expansion around P1Harmony

P1Harmony has broadened its global fandom, mainly in the Americas, and is lifting album sales, concert revenue and merchandise together. An independent research firm said the mini album No. 8 released in 2025 sold about 460,000 copies, up 35% from the previous release.

The first quarter of 2026 delivered an operating profit of KRW 803 million, which bullish observers cite as evidence for the turnaround case.

Shift in earnings toward junior artists

According to an independent research firm, the share of profit from junior artists rose from 3% in 2023 to 59% in 2025. Senior acts such as FT Island and CNBLUE support the earnings floor while AMPERS&ONE and AxMxP drive growth in a generational handover. Bullish observers argue this structure could reduce the impact of any single artist's inactivity on results.

Linking drama production with IP strategy

Subsidiary FNC Story links newcomers' visibility, fandom and content production capabilities by introducing them through drama appearances, according to the Korea IR Council.

The 2025 drama Season of Spring was co-produced with SBS subsidiary Studio S and KBS subsidiary Monster Union, and overseas broadcasts were noted. Revenue in 2025 was KRW 102.4 billion, up 18.7% from the prior year.

Bear factors

Continued operating losses and renewed quarterly losses in 2026

Operating losses persisted from 2022 through 2025, and the second quarter of 2026 swung to an operating loss of KRW 3.39 billion. The third quarter of 2026 recorded an operating loss of KRW 615 million, turning from a profit a year earlier.

Compared with Kiwoom's May 2026 estimate of KRW 2.7 billion in operating profit for 2026, the trend since the second quarter sits well below that projection.

Rising debt and convertible bond burden

The debt ratio rose from 115.3% in 2022 to 464.4% in 2025, when total liabilities stood at KRW 90.7 billion against total equity of KRW 19.5 billion. In April 2026 the company issued KRW 12 billion of additional convertible bonds at a 3.0% coupon. According to the disclosure, outstanding bonds would total KRW 32.34 billion after that issuance.

Net income swings from non-operating factors

The attributable net loss widened to KRW 11.8 billion in 2025 from KRW 6.63 billion in 2024. The company said this reflected a valuation loss on convertible bond derivatives linked to a higher share price, which it described as a non-cash bookkeeping item.

Even so, the attributable net loss over the latest four quarters totaled KRW 14.7 billion, and operating and net results have often pointed in different directions.

Risk factors

Concentration on artists and IP

Junior artists accounted for 59% of profit in 2025, so the impact of newer IP activity has grown. The weak first-quarter 2025 results largely reflected timing, with artist activity concentrated after the second quarter, so quarterly results can swing widely when activity shifts to a later quarter. If a given IP's fandom growth slows or album schedules slip, quarterly revenue can move sharply.

Balance sheet and funding

The debt ratio stands at 464.4%, with total liabilities of KRW 90.7 billion well above total equity of KRW 19.5 billion. The company is relying on convertible bonds for funding and plans to spend KRW 8 billion on artist investment and production in 2027.

Because valuation of bond derivatives moves net income with the share price, quarterly net results can become more volatile.

Overseas fandom and concert conditions

P1Harmony's results depend on concerts and merchandise sales centered in the Americas, so changes in overseas tour schedules or local consumer conditions could shift the revenue mix.

When album and concert demand concentrates in specific markets, external factors such as exchange rates and local regulation carry more weight. A slowdown in overseas demand could push the operating result back into losses.

Overall view

FNC Entertainment's operating loss narrowed every year from 2022 to 2025, lifting the operating margin from -20.0% to -0.6%, and 2025 revenue of KRW 102.4 billion was the highest of the 2022 to 2025 period.

However, operating losses reappeared in the second and third quarters of 2026, so the direction of quarterly results is not yet settled.

The first-quarter 2026 operating profit of KRW 803 million and the second-quarter operating loss of KRW 3.39 billion within the same year are the starting point for reading the company.

On the balance sheet, the debt ratio rose to 464.4% at end-2025, and in April 2026 the company issued KRW 12 billion of convertible bonds at a 3.0% coupon. Net results have often diverged from operating results because of non-operating items such as convertible bond derivative valuation.

Bullish points include fandom growth around P1Harmony and the rising profit share of junior artists, while bearish points include the record of consecutive losses, the high debt ratio and quarterly volatility tied to activity schedules.

With no PER calculable on the latest four quarters of net losses, the operating margin and cash flow trends serve as the reference points. The next checkpoints are the full-year operating result and convertible bond balance in the 2026 annual report, followed by the first-quarter 2027 report.
