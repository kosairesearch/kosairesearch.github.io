LNG Containment Earnings Recover; Customer and Business Diversification Is the Test

Hankuk Carbon's operating profit rose 188.1% to KRW 131 billion in 2025 on LNG carrier insulation demand, and third-quarter 2026 operating profit still rose year on year. Quarterly revenue has been volatile, and the shipbuilding customers' LNG carrier capacity is cited as a ceiling on growth.

02

Business structure

Hankuk Carbon makes cryogenic cargo-containment insulation and carbon composite materials that go into LNG carrier tanks. The business runs on two axes, marine insulation and high-performance composites, and LNG carrier containment accounts for roughly 70% to 80% of revenue according to industry reporting.

Of 2025 revenue of KRW 908.8 billion, industrial products were KRW 856.6 billion (94.25%) and general products such as carbon sheets for fishing rods, golf and leisure were KRW 52.2 billion (5.75%), according to a data provider's tally.

Exports account for 85% of sales, or KRW 768.3 billion, so the company effectively supplies global shipbuilders. Its containment systems are delivered as a set of core materials, including rigid polyurethane foam and triplex.

The company holds GTT's membrane-type certification and, alongside its existing Mark III customers, began supplying NO96-SUPER+ containment for Hanwha Ocean in the first quarter of 2026.

Rising Mark III orders from large Chinese shipbuilders have made exports of higher-margin secondary barrier (SB) products a profit driver. At the end of 2025 the LNG carrier tank panel order backlog was reported at about USD 1.14 billion.

The company has also outlined plans to expand into defense, aerospace, automotive and electric vehicle materials. In 2025 it raised KRW 40 billion through exchangeable and convertible bonds to add new foaming lines.

04

Earnings analysis

Based on the annual figures in the DART periodic reports, revenue rose from KRW 369.3 billion in 2022 to KRW 594.4 billion in 2023 (up 61.0%), KRW 741.7 billion in 2024 (up 24.8%) and KRW 908.8 billion in 2025 (up 22.5%), an increase in each of the three years from 2023 to 2025.

Operating profit fell from KRW 24.8 billion in 2022 to KRW 16.5 billion in 2023 (down 33.6%), then rose to KRW 45.4 billion in 2024 (up 176.0%) and KRW 131 billion in 2025 (up 188.1%), an increase in each of 2024 and 2025.

Operating margin moved from 6.7% in 2022 and 2.8% in 2023 to 6.1% in 2024 and 14.4% in 2025, and 2025 was the largest year of 2022 to 2025 for operating profit. Operating profit grew far faster than revenue, so margin improvement rather than volume drove the profit jump.

In its 2025 disclosure the company attributed the higher profitability to revenue growth in its main business segments, internal cost reduction and currency effects.

A data provider's tally shows the cost of sales ratio fell to 76.6% in 2025 from 84.1% a year earlier, as higher LNG insulation prices and utilization lifted unit prices while the price of carbon precursor fell.

Profit attributable to controlling shareholders went from KRW 20.3 billion in 2022 to a net loss of KRW 13.4 billion in 2023, a swing from profit to loss, then back to KRW 20.3 billion in 2024 and KRW 101.7 billion in 2025 (up 400.7%).

By quarter, revenue and operating profit moved from KRW 221.1 billion and KRW 29.9 billion in the third quarter of 2025 to KRW 231.8 billion and KRW 38.3 billion in the fourth quarter (operating profit up 28.4% from the prior quarter), and press data put the fourth-quarter operating margin at 16.5% versus 8.9% a year earlier.

First-quarter 2026 revenue fell 8.6% from the prior quarter to KRW 211.8 billion, while operating profit rose 7.1% to KRW 41.1 billion.

The company said second-quarter 2026 revenue of KRW 243.4 billion and operating profit of KRW 50.4 billion (up 22.7% from the prior quarter) reflected LNG carrier ordering, currency effects and higher SB deliveries.

Third-quarter 2026 revenue was KRW 197.5 billion (down 18.8% from the prior quarter and down 10.7% year on year), and operating profit was KRW 32.6 billion (down 35.2% from the prior quarter and up 9.3% year on year), so quarter-to-quarter swings were large. Profit attributable to controlling shareholders over the latest four quarters totals KRW 103.5 billion.

05

Industry analysis

Demand for the company's products is tied directly to the LNG carrier ordering cycle. LNG containment is an essential material for keeping LNG liquefied, and LNG carrier newbuilding demand surged after COVID-19 as global energy supply chains were restructured.

The company said that demand for cryogenic containment materials, driven by strong LNG carrier orders, also led its results in the second quarter of 2026. Brokers expect the LNG ship ordering cycle to intensify in quantity in the second half.

On the other side, the LNG carrier capacity of HD Korea Shipbuilding & Offshore Engineering and Samsung's shipyard is cited at around 50 vessels, which could limit volume growth.

Daol Investment & Securities analyst Choi Kwang-sik said the resumption of LNG carrier construction in Japan means containment demand would stay flat or rise even in the worst case.

SK Securities said global LNG project expansion and Japanese shipbuilders setting LNG carrier production targets are favorable for volume growth. On competition, the company holds GTT membrane certification and supplies both Mark III and NO96 designs.

On inputs, a lower carbon precursor price helped costs, but polyurethane foam raw material prices remain a cost variable.

06

Outlook

The nearest business milestone the company has disclosed is a KRW 72 billion supply contract signed in May 2026 for cryogenic containment materials for LNG carrier tanks. The company said the contract runs through December 26, 2027 and that the period may change with vessel construction schedules.

In April 2026 the company invested about USD 20 million in a U.S. subsidiary, about KRW 30 billion, to expand containment manufacturing infrastructure linked to the MASGA shipbuilding cooperation project between Korea and the United States.

Entry into defense and aerospace materials has also been reported as becoming more concrete. The company said it will use funds raised through exchangeable and convertible bonds in 2025 to add new foaming lines with capacity close to about 40 vessels' worth of volume.

At the end of 2025 the order backlog was reported at about USD 1.14 billion, which underpins revenue visibility. In press coverage ahead of second-quarter results, brokers forecast 2026 revenue of KRW 946.3 billion and operating profit of KRW 181.4 billion, and 2027 revenue of KRW 994.8 billion.

SK Securities projected 2026 revenue of KRW 941.1 billion and operating profit of KRW 183.2 billion in a report just before the second-quarter release.

These estimates should be compared against the quarterly results already disclosed through the third quarter, and the fourth-quarter and full-year 2026 disclosures are the next points of comparison.

08

Bull factors

Structural margin improvement

Operating margin rose from 2.8% in 2023 to 14.4% in 2025, and second-quarter 2026 operating profit reached KRW 50.4 billion. Higher unit prices, growing SB exports to China and currency effects combined, according to the company and industry reporting. A lower carbon precursor price also supported margins. Whether this structure holds is the next point to check.

Customer and product diversification

A new shipbuilding customer adopting the NO96-SUPER+ design added to a customer base centered on Mark III. The May 2026 supply contract of KRW 72 billion is expected to translate into deliveries to that customer.

The April 2026 U.S. subsidiary investment is described as expanding manufacturing linked to the MASGA project. Plans to enter defense and aerospace materials also aim to widen the business.

Shareholder returns and capital structure

In March 2026 the company decided to cancel all 1.54 million remaining treasury shares. Exchange rights on exchangeable bonds backed by 1.15 million treasury shares were fully exercised in 2025. The debt ratio rose from 44.8% in 2022 to 79.8% in 2025, while total equity reached KRW 580.2 billion. Capital strengthening alongside shareholder returns is cited as a positive.

09

Bear factors

Customer capacity as a growth ceiling

The LNG carrier capacity of HD Korea Shipbuilding & Offshore Engineering and Samsung's shipyard is cited at around 50 vessels, raising concern that revenue could move sideways in the KRW 900 billion range.

Brokers' 2027 revenue estimate of KRW 994.8 billion sits above their 2026 estimate, but the 2026 operating profit growth estimate was presented as larger. The bear case rests on revenue growth lagging profit growth.

High quarterly volatility

Third-quarter 2026 revenue fell 18.8% from the prior quarter to KRW 197.5 billion, and operating profit fell 35.2% to KRW 32.6 billion. Profit attributable to controlling shareholders also dropped from KRW 36.6 billion in the second quarter to KRW 16.6 billion in the third. Even with a strong annual trend, large quarter-to-quarter swings are a bearish factor.

Currency, inputs and governance concerns

The company cited currency effects among the drivers of its 2025 profit improvement, and a reversal in the exchange rate could weigh on margins. Rising costs for raw materials such as MDI, a polyurethane foam input, could add cost pressure. The fact that all inside directors belong to the founding family is a governance point to watch.

10

Risk factors

Customer and order concentration

Most revenue depends on LNG carrier containment, and exports account for 85% of sales. Changes in major customers' construction schedules or ordering flows could shift the timing of revenue recognition. The company's statement that contract periods may change with vessel construction schedules ties into this risk.

Raw material and currency swings

A lower carbon precursor price helped the cost ratio, but raw material prices can move the other way. The company cited currency effects as a factor in profit gains in 2025 and the second quarter of 2026. If the exchange rate turns unfavorable, the pace of margin gains could slow.

Execution of diversification

Defense, aerospace and automotive businesses have been introduced as plans still in progress. How far they reduce reliance on containment will need to be checked in segment revenue in the next periodic report. If new businesses fail to deliver returns on capital invested, the investment burden could remain.

12

Overall view

Hankuk Carbon's revenue has risen in each of the three years from 2023, and operating profit rose in 2024 after the 2023 low. Operating margin moved from 2.8% in 2023 to 14.4% in 2025, and operating profit in the third quarter of 2026 still rose year on year.

The recovery reflects strong LNG carrier ordering, higher unit prices, SB exports and currency effects. On the other side, quarterly revenue has swung widely, and the LNG carrier capacity of major customers is cited as a growth ceiling.

The May 2026 Hanwha Ocean contract, the U.S. subsidiary investment and entry into defense and aerospace are attempts to broaden customers and products.

Broker estimates put 2026 operating profit in the KRW 180 billion range, but these came before the third-quarter disclosure and should be checked against fourth-quarter results. The next checkpoints are full-year 2026 results around February 2027 and the 2026 annual report due by the end of March 2027.
