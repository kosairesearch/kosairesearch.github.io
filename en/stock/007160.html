02

Business structure

Founded in 1971, Sajo Industries began in distant-water fishing and expanded into food manufacturing, distribution and meat processing, making it a diversified food company.

A domestic research firm estimated 2025 revenue mix at 31% fishing, 56% food, 7% livestock and 6% leisure and other; these are broker estimates, not company-disclosed segment figures. The food segment spans canned tuna, ham, fish cake, soy-based seasonings and seaweed, and accounts for more than half of revenue.

The firm is cited as the number-two player in domestic canned tuna. Tuna caught in distant-water operations is largely used for sashimi-grade products shipped mainly to Japan, and exports accounted for about 32% of revenue in the first half of 2025, so overseas sales depend on Japanese demand and raw-material access.

Because catching, processing and sales sit within one company, changes in catch volumes and raw-material prices feed directly into food-segment margins. Sajo SEA Food, Sajodaerim, Sajodongaone and Oyang share the Sajo name but are separately listed companies. A major shareholder, Sajo Systems, held 30.27% as of September 11, 2026.

04

Earnings analysis

Annual revenue fell from KRW 661 billion in 2022 to KRW 632.2 billion in 2023 (down 4.3% year on year), then rose to KRW 635.2 billion in 2024 (up 0.5%) and KRW 706.2 billion in 2025 (up 11.2%).

Revenue has increased for two consecutive years from 2024 through 2025, with 2025 the largest year and 2023 the smallest in the 2022 to 2025 window. Profitability moved more sharply.

Operating profit of KRW 55.2 billion in 2022 became an operating loss of KRW 23.9 billion in 2023, and a narrower operating loss of KRW 9.38 billion in 2024, before turning to operating profit of KRW 33.2 billion in 2025, with operating margins of 8.3%, -3.8%, -1.5% and 4.7% respectively.

Attributable net profit fell from KRW 80.1 billion in 2022 to KRW 19.2 billion in 2023 (down 76.0%) and KRW 4.63 billion in 2024 (down 75.9%), then turned into an attributable net loss of KRW 29.1 billion in 2025.

The attributable profit in 2024 despite an operating loss points to non-operating items that deserve attention alongside operating results. The 2025 net loss including non-controlling interests was KRW 42.8 billion, larger than the attributable loss; the difference reflects the non-controlling share.

By quarter, 3Q 2025 operating profit of KRW 11.9 billion and attributable net profit of KRW 15.6 billion gave way to a 4Q 2025 operating loss of KRW 5.79 billion and an attributable net loss of KRW 74 billion.

In 1Q 2026 operating profit returned at KRW 4.67 billion on revenue of KRW 143.3 billion, and 2Q 2026 revenue of KRW 186.7 billion and operating profit of KRW 16.4 billion improved, yet the attributable net loss widened to KRW 40.9 billion.

In 3Q 2026 revenue was KRW 177.3 billion, with an operating loss of KRW 1.39 billion and an attributable net loss of KRW 11.4 billion. At year-end 2025, total equity was KRW 636.2 billion, of which KRW 534.5 billion was attributable to controlling shareholders, and total liabilities were KRW 617.1 billion.

Operating cash flow went from KRW 27.4 billion in 2024 to -KRW 20.5 billion in 2025. Because operating profit improved while bottom-line results weakened, the size of non-operating and one-off items is the next thing to confirm in the regular filings.

05

Industry analysis

Sajo Industries faces two demand streams: Japanese demand for sashimi-grade tuna and domestic consumption of processed foods. Canned tuna, ham and fish cake margins move with raw-material prices and consumer demand.

A domestic research firm tied the company to the K-food convenience trend, arguing its food portfolio fits that shift. The food segment covers canned tuna, ham, fish cake, soy-based seasonings and seaweed.

Demand growth in convenience foods has not yet translated into steady profitability, however: operating margins were negative in 2023 and 2024, turned positive in 2025 at 4.7%, and an operating loss appeared again in 3Q 2026.

The distant-water fishing business is exposed to catch volumes, fish prices and vessel operating costs, so its earnings pattern differs from the processed-food business.

Compared with processed-food-focused listed peers such as Lotte Wellfood, Otoki, Nongshim and Samyang Foods, Sajo's differentiator is that it reaches back to raw-material sourcing, but that also means cost pressure rises when raw-material prices climb.

In domestic canned tuna, maintaining the number-two position matters, while in overseas sales Japanese demand is the main driver. The Sajo-named companies Sajo SEA Food, Sajodaerim, Sajodongaone and Oyang are separately listed with different business structures and should be read as distinct peers.

Bull factors

Return to profit and margin recovery in 2025

The operating margin improved from -1.5% in 2024 to 4.7% in 2025, moving out of the loss zone. Operating profit of KRW 33.2 billion in 2025 compares with an operating loss of KRW 9.38 billion in 2024.

The return to operating profit suggests some improvement in the cost and sales structure, although the same year also saw a net loss.

Operating profit improvement in 2Q 2026

Revenue in 2Q 2026 rose 30.2% quarter on quarter to KRW 186.7 billion, and operating profit grew 251.1% to KRW 16.4 billion. Following KRW 4.67 billion of operating profit in 1Q 2026, the quarterly series shows operating earnings recovering.

The link between a revenue rebound and a sharp profit increase points to operating leverage, although the 3Q operating loss means quarter-to-quarter swings remain large.

Export base and canned tuna market position

About 32% of revenue in the first half of 2025 came from exports, with most sashimi-grade tuna shipped to Japan. In domestic canned tuna the company holds a number-two position among leading players. A structure that links distant-water catching to processing offers an advantage in securing raw materials.

If Japanese demand holds and domestic processed-food consumption remains steady, this base can support the lower end of revenue.

In 2Q 2026, the attributable net loss widened to KRW 40.9 billion even as operating profit rose, so the gap between operating results and net income has not closed.

Operating loss and revenue decline in 3Q 2026

The 3Q 2026 operating loss was KRW 1.39 billion, compared with KRW 11.9 billion of operating profit a year earlier. Revenue for the quarter was KRW 177.3 billion, down 4.4% year on year and 5.0% quarter on quarter.

The direction of quarterly results reversed from a 2Q improvement to a 3Q deterioration, which is the main bearish evidence.

Rising leverage and negative operating cash flow

The debt-to-equity ratio rose from 83.4% in 2024 to 97.0% in 2025, with total liabilities of KRW 617.1 billion. Operating cash flow was -KRW 20.5 billion in 2025, reversing from KRW 27.4 billion in 2024. Of total equity of KRW 636.2 billion, KRW 534.5 billion is attributable to controlling shareholders.

How long the balance sheet can absorb continued losses while sustaining cash generation is the financial concern.

Risk factors

Raw materials and export dependence

About 32% of revenue in the first half of 2025 came from exports, with most sashimi-grade tuna going to Japan. A shift in Japanese demand or in currency conditions could move both sales and margins.

Catch volumes and raw-material prices also feed directly into processed-food margins, so this exposure is structural and requires tracking quarterly cost trends.

Financial and cash flow

The debt-to-equity ratio reached 97.0% in 2025, and operating cash flow was -KRW 20.5 billion. Cumulative attributable losses over four quarters reduce the equity cushion.

Even with operating profit, a drag from non-operating items and financing costs could weigh on earnings, so debt repayment capacity and cash generation both need confirmation.

Governance and shareholder structure

An affiliated major shareholder, Sajo Systems, is reported to hold 30.27%, and changes in major-shareholder holdings were disclosed in July and August 2026. A concentrated shareholder structure can limit the free float and trading activity.

Several listed companies with the Sajo name add a risk of confusion when reading company data, so governance changes should be tracked through regular disclosures.

Overall view

Sajo Industries returned to operating profit in 2025, with operating profit of KRW 33.2 billion and a 4.7% operating margin, yet the attributable result swung to a net loss of KRW 29.1 billion.

Attributable net losses have continued for four consecutive quarters from 4Q 2025 through 3Q 2026, totaling KRW 129.9 billion over the last four quarters. Operating profit recovered to KRW 16.4 billion in 2Q 2026 but turned to a KRW 1.39 billion operating loss in 3Q.
