02

Business structure

CHUNGDAM GLOBAL, founded in 2017 and listed on KOSDAQ, distributes beauty and lifestyle products to overseas markets. It began with a B2B model sourcing global brands for distribution, but its center of gravity is shifting toward S2C and live-commerce businesses that use influencers.

The company has built B2B distribution networks in the United States and Canada through which many brands are entering overseas markets.

Of consolidated revenue of KRW 96.0 billion in the first half of 2025, the cosmetics distribution segment accounted for KRW 71.0 billion, or 74%, and the non-pharmaceutical manufacturing and distribution segment for KRW 17.6 billion, or 18%.

Overseas sales in the same period were KRW 84.4 billion, up 21.6% year on year, and their share of total revenue rose from 57.5% to 87.9%. Customers skew toward small and independent domestic beauty brands rather than large brands, and the company positions itself as a platform opening overseas channels for them.

As of July 2026, brands participating in its pop-up and expo projects numbered 112. According to a February 2026 report, the company signed a vendor supply agreement with Joybuy, an e-commerce platform launched in France by JD.com of China. A 2022 securities report named JD.COM as its main customer.

On competition, intensifying competition in China's live-commerce market and a slowdown in consumption were cited as factors behind weaker results in its core Beauty and Life distribution segment. As of the first half 2025 semiannual report, eight of its 15 consolidated subsidiaries were loss-making.

04

Earnings analysis

On a DART-filed annual basis, revenue rose from KRW 216.3 billion in 2022 to KRW 237.8 billion in 2023, an increase of 9.9%, marking the peak year. It then fell 1.5% to KRW 234.3 billion in 2024 and 7.2% to KRW 217.4 billion in 2025, a decline in each of 2024 and 2025.

Of 2022 to 2025, 2023 was the largest year by revenue and 2022 the smallest. Operating profit went from KRW 7.7 billion in 2022 to KRW 5.74 billion in 2023, a decline of 25.5%, then recovered to KRW 6.87 billion in 2024, up 19.7%, before dropping to KRW 5.37 billion in 2025, down 21.8%.

Operating profit was largest in 2022 and smallest in 2025. The operating margin moved from 3.6% in 2022 to 2.4% in 2023, 2.9% in 2024 and 2.5% in 2025, staying at a low level.

Net income attributable to controlling shareholders swung to a loss of KRW 1.94 billion in 2023, turned positive at KRW 3.27 billion in 2024, and came to KRW 3.08 billion in 2025, down 5.9%. Net income including non-controlling interests is a different figure and was also KRW 3.08 billion in 2025.

Operating cash flow went from KRW 1.62 billion in 2022 to -KRW 10.9 billion in 2023, -KRW 8.6 billion in 2024 and -KRW 21.8 billion in 2025, a widening deficit.

The debt-to-equity ratio rose from 47.3% in 2022 to 118.0% in 2024 and fell to 77.0% at end-2025, when total equity was KRW 117.2 billion, of which KRW 86.4 billion belonged to controlling shareholders. Quarterly, Q3 2025 revenue was KRW 51.5 billion with operating profit of KRW 3.07 billion.

In Q4 2025 revenue rose to KRW 69.9 billion, up 35.8% from the prior quarter, but the company recorded an operating loss of KRW 5.46 billion.

Q1 2026 returned to operating profit of KRW 2.55 billion on revenue of KRW 48.3 billion, followed by KRW 1.95 billion in Q2 and KRW 2.59 billion in Q3, so operating profit was positive in all three quarters.

Net income attributable to controlling shareholders, however, moved from a KRW 6.3 billion profit in Q1 2026 to a KRW 1.98 billion loss in Q2 and a KRW 9.65 billion loss in Q3, with losses in two consecutive quarters.

Q3 revenue grew 9.3% year on year, but operating profit fell 15.5%, so the top-line growth did not carry through to earnings.

05

Industry analysis

CHUNGDAM GLOBAL's end demand splits into China's live-commerce market and pop-up and offline channels in the United States and Europe. In China, intensifying competition in live commerce and slower consumption have pressured its core distribution segment, according to company earnings commentary dated June 8, 2026.

In response, the company launched its TDMS solution, a platform that automatically connects brands with influencers, and said it would use its Greater China capabilities to extend S2C business to the United States, Europe and Southeast Asia.

In the United States and Europe, collecting local consumer purchase data through pop-ups and expos and linking it to shelf placement and long-term contracts is spreading.

According to a July 2026 announcement, the company planned to open additional stores in Southern California through August and to attend a Warsaw beauty expo in Poland in September.

Data on industry-wide demand and the stage of the cycle are limited, so changes in the sector are best read indirectly through the company's quarterly revenue and operating profit trends.

Because the company distributes other brands on an intermediary basis, its margin structure differs from firms that sell their own products, which helps explain an operating margin that stayed between 2.5% and 3.6%.

Against peers, its position rests on a small structure focused on overseas B2B intermediation and live-commerce platforms.

Outlook

Three directions are visible in the company's outlook. The first is overseas pop-ups and expos. As of July 2026, 112 brands were participating, and the company said it operates pop-up stores in key Western US shopping districts and is stepping up efforts in North America and Europe. The second is funding.

The proceeds of the KRW 32.8 billion bond with warrants issued in February 2026 were described as working capital for securing inventory and covering logistics and marketing costs when entering overseas offline channels.

The bond includes a call option under which the company can require early redemption at 100%, which the company said is meant to minimize dilution. The third is business expansion.

At the annual general meeting on March 31, 2026, the company added rare-earth neodymium magnet distribution to its business purposes, and it said this was aimed at securing new revenue sources tied to demand from electric vehicles, eco-friendly energy and advanced electronics.

How much this business will contribute to earnings does not yet appear in the DART-filed results. The nearest checkpoint is the 2026 annual report, where the next steps are overseas sales share, subsidiary-level profit and loss, and whether the new business has begun generating revenue.

Whether overseas expansion translates into operating profit recovery has not yet been demonstrated in quarterly results.

Bull factors

Wider overseas network and pop-ups

As of July 2026, brands participating in pop-up and expo projects reached 112, and numerous brands have entered the B2B distribution network in the United States and Canada. Overseas sales accounted for 87.9% of revenue in the first half of 2025.

Local purchase data gathered through pop-ups is used to shape participating brands' distribution strategies, which is cited as a strength. If overseas access converts into actual shelf placement and long-term contracts, the revenue base would broaden.

Quarterly operating profit recovery

Q1 2026 operating profit of KRW 2.55 billion turned positive from an operating loss in Q4 2025, and Q3 2026 operating profit rose 33.1% from the prior quarter. Operating profit was positive in each of Q1, Q2 and Q3 2026. Q3 2026 revenue was KRW 56.3 billion, up 9.3% year on year.

The operating margin itself is low, but staying positive at the quarterly operating level can be read as a sign of underlying business capacity.

New business line and funding

At the March 31, 2026 annual general meeting, rare-earth neodymium magnet distribution was added to the company's business purposes. The company described it as a new revenue source aimed at demand from electric vehicles, eco-friendly energy and advanced electronics.

It said the KRW 32.8 billion bond with warrants issued in February 2026 would fund working capital for overseas channel expansion. The vendor supply agreement with Joybuy can be seen as a link to European channel expansion.

Bear factors

Wider net loss in Q3 2026

The net loss attributable to controlling shareholders in Q3 2026 was KRW 9.65 billion, widening from a KRW 1.98 billion loss in Q2, and losses have now run for two consecutive quarters. The four-quarter total through Q3 2026 was KRW 9.46 billion.

Operating profit stayed positive, but a net loss appeared, so differences across profit levels, including non-operating items and taxes, need to be checked in the annual report. The longer losses persist at the net income level, the greater the pressure on capital capacity.

Gap between revenue and cash generation

Revenue in 2025 was KRW 217.4 billion, down 7.2% year on year, and declined for the second year in a row after 2024. Operating cash flow in 2025 was -KRW 21.8 billion, a wider deficit than -KRW 8.6 billion in 2024. The operating margin was 2.5% in 2025, below 3.6% in 2022.

The debt-to-equity ratio fell to 77.0% at end-2025 but was 118.0% in 2024, and both figures should be read together.

China competition and weaker cosmetics distribution

Cosmetics distribution revenue in the first half of 2025 was KRW 71.0 billion, down 27% from KRW 97.4 billion a year earlier. Q1 2026 results were described as affected by intensifying competition in China's live-commerce market and slower consumption.

Because overseas sales make up a large share, changes in local competition feed directly into results. Whether faster overseas expansion can offset the decline in China is the key question.

Risk factors

Financing and dilution

The KRW 32.8 billion bond with warrants issued in February 2026 would bring new shares into issue if the warrants are exercised, creating dilution. The company has said it holds a call option allowing early redemption, but the scale and timing of any exercise must be checked in the annual report notes.

Total liabilities at end-2025 were KRW 90.2 billion against total equity of KRW 117.2 billion, which makes the financing structure a variable. How financing costs and dilution interact with the pace of expansion is central.

Overseas working capital burden

Entering overseas offline channels requires advance inventory and logistics and marketing spending, so cash outflows come before revenue. Operating cash flow of -KRW 21.8 billion in 2025 reflects this.

With overseas sales at 87.9% of revenue in the first half of 2025, exchange rates and changes in local regulation also carry significant weight. Whether cash collection from overseas channels speeds up is the point to check.

Customer concentration and new business uncertainty

A 2022 securities report named JD.COM as a main customer, indicating dependence on a particular platform. As competition in China's live-commerce market intensifies, changes at key channels could directly affect revenue.

Rare-earth neodymium magnet distribution has only just been added to the business purposes, so the timing of any earnings contribution and supply chain terms remain uncertain. How much the new business changes existing distribution margins also needs to be confirmed.

Overall view

CHUNGDAM GLOBAL's annual revenue was KRW 216.3 billion in 2022 and KRW 217.4 billion in 2025, roughly the same level, while the operating margin stayed between 2.5% and 3.6%.

Quarterly operating profit stayed positive from Q1 to Q3 2026, but the net loss attributable to controlling shareholders reached KRW 9.65 billion in Q3 2026, marking two consecutive loss-making quarters.

The overseas pop-up and B2B distribution network has expanded to 112 participating brands, and overseas sales accounted for 87.9% of revenue in the first half of 2025.

On the other side, competition in China's live-commerce market, a slowdown in consumption, negative operating cash flow and the issuance of bonds with warrants remain financial burdens.

The P/E ratio cannot be calculated because of net losses over the last four quarters, and the price-to-book ratio sits near the low end of its historical range.

Strengths are the overseas distribution base and sustained quarterly operating profit, while weaknesses are the widening net loss attributable to controlling shareholders and weak cash generation.

With both sides weighted equally, the next checkpoint is the profit structure by level and cash flow in the 2026 annual report.
