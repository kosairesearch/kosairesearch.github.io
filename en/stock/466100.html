Revenue Rising, Losses Narrowing, Profit Still Ahead

02

Business structure

Clobot is a specialist in robot operation platforms, holding the CHAMELEON autonomous driving solution and the CROMS integrated control platform for mixed robot fleets as its core software assets.

According to the company, its industrial cleaning robot lineup R3 and an operation business built on the Charamel control solution for cleaning robots are spreading to schools and public facilities.

After a strategic partnership with Boston Dynamics in 2024, Clobot became the official distributor and solution provider for the Spot quadruped robot and obtained Spot System Integration Certification in January 2026.

The Spot business unit announced in late May 2026 that it had signed a supply contract worth more than KRW 2 billion with a domestic semiconductor plant. Its subsidiary ROAS handles robot hardware and automation solutions and leads entry into North American logistics automation.

ROAS recorded cumulative third-quarter 2026 revenue of about KRW 18.1 billion, operating profit of about KRW 1.3 billion and an operating margin of about 7.0%, according to the company. Doosan Logistics Solution (DLS) has been consolidated since September 2026, adding logistics automation system integration capability.

The company is pursuing an end-to-end orchestration model that combines CHAMELEON and CROMS with DLS's logistics technology, and it aims to build recurring revenue through operation, control and maintenance services after system delivery.

Its customer base is widening from semiconductor and automotive plants to logistics centers and public agencies, including a contract with the Korean National Police Agency. The company says its differentiation lies in covering design, integration and after-sales maintenance rather than stopping at hardware supply.

04

Earnings analysis

On an annual basis, revenue was KRW 24.2 billion in 2023, KRW 33.4 billion in 2024 (up 38.0% year on year) and KRW 41.4 billion in 2025 (up 24.0%), so revenue has risen for two consecutive years from 2024 to 2025, and 2025 was the largest year of 2023 to 2025.

The operating loss was KRW 5.76 billion in 2023, widened to KRW 7.49 billion in 2024, and narrowed to KRW 3.18 billion in 2025, with operating losses recorded in each of the three years. The operating margin moved from -23.8% in 2023 to -22.4% in 2024 and -7.7% in 2025.

The company attributed the 2025 improvement to revenue growth from higher orders, lower payment commission fees and efficiency in other selling and administrative expenses.

The attributable net loss narrowed from KRW 22.4 billion in 2023 to KRW 6.73 billion in 2024, and the net loss including non-controlling interests moved the same way.

At the end of 2025, total equity was KRW 60.4 billion, all of it attributable to controlling shareholders, against total liabilities of KRW 12.6 billion, and the debt ratio was 32.5% in 2023, 15.9% in 2024 and 20.8% in 2025.

Operating cash flow stayed negative at -KRW 3.58 billion in 2023, -KRW 7.52 billion in 2024 and -KRW 2.9 billion in 2025.

Quarterly revenue rose from KRW 10.7 billion in the third quarter of 2025 to KRW 17.9 billion in the fourth quarter, fell 54.6% to KRW 8.12 billion in the first quarter of 2026, edged up to KRW 8.86 billion in the second quarter, and reached KRW 21.7 billion in the third quarter, with two consecutive quarters of growth.

The company said the DLS consolidation from September was the main driver of the third-quarter increase.

Operating profit was KRW 771 million in the third quarter of 2025 and KRW 1.31 billion in the fourth quarter, then turned into operating losses of KRW 2.5 billion in the first quarter of 2026 and KRW 2.84 billion in the second quarter, before narrowing to a KRW 2.07 billion loss in the third quarter.

Third-quarter attributable net income of KRW 1.87 billion turned positive while an operating loss persisted, with financial income and other gains contributing, so part of the result is one-off in nature. Because quarterly revenue moves sharply with project timing, the annual trend is the more reliable reading.

05

Industry analysis

The robotics industry is expanding around demand for logistics automation and safety inspection in manufacturing and industrial sites.

Government support is also present: the Ministry of Trade, Industry and Energy selected Clobot as lead research institution for a spatial-intelligence robot foundation model (RFM) national R&D project.

The project carries total R&D costs of about KRW 7.3 billion, government funding of about KRW 5.9 billion, and runs from August 2026 through December 2029.

Seven institutions, including Samsung Heavy Industries, Inha University, DGIST and the Korea Institute of Machinery & Materials, are participating to develop autonomous movement for humanoid and quadruped robots in shipyards and factories with stairs and uneven terrain.

This aligns with the company's plan to combine spatial intelligence with CHAMELEON and CROMS and extend applications to shipbuilding, logistics, manufacturing and plants.

The company has said that domain expansion carries learning costs early on, and that operating efficiency and profitability improve once a sufficient volume of data accumulates.

The Spot distribution structure depends on Boston Dynamics as a global hardware maker, and in domestic semiconductor and automotive sites the company emphasizes its SI certification and a first-response process within 24 hours.

Overseas, the company is entering North American logistics automation through ROAS and pursuing Southeast Asian manufacturing and logistics automation under a memorandum of understanding with Malaysia's Moverobotics.

06

Outlook

The company has said it will focus on expanding joint orders and improving project cost efficiency to lift consolidated profitability, with 2027 as the target year for turning these efforts into results.

Joint bidding with DLS is intended to widen opportunities for large logistics automation projects, and standardizing software integration features is meant to reduce construction costs.

The company also plans to pair system delivery with operation, control and maintenance services to expand recurring revenue, and to extend its subscription robot service model (RaaS) into logistics. For 2026, the Spot business unit projected growth of about 70% over 2025.

Spot business backlog at the first quarter of each year rose from KRW 11.5 billion in 2024 to KRW 19.2 billion in 2025 and KRW 23.6 billion in 2026.

Among recent orders, a contract with the Korean National Police Agency's counter-terrorism division for quadruped robots, worth KRW 2.07 billion, was disclosed, covering June 8, 2026 to February 3, 2027.

The company also announced a KRW 970 million contract with a manufacturing customer in Gyeongju for a process automation system in June 2026. The KRW 7.3 billion national R&D project runs from August 2026 through December 2029.

On funding, of the KRW 200 billion rights offering decided in April 2026, KRW 162.3 billion was planned for acquiring securities of other companies, including DLS, and KRW 37.6 billion for operating funds. How this allocation was actually spent should be checked against later disclosures.

Bull factors

Accelerating revenue growth

Third-quarter 2026 revenue of KRW 21.7 billion rose 145.2% from the prior quarter and 102.4% year on year. Cumulative third-quarter revenue of KRW 38.7 billion grew 64.7%, and revenue has increased for two consecutive quarters. The DLS consolidation was the main contributor, and the expansion is tied to logistics automation capability.

Narrowing losses and segment efficiency

The operating margin improved from -23.8% in 2023 to -7.7% in 2025, and the third-quarter 2026 operating loss of KRW 2.07 billion was smaller than the KRW 2.84 billion in the second quarter.

Subsidiary ROAS reported cumulative third-quarter 2026 revenue of about KRW 18.1 billion and operating profit of about KRW 1.3 billion, an operating margin of about 7.0%, which shows a profit contribution from its hardware and automation business.

Expanding order pipeline

Spot business backlog at the first quarter of each year grew from KRW 11.5 billion in 2024 to KRW 23.6 billion in 2026. In 2026, a KRW 2.07 billion quadruped robot contract with a police agency and a KRW 970 million manufacturing automation contract were announced.

The national R&D project led by the company has total R&D costs of about KRW 7.3 billion and runs from August 2026 to December 2029.

Bear factors

Persistent losses and quarterly swings

Operating losses have been reported in each year from 2023 to 2025, and 2026 has continued with operating losses of KRW 2.5 billion in the first quarter and KRW 2.84 billion in the second, three consecutive quarters of losses.

The third-quarter attributable profit reflected financial income and other gains, so operating profitability has not yet turned positive. Quarterly revenue fell 54.6% from KRW 17.9 billion in the fourth quarter of 2025 to KRW 8.12 billion in the first quarter of 2026, which shows large swings.

Part of growth from the acquisition

The company attributed the main driver of cumulative third-quarter revenue growth to the consolidation of DLS. Organic growth excluding the acquisition cannot be separated from this data alone.

Whether the revenue increase translates into profit improvement in the existing robot software business therefore needs to be checked. Cost efficiency gains after the acquisition have not yet appeared in disclosed figures.

Dilution and capital burden

The rights offering decided on April 3, 2026 totals KRW 200 billion, structured as a shareholder allotment followed by a general public offering of unsubscribed shares. Of the proceeds, KRW 162.3 billion is earmarked for acquiring securities of other companies including DLS, and KRW 37.6 billion for operating funds.

Relative to total equity of KRW 60.4 billion at the end of 2025, the offering is large and dilutes existing shareholders as the share count rises. Operating cash flow of -KRW 2.9 billion in 2025 also shows that internal cash generation remains weak.

10

Risk factors

Integration risk

The key variable is whether the profitability and cost structure of the logistics system integration business improve as expected after the DLS consolidation. Large projects have long build periods and variable costs, which can widen quarterly earnings swings. The company itself has identified construction cost improvement and wider joint bidding as its priorities.

Dilution and share supply

The KRW 200 billion rights offering increases the number of shares outstanding. Because the stock is sensitive to robotics-related theme trading, its price can move sharply with changes in supply and demand. The timing and use of the offering proceeds should be checked in subsequent disclosures.

Partner and customer concentration

The Spot business rests on the partnership with Boston Dynamics, and a change in that relationship could undermine the business premise. As public and government contracts and large projects take a bigger share, quarterly revenue recognition may shift with the timing of orders.

Overall view

From 2023 to 2025, Clobot's revenue rose from KRW 24.2 billion to KRW 41.4 billion and its operating loss narrowed from KRW 5.76 billion to KRW 3.18 billion. In 2026, the DLS consolidation lifted third-quarter revenue to KRW 21.7 billion, the largest of the last five reported quarters.

Operating losses, however, have persisted for three consecutive quarters from the first quarter through the third quarter of 2026, and the third-quarter attributable profit reflects a mix of financial income and other gains.

Strengths include the CHAMELEON and CROMS software assets, a growing Spot backlog, leadership of a KRW 7.3 billion national R&D project, and the profit contribution from subsidiary ROAS.

Weaknesses include continuing losses, possible reliance on acquisition effects, and dilution from the KRW 200 billion rights offering. Because valuation is hard to compare on earnings, the relationship between market capitalization and net assets or revenue is itself a key observation point.

The next checkpoints are full-year 2026 results around mid-February 2027, the 2026 annual report around late March 2027, and first-quarter 2027 results around mid-May 2027. This report is provided for information only and does not include a buy or sell opinion.
