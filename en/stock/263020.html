02

Business structure

DK&D makes synthetic leather and nonwoven fabrics and manufactures sports caps on an OEM and ODM basis. Its business is organized into four units: synthetic leather, nonwovens, caps, and product distribution, and the company said all four units grew sales in the first half of 2026 versus a year earlier.

In synthetic leather, the company has extended its application into apparel materials for global brands such as Bose and Moncler, and it is also growing supply of military procurement items and automotive seat fabrics for domestic and overseas carmakers.

The cap business works with global brands including Tommy Hilfiger, Ralph Lauren and Carhartt under an OEM and ODM structure. Nonwovens are centered on the Vietnamese production unit DK VINA, where the company is raising the share of higher-value products such as nonwovens for footwear.

Product distribution also contributed to sales growth. A 2023 research report put end-market demand at 40% automotive, 30% apparel, 20% headsets and 10% other, and from the second half of 2023 the company reduced lower-margin automotive volume and broadened demand toward apparel and headsets.

Its competitive base rests on customer qualification, including vendor registration with global apparel brands and exclusive supply agreements. Data providers classify the company under WICS as textiles, apparel, footwear and luxury goods.

04

Earnings analysis

Annual sales on a disclosed basis were KRW 110.6 billion in 2022, KRW 89.8 billion in 2023 (down 18.8% year on year), KRW 112.5 billion in 2024 (up 25.3%), and KRW 142.6 billion in 2025 (up 26.7%).

Operating income fell from KRW 11.5 billion in 2022 to KRW 8.16 billion in 2023 (down 28.9%), rose to KRW 8.47 billion in 2024 (up 3.8%), and reached KRW 12.2 billion in 2025 (up 44.4%). Sales grew 25.3% in 2024, yet the operating margin of 7.5% that year was the lowest of the 2022–2025 period.

The operating margin moved from 10.4% in 2022 to 9.1% in 2023, 7.5% in 2024, and 8.6% in 2025. Net income attributable to controlling shareholders was KRW 7.06 billion in 2022, KRW 6.84 billion in 2023 (down 3.0%), KRW 6.85 billion in 2024, and KRW 7.88 billion in 2025 (up 15.1%).

Net income including non-controlling interests was KRW 8.65 billion in 2025, higher than the figure attributable to controlling shareholders. At the end of 2025, total equity was KRW 81.1 billion, of which KRW 76.8 billion belonged to controlling shareholders, and total liabilities were KRW 29.7 billion.

The debt ratio fell from 77.3% in 2022 to 37.2% in 2023 and was 36.6% in 2025. Operating cash flow was KRW 10.5 billion in 2022, KRW 12.5 billion in 2023 and KRW 12.3 billion in 2024, then dropped to KRW 4.11 billion in 2025.

On a quarterly basis, Q3 2025 sales of KRW 36.3 billion were reported by local media as a quarterly record, followed by KRW 38.8 billion in Q4 2025, KRW 39.7 billion in Q1 2026 and KRW 48.1 billion in Q2 2026.

Q2 2026 operating income of KRW 5.19 billion rose 139.4% from the prior quarter, but in Q3 2026 sales fell to KRW 36.8 billion and operating income to KRW 2.71 billion. First-half 2026 sales totaled KRW 87.7 billion, which the company described as a first-half record. Net income attributable to controlling shareholders over the latest four quarters totaled KRW 9.16 billion.

05

Industry analysis

Synthetic leather demand is split across apparel, automotive interiors, headsets and military procurement, and the company emphasizes a structure that does not rely on any single industry. Its 2026 disclosures also described lower dependence on any one business.

Automotive was the largest demand category in the past, but from the second half of 2023 the company reduced lower-margin automotive volume and shifted toward apparel and headsets.

In headsets, the company supplies fabric to a U.S. global audio company, while in apparel it is widening its application through brand collaborations with Bose and Moncler.

A 2023 Daishin Securities report cited the share of China in U.S. imports falling from 68% in 2010 to 48% in 2022 and pointed to a possible benefit from supply-chain diversification away from China. The same report said the cap business draws on its Bangladesh production base for price competitiveness.

Synthetic leather and nonwovens operate under customer qualification, so competitiveness is tied to customer relationships. Robot skin is at the stage of seeking entry into the humanoid robot supply chain.

06

Outlook

The company calls 2026 the first year of shareholder value enhancement and has set three strategic priorities: strengthening synthetic leather manufacturing competitiveness, executing strategic M&A, and entering advanced robot skin materials.

Its M&A approach targets companies that can create synergies with the core business. R&D investment in robot skin materials is being expanded. In April 2026, press reports covered cooperation with a global humanoid robot company and a strategic investment in autonomous driving company Stradvision.

On shareholder returns, the company set a 2026 payout ratio target of 24.4% and established a principle of paying cash dividends equal to 20% of separate net income. It plans cumulative shareholder returns of at least about KRW 8 billion over three years through 2027.

The shareholder return ratio for 2025 was 44%, above the original 34% target. In a Q1 2026 disclosure, the company decided to cancel more than 660,000 treasury shares. However, the company materials do not specify when robot skin and M&A will contribute to sales and profit.

The next checkpoints are the annual results and dividend payout in the 2026 annual report, and progress on shareholder returns through 2027.

08

Bull factors

Record first half and growth across all units

First-half 2026 sales of KRW 87.7 billion were the highest first-half figure on record, and the company said all four units grew year on year. Full-year 2025 sales of KRW 142.6 billion and operating income of KRW 12.2 billion were also the largest in the 2022–2025 period.

Synthetic leather expansion into apparel brands, military procurement and automotive seat fabrics is cited as the basis of this growth.

Expanded shareholder return plan

The September 2026 value-up plan set a 24.4% payout target and a principle of paying cash dividends equal to 20% of separate net income. The company plans at least about KRW 8 billion in cumulative returns over three years through 2027.

The 2025 shareholder return ratio of 44% exceeded the original 34% target, and in Q1 2026 the company decided to cancel more than 660,000 treasury shares. Execution will be confirmed in the next periodic report.

Robot skin as a new growth business

The company has set robot skin, the outer covering material for humanoid robots, as a mid- to long-term growth pillar and is raising R&D spending. It plans to use its synthetic leather technology and production capacity to develop next-generation robot materials and enter the global humanoid supply chain.

In April 2026, press reports covered cooperation with a global humanoid robot company. However, the scale and timing of sales contribution have not been specified.

09

Bear factors

Wider quarterly earnings swings

Q3 2026 sales of KRW 36.8 billion fell 23.4% from the prior quarter, and operating income fell 47.8% to KRW 2.71 billion. Year on year, operating income declined 16.2% and net income attributable to controlling shareholders declined 72.9%.

Compared with Q2 2026 operating income of KRW 5.19 billion, the gap between quarters is wide, which is viewed as a weakness in earnings stability.

Limits to margin recovery

The operating margin fell from 10.4% in 2022 to 7.5% in 2024 and recovered to 8.6% in 2025, still below the 2022 level. Operating cash flow dropped to KRW 4.11 billion in 2025 from KRW 12.3 billion in 2024. Profit growth without matching cash generation is a bearish factor.

Investment burden and execution risk

Robot skin R&D, strategic M&A and strategic investments are proceeding at the same time, so the investment burden could grow alongside core earnings. Because the timing of sales contribution from new businesses has not been specified, the payback period is hard to estimate. Core demand spans apparel, automotive and headsets, so quarterly results may swing with industry conditions.

Risk factors

Quarterly earnings volatility

Sales fell from KRW 48.1 billion in Q2 2026 to KRW 36.8 billion in Q3, showing large quarterly swings. Net income attributable to controlling shareholders over the latest four quarters totaled KRW 9.16 billion, a small base in which single quarters can weigh heavily on annual results. Whether first-half strength carries into the second half will be shown in the Q4 results.

Currency and one-off factors

The company said Q1 2026 profitability was maintained by a restructured business from expanded overseas exports and higher foreign exchange gains. Because exchange gains feed into reported earnings, net income can swing with currency moves. Operating income and net income can also move in different directions, which affects how results are read.

Customer concentration and execution of new businesses

Demand changes at major customers such as Bose, Moncler and automotive carmakers feed directly into results. Robot skin supply contracts and mass-production schedules have not been disclosed, leaving execution risk in the new business. Acquired companies would also bring integration risk after any M&A.

12

Overall view

DK&D posted record first-half 2026 sales of KRW 87.7 billion, and full-year 2025 sales of KRW 142.6 billion and operating income of KRW 12.2 billion were also the largest in the 2022–2025 period.

Growth across all four units, synthetic leather, nonwovens, caps and product distribution, is the basis for a broader business foundation.

On the other side, Q3 2026 sales and operating income fell from the prior quarter, and net income attributable to controlling shareholders fell 72.9% year on year, confirming quarterly volatility.

The operating margin recovered to 8.6% in 2025 but remains below the 10.4% of 2022, and operating cash flow fell to KRW 4.11 billion in 2025.

On shareholder returns, the company has set a 24.4% payout target for 2026, reported a 44% return ratio for 2025, and planned at least about KRW 8 billion in cumulative returns through 2027.

Robot skin and M&A are presented as medium- to long-term initiatives, but the timing of their sales contribution has not been disclosed. The next checkpoints are the operating margin and cash flow in the 2026 annual report, and the execution of dividends and shareholder returns.
