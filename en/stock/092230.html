KPX Holdings: Quarterly Profit Swings Under a Holding Structure

KPX Holdings is a holding company built around its PPG subsidiary KPX Chemical, and its 2026 third-quarter operating profit rose even as it swung to a controlling-shareholder net loss, keeping quarterly earnings highly volatile.

02

Business structure

KPX Holdings is a listed holding company whose core subsidiary is KPX Chemical, a domestic leader in polypropylene glycol (PPG). KPX Chemical manufactures, synthesizes and sells PPG and has also made and sold electronic materials.

The electronic materials business was split off into a separate company in December 2025, which narrowed the group's center of gravity to PPG. PPG is a petrochemical intermediate, so its demand moves with the business cycle of manufacturing and construction customers.

Research spending concentrates on low-density, high-quality PPG, low-VOC and low-odor grades, and flame-retardant PPG for insulation. Overseas, the group runs manufacturing or sales entities in China, the United States, India, Vietnam, Thailand, Indonesia and Malaysia.

In March 2023 KPX Chemical acquired stakes in its Thai and Indonesian units and set up a Malaysian unit to widen its Southeast Asian footprint. Between October 21 and 28, 2026, KPX Holdings bought KPX Chemical shares on the open market, lifting its stake to 58.25% as of October 28, 2026.

According to a financial-rating aggregator, the subsidiary's 2025 revenue was KRW 613.4 billion, slightly over half of group revenue of KRW 1.16 trillion.

Customers are urethane producers and construction-related industrial users, and competition centers on KPX Chemical's domestic top position alongside its expanding overseas production.

04

Earnings analysis

Annual revenue slid from KRW 1.24 trillion in 2022 to KRW 1.21 trillion in 2023 and KRW 1.22 trillion in 2024 before reaching KRW 1.16 trillion in 2025, with 2022 the largest year and 2025 the smallest in the 2022 to 2025 window.

Operating profit peaked at KRW 65.4 billion in 2023, a 17.7% rise, and then declined two years in a row to KRW 51.1 billion in 2024 and KRW 43 billion in 2025. The operating margin traced the same arc, from 4.5% in 2022 and 5.4% in 2023 down to 4.2% in 2024 and 3.7% in 2025, the lowest of the four years.

Controlling-shareholder net income jumped from KRW 8.91 billion in 2022 to KRW 84 billion in 2023, a rise of 842.4%, then eased to KRW 77.8 billion in 2024 and edged up to KRW 79.3 billion in 2025.

Net income including non-controlling interests ran at KRW 137.4 billion in 2023, KRW 118.8 billion in 2024 and KRW 116.7 billion in 2025, well above the controlling-shareholder figure, which points to a sizeable minority share of profits.

Operating cash flow moved the other way, rising from KRW 109.6 billion in 2024 to KRW 119.5 billion in 2025 even as operating profit fell. On a quarterly basis, the fourth quarter of 2025 was the trough, with revenue of KRW 285.9 billion and operating profit of KRW 2.79 billion, down 80.4% from the prior quarter.

Operating profit then recovered to KRW 13.3 billion in the first quarter of 2026 and KRW 31 billion in the second, the highest of the last five quarters, before reaching KRW 14.9 billion in the third quarter on revenue of KRW 369.7 billion.

Controlling-shareholder net income went from KRW 22.2 billion in the first quarter of 2026 to KRW 214.1 billion in the second and into a net loss of KRW 103.8 billion in the third.

Because operating profit stayed positive while the bottom line turned negative, non-operating items or taxes likely carried heavy weight, and the breakdown should be checked in the notes to the quarterly report.

Press reports put the consolidated net loss for the third quarter at KRW 133.3 billion, against a net profit of KRW 60.9 billion a year earlier.

The core subsidiary KPX Chemical posted third-quarter 2026 revenue of KRW 264.3 billion, operating profit of KRW 10 billion and a net loss of about KRW 27 billion, after second-quarter revenue of KRW 245 billion, operating profit of KRW 23 billion and net income of KRW 61.2 billion.

Over the last four quarters, controlling-shareholder net income totals KRW 153.9 billion, a figure that includes the large second-quarter gain and therefore varies sharply from quarter to quarter.

05

Industry analysis

PPG is a petrochemical intermediate, so it is exposed to the cycles of construction and manufacturing demand. For the first nine months of 2025, KPX Chemical's revenue fell 4.2% year on year and its operating profit fell 16.6%.

A financial-rating aggregator puts KPX Chemical's 2025 revenue at KRW 613.4 billion, down 11.5%, and its operating profit at KRW 20.8 billion, down 44.8%. Domestically, KPX Chemical is the number-one player in the PPG market.

Overseas plants in China, Vietnam, Thailand, Indonesia and Malaysia spread exposure beyond a single home market. The December 2025 electronic-materials split makes PPG the main profit driver, so the petrochemical cycle reaches the holding company's earnings through a shorter path.

Compared with other holding companies in the sector, KPX Holdings' earnings depend heavily on one operating subsidiary. Margins are shaped by the gap between raw-material costs and selling prices. For the group's profit direction to change, PPG sales volumes and selling prices would both need to improve.

Outlook

The factors that will decide the next earnings direction are PPG demand recovery, overseas subsidiary profitability and whether non-operating gains and losses stabilize.

Operating profit rose in the first two quarters of 2026 but fell to KRW 14.9 billion in the third, so where it settles from the fourth quarter onward is the first operating checkpoint.

KPX Chemical's third-quarter operating profit of KRW 10 billion, down from KRW 23 billion in the second quarter, suggests that the recovery in PPG sales has not yet reached profit, so both volume and margin trends need tracking.

On the ownership side, KPX Holdings raised its stake in KPX Chemical from 57.09% to 58.25% through on-market purchases, bringing its holding to 2,415,540 shares. At the March 2026 shareholders' meeting, the 20th-term financial statements were approved and a cash dividend was resolved.

With the electronic-materials business now separate, the group's mix centers on PPG, and how later filings describe its segments will show whether that focus holds. The 2026 annual report is the next checkpoint for whether the annual operating margin exceeds the 3.7% recorded in 2025.

Whether the disclosures in the quarterly notes explain the non-operating items behind the third-quarter swing is also a point to track.

Bull factors

Domestic PPG leader and room for margin recovery

KPX Chemical is the number-one PPG supplier in Korea. The group's operating margin was 5.4% in 2023, so there is room on operating metrics to recover from 3.7% in 2025. Overseas production and sales bases across several countries spread exposure to weakness in any single regional market.

Operating profit recovery in the first half of 2026

Operating profit fell to KRW 2.79 billion in the fourth quarter of 2025, then rose to KRW 13.3 billion in the first quarter of 2026 and KRW 31 billion in the second. The third-quarter figure of KRW 14.9 billion was below the second quarter but 4.7% above the prior-year quarter. Third-quarter revenue of KRW 369.7 billion grew 28.2% year on year.

Stake increase and cash dividend

Between October 21 and 28, 2026, KPX Holdings bought KPX Chemical shares on the open market, raising its stake from 57.09% to 58.25%. At the March 2026 shareholders' meeting, the 20th-term financial statements were approved and a cash dividend was resolved. Strengthened control of the subsidiary and shareholder returns are seen as positive factors.

Bear factors

Third-quarter net loss and earnings volatility

Controlling-shareholder net income swung from a KRW 214.1 billion profit in the second quarter of 2026 to a KRW 103.8 billion net loss in the third, reversing direction in a single quarter. Because operating profit stayed positive at KRW 14.9 billion, non-operating items may have weighed heavily. Difficulty reading earnings trends over one or two quarters is a bearish factor.

Slower profitability at subsidiary KPX Chemical

KPX Chemical's third-quarter 2026 revenue rose to KRW 264.3 billion, but operating profit fell to KRW 10 billion from KRW 23 billion in the second quarter. The subsidiary posted a net loss of about KRW 27 billion in the same quarter.

Because PPG demand is tied to construction and manufacturing, a delayed demand recovery could postpone the subsidiary's profit rebound.

Revenue and margin trend and rising debt ratio

Revenue declined from KRW 1.24 trillion in 2022 to KRW 1.16 trillion in 2025, and the operating margin fell from 5.4% in 2023 to 3.7% in 2025. Operating profit decreased two years in a row in 2024 and 2025. The debt ratio rose from 30.2% in 2024 to 32.6% in 2025, so the change in financial capacity bears watching.

Risk factors

Industry demand

PPG demand is sensitive to construction and manufacturing cycles, so a slowdown could pressure both volumes and margins. Overseas units face local economic differences that add to earnings volatility. A narrowing spread between raw-material costs and selling prices would slow any margin recovery.

Non-operating items and earnings interpretation

Operating profit and controlling-shareholder net income moved in opposite directions in the same quarter, which raises uncertainty in interpreting results. The gap between controlling-shareholder net income and total net income is large, so the non-controlling structure must be read too. The items behind that gap are to be checked in the quarterly report notes.

Holding structure and business concentration

Group earnings lean heavily on the profit of one operating subsidiary, KPX Chemical. After the December 2025 split of the electronic-materials business, the portfolio narrowed around PPG. Whether the stake increases and dividends continue will be visible in the periodic reports.

Overall view
