Bloom Energy Bet Meets Consecutive Losses

Amosense is building a Bloom Energy-linked ceramic substrate business alongside its digital key modules, but operating losses in the second and third quarters of 2026 make the pace at which that revenue reaches quarterly results the key item to track.

02

Business structure

Amosense was founded in 2008 and listed on KOSDAQ in 2021 as a maker of electronic materials and components. Its business is divided into materials, modules, and devices and platforms, and the module segment was the largest by sales at 58.1% in the third quarter of 2025.

Key module products are digital key and wireless charging modules and automotive RF modules, while the devices and platforms segment includes smart trackers and IoT devices. The flagship materials product is the shielding sheet for wireless charging, which the company says it was the first to develop.

Named product customers include Samsung Electronics for smartphone wireless charging shielding sheets and Hyundai Motor Group for UWB digital key modules, and the company has supplied digital key modules to domestic automakers for about five years.

The new business is ceramic substrates for solid oxide fuel cells (SOFC), with first shipments to Bloom Energy completed in the first quarter of 2026. The company set a 2026 revenue target of KRW 10 billion for the ceramic substrate business.

In competition, Bloom Energy had relied on Japanese and Chinese suppliers for ceramic substrates, and Amosense has been cited as a key alternative supplier as the supply chain diversifies. Analysts also note that Chinese-linked vendors account for more than 80% of Bloom's customer supply.

The module segment is pursuing expansion in automotive electronics as carmakers shift toward software-defined vehicles.

04

Earnings analysis

On a disclosed basis, revenue rose from KRW 63 billion in 2022 to KRW 87.5 billion in 2023 (up 38.8% year on year) and KRW 134.2 billion in 2024 (up 53.4%), then fell to KRW 122.1 billion in 2025 (down 9.0%). Among 2022 to 2025, 2024 was the largest year and 2022 the smallest.

Operating profit was KRW 852 million in 2022 and KRW 796 million in 2023 (down 6.6%), then jumped to KRW 7.16 billion in 2024 (up 799.8%) before falling to KRW 5.32 billion in 2025 (down 25.7%). The operating margin was 1.4% in 2022, 0.9% in 2023, 5.3% in 2024 and 4.4% in 2025, sitting in the 4% to 5% range since 2024.

Net income attributable to controlling shareholders moved from a loss of KRW 905 million in 2022 and a loss of KRW 1.42 billion in 2023, which was a continued loss, to a profit of KRW 1.74 billion in 2024, a turn to profit, and then KRW 497 million in 2025 (down 71.4%).

Net income including non-controlling interests was KRW 497 million in 2025 as disclosed. One media report attributed the 2025 earnings swing to higher R&D spending on new products as a temporary factor.

On a quarterly basis, revenue peaked at KRW 33.6 billion in the fourth quarter of 2025 (up 18.8% from the prior quarter), after KRW 28.3 billion in the third quarter of 2025, and then declined for three consecutive quarters to KRW 27.8 billion in the first quarter of 2026 (down 17.3%), KRW 27.2 billion in the second quarter (down 2.3%) and KRW 26.9 billion in the third quarter (down 1.0%, and down 5.0% year on year).

Operating profit rose from KRW 36.25 million in the third quarter of 2025 to KRW 2.46 billion in the fourth quarter (up 6,695.9% from the prior quarter), then fell to KRW 531 million in the first quarter of 2026 (down 78.4%), followed by an operating loss of KRW 1.42 billion in the second quarter, which was a turn to loss, and an operating loss of KRW 1.98 billion in the third quarter, which was a loss that widened and a turn to loss year on year, making two consecutive loss quarters.

Net income attributable to controlling shareholders went from a profit of KRW 1.08 billion in the fourth quarter of 2025 to KRW 177 million in the first quarter of 2026 (down 83.6%), then a net loss of KRW 1.6 billion in the second quarter and a net loss of KRW 1.8 billion in the third quarter, and the four-quarter total of net losses attributable to controlling shareholders is KRW 2.14 billion.

Operating cash flow went from negative KRW 2.68 billion in 2022, negative KRW 6.36 billion in 2023 and negative KRW 494 million in 2024 to KRW 11.4 billion in 2025.

The debt ratio at the end of 2025 was 217.1% (141.4% in 2022), with total equity of KRW 43.2 billion, equity attributable to controlling shareholders of KRW 43.2 billion, and total liabilities of KRW 93.9 billion.

05

Industry analysis

The market Amosense serves splits into two areas: wireless charging for smartphones and wearables and automotive electronics, and fuel cell parts linked to power demand from AI data centers. In a January 2026 report, SK Securities noted that Bloom Energy plans to expand its SOFC production capacity to 2 GW in 2026.

In a February 2026 report, Hanyang Securities explained that SOFC demand is growing on shorter lead times of roughly six months to one year and lower upfront installation costs.

On the supply side for ceramic substrates, Bloom Energy had depended on Japanese and Chinese vendors and is diversifying its supply chain to reduce the share of Chinese suppliers, which brokers point to as the backdrop.

SK Securities estimated that vendors linked to China account for more than 80% of Bloom's supplier base. Ceramic substrates determine SOFC cell performance and yield, and durability, uniformity and mass-production stability are considered important.

In competition, Amosense is described as a domestic supplier newly entering this market, drawing on mass-production experience from its existing ceramic materials business. On the automotive side, the shift of vehicles toward software-defined architectures is cited as driving wider adoption of UWB digital keys.

Compared with large component makers in the same sector, Samsung Electro-Mechanics and LG Innotek, Amosense has a smaller revenue base and a business mix narrowed to materials and modules, so a change in any one business has a relatively larger effect on company-wide results.

Coverage in 2026 also pointed to wireless charging technology spreading into electric vehicles and robots beyond smartphones.

06

Outlook

The company's stated target is for the ceramic substrate business to start from KRW 10 billion in revenue in 2026 and grow steadily. In 2026 press coverage, a company official said initial ceramic substrate supply would begin in the first quarter, with full revenue recognition from the third quarter.

In the same coverage, the company said it would establish mass-production capacity of 600,000 units a year by the third quarter and was considering additional expansion.

However, the unit basis differs: the company's capacity figure was reported as an annual figure, while Hanyang Securities described 200,000 units a month rising to more than 600,000 units a month after a third-quarter expansion.

Brokerage estimates were DS Investment for 2026 revenue of KRW 136.4 billion and operating profit of KRW 7.8 billion (January 2026), SK Securities for KRW 137.7 billion and KRW 8.1 billion (January 2026), and Hanyang Securities for KRW 142.8 billion and KRW 11.0 billion (February 2026).

Disclosed revenue fell for three consecutive quarters from the first quarter of 2026 through the third quarter, and operating losses have continued since the second quarter, so there is a gap between these estimates and the quarterly trend.

For 2027, SK Securities saw ceramic substrate revenue growing two to three times annually if mass-production volumes expand, and DS Investment estimated 2027 materials segment revenue at KRW 49.3 billion (January 2026 report).

SK Securities judged that the operating margin of the ceramic substrate business could reach 20% to 30% depending on yield stabilization. For the existing module segment, new vehicle launches and higher adoption of digital key options were expected to support steady growth.

Whether ceramic substrate capacity turns into actual revenue can be checked in segment sales in the 2026 annual report.

Bull factors

Ceramic substrate business for Bloom Energy

Initial shipments to Bloom Energy were completed in the first quarter of 2026, and the company set a 2026 revenue target of KRW 10 billion for the ceramic substrate business. The company has said this business carries a higher operating margin than its existing businesses and could lift company-wide margins. Amosense is cited as a key alternative supplier as Bloom reduces its reliance on Japanese and Chinese vendors.

Automotive module and digital key base

The module segment was the largest by sales at 58.1% in the third quarter of 2025. The company has supplied digital key modules to domestic automakers for about five years, and 2026 brokerage reports cited wider adoption of models with digital keys as a factor for profitability improvement.

Expanded supply of UWB digital keys tied to the software-defined vehicle shift is cited as a growth path for this segment.

Q4 2025 profit turn and positive operating cash flow

The company returned to a controlling-shareholder net profit of KRW 1.08 billion in the fourth quarter of 2025, and operating profit that quarter was KRW 2.46 billion. Operating profit in 2024 was KRW 7.16 billion, up 799.8% from 2023.

Operating cash flow turned positive at KRW 11.4 billion in 2025, so cash generation improved even though operating profit declined that year.

Bear factors

Consecutive operating losses in Q2 and Q3 2026

The company recorded an operating loss of KRW 1.42 billion in the second quarter of 2026 and KRW 1.98 billion in the third quarter, making two consecutive loss quarters. The third-quarter loss was a turn to loss year on year.

The four-quarter total of net losses attributable to controlling shareholders is KRW 2.14 billion, and revenue declined for three consecutive quarters from the first quarter through the third quarter of 2026. The quarterly trend is some distance from brokerage estimates made in early 2026.

Financial load with a 217.1% debt ratio

Total liabilities at the end of 2025 were KRW 93.9 billion against total equity of KRW 43.2 billion, a debt ratio of 217.1%. The debt ratio rose from 141.4% in 2022 to 205.0% in 2023 and 199.0% in 2024, then to 217.1% in 2025. While operating losses continue, there is less earnings capacity to offset the debt burden.

Chinese-linked vendor share and yield variables

Bloom Energy's ceramic substrate sourcing had centered on Japanese and Chinese suppliers, and analysts say vendors linked to China account for more than 80% of its supplier base. How much of that share Amosense can win depends on orders and yields. If yield stabilization that brokers assumed is delayed, the operating margin they projected may not be reached.

Risk factors

Customer concentration risk

Sales growth in the new business depends heavily on Bloom Energy's SOFC capacity expansion and order growth. If Bloom slows its supply-chain shift or chooses other suppliers, ceramic substrate volumes may not rise as expected. The existing module segment also depends on model adoption by automakers and large device makers.

Financial structure risk

Total liabilities at the end of 2025 were KRW 93.9 billion, above total equity of KRW 43.2 billion. With a four-quarter net loss total attributable to controlling shareholders of KRW 2.14 billion, earnings are reducing the financial burden slowly. If operating losses persist, managing borrowing and repayment schedules could become more burdensome.

Capacity expansion and yield risk

The ceramic substrate expansion plan was presented as roughly 600,000 units a month by the third quarter of 2026, although coverage used annual and monthly units inconsistently. SK Securities judged that the operating margin could reach 20% to 30% depending on yield stabilization. If expansion is delayed or yields fall short, the 2027 volume outlook could be revised.

Overall view

Amosense is a materials and module company built on digital key and wireless charging modules and shielding sheets, and it is growing ceramic substrates for Bloom Energy's SOFC units as a new business.

On a disclosed basis, operating profit rose to KRW 7.16 billion in 2024 but fell to KRW 5.32 billion in 2025, and after a turn to profit in the fourth quarter of 2025, operating losses continued in the second and third quarters of 2026.

Revenue also declined for three consecutive quarters after peaking at KRW 33.6 billion in the fourth quarter of 2025. The revenue target for ceramic substrates in 2026 of KRW 10 billion and the brokerage outlook for higher volumes in 2027 have not yet been confirmed in quarterly results.

The debt ratio was 217.1% at the end of 2025, and the four-quarter total of net losses attributable to controlling shareholders is KRW 2.14 billion, so the pace of earnings recovery matters.

Positive factors are the expansion of the new supply business and the turn to positive operating cash flow in 2025, while negative factors are consecutive operating losses and financial leverage.

The next checkpoints are ceramic substrate revenue in the 2026 annual report and the operating profit trend in the first-quarter 2027 report.
