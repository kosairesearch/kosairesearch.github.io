Report overview

Holding Company Profit Under Domestic Snack Pressure

CROWNHAITAI Holdings is a pure holding company whose earnings depend on its snack subsidiaries. Revenue grew through 2025, but operating profit has fallen since its 2023 peak and has declined for two straight quarters since the first quarter of 2026.

02

Business structure

CROWNHAITAI Holdings was spun off from its food division in 2017 and converted into a pure holding company. Its income comes mainly from CROWN brand royalties, service fees and dividends rather than direct product sales.

The main confectionery subsidiaries are Crown Confectionery and the company that makes Ace and Honey Butter Chip. In 2024 the latter booked KRW 629.2 billion of sales and Crown Confectionery KRW 438.1 billion.

Core brands include Jolipong, Corn Chip and Ha-im at Crown Confectionery, and Honey Butter Chip, Oyes and Homerun Ball at the other subsidiary.

Production is centred on Asan in Chungnam, where a biscuit plant opened in 2022 and a snack plant was expanded in April 2024, and the new snack plant has annual capacity of up to KRW 240 billion.

Domestic sales account for more than 90% of consolidated revenue, and with no overseas manufacturing subsidiaries the company produces at home and exports. Overseas sales make up 6.2% of Crown Confectionery's sales and 8.8% of the other subsidiary's, both in the first half of 2026.

Two larger rivals run 21 plants in seven countries and 11 plants in four countries, respectively. In the first quarter of 2026 average utilization was 61.1% at Crown Confectionery, 69.0% at the other confectionery subsidiary and 26.8% at a smaller subsidiary.

04

Earnings analysis

Revenue rose from KRW 979.1 billion in 2022 to KRW 1.04 trillion in 2023, KRW 1.05 trillion in 2024 and KRW 1.07 trillion in 2025, growing for three consecutive years from 2023 through 2025.

Operating profit climbed from KRW 38.4 billion in 2022 to KRW 71 billion in 2023, an 84.8% increase that made 2023 the largest year of the period, and then fell to KRW 67.6 billion in 2024 and KRW 57.3 billion in 2025, a two-year decline.

The operating margin moved from 3.9% in 2022 to 6.9% in 2023, 6.5% in 2024 and 5.4% in 2025, so it has come down since 2023. Press reports on the first half of 2026 said the cost of sales ratio rose to 67.8% from 67.2% a year earlier, and the operating margin fell from 6.5% to 5.2%.

Attributable net income fell from KRW 23.8 billion in 2024 to KRW 18.3 billion in 2025, a 23.2% decline.

Net income including non-controlling interests was KRW 33.6 billion in 2025 against KRW 43.6 billion in 2024, and the gap between the two net income measures reflects the share belonging to non-controlling interests.

By quarter, operating profit peaked at KRW 14.9 billion in the first quarter of 2026, then fell to KRW 12.6 billion and KRW 7.61 billion, a two-quarter decline, and third-quarter attributable net income of KRW 2.32 billion was down 21.0% from a year earlier.

Operating cash flow fell from KRW 111.2 billion in 2024 to KRW 72.5 billion in 2025, but press reports on a filing said it rose 234% to KRW 15.7 billion in the first quarter of 2026.

Selling and administrative expenses rose 3.3% to KRW 70.5 billion in the first quarter of 2026 while gross profit fell 3.6%, and this was cited as the main driver of weaker profitability.

In the third quarter of 2026, both snack subsidiaries reported operating profit declines of more than 30% as naphtha and imported raw material costs rose. One non-recurring factor cited is that the tax reduction effect contributed significantly to net income in the first half of 2026.

05

Industry analysis

The domestic snack market is in a phase of slower growth amid weak consumer spending and a slowing economy. A shrinking school-age population is cited as a burden because it reduces the core consumer base for snacks.

A portfolio built around long-established brands is seen as limited in responding to consumers who prize new experiences. On distribution, reliance on large discount stores and convenience stores has grown, and competition has intensified with the entry of global brands, which raises selling costs.

On inputs, higher prices for naphtha-based and imported raw materials are squeezing costs. Among rivals, one operates 21 plants in seven countries and another 11 plants in four countries, serving local demand, which contrasts with the company's lack of overseas plants.

Industry observers note that lower utilization raises fixed-cost burdens and can push up unit production costs. The company raised its R&D spending ratio from 0.55% in 2023 to 0.63% in 2024, and it points to health functional foods and wellness products as new markets.

06

Outlook

The company has said it plans to improve profitability through new product launches and cost reduction. When it reported third-quarter 2026 results, management said it expects the effect of price adjustments to be reflected positively in future earnings.

Trend-driven products such as matcha and pistachio flavours, low-sugar lines and character collaborations are expanding. The company plans to cut logistics costs by using the automated logistics system at the new Asan plant, which sits near Pyeongtaek port.

On exports, the company has set a target of lifting the overseas sales share to double digits. Export lines centre on Jolipong and Corn Chip at Crown Confectionery and on Honey Butter Chip and Oyes at the other subsidiary.

The annualised inventory turnover in the first quarter of 2026 was 8.1 times, below the 8.6 times for full-year 2025, which indicates that products are selling through more slowly. Analysts say the timing of a earnings recovery depends on the success of new products and a normalisation of production efficiency.

The next checkpoints are the cost of sales and selling expense ratios in the fourth-quarter 2026 results and the 2026 annual report, and whether plant utilisation recovers.

Bull factors

Balance sheet repair continues

The debt ratio fell from 122.8% in 2022 to 96.8% in 2025, and total borrowings were KRW 254.3 billion in the first half of 2026, down from KRW 260.3 billion in 2025. Operating cash flow stayed at or above KRW 72.5 billion in each year from 2023 to 2025. Operating cash flow in the first quarter of 2026 was up 234% from a year earlier.

Asan plant and new product pipeline

The new Asan plant has annual capacity of up to KRW 240 billion and sits near Pyeongtaek port, which is described as advantageous for export logistics. Across the group, 122 new confectionery products were launched in 2024, and the R&D spending ratio rose from 0.55% to 0.63%.

Trend products such as matcha and pistachio flavours and low-sugar lines are being launched to attract younger consumers.

Room for cost savings and pricing gains

The company said it is stepping up internal cost reduction in response to higher naphtha and other raw material prices. It also said it expects the effect of price adjustments to show up in future results. Its plan to cut logistics costs through an automated system adds another lever on operating metrics.

Bear factors

Declining operating profit

Operating profit fell from its KRW 71 billion peak in 2023 to KRW 67.6 billion in 2024 and KRW 57.3 billion in 2025. The third quarter of 2026 brought KRW 7.61 billion, down 29.8% from a year earlier.

Attributable net income over the last four quarters totaled KRW 16.8 billion, and quarterly profit has declined for two straight quarters since the first quarter of 2026.

Cost and selling expense pressure

The cost of sales ratio rose 0.6 percentage points to 67.8% in the first half of 2026, and selling and administrative expenses rose 3.3% in the first quarter. Both snack subsidiaries reported operating profit falls of more than 30% in the third quarter as raw material costs climbed. Low utilisation is raising fixed-cost burdens, according to industry observers.

Domestic-centred model limits growth

More than 90% of consolidated sales in the first half of 2026 came from the domestic market, and the company has no overseas manufacturing subsidiaries. The shrinking school-age population, reliance on large retail channels and the entry of global brands all weigh on the outlook. Long-running brands are seen as limited in meeting shifts in consumer tastes.

Risk factors

Raw materials and costs

Higher prices for naphtha-based and imported raw materials feed directly into subsidiary costs. Both snack subsidiaries reported operating profit falls of more than 30% in the third quarter of 2026 for this reason. How much price increases can pass through to consumers will determine the impact.

Distribution and consumer environment

Heavy reliance on large discount stores and convenience stores can raise promotion and selling costs. The shrinking school-age population and rising competition from global brands are narrowing the core market. If new products fall short of expectations, higher selling costs could flow straight through to lower profit.

Ownership structure and free float

As of August 2025, the largest shareholder group held 55.02% of the shares, and as of September 2025 the free float ratio was 41.38%. A low free float can affect trading liquidity and share price volatility.

Because the holding company relies on subsidiary earnings and dividend policy, those links also need to be watched.

Overall view

CROWNHAITAI Holdings is a pure holding company whose earnings track its snack subsidiaries. Revenue grew for three consecutive years from 2023 through 2025, but operating profit has declined since its 2023 peak.

Operating profit in the third quarter of 2026 was KRW 7.61 billion, down 29.8% from a year earlier, and higher raw material costs and rising selling expenses are the main pressures on profitability. On the balance sheet, the debt ratio fell to 96.8% in 2025 and borrowings have declined, a clear improving trend.

Operating cash flow rebounded sharply in the first quarter of 2026, but low utilisation and weak profitability metrics mean some observers do not yet view it as a full earnings recovery. On growth, domestic sales above 90% of revenue and the absence of overseas plants are cited as constraints.

Strengths include the improved balance sheet, the Asan plant's production and logistics base and the new product pipeline, while weaknesses include falling profit, cost pressure and a domestic-centred model.

Valuation sits at a large discount to net asset value, but it must be read alongside the size of non-controlling interests and quarterly earnings volatility.

The next checkpoints are the fourth-quarter 2026 results and the 2026 annual report, and whether cost and selling expense trends change in the quarterly reports that follow.
