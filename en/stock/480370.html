Battery dry-room maker widens into semiconductor and data-center work

02

Business structure

Founded in 2004, CK Solution designs and builds dry-room and clean-room systems that control airborne moisture to improve battery quality and yield, and this dry-room business remains its core.

Its scope has widened from secondary-battery dry rooms to semiconductor clean rooms, data-center air conditioning, bio clean rooms, defense and nuclear power work. The CEO said battery work accounted for about 90% of total revenue in the past, while semiconductors reached roughly one-third of new orders in 2026.

In the same interview, he projected a mix of about 50% batteries, 40% semiconductors and 10% data centers and other business. According to FnGuide, the December 2024 revenue mix was 88.97% secondary batteries, 6.50% FED (defense) and 4.04% advanced semiconductors.

Technically, the company holds the exclusive domestic supply right for Nichias rotors and maintains dew points below -40 degrees Celsius in its dry rooms.

Its main customers are domestic battery makers including LG Energy Solution, Samsung SDI and SK On, and it is expanding to materials firms such as L&F and POSCO Future M. Overseas, LG Energy Solution and Honda's joint-venture plant in Ohio received KRW 25 billion of equipment supply, announced in April 2026.

Its overseas subsidiaries are in the United States, Canada, Mexico, Brazil, Hungary, Poland and Malaysia. Although classified under automobiles, its real customers are battery makers, so delays in battery capacity investment feed directly into order intake.

04

Earnings analysis

On a reported basis, revenue rose 37.4% from KRW 215.3 billion in 2023 to KRW 295.8 billion in 2024, the largest year in the 2023–2025 span, before declining 1.6% to KRW 291 billion in 2025. Profitability peaked earlier.

Operating profit fell 6.5% from KRW 17.7 billion in 2023 to KRW 16.5 billion in 2024, then turned into an operating loss of KRW 36.6 billion in 2025. The operating margin moved from 8.2% in 2023 and 5.6% in 2024 to -12.6% in 2025.

Net profit attributable to controlling shareholders climbed 31.2% to KRW 19.8 billion in 2024, but swung to a loss of KRW 40.1 billion in 2025. Net income including non-controlling interests was a loss of KRW 40.1 billion in 2025, the same figure as the controlling-shareholder loss.

Operating cash flow was KRW 17.7 billion in 2023, -KRW 38.7 billion in 2024 and KRW 16.3 billion in 2025, a wide swing.

Quarterly revenue rose from KRW 67 billion in the third quarter of 2025 to KRW 86.6 billion in the fourth quarter, up 29.2% from the prior quarter, while the operating loss widened to KRW 10.2 billion.

In the first quarter of 2026, revenue fell 37.2% quarter on quarter to KRW 54.3 billion and the operating loss deepened to KRW 16.2 billion. The second quarter of 2026 recovered to KRW 85 billion of revenue, up 56.4% from the prior quarter, and turned operating profit positive at KRW 608 million.

The third quarter brought revenue of KRW 107.7 billion, up 26.7% quarter on quarter and 60.7% year on year, with operating profit of KRW 10.9 billion.

However, third-quarter net profit attributable to controlling shareholders was KRW 1.05 billion, far below operating profit, which means non-operating items and taxes sharply reduced the bottom line. The controlling-shareholder net loss over the last four quarters totals KRW 26.4 billion.

The company attributed first-quarter weakness to delayed battery capacity investment that slowed orders, and credited second-quarter improvement to the end of legacy loss-making projects.

05

Industry analysis

Battery dry-room demand is tied to the capacity investment cycle of cell makers. The first-quarter 2026 operating loss of KRW 16.2 billion also reflected slower orders after major battery makers delayed capacity expansion.

The company says new projects tied to batteries and energy storage systems (ESS) are becoming visible in North America and Europe. It also says it is responding to customers' investments in converting lines to ESS.

Domestically, the company expects semiconductor capacity expansion to continue around the Yongin semiconductor cluster and at Samsung Electronics and SK hynix sites. In response, it is strengthening its semiconductor infrastructure construction capabilities.

On competition, the company describes rivalry as growing more intense. It points to its combined base technology spanning HVAC and refrigeration, clean rooms and dry rooms as a differentiator. Its exclusive domestic supply right for Nichias rotors is also cited as a technical link.

06

Outlook

The CEO set targets of 15–20% revenue growth in 2026, about 15% growth in 2027 over 2026, and a recovery of the operating margin to 4–5%. He also said the company aims to reach break-even in 2026 and build a growth base from 2027.

The company says it is approaching its cumulative 2026 order target of KRW 500 billion to KRW 600 billion. According to an October 2026 report, it began its first transaction with SK hynix on an advanced packaging plant project in Indiana, with a contract worth KRW 147.7 billion, equal to 30% of the annual target.

That plant will house an HBM mass-production line and an advanced packaging R&D test bed, and the company plans to supply next-generation HBM from the site to U.S. customers from the second half of 2029.

The company says the Samsung Electronics Pyeongtaek P5 finishing contract won in August 2026 and data-center projects will be recognized in revenue in sequence over 2027–2028.

In battery work, it signed a KRW 35.4 billion contract with LG Energy Solution Michigan for coater equipment installation work, running through December 31, 2026. In June 2026, it also signed a KRW 9.19 billion supply contract with Hanwha Systems for cooling-facility renewal at a data center.

For its own equipment business, it plans to build a manufacturing plant on about 4,000 pyeong of land in Asan, Chungnam and develop compact equipment under the CK brand for semiconductor and dry-room use.

Dependence on battery capacity investment timing

The company swung to an operating loss of KRW 36.6 billion in 2025 and posted a further operating loss of KRW 16.2 billion in the first quarter of 2026. Management blamed that weakness on delayed capacity expansion by major battery makers, which slowed orders.

The secondary-battery share of revenue was about 89% in December 2024, leaving heavy exposure to customers' investment cycles.

Cumulative losses and balance sheet strain

The controlling-shareholder net loss over the last four quarters totals KRW 26.4 billion, and one profitable quarter in the third quarter of 2026 has not erased it. Operating cash flow was -KRW 38.7 billion in 2024, and the debt-to-equity ratio rose to 193.0% at the end of 2025. Total liabilities of KRW 139.2 billion exceed total equity of KRW 72.1 billion.

Large swings in quarterly results

Revenue moved from KRW 67 billion in the third quarter of 2025 to KRW 54.3 billion in the first quarter of 2026, then to KRW 107.7 billion in the third quarter of 2026. Quarter-to-quarter swings are large, so the earnings trend can change sharply within one or two quarters.

The company says revenue from semiconductor and data-center projects will be recognized over 2027–2028, creating a timing gap.

Risk factors

Customer capex cycle risk

When battery makers delay capacity expansion, dry-room orders slow directly, as seen in the first quarter of 2026. The North American and European ESS projects remain at the level of company description, and the timing of their revenue contribution is not yet clear.

Project profitability risk in construction-based work

Dry-room and clean-room work is centered on design and construction, so cost overruns on individual projects can move quarterly results. The company said second-quarter results improved as legacy loss-making projects wound down.

Large semiconductor and data-center projects will be recognized over 2027–2028, which makes project-level profitability management important.

Balance sheet and share count risk

The debt-to-equity ratio rose to 193.0% at the end of 2025 from 106.8% in 2024. A bonus issue allocating two new shares for each existing share was decided in March 2026, increasing the share count.

With 12 consolidated overseas affiliates, currency exposure and the burden of managing overseas sites also come with the business.
