Report overview

Holding Company Sales Recover, Quarterly Profit Remains Volatile

Miwon Holdings is a holding company whose subsidiaries make energy-curable resins, inkjet materials and surfactants. Annual sales have risen for two straight years since 2024, but operating profit and net profit attributable to controlling shareholders swing sharply from quarter to quarter.

  1. 1

    Annual sales reached KRW 492.7 billion in 2025, rising for two consecutive years from 2024, with 2023 the smallest year in the period.

  2. 2

    Operating profit was KRW 20.3 billion in 2025, down 11.7% year on year, and the operating margin fell to 4.1%.

  3. 3

    Third-quarter 2026 sales were KRW 153.9 billion, up 20.0% year on year, while net profit attributable to controlling shareholders fell 76.0% to KRW 2.18 billion.

  4. 4

    Net profit attributable to controlling shareholders totals KRW 45.4 billion over the last four quarters, with the second quarter of 2026 the largest at KRW 27.5 billion.

  5. 5

    Demand for electronic materials linked to AI data center investment is supporting subsidiary results, while the final US antidumping duty ruling remains an export uncertainty.

02

Business structure

Miwon Holdings was spun off from Miwon Commercial in 2009 and converted into a holding company structure in 2017, earning investment, service and rental income. Subsidiaries account for 96.54 percent of revenue composition according to Alphasquare.

The core subsidiary is Miwon Specialty Chemical, which produces UV/EB curable resins supplied to coatings, inks, adhesives, displays and semiconductor applications; Miwon Holdings owns 33.94 percent of it.

The company's inkjet and printed-electronics subsidiary was acquired in May 2021 and makes printer inks, electronic materials and UV inkjet curing printers. Dongnam Synthetic, a surfactant maker, supplies detergent, textile and paper industries, and Miwon Holdings holds 42.29 percent of it.

Valueline describes Miwon Specialty Chemical as holding a domestic market share above 50 percent and a global share of about 15 percent. Overseas, the group operates subsidiaries in the United States, Germany, Spain, Austria and China, and opened a Gwanggyo research center in January 2024.

Bizwatch reported in September 2026 that the founding family holds 50.01 percent and that related parties hold 75.87 percent in total. The ownership picture also reflects a succession process that has moved to the founder's grandson, Kim Tae-jun.

04

Earnings analysis

Annual sales fell 11.2 percent from KRW 499.3 billion in 2022 to KRW 443.2 billion in 2023, then rose 7.5 percent to KRW 476.6 billion in 2024 and 3.4 percent to KRW 492.7 billion in 2025, increasing for two straight years from 2024 to 2025. 2022 was the largest year for sales and 2023 the smallest.

Operating profit was KRW 22.8 billion in 2022, KRW 15.6 billion in 2023 (down 31.9 percent), KRW 23 billion in 2024 (up 48.0 percent) and KRW 20.3 billion in 2025 (down 11.7 percent), with 2024 the largest year.

The operating margin moved from 4.6 percent in 2022 to 3.5 percent in 2023, 4.8 percent in 2024 and 4.1 percent in 2025, so margins fell in 2025 even as sales grew.

Net profit attributable to controlling shareholders was KRW 20.2 billion in 2022, KRW 13.2 billion in 2023 (down 34.5 percent), KRW 25.3 billion in 2024 (up 91.1 percent) and KRW 26.2 billion in 2025 (up 3.6 percent), with 2025 the largest year.

Net profit including non-controlling interests was KRW 30.4 billion in 2025, and the gap reflects the non-controlling share.

At the end of 2025, total equity was KRW 326.7 billion, of which KRW 289.5 billion belonged to controlling shareholders, and total liabilities were KRW 201.2 billion; the debt ratio fell from 89.6 percent in 2022 to 61.6 percent in 2025.

Operating cash flow was KRW 23.1 billion in 2022, KRW 21.4 billion in 2023, KRW 12.4 billion in 2024 and KRW 28.6 billion in 2025.

On a quarterly basis, sales were KRW 128.2 billion in the third quarter of 2025, KRW 118 billion in the fourth quarter (down 7.9 percent quarter on quarter), KRW 147.7 billion in the first quarter of 2026, KRW 160.5 billion in the second quarter and KRW 153.9 billion in the third quarter (down 4.1 percent quarter on quarter, up 20.0 percent year on year).

Operating profit fell to KRW 2.23 billion in the fourth quarter of 2025, then recovered to KRW 9.28 billion in the first quarter of 2026 and KRW 17.5 billion in the second quarter, before dropping to KRW 7.58 billion in the third quarter (down 56.6 percent quarter on quarter, up 9.7 percent year on year).

Net profit attributable to controlling shareholders was KRW 9.06 billion in the third quarter of 2025, KRW 2.08 billion in the fourth quarter, KRW 13.6 billion in the first quarter of 2026, KRW 27.5 billion in the second quarter and KRW 2.18 billion in the third quarter, which was down 76.0 percent year on year.

The second quarter's controlling-shareholder profit exceeded its operating profit, so the composition of non-operating items is a point to verify in the 2026 annual report.

Bizwatch reported in September 2026 that the subsidiary Miwon Specialty Chemical posted first-half 2026 sales of KRW 304.1 billion, up 11.5 percent, and operating profit of KRW 56.4 billion, up 51.6 percent; these are subsidiary-level figures and differ from the holding company's consolidated results.

05

Industry analysis

Because the core subsidiaries make energy-curable resins and electronic materials, results are sensitive to demand from electronics, display and coatings markets.

According to a company profile summary, AI data center investment in 2026 has lifted demand for ESS and power components, which in turn expanded mass-production sales of electronic materials.

The same source notes that the final US antidumping duty ruling could affect exports, so demand growth and trade risk coexist in the same business.

Valueline reports a domestic market share above 50 percent for Miwon Specialty Chemical in energy-curable resins and a global share of about 15 percent, which points to room for share gains abroad.

Resins, inks and surfactants follow different industry cycles, so weakness in one line can be offset by another, although subsidiary-level swings still flow directly into consolidated profit. Against the holding and conglomerate sector, Alphasquare data shows an operating margin well below the sector average.

Peers in the holding-company group include SK Square, Hanjinkal, Hyosung and Hankook & Company. Third-quarter 2026 sales grew 20.0 percent year on year, which signals demand recovery, while operating profit rose only 9.7 percent over the same period, suggesting that cost and product-mix effects weighed on margins.

06

Outlook

The outlook that can be traced in the company's public materials centers on electronic materials. The first-quarter 2026 improvement was attributed to rising electronic-materials demand for the energy-curable resin subsidiary and growth in the inkjet subsidiary's electronic-materials business.

If demand tied to AI data center ESS and power components persists, that segment could keep contributing to sales. However, the final US antidumping duty ruling could affect exports, so the share of US-bound volume is a key point to monitor.

Bizwatch reported that Miwon Specialty Chemical grew both sales and operating profit in the first half of 2026.

In a 2024 interview, management said the company would extend its photosensitizer technology toward OLED materials and emphasized partnerships with cell makers in battery materials such as electrolyte additives and binders; that statement reflects 2024 plans and progress should be checked in filings.

In September 2025 the company decided to buy back 90,000 of its own shares on the open market through December 2025, and in March 2026 a major shareholder disclosed an increase in its stake to 10.56 percent.

These facts show shareholder-return and ownership moves but do not by themselves indicate the direction of operating results.

The next regular filing is the 2026 annual report, which will disclose fourth-quarter 2026 results and full-year totals; the key checks are whether full-year sales exceed the KRW 492.7 billion reported for 2025 and what fourth-quarter operating profit looks like.

The annual report is also where the effect of non-operating items on controlling-shareholder profit can be examined.

Net profit attributable to controlling shareholders reached KRW 27.5 billion in the second quarter of 2026, compared with KRW 2.08 billion in the fourth quarter of 2025. Third-quarter 2026 sales of KRW 153.9 billion, up 20.0 percent year on year, show the demand recovery in the top line.

Subsidiary Profit Growth in the First Half

Bear factors

Sharp Swings in Quarterly Profit

Third-quarter 2026 operating profit was KRW 7.58 billion, down 56.6 percent from the prior quarter, and net profit attributable to controlling shareholders was KRW 2.18 billion, down 76.0 percent year on year.

Over the last four quarters, net profit attributable to controlling shareholders totals KRW 45.4 billion, and a large part of it came in the second quarter of 2026 at KRW 27.5 billion. When profit concentrates in one quarter, the quarterly trend alone is hard to read.

Lower Operating Margin Despite Higher Sales

Sales in 2025 rose 3.4 percent to KRW 492.7 billion, but operating profit fell 11.7 percent to KRW 20.3 billion. The operating margin declined from 4.8 percent in 2024 to 4.1 percent in 2025. In the third quarter of 2026 sales also grew, but operating profit rose only 9.7 percent year on year.

Concentrated Control and Limited Free Float

According to Bizwatch in September 2026, the founding family holds 50.01 percent, and including related parties the total reaches 75.87 percent. This structure limits the number of shares available for trading and can act as a constraint on liquidity. Ongoing succession may also create share transfers that affect the stock.

Risk factors

Trade and Export Regulation

A company profile summary notes that the final US antidumping duty ruling could affect exports. Because electronic-materials sales are growing, any sizable US-bound volume would expose revenue directly to the duty. The final rate and scope should be checked against filings and disclosures.

Demand Cycles and Quarterly Volatility

Electronic materials demand is tied to the AI data center investment cycle, so a slowdown in that spending could weigh on volumes. Quarterly results have moved sharply, from KRW 17.5 billion of operating profit in the second quarter of 2026 to KRW 7.58 billion in the third.

Until the drivers of second-quarter controlling-shareholder profit, which exceeded operating profit, are identified in filings, the quality of that profit is hard to assess.

Governance and Succession

Bizwatch reported in September 2026 that succession from the founder to his eldest son and grandson is proceeding centered on Miwon Holdings and Miwon Specialty Chemical. Business Post reported in 2024 that the third-generation succession was unclear and raised the possibility of group separation. Share transfers and changes in group structure during succession remain governance risks.

Overall view

Miwon Holdings is a holding company with subsidiaries in energy-curable resins, inks and surfactants. Annual sales rose for a second straight year to KRW 492.7 billion in 2025. Operating profit, however, fell 11.7 percent to KRW 20.3 billion, and the operating margin declined to 4.1 percent.

Third-quarter 2026 sales rose 20.0 percent to KRW 153.9 billion, but net profit attributable to controlling shareholders fell 76.0 percent to KRW 2.18 billion, so quarterly profit is volatile.

Supporting factors include demand for electronic materials linked to AI data centers, higher first-half 2026 profit at subsidiary Miwon Specialty Chemical, and a lower debt ratio.

Offsetting factors include the final US antidumping duty ruling, concentration of profit in a few quarters, and a governance structure centered on the founding family, with succession underway. Valuation is described only through factual comparisons with book value and sector averages.
