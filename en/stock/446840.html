02

Business structure

Gitsn develops, manufactures and sells wireless eavesdropping detection, wireless-backdoor hacking detection and illegal filming detection systems based on radio frequency (RF) analysis technology.

According to company profile data, Gitsn was founded in 2000 and listed on KOSDAQ after a 2025 merger with the SPAC Kiwoom No.8. By product and service type, product sales made up 85% of revenue in Q2 2026, service sales 13% and rental sales 2%, based on a Butler data compilation.

Its flagship product, Alpha-H, is a wireless-backdoor detection system that monitors 24 hours a day for unauthorized radio signals from ultra-small spy chips secretly installed in IT and computing equipment. The company said Alpha-H sales in the first half of 2026 grew about seven times year on year.

Alpha-C is a constant-monitoring system that detects illegal filming attempts inside public restrooms; by the company's count, it held a cumulative 51.31% share of the public procurement market from 2021 through June 30, 2026, ranking first, based on the Public Procurement Data Hub.

Customers include financial institutions, government and public agencies, and IT and semiconductor companies, and the company says it supplies systems to more than 450 institutions.

Alpha-H is operated by 26 institutions in Korea and has been supplied to five overseas countries including the United States, the United Kingdom and Singapore.

On competition, Yuanta Securities, in an April 2026 report, referred to Gitsn's exclusive supplier position in wireless-backdoor detection in Korea and cited public-market shares of 98.8% for constant-monitoring eavesdropping detection and 60.5% for illegal filming detection.

Those shares are broker estimates and should be read separately from the company's own counts. With product sales at 85% of revenue, quarterly sales tend to move sharply with the timing of equipment deliveries.

04

Earnings analysis

Annual revenue was KRW 11.3 billion in 2025. Operating losses were KRW 10.55 million in 2022, KRW 21.29 million in 2023, KRW 25.66 million in 2024 and KRW 6.64 billion in 2025, a run of four consecutive loss-making years from 2022 to 2025.

The 2023 and 2024 losses continued from the prior year and widened, and the 2025 operating margin was -58.8%.

Net profit attributable to controlling shareholders swung from a net loss of KRW 16.18 million in 2022 to a profit of KRW 122 million in 2023, rose to KRW 231 million in 2024, up 89.4% year on year, and turned into a net loss of KRW 10.7 billion in 2025.

Net profit including non-controlling interests was disclosed at the same values for those years. Because the 2025 net loss was larger than the operating loss, non-operating items appear to have added to the deterioration.

Operating cash flow moved from an inflow of KRW 265 million in 2024 to an outflow of KRW 4.18 billion in 2025. The liabilities-to-equity ratio rose from 9.2% in 2024 to 155.3% in 2025, when total equity was KRW 7.57 billion and total liabilities were KRW 11.8 billion.

Quarterly, Q3 2025 revenue of KRW 8.55 billion carried an operating loss of KRW 3.62 billion; Q4 2025 revenue fell 67.9% quarter on quarter to KRW 2.74 billion, with an operating loss of KRW 2.96 billion. Q1 2026 revenue was KRW 2.46 billion with an operating loss of KRW 1.89 billion.

Q2 2026 revenue rose 99.0% quarter on quarter to KRW 4.9 billion and the company posted an operating profit of KRW 462 million, a swing to profit, with net profit attributable to controlling shareholders of KRW 389 million. The company said revenue growth absorbed fixed costs.

Q3 2026 revenue was KRW 2.02 billion, down 58.8% quarter on quarter and 76.4% year on year, and the operating loss of KRW 1.93 billion marked a return to loss. Net loss attributable to controlling shareholders was KRW 2.12 billion.

The last four quarters' combined net loss attributable to controlling shareholders of KRW 6.56 billion shows how often quarterly results have changed direction.

05

Industry analysis

Demand for security is centered on constant-monitoring systems adopted by government agencies and financial institutions. The company argues that AI advances have strengthened software security, pushing attacks toward the hardware layer and raising demand for wireless-backdoor detection.

On regulation, Yuanta Securities said a 2024 amendment to Korea's Information and Communications Network Act opened a new market for wireless-backdoor security.

Wireless eavesdropping security is supported by mandatory installation under the national information security baseline guidelines and a shorter service life for eavesdropping-prevention devices, from ten years to eight.

For illegal filming detection, the main issue is expanded installation following an amendment to the Public Toilet Act and local ordinances by metropolitan and provincial education offices.

Based on the Ministry of the Interior and Safety's public toilet dataset, the company estimates mandatory-installation demand at about 264,000 units in Korea, or roughly KRW 290 billion at selling prices excluding VAT.

Including schools, commercial buildings and cultural, medical and welfare facilities, the company puts the total potential market at about 2.33 million units and KRW 2.56 trillion. These are company estimates, and the pace of actual installation and budget allocation is a separate variable.

In cycle terms, Alpha-H has moved past initial adoption by financial institutions into full-scale adoption, and supply to new industries such as semiconductors has begun.

The exchange classifies Gitsn under electrical equipment, but its business sits in security equipment and software, so direct demand comparisons with listed electrical equipment makers are limited.

Outlook

The company said it will keep expanding adoption of Alpha-H in the second half of 2026 and respond to rising Alpha-C demand from the amended Public Toilet Act, aiming to sustain revenue growth and profitability gains.

In July 2026, the company said financial institutions were moving to full adoption and that supply to new industries such as semiconductors was continuing. It also said more than 20 institutions, including telecom and power companies, are reviewing additional adoption.

In April 2026, Yuanta Securities forecast 2026 revenue of KRW 28.1 billion and operating profit of KRW 4.6 billion, a turn to profit and the highest operating profit on record. That forecast, however, was made before the Q3 2026 operating loss was disclosed.

The WiseReport consensus average of broker estimates as of November 13, 2026 shows 2027 revenue of KRW 25 billion and operating profit of KRW 3.9 billion. The company's KRW 290 billion Alpha-C market estimate matters mainly in terms of how quickly installations after the law take effect show up in reported revenue.

The Q2 2026 profit did not carry into Q3, so the key question is how steadily financial-sector full adoption shows up in quarterly revenue. The next checkpoints are the Q4 2026 result confirmed in the 2026 annual report and the Q1 2027 quarterly report.

Whether overseas supply to five countries contributes meaningfully will show up only if the company reports it in amounts.

Bull factors

Steep growth in wireless-backdoor detection sales

According to the company, Alpha-H sales in the first half of 2026 rose about seven times year on year and exceeded twice the full-year 2025 wireless-backdoor security sales. Q2 2026 revenue of KRW 4.9 billion and an operating profit of KRW 462 million marked the turn to profit.

Yuanta Securities, in an April 2026 report, described the segment as having an exclusive supply structure in Korea.

Accumulating policy and regulatory demand

Yuanta Securities said the 2024 amendment to Korea's Information and Communications Network Act opened a new market for wireless-backdoor security. Mandatory installation under national information security guidelines and a shorter service life for eavesdropping-prevention devices were also cited as demand drivers. The company expects the amended Public Toilet Act to raise new Alpha-C installation demand.

Public references and a widening customer base

The company said Alpha-C held a 51.31% cumulative share of the public procurement market from 2021 through June 30, 2026, ranking first in the Public Procurement Data Hub.

Alpha-H is operated by 26 institutions in Korea and supplied to five overseas countries, and more than 20 institutions including telecom and power companies are reviewing adoption. The company also said it signed supply contracts with global semiconductor manufacturers in June 2026.

Concentration in product and public demand

Overall view

Gitsn's operating profit turned positive in Q2 2026 as sales of its Alpha-H wireless-backdoor detection system surged, and first-half revenue rose 50.8% year on year. However, Q3 2026 revenue fell to KRW 2.02 billion and the operating loss of KRW 1.93 billion returned it to loss.

Whether the quarterly profit becomes a trend is currently read differently depending on how much weight is given to the Q3 result.

On the balance sheet, the liabilities-to-equity ratio rose to 155.3% at end-2025, with total equity of KRW 7.57 billion and a combined net loss attributable to controlling shareholders of KRW 6.56 billion over the last four quarters.

Operating losses ran from 2022 through 2025, so whether the turn to profit lasts is the largest open variable.

On the market side, the amended Public Toilet Act and wider adoption by financial institutions and semiconductor companies are cited as growth drivers, and the company estimates the Alpha-C potential market at KRW 290 billion.

That is the company's own estimate, and the timing of earnings recognition depends on actual installations and budget execution. On valuation, the P/E ratio is not calculated while the company is loss-making, and the price-to-book ratio sits in a premium zone relative to net assets.

In sum, operating indicators show signs of improvement, but earnings stability has not yet been demonstrated. The next checkpoints are the Q4 2026 results in the 2026 annual report and the Q1 2027 quarterly report.
