Daiyang Metal: What the Q3 2026 Operating Loss Signals

02

Business structure

Daiyang Metal, founded in 1973 and listed on the KOSPI in 1994, is a stainless cold-rolled steel specialist. Its core product is stainless cold-rolled steel, which it makes by cold-rolling hot coil sourced externally. According to wisereport data, the 2025 sales mix as of December was 47.41% domestic and 52.59% export.

Exports go mainly to Europe, Southeast Asia and North America, and the export share was about 52% in the first quarter of 2026. End uses include home appliances, sinks, automotive parts, boilers, industrial parts and building materials.

The company's share of the domestic stainless cold-rolled market was 6.00% as of June 2026 according to the same data source. Because it has no in-house steelmaking, it is sensitive to raw coil prices and processing margins.

Nickel prices and exchange rates drive product spreads, and the company plans to expand into areas that can synergize with its steel processing business.

04

Earnings analysis

Annual revenue fell 21.2% from KRW 255 billion in 2022 to KRW 201 billion in 2023, the smallest year in the period, then rose 7.3% to KRW 215.6 billion in 2024 and 3.7% to KRW 223.5 billion in 2025, a two-year increase from 2024 through 2025. 2022 was the largest year of 2022 to 2025.

Operating income swung from KRW 13.7 billion in 2022 to an operating loss of KRW 2.5 billion in 2023, returned to KRW 1.89 billion in 2024, and reached KRW 5.62 billion in 2025, up 197.4%.

The operating margin moved from 5.4% in 2022 to -1.2% in 2023, 0.9% in 2024 and 2.5% in 2025, a recovery that has not yet reached the 2022 level.

Net income attributable to controlling shareholders fell from KRW 8.74 billion in 2022 to a net loss of KRW 35.3 billion in 2023 and a net loss of KRW 15.3 billion in 2024, then turned to a KRW 9.4 billion profit in 2025.

Total net income including non-controlling interests was a net loss of KRW 38.6 billion in 2023, a net loss of KRW 15.3 billion in 2024 and a profit of KRW 9.4 billion in 2025, a different figure from the controlling-shareholder measure.

Operating cash flow declined from KRW 13.1 billion in 2022 to KRW 2.58 billion in 2025, even as the debt ratio improved from 129.7% to 44.3%. At year-end 2025, total equity was KRW 100.1 billion and total liabilities were KRW 44.4 billion.

Quarterly revenue went from KRW 57.2 billion in the third quarter of 2025 to KRW 54.9 billion in the fourth quarter (down 4.2%) and KRW 55.2 billion in the first quarter of 2026 (up 0.6%), the peak, before falling to KRW 52.9 billion in the second quarter (down 4.1%) and KRW 47.1 billion in the third quarter (down 11.0% from the prior quarter and 17.7% from a year earlier).

Operating income was KRW 1.34 billion, KRW 1.68 billion, KRW 1.05 billion and KRW 1.94 billion across the third quarter of 2025 through the second quarter of 2026, then an operating loss of KRW 2.63 billion in the third quarter of 2026, a swing into loss from a year earlier.

In the first quarter of 2026, legal fees and shareholder meeting costs tied to the control dispute were booked, and a company official said operating income excluding those one-off costs would have been about KRW 2.4 billion.

The company also said first-half 2026 net income fell because of one-off selling and administrative expenses linked to capital raising.

Net income attributable to controlling shareholders peaked at KRW 5.67 billion in the fourth quarter of 2025, then fell to KRW 837 million in the first quarter of 2026, followed by a net loss of KRW 1.23 billion in the second quarter and KRW 5.34 billion in the third quarter.

The specific cause of the third-quarter loss is a point to check in the cost structure disclosed in the annual report.

05

Industry analysis

Demand for stainless cold-rolled steel moves with end markets such as home appliances, automotive parts and building materials, and when the global steel cycle weakens, volumes and product prices tend to fall.

The wisereport company summary attributes the 2023 and 2024 pressure to weaker stainless demand, lower nickel prices that cut product prices, and protectionism that changed market conditions.

Reports in 2026 pointed to a rise in global stainless prices, and higher nickel prices led to supply price increases and inventory valuation gains. A higher exchange rate has also been cited as a margin tailwind for exporters.

Against peers, Daiyang Metal is a cold-rolling specialist that buys hot coil externally, so it is more exposed to raw material price swings than producers that run integrated steelmaking. The company's domestic stainless cold-rolled market share was 6.00% as of June 2026 according to the same data source.

The key industry questions are how quickly export diversification shows up in margins and how fast nickel price moves pass through to selling prices.

08

Bull factors

Export mix and customer diversification

Exports accounted for 52.59% of sales as of December 2025, with sales expanding to Europe, Southeast Asia and North America. First-quarter 2026 export sales were about KRW 28.7 billion, up 1.4% year on year, and the company credited ultra-thin products for part of the gain. A stronger exchange rate has been cited as supporting won-denominated profitability on exports.

Nickel price pass-through

Nickel makes up 60–70% of stainless production cost, and reports say recent nickel price gains raised product prices and boosted inventory valuation gains. Some reports expect exports and product differentiation to support earnings amid rising global stainless prices.

If selling price increases pass through faster than cost pressure, room for operating margin improvement widens.

Cost improvement and operating profit recovery

Second-quarter 2026 operating income was KRW 1.94 billion, up 84.8% from the prior quarter, and first-half operating income was about KRW 3 billion per the semi-annual report. The company said it is pursuing cost reductions and surface quality gains through CGL equipment upgrades. The 2025 operating margin of 2.5% was above 0.9% in 2024.

Bear factors

Q3 2026 operating loss and falling sales

Third-quarter 2026 revenue was KRW 47.1 billion, down 11.0% from the prior quarter and 17.7% from a year earlier. The same quarter posted an operating loss of KRW 2.63 billion, a swing from profit, and a net loss attributable to controlling shareholders of KRW 5.34 billion. Profit deteriorating alongside two consecutive quarters of falling sales is the most direct bearish signal.

Weaker cash flow and dilution concerns

Operating cash flow fell from KRW 13.1 billion in 2022 to KRW 2.58 billion in 2025. In 2026, a third-party allotment rights issue of about KRW 5 billion was pursued, and its payment date was pushed back to August 31, 2026. Raising capital supports financial capacity but brings a higher share count and dilution concerns.

Raw material prices and demand cycle

Nickel accounts for 60–70% of cost, so falling nickel prices pull down product prices and pressure margins. The wisereport commentary attributes deteriorating results to a global steel slump, lower nickel prices that cut product prices, and reduced demand. Growing trade protectionism adds uncertainty for an export-heavy business.

Risk factors

Raw materials and FX

Nickel prices and exchange rates drive product spreads, and supply price increases reflect with a lag. A sharp fall in nickel could cause inventory valuation losses, while a sharp rise could dampen customer demand. With exports above half of sales, the direction of the exchange rate feeds directly into profitability.

Governance and management

Responding to a hostile M&A attempt by an outside group in 2024 left the company with about a year of management disruption. The control dispute ended in 2025, but legal fees and extraordinary shareholder meeting costs were booked in the first quarter of 2026. The company said operating income excluding those one-off costs would have been about KRW 2.4 billion.

Funding and financial capacity

Operating cash flow dropped to KRW 2.58 billion in 2025, and the company raised operating funds through a rights issue and a small public offering in 2026. At year-end 2025, total liabilities were KRW 44.4 billion against total equity of KRW 100.1 billion, a debt ratio of 44.3% that improved from 129.7% in 2022. Further capital raising would increase the share count and affect per-share measures.

Overall view

Daiyang Metal processes and sells stainless cold-rolled steel from externally sourced hot coil, and exports make up more than half of sales. After operating and net losses in 2023, it returned to operating profit in 2024 and to net profit attributable to controlling shareholders in 2025, a recovery in annual results.

Operating income stayed positive in the first and second quarters of 2026, but in the third quarter revenue fell to KRW 47.1 billion and the company posted an operating loss of KRW 2.63 billion.

Net loss attributable to controlling shareholders over the last four quarters totals KRW 65.81 million, with losses in two consecutive quarters, the second and third of 2026. Bullish factors include nickel-linked price increases, export customer diversification and cost improvement efforts.

Bearish factors include third-quarter revenue decline and operating loss, weaker operating cash flow and possible dilution from rights issues. Nickel prices and exchange rates move margins in both directions.

The next checkpoints are the 2026 annual report and the first-quarter 2027 report, where the direction of operating margin and operating cash flow will show the trend.
