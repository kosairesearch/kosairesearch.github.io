02

Business structure

Kyeong Nam Steel was founded in 1990 and is a steel processing company. It receives cold-rolled and hot-rolled steel from POSCO through a processing-center arrangement and cuts and shears it to customer specifications before sale, according to WiseReport company overview data.

Its main raw materials are cold-rolled steel sheets, pickled coil and galvanized steel sheets. Its end markets are automotive and home-appliance parts, and the company describes its Changwon-area base as serving appliance suppliers and automotive production lines.

It expanded its Gwangyang No. 2 plant in July 2020 and installed an electrical steel slitting line in June 2024 with annual capacity of 24,000 tons. The company also runs automated lines for leveling and shipment, according to press coverage.

A trade publication reported shearing capacity of 169,000 tons, slitting capacity of 713,000 tons and storage capacity of about 100,000 tons.

Because it buys and distributes POSCO products, its cost base depends on POSCO's hot-rolled pricing, and an analyst noted that its exclusive purchase and sales rights with POSCO limit its room to adjust purchase prices or volumes.

Press coverage presented its cooperation with POSCO and Hyundai Steel Company as the basis of its competitiveness. Besides steel processing, the company also leases real estate.

04

Earnings analysis

Annual revenue was KRW 415.3 billion in 2022, the largest year in the 2022–2025 span, then KRW 390.3 billion in 2023 (down 6.0% year on year), KRW 395.5 billion in 2024 (up 1.3%), and KRW 357.1 billion in 2025 (down 9.7%), the smallest year.

Operating profit went from KRW 11.4 billion in 2022 to KRW 10.7 billion in 2023 (down 5.9%), KRW 9.45 billion in 2024 (down 11.9%) and KRW 5.96 billion in 2025 (down 36.9%), a decline in each of the last three years. The operating margin was 2.7% in 2023, the same as in 2022, then fell to 2.4% in 2024 and 1.7% in 2025.

In 2024 revenue rose but the margin declined, so operating profit still fell. The company attributed the 2025 deterioration to lower sales from weaker downstream demand and higher bad-debt expenses after a customer entered corporate rehabilitation, according to coverage of the February 2026 disclosure.

In 2025 operating profit fell by a larger percentage than revenue, 36.9% against 9.7%. Net profit attributable to controlling shareholders fell from KRW 9.66 billion in 2022 to KRW 4.63 billion in 2025, and net profit including non-controlling interests was reported at the same amounts.

Operating cash flow was KRW 6.27 billion in 2022, KRW 1.07 billion in 2023, KRW 9.39 billion in 2024 and KRW 15.8 billion in 2025, the largest of the four years. By quarter, operating profit was KRW 1.71 billion in the third quarter of 2025 and KRW 511 million in the fourth quarter (down 70.1% from the prior quarter).

It then rose to KRW 1.41 billion in the first quarter of 2026 (up 175.4%) and KRW 2.25 billion in the second quarter (up 59.7%), before falling to KRW 674 million in the third quarter (down 70.0%).

Third-quarter 2026 revenue was KRW 91.7 billion, up 6.1% year on year, while operating profit fell 60.6% and net profit attributable to controlling shareholders was KRW 37.44 million, down 97.3%.

Net profit attributable to controlling shareholders for the last four quarters, from the fourth quarter of 2025 to the third quarter of 2026, totals KRW 4.1 billion. WiseReport summarized that first-quarter 2026 separate-basis operating profit fell 4.9% year on year while net profit rose 26.5%.

05

Industry analysis

Kyeong Nam Steel's steel service center business buys products from POSCO, processes them and sells them on, so it is exposed to both steel market conditions and downstream demand.

Company-related data for 2024 described weak global steel market conditions, falling product prices and intensifying competition as pressures on profitability. In 2025 weaker downstream demand reduced sales.

WiseReport's first-quarter 2026 summary said stable demand from automotive and home-appliance end markets lifted sales, while delayed construction recovery and a flat automotive sector limited profitability.

A Nice D&B analyst report, covered in February 2026, said rising POSCO hot-rolled prices would weigh heavily on the company's costs. An earlier analysis from the same firm argued that a slowdown in the industry decline and stable raw material prices could gradually improve profitability.

Unlike Hyundai Steel Company, which produces steel itself, Kyeong Nam Steel processes and supplies POSCO products, so its sourcing and pricing structure differs. Demand signals are mixed, with home-appliance and automotive end markets moving in different directions.

06

Outlook

The core of the 2026 corporate value-up plan that the company disclosed in March 2026 is a profitability-focused management structure, financial soundness management and a payout ratio of 40% or higher.

To achieve this, the company plans to pursue cost competitiveness and production efficiency improvements and to manage financial risk by stabilizing its borrowing structure. The plan contains forward-looking information and may change with market or operating conditions.

In a 2025 press report, the company said it would focus its capacity on stable sales volume and profitability even as steel market difficulties persist.

The electrical steel slitting line installed in June 2024 is linked to demand for materials used in electric motors and was reported as expanding the company's processing scope. The first checkpoint on earnings is how the annual operating margin in the 2026 annual report moves from 1.7% in 2025.

The same report will make the fourth quarter of 2026 part of the disclosed results for the first time. It is also worth watching whether bad-debt expenses linked to customer rehabilitation recur after 2026.

POSCO's hot-rolled price trend is the external variable that feeds into quarterly profit most quickly through costs.

08

Bull factors

Lower debt burden and a simple capital structure

The debt ratio fell to 39.7% at the end of 2025 from 62.4% in 2022, with total liabilities of KRW 45.2 billion and total equity of KRW 114 billion. All equity is attributable to controlling shareholders, so the capital structure has no non-controlling interests. Operating cash flow was KRW 15.8 billion in 2025, up from KRW 6.27 billion in 2022.

A stated dividend policy and a high payout ratio

In its 2026 corporate value-up plan, the company stated a goal of maintaining a payout ratio of 40% or higher. The 2025 payout ratio was 69.88%. Even after profit declined, the company kept a high share of earnings flowing to dividends.

Quarterly profit recovery in the first half of 2026

Operating profit rose to KRW 1.41 billion in the first quarter of 2026, up 175.4% from the prior quarter, and to KRW 2.25 billion in the second quarter. Revenue in the same period was KRW 95.6 billion and KRW 98.3 billion, above KRW 81.7 billion in the fourth quarter of 2025. Whether the sales recovery carries into margin recovery is the next point to confirm.

09

Bear factors

Profit base has declined for three straight years

Operating profit fell for three consecutive years from 2023 through 2025, and the operating margin declined from 2.7% in 2022 to 1.7% in 2025. Net profit attributable to controlling shareholders was KRW 4.63 billion in 2025, less than half of KRW 9.66 billion in 2022.

Third-quarter 2026 operating profit was KRW 674 million and net profit attributable to controlling shareholders was KRW 37.44 million, both low on a quarterly basis.

Customer credit risk has already hit earnings

The company cited higher bad-debt expenses after a customer entered corporate rehabilitation as a factor in the 2025 deterioration. The episode shows that a single customer's credit problem can move operating profit sharply. If similar exposure coincides with lower sales volumes, the decline in profit could widen.

Dependence on POSCO and limits to passing on costs

Because raw materials are bought from POSCO, hot-rolled price increases feed directly into the company's costs. An analyst pointed out that exclusive purchase and sales rights with POSCO make it hard for the company to decide purchase prices or volumes. In a period of weak demand, passing higher costs on to selling prices may be difficult.

Risk factors

Customer credit and bad debt

A customer's corporate rehabilitation filing was reflected in 2025 results as bad-debt expenses. If receivable collection risk recurs, operating profit and net profit attributable to controlling shareholders would bear the impact directly. The size of bad debts and the customer mix can be checked in the notes to the annual report.

Raw material prices and dependence on POSCO

Because the company buys and distributes POSCO products, hot-rolled price changes drive profitability. A Nice D&B report said rising POSCO hot-rolled prices would weigh heavily on the company's costs.

Since purchase prices and volumes are hard for the company to adjust, this risk is difficult for management to reduce alone.

Downstream demand cycle

Sales are determined by demand for automotive and home-appliance parts. WiseReport's assessment of the first quarter of 2026 cited a delayed construction recovery and a flat automotive sector as limits on profitability. The company itself attributed the 9.7% revenue decline in 2025 to weaker downstream demand.

Overall view

Kyeong Nam Steel processes POSCO products and supplies automotive and home-appliance parts makers, and in 2025 both revenue and operating profit declined. Operating profit fell for three consecutive years from 2023, and the operating margin dropped to 1.7%.

The company attributes the main causes to weaker downstream demand and higher bad-debt expenses after a customer entered corporate rehabilitation. The financial structure shows a debt ratio of 39.7% and total equity of KRW 114 billion.

Quarterly results in 2026 are mixed, with recovery in the first and second quarters, at KRW 1.41 billion and KRW 2.25 billion in operating profit, followed by a sharp drop to KRW 674 million in the third quarter, so a clear trend is hard to confirm.

On shareholder returns, the company set a target payout ratio of 40% or higher, and the 2025 payout ratio was 69.88%.

Strengths include a lower debt burden, a stated dividend policy and a recent quarterly recovery, while weaknesses include a thinner profit base, customer credit risk and dependence on POSCO's supply structure.

The next checkpoints are the annual operating margin and bad-debt trend in the 2026 annual report, and first-quarter 2027 results.
