Logen: Parcel Profitability Recovery Is the Test

Logen is now a parcel-focused company after its fashion business was separated, and whether profitability recovers from a 2025 operating profit of KRW 22.7 billion and a Q3 2026 operating loss of KRW 351 million is the central question.

  2. 2

  3. 3

02

Business structure

Logen was founded in 1999 as a parcel delivery company and, after the fashion business was separated, remains a parcel-centric operating company. Transport accounted for 89.4% of revenue in 2024, up from 27.6% in 2021, according to a press report.

The company runs a network of 348 branches and about 10,000 small-business operators nationwide. Its volume is weighted toward C2C (consumer-to-consumer) shipments, which a 2019 report put at 80 to 90% of total volume.

C2C parcels carry higher per-parcel fees than corporate parcels, but the company has fewer hub terminals than the large carriers and is described as asset-light. Parcel volume rose from 309 million pieces in 2022 to 318 million in 2024.

In January 2026 the company signed an MOU with Dilboxx Central to stabilize next-day delivery and expand same-day and holiday delivery in the capital region, and in September it joined the Homepick platform for individual shipments.

In the 11th KBRI survey for 2026, Logen was ranked first in courier services for the ninth consecutive year, according to a November 13, 2026 report. A 2019 report said three majors, CJ Logistics, Lotte Global Logistics and HANJIN Logistics, held 80% of the market.

04

Earnings analysis

On a reported basis, revenue fell from KRW 1.19 trillion in 2022 to KRW 808 billion in 2023, down 32.3% year on year. It was flat at KRW 808.6 billion in 2024, up 0.1%, before declining to KRW 793.2 billion in 2025, down 1.9%, making 2025 the smallest year of 2022 to 2025.

Operating profit fell from KRW 101.7 billion in 2022 to KRW 34.4 billion in 2023 (down 66.1%), KRW 33.1 billion in 2024 (down 3.9%) and KRW 22.7 billion in 2025 (down 31.5%), a three-year consecutive decline from 2023 to 2025.

The operating margin narrowed from 8.5% in 2022 to 4.3% in 2023, 4.1% in 2024 and 2.9% in 2025. The company attributed the 2025 decline to the opening of new parcel terminals and investment in IT systems, a cost burden that weighed on profit while revenue was shrinking.

Net income attributable to controlling shareholders was KRW 61.6 billion in 2022, KRW 43.6 billion in 2023 (down 29.2%), KRW 17.3 billion in 2024 (down 60.3%) and KRW 21 billion in 2025 (up 21.2%). Net income including non-controlling interests was KRW 20.7 billion in 2025, up from KRW 18.4 billion in 2024.

Quarterly revenue peaked at KRW 207.7 billion in Q4 2025 and then stood at KRW 192.5 billion in Q1 2026 (down 7.3% quarter on quarter), KRW 190.1 billion in Q2 (down 1.2%) and KRW 190.1 billion in Q3 (flat, down 5.7% year on year).

Operating profit was KRW 2.36 billion in Q4 2025, recovered to KRW 3.02 billion in Q1 2026 and KRW 2.88 billion in Q2, then turned to an operating loss of KRW 351 million in Q3.

Net income attributable to controlling shareholders swung to KRW 6.66 billion in Q4 2025 from a loss in Q3 2025, then fell to KRW 383 million in Q1 2026 and turned to losses of KRW 1.27 billion in Q2 and KRW 2.72 billion in Q3 2026. The last four quarters together total KRW 3.06 billion for controlling shareholders.

An October 2026 press report pointed to intensifying parcel price competition and higher labor and fuel costs as drags on profitability.

05

Industry analysis

The parcel market is dominated by three majors, CJ Logistics, Lotte Global Logistics and HANJIN Logistics, and Logen is a smaller player. A 2026 press report noted that the company absorbed higher labor and fuel costs amid intensifying price competition.

The same coverage said it is hard to say whether the company can break into a market structure dominated by large carriers and restore profitability. Large carriers handle mostly B2C volume and run large hub networks, whereas Logen's C2C-weighted model means fewer distribution centers.

A 2021 report said CJ Logistics and Lotte Global Logistics operated more than ten hub terminals, while Logen had eight. Logen's own volume is the most direct demand indicator: volume rose from 2022 to 2024 while revenue fell after 2023, so volume growth has not translated directly into top-line growth.

Expansion of individual-shipment channels and strong delivery-quality ratings support its position in the C2C segment.

06

Outlook

The company has said it plans to widen its air logistics through cargo cooperation with affiliated airlines and to pursue infrastructure efficiency.

The costs of the 2025 terminal openings and IT investment are already reflected in operating profit, so the next check is whether higher utilization of those assets lifts earnings.

Stabilizing next-day delivery and expanding same-day and holiday delivery in the capital region are tasks for service quality, and the extra costs they bring are worth tracking.

A press report expected that whether Logen can prove its core competitiveness and find a path back to profit will be a key indicator for easing the parent's financial burden. The same report said the parent's debt guarantees reached KRW 338.9 billion, raising liquidity concerns at group level.

Because Logen's recovery and the group's financial burden are intertwined, Logen's standalone profit trend alone does not show the full picture. The next annual report will show whether the 2026 operating margin exceeds the 2.9% recorded in 2025, and how Q4 results compare with the loss trend.

08

Bull factors

Brand ratings and delivery quality

A November 2026 report said Logen ranked first in courier services for the ninth consecutive year in the 11th KBRI survey. Speed was the top recommendation keyword. In a C2C-weighted business, delivery reputation can support volume retention. Whether that reputation converts into profit should be checked against the operating margin trend.

C2C-weighted volume and per-parcel fees

Reports have said C2C shipments carry higher per-parcel fees than corporate parcels, and Logen also handles some bulky items larger carriers avoid. Volume rose from 309 million pieces in 2022 to 318 million in 2024. This mix could offer some pricing room when price competition intensifies.

Volume growth and delivery infrastructure

In January 2026 the company signed an MOU with Dilboxx Central to stabilize next-day delivery and expand capital-region same-day and holiday service. In September it joined the Homepick platform to widen its individual-shipment intake. Infrastructure that broadens the volume base could be a starting point for a top-line recovery.

Bear factors

Three years of falling operating profit and a swing to losses

Operating profit declined for three consecutive years from 2023 to 2025, and the 2025 operating margin was 2.9%. Q3 2026 posted an operating loss of KRW 351 million. Net income attributable to controlling shareholders has been negative in Q2 and Q3 2026, and the last four quarters total only KRW 3.06 billion.

Price competition and cost inflation

A 2026 report said intensifying price competition left the company exposed to higher labor and fuel costs. With revenue flat in the 190 billion won range since Q1 2026, that cost pressure could compress margins further.

Large-carrier dominance and infrastructure gap

A 2026 press report said breaking into a market dominated by large carriers will be difficult. Earlier reporting noted Logen had fewer hub terminals than the largest carriers. An asset-light structure limits fixed costs but can be a disadvantage in large-scale volume competition.

10

Risk factors

Parent-company financial burden

A press report said the parent's debt guarantees reached KRW 338.9 billion. It also argued that Logen's weak results feed into the parent's financial burden. Group-level liquidity management could affect Logen's room for investment.

Fixed costs and investment burden

The 2025 profit decline reflected new terminal openings and IT system investment. Earlier reporting said leasing terminals raises fixed-cost pressure. If investment does not lift revenue, margin recovery could be delayed.

Cost and pricing variables

Parcel pricing moves with competitive intensity, and labor and fuel costs are external variables. When both rise together, per-parcel margins come under pressure. If pricing stabilizes, volume growth could translate into profit.

12

Overall view

Logen is a parcel-centric transport company, and after the fashion business was separated, its parcel core became essentially the whole business. Revenue fell from KRW 1.19 trillion in 2022 to KRW 793.2 billion in 2025, and operating profit declined for three consecutive years from 2023.

Q3 2026 posted an operating loss of KRW 351 million, and net income attributable to controlling shareholders has been negative for two consecutive quarters. Strengths include a C2C-weighted volume mix with higher per-parcel fees and delivery ratings such as the ninth straight KBRI first place.

Weaknesses include price competition, higher labor and fuel costs, and a market dominated by large carriers. The shares trade at a large discount to controlling-shareholder book value, while earnings-based multiples look high given small profits.

The parent's financial burden is intertwined with Logen's results and should be considered alongside them. The next checkpoints are the operating margin in the 2026 annual report and whether losses persist in the Q1 2027 quarterly report.
