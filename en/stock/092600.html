NC&: A Single Dashcam Business After Deconsolidation

NC& is reshaping into a single automotive dashcam business as it moves its subsidiaries out of consolidation, and its 2025 net profit turn also reflects a subsidiary disposal gain.

  4. 4

02

Business structure

NC& focuses on image-processing semiconductors and automotive dashcams, and dashcams account for all of its revenue as of the second quarter of 2026.

Its own brand Vueroid is the core product line, and the Vueroid S1 QHD, launched in 2026, is a premium model with QHD resolution front and rear that first entered overseas e-commerce channels such as Amazon in the United States.

The Vueroid CV is a commercial-vehicle safety device that reduces blind spots for large trucks and special-purpose vehicles and targets business-to-business sales to fleet operators.

A 2021 technology analysis report described the company as producing dashcams on an ODM basis while also selling its own brand, with image signal processing (ISP) and analog video transmission (AHD) technologies at its core.

Chip design for automotive image processing had been handled by the subsidiary NEXTCHIP, which moved out of consolidation at the end of 2025 and became an associate, and NC& held about 23% of it as of the end of June 2026.

The bio subsidiary Ancibt is going through a change of control tied to outside investment, and a company official said it could leave the consolidated results in the second half. As a result, the consolidated business is narrowing to finished dashcam sales.

The company competes with domestic and foreign dashcam brands and points to image quality, including day and night picture quality and license plate recognition, as its differentiator.

04

Earnings analysis

On a reported basis, annual revenue fell from KRW 92.6 billion in 2022 to KRW 61.7 billion in 2023, a 33.4% decrease, then rose to KRW 89.7 billion in 2024, up 45.4%, and to KRW 102.6 billion in 2025, up 14.3%, so revenue has grown for two straight years since 2023.

Of the years from 2022 to 2025, 2025 was the largest and 2023 the smallest.

Operating losses were KRW 32.1 billion in 2022, KRW 35.7 billion in 2023, KRW 26.7 billion in 2024 and KRW 17.5 billion in 2025, and the operating margin moved from -34.7% to -57.9%, then -29.8%, and -17.1% in 2025, the smallest loss margin in the period.

The company attributed the 2025 revenue increase and smaller operating loss to higher sales at its subsidiaries. Net loss attributable to controlling shareholders of KRW 22 billion in 2022, KRW 18.2 billion in 2023 and KRW 18.7 billion in 2024 gave way to net income of KRW 12.5 billion in 2025.

Net income including non-controlling interests was KRW 2.84 billion in 2025, a different figure from the controlling-shareholder number.

The 2025 turn was helped by a gain on disposal of subsidiary shares recognized when NEXTCHIP became an associate, so the reduced operating loss and this one-time gain should be read separately.

The debt ratio rose from 54.9% in 2022 to 328.5% in 2024 before falling to 55.1% in 2025, when total equity was KRW 39.1 billion and total liabilities KRW 21.5 billion. Operating cash flow was negative in every year from 2022 to 2025, at KRW -18.5 billion in 2025.

On a quarterly basis, third-quarter 2026 revenue of KRW 12.7 billion fell 48.1% year on year, and the operating result went from a loss of KRW 6.64 billion in the third quarter of 2025 to an operating profit of KRW 672 million in the second quarter of 2026 before a loss of KRW 945 million in the third quarter.

The KRW 23.3 billion controlling-shareholder profit in the fourth quarter of 2025, against an operating loss of KRW 1.16 billion in the same quarter, points to a large non-operating contribution.

The sum of controlling-shareholder net income over the last four quarters, KRW 22.9 billion, depends heavily on that single fourth quarter of 2025.

05

Industry analysis

NC&'s core market is automotive dashcams, so it does not sit on the same cycle as the global chip upturn driven by memory and AI accelerators.

One report cited industry statistics projecting that global semiconductor sales would pass USD 1 trillion for the first time in 2026, which points to demand led by AI chips and memory.

Dashcam image-processing chips and finished units, however, depend more on vehicle production, replacement demand and the pace of driver-assistance adoption.

A 2021 technology analysis report by a securities firm said the dashcam industry was growing alongside autonomous-driving issues, and noted the company's revenue rose 11.9% that year.

The company is pursuing premium consumer dashcams and commercial-vehicle safety devices built on its own image-processing technology, a niche position in the market.

Its end customers are vehicle owners and fleet operators rather than automakers, so vehicle sales and dashcam sales should not be assumed to move together.

Against peers, the company combines its own brand with ODM manufacturing and is only now opening overseas e-commerce channels, which gives it a different channel mix from domestic-focused competitors.

Its industry position therefore depends more on the growth of the dashcam market and its product competitiveness than on the broader chip upturn.

06

Outlook

After completing its subsidiary restructuring at the end of 2025, the company said it would raise the share of its own dashcam brands to secure profitability. In a June 2026 report, its CEO said the own-brand lineup was in place and that the company would work to make the turnaround visible.

On products, it is widening overseas e-commerce sales of the Vueroid S1 QHD and stepping up consumer marketing centered on its own online store.

It plans to expand supply of the Vueroid CV, a commercial-vehicle device for large trucks and special-purpose vehicles, to strengthen its position in business-to-business sales.

A report from October 2026 said the company is pursuing new plants of about 10,000 pyeong in total in Anseong and Hwaseong, and that once all new plants are completed in 2028 it would have annual production capacity of roughly KRW 200 billion, about four times its current revenue according to that report.

Turning that capacity target into demand would require reversing the year-on-year revenue decline seen in the third quarter of 2026.

B2EN, now the largest shareholder, has said it plans to connect its AI and data operations with vehicle imaging and semiconductors, but specific joint projects and revenue scale have not been set. Progress on those joint projects and their commercialization is therefore the next key point to watch.

08

Bull factors

Operating profit returned in Q2 2026

In the second quarter of 2026 the company posted operating profit of KRW 672 million, reversing the prior quarter's operating loss. Net income attributable to controlling shareholders turned to KRW 12.5 billion in 2025, and the operating loss narrowed to KRW 17.5 billion, the smallest of the four years.

Whether this pattern holds beyond the fourth quarter of 2026 is the point that will decide how far this argument goes.

Subsidiary exits reduce consolidated burden

NEXTCHIP moved out of consolidation at the end of 2025 and became an associate, and the company has said its bio subsidiary could follow in the second half. The company describes this as shedding subsidiary risk.

Once chronically loss-making subsidiaries leave the consolidated results, the parent's own performance becomes easier to read.

Own brands and overseas channels expand

The Vueroid S1 QHD, with QHD resolution front and rear, has entered overseas e-commerce markets including Amazon in the United States. The Vueroid CV line for large trucks and special-purpose vehicles targets business-to-business demand. The company argues that a larger share of own-brand products can improve profitability.

Bear factors

Q3 2026 revenue drop and renewed losses

Third-quarter 2026 revenue of KRW 12.7 billion fell 48.1% year on year and 29.2% from the prior quarter. An operating loss of KRW 945 million returned. Revenue also dropped 40.3% from the prior quarter in the first quarter of 2026, so the recovery has been uneven from quarter to quarter.

Quality of profit and one-off items

The 2025 turn to profit was helped by a gain on disposal of subsidiary shares. The four-quarter controlling-shareholder profit total of KRW 22.9 billion leans heavily on the KRW 23.3 billion fourth quarter of 2025. Operating cash flow was negative in every year from 2022 to 2025, at KRW -18.5 billion in 2025.

Accumulated losses and shifting debt structure

Operating losses narrowed from KRW 32.1 billion in 2022 to KRW 17.5 billion in 2025, but the company has posted losses for four straight years. The debt ratio climbed to 328.5% in 2024 before falling to 55.1% in 2025, a shift that appears tied to subsidiary restructuring as well as changes in the capital base. Total equity at the end of 2025 was KRW 39.1 billion.

Risk factors

Listing requirements

Market capitalization below KRW 20 billion is the background to the notice on possible administrative-issue designation. A 5-for-1 share consolidation took effect on April 28, 2026. Further steps tied to listing standards would appear in exchange disclosures.

Concentration and demand swings

Dashcams account for all of revenue as of the second quarter of 2026, leaving the product line and customer base concentrated in one segment. Demand for dashcams is sensitive to vehicle penetration, replacement cycles and consumer spending. Overseas e-commerce is at an early stage, so its sales performance has yet to be tested over a full cycle.

Capital raising and governance change

The company carried out a third-party allotment rights issue in 2026, and B2EN acquired 2.5 million new shares, reaching 27.73% and 44.36% including related parties. The newly issued shares are subject to a one-year lock-up. Changes in business direction or in how subsidiaries are handled could follow the change in control.

12

Overall view

NC& is moving toward a single automotive dashcam business as it takes its subsidiaries out of consolidated results. On a reported basis, revenue grew for two straight years from 2024 to 2025, and net income attributable to controlling shareholders turned to KRW 12.5 billion in 2025.

Even so, the operating loss of KRW 17.5 billion that year marks a fourth consecutive year of losses, and a gain on disposal of subsidiary shares helped the net profit turn.

Quarterly results in 2026 are mixed, with an operating profit in the second quarter followed by a revenue drop of 48.1% year on year and a renewed loss in the third quarter. B2EN has become the largest shareholder, and the company has been flagged over market capitalization below the administrative-issue threshold.

The expansion of production capacity and overseas channels is the growth path management has described, but it still needs to be proven in demand and results. The strengths are the narrowing operating loss and the exit from subsidiaries, while the weaknesses are quarterly revenue swings and reliance on one-time gains. The next checkpoints are the 2026 annual report and first-quarter 2027 results.
