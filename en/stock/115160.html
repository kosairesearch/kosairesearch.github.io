Report overview

Humax: Mobility Infrastructure Pivot as Set-top Box Sales Shrink

Humax's revenue has declined for three straight years since 2023 and the company has posted losses for four straight years since 2022, while its mobility subsidiaries in parking and EV charging have moved to positive EBITDA and operating profit.

  1. 1

  2. 2

  3. 3

    Operating results were a loss in the second and third quarters of 2026 after a 1Q 2026 operating profit of KRW 2.82 billion, a two-quarter run of losses.

  4. 4

  5. 5

02

Business structure

Humax started as a set-top box maker and is now shifting its center of gravity toward automotive electronics, EV charging solutions and mobility infrastructure.

According to the company overview, it supplies digital broadcast transmission-to-terminal solutions to pay-TV operators and also runs automotive electronics including in-vehicle antennas.

The decline in the set-top box core can be traced through gateway revenue reported by a Korean financial daily, which fell from about KRW 1,290.7 billion in 2018 to KRW 196.2 billion in 2025 and KRW 101.2 billion in the first half of 2026.

In automotive, in-vehicle antennas were cited as a 2025 drag because they are exposed to global economic and auto-industry swings. For EV charging, the company said it is extending its European experience, starting with UK OZEV certification, into Germany and France. Mobility operates through subsidiaries.

The parking unit runs about 1,400 sites and 290,000 spaces and, according to the company in February 2026, grew 2025 revenue about 8.2 percent to roughly KRW 200 billion. The charging unit operates more than 20,000 chargers and turned EBITDA positive in 2025. A car-sharing platform, fleet management and ride services sit on top of this infrastructure.

04

Earnings analysis

Annual revenue went from KRW 689.5 billion in 2022 to KRW 652.6 billion in 2023 (down 5.3 percent year on year), KRW 535.6 billion in 2024 (down 17.9 percent) and KRW 394.2 billion in 2025 (down 26.4 percent), a three-year decline from 2023 through 2025.

The largest year in the 2022-2025 window was 2022 and the smallest was 2025. Operating results swung from an operating loss of KRW 5.26 billion in 2022 to an operating profit of KRW 11.2 billion in 2023, then KRW 6.2 billion in 2024 (down 44.7 percent), and an operating loss of KRW 29.2 billion in 2025.

The operating margin was -0.8 percent in 2022, 1.7 percent in 2023, 1.2 percent in 2024 and -7.4 percent in 2025. The net loss attributable to controlling shareholders was KRW 38.4 billion, KRW 61.5 billion, KRW 57 billion and KRW 78.1 billion across 2022 to 2025, a loss in each of those four years.

The net loss including non-controlling interests was KRW 51.1 billion, KRW 78.4 billion, KRW 60.9 billion and KRW 81.4 billion, and the gap versus the parent figure reflects losses borne by non-controlling interests. The set-top box market slump and mobility investment were reported as the causes of the 2025 loss.

The debt-to-equity ratio rose from 212.4 percent in 2022 to 410.8 percent in 2025, with total liabilities of KRW 394.9 billion against total equity of KRW 96.1 billion, of which KRW 90.7 billion belongs to controlling shareholders.

Operating cash flow went from negative KRW 22.7 billion in 2022 to KRW 36.8 billion in 2023 and KRW 81.8 billion in 2024, then KRW 17.9 billion in 2025, which stayed positive even in the loss year.

Quarterly revenue was KRW 97.6 billion in 3Q 2025, KRW 86.7 billion in 4Q 2025 (down 11.1 percent quarter on quarter), KRW 92.1 billion in 1Q 2026, KRW 109.6 billion in 2Q 2026 (up 19.0 percent) and KRW 76.3 billion in 3Q 2026 (down 30.3 percent quarter on quarter and down 21.8 percent year on year).

Operating profit was KRW 2.82 billion in 1Q 2026, then an operating loss of KRW 1.5 billion in 2Q and KRW 7.19 billion in 3Q, a two-quarter run of losses.

Net profit attributable to controlling shareholders was KRW 35.2 billion in 1Q 2026, far above that quarter's operating profit, which points to non-operating or one-off items; it fell to KRW 1.28 billion in 2Q (down 96.4 percent) and turned to a KRW 5.2 billion loss in 3Q. For the four quarters from 4Q 2025 through 3Q 2026, the sum of such net profit was KRW 5.14 billion.

05

Industry analysis

The set-top box market entered a slump as OTT adoption reduced demand, and reporting consistently ties this to the sharp drop in gateway revenue. In automotive electronics, in-vehicle antennas are sensitive to vehicle production and shifts in global trade, which makes the cycle position difficult to pin down.

In EV charging, the industry debate has moved from installation races toward operating quality and utilization. Humax positions the combination of parking, charging and fleet management as a single operating system that sets it apart.

The parking unit said that after introducing data-driven vacant-space analysis and dynamic pricing, its 1Q 2026 operating profit rose 159 percent year on year. The charging unit reported 1Q 2026 revenue up 46 percent year on year.

In September 2026 a robotaxi fleet operations consortium of six companies was launched, and the company's strategy rests on the idea that parking and charging sites can serve as robotaxi infrastructure. No schedule for when such demand converts into revenue has been disclosed. In set-top boxes, competition is shaped by shrinking demand combined with price and volume pressure.

06

Outlook

Three directions are confirmed. First, the absorption merger of Humax Holdings, announced in July 2026, took effect on October 1, 2026, leaving the operating company Humax as the single listed entity, which the company presents as simplifying governance and reflecting its business restructuring in value.

Second, the mobility subsidiaries reported a first-half 2026 operating profit of KRW 1.85 billion and two consecutive quarters of profit, and management said it will strengthen parking operations and AI-based data management to keep the business profit-focused.

Third, EV charging is expanding from the UK to Germany and France, and the company joined a robotaxi operations infrastructure consortium. The company also announced plans to combine its enterprise AI transformation technology with the mobility business.

No public guidance on a timeline for a recovery in the core set-top box revenue has been disclosed. As long as gateway revenue keeps declining, the key question is whether mobility growth can offset it, and the 30.3 percent quarter-on-quarter drop in 3Q 2026 revenue bears directly on that question. The next checkpoint is the annual operating margin and debt structure in the 2026 annual report.

08

Bull factors

Profit build-up at the mobility subsidiaries

The parking and charging subsidiaries reported positive EBITDA in 2025, and the parking unit followed its 1Q 2026 operating profit with a first-half profit. The mobility subsidiaries reported a first-half 2026 operating profit of KRW 1.85 billion and two straight quarters of profit. Operating infrastructure is widening the profit base as the set-top box business shrinks.

Restructuring and simpler governance

The absorption merger of Humax Holdings took effect on October 1, 2026, leaving Humax as the only listed company in the group. Ending the dual-listing structure also removed the listing-maintenance risk at the holding company.

The company describes the merger as a way to fully reflect the results of its business restructuring in corporate value.

Expansion in EV charging and robotaxi infrastructure

The EV charging solution has secured UK OZEV certification and is expanding into Europe, including Germany and France. The company argues that its nationwide parking sites and charging network could serve as operating infrastructure once robotaxi services scale.

09

Bear factors

Continued shrinkage of the core business

Annual revenue declined for three straight years from 2023 through 2025, and 2025 revenue was down 26.4 percent from 2024. Gateway revenue fell from KRW 196.2 billion in 2025 to KRW 101.2 billion in the first half of 2026.

Third-quarter 2026 revenue of KRW 76.3 billion fell 30.3 percent quarter on quarter, which gives little sign of a near-term rebound.

Recurring losses and quarterly volatility

The net loss attributable to controlling shareholders continued for four straight years from 2022 through 2025, and operating losses appeared in both the second and third quarters of 2026.

The KRW 35.2 billion of controlling-shareholder profit in 1Q 2026 far exceeded that quarter's operating profit, which suggests a one-off character. Second-quarter net profit fell 96.4 percent to KRW 1.28 billion, and third quarter turned to a KRW 5.2 billion net loss.

Financial burden and debt structure

The debt-to-equity ratio climbed from 212.4 percent in 2022 to 410.8 percent in 2025, with total liabilities of KRW 394.9 billion at the end of 2025 well above total equity of KRW 96.1 billion.

A financial news report said derivative liabilities stood at KRW 110.6 billion at the end of June 2026, with nearly KRW 99.6 billion of that current and due within one year. Put-option liabilities linked to the mobility subsidiary were reported at KRW 83.6 billion.

10

Risk factors

Financial and derivative liabilities

Derivative and put-option liabilities carry uncertain timing for cash outflows. A debt-to-equity ratio of 410.8 percent at the end of 2025 limits financial flexibility. Even with positive operating cash flow, settlement amounts could reshape the balance sheet quickly.

Listing requirements and governance

A financial news report said the KOSDAQ delisting threshold for market capitalization rises from KRW 20 billion to KRW 30 billion from 2027. The ownership structure changed in two steps, with a family company of the second generation becoming the largest shareholder. Together, the governance change and the higher listing threshold could weigh on shareholder confidence.

Automotive exposure and new-business investment

In-vehicle antennas are exposed to global swings in the auto industry, and they were cited as a 2025 earnings drag. The mobility subsidiaries have turned profitable, but investment expansion was reported as a cause of the group's losses.

Because no timeline for the shift to robotaxi services has been disclosed, the payback period for infrastructure spending is hard to estimate.

12

Overall view

Humax is moving its business toward automotive electronics and mobility infrastructure while set-top box revenue has declined for three straight years.

On the basis of filed results, the company has posted net losses attributable to controlling shareholders for four straight years since 2022, and operating losses in both the second and third quarters of 2026.

On the other side, the parking and charging subsidiaries reported positive EBITDA in 2025 and a first-half 2026 operating profit, which points to improving profitability in operating infrastructure.

The merger has consolidated the group into a single listed company, and European charging and the robotaxi consortium are presented as new pillars. Weak points include continuing revenue decline, quarterly volatility, a 410.8 percent debt-to-equity ratio and the burden of derivative and put-option liabilities.

Strong points include the profit build-up at the mobility subsidiaries and the governance simplification from the restructuring. Whether these forces offset each other is expected to become clear from the annual operating margin and debt structure in the 2026 annual report.
