02

Business structure

Global Technologies is a fabless company founded in 2013 that designs LED driver ICs (LDI) as its core product. The company listed on KOSDAQ in September 2026 as a semiconductor design firm.

LDI are chips that control the brightness of the LEDs behind liquid-crystal displays, and they are used for local dimming that adjusts brightness by screen zone. The company describes itself as an LDI company rather than a DDI company, and reports that about 70% of its revenue currently comes from TV products.

Based on the first half of 2026, reported revenue mix was 53.0% LDI, 26.1% merchandise, 14.5% discrete semiconductors and 5.0% commission. Its structure is shifting away from low-margin distribution goods toward in-house designed products.

Production is outsourced, and the Pyeongtaek pilot line is used for R&D and prototypes. Two Chinese packaging and testing vendors account for 72.3% of outsourced processing costs, so production or yield problems at those vendors can flow directly into customer delivery schedules.

On the customer side, the company was registered as a first-tier vendor of a major consumer customer in October 2025 and began volume LDI supply that December. Applications are expanding to monitors, automotive interior mood lighting (SAL) and in-vehicle communication chips (CAN), transparent LED and Micro LED. Supply share and sole-source status within customers have not been disclosed.

03

Recent trends

Based on DART filings, cumulative revenue for the first three quarters of 2026 was KRW 13.1 billion, up 86.9% from the same period last year. Operating loss was KRW 439 million and net loss was KRW 688 million, with losses narrower than in the same period last year.

Revenue growth is large, but the absolute base is small, and given the fixed-cost structure of design and R&D, the volume needed to break even is not yet clearly reached.

Full-year 2025 revenue was KRW 296.89 million in listing materials, and reports said part of the 2025 net loss came from valuation losses on redeemable convertible preferred shares issued before listing, which are non-cash accounting losses.

The company's margin argument is that as it enters a period of fixed-cost leverage, higher revenue should lift profitability. The IPO price was set at KRW 10,000, and the first trade on listing day was KRW 17,720.

The current share price is KRW 5,360 (up 4.28% on the day), with a market capitalization of KRW 105.3 billion. Recent reports noted a 26.76% one-day rise to KRW 9,000 with no disclosure or confirmed catalyst, followed by high volatility.

At listing, the CEO held 31.0% and four other parties held 59.3% in total, and pre-IPO investor Sungho Electronics held about 8.8%.

04

Outlook

The company has guided 2026 revenue to KRW 61.1 billion, with a 2029 revenue target of KRW 169.5 billion and a 2029 operating margin target of 21.8%. Cumulative Q3 revenue of KRW 13.1 billion means reaching the guidance would require a large concentration of revenue in Q4.

The CEO said at least 90% of this year's target is achievable, but this is the company's own view. In Q4, the items to check are the scale of LDI revenue, whether volume supply of LDI for a Taiwanese monitor customer begins as planned in December 2026, and whether SAL moves into mass production in Q4 2026.

Targets for CAN and automotive RGB LDI mass production in 2027 make schedule adherence in the automotive segment the next checkpoint. The optical-communication project with ADST began in October 2026, but no revenue timing has been set.

Expansion to Chinese TV and monitor makers could reduce customer concentration, but order sizes have not been disclosed. Whether the fixed-cost leverage the company describes shows up in quarterly margins will need to be confirmed through future quarterly filings.

05

Bull factors

LDI mass production and customer adoption

Volume LDI supply began at the end of 2025, and cumulative shipments are rising. The company says it built its supply base as an official tier-one supplier to a global customer. Its explanation is that products adopted by customers generate repeat revenue tied to follow-on models and finished-goods sales. Such adoption history can raise switching costs for customers.

Automotive and optical-communication pipeline

For automotive, SAL has received a first purchase order and entered mass-production preparation. The CAN communication chip is targeted for mass production in 2027. In optical communication, the company began developing UV-curing LED driver ICs for CPO and high-speed optical transceiver alignment equipment with ADST. The strategy extends TV-proven LDI technology into vehicles and optical communication.

Shift toward in-house designed products

The company is changing its business structure to raise the share of its own designed products over lower-margin merchandise. As product revenue grows, fixed design and R&D costs are spread over a larger base. A declining merchandise share can be read as a gross-margin improvement factor. The pace and extent of the shift will need to be tracked through quarterly revenue-mix disclosures.

06

Bear factors

TV concentration and customer dependence

About 70% of current revenue comes from TV products. In Q1 2026, revenue through one major customer's parts-purchasing platform accounted for 44.0% of the total. Because supply share and sole-source status within customers are undisclosed, high concentration could magnify the impact of order cuts or price reductions. The CEO has described the TV panel market as mature, so volume growth depends on rising LDI content per set.

Continued losses and a guidance gap

Cumulative Q3 operating loss was KRW 439 million and net loss was KRW 688 million, based on DART filings. There is a wide gap between the KRW 61.1 billion guidance and cumulative Q3 revenue of KRW 13.1 billion.

Micro LED products are still in design verification, and automotive products have not yet moved to mass production. Until the margin argument shows up as smaller quarterly losses, profitability uncertainty remains.

Share-price volatility and a decline from the IPO price

The confirmed IPO price was KRW 10,000, while the current price of KRW 5,360 is below that level. The expected market capitalization at pricing was KRW 254.6 billion to KRW 293.7 billion, compared with the current KRW 105.3 billion.

Reports noted sharp short-term rises without a clear disclosed catalyst, and warned of volatility from profit-taking. Float structure and changes in trading flows can also drive price movement.

07

Risk factors

Macro

Demand swings in global TV and monitor panel markets are the key variable. The panel market is viewed as mature, so slower set shipments or intensifying price competition could affect the pace of LDI volume growth. Currency and trade-environment changes could also affect the profitability of overseas customer sales.

Supply chain

Most outsourced processing costs are concentrated in two Chinese vendors, so production disruption or yield problems at one vendor could translate directly into delivery delays. Geopolitical tensions or tighter trade rules could raise costs to diversify production bases. The Pyeongtaek pilot line is not a mass-production fab, so backup production capacity is limited.

Business and financial

Many new products have no set revenue timing, so growth scenarios depend on successful mass production of specific products. The company plans to invest KRW 78.7 billion through 2029, with large allocations to R&D and operating funds. If investment does not translate into revenue, cash burn could accelerate.

08

Overall view

Global Technologies is in a stage where revenue is scaling quickly on LDI mass production and customer adoption. Based on DART filings, cumulative Q3 2026 revenue rose 86.9% to KRW 13.1 billion, but operating loss of KRW 439 million and net loss of KRW 688 million continued.

Strengths include the mass-production track record of TV driver chips, the automotive and optical-communication pipeline, and a revenue mix shifting toward in-house designed products.

Weaknesses include concentration in TV and in a few customers and outsourced vendors, the gap between company guidance and quarterly results, and undetermined revenue timing for new businesses. The current price of KRW 5,360 is below the KRW 10,000 IPO price, and market capitalization is about KRW 105.3 billion.

The next checkpoints are whether Q4 results and customer mass-production transitions translate into actual revenue and profit improvement. This is a qualitative summary for informational purposes.
