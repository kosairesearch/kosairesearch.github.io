Report overview

Loss-making Atum tests its pivot to EV and data-center power

Atum is widening its business from planar transformers into EV electronics and AI data-center power, but after three consecutive quarters of net losses attributable to controlling shareholders through the third quarter of 2026, whether its profit structure actually changes remains the key open question.

02

Business structure

Atum is an electronic components company whose core technology is planar (flat) transformers.

Its business is divided into transformer manufacturing (headquarters and Vietnam), MLCC distribution through its subsidiary Cheonghan Electronics, and precision processing of ship and industrial-machinery parts through DST, a company acquired in 2025.

Hana Securities said in a June 2026 report that parts distribution and ship and machinery parts currently carry the larger share of the top line, while new revenue from planar transformers is only at an early stage of expansion.

Transformers convert power inside chargers, and demand started with mobile chargers and televisions before extending to EV and data-center power supplies. Hana Securities' December 2025 report identified mobile electronics and premium home appliances as the existing core markets.

At an extraordinary shareholders' meeting on August 19, 2026, the company added EV electronic components, AI data-center power solutions, and control technology with software and firmware development to its business purpose.

The most visible EV case is a long-term supply contract for common-mode filters used in the ICCU of a Hyundai Mobis EV platform. In data-center power, Atum is preparing to enter by leveraging transformer technology that receives power first inside SMPS units.

Against larger components makers in the same sector, such as Samsung Electro-Mechanics and LG Innotek, the gap in revenue scale and customer base is wide. Atum concentrates on the narrow technology area of planar transformers, which is both a strength and a constraint.

04

Earnings analysis

Annual revenue went from KRW 38.5 billion in 2023 to KRW 20.4 billion in 2024, a 46.9% decline, before rising to KRW 70.4 billion in 2025, a 244.6% increase. Of the years 2023 to 2025, 2025 was the largest and 2024 the smallest.

The operating loss was KRW 4.15 billion in 2023, KRW 2.61 billion in 2024, and KRW 6.54 billion in 2025. The operating margin improved to -9.3% in 2025 from -12.8% in 2024, but the operating loss in won terms widened as revenue grew.

The net loss attributable to controlling shareholders was KRW 4.5 billion in 2023, KRW 2.16 billion in 2024, and KRW 8.47 billion in 2025, a three-year streak of losses from 2023 to 2025.

Net loss including non-controlling interests was KRW 4.72 billion in 2023, KRW 2.25 billion in 2024, and KRW 8.46 billion in 2025, and it should be read separately from the figure attributable to controlling shareholders. The 2025 revenue increase is linked to the acquisition of a stake in DST.

In the nine-month 2025 explanation, shipbuilding and industrial-machinery parts sales were added, but the operating loss widened on one-off and business expansion costs. Operating cash flow was -KRW 5.02 billion in 2023, -KRW 1.19 billion in 2024, and -KRW 5.87 billion in 2025, negative in all three years.

The debt ratio rose from 166.1% in 2023 to 221.5% in 2024 and 415.8% in 2025. At the end of 2025, total equity was KRW 14.1 billion, of which the controlling shareholders' stake was KRW 2.79 billion, and total liabilities were KRW 58.7 billion.

On a quarterly basis, revenue was KRW 25.5 billion and the operating loss KRW 1.11 billion in the third quarter of 2025. In the fourth quarter, revenue rose to KRW 27.7 billion, an 8.6% increase from the prior quarter, and the operating loss narrowed to KRW 960 million.

In the first quarter of 2026, revenue was KRW 26.1 billion, down 5.6% from the prior quarter, and the operating loss widened to KRW 1.39 billion. In the second quarter, revenue rose 23.7% to KRW 32.3 billion and the operating loss narrowed to KRW 995 million.

Third-quarter 2026 revenue was KRW 25.3 billion, down 21.7% from the prior quarter and down 0.7% year on year, and the company posted an operating profit of KRW 586 million, a swing to profit from a year earlier.

However, the net loss attributable to controlling shareholders was KRW 2.18 billion, larger than the KRW 1.19 billion loss in the second quarter. The gap between the operating profit and the attributable net loss needs item-by-item confirmation, including non-operating items and financing costs.

The net loss attributable to controlling shareholders over the last four quarters, from the fourth quarter of 2025 to the third quarter of 2026, totals KRW 5.03 billion. The fourth quarter of 2025 showed a net profit attributable to controlling shareholders of KRW 1.36 billion, a swing to profit from the prior quarter.

Industry analysis

A June 2026 report noted that automotive parts, once adopted into an automaker's supply chain, are hard to replace for long periods, so early entry itself carries competitive value.

The same report said Atum secured its first six-year supply contract for common-mode filters used in the ICCU of a Hyundai Mobis EV platform.

In the data-center power market, transformers are described as the component that receives power first inside SMPS units, and the same report judged Atum to be at an early stage of entry. In a July 2026 report, Hana Securities characterized Atum's data-center business in its title as one entering a certification phase.

The company is pursuing a strategy to shift its core markets from mobile and TV components to EV electronics and data-center power.

By contrast, a September 2026 corporate earnings comment said demand recovery in the transformer segment has been delayed, and that the segment still posts operating losses, though the loss has narrowed from a year earlier.

Against larger components makers in the same sector, such as Samsung Electro-Mechanics and LG Innotek, Atum trails in customer base and scale. It is building barriers by concentrating on the specific technology area of planar transformers.

An industry comparison from Alphasquare for the electronic equipment sector, based on full-year 2025 data, shows Atum's operating margin slightly above the sector average, while its debt ratio ranks high within the sector.

Both the EV and data-center markets are still at the stage of securing new customers, so the timing when industry-cycle benefits reach reported earnings is unclear.

06

Outlook

Based on schedules the company has disclosed, Atum targets KC certification for its 3.3kW module within 2026. For the 12kW-class module it targets certification in the first half of 2027, and it plans to start developing a 50kW module afterward. Hana Securities relayed this schedule in a June 2026 report.

Atum has signed a joint development agreement with Sungho Electronics for data-center and optical-communication power supplies, which the company presents as an attempt to move beyond standalone transformer supply into finished products.

At the August 2026 extraordinary shareholders' meeting, a proposal to raise the cap on stock acquisition rights from 30% to 60% of total issued shares was approved. The company said its recent rights offering drew a subscription rate of 101.11% and secured KRW 14.0 billion of growth funding.

The company's account is that the funds will support investment in new businesses. Separately, Hana Securities' December 2025 report projected 2026 improvement, citing the consolidation of DST, mix gains from higher-specification products, and automation at the Vietnam plant.

That report's 2026 revenue estimate was KRW 195.0 billion and its operating profit estimate was about KRW 15.3 billion.

Quarterly revenue in the first three quarters of 2026, however, ranged from KRW 25.3 billion to KRW 32.3 billion, and net losses attributable to controlling shareholders continued through the third quarter, so the gap from that estimate is wide.

Fourth-quarter 2026 results have not yet been disclosed in a regular report.

The next confirmation point is the 2026 annual report, which will show full-year revenue, the operating margin, and the direction of the net loss attributable to controlling shareholders, along with how the rights offering after the end of 2025 is reflected in the balance sheet.

A September 2026 corporate earnings comment said the first-half 2026 operating loss narrowed from a year earlier.

Funding and an expanded business scope for new businesses

At the August 2026 extraordinary shareholders' meeting, EV electronic components and AI data-center power solutions were added to the business purpose. The company said its recent rights offering drew a 101.11% subscription rate and secured KRW 14.0 billion of growth funding. With funding in place for new-business investment, the company has more room to pursue its transition.

Bear factors

In the third quarter of 2026, the net loss attributable to controlling shareholders widened to KRW 2.18 billion from KRW 1.19 billion in the second quarter, despite an operating profit.

High debt ratio and negative operating cash flow

The debt ratio rose from 166.1% in 2023 to 415.8% in 2025. Total liabilities at the end of 2025 were KRW 58.7 billion, large relative to total equity of KRW 14.1 billion. Operating cash flow was negative in each year from 2023 to 2025, at -KRW 5.87 billion in 2025.

Timing of new-business revenue and dilution risk

The data-center power business is at an early entry stage, and the 3.3kW module is still at the certification target stage. Demand recovery in the transformer segment has been delayed.

Because the cap on stock acquisition rights was raised to 60% of total issued shares, future dilution of existing shareholders remains possible.

10

Risk factors

Financial stability

The debt ratio is 415.8% and total liabilities are KRW 58.7 billion, while the controlling-shareholder stake is only KRW 2.79 billion. With operating cash flow negative in each of the last three years, borrowing and working-capital capacity could determine how fast earnings recover.

Whether the rights offering after the end of 2025 is reflected in the balance sheet is a central point to verify.

Execution of the business pivot

EV and data-center businesses only become revenue after certification, mass production, and customer adoption occur in sequence. At present, parts distribution and ship and machinery parts carry a larger share of the top line, so there may be a lag before new businesses show up in profit and loss. If the certification schedule slips, the pace of the transition could slow.

Shareholder dilution

At the August 2026 extraordinary shareholders' meeting, the cap on stock acquisition rights was raised from 30% to 60% of total issued shares. Following a rights offering that raised KRW 14.0 billion, the company has opened room for additional fundraising. Whether existing shareholders' stakes are diluted will depend on the scale of future financing.

Overall view

Atum recorded annual operating losses and net losses attributable to controlling shareholders in each year from 2023 to 2025, and its 2025 revenue grew sharply to KRW 70.4 billion.

Operating profit of KRW 586 million in the third quarter of 2026 marked a swing to profit, but the net loss attributable to controlling shareholders remained at KRW 2.18 billion, so whether operating gains carry through to net income is the central issue.

In EVs, a six-year supply contract with a Hyundai Mobis EV platform has been reported, while data-center power is at the joint development stage with Sungho Electronics and the 3.3kW module is at the certification target stage.

On the other side, the debt ratio is 415.8%, operating cash flow has been negative for three straight years, and the cap on stock acquisition rights has been raised to 60%, which leaves dilution possible.

Because net losses continue, the PER cannot be calculated, so valuation comparisons should be read alongside changes in operating indicators.

The next confirmation points are the KC certification target within 2026, the 2026 annual report due by March 31, 2027, and the 12kW-class certification target in the first half of 2027.
