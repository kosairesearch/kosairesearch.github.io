Jeil M&S: Quarterly Profit Returns Amid Rehabilitation M&A

Jeil M&S has been under court-supervised rehabilitation since December 2025, and its operating profit and net profit attributable to owners turned positive in the second quarter of 2026, while three straight years of losses, capital restructuring and listing status remain the central issues.

02

Business structure

Jeil M&S, founded in 1986, manufactures mixing equipment that blends electrode slurry for lithium-ion battery production. It has supplied large-scale mixers to Samsung SDI since 2007 and has maintained ties with LG Energy Solution and other major Korean battery makers.

In 2020 it was selected as an equipment supplier for Sweden's Northvolt, a relationship that became a problem when that customer went bankrupt and receivables could not be collected.

The company pairs equipment manufacturing with process engineering, so whether revenue is recognized at shipment or as work progresses became a point of contention in the audit. A job-data service's breakdown as of March 2026 shows the large majority of revenue coming from the secondary battery segment.

In defense, the company supplies high-viscosity mixers, and a February 2025 report cited Hanwha Aerospace, LIG Nex1 and Poongsan as customers while describing the company as the only domestic supplier of such equipment.

Defense revenue was KRW 3.3 billion in the third quarter of 2024, compared with KRW 500 million for full-year 2023, according to a 2024 news report. The company is also developing a continuous mixing system aimed at lowering battery manufacturing costs and is seeking to widen its customer base into battery materials. Concentration among a few large battery makers remains a structural feature of the business.

04

Earnings analysis

On a disclosed basis, annual revenue rose from KRW 58.4 billion in 2023 to KRW 149.5 billion in 2024, an increase of 156.2%, and then to KRW 151.3 billion in 2025, a 1.2% gain, marking two consecutive years of growth starting in 2024. Within 2023 to 2025, 2025 was the largest year by revenue and 2023 the smallest.

Operating losses were KRW 7.66 billion in 2023, KRW 87.8 billion in 2024 and KRW 7.24 billion in 2025, which makes three consecutive loss years.

A news report in February 2025 quoted the company saying the wider 2024 loss reflected a lump-sum bad-debt expense on KRW 58.7 billion of Northvolt-related receivables that could not be collected. The operating margin moved from -13.1% in 2023 to -58.7% in 2024 and then narrowed to -4.8% in 2025.

The net loss attributable to owners was KRW 1.13 billion in 2023, KRW 10.49 billion in 2024 and KRW 1.57 billion in 2025, and the consolidated net loss including non-controlling interests shows the same yearly values.

Operating cash flow was negative in each year: KRW -908 million in 2023, KRW -6.4 billion in 2024 and KRW -3.17 billion in 2025. At the end of 2025, total equity was KRW 1.15 billion, all attributable to owners, against total liabilities of KRW 53.95 billion, so debt far exceeds equity.

The debt ratio is shown as 922.9% in 2023, -2,499.6% in 2024 and -4,706.9% in 2025, and because the equity base is small, the ratio swings widely.

Quarterly results moved from an operating loss of KRW 1.99 billion and a net loss attributable to owners of KRW 2.13 billion in the third quarter of 2025 to an operating profit of KRW 570 million and a net profit attributable to owners of KRW 636 million in the fourth quarter, a return to profit.

In the first quarter of 2026, revenue was KRW 15.5 billion, operating profit KRW 1.28 billion (down 77.5% from the prior quarter) and the net loss attributable to owners KRW 913 million, a return to loss.

In the second quarter of 2026, revenue reached KRW 111.6 billion, up 622.0% from the prior quarter, with operating profit of KRW 3.75 billion and net profit attributable to owners of KRW 4.14 billion, a swing back to profit from a year earlier.

Revenue swings of this size are linked to how long installation takes, since the timing of revenue recognition was a point of contention in the audit. The cumulative net loss attributable to owners over the latest four quarters is KRW 11.7 billion.

05

Industry analysis

The company's end market is secondary battery capital spending, and orders depend on when customers place new line orders. In a 2024 report the company said it would lead with volume production equipment that lowers battery manufacturing costs even as electric vehicle demand growth stalled.

A 2025 report said that a slowdown in the battery sector combined with a weaker equipment investment cycle prolonged the earnings slump. In cycle terms, equipment orders are at a thin point, and customer investment in new North American plants was cited as a variable for recovery.

Domestic mixing equipment is supplied by a small number of localized makers to the major battery customers. For its 2024 listing, the lead underwriter used People & Technology among its comparable companies.

For high-viscosity defense mixers, two overseas firms were described as holding relevant patents, and Jeil M&S was presented as the only domestic player. Defense diversifies the end market, but defense revenue is still small relative to battery revenue. In Europe, finding new battery customers after Northvolt's bankruptcy remains a challenge.

06

Outlook

Milestones under the rehabilitation plan are the key variables for the coming months.

The Suwon Bankruptcy Court approved the plan on October 8, 2026, and a 95.24% capital reduction on common shares and a third-party rights offering of 31 million new shares at KRW 500 each, raising KRW 15.5 billion, are being carried out in sequence.

After the offering, the Finger group affiliate FG Investment No. 3 is set to hold about 79.9%, and the existing shareholders' stake is set to fall to roughly 2 to 3%.

The company said the KRW 15.5 billion raised will be used in full to repay secured and unsecured rehabilitation claims, and the acquirer is separately raising KRW 17.5 billion through bonds.

The listing date for the new shares is scheduled for November 18, 2026, though the company noted it may change under court decisions and procedures. Whether the shares remain listed is a separate issue.

The Korea Exchange decided on September 17, 2026 to delist the company over a disclaimer of audit opinion, and the company filed for an injunction to suspend that decision and is awaiting the court's ruling. Even if the injunction is granted, the delisting decision would still need to be contested on the merits.

On the business side, expanding defense mixer orders and launching a continuous mixing system for volume production have been presented as growth drivers.

In late 2024 the company projected defense orders above KRW 20 billion for 2025, and how that projection translates into quarterly results will be visible in the next periodic report.

08

Bull factors

Profit returns on a quarterly basis

Operating profit in the second quarter of 2026 was KRW 3.75 billion and net profit attributable to owners was KRW 4.14 billion, returning to profit. The company had turned profitable in the fourth quarter of 2025, posted a loss in the first quarter of 2026, and then returned to profit.

Revenue of KRW 111.6 billion, up sharply from the prior quarter, is consistent with the recovery in profitability. Because quarterly results are volatile, whether profit is sustained will show in the following quarters.

Defense mixer differentiation

The company says it is the only domestic supplier of high-viscosity mixers for defense use. Defense revenue was KRW 3.3 billion in the third quarter of 2024, well above the KRW 500 million recorded for full-year 2023, according to a 2024 report. The company projected new defense orders exceeding KRW 20 billion in 2025. Defense demand follows a different cycle from batteries, which supports diversification.

Capital restructuring through rehabilitation

The Suwon Bankruptcy Court approved the rehabilitation plan on October 8, 2026, which will be followed by a large capital reduction and a KRW 15.5 billion third-party rights offering. The acquirer is putting in KRW 31 billion across the equity and bonds.

Because the raised funds will repay rehabilitation claims, the plan provides a legal framework to reduce the debt burden.

Bear factors

Three straight loss years and capital structure

Operating losses were KRW 7.66 billion in 2023, KRW 87.8 billion in 2024 and KRW 7.24 billion in 2025, three consecutive loss years. Total liabilities at the end of 2025 were KRW 53.95 billion, far above total equity of KRW 1.15 billion. Operating cash flow was negative in every year from 2023 to 2025. Quarterly profit has returned, but a full-year turnaround has not yet appeared in the disclosed figures.

Listing status and trading uncertainty

The Korea Exchange decided on September 17, 2026 to delist the company over a disclaimer of audit opinion. The company filed for an injunction and is awaiting the court's ruling, and trading is suspended with the stock designated as a management issue.

A news report said the half-year 2026 review also resulted in a disclaimer. Until the listing question is settled, access and liquidity remain constrained.

Customer concentration and sector slowdown

The bankruptcy of key customer Northvolt led to KRW 58.7 billion of receivables being written off. A 2025 report pointed to a slowdown in the battery sector and a weaker equipment investment cycle as drivers of the earnings slump.

Quarterly revenue swung from KRW 15.5 billion in the first quarter of 2026 to KRW 111.6 billion in the second. Without broader customer diversification, this volatility could persist.

10

Risk factors

Audit and listing risk

After a disclaimer of opinion for the 2024 audit, a news report said the 2026 half-year review also carried a disclaimer. The 2024 business report was corrected and refiled on October 29, 2026.

The timing of any trading resumption and the accessibility of the stock depend on the outcome of the injunction against the delisting decision. If the audit issue is not resolved, meeting the listing requirements remains an open question.

Rehabilitation plan execution risk

The rehabilitation plan sets out a sequence of capital reduction, debt-to-equity conversion, the third-party rights offering and repayment of rehabilitation claims. The company said the November 18, 2026 listing date may change under court decisions and procedures.

Because the KRW 15.5 billion raised is earmarked for repayment, any delay would also slow capital normalization. The direction of management after the change in the largest shareholder also matters.

Customer and revenue concentration risk

Revenue depends heavily on a few large battery customers and the replacement cycle for battery equipment orders. As the Northvolt case showed, a credit problem at a single customer can directly affect earnings and receivable collection.

Large swings in quarterly revenue can keep profit and loss alternating from quarter to quarter. Defense and other new segments are still small, so their cushioning effect is limited.

12

Overall view

Jeil M&S makes battery mixing equipment as its core business and has moved into the approval and capital restructuring stage after rehabilitation proceedings began in December 2025.

On a disclosed basis, operating losses and net losses attributable to owners have continued for three straight years from 2023 to 2025, and total liabilities of KRW 53.95 billion against total equity of KRW 1.15 billion at the end of 2025 remain a financial burden.

On the other side, quarterly profit returned in the fourth quarter of 2025 and the second quarter of 2026, and the company holds a differentiated position in defense mixers. Whether the shares remain listed depends on the disclaimer of audit opinion and the outcome of the injunction.

The next checkpoints are whether profit holds in the third-quarter 2026 report, the November 18 listing schedule for new shares, and the injunction ruling. This report offers no investment opinion, rating or target price and is provided for information only.
