After Rehabilitation Approval, Listing Status Is the Key

02

Business structure

Jeil M&S, founded in 1986, makes battery electrode slurry mixing equipment. It builds and supplies mixing equipment and systems against customer purchase contracts, so the business is order-driven.

It first supplied large mixing equipment to Samsung SDI in 2007 and was named an equipment partner of Northvolt of Sweden in 2020. According to an Eugene Investment & Securities report from before the April 2024 listing, 2023 revenue was 99.5% secondary batteries, 0.3% defense and 0.2% food and pharmaceuticals.

The same report listed Samsung SDI, LG Energy Solution, Hanwha Aerospace and LIG Nex1 among its customers. Segment revenue for later periods could not be confirmed in recent filings, so the mix is given for 2023 only.

The structural weakness is dependence on a few customers' capital spending, and Northvolt's bankruptcy filing in November 2024 left roughly KRW 58.7 billion of receivables uncollected, which was reported as a major hit.

Afterward, the Suwon Bankruptcy Court opened rehabilitation proceedings on December 16, 2025, and the rehabilitation plan was approved on October 8, 2026. No recent material describing the competitive landscape could be confirmed.

04

Earnings analysis

Annual revenue rose from KRW 58.4 billion in 2023 to KRW 149.5 billion in 2024 (up 156.2% year on year) and reached KRW 151.3 billion in 2025 (up 1.2%), the largest of the three years.

The operating loss was KRW 7.66 billion in 2023, KRW 87.8 billion in 2024 (wider) and KRW 7.24 billion in 2025 (narrower), with operating margins of -13.1%, -58.7% and -4.8%.

The controlling-shareholder net loss was KRW 11.3 billion, KRW 104.9 billion and KRW 15.7 billion, and the net loss including non-controlling interests was the same in each year, so no non-controlling effect is visible.

Operating cash flow was -KRW 9.08 billion, -KRW 64 billion and -KRW 31.7 billion, negative in all three years. On a quarterly basis, Q3 2025 revenue was KRW 118.8 billion with an operating loss of KRW 19.9 billion (narrower), followed by an operating profit of KRW 5.7 billion in Q4 2025 (a swing to profit).

Q1 2026 revenue was KRW 15.5 billion with operating profit of KRW 1.28 billion (down 77.5% from the prior quarter), and Q2 2026 revenue was KRW 111.6 billion (up 622.0% quarter on quarter) with operating profit of KRW 3.75 billion (up 192.7% quarter on quarter and a swing to profit from a year earlier).

Controlling-shareholder net income was a loss of KRW 913 million in Q1 2026 and a profit of KRW 4.14 billion in Q2 2026, yet the last four quarters (Q3 2025 to Q2 2026) add up to a net loss of KRW 11.7 billion.

Quarterly revenue ranging between KRW 15.5 billion and KRW 111.6 billion fits a business whose results depend on equipment delivery, acceptance timing and revenue recognition, and the company changed its revenue recognition from a delivery-completion basis to recognition over time.

Also, outside auditors were reported to have issued consecutive disclaimers of opinion, so the possibility of later revisions to filed figures cannot be ruled out.

05

Industry analysis

The company's end market is capital spending on electrode processes by battery cell and materials makers. Mixing equipment is used at the first step of the electrode process, so orders come only after new plant expansions are confirmed.

In 2024, it was pointed out that the battery market's slowdown had often delayed equipment delivery and installation, and with them revenue recognition.

Brokerage material from around the same time highlighted a customer base widening into defense and pharmaceuticals, but in 2023 secondary batteries still made up the overwhelming share of revenue.

As the Northvolt case shows, a customer's financial health directly affects an equipment maker's receivable collection and revenue recognition. Recent industry data and market share against competitors could not be confirmed in this research.

The company's position therefore appears to depend more on its ability to resume winning orders and rebuild trust after rehabilitation than on the industry cycle itself.

06

Outlook

No recent earnings guidance from the company could be confirmed. What can be confirmed is the schedule for rebuilding the balance sheet.

FG Investment No. 3 is taking KRW 15.5 billion of new shares and KRW 15.5 billion of corporate bonds, KRW 31 billion in total, and KRW 17.5 billion of that is raised through its own bond issue. The proceeds are used to repay secured and unsecured rehabilitation claims under the approved plan.

Finger aims to use the deal to broaden its financial-IT-centered business into manufacturing and equipment. However, press coverage of the Finger deal named Jeil M&S's financial condition as the key variable.

The listing date for the new shares is November 18, 2026, as disclosed, and it may change depending on the rehabilitation court's decision and procedures.

The order backlog was KRW 278.2 billion in a May 2025 press report, but no updated figure could be confirmed, so the second-half 2026 revenue trend should be judged after the third-quarter report.

08

Bull factors

Balance sheet repair and fresh funding

Following approval of the rehabilitation plan, a 95.24% capital reduction and a KRW 15.5 billion third-party rights offering went ahead. New shares of 145.29 million were issued to creditors, converting part of the debt into equity. With proceeds going to repay rehabilitation claims, the debt burden is set to shrink. Funding from the new largest shareholder is a supportive factor for securing working capital.

Quarters of operating profit

Operating profit was KRW 5.7 billion in Q4 2025, KRW 1.28 billion in Q1 2026 and KRW 3.75 billion in Q2 2026, so the company moved out of loss territory in these quarters. Q2 2026 revenue was KRW 111.6 billion, up 622.0% from the prior quarter.

Room for operating margin improvement depends on how much the fixed-cost burden eases if revenue holds at that scale.

Technology record and customer base

The company is a mixing equipment specialist with more than 40 years of operating history. It has a record of supplying Samsung SDI, LG Energy Solution, Hanwha Aerospace and LIG Nex1, among others.

Extending applications to defense and pharmaceuticals is cited as a path to lower its dependence on secondary batteries alone.

09

Bear factors

Delisting decision and trading suspension

The Korea Exchange decided on delisting on September 17, 2026 and announced a liquidation trading schedule. The process is on hold and trading is suspended until the court's injunction decision is confirmed.

Even if the injunction is granted, the effect is only a temporary suspension, and the matter must be contested again in the main case. If it is rejected, investors' opportunity to trade their shares may be limited.

Full capital impairment and reliability of filings

At the December 2025 year-end, total assets were KRW 528.1 billion and total liabilities KRW 539.5 billion, a state of full capital impairment. The filed debt ratio is -4,706.9%. Reports of consecutive disclaimers of opinion by outside auditors raise a question over the reliability of the filed figures.

How the capital structure changed after the capital reduction and debt-for-equity swap must be confirmed in the next periodic report.

Shareholder dilution and volatile revenue

Existing shareholders' stake falls below 3% after the 95% capital reduction, while creditors hold about 17% through the debt-for-equity swap. As a result, existing shareholders' share of any later earnings improvement is much smaller.

Revenue swung from KRW 15.5 billion in Q1 2026 to KRW 111.6 billion in Q2 2026, and operating cash flow was negative in each year from 2023 to 2025.

10

Risk factors

Listing and trading risk

The outcome and timing of the injunction request against the delisting decision are undetermined.

The Seoul Southern District Court granted an injunction on October 2, 2026 in a delisting case based on market capitalization, but Jeil M&S's delisting ground is a rejected audit opinion, so whether the same reasoning applies is a separate question. The listing date for the new shares may also change.

Accounting and audit risk

The change in revenue recognition from a delivery-completion basis to recognition over time was at the center of the audit dispute. There is also a case in which the company's preliminary 2024 results differed greatly from the audited figures. Investors should therefore note the possibility that filed figures may change after the fact.

Governance and financing risk

With FG Investment No. 3 now the largest shareholder, management policy may change. FG Investment No. 3 also plans to buy corporate bonds, and it was noted that Finger's disclosure did not state the bond amount or terms. How the new investment funds are raised and repaid could affect future liquidity.

Overall view

In filed results, Jeil M&S cut its 2025 operating loss to KRW 7.24 billion and reported operating profit in each quarter from Q4 2025 through Q2 2026.

Against that, full capital impairment at the end of 2025, reports of consecutive disclaimers of opinion, and the delisting decision with a trading suspension mean that earnings improvement alone cannot settle shareholders' position.

After the rehabilitation plan was approved, FG Investment No. 3 became the largest shareholder with 79.91%, and the capital reduction ratio was 95.24%.

The debt burden and ownership structure have changed substantially, but how capital and earnings quality will show up in new financial statements has not yet been filed. The bullish side points to fresh funding and quarterly profits, while the bearish side points to listing uncertainty and shareholder dilution.

The next points to check are the share acquisition and new-share listing schedule in November 2026, the third-quarter report, and the injunction decision. This report is for information only and contains no investment opinion or target price.
