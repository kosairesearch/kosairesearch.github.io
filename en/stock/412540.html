Jeil M&S: Rehabilitation M&A Under Way, Quarterly Profits Mixed

Jeil M&S is moving through a court-approved rehabilitation M&A with a capital reduction and new equity, while quarterly results swing between profit and loss after three straight years of annual operating losses.

  1. 1

    Trading status: The rehabilitation plan was approved on October 8, 2026, followed by a 95.24% common-share capital reduction and a KRW 15.5 billion third-party rights offering. Reports describe the stock as designated for management and under trading suspension.

  2. 2

    Annual results: Revenue rose from KRW 58.4 billion in 2023 to KRW 151.3 billion in 2025, yet the company posted operating losses in each of the three years.

  3. 3

    Quarterly swings: Operating profit of KRW 5.7 billion in Q4 2025 and KRW 3.75 billion in Q2 2026 gave way to an operating loss of KRW 407 million in Q3 2026, when revenue fell to KRW 1.01 billion.

  4. 4

    Bear factors: Q3 2026 revenue of KRW 1.01 billion fell 99.1% from the prior quarter, and total liabilities of KRW 539.5 billion far exceed total equity of KRW 11.5 billion.

  5. 5

02

Business structure

Jeil M&S is a maker of battery electrode slurry mixing equipment founded in 1986, and secondary battery mixing systems are the core of its business. The company first supplied large mixers to Samsung SDI in 2007 and was named an equipment supplier to Sweden's Northvolt in 2020.

The company describes itself as the first in Korea to enter secondary battery mixing equipment. Its main customers are Samsung SDI and LG Energy Solution, and press reports say it has recently expanded into materials alongside cell manufacturing.

In Q1 2025, 97.4% of total revenue came from secondary battery products, and overseas sales accounted for 95%. Its defense segment supplies mixing equipment for defense use; a 2024 report said defense revenue in Q3 2024 reached KRW 3.3 billion, up sharply from KRW 500 million for full-year 2023.

The company has emphasized that it is the only domestic supplier of defense-use mixers. Installation and setup can take a year or more, which makes the timing of orders and revenue recognition prone to mismatch, a point raised within the industry.

Revenue tends to move sharply from quarter to quarter in line with customers' facility investment schedules. Detailed segment revenue figures should be checked in the original filings, but battery equipment appears to dominate the revenue mix.

04

Earnings analysis

On a filed basis, revenue rose from KRW 58.4 billion in 2023 to KRW 151.3 billion in 2025, but operating losses and losses attributable to controlling shareholders persisted for three straight years from 2023 to 2025.

Revenue jumped to KRW 149.5 billion in 2024, up 156.2% year on year, and rose a further 1.2% to KRW 151.3 billion in 2025, marking two consecutive years of growth. Among 2023 to 2025, 2025 was the largest year and 2023 the smallest.

Operating loss widened from KRW 7.66 billion in 2023 to KRW 87.8 billion in 2024 before narrowing to KRW 7.24 billion in 2025, with operating margins of -13.1%, -58.7%, and -4.8%.

Net loss attributable to controlling shareholders was KRW 11.3 billion in 2023, KRW 104.9 billion in 2024, and KRW 15.7 billion in 2025, and the consolidated net loss including non-controlling interests is reported at the same amounts.

For the 2024 deterioration, press reports attributed part of the loss to one-off costs linked to the bankruptcy filing of European customer Northvolt, and also pointed to a disagreement between the auditor and the company over revenue and cost recognition that led to the disclaimer of opinion.

The company carried out a re-audit of fiscal 2024 and amended its business report and audit report on October 29, 2026.

Quarterly revenue moved from KRW 118.8 billion in Q3 2025 to KRW 111.6 billion in Q2 2026, with Q1 2026 at KRW 15.5 billion and Q3 2026 at KRW 1.01 billion; no Q4 2025 revenue figure appears in the filed quarterly table.

Operating results went from an operating loss of KRW 19.9 billion in Q3 2025 to an operating profit of KRW 5.7 billion in Q4 2025, then KRW 3.75 billion in Q2 2026, before turning to an operating loss of KRW 407 million in Q3 2026.

A 2025 press report explained that revenue deferred from the prior year was recognized in the first quarter, which points to timing effects as a driver of the large quarterly swings. Controlling-shareholder net income for the last four quarters, from Q4 2025 to Q3 2026, totals KRW 8.23 billion.

At the end of 2025, total equity was KRW 11.5 billion, of which KRW 11.5 billion belonged to controlling shareholders, against total liabilities of KRW 539.5 billion, and operating cash flow was negative in all three years, at -KRW 9.08 billion, -KRW 64 billion, and -KRW 31.7 billion.

05

Industry analysis

The secondary battery mixing equipment market that Jeil M&S serves is highly sensitive to the facility investment cycle of battery makers. Cumulative revenue for the first three quarters of 2025 fell 15.8% year on year, and both operating income and net income swung into loss.

A financial information service summarized the drivers as slower facility investment by battery manufacturers and uncertain EV demand, which cut mixer sales and raised cost burdens.

A 2024 press report said the company was setting record results on overseas facility expansion by customers, so the industry appears to have moved from a 2024 upcycle into a 2025 adjustment phase. In March 2025, press coverage linked Samsung SDI's KRW 2 trillion rights offering to higher expectations for mixer supply.

As of Q1 2025, overseas sales reached 95% of revenue, so results depend heavily on investment schedules of North American and European customers. On competition, the company highlights its early domestic localization and its defense-use mixer supply as differentiators.

However, a payment and inspection dispute with customers has emerged as an industry variable, adding uncertainty over how durable its edge is. The turning point of the cycle can be gauged by whether customers resume new line orders.

06

Outlook

The company's path is now tied to its rehabilitation plan. The Suwon Bankruptcy Court approved the plan on October 8, 2026, and the company said board and shareholder resolutions are replaced by court approval.

Under the plan, the company is carrying out debt-to-equity conversion, a capital reduction, and a third-party rights offering in sequence, and the 95.24% common-share reduction is part of this process.

The KRW 15.5 billion third-party offering is to be used in full for rehabilitation M&A purposes, including repayment of secured and unsecured rehabilitation claims. The new shares are scheduled to list on November 18, 2026, and the company has noted that the date may change depending on court decisions and procedures.

The buyer process moved from the selection of Pureun Investment as preferred acquirer in May 2026 to a transfer of the investment contract position in early October, and press reports describe the acquirer as a group affiliated with Finger.

On trading, the Korea Exchange decided on September 17 to delist the company over the disclaimer of opinion, and press reports say the company filed a motion to suspend that decision and is awaiting a ruling. The 2026 annual business report will show full-year revenue, operating and net results, and the audit opinion.

Because the disclaimer of opinion appeared both for fiscal 2024 and in the 2026 half-year review, restoring a clean audit opinion is the key variable for resuming trading.

Bull factors

Balance sheet restructuring through rehabilitation

Under the plan approved in October 2026, a 95.24% common-share capital reduction and a KRW 15.5 billion third-party rights offering are being executed in sequence. The filing states that the proceeds will be used in full to repay secured and unsecured rehabilitation claims.

If debt repayment proceeds, the capital structure could change and part of the financial risk could ease. However, timing and amounts may be adjusted by court decisions.

Quarters of profit in the record

Controlling-shareholder net income was KRW 6.36 billion in Q4 2025 and KRW 4.14 billion in Q2 2026, marking two profitable quarters. Over the last four quarters the total is KRW 8.23 billion. Operating profit of KRW 3.75 billion in Q2 2026 stands out against the annual operating losses recorded from 2023 to 2025. Whether profitable quarters repeat is the next point to watch.

Localization track record and customer base

The company says it was the first in Korea to localize secondary battery mixing equipment, and it counts Samsung SDI and LG Energy Solution among its main customers. A 2024 report quoted the company stressing that it is the only domestic defense-use mixer supplier.

Overseas sales at 95% tie its demand base to global battery investment schedules. Much of the claimed technical differentiation rests on the company's own description.

09

Bear factors

Trading suspension and listing uncertainty

Reports describe the stock as designated for management and under trading suspension, and the exchange decided on September 17 to delist over a disclaimer of opinion. The company filed a motion to suspend that decision, and the merits would be contested separately afterward.

Until the listing status is settled, the timing of resumed trading remains uncertain. The capital reduction shrinks existing shareholders' stake to below 3%.

Capital structure and operating cash burden

Total liabilities at the end of 2025 were KRW 539.5 billion, far above total equity of KRW 11.5 billion. Operating cash flow was negative in all three years, at -KRW 9.08 billion in 2023, -KRW 64 billion in 2024, and -KRW 31.7 billion in 2025.

The filed debt-to-equity ratio moved from 922.9% in 2023 to -2,499.6% in 2024 and -4,706.9% in 2025, pointing to a heavy capital structure burden.

Quarterly volatility and customer dispute

Q3 2026 revenue of KRW 1.01 billion fell 99.1% from the prior quarter, and the loss attributable to controlling shareholders was KRW 1.35 billion. Q1 2026 operating profit had already fallen 77.5% from the prior quarter.

The company filed a complaint with the Fair Trade Commission against LG Energy Solution and its Canadian joint venture NextStar Energy over the balance payment for roughly USD 150 million of mixing equipment, in a dispute over installation and inspection. The disclaimer of opinion in the 2026 half-year review adds to the burden.

Risk factors

Listing and legal process risk

The timing of resumed trading depends on the outcome of the motion to suspend the delisting and any later merits ruling. Even after plan approval, the listing date for new shares may change with court decisions.

If the rehabilitation M&A does not proceed as planned, the route to recapitalization and debt repayment could shift. These matters depend on rulings by the court and the exchange.

Accounting and audit risk

A disclaimer of opinion was issued for fiscal 2024, and the 2026 half-year review also carried a disclaimer. A disagreement between auditor and company over revenue and cost recognition led to a re-audit and amended filings. The 2024 business report and audit report were amended on October 29, 2026. If the 2026 annual audit opinion is again a disclaimer, it could weigh on the listing decision.

Customer and cycle risk

A balance-payment dispute with major customer LG Energy Solution has led to a Fair Trade Commission complaint. Uncertain EV demand and slower facility investment by battery makers are weighing on mixer demand, according to analyses.

With overseas sales at 95%, results are sensitive to changes in overseas customers' investment plans. Uncollected receivables linked to Northvolt's bankruptcy were reported as a factor behind earlier earnings swings.

Overall view

Jeil M&S recorded annual operating losses from 2023 to 2025 while its revenue grew to around KRW 150 billion. Quarterly results through Q3 2026 alternate between profit and loss, and controlling-shareholder net income for the last four quarters totals KRW 8.23 billion.

On the balance sheet, total liabilities of KRW 539.5 billion against total equity of KRW 11.5 billion remain, and the rehabilitation plan's capital reduction and KRW 15.5 billion rights offering are the tools to reshape them.

On trading, the management designation, trading suspension, and the motion to suspend the delisting are linked, so the timing of resumed trading depends on court and exchange procedures.

On the business side, its early localization and customer base with Samsung SDI and LG Energy Solution are cited as strengths, while weaker EV demand and slower facility investment are weighing on orders.

Bullish and bearish factors deserve equal weight, and the sharp Q3 2026 revenue drop and the LG Energy Solution payment dispute are variables for earnings stability.

The next checkpoints are the scheduled listing of new shares on November 18, 2026, the ruling on the delisting motion, and the audit opinion in the 2026 annual report due by the end of March 2027.
