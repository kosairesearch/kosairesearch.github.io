02

Business structure

Pintel, founded in 2015, is a high-resolution AI video analytics company whose core technology analyzes original-resolution footage without loss.

Its main product lines are the stand-alone AVAX and the client-server PREVAX, and its 2023 annual report also introduced Pintel Net, a next-generation system that detects objects without GPUs.

At the Gangneung ITS World Congress in October 2026, the company unveiled PREVAX 4.0, a platform that integrates traffic, security and safety control and uses large language and vision-language models to interpret situations and propose response scenarios.

The center of the business is smart transportation, and a May 2026 security industry report said Pintel has taken a specialized approach to ITS projects.

Past traffic references include the Anyang smart school zone, signal verification at Goyang intersections, and a smart intersection system covering 51 intersections in Suwon.

Security and surveillance applications have also expanded, and recently a video surveillance project at the outer areas of Incheon International Airport and an Air Force surveillance system added defense and airport use cases.

A KRW 3.0 billion small-scale rights offering, with payment completed on September 11, 2026, will be used as operating funds, according to the company. The company says it aims to diversify its revenue base by widening demand for solutions it has verified in the field. A security industry report in May 2026 compared Pintel's 2023 to 2025 sales with those of Hanwha Vision.

04

Earnings analysis

Based on filed results, annual sales rose from KRW 8.77 billion in 2022 to KRW 12.8 billion in 2023, a 45.5% increase year on year, before peaking that year.

Sales then fell 16.5% to KRW 10.6 billion in 2024 and 45.8% to KRW 5.77 billion in 2025, two consecutive years of decline, and 2025 was the smallest year of the 2022 to 2025 period.

The operating loss narrowed from KRW 3.66 billion in 2022 to KRW 2.68 billion in 2023 and KRW 1.53 billion in 2024, then widened to KRW 3.23 billion in 2025. Operating margin was -41.7% in 2022, -21.0% in 2023, -14.4% in 2024 and -56.0% in 2025, as the loss ratio rose sharply alongside falling sales.

In 2024 net income attributable to controlling shareholders was KRW 66.65 million, a turn from loss to profit, but in 2025 it returned to a net loss of KRW 3.76 billion. Net loss including non-controlling interests was also KRW 3.76 billion in 2025.

Operating cash flow was negative every year, at -KRW 2.11 billion in 2022, -KRW 1.48 billion in 2023, -KRW 490 million in 2024 and -KRW 2.01 billion in 2025. Quarterly sales peaked at KRW 2.83 billion in Q1 2026, the same quarter in which the operating loss of KRW 670 million was the largest of the last five quarters.

Sales then fell 44.8% quarter on quarter to KRW 1.56 billion in Q2 2026 and 9.1% to KRW 1.42 billion in Q3 2026, two consecutive quarterly declines.

In Q4 2025 sales rose 63.6% quarter on quarter to KRW 2.75 billion, yet the operating loss narrowed to KRW 144 million while the net loss attributable to controlling shareholders widened to KRW 1.24 billion.

The gap between operating and net results suggests non-operating items were significant, and details should be checked in the annual report notes. Over the latest four quarters, the net loss attributable to controlling shareholders totaled KRW 3.03 billion.

05

Industry analysis

According to the 2023 annual report, the company projected the Korean video analytics software and service market would reach KRW 173.7 billion in 2025, and the total domestic market including hardware KRW 607.8 billion. A compound annual growth rate of 21.30% for 2020 to 2025 was presented.

These are the company's own past estimates and may differ from actual market totals. A May 2026 security industry report named AI, cloud and global market expansion as the three main growth drivers of the video security industry.

Traffic signal and security control are areas where government and public procurement are significant, so quarterly sales move sharply with procurement timing. Filed results show Q4 2025 sales rising 63.6% quarter on quarter, followed by a 44.8% decline in Q2 2026.

On technology, the 2023 annual report said the company had collected about 1.8 billion video frames and built 990,000 training data items across 80 object classes. A news report said the PMOT multi-object tracking technology ranked first in the MOT Challenge.

In competition, Pintel is often compared with video security companies such as Hanwha Vision, and its differentiation lies in high-resolution analysis and traffic signal control references.

Outlook

Applications are widening through a video surveillance project at the outer areas of Incheon International Airport and an Air Force surveillance system.

The KRW 3.0 billion rights offering, with payment completed in September 2026, will be used as operating funds, and the company presented expanding demand for verified solutions as a way to diversify its revenue base.

At the March 2026 shareholder meeting, the approval of the 11th-term (2025) financial statements, a partial amendment to the articles of incorporation and a stock option grant were all approved as proposed.

The company said it will pursue legal procedures while working to improve business results and enhance corporate value.

On governance, a February 2026 news report described a transaction totaling KRW 57.3 billion, combining a KRW 30.2 billion sale of existing shares and KRW 27.1 billion of new share and mezzanine investment, with settlement dates postponed several times.

Whether this transaction is completed and whether a change in the largest shareholder is disclosed will be key checkpoints. The next periodic report, the 2026 annual report, will confirm 2026 full-year sales and operating loss. The filing deadline is 90 days after fiscal year-end, which means the end of March 2027.

08

Bull factors

Differentiated high-resolution analytics technology

The company presents a patented technology that keeps original resolution and identifies small objects at long range as its core advantage. It said it combined this technology with the PMOT multi-object tracking method in PREVAX 4.0, and a news report noted PMOT ranked first in the MOT Challenge. Whether this technical edge leads to public-sector contract wins is the key question.

Expanding public-sector applications

Beyond traffic signals and security control, a video surveillance project at Incheon International Airport's outer areas and an Air Force surveillance system have been added as applications.

The company said the integrated PREVAX 4.0 platform, which combines traffic, security and safety control, supports wider supply to public institutions. Filed results also show Q1 2026 sales peaking at KRW 2.83 billion.

Balance sheet and a past turn to profit

Total liabilities at the end of 2025 were KRW 3.13 billion, with a debt-to-equity ratio of 18.2%. In 2024 the company turned from loss to a net profit attributable to controlling shareholders of KRW 66.65 million.

Total equity of KRW 17.2 billion provides a cushion against losses, but continued losses would reduce that equity.

Bear factors

Continued sales decline

Annual sales peaked at KRW 12.8 billion in 2023 and then fell to KRW 10.6 billion in 2024 and KRW 5.77 billion in 2025. Q3 2026 sales of KRW 1.42 billion were down 15.8% year on year, so the decline continued into 2026.

Because falling sales coincide with wider operating losses, the weakness will persist until a recovery in business scale is confirmed.

Accumulated losses and wider deficits

The operating loss widened to KRW 3.23 billion in 2025 from KRW 1.53 billion in 2024. Losses have been disclosed for five consecutive quarters from Q3 2025 through Q3 2026, and operating cash flow was negative in each year from 2022 to 2025. As net losses continued over the latest four quarters, the burden of eroding equity could grow.

Pressure from listing requirements

KRX decided on September 30, 2026 to delist the company, citing a market capitalization below KRW 20 billion. The company objected to the timing of the threshold and how it applies to technology-listed firms, and filed for an injunction on October 1.

A February 2026 news report pointed out that a sale process after its technology-special listing had been postponed several times.

Risk factors

Commercialization of new functions

PREVAX 4.0 integrates traffic, security and safety control and uses large language and vision-language models to interpret situations and propose response scenarios. Whether these new functions pass field verification and lead to actual supply contracts has not yet been determined.

If verification is delayed, the planned expansion of platform supply to public institutions in Korea and abroad could also slip. In that case, the effect of diversifying the revenue base through verified solutions could be limited.

Execution of large build-out projects

The Incheon International Airport outer-area video surveillance project and the Air Force surveillance system build-out were presented as grounds for widening public-sector applications. Whether the construction schedules and completion timing of both projects proceed as planned has not yet been determined.

If construction is delayed or its scope is adjusted, revenue recognition for airport and defense applications could also move later. It is also uncertain whether the projects will lead to follow-on supply after completion.

Order volatility and market alerts

Filed quarterly sales moved sharply, rising 63.6% in Q4 2025 and then falling 44.8% in Q2 2026. Market alerts citing concentrated selling by a small number of accounts or short-term price surges were repeated three times in 2026, which also points to periods of high share-price volatility. If sales depend on public procurement timing, quarterly volatility could persist.
