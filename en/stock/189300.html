02

Business structure

Intellian Technologies makes satellite communication antennas for vessels and ground sites, along with ground gateway equipment that links satellite networks to terrestrial networks.

Its product lines include maritime antennas, ground gateway systems, flat-panel antennas for low-earth-orbit (LEO) satellites, the military portable terminal Manpack, and aviation equipment.

Hana Securities noted in a February 2026 report that the maritime segment carries the largest revenue share, with gateway products also driving growth. The same report estimated that LEO products rose from 37% of revenue in 2023 to 48% in 2024 and about 70% in 2025 (Hana estimate).

The Manpack military portable antenna is cited by the company as a driver of demand after its launch last year. Its customers are mainly global satellite operators outside Starlink, including Eutelsat OneWeb, SES and AST SpaceMobile. Gateway backlog at end-2025 was KRW 292.2 billion according to Yuanta Securities.

In September 2026 the company signed a satellite terminal supply contract for defense and security markets with Network Innovations. The company is building on the operating record of a maritime antenna delivered to the U.S. Navy. Operations at its Irvine, California plant are reported to have supported a local North American supply base.

04

Earnings analysis

Annual revenue rose from KRW 239.5 billion in 2022 to KRW 305 billion in 2023 (up 27.4%), fell to KRW 257.8 billion in 2024 (down 15.5%), and reached KRW 319.6 billion in 2025 (up 24.0%), the largest year of 2022–2025.

Operating margin fell from 6.4% in 2022 to 3.5% in 2023, then was -7.5% in 2024 with an operating loss of KRW 19.4 billion. Operating profit was KRW 12 billion in 2025, a 3.7% margin, turning the 2024 loss into a profit.

Net income attributable to controlling shareholders was KRW 16 billion in 2022, KRW 5.53 billion in 2023 (down 65.5%), a net loss of KRW 3.01 billion in 2024, and KRW 7.46 billion in 2025. With no non-controlling interests, net income was disclosed at the same values.

Quarterly revenue went from KRW 77.2 billion with operating profit of KRW 2.37 billion in Q3 2025 to KRW 124.3 billion with operating profit of KRW 19.9 billion in Q4 2025 (up 61.0% quarter on quarter).

Q1 2026 revenue was KRW 64.7 billion, down 47.9% from the prior quarter, with operating profit of KRW 593 million and net income attributable to controlling shareholders of KRW 13.5 billion.

Q2 2026 recovered to revenue of KRW 97.2 billion, operating profit of KRW 7.14 billion and net income attributable to controlling shareholders of KRW 4.75 billion.

Q3 2026 revenue was KRW 76.2 billion (down 1.4% year on year), with an operating loss of KRW 2.76 billion and a net loss attributable to controlling shareholders of KRW 24.4 billion.

Several quarters show controlling-shareholder net income moving far from operating profit, which points to a large non-operating effect; the components should be checked in the periodic report notes.

Operating cash flow was -KRW 26.1 billion in 2022, KRW 29.8 billion in 2023, KRW 3.96 billion in 2024 and -KRW 18.2 billion in 2025, remaining negative in 2025 despite the profit. The debt ratio fell from 107.3% in 2022 to 66.5% in 2024 and rose to 86.4% in 2025.

05

Industry analysis

The satellite communications market is entering a phase in which ground equipment demand grows alongside the shift to LEO constellations. Hana Securities' analysis describes satellite network structures moving from geostationary orbit toward LEO or mixed LEO and GEO configurations.

A brokerage view relayed by Daily Invest regards 2026 as the first year of business expansion for the non-Starlink camp, with gateway and maritime antenna sales growing together. Infrastructure builds by OneWeb, AST SpaceMobile and other non-Starlink LEO operators are expected to give the company structural benefits.

Defense satellite communications is also widening, and the contract counterparty Network Innovations said the two companies plan to pursue defense markets jointly in the United States, the European Union, Canada, Japan and Australia.

On competition, the company highlights multi-orbit tracking and multi-band RF technology in its terminals. Starlink operates its own satellite network and terminals, so Intellian supplies equipment to operators outside that ecosystem.

The maritime business is an established line and its growth pace differs from LEO products, but its stability supports the overall revenue base. Shinhan Investment Corp. has noted that results could fluctuate with the pace of ground infrastructure investment and subscriber acquisition by customers.

06

Outlook

Among the quarterly results, the company reported record same-quarter revenue in both Q1 and Q2 2026. Q2 2026 revenue rose 30% year on year to a record for a second quarter. Q3 2026 revenue of KRW 76.2 billion, down 1.4% year on year, shows that quarterly growth has not been steady.

On orders, Yuanta Securities reported that backlog at end-2025 reached KRW 428.6 billion, up 68.7%, with gateway backlog at KRW 292.2 billion leading growth.

According to Hana Securities' February 2026 data, the Telesat gateway contract runs through December 31, 2027 and the SES terminal contract through September 30, 2029. The September 2026 Network Innovations contract covers a period from September 10, 2026 to December 31, 2032, about six years and three months.

The company also said it began shipping antennas from its U.S. plant. Securities firms estimated 2026 revenue of KRW 410.7 billion, up 28.5%, and operating profit of KRW 40.1 billion.

Operating profit for Q1–Q3 2026 was KRW 593 million, KRW 7.14 billion and an operating loss of KRW 2.76 billion, adding up to less than KRW 10 billion. Whether operating profit recovers in Q4 and later is therefore the key point for narrowing the gap with estimates.

New gateway projects from customers preparing next-generation satellite networks are also cited as an order opportunity.

08

Bull factors

LEO infrastructure expansion

As services by non-Starlink operators such as Eutelsat OneWeb, SES and AST SpaceMobile scale up, demand for flat-panel antennas and gateways is growing together. Hana Securities estimates LEO products reached about 70% of 2025 revenue. The company said Q1 2026 revenue rose 49.3% year on year to a record first quarter.

Backlog and contract visibility

Backlog at end-2025 reached KRW 428.6 billion, up 68.7%, with gateways at KRW 292.2 billion (Yuanta Securities). The defense ground terminal contract signed in September 2026 is worth KRW 40.94 billion and runs through 2032. The parties have said the contract value could be expanded further by agreement.

Diversification and local production

Beyond maritime antennas, the Manpack military portable antenna and defense ground terminals have been added to the revenue mix. Operations at the Irvine plant are cited as supporting a North American supply base. Q2 2026 revenue of KRW 97.2 billion rose 30% year on year, a record for a second quarter.

09

Bear factors

Swing to loss in Q3 2026

Q3 2026 recorded an operating loss of KRW 2.76 billion and a net loss attributable to controlling shareholders of KRW 24.4 billion, reversing the profitable Q2. Revenue of KRW 76.2 billion fell 1.4% year on year. Whether the profit run continues will be visible in the preliminary Q4 2026 results.

Cash flow and balance sheet pressure

Operating cash flow was -KRW 18.2 billion in 2025, negative even in a profit year, and was -KRW 26.1 billion in 2022. The debt ratio rose to 86.4% in 2025 from 66.5% in 2024, with total liabilities of KRW 230.2 billion and total equity of KRW 266.3 billion.

Customer concentration and estimate gap

Some product lines, such as gateways for one major customer, depend on a specific client, and results could fluctuate with customers' ground infrastructure spending and subscriber growth (Shinhan Investment Corp.).

Reported operating profit for Q1–Q3 2026 is far below the full-year forecasts, which makes the reliability of estimates a variable.

10

Risk factors

Customer and order concentration

Part of the gateway backlog is tied to contracts with a few customers, so delays in their infrastructure schedules could push back revenue recognition. Contract periods run from 2027 to 2032, so quarterly revenue can swing with the delivery timing of individual projects. Even when orders are disclosed, actual revenue depends on delivery schedules.

Competition from Starlink and others

Starlink's integrated satellite network and terminal model is a variable for the growth of the non-Starlink camp. If services by non-Starlink operators do not expand on schedule, demand for flat-panel antennas may fall short of expectations. Competing equipment makers' technology and pricing could also create margin pressure.

Defense contracts and overseas production

Defense terminal contracts can shift in delivery timing with government budgets and approval schedules. Early operations at the U.S. plant require time for production efficiency and cost structure to settle.

The pace of entry into national defense markets and the trade environment are also variables for overseas revenue.

Overall view

Intellian Technologies swung from a 2024 operating loss to 2025 operating profit of KRW 12 billion on revenue of KRW 319.6 billion, up 24.0%, and the company reported record same-quarter revenue in Q1 and Q2 2026.

Backlog rose to KRW 428.6 billion at end-2025, and the September 2026 defense ground terminal contract of KRW 40.94 billion was added.

Q3 2026 brought an operating loss of KRW 2.76 billion and a net loss attributable to controlling shareholders of KRW 24.4 billion, so whether quarterly profitability continues remains uncertain. Operating cash flow was -KRW 18.2 billion in 2025 and the debt ratio rose to 86.4%.

Bullish factors are LEO infrastructure expansion and order visibility, while bearish factors are quarterly earnings volatility, customer dependence and the gap between estimates and reported results.

On valuation, market capitalization is large relative to the KRW 16.1 billion of controlling-shareholder net income over the last four quarters, and comparisons with past price-to-book ranges can be read in different ways.

The next checkpoints are the preliminary Q4 2026 results and the 2026 annual report, with the direction of operating margin and operating cash flow as the key items.
