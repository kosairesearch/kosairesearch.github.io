HANIL CEMENT CO: Cost Cuts Offset Weak Cement Volumes

02

Business structure

HANIL CEMENT CO makes and sells cement, dry-mix mortar (remital) and ready-mixed concrete, operating plants in Danyang and Yeongwol. The company was established in 2018 through a spin-off from Hanil Holdings and holds Hanil E&C, a construction subsidiary.

It absorbed its subsidiary Hanil Hyundai Cement on November 1, 2025, and that subsidiary's results are now reflected directly in its standalone accounts. After the merger, the company has been pursuing efficiency by reducing duplicate investment and consolidating assets and infrastructure.

Cement segment revenue in the first half of 2026 was KRW 365.9 billion, down 6.9% from KRW 393.1 billion a year earlier, according to a November 2026 report citing Korea Cement Association data. Dry-mix mortar plant utilization was 27.1% in the first half of 2026, continuing a decline from 42.2% in 2024.

In October 2026 the company ranked first in three categories of the Korea Quality Satisfaction Index: cement, dry mortar and ready-mixed concrete. To strengthen sales of specialty cement used to improve site productivity, it created a dedicated specialty cement sales team that handles the early-stage 'Hysem' product.

Revenue is tied directly to residential and other construction activity, so construction slowdowns weigh heavily on it. On a 2024 consolidated revenue basis the company was ahead of a domestic rival, and commentary after the merger described it as reinforcing its industry leadership.

However, a November 2026 report ranked the company second in cement, so the ranking depends on the measurement basis used.

04

Earnings analysis

On a reported annual basis, revenue rose from KRW 1.49 trillion in 2022 to KRW 1.8 trillion in 2023, up 21.0%, and then declined 3.2% to KRW 1.74 trillion in 2024 and 18.2% to KRW 1.42 trillion in 2025.

Revenue fell for two consecutive years from 2024 through 2025, and 2025 was the smallest year in the 2022 to 2025 range. Operating profit was KRW 246.5 billion in 2023, up 108.9%, and KRW 271.4 billion in 2024, up 10.1%, which was the largest year in the range. It then dropped to KRW 132.7 billion in 2025, down 51.1%.

The operating margin moved from 7.9% in 2022 to 13.7% in 2023 and 15.6% in 2024, before falling to 9.3% in 2025. The company attributed the 2025 profit decline to demand for cement falling to its lowest levels since the 1990s.

Profit attributable to controlling shareholders rose from KRW 78.8 billion in 2022 to KRW 170.2 billion in 2023 and KRW 184.1 billion in 2024, then fell 59.7% to KRW 74.2 billion in 2025.

Net income including non-controlling interests was KRW 81.1 billion in 2025, a different figure from profit attributable to controlling shareholders.

Quarterly operating profit went from KRW 50.7 billion in the third quarter of 2025 to KRW 17.6 billion in the fourth quarter and KRW 17 billion in the first quarter of 2026. It recovered to KRW 61.6 billion in the second quarter of 2026, up 261.5% from the prior quarter, on revenue of KRW 371 billion.

In the third quarter of 2026, revenue was KRW 332.2 billion, down 7.2% year on year, and operating profit was KRW 28.5 billion, down 43.8%, while profit attributable to controlling shareholders was KRW 59.3 billion, up 72.1%.

Because the two measures moved in opposite directions, non-operating items likely explain the gap. Profit attributable to controlling shareholders over the last four quarters totaled KRW 126.6 billion.

Operating cash flow was KRW 246.3 billion in 2023, KRW 244.7 billion in 2024 and KRW 160.6 billion in 2025, after KRW -923 million in 2022.

05

Industry analysis

Domestic cement shipments peaked in 2017, declined through 2020, rose modestly from 2021 to 2023 on base effects and government policy, and then fell again from 2024. Shipments in 2025 were 38.1 million tonnes, the lowest level since 1991.

The Korea Cement Association projects 2026 domestic demand at 38.71 million tonnes and 2027 demand at 39.65 million tonnes, up 2.4%. The same outlook sees demand returning above 40 million tonnes only in 2029. Many analysts say the decline in housing sales and construction starts is holding back a second-half rebound.

The industry also faces rising capital spending for greenhouse gas reduction and environmental regulation. With demand falling while costs and regulatory expenses rise, leading cement makers have continued to report weaker results.

In competitive terms, the 2025 merger was seen as strengthening HANIL CEMENT CO's leadership, although a November 2026 report ranked it second in cement. Plant utilization fell from 76% in 2022 to 59% in 2025, and reached 58.9% in the first half of 2026, below 60%.

Because the company points to a shipment recovery from 2027, whether the cycle turns will be visible in data from that period onward.

06

Outlook

The company expects 2026 domestic cement shipments to rise 1% to 2% to between 38.5 million and 39.0 million tonnes. A delayed recovery in housing supply has pushed a meaningful demand improvement to 2027 or later.

The company expects large-scale housing supply and industrial construction starts to pick up from 2027, lifting shipments. A company official said in August 2026 that cost reductions alone have limits and that second-half profit improvement is hard to expect without a construction recovery first.

The ECO eco-investment program runs from 2021 to 2028 with a total plan of KRW 651.4 billion, of which KRW 500.5 billion (76.8%) has been spent and KRW 150.9 billion remains. The company expects the program to cut coal use by 30% and reduce greenhouse gas emissions by about 36,000 tonnes a year.

A report said the final payment of KRW 75 billion for the Busan plant sale was pushed to late September, and completion will be confirmed in later filings. Cash and cash equivalents were KRW 195.3 billion at the end of the first quarter of 2026, up 51.1% from year-end 2025.

First-half 2026 operating cash flow was KRW 75.47 billion, up 71.3% year on year. The company kept its per-share dividend for the 2025 year-end payout at the prior year's level, while total dividends rose from KRW 69.2 billion to KRW 73.5 billion because the share count increased after the merger.

08

Bull factors

Cost reduction lifts margins

First-half 2026 revenue fell 5.9%, yet operating profit rose 21.9% and the margin reached 11.8%. The company credits waste-heat power generation and greater use of recycled inputs for lower cost of sales.

Merger effect in standalone results

First-half 2026 standalone revenue rose 33.2% to KRW 624.5 billion and standalone operating profit rose 35.5% to KRW 84.3 billion. Commentary credits the merger with reducing duplicate investment and consolidating assets.

Eco-investment and cash generation

The ECO program is 76.8% spent, and the Yeongwol plant now generates about 30% of its own electricity from waste heat. First-half 2026 operating cash flow rose 71.3% to KRW 75.47 billion.

09

Bear factors

Shipments and utilization near lows

Domestic cement shipments in 2025 were the lowest in 34 years. Plant utilization was 59% in 2025 and 58.9% in the first half of 2026, below 60%, and dry-mix mortar utilization fell to 27.1%.

Third-quarter profit and revenue decline

Third-quarter 2026 revenue fell 7.2% year on year to KRW 332.2 billion, and operating profit fell 43.8% to KRW 28.5 billion. A company official said second-half improvement is hard to expect without a construction recovery.

Investment burden and leverage

KRW 150.9 billion of ECO investment remains, and the final payment for the Busan plant sale was pushed to late September. The debt ratio was 62.4% at year-end 2025, and total liabilities were KRW 1.15 trillion at mid-2026.

10

Risk factors

Construction cycle and housing starts

Cement demand is directly linked to housing sales and construction starts. If the housing supply recovery keeps slipping, the expected 2027 rebound could be delayed and shipments and revenue could fall further.

Environmental rules and capex

Greenhouse gas reduction and environmental rules are raising capital spending across the cement industry. The company is also pursuing follow-on ECO investments, so rising project costs could weigh on cash flow.

Cost inflation and limits of savings

Much of the first-half 2026 profit gain came from cost savings and a low base effect. The company has said cost reduction alone has limits, and rising materials and labor costs have increased project spending.

12

Overall view

HANIL CEMENT CO is raising operating profit through cost reductions while cement shipments remain near a 34-year low. On a reported basis, revenue declined for two consecutive years after peaking in 2023, and operating profit fell to KRW 132.7 billion in 2025.

In the first half of 2026, operating profit rose 21.9% despite lower revenue, restoring the margin. However, third-quarter 2026 operating profit fell 43.8% year on year, so the durability of the recovery is still being tested.

Positive factors include cost savings, merger benefits and improved cash generation, while negatives include shipments and utilization near lows, a weak construction cycle and investment burdens. Industry demand is projected to rebound modestly from 2027, but a return above 40 million tonnes is seen only in 2029. The next checkpoints are the operating margin in the 2026 annual report and whether shipments rebound in 2027.
