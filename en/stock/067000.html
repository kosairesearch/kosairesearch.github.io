JoyCity: A Test of New Releases Amid Persistent Losses

JoyCity's loss streak since 2025 now depends largely on how its football title Freestyle Football 2 performs after its global launch on December 3, 2026.

02

Business structure

JoyCity develops and publishes games. Its live title 3on3 Freestyle has been described in market coverage as a steady revenue contributor, and a Disney-related game is also counted among its existing titles.

The new growth axis is Freestyle Football 2, an online football game developed by a subsidiary, built around 5-versus-5 team play and role-based characters. It supports crossplay between PC and console and targets PC, PS5 and Xbox Series X|S.

On the publishing side, JoyCity signed a publishing deal in February 2026 for Project Imjin, a war MMORPG being developed by Red Ginkgo Games.

Resident Evil Survival Unit, co-developed with Aniplex, was shown at Taipei Game Show 2026, and the company said it is preparing a global service including Korea after localization for Taiwan and Asia. The portfolio mixes a proprietary football franchise, externally licensed strategy games and existing live titles. Segment revenue shares are not presented here; the discussion focuses on each title's role.

04

Earnings analysis

On a disclosed annual basis, revenue went from KRW 163.8 billion in 2022 to KRW 149.6 billion in 2023 (down 8.6% year on year), KRW 142.8 billion in 2024 (down 4.5%) and KRW 140.4 billion in 2025 (down 1.7%), a three-year decline. 2022 was the largest year in the 2022–2025 window and 2025 the smallest.

Operating profit rose from KRW 7.4 billion in 2022 to KRW 25.3 billion in 2023 (up 242.3% year on year), the peak, then fell to KRW 11.6 billion in 2024 (down 54.1%) and KRW 5.66 billion in 2025 (down 51.2%), a two-year decline. The operating margin moved from 16.9% in 2023 to 4.0% in 2025, close to the 4.5% of 2022.

Market coverage linked the revenue pressure to a gap in new releases: live titles were stable, but revenue growth was limited without new games.

Net income attributable to controlling shareholders went from KRW 5.51 billion in 2023 (up 72.5%) to a loss of KRW 5.55 billion in 2024, a turn into losses, and a wider loss of KRW 9.23 billion in 2025.

The 2025 net loss including non-controlling interests was KRW 10.4 billion; the gap with the controlling-shareholder figure is read as the non-controlling share. Operating cash flow was KRW 8.14 billion in 2023 and KRW 5.26 billion in 2024, then turned to -KRW 1.86 billion in 2025.

Quarterly, Q3 2025 revenue of KRW 26.5 billion and an operating loss of KRW 2.96 billion were followed by Q4 2025 revenue of KRW 48.2 billion (up 82.0% quarter on quarter) and an operating profit of KRW 3.29 billion, while the controlling-shareholder net loss widened to KRW 7.77 billion.

Revenue then fell for three straight quarters in 2026, to KRW 42.2 billion in Q1, KRW 38.6 billion in Q2 and KRW 29.6 billion in Q3. Q3 2026 revenue was up 11.6% year on year, but the low base of Q3 2025 should be kept in mind.

Q2 2026 posted an operating profit of KRW 583 million yet a controlling-shareholder net loss of KRW 682 million, and Q3 2026 showed an operating loss of KRW 3.21 billion and a controlling-shareholder net loss of KRW 6.69 billion.

The items that separate operating results from controlling-shareholder net income are worth checking in the annual report notes.

05

Industry analysis

The games sector has seen new releases concentrate among large companies. A market report from late 2025 said this concentration adds uncertainty to JoyCity's share price. Competition in mobile games has also intensified, making it harder to hold share for existing titles.

JoyCity's 2025 revenue decline reads as the result of a new-release gap in this environment. In consoles, Freestyle Football 2 drew strong feedback in its Xbox and PS5 closed beta, with praise for one-character-per-player control and cross-platform server stability, according to February 2026 coverage.

On the overseas side, the Chinese publishing approval for 3on3 Freestyle was reported as a sign of fuller entry into that market. Among listed peers, Krafton, Netmarble and NEXON Games are major competitors. Compared with them, JoyCity's results appear more sensitive to the performance of one or two new titles.

Industry-wide release momentum and the early performance of individual titles will therefore shape the earnings direction.

Whether Q4 revenue recovers from KRW 29.6 billion in Q3 and whether operating profit stays positive are the points to watch. The full revenue effect of the new release is more likely to appear from Q1 2027 onward.

A 10% discount on full editions was offered during pre-sale, and global wishlists were reported above 100,000. Its Xbox and PS5 closed beta earlier in 2026 drew praise for control feel and server stability.

Steady contribution from existing live titles

Existing titles such as 3on3 Freestyle have continued to contribute revenue during the gap in new releases, according to market coverage. A Chinese publishing approval is expected to support fuller entry into that market. Overseas revenue growth could ease the annual decline.

Operating profit back in the black in Q2 2026

Operating profit in Q2 2026 was KRW 583 million, up 86.7% from the prior quarter. Together with Q1 2026, it shows the operating loss narrowing at the business level. Q4 2025 also turned to operating profit, which can be read as a sign of operating improvement.

Bear factors

Three straight quarters of revenue declines

Revenue fell to KRW 42.2 billion in Q1 2026, KRW 38.6 billion in Q2 and KRW 29.6 billion in Q3, three straight quarterly declines. Q3 2026 revenue was down 23.5% from the prior quarter. Year on year it rose 11.6%, but the low base of Q3 2025 needs to be considered.

Persistent losses and a wider deficit

The operating loss in Q3 2026 was KRW 3.21 billion, a wider loss than a year earlier. The controlling-shareholder net loss that quarter was KRW 6.69 billion, the second straight loss-making quarter since Q2 2026.

Over the last four quarters, controlling-shareholder net losses total KRW 14.7 billion, and profit and loss swap from quarter to quarter.

Financial burden and reversed cash flow

Total liabilities at end-2025 were KRW 124.9 billion, and the debt ratio stood at 144.8%. Operating cash flow turned to -KRW 1.86 billion in 2025 from KRW 5.26 billion in 2024. The reversal may be linked to development and operating costs, a point to confirm in the annual report.

Risk factors

New-title performance risk

If the global launch of Freestyle Football 2 is delayed or early results fall short, revenue recovery could slip beyond 2026. Because the earlier revenue decline was linked to a gap in new releases, dependence on a single title is high. Console entry and overseas user response are key variables.

Competition and sector risk

Large companies are concentrating new releases, intensifying competition for user time and marketing resources. Maintaining share for existing mobile titles has also been described as difficult. Compared with large publishers such as Krafton and Netmarble, JoyCity's earnings are more sensitive to one or two titles.

Balance sheet and earnings volatility risk

With total equity of KRW 86.3 billion against total liabilities of KRW 124.9 billion at end-2025, the debt load is large relative to equity. The gap between operating results and controlling-shareholder net income varies widely by quarter, making earnings quality hard to judge. Continued losses could shrink net assets and weaken the financial cushion.
