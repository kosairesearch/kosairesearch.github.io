Report overview

Shifting to precision casting amid operating losses

DaedongMetal is moving from farm-equipment and auto castings toward precision castings for semiconductor, power-generation and marine uses, but it has posted operating losses for three straight quarters through Q3 2026.

02

Business structure

DaedongMetal, founded in 1947, is a casting materials and parts specialist. Its products include cylinder heads and blocks for farm-equipment and automotive engines, main control valves (MCV) for construction machinery, marine cylinder heads, and vacuum-pump parts for semiconductor equipment.

In a July 2026 company presentation, automotive accounted for 43 percent of sales, farm equipment 21 percent and semiconductors 20 percent, so automotive and farm equipment together made up about 64 percent. These shares are as presented at that time; segment sales amounts are not part of the DART filings.

Sales to customers outside the group rose 2.4 percentage points to 88.5 percent. The 2025 business-year filing was prepared on a standalone basis. In Q1 2025, intra-group sales were 9.2 percent, down from 16.8 percent in Q1 2024.

The product mix has three parts: general-purpose castings for automotive, farm and construction machinery; high-value precision castings such as vacuum-pump parts for chip equipment and engine parts for ships and power generation; and new businesses in robot and smart-farm alloys and structural parts.

Order intake in 2025 was KRW 48.4 billion, about 48 percent of annual sales. In September 2026 the company completed a KRW 9 billion capital raise and consolidated a hydraulic-parts firm in which it acquired a 94.7 percent stake, according to company statements.

Competition is shaped by cost and quality in general castings, while customer qualification and mass-production track records matter more in precision castings.

04

Earnings analysis

Based on DART annual filings, sales were KRW 146.9 billion in 2022, KRW 133.1 billion in 2023 (down 9.4 percent year on year), and KRW 99.5 billion in 2024 (down 25.2 percent). Sales rose 2.3 percent to KRW 101.8 billion in 2025. Among 2022 to 2025, 2022 was the largest year and 2024 the smallest.

Operating profit fell from KRW 2.34 billion in 2022 to KRW 1.3 billion in 2023 (down 44.3 percent), then turned into an operating loss of KRW 6.5 billion in 2024. In 2025 it returned to operating profit of KRW 100 million.

The company cited higher sales from key customers, higher selling prices and an improved cost structure as drivers of the 2025 turnaround. Operating margins were 1.6 percent in 2022, 1.0 percent in 2023, -6.5 percent in 2024 and 0.1 percent in 2025.

Net income attributable to controlling shareholders was KRW 2.18 billion in 2022 and KRW 830 million in 2023 (down 61.9 percent). It turned to a net loss of KRW 5.82 billion in 2024, and the 2025 net loss of KRW 1.39 billion was smaller than the year before.

Operating cash flow fell from KRW 8.46 billion in 2022 to KRW 3.48 billion in 2023, then was -KRW 3.25 billion in 2024 and -KRW 1.25 billion in 2025. The debt-to-equity ratio rose from 95.8 percent in 2023 to 127.0 percent in 2025.

At year-end 2025, total equity was KRW 43.5 billion and total liabilities KRW 55.3 billion. Quarterly, Q4 2025 sales of KRW 20.8 billion (down 24.4 percent quarter on quarter) were the low point.

Sales then rose to KRW 23.3 billion in Q1 2026, KRW 27.7 billion in Q2 2026 and KRW 27.9 billion in Q3 2026, three straight quarterly increases.

Operating results moved the other way: after Q4 2025 operating profit of KRW 1.31 billion, the company reported operating losses of KRW 723 million in Q1 2026, KRW 1.8 billion in Q2 2026 and KRW 3.69 billion in Q3 2026.

Net loss attributable to controlling shareholders was KRW 3.65 billion in Q3 2026, and the last four quarters total KRW 6.18 billion. Losses widened while sales grew, so whether higher sales translate into margins is the next point to check. One report also said gross profit fell from KRW 9.9 billion to KRW 1.1 billion in 2024 (Newsworker analysis).

05

Industry analysis

Demand from downstream markets falls into three groups. Farm-equipment, construction-machinery and automotive parts still rely mainly on general-purpose castings. Vacuum-pump parts for semiconductor manufacturing are tied to chip-equipment investment linked to AI build-outs (July 2026 press coverage).

The company said power-generation engine demand is rising with AI data-center growth and that castings demand from shipbuilding and defense is also increasing. Semiconductor demand and sales of marine and power-generation engine parts were cited as drivers of the 2025 turnaround.

In autos, electrification and lightweighting have increased the need for high-strength lightweight materials that can replace conventional cast iron, according to the company.

In smart farming, one report projects the domestic greenhouse smart-farm market at KRW 9.4 trillion by 2030, with about 35 percent classified as structural-material demand.

On production, one report from late 2025 said utilization fell to around 50 percent, and because casting carries high fixed costs, utilization weighs heavily on profitability. On competition, the company is trying to widen its customer base beyond group farm-equipment demand.

06

Outlook

The company's 2026 targets cover orders and sales. In a July 2026 presentation it raised its annual order target from KRW 70 billion to KRW 105 billion, and its chief executive said more than KRW 100 billion was achievable.

A September 2026 report said the company was working toward exceeding KRW 113.4 billion in 2026 sales under its value-up plan, while a July 2026 report listed the total sales target as KRW 115 billion, so the figures differ by outlet.

Sales of semiconductor, power-generation and marine parts are projected at KRW 39.7 billion in 2026, up 37.1 percent from about KRW 29 billion in 2025. First-half sales of these parts were KRW 16.5 billion, up 11.3 percent year on year.

First-half new orders totaled KRW 27.9 billion on five-year expected sales, mainly power-generation engine parts at KRW 15.7 billion and vacuum-pump parts at KRW 3.4 billion. Production of these orders is scheduled in stages from late 2026 through 2028 according to customer schedules.

For the medium term, the company targets KRW 240 billion in sales and KRW 12.5 billion in operating profit by 2030, with new businesses at 28 percent of sales and overseas sales at 30 percent.

In new alloys, it is co-developing with the Korea Institute of Materials Science (KIMS) for robot joint and reducer housings and similar parts, aiming for first sales in 2027 and customer performance validation by 2029. Smart-farm structural parts are also targeted for first sales in 2027.

The company plans to invest at least KRW 400 million in an AI manufacturing program in 2026 and more than KRW 1.2 billion from 2027 for an autonomous plant.

Bull factors

Expanding high-value precision casting orders

Order intake was KRW 48.4 billion in 2025, and the company raised its 2026 order target to KRW 105 billion (July 2026). Sales of semiconductor, power-generation and marine parts are projected at KRW 39.7 billion for 2026, up 37.1 percent from about KRW 29 billion in 2025. First-half 2026 new orders reached KRW 27.9 billion.

2025 turnaround and cost improvements

Operating profit of KRW 100 million in 2025 marked a turnaround from the 2024 operating loss. The company cited higher sales from key customers, higher selling prices and cost-structure improvements. Quarterly sales rose for three straight quarters after the Q4 2025 low.

New businesses and shareholder returns

The company is developing robot alloys with KIMS and targets first sales of smart-farm structural parts in 2027. It said it has paid dividends for 31 consecutive years and in 2026 approved a KRW 1 billion share buyback trust with plans to cancel all repurchased shares.

09

Bear factors

Widening operating losses and accumulated deficits

Operating losses have continued for three straight quarters from Q1 through Q3 2026, and the Q3 2026 loss of KRW 3.69 billion widened from a year earlier. Net loss attributable to controlling shareholders for the last four quarters totals KRW 6.18 billion. Operating cash flow was negative in both 2024 and 2025.

Sales growth not converting into profit

Sales rose to KRW 27.7 billion in Q2 2026 and KRW 27.9 billion in Q3 2026, yet operating losses widened to KRW 1.8 billion and KRW 3.69 billion. A 2025 report said utilization had fallen to around 50 percent, and high fixed costs in casting magnify the effect of low utilization on profit.

Target difficulty and short-term price swings

The 2030 target of KRW 240 billion in sales is more than double the KRW 101.8 billion recorded in 2025, and it requires lifting overseas sales from about 10 percent to 30 percent. Because the sharp short-term rise led to the investment-warning designation, price volatility may increase afterward.

10

Risk factors

Financial structure and funding

The debt-to-equity ratio rose to 127.0 percent in 2025 from 95.8 percent in 2023, and total liabilities were KRW 55.3 billion at end-2025. Equity was expanded in 2026 through a capital raise that funded a subsidiary acquisition, so further funding needs could bring additional dilution.

Ownership structure and free float

The largest shareholder's stake rose to 68.4 percent after the capital raise, and 3 million new shares are locked up for one year. Intra-group sales were 9.2 percent in Q1 2025, lower than before, but the group's control over the company remains significant.

Execution risk in the business transition

Integrating the acquired hydraulic business and completing customer performance validation for the new alloys could run to 2029. Mass production of new semiconductor, power-generation and marine orders is set to start in stages from late 2026 through 2028, so revenue recognition could be delayed.

12

Overall view

DaedongMetal is shifting from farm-equipment and automotive castings toward precision castings for semiconductor, power-generation and marine applications, along with new businesses.

In DART filings, operating profit turned positive in 2025 at KRW 100 million, and the company has set targets for 2026 orders and growth in semiconductor, power and marine parts. On the other side, operating losses have continued from Q1 through Q3 2026, with the Q3 loss widening to KRW 3.69 billion.

Sales have risen without a matching profit gain, so margin recovery is the key point to track. Equity expanded through a capital raise, the largest shareholder's stake increased, and the share price move led to an investment-warning designation.

The 2030 sales target is far above current results, so tracking quarterly order execution and operating margins is the way to follow the path. The next checkpoint is how the 2026 annual report shows the full-year operating margin and the effect of the newly consolidated subsidiary.
