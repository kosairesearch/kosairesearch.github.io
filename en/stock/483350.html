Report overview

Earnings Improve, Share Price Diverges

Dealicious, a Dongdaemun fashion B2B platform operator, is growing operating profit quickly, yet its share price has fallen to less than one-third of the IPO price within about two and a half months of listing.

02

Business structure

Dealicious was founded in 2011 and launched its fashion B2B platform Sinsang Market in 2013. Its core business is operating a B2B platform that links Dongdaemun fashion wholesalers with domestic and overseas retailers.

The market classification reads food and beverage, but the actual business is a fashion platform unrelated to food. Direct comparison with listed food and beverage companies such as KT&G, Nongshim and Orion is therefore difficult.

The revenue model consists of Sinsang Ad, an advertising product for wholesalers, Sinsang Membership, a subscription service, and Plus Membership for retailers.

According to the company, more than 80% of 2025 operating revenue came from platform businesses such as advertising and subscriptions, which carry almost no variable cost. The contribution margin for 2025 was reported at 81%.

Of 2025 revenue of KRW 30.7 billion, about KRW 28.6 billion came from Korea and about KRW 2.0 billion from overseas, which shows a high domestic dependence.

The company says it holds a 97% share of daily users among fashion B2B platforms. Independent research house ValueFinder reported that, as of September 2025, its daily active users far exceeded those of two competing platforms.

Overseas, it offers a delivery service covering sourcing through shipping to retailers in roughly 90 countries. In 2026 it also unveiled Jangkki Note, a B2B SaaS solution that automates the organizing of Dongdaemun trade receipts.

03

Recent trends

Per disclosed results, first-half 2026 revenue was KRW 9.75 billion, up 15.8% year on year. Operating profit rose 116.9% to KRW 2.56 billion, while net income fell 40.1% to KRW 2.65 billion.

However, the company's own announcement put first-half consolidated operating revenue at KRW 17.6 billion and operating profit at KRW 3.2 billion, and separately gave second-quarter operating revenue of KRW 9.75 billion and operating profit of KRW 2.56 billion.

Because the disclosed half-year figures match the second-quarter figures, the period of aggregation should be checked against the original filing.

Sales from platform businesses, namely Sinsang Ad, Sinsang Membership and subscription services, rose 21.8% to KRW 14.82 billion in the first half. Media reports noted that operating profit improved but financial costs and other factors limited the increase in net income.

In 2025, revenue grew 22.3% to KRW 30.7 billion and operating profit turned positive at KRW 1.6 billion. That turnaround followed operating losses of KRW 11.8 billion in 2023 and KRW 5.7 billion in 2024.

The shares listed on August 12, 2026 at an IPO price of KRW 7,000, raising KRW 15.4 billion. The first-day close was KRW 5,140, down 26.57% from the IPO price. The close on September 9, 2026 was KRW 3,055.

At the time of writing, the price is KRW 1,995, market capitalization is KRW 44.7 billion and trading value is KRW 200 million. In the book-building, 436 of 441 participating institutions did not commit to a lock-up.

04

Outlook

The 2026 targets presented during the IPO were operating revenue of KRW 37.2 billion and operating profit of KRW 9.0 billion. First-half 2026 operating profit was KRW 3.2 billion by the company's announcement, so reaching the target requires a considerably larger second-half profit.

CEO Chang-han Jung described an operating leverage structure in which a KRW 10 billion rise in operating revenue lifts operating profit by KRW 8 billion. Whether that structure shows up in quarterly results is the central question.

Of the roughly KRW 15 billion in net IPO proceeds, KRW 4.4 billion is earmarked for overseas advertising and local partnerships, KRW 2.0 billion for logistics automation, and KRW 8.6 billion for acquiring or investing in e-commerce SaaS providers.

In the first half of 2026, new customers in Taiwan and Hong Kong rose 27%, and the repurchase rate among local businesses exceeded 57% in roughly the first two months after direct delivery was introduced.

The observation period is short, so later quarterly results will show whether overseas revenue becomes a meaningful share.

According to a lock-up release schedule, the tradable share ratio rises to 70.2% on November 12, 2026. This schedule is cited as a supply-and-demand variable. On the listing day, commentary linked the weakness to a cooling IPO market and a soft KOSDAQ.

Market conditions, post-half-year earnings and overseas expansion results are the main variables in the share price discussion.

05

Bull factors

Operating leverage on a fixed-cost base

The company explains that platform businesses carry small variable costs, so profit grows faster than sales. Per disclosed results, first-half 2026 operating profit rose 116.9%, well above revenue growth of 15.8%. The contribution margin for 2025 was reported at 81%. Room for further operating margin improvement depends on whether this structure holds.

Domestic platform position and a settled revenue model

The company cites a 97% share based on daily users. Sales from the advertising- and subscription-centered platform businesses rose 21.8% to KRW 14.82 billion in the first half of 2026. Profit growth continued after the annual operating profit turned positive in 2025.

The fact that a platform whose main investment is done does not need much additional fixed cost is cited as a strength.

Funding for overseas expansion and SaaS

IPO proceeds were allocated to overseas advertising, logistics automation, and SaaS acquisitions and equity investments. New customers in Taiwan and Hong Kong grew 27% year on year in the first half of 2026. As of 2025, the company also kept a net cash position backed by ample cash and equivalents. Growth in non-domestic revenue sources could reduce the domestic dependence.

06

Bear factors

Falling net income and the burden of the target

Per disclosed results, first-half 2026 net income fell 40.1% to KRW 2.65 billion, diverging from operating profit. Reports explained that financial costs and other factors limited the increase in net income.

One commentary noted that the 2026 operating profit target of KRW 9.0 billion is more than five times the 2025 result and determines whether the IPO price was justified. If the gap is not closed in the second half, doubts about earnings credibility could grow.

Supply pressure and thin liquidity

The lock-up commitment ratio was 0.1% of applied volume, the lowest among KOSDAQ listings in 2026. After listing, the price fell from the IPO price of KRW 7,000 to KRW 1,995. Trading value at the time of writing was only KRW 200 million, indicating thin trading. A structure in which shares may come to market at each lock-up release is pointed out as a weakness.

Domestic dependence and execution of expansion

In 2025, revenue was KRW 28.6 billion from Korea and KRW 2.0 billion from overseas, so the domestic share is overwhelming. Proving that the advertising and subscription model built in Korea can be extended overseas is cited as the key task.

KRW 8.6 billion of IPO proceeds is planned for SaaS acquisitions or equity investments, so target selection and integration results matter. The repurchase rate from direct delivery rests on about two months of data and is too early to read as a long-term trend.

07

Risk factors

Macro

A downturn in retail conditions or fashion consumption could reduce subscription spending by retailers and advertising spending by wholesalers. On the listing day, commentary said a cooling IPO market and a weak KOSDAQ weighed on the shares.

The strategy of growing overseas sales is also exposed to exchange rates and consumption conditions in markets such as Taiwan and Hong Kong. Specific sensitivities are not confirmed in public materials.

Industry

The business is concentrated in the Dongdaemun fashion wholesale and retail ecosystem, so it is affected by structural change in wholesaling. The 97% share is a figure presented by the company, and the revenue model could be shaken if competing platforms change pricing or sellers become less receptive to advertising.

The fashion SaaS field could bring new competitors. Whether new solutions such as Jangkki Note translate into revenue needs to be confirmed later.

Company-specific

The company entered KOSDAQ under the special listing track for companies not yet profitable. Accumulated deficits from past large losses remain, so profit improvement takes time to translate into stronger capital. Under the lock-up release schedule, the tradable share volume increases after November 12, 2026.

The stake of the largest shareholder and related parties and the history of new share issuance are also items for checking the shareholding structure.

08

Overall view

Dealicious is classified under food and beverage, but it actually operates a fashion B2B platform in Dongdaemun.

Per disclosed results, first-half 2026 operating profit rose 116.9% to KRW 2.56 billion, and the company explains that its advertising- and subscription-based model is expanding profit against a largely fixed cost base.

In contrast, net income fell 40.1% to KRW 2.65 billion, and the gap to the 2026 operating profit target of KRW 9.0 billion has to be checked against second-half results.

The share price of KRW 1,995 is far below the IPO price of KRW 7,000, and a low lock-up commitment ratio and the continuing lock-up release schedule are cited as supply-and-demand variables. Overseas direct delivery, Jangkki Note and the use of IPO proceeds are growth variables, and they also carry execution burdens.

Because bullish and bearish factors coexist, quarterly results, overseas indicators and changes in tradable volume should be weighed together.
