Sunjin: Quarterly profit slows after the Q1 2026 peak

02

Business structure

Sunjin was founded in 1979 to make and sell compound feed and has been listed on the KOSPI market since its 2011 spin-off. Its main businesses are compound feed for hogs, cattle and poultry, hog farming for market hogs, meat products, and processed meat such as sausages and patties.

According to a Korea IR Council report, before consolidation adjustments the sales mix was 41.9% compound feed, 25.2% meat, 17.6% hog farming, 12.3% processed meat and 3.0% other. Another breakdown puts feed and livestock at about 78% of total sales, so these two businesses sit at the center of the revenue structure.

The two breakdowns use different segment definitions, so their shares cannot simply be added together. The company is described as building cost competitiveness through vertical integration from feed to livestock and processed meat.

Rising meat demand and consumer trust in domestic pork are cited as support for volumes. Hog prices in Korea and Vietnam are cited as key swing factors for earnings. Hog prices and herd sizes affect both the hog farming segment and feed sales volumes at the same time.

The company runs six logistics centers nationwide to support distribution. Harim, which combines feed, pork and processed meat, is a useful comparison for the business structure.

04

Earnings analysis

Annual sales rose from KRW 1.87 trillion in 2022 to KRW 1.91 trillion in 2023 (up 1.9% year on year), fell to KRW 1.68 trillion in 2024 (down 11.8%) and recovered to KRW 1.9 trillion in 2025 (up 12.7%). Among 2022 to 2025, 2023 was the largest year and 2024 the smallest.

Operating profit grew from KRW 70.6 billion in 2022 to KRW 97.5 billion in 2023 (up 38.1%), KRW 124.1 billion in 2024 (up 27.2%) and KRW 179.2 billion in 2025 (up 44.4%), rising three years in a row from 2023 through 2025.

The operating margin widened from 3.8% in 2022 to 5.1% in 2023, 7.4% in 2024 and 9.5% in 2025, and kept improving even in 2024, when sales declined.

Net profit attributable to controlling shareholders was KRW 23.2 billion in 2022, KRW 13.9 billion in 2023 (down 40.0%) and KRW 118.9 billion in 2025, with no data for 2024.

Net profit including non-controlling interests, a different measure, was KRW 23 billion in 2022, KRW 10.5 billion in 2023, KRW 6.64 billion in 2024 and KRW 126 billion in 2025.

In 2023 and 2024 operating profit rose while net profit fell, and an earlier analysis pointed to foreign-exchange derivative results as the cause of weak net profit.

Total equity at the end of 2025 was KRW 582.1 billion, of which KRW 563.1 billion belonged to controlling shareholders, against total liabilities of KRW 779.6 billion, and operating cash flow was KRW 3.92 billion in 2022, KRW 66.6 billion in 2023 and KRW 129 billion in 2025.

Quarterly sales rose from KRW 473.2 billion in Q3 2025 to KRW 533 billion in Q1 2026 and KRW 541.1 billion in Q2 2026 (up 1.5% from the prior quarter), then fell to KRW 352.7 billion in Q3 2026 (down 34.8% from the prior quarter and 25.5% from a year earlier).

Operating profit peaked at KRW 61.4 billion in Q1 2026, then fell to KRW 43.4 billion in Q2 (down 29.3% from the prior quarter) and KRW 8.2 billion in Q3 (down 81.1% from the prior quarter and 78.6% from a year earlier).

Net profit attributable to controlling shareholders in Q3 2026 was KRW 14.3 billion, up 5.2% from a year earlier, so it moved in the opposite direction to operating profit. A summary of first-half 2026 results put sales up 16.9% and operating profit up 4.3% year on year, while net profit fell 36.7%. The last four quarters of controlling-shareholder net profit total KRW 86.2 billion.

05

Industry analysis

Demand for domestic pork is described as holding up amid rising meat consumption and consumer trust in domestic pork. Feed and livestock make up about 78% of sales, so the operating environment is directly tied to the cycle of hog prices and feed raw material costs.

High hog prices support feed demand from farms, while higher grain prices raise cost pressure, and both forces coexist. An earlier analysis by Yuanta Securities noted that a weaker won and stable grain prices had lowered the cost ratio, and described hog prices in Korea and Vietnam as firm.

Vertical integration lets the livestock segment absorb feed costs, which is cited as a buffer when feed prices move. In competition, Sunjin is compared with Harim, which also runs feed, pork and processed meat. The processed meat segment, which makes sausages and patties, links meat raw materials to finished products.

Whether the 34.8% quarter-on-quarter sales decline in Q3 2026 reflects a slowdown in the industry or a one-time factor is a point to check in the next segment disclosures. Separating hog price effects from volume effects is the starting point for reading this cycle.

Outlook

The Q1 2026 operating profit of KRW 61.4 billion was the peak, and the two consecutive quarterly declines since then are the most important point to track now.

Q3 2026 sales of KRW 352.7 billion fell from the prior quarter, but controlling-shareholder net profit of KRW 14.3 billion rose from a year earlier, so whether operating profit recovers in Q4 is the key question.

The Q4 2026 and full-year 2026 preliminary results are typically announced around January and February 2027, and that release will show whether the annual operating margin holds near the 2025 level of 9.5%.

The 2026 annual business report is due by the end of March 2027, when full-year controlling-shareholder net profit and segment sales will be confirmed on DART. The company runs six logistics centers nationwide and is reported to be developing new markets in line with demand for safer food.

For the livestock segment, hog prices and feed raw material costs are the main issues, and hog price trends in Vietnam are cited alongside Korea as earnings variables. First-half 2026 sales rose 16.9% while net profit fell 36.7%, so the operating-level and net-level trends need to be read together. Whether demand for processed meat products holds will show up in segment sales.

08

Bull factors

Improved earnings profile

The operating margin rose from 3.8% in 2022 to 9.5% in 2025, and operating profit increased for three consecutive years from 2023 to 2025.

Operating profit of KRW 179.2 billion in 2025 was the largest of any year from 2022 to 2025, and the growth rate of operating profit (44.4%) exceeded the growth rate of revenue (12.7%). According to some tallies, operating profit in the first half of 2026 also grew 4.3% year over year.

Lower financial burden

Behind the lower debt burden is a larger equity base. Operating cash flow in 2025 was KRW 129.0 billion, up sharply from KRW 3.92 billion in 2022. As its cash-generating capacity has grown, the financial structure has shifted toward a more comfortable position than before.

Vertical integration and demand base

The feed and livestock segment accounts for about 78% of revenue, and it is assessed to have secured cost competitiveness through vertical integration. Explanations say that rising meat demand and consumer trust in domestic pork support demand. Six logistics centers nationwide form the foundation of the sales system.

Bear factors

Rapid quarterly profit slowdown

Operating profit fell for two consecutive quarters, from KRW 61.4 billion in Q1 2026 to KRW 43.4 billion in Q2 and KRW 8.2 billion in Q3. Q3 revenue of KRW 352.7 billion was down 34.8% from the previous quarter, and operating profit was down 78.6% year over year. The trend of rising annual profit appears to have weakened on a quarterly basis.

Volatile net profit

Net profit attributable to controlling shareholders was KRW 13.9 billion in 2023, down 40.0% from the previous year, and net income fell as low as KRW 6.64 billion in 2024. According to some tallies, net income in the first half of 2026 fell 36.7% year over year.

There were years in which net profit did not rise along with operating profit, so fluctuations at each level of profit need to be viewed together.

Concentration and raw material exposure

The feed and livestock segment accounts for about 78% of revenue, so the company has large exposure to fluctuations in hog prices and grain prices. In 2024, revenue fell 11.8% from the previous year, so the top line contracted within a single year. Hog price movements at the Vietnam operations are also cited as a variable for results.

10

Risk factors

Livestock disease and hog price swings

A drop in hog prices or an outbreak of disease is a variable that can shake both herd size and selling prices at the same time. When hog prices fall, revenue and profit in the hog farming segment can decline, and feed demand can fall along with them. If raw material grain prices rise, the cost burden grows.

The domestic and Vietnam operations may show different hog price trends, so regional fluctuations could also overlap.

Non-operating items and derivatives

In the past, derivative gains and losses related to exchange rates have been pointed to as the cause of net income fluctuating far more than operating profit. Because the structure allows net profit to decline even when operating profit improves, each level of profit needs to be checked.

In Q3 2026, net profit attributable to controlling shareholders rose from a year earlier while operating profit fell 78.6%, so it is a point where one must confirm which items moved.

Quarterly earnings volatility

Quarterly revenue and operating profit swing widely, so it is too early to judge the trend from the movement of just one or two quarters. For 2024, data on net profit attributable to controlling shareholders is missing from the disclosed results, leaving a gap in year-by-year profit comparisons.

The sum of net profit attributable to controlling shareholders over the last four quarters, KRW 86.2 billion, differs from the annual figure, so results vary depending on the period basis.

Overall view

Sunjin's operating profit grew from KRW 70.6 billion in 2022 to KRW 179.2 billion in 2025, and the operating margin improved from 3.8% to 9.5%. The debt ratio at the end of 2025 was 133.9%, below the 202.7% level of 2022.

In 2026, however, operating profit peaked at KRW 61.4 billion in Q1 and then slowed to KRW 43.4 billion in Q2 and KRW 8.2 billion in Q3, while Q3 sales fell 34.8% from the prior quarter.

Controlling-shareholder net profit in the same Q3 rose from a year earlier, so it moved in the opposite direction to operating profit. Strengths include the improved earnings profile, lower financial burden and cost competitiveness from vertical integration.

Weaknesses include the quarterly slowdown, net profit volatility and exposure to the hog price cycle. With feed and livestock making up about 78% of sales, hog prices and raw material costs remain the core variables.

The next checkpoints are the Q4 2026 and full-year preliminary results and the 2026 annual business report, where operating margin and segment sales will show the trend.
