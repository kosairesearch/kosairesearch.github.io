02

Business structure

RootLoc, founded in 2014, is a specialist manufacturer of implantable medical devices, centered on artificial knee joints, spinal implants and medical consumables. It listed on KONEX on December 24, 2020, and market data sites classify it as an other specialized wholesale company.

The consumer and retail sector label therefore reflects that wholesale classification, while the actual business is orthopedic medical devices.

Its knee implant won FDA approval in September 2018, reportedly the second among domestic companies, and is supplied under the CentLoc brand to universities and orthopedic specialty hospitals. Products are tailored to Korean body types and are made domestically through every process step.

Based on 2025 data compiled by JobKorea, medical consumables account for 60% of revenue, artificial joints for 23% and spinal fixation devices for 16%. The company has completed development of antibacterial bone cement, while hip implants and spinal fixation devices remain under development.

L&K Biomed, a spinal implant specialist that led the founding investment, is the largest shareholder and held 39.44% at the time of the 2020 listing. An August 2025 press report put that stake at 36.55%.

The company has set a goal of roughly 10% market share in each of the artificial joint and spinal fixation device markets.

03

Recent trends

Revenue in 2025 was KRW 9.68 billion, down 3.24% from KRW 10.01 billion in 2024. Gross profit fell to about KRW 1.89 billion from about KRW 2.82 billion. The operating loss widened to about KRW 1.71 billion from about KRW 1.45 billion in 2024, for an operating margin of -17.65%. The net loss was about KRW 2.39 billion.

In 2024, revenue of KRW 10.0 billion rose and the operating loss of KRW 1.5 billion narrowed, yet year-end equity of negative KRW 1.1 billion meant full capital impairment.

For the first half of 2025, RootLoc reported revenue of KRW 4.73 billion and an operating loss of KRW 714 million, with equity of negative KRW 2.86 billion and total liabilities of KRW 19.65 billion. Year-end 2025 equity and first-half 2026 results could not be confirmed in this research.

The shares traded at KRW 1,360 on March 31, 2026, and at KRW 637 on October 13, 2026, and closed at KRW 570 on October 27. As of November 2025, the 52-week range was KRW 810 to KRW 1,950, and three-month average volume was around 586 shares.

Reported trading value on October 27 was KRW 570, which suggests the 14.00% daily move came on extremely few executions.

04

Outlook

The company points to rising demand for procedures driven by degenerative arthritis in an aging population as a backdrop for revenue growth. In its regulatory filings, the company stated a direction of replacing imported products and developing items suited to Asian body types.

Hip implants and spinal fixation devices are still in development, so a broader lineup could change the scope of its hospital sales. The company framed its strategy around cooperating with the largest shareholder's overseas sales network.

On operating metrics, the margin improved from -66.27% in 2022 to -14.51% in 2024, but slipped back to -17.65% in 2025. Recovery in gross margin is therefore the key gauge of how much room operating profitability has to improve. In June 2024, RootLoc raised KRW 5.17 billion from L&K Biomed and other investors.

No later funding plan was confirmed. A July 31, 2026 report said L&K Biomed carries a receivables burden from overseas subsidiaries and negative operating cash flow. The financial capacity of the controlling shareholder is therefore not unrelated to RootLoc's future funding.

05

Bull factors

FDA approval and a domestic product base

The knee implant received FDA approval in September 2018 and is supplied to domestic universities and orthopedic specialty hospitals. Domestic production of products tailored to Korean body types supports the import-substitution argument. Having regulatory approvals already in hand is a relatively rare asset for a small KONEX company.

Aging demand and lineup expansion

An aging population is increasing degenerative arthritis cases and expanding demand for procedures. The company has completed antibacterial bone cement and is developing hip implants and spinal fixation devices to build a full surgical lineup. A completed lineup would widen the range of items sold to each hospital.

Revenue scale-up and a record of narrowing loss ratios

Revenue more than doubled from KRW 4.34 billion in 2022 to KRW 9.68 billion in 2025. Over the same period the operating margin rose from -66.27% in 2022 to -14.51% in 2024. Fixed-cost burden eased as scale grew, which supports a reading that room remains for improvement in operating metrics.

06

Bear factors

Persistent losses and capital impairment

RootLoc again posted an operating loss of about KRW 1.71 billion and a net loss of about KRW 2.39 billion in 2025. Equity at the end of 2024 was negative KRW 1.1 billion, meaning full capital impairment. By the first half of 2025, equity had fallen further to negative KRW 2.86 billion.

Low share of own-made joints and stalled revenue

In 2025, medical consumables made up 60% of revenue, with artificial joints at 23% and spinal fixation devices at 16%. Revenue fell 3.24% in 2025, which halted the growth trend. Gross profit also declined from about KRW 2.82 billion to about KRW 1.89 billion, which weakened the earnings structure.

Thin trading and large price swings

As of November 2025, three-month average volume was around 586 shares. Reported trading value on October 27 was KRW 570, and the share price rose 14.00% in a single day. On March 31, 2026, it also rose 14.29% to KRW 1,360, but it now stands at KRW 570, which shows that prices can move sharply on very few trades.

07

Risk factors

Macro

Interest-rate and exchange-rate moves can affect funding costs and the burden of imported inputs. With liabilities above assets, a tougher funding environment could weigh more heavily on RootLoc. Hospital surgical volumes and the healthcare financing environment also act indirectly on consumables and implant sales.

Industry and Regulatory

Hip implants and spinal fixation devices are still at the development stage. Launch timing for medical devices can shift with approval and clinical documentation requirements.

The orthopedic implant market has incumbent imported products and domestic rivals competing for hospital accounts, so new products may take time to gain traction.

Financial and Ownership

As of the first half of 2025, equity was negative KRW 2.86 billion and total liabilities were KRW 19.65 billion. L&K Biomed holds receivables from RootLoc of KRW 3.07 billion. L&K Biomed itself has drawn scrutiny over the full capital impairment of four overseas subsidiaries and negative operating cash flow. Any capital increase or debt restructuring could dilute existing shareholders.

08

Overall view

RootLoc is a small orthopedic device company with an FDA-approved knee implant and a domestic production setup. Demand from an aging population and the import-substitution argument are cited as business strengths.

On the other side, losses continued in 2025, and capital impairment and heavy liabilities are the main burdens on the financial structure. The decline in gross profit suggests that profitability of the consumables line, which carries the largest revenue share, also needs to be checked.

The relationship with the largest shareholder, L&K Biomed, is both a basis for collaboration and a variable for funding. Very thin trading on KONEX also means price volatility is large.
