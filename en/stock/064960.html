Report overview

SNT Motiv: chip equipment partly offsets defense slump

SNT Motiv's third-quarter 2026 revenue edged up year on year, but net income attributable to controlling shareholders fell 85.2%, as chip-equipment growth and delayed defense deliveries pulled in opposite directions.

  1. 1

    Third-quarter 2026 revenue was KRW 234.3 billion, up 1.4% year on year, while operating profit was KRW 21.3 billion, down 3.3%, so the operating-level change was modest.

  2. 2

    Net income attributable to controlling shareholders was KRW 3.14 billion, down 85.2%. The pre-tax profit decline of 86.3% was far larger than the operating profit decline, pointing to a bigger role for non-operating items.

  3. 3

    Cumulative chip-equipment component revenue reached KRW 79.3 billion through the third quarter of 2026, above the full-year 2025 total of KRW 76.1 billion, according to a Hana Securities report.

  4. 4

    Defense revenue fell by roughly 50% year on year in the third quarter of 2026, with delayed export deliveries linked to the Middle East conflict cited as the main cause.

  5. 5

    Operating margin ran at 10.7% in 2022, 10.3% in 2023, 10.1% in 2024 and 10.2% in 2025, while the debt-to-equity ratio stood at 25.5% at the end of 2025.

02

Business structure

SNT Motiv divides its business into three areas: automotive parts, defense and chip-equipment components. The company's official site describes its mobility, defense and semiconductor equipment parts businesses.

Press coverage describes the auto parts business as spanning six product lines, including motors, engine and powertrain parts, electronics, chassis components and airbags.

In the 2022 annual report, motor products accounted for about 40.5% of total revenue and engine and powertrain parts for about 26.3%, so the two together made up more than half. Eco-friendly vehicle motors are supplied mainly to Hyundai Motor and KIA, while GM was the largest customer for powertrain parts.

However, GM's small electric vehicle, the Bolt EV, was discontinued in November 2024, and GM's revenue share fell. A Korean industry outlet reported in November 2024 that GM's share had dropped by about 9 percentage points to 25.5% at the end of the third quarter of 2024.

The other segment includes chip-equipment components and defense products such as K2-series rifles. Chip-equipment revenue reached KRW 79.3 billion in cumulative 2026 third-quarter sales, lifting its share of total revenue from 7.6% for full-year 2025 to 11.2% on a nine-month basis.

A brokerage analysis said defense exports of rifles to the Middle East began in the second half of 2025. On the auto parts side, a 2025 brokerage analysis noted that Hyundai Mobis is focusing on EV drive motors, which could shift the supply of hybrid drive motors to other suppliers.

04

Earnings analysis

Annual revenue rose from KRW 1.04 trillion in 2022 to KRW 1.14 trillion in 2023, an increase of 8.7%, then fell 14.7% to KRW 968.9 billion in 2024 before recovering 3.9% to KRW 1.01 trillion in 2025. The 2024 decline was reported to reflect the lack of replacement volume after the Bolt EV was discontinued.

Operating profit went from KRW 111.9 billion in 2022 to KRW 116.6 billion in 2023 (up 4.2%), KRW 98.1 billion in 2024 (down 15.9%) and KRW 102.6 billion in 2025 (up 4.5%). Operating margin stayed in a narrow band, from 10.7% in 2022 to 10.3% in 2023, 10.1% in 2024 and 10.2% in 2025.

Margins held in the low double digits even as revenue moved up and down, which is a notable feature of the earnings structure.

Net income attributable to controlling shareholders, by contrast, was KRW 87.5 billion in 2022, KRW 87 billion in 2023 (down 0.6%), KRW 104.3 billion in 2024 (up 20.0%) and KRW 69.6 billion in 2025 (down 33.3%).

In 2025 operating profit rose 4.5% while attributable net income fell 33.3%, suggesting that items outside operating profit drove the earnings trend. Operating cash flow was KRW 108.5 billion in 2022, KRW 96.5 billion in 2023, KRW 130.1 billion in 2024 and KRW 73.8 billion in 2025.

On a quarterly basis, revenue reached KRW 291.6 billion in the fourth quarter of 2025, up 26.2% from the prior quarter, before falling 26.1% to KRW 215.6 billion in the first quarter of 2026, rising 19.7% to KRW 258 billion in the second quarter and falling 9.2% to KRW 234.3 billion in the third quarter.

Operating profit was KRW 21.5 billion in the first quarter of 2026, KRW 25.8 billion in the second quarter and KRW 21.3 billion in the third quarter, and the third-quarter operating margin was 9.1%.

Net income attributable to controlling shareholders peaked at KRW 24.9 billion in the first quarter of 2026, then declined for two consecutive quarters to KRW 23.8 billion in the second quarter and KRW 3.14 billion in the third.

In the third quarter defense revenue fell by about 50%, while chip-equipment revenue rose 68%, partly offsetting the defense weakness according to Hana Securities. Attributable net income for the last four quarters, from the fourth quarter of 2025 through the third quarter of 2026, totaled KRW 64.7 billion.

05

Industry analysis

The end market for auto parts is shifting from electric vehicles toward hybrids. A 2024 report cited the company's explanation that weaker EV demand had also reduced revenue from Hyundai Motor and KIA.

By contrast, a 2025 brokerage analysis pointed to SNT Motiv's exclusive supply of hybrid starter motors to the Hyundai Motor Group and treated that hybrid volume as a key pillar.

In EV drive motors, Hyundai Mobis's focus on electric drive motors raised the possibility that hybrid drive motor supply could move to other suppliers. Whether the company wins drive-motor orders is seen as an important variable. Defense is sensitive to geopolitical risk, particularly the Middle East conflict.

Delayed export deliveries were cited as the reason for the third-quarter decline in defense revenue, and Hana Securities said the delivery delay had not yet ended. Chip-equipment components depend on semiconductor capital spending, and this business has grown at an average of 14% a year over ten years.

Quarterly growth accelerated from 12% in the first quarter of 2026 to 63% in the second quarter and 68% in the third. In the cycle, auto parts are going through volume swings during the electrification transition, while chip equipment and defense are partly offsetting that trend.

Outlook

In a report in early November 2026, Hana Securities projected 2026 revenue of KRW 988 billion and operating profit of KRW 95.4 billion, down 1.8% and 7.0% from the prior year respectively. These are brokerage estimates, not company guidance.

The same report expected chip-equipment revenue to rise about 40% to KRW 106.5 billion in 2026, above KRW 100 billion for the year. If that estimate holds, chip equipment would become a more significant pillar of annual earnings.

For defense, whether export deliveries resume is the key checkpoint, and the same report said the delivery delay had not yet ended. A research report from early 2026 cited hybrid starter motor and oil pump supply, along with Middle East defense sales, as growth drivers for 2026.

In EV drive motors, a company official said in 2024 that automakers might expand outsourcing and that the company was worth pursuing additional orders. Any new drive-motor orders will need to be confirmed through future disclosures. The company has no official annual guidance figure, so the outlook centers on brokerage estimates.

Bull factors

Chip-equipment growth continues

Cumulative chip-equipment revenue reached KRW 79.3 billion through the third quarter of 2026, already above the full-year 2025 total of KRW 76.1 billion. Quarterly growth rose from 12% in the first quarter to 63% in the second and 68% in the third.

Hana Securities projected 2026 chip-equipment revenue of KRW 106.5 billion. The bull case is that this business is becoming a pillar that partly offsets the defense weakness.

Room for defense deliveries to normalize

Defense revenue fell about 50% year on year in the third quarter of 2026, with the decline attributed to delayed export deliveries linked to the Middle East conflict. The bull case is that a resumption of deliveries could allow defense revenue to rebound.

Middle East rifle exports, which began in the second half of 2025, were also cited as a growth driver in an early 2026 report. Hana Securities noted, however, that the delivery delay had not yet ended.

Financial capacity and margin resilience

At the end of 2025 the debt-to-equity ratio was 25.5%, with total liabilities of KRW 260 billion and total equity of KRW 1.02 trillion. Operating margin held in the low double digits, moving from 10.7% in 2022 to 10.2% in 2025. The bull case is that this balance sheet leaves room for capital spending and for responding to new orders.

Bear factors

Continued decline in attributable profit

The third-quarter figure was down 85.2% year on year. Operating profit fell only 3.3%, but pre-tax profit fell 86.3%, which points to volatility outside operating results as a bearish factor. Attributable net income had already dropped 33.3% in 2025.

Operating profit below expectations

Hana Securities said third-quarter 2026 operating profit missed market expectations by 13%. Operating margin fell 0.4 percentage points to 9.1%. The roughly 50% drop in defense revenue was cited as the main drag. The bear case is that chip-equipment growth alone has not fully filled the defense gap.

Customer concentration and EV demand

After GM discontinued the Bolt EV in November 2024, GM's share of revenue declined, and the company said weaker EV demand also reduced sales to Hyundai Motor and KIA. With no replacement volume secured at the time, new drive-motor orders remained the key to a recovery. The structure ties earnings directly to changes in customers' industries.

Risk factors

Customer and electrification exposure

If EV demand weakens in a revenue base centered on Hyundai Motor and KIA, volumes could fall. The discontinuation of the GM Bolt EV led to a smaller top line, so new drive-motor orders are needed to fill the gap. Changes in automakers' electrification strategies could alter the timing and scale of orders.

Because customer concentration is high, a change in one customer's volumes shows up strongly in quarterly results.

Defense exports and geopolitics

Delayed export deliveries tied to the Middle East conflict cut third-quarter 2026 defense revenue by about 50%. If the delay persists, earnings volatility in quarters with a larger defense share will rise. Hana Securities noted that the delivery delay had not yet ended when it looked at the 2026 outlook. Defense results can swing sharply from quarter to quarter depending on international conditions.

Earnings volatility and non-operating items

Third-quarter 2026 pre-tax profit was KRW 3.94 billion, down 86.3% year on year, a far steeper drop than operating profit. When operating profit and attributable net income move in opposite directions, as in the fourth quarter of 2025 and the first quarter of 2026, earnings forecasts become less reliable. Interpreting results requires looking at items beyond operating profit as well.

Overall view

SNT Motiv's third-quarter 2026 revenue rose 1.4% year on year to KRW 234.3 billion, and operating profit of KRW 21.3 billion did not fall sharply. Attributable net income, however, dropped 85.2% to KRW 3.14 billion. The earnings picture can be summarized as chip-equipment growth offsetting delayed defense exports.

Cumulative chip-equipment revenue reached KRW 79.3 billion through the third quarter of 2026, already above full-year 2025, and quarterly growth has been rising. Defense revenue, however, fell about 50% in the third quarter on Middle East-related delivery delays, and Hana Securities said the delay had not yet ended.

Annual operating margin ran between 10.1% and 10.7% from 2022 through 2025, so the margin base appears intact. Still, attributable net income fell 33.3% in 2025 and has declined for two consecutive quarters in 2026, so the direction of earnings is not yet settled.

On the balance sheet, the debt-to-equity ratio was 25.5% at the end of 2025, and the current market capitalization is below attributable equity. The checkpoints are whether defense deliveries resume in the fourth quarter of 2026, full-year results in early 2027, and the 2026 annual report due by the end of March 2027.
