02

Business structure

Sunic System is a specialist maker of OLED deposition equipment, with three business lines: large-area IT OLED deposition tools, OLEDoS (micro OLED) deposition tools, and perovskite solar cell deposition tools.

In large OLED, Gen 8.6 FMM (fine metal mask) tools are the core product, and the company said at a September 2026 investor event that it had won four of the six Gen 8.6 FMM lines worldwide.

In OLEDoS, the company disclosed supply contracts in January 2026 with Jinan Goerpixel worth about KRW 41 billion and in February 2026 with Anhui Hongxi Weixian Technology worth about KRW 20.6 billion, both for mass-production deposition tools.

In March 2026 it signed a further order with SeeYA Display, but the contract amount was withheld from disclosure under confidentiality terms. Its customers are centered on Chinese display makers, and reports describe OLEDoS investment as concentrated in the Chinese market.

In perovskite, the company said it won a Gen 8 pilot line, the first of its kind globally, and completed the establishment of a U.S. local subsidiary. Revenue is recognized after design and manufacturing, shipment, and installation and inspection, so quarterly results vary with project timing.

Its production base includes a new Pyeongtaek Brain City plant, and when Buildings A and B are complete, floor space is set to rise from 15,850 square meters to 28,850 square meters, about 1.8 times.

In competition, the Gen 8.6 tool market had been led by a Japanese rival, and press coverage framed this award as breaking that dominance.

04

Earnings analysis

On the basis of regular filings, annual results recovered clearly from a trough in 2023.

Revenue went from KRW 74.1 billion in 2022 and KRW 62.4 billion in 2023 (down 15.7% year on year) to KRW 112.9 billion in 2024 (up 80.9%) and KRW 515.8 billion in 2025 (up 356.7%), making 2025 the largest year of the 2022 to 2025 period.

Operating profit moved from KRW 4.43 billion in 2022 to an operating loss of KRW 4.06 billion in 2023, which was a swing into loss, then to KRW 7.91 billion in 2024, a swing back into profit, and KRW 111.5 billion in 2025 (up 1,310.5%). The operating margin rose from -6.5% in 2023 to 7.0% in 2024 and 21.6% in 2025.

Net loss attributable to controlling shareholders widened to KRW 9.23 billion in 2023 and KRW 28.1 billion in 2024, then turned to a net profit of KRW 96.5 billion in 2025. Net income including non-controlling interests was also KRW 96.5 billion in 2025.

The 2025 results were concentrated in the fourth quarter, when revenue reached KRW 221 billion (up 149.0% from the prior quarter) and operating profit reached KRW 53.6 billion (up 170.6%).

In the first quarter of 2026, revenue fell to KRW 16.1 billion (down 92.7% from the prior quarter), operating profit to KRW 1.47 billion (down 97.2%), and the net loss attributable to controlling shareholders was KRW 19.6 billion, a swing into loss.

In the second quarter, revenue rose 25.7% sequentially to KRW 20.2 billion, but the company posted an operating loss of KRW 10.1 billion, a swing into loss, while the controlling-shareholder net loss narrowed to KRW 7.95 billion.

Third-quarter revenue recovered to KRW 69.7 billion (up 244.5% from the prior quarter) but was down 21.4% year on year, with operating profit of KRW 9.26 billion (down 53.2% year on year) and a net profit attributable to controlling shareholders of KRW 6.62 billion, a swing back into profit.

For the first half of 2026, the company's preliminary revenue of KRW 36.3 billion and operating loss of KRW 8.7 billion (announced August 18, 2026) were attributed by the company to delayed revenue recognition tied to equipment delivery and to warranty provisions and bad-debt allowances. Net profit attributable to controlling shareholders over the last four quarters totals KRW 13.1 billion.

05

Industry analysis

Demand for display equipment splits into three streams. In August 2026 the company said AI expansion from data centers into AI PCs and smart glasses should lift demand for deposition tools for IT OLED and for smart-glass OLEDoS.

OLEDoS, which deposits organic material on silicon wafers to achieve very high resolution, is described as a core component of next-generation AR and XR devices. Industry participants expect production equipment investment in OLEDoS to rise as smart glasses and XR devices move toward commercialization.

On the cycle, large OLED is in a phase of Gen 8.6 line investment, and the company has won four of six Gen 8.6 lines. In competition, the company has entered a Gen 8.6 tool market that a Japanese rival had led, a development that drew press attention.

One report assessed that only a small number of companies worldwide can supply OLEDoS mass-production deposition tools.

On the other side, every OLEDoS contract counterparty disclosed in 2026 was a Chinese company, so results are highly sensitive to the investment pace of Chinese panel makers and the local policy environment.

Perovskite is a new market applying OLED deposition know-how, and DS Investment said in April 2026 that a follow-on mass-production tool order could come as early as the first quarter of 2027.

06

Outlook

In the company's own account, the main driver of second-half 2026 results is equipment shipments. In August 2026 the company said major shipments are concentrated in the second half and that backlog should convert to sales in sequence.

The backlog of KRW 642 billion at the end of June 2026 was up from KRW 582.3 billion at the end of the first quarter of 2026. Press reports said the first building of the new Pyeongtaek Brain City plant was completed in July 2026, with the second building scheduled for completion in October 2026.

Whether capacity from that second building shows up in results is a point to check in the next regular filing. On October 21, 2026 the company said it had won an additional OLEDoS deposition tool order from an overseas customer.

The company describes Chinese customers' mass-production investment plans in OLEDoS as becoming more concrete.

In June 2026 IBK Securities cut its target price to KRW 141,000 and said it expected second-quarter results to fall short of expectations, while also noting that earnings visibility after the second half was high. In perovskite, the next step after winning a Gen 8 pilot line is a mass-production tool order.

The company said it plans to secure additional business opportunities in large OLED, OLEDoS and perovskite. The next checkpoint is the quarterly revenue recognition scale and the trend in warranty-related costs disclosed in the 2026 annual report.

08

Bull factors

Backlog scale

The backlog at the end of June 2026 was KRW 642 billion, which the company said exceeds 2025 annual revenue of KRW 515.8 billion. IBK Securities assessed that the backlog of KRW 582.3 billion at the end of the first quarter of 2026 set a record high. If second-half shipments convert into results, the annual revenue base would widen.

Wider Gen 8.6 tool share

At a September 2026 investor event the company said it had won four of six Gen 8.6 FMM lines worldwide. Press coverage said the award broke a monopoly held by a Japanese rival. The company said it plans to secure further opportunities in large OLED.

Business diversification

In OLEDoS, the company disclosed mass-production tool contracts worth about KRW 41 billion in January 2026 and about KRW 20.6 billion in February, and announced further orders in March and October. The company said it is extending its applications beyond IT OLED into OLEDoS and perovskite.

09

Bear factors

Delayed revenue recognition

First-quarter 2026 revenue fell 92.7% from the prior quarter to KRW 16.1 billion, and the second quarter swung to an operating loss of KRW 10.1 billion. The preliminary first-half operating loss was KRW 8.7 billion. One outlet pointed to the slow pace at which backlog is converting into revenue.

Cost burden

The company said warranty provisions tied to quality guarantees and bad-debt allowances on receivables affected first-half 2026 results. Some commentary points to post-delivery warranty costs eroding profit as a risk specific to equipment makers. Such costs can move quarterly profit.

Shrinking earnings base

Third-quarter 2026 revenue of KRW 69.7 billion was down 21.4% year on year, and operating profit of KRW 9.26 billion was down 53.2%. The four-quarter total of net profit attributable to controlling shareholders is KRW 13.1 billion, a sharp drop from the KRW 96.5 billion recorded for 2025. Controlling-shareholder net losses were recorded in the first and second quarters of 2026.

10

Risk factors

Customer and regional concentration

Every OLEDoS contract counterparty disclosed in 2026 was a Chinese company, and Gen 8.6 orders are tied to investment by Chinese panel makers. If customers change order timing or equipment investment plans, delivery and revenue recognition slip together. Changes in Chinese policy and the local investment climate are also variables.

Warranty and bad-debt costs

Warranty provisions for post-delivery defects and bad-debt allowances on receivables pulled down first-half 2026 results. The company said the figures include non-cash accounting effects, but whether such costs recur should be checked in the notes to the annual report. Concentrated large deliveries in a single quarter would widen profit swings.

Timing of new-business commercialization

OLEDoS and perovskite depend on external schedules, such as commercialization of AR and XR devices and the move to mass production in energy applications. Perovskite has a U.S. subsidiary in place, but the timing of mass-production tool orders is not yet set. If new-business revenue is delayed, dependence on large OLED will remain high.

12

Overall view

Sunic System moved out of a loss phase in 2022 and recorded annual revenue of KRW 515.8 billion and operating profit of KRW 111.5 billion in 2025. The backlog of KRW 642 billion at the end of June 2026 exceeds 2025 annual revenue, which the company and some brokers read as a broader medium-term order base.

On the other hand, quarterly revenue in 2026 has swung from KRW 16.1 billion to KRW 69.7 billion, and the preliminary first-half operating loss of KRW 8.7 billion reflects warranty and bad-debt costs.

Bullish factors are four of six Gen 8.6 lines, a series of OLEDoS contract disclosures, and a perovskite pilot-line award. Bearish factors are the slow conversion of backlog into revenue, concentration on Chinese customers, and third-quarter 2026 revenue down 21.4% year on year.

Financially, the debt ratio fell from 552.5% in 2024 to 154.2% in 2025, and total equity at the end of 2025 was KRW 143.8 billion.

The next checkpoints are the expiry of the SeeYA Display contract period on November 20, 2026, and the 2026 annual report due by the end of March 2027, which will show annual operating margin and provision levels.
