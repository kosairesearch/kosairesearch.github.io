02

Business structure

DongilTechnology, founded in March 1986, manufactures electronic components such as EMI filters and cluster ion generators and also runs an industrial electronics business.

Its main revenue comes from the electronic components segment, and the company is diversifying beyond single filter products by developing items such as anion clusters based on its ceramic materials technology.

Product mix data from WiseReport's company monitor as of December 2025 show that electronic component products were 63.16% domestic and 36.84% export, while industrial products were 81.36% domestic and 18.64% export. The same data classify electronic components sold as merchandise as 100% export.

These are sales mix ratios, not segment revenue amounts. EMI filters are essential parts of electronic products, and the company is viewed as having built a customer base on this technology and market position.

Overseas production is handled by its 100%-owned Chinese subsidiary in Weihai, Shandong, whose main business is electronic component assembly. At the end of 2025, consolidated total assets were KRW 68.6 billion, total equity KRW 64.4 billion, and total liabilities KRW 4.27 billion.

Peers in the electronic components sector include Samsung Electro-Mechanics, LG Innotek and Seojin System, but differences in product range and scale make direct comparison of results limited.

04

Earnings analysis

Annual results show sales declines and shrinking operating profit happening together. Revenue fell from KRW 26.7 billion in 2022 to KRW 25.2 billion in 2023 (down 5.5% year on year), KRW 24.1 billion in 2024 (down 4.5%) and KRW 21.6 billion in 2025 (down 10.6%), a three-year decline from 2023 through 2025.

Operating profit fell from KRW 1.1 billion in 2022 to KRW 423 million in 2023 (down 61.6%) and KRW 192 million in 2024 (down 54.7%), and turned into an operating loss of KRW 61.86 million in 2025. The operating margin moved from 4.1% in 2022 to 1.7% in 2023, 0.8% in 2024 and -0.3% in 2025.

The company disclosed that lower sales raised its cost of sales ratio and selling and administrative expense ratio, which reduced operating profit.

Net income attributable to controlling shareholders was KRW 1.12 billion in 2022, KRW 2.23 billion in 2023 (up 99.6%), KRW 776 million in 2024 (down 65.2%) and KRW 6.48 billion in 2025 (up 735.1%).

Total net income in 2025 of KRW 6.54 billion, including non-controlling interests, rose on mark-to-market gains from held financial assets, so the contribution of valuation gains was larger than any improvement in core operations. The first half of 2026 moved in a different direction.

Revenue of KRW 12.5 billion rose 14% from KRW 11.0 billion a year earlier, and operating profit of KRW 500 million turned positive from a year-earlier operating loss, according to an IB Tomato report from August 2026.

On a quarterly basis, revenue bottomed at KRW 5.24 billion in the fourth quarter of 2025 and then rose to KRW 6.15 billion in the first quarter of 2026, KRW 6.38 billion in the second and KRW 6.9 billion in the third, three straight quarterly gains; third-quarter revenue was up 28.8% year on year.

Operating profit alternated between profit and loss, at KRW 301 million in the third quarter of 2025, an operating loss of KRW 155 million in the fourth, KRW 589 million in the first quarter of 2026, an operating loss of KRW 94.79 million in the second and KRW 1.05 billion in the third.

Net income attributable to controlling shareholders rose to KRW 2.66 billion in the fourth quarter of 2025, KRW 3.52 billion in the first quarter of 2026 and KRW 2.88 billion in the second, then swung to a net loss of KRW 246 million in the third.

Over the last four quarters, net income attributable to controlling shareholders totaled KRW 8.81 billion. A quarter with operating profit but a net loss to controlling shareholders points to a large share of non-operating items, especially mark-to-market swings in financial assets. Cost burden is cited as the main constraint on profitability by WiseReport's company monitor.

05

Industry analysis

EMI filters are essential components in electronic products, which gives the category a broad demand base. WiseReport's company monitor says the company has built technology and market strength in this field, enabling it to win a diversified customer base and grow sales.

The same source notes that cost burden limits profitability. On the demand side, factory automation is the main area cited, with FnGuide's company summary expecting higher demand for factory-automation static eliminators, vision cameras and ultrasonic sensors.

Analysis also points to growth in the medical device market, supported by aging populations and rising interest in cosmetic procedures. The cycle position can be gauged from the sales decline from 2023 through 2025, which FnGuide attributed to weaker demand for electronic components and industrial products.

The three straight quarterly revenue gains in 2026 can be read as a recovery after that demand trough, though the size of the rebound is confined to the quarterly figures reported so far.

Compared with larger component makers such as Samsung Electro-Mechanics and LG Innotek, the product range and customer mix differ and the scale gap is wide, so sector averages do not settle the company's position.

How far the ceramic-based diversification into anion clusters will reshape the revenue mix remains a question for which the available data are still limited.

Outlook

The company reported third-quarter 2026 revenue of KRW 6.9 billion, up 28.8% from a year earlier, so quarterly sales have kept rising for three straight quarters. Operating profit turned positive in the first half of 2026, and whether this carries through the full year is a point to check in the next periodic report.

On shareholder returns, cash dividend payments in the first half of 2026 reached KRW 1.67 billion, up from KRW 160 million a year earlier, and treasury share purchases were KRW 1.05 billion against KRW 11.28 million a year earlier, according to an IB Tomato report from August 2026.

The major shareholder has also been adding shares: a 10%-plus shareholder bought 9,938 shares on the market on November 5 and 6, 2026, bringing its holding to 10,892,491 shares, or 61.91% as of November 6, 2026, according to a DigitalToday report from November 2026.

On the business side, the company is pursuing diversification into ceramic-based products such as anion clusters, and analysts expect demand to grow for factory-automation static eliminators and vision cameras.

Because non-operating swings are large, the direction of net income attributable to controlling shareholders in the coming quarters may depend on mark-to-market changes in held financial assets.

In the 2026 annual report, the operating margin versus -0.3% in 2025 and whether core operating profit excluding valuation effects has stayed in the black are the points to check. How the quarterly trend through the third quarter of 2026 feeds into the full-year figures is also worth watching.

10

Risk factors

Swings in Mark-to-Market Gains

If the share prices of listed holdings fall, mark-to-market gains could shrink or turn into losses, causing net income to fluctuate. The third quarter of 2026 showed this structure, with operating profit positive but a net loss attributable to controlling shareholders. Because these are unrealized book gains, they can move in a different direction from core operating results.

Profitability and Cost Structure

Revenue centered on EMI filters carries cost pressure that limits profitability. In 2025, lower sales raised both the cost ratio and the selling and administrative expense ratio, and operating profit turned into a loss. If sales fall again, fixed cost burden could hit results quickly.

Ownership Concentration and Free Float

The 10%-plus shareholder holds 61.91%, a majority stake. This can limit the number of shares freely traded in the market. If on-market buying by major holders and treasury share purchases continue, the available float could narrow further, making supply and demand conditions a factor to track.

Overall view

DongilTechnology swung into an operating loss in 2025 and then recorded three straight quarters of revenue growth and operating profit through the third quarter of 2026.

However, the net loss attributable to controlling shareholders of KRW 246 million in the third quarter of 2026 shows that net income depends on both operating results and mark-to-market gains on financial assets.

The company attributed the 2025 rise in net income to valuation gains on financial assets, and an analysis said such gains made up 87% of first-half 2026 net income, so the durability of core operating profit has limited evidence so far.

Bullish factors are the quarterly revenue recovery, the return to operating profit, higher dividends and treasury share purchases, on-market buying by a 10%-plus shareholder, and a low debt burden.

Bearish factors are three years of falling sales, the 2025 operating loss, limited profitability from cost pressure, and reliance on valuation gains. The price-to-book ratio is below 1x, but that fact alone does not indicate the direction of the share price.

The next checkpoints are the operating margin and core operating profit excluding valuation effects in the 2026 annual report, and the first-quarter 2027 quarterly report.
