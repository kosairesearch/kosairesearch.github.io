Four years of losses; new businesses and funding are the test

02

Business structure

Davolink, founded in 2000, is a communications equipment maker that listed on KOSDAQ in 2021 through a merger with a special purpose acquisition company. Its core products are Wi-Fi-based wireless access points (APs) and gateways.

A press report based on regulatory filings said APs accounted for KRW 48.5 billion of KRW 49.5 billion in nine-month consolidated revenue through the third quarter of 2025. The same report said AP revenue fell from KRW 70.2 billion in 2023 to KRW 58.5 billion in 2024.

Its customers are mainly telecom operators, for which it has supplied network equipment as a first-tier vendor to KT and LG Uplus. The company plans to extend its high-performance wireless AP and wired-wireless integration technology into AI data centers, edge computing, smart factories and logistics automation.

In security, its Axio VPN and Axio Server products have completed public procurement registration, and it is preparing sales to central government agencies, local governments and educational institutions.

In robotics, it was named a domestic exclusive distributor and robot systems integration partner for Dobot, a collaborative robot maker, and has built a humanoid showroom in Gangnam, Seoul.

In March 2026 it said it would add humanoid, industrial robot and drone and anti-drone businesses to its articles of incorporation. A January 2026 report said most businesses added through earlier shareholder meetings were not yet operating. On competition, the company attributed a slight 2025 revenue decline to new competitors entering the market.

04

Earnings analysis

Annual revenue rose from KRW 64.9 billion in 2022 to KRW 75.4 billion in 2023, a 16.2% increase that marked the peak, then fell 12.5% to KRW 65.9 billion in 2024 and 4.1% to KRW 63.2 billion in 2025, two consecutive years of decline.

Operating losses were KRW 2.94 billion in 2022, KRW 908 million in 2023, KRW 1.65 billion in 2024 and KRW 1.9 billion in 2025, four consecutive years of losses. The operating margin was -4.5% in 2022, -1.2% in 2023, -2.5% in 2024 and -3.0% in 2025, so the loss rate widened again after its 2023 low.

Net losses attributable to controlling shareholders were KRW 6.14 billion, KRW 1.78 billion, KRW 5.64 billion and KRW 5.01 billion for 2022 through 2025, and the net loss including non-controlling interests was reported at the same amounts.

Operating cash flow was negative in all four years, at -KRW 1.66 billion, -KRW 2.91 billion, -KRW 847 million and -KRW 1.84 billion.

Quarterly results show revenue of KRW 20.7 billion and an operating profit of KRW 1.19 billion in the third quarter of 2025, followed by a fourth-quarter operating loss of KRW 1.28 billion on revenue of KRW 13.6 billion, a 34.0% quarter-on-quarter decline.

Revenue was KRW 12.6 billion in the first quarter of 2026 and KRW 16.7 billion in the second quarter, up 32.4% from the prior quarter, before falling to KRW 15.8 billion in the third quarter, down 23.7% from a year earlier, when the operating loss widened to KRW 1.36 billion.

Losses attributable to controlling shareholders were KRW 4.37 billion in the fourth quarter of 2025, KRW 4.2 billion in the first quarter of 2026, KRW 292 million in the second quarter and KRW 2.4 billion in the third quarter.

The gap between operating losses and controlling-shareholder net losses in the fourth quarter of 2025 and the first quarter of 2026 is a period that requires reviewing non-operating items.

The debt-to-equity ratio rose from 99.8% in 2022 to 168.4% in 2025, with total equity of KRW 15.2 billion and total liabilities of KRW 25.5 billion at the end of 2025.

A press report based on filings put the consolidated ratio at 275.4% at the end of the third quarter of 2025, and convertible bond balances rose from KRW 700 million at the end of 2024 to KRW 8.4 billion at the end of that quarter.

05

Industry analysis

Davolink's core business is tied to the network investment cycle of telecom operators. Because it has supplied network equipment as a first-tier vendor to KT and LG Uplus, its revenue moves with the equipment order flow of its main customers.

The company attributed a slight 2025 revenue decline to new competitors entering the market.

Cost structure is also a concern: for the first nine months of 2025, cost of sales was KRW 42.2 billion, gross profit was KRW 7.4 billion and selling and administrative expenses were KRW 8.0 billion, and reports describe fixed costs such as salaries, R&D and commissions as making it hard to retain profit.

On the demand side, the company cites low-latency, highly reliable networks that connect many devices in AI data centers, edge computing, smart factories and logistics automation, although this is the direction the company itself has presented.

Internationally, it announced ORION certification for open RAN-based equipment in a March 2026 report.

Robotics, drones and defense are capital-intensive, and investment industry commentary cited in press reports holds that a communications equipment maker needs considerable time and funds to establish itself in these fields.

06

Outlook

The core plans the company has described in press coverage fall into three areas: AI networking, public security and robotics systems integration. The direction presented is to extend its AP technology into AI data centers, edge computing, smart factories and logistics automation.

In security, Axio VPN and Axio Server have completed public procurement registration, and the company plans sales, proof-of-concept projects and technology seminars aimed at central government agencies, local governments and educational institutions.

In robotics, a Korean subsidiary of Dobot is being set up, and technology verification and field trials are under way at a humanoid showroom in Gangnam, Seoul. The goal is to supply an integrated robot SI solution that connects and controls multiple AI robots in factories, logistics centers and smart buildings.

Press commentary holds that these new businesses, still at the briefing and demonstration stage, must convert into orders and revenue before their growth strategy shows results.

On funding, a third-party allotment rights issue of KRW 999,998,000 was decided in January 2026, and according to an August 2026 report the payment date for an KRW 8.0 billion third-party allotment was postponed several times.

A change in the largest shareholder, agreed through a share purchase contract disclosed in December 2024, was reported to have been delayed for more than a year.

A public offering rights issue decided on October 1, 2026 was withdrawn, with a correction filed on October 12, and the Korea Exchange gave notice of a possible designation as an unfaithful disclosure company, with a designation decision due by December 4, 2026.

08

Bull factors

Public-security procurement base

Axio VPN and Axio Server completed public procurement registration, creating a sales channel to central government agencies, local governments and educational institutions. The company plans to build public-sector supply records through this channel.

Security equipment is cited as technically adjacent to its existing AP business. Actual revenue scale has not appeared in disclosures, so whether orders follow is the key question.

Room to extend into AI networking

The company plans to extend its high-performance wireless AP and wired-wireless integration technology into AI data centers, edge computing, smart factories and logistics automation.

According to a March 2026 report, it obtained ORION certification for open RAN-based equipment, which provides a technical basis for pursuing global telecom markets. Some interpret its experience as a first-tier telecom vendor as lowering barriers to new markets. This direction, however, rests on the company's own explanation.

Quarterly loss swings

Second-quarter 2026 revenue was KRW 16.7 billion, up 32.4% from the prior quarter, and the operating loss narrowed to KRW 932 million from KRW 1.01 billion in the first quarter.

Net losses attributable to controlling shareholders also shrank from KRW 4.2 billion in the first quarter of 2026 to KRW 292 million in the second. The pattern shows that core revenue can recover quarter to quarter.

However, the operating loss widened again to KRW 1.36 billion in the third quarter, so quarterly volatility remains high.

Bear factors

Four straight years of losses and a shrinking top line

Operating losses have continued for four consecutive years from 2022 through 2025. Third-quarter 2026 revenue of KRW 15.8 billion fell 23.7% from a year earlier.

Losses have persisted in each quarter from the fourth quarter of 2025 through the third quarter of 2026, with controlling-shareholder net losses totaling KRW 11.3 billion over the last four quarters. Press assessments attribute this to fixed costs exceeding gross profit.

Rising debt and funding strain

The debt-to-equity ratio rose from 99.8% in 2022 to 168.4% in 2025, and a press report put the consolidated ratio at 275.4% at the end of the third quarter of 2025. Convertible bond balances increased from KRW 700 million at the end of 2024 to KRW 8.4 billion at the end of the third quarter of 2025.

According to an August 2026 report, the payment date for an KRW 8.0 billion rights issue was postponed several times. Cash and cash equivalents were KRW 1.8 billion at the end of the third quarter of 2025, below the level at the end of 2024.

Governance uncertainty and disclosure risk

The change in the largest shareholder, agreed in December 2024, was reported to have been delayed for more than a year, and the chief executive has changed several times.

An August 2026 filing showed the company's de facto controlling shareholder buying common shares on the open market, but on October 27 and 28, 2026 open-market sales followed and the reported stake fell from 8.55% to 6.67%.

The Korea Exchange gave notice of a possible unfaithful-disclosure designation after a rights issue decision was withdrawn in October 2026. Critics point out that new business purposes have been added repeatedly without clear revenue results.

Risk factors

Listing maintenance

Under KOSDAQ rules, a company whose closing price stays below KRW 1,000 for 30 consecutive trading days is designated as a managed issue, and failure to recover the price requirement afterward can lead to delisting.

The stock hit the 25-day threshold in July 2026 and was flagged as a potential managed issue before the streak broke, then fell below KRW 1,000 again in August. A designation decision on unfaithful disclosure adds to this pressure.

Funding and dilution

Press reports put the maturity of the third convertible bond batch at September 25, 2027 and the second at March 26, 2028. A third-party rights issue of KRW 999,998,000 was completed in early 2026, and the payment for a later KRW 8.0 billion issue was postponed.

With weak cash generation from operations, reliance on external funding could keep increasing the share count. The financing path involves a trade-off between financial burden and shareholder dilution.

New-business execution and erosion of the core

Core AP revenue fell from KRW 70.2 billion in 2023 to KRW 58.5 billion in 2024, and the company cited new competitors as a reason for the decline. Robotics, drones and defense are capital-intensive, and press assessments say results will take time and funding.

Spreading resources across new businesses could delay cost-ratio improvement in the core. The timing of revenue from new businesses remains uncertain.

Overall view

Davolink posted operating losses in each year from 2022 through 2025, and revenue fell from a peak of KRW 75.4 billion in 2023 to KRW 63.2 billion in 2025.

Losses have continued in each quarter from the fourth quarter of 2025 through the third quarter of 2026, and the operating loss widened to KRW 1.36 billion in the third quarter of 2026 from the prior quarter.

Supporting points cited on the bullish side include public-procurement registration for its security products, its robot systems integration partnership with Dobot, ORION certification, and the narrowing of losses in the second quarter of 2026 compared with the first.

On the bearish side, rising leverage, convertible bond growth, delayed rights-issue payments, a delayed change in the largest shareholder and a possible unfaithful-disclosure designation all appear at once. Industry commentary holds that capital-intensive new businesses will take time before results show up in revenue.

The points to confirm are therefore a recovery in core revenue, the direction of the operating margin, and the outcome of the funding and largest-shareholder changes. The next check point is how the full-year operating margin and debt ratio appear in the 2026 annual report. This report does not offer an investment opinion or a target price.
