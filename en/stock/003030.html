02

Business structure

SeAH Steel Holdings was founded in 1960 as Busan Cheolgwan Industrial and became a pure holding company in September 2018, when its pipe manufacturing and sales business was spun off into the new entity SeAH Steel.

A 2020 quarterly report showed that the holding company's income comes from dividends paid by subsidiaries and royalties for use of the SeAH brand.

SeAH Steel is the core consolidated subsidiary and the center of pipe production, while a steel sheet subsidiary and an intermediate holding company for overseas affiliates also sit under the group.

In 2024, SeAH Steel acquired a structural pipe distributor and a structural pipe maker, broadening its structural pipe lineup. Overseas, the US unit SeAH Steel USA, along with operations in Vietnam, the UAE and Italy, handles local production and sales.

The main demand drivers behind recent results are OCTG for North American oil and gas operators and pipe for data-center construction. The UK offshore wind substructure producer SeAH Wind has run local production facilities since 2021.

A credit rating agency said SeAH Steel keeps its top position in the domestic pipe market and has a diversified export network built through overseas affiliates.

04

Earnings analysis

Based on annual results disclosed to the regulator, revenue went from KRW 3.95 trillion in 2022 to KRW 3.91 trillion in 2023 (down 1.0% year on year) and KRW 3.68 trillion in 2024 (down 6.1%), before recovering to KRW 3.76 trillion in 2025 (up 2.3%).

Operating profit rose from KRW 567.2 billion in 2022 to KRW 590.9 billion in 2023, up 4.2%, which was the largest year in the 2022 to 2025 window. It then fell to KRW 211.6 billion in 2024 (down 64.2%) and KRW 205.8 billion in 2025 (down 2.7%), two consecutive annual declines.

The operating margin was 14.3% in 2022 and 15.1% in 2023, then dropped to 5.8% in 2024 and 5.5% in 2025. Revenue grew in 2025, but margins did not recover with it.

Net profit attributable to controlling shareholders went from KRW 376.3 billion in 2022 and KRW 409.7 billion in 2023 to KRW 42.8 billion in 2024 (down 89.5%), then rebounded to KRW 63.3 billion in 2025 (up 47.7%).

Net profit including non-controlling interests was KRW 117 billion in 2024 and KRW 97.9 billion in 2025, and the gap to attributable profit reflects the share belonging to outside shareholders.

Operating cash flow fell from KRW 399 billion in 2022 and KRW 555.7 billion in 2023 to KRW 280.7 billion in 2024 and KRW 17.4 billion in 2025.

On a quarterly basis, 4Q 2025 revenue of KRW 868.6 billion (down 5.9% from the prior quarter) was the low point, and revenue then rose for three consecutive quarters to KRW 991.9 billion in 1Q 2026, KRW 1.17 trillion in 2Q 2026 and KRW 1.23 trillion in 3Q 2026.

The company said 3Q 2026 revenue was the largest quarterly figure since the 2018 spin-off.

Operating profit was KRW 39.8 billion in 3Q 2025 and KRW 16 billion in 4Q 2025 (down 59.8% from the prior quarter), then KRW 26.7 billion in 1Q 2026, an operating loss of KRW 50.8 billion in 2Q 2026, and KRW 90.5 billion in 3Q 2026.

Reports said a provision for an offshore wind project was booked early in 2Q, and the loss at SeAH Wind narrowed in 3Q.

Net profit attributable to controlling shareholders was KRW 13.6 billion in 3Q 2025, then a loss of KRW 32.1 billion in 4Q 2025, KRW 4.05 billion in 1Q 2026, KRW 94.8 billion in 2Q 2026 and KRW 2.95 billion in 3Q 2026, four consecutive quarters of losses. The attributable net loss over the last four quarters totals KRW 133.9 billion.

05

Industry analysis

The market that drives SeAH Steel Holdings' results is North American energy and data-center construction. Reports say AI-driven power demand and an energy crisis triggered by the Middle East war are pushing large-scale oil and gas development in the United States, while Europe is expanding renewable energy.

The company says this has increased demand for energy pipe that requires advanced technology. Logistics uncertainty in the Middle East continues, and although restrictions in the Strait of Hormuz have partly eased, the risk remains.

Changes in US trade conditions once squeezed export volumes, and a credit rating agency pointed to a higher US tariff rate in the second half of 2025 as a factor weighing on SeAH Steel's results.

In the domestic market, weak construction activity cut structural pipe demand and pressured the profitability of SeAH Steel and its structural pipe subsidiaries, according to analysis. Per press reports, SeAH Steel's standalone revenue in 2025 was KRW 1.37 trillion, down 23% from the prior year.

Competitively, SeAH Steel's strengths are its top position in the domestic pipe market and an export network built through overseas units. Reports broadly describe North American energy investment as expanding, while domestic structural pipe and steel sheet remain in a slower recovery.

06

Outlook

The company said it plans to raise its supply competitiveness for higher-value products through capital investment to supplement its US production unit, SeAH Steel USA.

It also said it aims to broaden profitability through Middle East offshore projects and by establishing mass-production capacity for offshore wind substructures at the UK unit SeAH Wind. On the sell-side outlook, Hana Securities expected SeAH Steel's profitability to improve in 4Q 2026 on a recovery in US OCTG demand.

This is a brokerage forecast, not a guidance figure the company has issued. How long the North American price increases hold depends on whether the 3Q 2026 pattern of higher volumes and higher prices continues into 4Q. US tariff conditions remain a variable that determines export volumes.

A credit rating agency noted that large overseas investment has raised financial burdens and said timely returns on those investments matter.

After the 2Q provision was booked early, the loss at SeAH Wind narrowed, and whether further provisions arise and how quickly mass production is established will affect future attributable net results. The next checkpoints are the 4Q 2026 preliminary results and the 2026 annual report.

08

Bull factors

North American energy and data-center pipe demand

Higher volumes and higher prices for North American oil and gas pipe lifted 3Q 2026 revenue to KRW 1.23 trillion, the largest of the last five quarters. Added demand for pipe used in data-center construction broadens the revenue base. The company said this was the largest quarterly revenue since the 2018 spin-off.

Return to operating profit

Operating profit in 3Q 2026 was KRW 90.5 billion, turning positive from an operating loss of KRW 50.8 billion in the prior quarter and up 127.4% year on year. Press reports said the standalone operating profit of the main subsidiary SeAH Steel also turned positive, at KRW 18 billion, in a November 4, 2026 announcement. The operating line shows a clear sign of recovery.

Overseas production base and new business expansion

US production unit SeAH Steel USA is investing to upgrade equipment and raise its competitiveness in higher-value products. The UK unit SeAH Wind aims to establish mass production of offshore wind substructures, and the group is also expanding into Middle East offshore projects. A credit rating agency assessed that overseas affiliates give the group a diversified export network.

09

Bear factors

Four quarters of attributable net losses

Attributable net losses have continued for four consecutive quarters from 4Q 2025 through 3Q 2026, totaling KRW 133.9 billion over the last four quarters. The main weakness is that operating profit has not turned into attributable net profit. The 3Q 2026 attributable net loss of KRW 2.95 billion was a swing to loss from the prior year.

Lower annual profitability and weaker cash generation

The operating margin fell from 15.1% in 2023 to 5.5% in 2025. Operating profit declined two years in a row to KRW 211.6 billion in 2024 and KRW 205.8 billion in 2025. Operating cash flow dropped sharply from KRW 399 billion in 2022 to KRW 17.4 billion in 2025.

Rising financial burden

A credit rating agency said large overseas investment has raised financial burdens and that the timing of investment returns matters. If returns from overseas investment are delayed, the financial burden could last longer.

10

Risk factors

Trade and tariff risk

Changes in US trade conditions once shrank export volumes, and a credit rating agency pointed to a higher US tariff rate in the second half of 2025 as weighing on SeAH Steel's results. A change in tariff policy could hit both North American sales and profitability. Because North America now carries a large share of volume, the impact of policy shifts is significant.

Middle East logistics and geopolitical risk

Logistics uncertainty in the Middle East continues, and although restrictions in the Strait of Hormuz have partly eased, the risk remains. Middle East offshore projects are exposed to the same uncertainty. Strong energy demand is an advantage that coexists with logistics cost and schedule risk.

Offshore wind project loss risk

The UK unit SeAH Wind booked a provision for offshore wind project losses early in 2Q, an example of project losses flowing into reported results. Additional provisions or delays in mass-production schedules could again pressure attributable net results. With the financial burden already higher from large overseas investment, the reach of any loss could widen.

12

Overall view

SeAH Steel Holdings is a pure holding company centered on its pipe subsidiary SeAH Steel. In 3Q 2026, supported by demand for North American energy pipe and data-center projects, it recorded revenue of KRW 1.23 trillion and operating profit of KRW 90.5 billion.

Turning from a KRW 50.8 billion operating loss in 2Q to a profit is the clearest change in the quarter. On an attributable basis, however, net losses have run for four consecutive quarters from 4Q 2025, adding up to KRW 133.9 billion over the last four quarters.

On an annual basis, 2023 was the peak with operating profit of KRW 590.9 billion, followed by two years of decline and an operating margin that fell to 5.5%.

Bullish factors include higher North American pipe prices, data-center demand and the return to operating profit, while bearish factors include continued attributable net losses, a debt-to-equity ratio that rose to 121.2%, and policy variables such as Middle East logistics and US tariffs.

On valuation, the price-to-earnings ratio cannot be calculated because of net losses, and the price-to-book ratio is at a low level, which means the share price sits low relative to asset value.

In sell-side commentary, Hana Securities expected profitability to improve in 4Q 2026, but that is a brokerage view rather than company guidance.

The next checkpoints are the 4Q 2026 preliminary results and the 2026 annual report, with the key question being whether operating profit carries through to attributable net profit.
