02

Business structure

HC BoKwang Industry is centered on the Daegu-Gyeongbuk region, with core operations in aggregate quarrying, ready-mixed concrete production, and asphalt concrete and recycled asphalt concrete manufacturing and sales.

Revenue is split with ready-mixed concrete accounting for more than half (55.04%), followed by aggregates at 33.39%, general asphalt concrete at 8.70%, and recycled asphalt concrete at 2.32%.

The company directly quarries and washes aggregates at its own quarry sites to supply KS-certified raw materials for its own production. In June 2025 the company changed its corporate name from Bokwang Industry to HC BoKwang Industry.

The controlling shareholder is HC Home Center, and the combined stake held by the controller and related parties has hovered in the high-60% range through 2026.

The broader group includes numerous unlisted affiliates spanning ready-mixed concrete, asphalt concrete, building materials, logistics, and fuel wholesale, with Home Center Holdings as another KOSDAQ-listed affiliate.

In November 2024 the company received a presidential citation for product quality at the national quality management competition.

With roughly 75 employees, it operates as a regional small- and mid-cap building materials manufacturer whose revenue is closely tied to local housing construction and SOC-related order volumes.

04

Earnings analysis

HC BoKwang Industry's annual revenue fell for three consecutive years, from KRW 87.5 billion in 2022 to KRW 85.5 billion in 2023 (down 2.3%), KRW 63.9 billion in 2024 (down 25.3%), and KRW 38.5 billion in 2025 (down 39.7%).

Operating profit rose from KRW 15.4 billion in 2022 to KRW 18.9 billion in 2023 (up 23.0%), then fell to KRW 8.86 billion in 2024 (down 53.1%) before turning into an operating loss of KRW 4.87 billion in 2025.

Controlling-interest net profit followed a similar path, rising from KRW 12.1 billion in 2022 to KRW 16.7 billion in 2023 (up 38.0%), falling to KRW 5.24 billion in 2024 (down 68.6%), and turning into a controlling-interest net loss of KRW 2.49 billion in 2025.

The operating margin peaked at 17.6% in 2022 and 22.1% in 2023 before dropping to 13.9% in 2024 and -12.7% in 2025. Operating cash flow, however, stayed positive throughout at KRW 18.7 billion, KRW 25.5 billion, KRW 15.1 billion, and KRW 14.2 billion over 2022-2025 despite the operating loss.

On a quarterly basis, Q3 2025 revenue of KRW 9.03 billion (down 11.4% quarter-on-quarter) came with an operating loss of KRW 478 million, Q4 2025 revenue rose to KRW 11 billion (up 22.2%) even as the operating loss widened to KRW 2.71 billion, and Q1 2026 revenue fell to KRW 7.86 billion (down 28.8%) with the operating loss narrowing to KRW 1.52 billion.

In Q2 2026, revenue jumped to KRW 15 billion (up 90.4% quarter-on-quarter and 46.9% year-on-year), operating profit turned positive at KRW 2.39 billion, and controlling-interest net profit turned positive at KRW 2.79 billion (up 850.0% year-on-year).

Still, the sum of controlling-interest net profit over the last four quarters (Q3 2025 through Q2 2026) was only KRW 319 million, indicating the Q2 2026 rebound largely offset losses accumulated over the prior three quarters rather than adding substantial new profit.

05

Industry analysis

The aggregate, ready-mixed concrete, and asphalt concrete segment HC BoKwang Industry operates in is a classic intermediate building-materials industry tied closely to local construction activity and public SOC order volumes.

The company cited a slowdown in housing construction activity in the Daegu-Gyeongbuk region as the primary reason for its 2025 earnings deterioration in its regulatory disclosure.

In October 2026, asphalt-concrete theme stocks broadly showed mixed performance amid expectations of expanded regional infrastructure investment and rising road-paving material demand, with HC BoKwang Industry posting a notably strong rally within that group.

One market commentator characterized the asphalt concrete industry as having "a stable demand structure based on road maintenance and public SOC investment." The sector also includes nationwide cement and ready-mixed concrete producers such as HANIL CEMENT CO, SAMPYO Cement, and Asia Cement, against which HC BoKwang Industry is a relatively small player in terms of revenue scale and regional coverage.

Its vertically integrated structure of quarrying and washing aggregates at its own sites is cited as a relative strength in raw-material supply stability versus some competing concrete producers, though its heavy regional concentration also makes it comparatively less exposed to, and less able to benefit from, demand shifts outside the Daegu-Gyeongbuk area.

06

Outlook

According to reporting on HC BoKwang Industry's amended semi-annual report filed on September 30, 2026, first-half (Q1-Q2 cumulative) revenue reached KRW 22.8 billion (up 23.9% year-on-year) with operating profit turning positive at KRW 900 million.

This means the sharp single-quarter rebound in Q2 2026 was enough to produce a cumulative first-half operating profit, but because the operating loss persisted through Q1, second-half shipment volumes and margin trends are seen as the key variable for whether a full-year return to profit can be achieved.

On the financing side, the company issued a new convertible bond worth KRW 7 billion in February 2026, with proceeds earmarked for repaying debt arising from the put-option exercise on an earlier convertible bond.

The new bond carries a conversion price of KRW 2,945 with monthly refixing and a floor at 80% of the initial conversion price (KRW 2,356), with the conversion request period beginning February 26, 2027. Full conversion would increase total shares outstanding and could dilute the controlling shareholder's stake.

No specific company-issued revenue or profit guidance, nor new capacity expansion plans, have been publicly confirmed, and since the Q3 2026 regular report has not yet been filed, whether the Q2 rebound continues will need to be verified in the next disclosure.

Operating cash flow stayed positive at KRW 18.7 billion in 2022, KRW 25.5 billion in 2023, KRW 15.1 billion in 2024, and KRW 14.2 billion in 2025, even in the year the company posted an operating loss. This indicates that actual cash generation did not break down despite the income-statement loss.

Bear factors

Three Straight Years of Revenue Decline and a 2025 Swing to Loss

Annual revenue fell for three straight years from KRW 87.5 billion in 2022 to KRW 38.5 billion in 2025, and 2025 saw a swing to an operating loss of KRW 4.87 billion and a controlling-interest net loss of KRW 2.49 billion. The operating margin also deteriorated sharply from 22.1% in 2023 to -12.7% in 2025.

The company cited a slowdown in housing construction activity in the Daegu-Gyeongbuk region as the cause in its disclosure. While Q2 2026 saw a rebound, the four-quarter cumulative controlling-interest net profit was only KRW 319 million, making it premature to call this a sustained recovery.

Rising Debt Ratio and Repeated Convertible Bond Issuance

The debt ratio rose steadily from 64.2% in 2022 to 90.6% in 2025. In February 2026 the company issued a new convertible bond worth KRW 7 billion to address the put-option exercise on an earlier convertible bond.

The new bond includes a monthly refixing clause under which the conversion price can be lowered if the share price falls. Full conversion into shares would increase the total share count outstanding and could dilute existing shareholders' equity value.

Volatility Tied to Regional Economic Dependence

A significant portion of revenue depends on housing construction and regional infrastructure order volumes specifically in the Daegu-Gyeongbuk area. The direct cause of the sharp 2025 earnings decline was disclosed as a slowdown in housing construction activity in this region.

Unlike nationwide cement and ready-mixed concrete producers, the company has limited diversification against swings in this specific regional economy. A decline in order volumes from this particular region could again widen earnings volatility.

Risk factors

Financial and Liquidity Risk

Investors exercised put options on an earlier convertible bond, creating a repayment obligation that the company addressed by issuing a new convertible bond in February 2026.

The new bond also carries a monthly refixing mechanism, meaning the conversion price and resulting dilution scale can shift with share price movements. The steady rise in the debt ratio from 64.2% in 2022 to 90.6% in 2025 is also a point warranting attention regarding financial buffer capacity.

Industry and Regional Economic Risk

The sharp 2025 earnings decline was disclosed as directly caused by a slowdown in housing construction in the Daegu-Gyeongbuk region.

Because revenue is concentrated in specific products (ready-mixed concrete, aggregates, asphalt concrete) and a specific region, earnings sensitivity increases when local construction orders slow.

Whether the Q2 2026 rebound stems from broader nationwide SOC investment expansion or a temporary demand factor requires confirmation through further disclosures.

Governance and Equity Dilution Risk

Part of the stake held by controlling shareholder HC Home Center and related parties has reportedly been pledged as collateral to banks including Kookmin Bank, Shinhan Bank, and Hana Bank.

Repeated convertible bond issuances with refixing clauses could dilute the controlling shareholder group's stake if conversions occur. Reports indicated that if the convertible bond issued in February 2026 were fully converted, the related-party stake could fall from roughly the high-60% range to around 64%.

Overall view

HC BoKwang Industry saw revenue decline for three straight years from KRW 87.5 billion in 2022 to KRW 38.5 billion in 2025, during which it swung to an operating loss of KRW 4.87 billion and a controlling-interest net loss of KRW 2.49 billion.

However, Q2 2026 confirmed a quarterly return to profit with revenue of KRW 15 billion and operating profit of KRW 2.39 billion, and reporting on the amended semi-annual report indicated the first-half cumulative result was also profitable.

Operating cash flow remained positive even through the loss period, suggesting cash-generating capacity itself did not break down.

On the other hand, the debt ratio climbed to 90.6%, and the company issued a new convertible bond with a refixing clause in February 2026 to cover repayment of an earlier bond, meaning financial strain and potential equity dilution coexist with the earnings rebound.

The high regional concentration of its business in Daegu-Gyeongbuk also means quarterly earnings swings could widen again depending on shifts in local construction activity.

The Q3 2026 quarterly report and the full fiscal year 2026 results are likely to be the next clues as to whether the Q2 rebound was a one-off event or a sustained recovery.
