02

Business structure

HC BoKwang Industry is a specialist in construction aggregates, supplying crushed aggregate for concrete, asphalt and ready-mixed concrete.

By product, sales are weighted toward ready-mixed concrete at 55.04% and aggregates at 33.39%, with general asphalt at 8.70%, recycled asphalt at 2.32%, asphalt trading at 0.34% and services at 0.21%, according to Alphasquare's breakdown.

Because the two main products account for most sales, shifts in local construction activity translate quickly into revenue swings. The company positions itself as a leader in recycled asphalt, a business that responds to environmental regulation, and it highlights KS certification as a quality advantage.

Its ready-mixed concrete business expanded coverage across Daegu by acquiring existing plants. Sales are concentrated in Daegu-Gyeongbuk, and the company cited the housing construction slump in that region as the main reason for its 2025 earnings swing.

External analysis points out that construction activity, regional demand and freight costs directly affect profitability. Its largest shareholder, HC Home Center, held 40.74% as of the February 2026 disclosure, and related parties held 68.92% in total.

Unlike cement-focused companies in the same construction materials sector, its core products are aggregates and ready-mixed concrete.

04

Earnings analysis

On a filed annual basis, revenue went from KRW 87.5 billion in 2022 to KRW 85.5 billion in 2023 (down 2.3% year on year), KRW 63.9 billion in 2024 (down 25.3%) and KRW 38.5 billion in 2025 (down 39.7%), declining for three consecutive years from 2023 to 2025. 2022 was the largest year and 2025 the smallest in the 2022 to 2025 period.

Operating profit rose from KRW 15.4 billion in 2022 to KRW 18.9 billion in 2023 (up 23.0%), then fell to KRW 8.86 billion in 2024 (down 53.1%) and swung to an operating loss of KRW 4.87 billion in 2025. Operating margin moved from 17.6% in 2022 and 22.1% in 2023 to 13.9% in 2024 and -12.7% in 2025.

Profit attributable to controlling shareholders went from KRW 12.1 billion in 2022 and KRW 16.7 billion in 2023 (up 38.0%) to KRW 5.24 billion in 2024 (down 68.6%), and then to a net loss of KRW 2.49 billion in 2025. Net income, which includes non-controlling interests, shows the same figures across these years.

The company attributed the decline to the housing construction slump in the Daegu-Gyeongbuk region. Operating cash flow was KRW 18.7 billion in 2022, KRW 25.5 billion in 2023, KRW 15.1 billion in 2024 and KRW 14.2 billion in 2025.

The debt-to-equity ratio rose from 64.2% in 2022 to 90.6% in 2025, with total liabilities of KRW 83.3 billion and total equity of KRW 91.9 billion at the end of 2025.

Quarterly revenue was KRW 9.03 billion in Q3 2025, KRW 11 billion in Q4 2025 (up 22.2% quarter on quarter), KRW 7.86 billion in Q1 2026 (down 28.8%), KRW 15 billion in Q2 2026 (up 90.4%) and KRW 9.52 billion in Q3 2026 (down 36.4% quarter on quarter, up 5.5% year on year).

Operating results went from a loss of KRW 478 million in Q3 2025 and a loss of KRW 2.71 billion in Q4 2025, to a loss of KRW 1.52 billion in Q1 2026, an operating profit of KRW 2.39 billion in Q2 2026, and a loss of KRW 1.51 billion in Q3 2026.

The first-half 2026 report showed revenue up 23.9% year on year and a return to operating profit. The net loss attributable to controlling shareholders over the last four quarters totals KRW 1.04 billion.

Because quarterly swings are large, the recurring earnings level is easier to judge by following the quarterly sequence together.

05

Industry analysis

Aggregates, ready-mixed concrete and asphalt are construction inputs, so their demand tracks construction activity, particularly local housing projects. The company itself cited the housing construction slump in Daegu-Gyeongbuk as the direct cause of its 2025 decline.

External analysis likewise holds that construction activity, regional demand and freight costs directly affect profitability. Because aggregates are heavy, freight costs shape how far products can be sold, which makes a regionally concentrated producer especially sensitive to transport economics.

Recycled asphalt is presented as having demand support from environmental regulation, and the company is described as a leader in that niche. Ready-mixed concrete competes on local supply, so plant ownership in a given area matters; the company's acquisition of existing plants to cover Daegu reflects that dynamic.

Among listed peers in the construction materials sector, cement producers sell different core products, while large builders such as Samsung C&T, Hyundai Engineering & Construction and DL E&C are customers of the regional supply business rather than direct comparables.

Whether the regional housing market is recovering or slowing sets the direction of local demand, so housing starts and sales in Daegu-Gyeongbuk are the key industry indicators to follow.

Q3 2026 revenue rose 5.5% year on year, yet the operating loss persisted, which leaves open whether price or volume is driving the recovery.

Outlook

The confirmed developments center on the financing structure and capital schedule. In February 2026 the company decided to issue a KRW 7 billion fifth-series convertible bond, with proceeds used to repay borrowings tied to the fourth-series bond.

The fourth-series bond's put option required a KRW 18.9 billion repayment on February 27, 2026. The fifth-series conversion price is KRW 2,945, and it can be adjusted down to a floor of KRW 2,356 if the share price falls.

If fully converted, the bond would issue 2,376,910 new shares, equal to 6.54% of the shares outstanding. Conversion becomes possible from February 26, 2027, and the company holds a call option on 30% of the bonds between February 26, 2027 and February 26, 2028.

Bondholders can exercise put options from February 26, 2028 through November 26, 2030. In February 2026, HC Partners, a related company of the largest shareholder, disclosed on-market purchases on February 20 and February 26.

The March 31, 2026 annual general meeting approved a cash dividend along with other agenda items. In mid-October 2026 the share price jumped sharply in a short period, and reporting said a direct trigger could not be confirmed.

The next reported checkpoint is the 2026 annual report and Q4 2026 operating results, and the key question is whether the operating loss seen through Q3 2026 turns into a profit.

Risk factors

Regional housing concentration

The company cited the housing construction slump in Daegu-Gyeongbuk as the main reason for its earnings swing. With a narrow geographic footprint, a drop in local housing starts could pressure both volume and prices, and freight costs add a further variable.

Convertible bond dilution

If the bonds were fully converted, shares outstanding would rise from about 36.3 million to about 38.7 million, which would lower the related parties' stake.

Call and put options open in stages from 2027 through 2030, so shareholder structure and cash outflows could change depending on share prices and funding conditions at exercise.

Trading volatility

Because the share price rose sharply, a decline in trading value or a break below intraday lows is cited as a short-term risk indicator. Reporting in mid-October 2026 said the direct trigger for the sharp rise could not be confirmed, so share price moves may diverge from the earnings trend.

Overall view

The return to a first-half 2026 profit is a positive change, but the operating loss of KRW 1.51 billion in Q3 2026 shows the trend is not yet stable. The Daegu-Gyeongbuk housing slump the company cited is the key variable, with freight costs and local demand also affecting results.
