02

Business structure

Taihan Textile is a textile manufacturer founded in 1953 that is headquartered in Jung-gu, Seoul, with plants in Jeonju and Daegu. A Jobplanet company page shows revenue split as 99.48% textile operations and 0.52% construction, though the page does not state the measurement period.

The same page lists 35,520 spindles, 82 weaving machines, 2 sets of dyeing and finishing facilities and 12 printing lines. According to the company introduction, products are mainly made to plan, while dyed and processed products are made to order from customer instructions.

The same material says the domestic-to-export sales mix is roughly 4 to 6, which means overseas sales are a meaningful share. Garment (finished apparel) sales were cited by fnguide as the driver of revenue growth in the nine months to Q3 2025, with orders from customers such as COSTCO increasing.

Because spinning and weaving capacity sits alongside finished garment business, utilization, cost structure and export-stage profitability all affect results together. Within the apparel and textile sector, Youngone and Hansae, both OEM exporters, are common comparison points.

Tariff shocks and trade policy changes directly hurt profitability in the nine months to Q3 2025, as the fnguide summary shows.

Earnings analysis

Annual revenue went from KRW 191.5 billion in 2022 to KRW 149.4 billion in 2023, down 22.0% YoY, and to KRW 133.9 billion in 2024, down 10.4% YoY, before recovering to KRW 158.3 billion in 2025, up 18.2% YoY. Among 2022 to 2025, 2022 was the largest year and 2024 the smallest.

Operating losses of KRW 3.89 billion in 2022 and KRW 1.81 billion in 2023 narrowed, and operating profit of KRW 2.42 billion in 2024 marked a turn to profit. In 2025, operating profit fell to KRW 697 million, down 71.2% YoY, and the operating margin slipped from 1.8% in 2024 to 0.4%.

Net income attributable to controlling shareholders swung from KRW 1.12 billion in 2024 to a net loss of KRW 611 million in 2025. The net loss including non-controlling interests was also KRW 611 million in 2025.

Operating cash flow went from KRW 3.22 billion in 2022, KRW 10.5 billion in 2023 and KRW 5.23 billion in 2024 to -KRW 22.3 billion in 2025, so cash outflow grew while earnings stayed small. fnguide reported that revenue rose 14.1% in the nine months to Q3 2025 while operating and net results both turned to losses.

Quarterly, Q3 2025 revenue was KRW 40.3 billion with operating profit of KRW 79.27 million and a net loss attributable to controlling shareholders of KRW 310 million.

Q4 2025 revenue was KRW 40.4 billion, up 0.3% QoQ, with operating profit of KRW 1.05 billion and net income attributable to controlling shareholders of KRW 278 million.

Q1 2026 revenue fell 13.5% QoQ to KRW 34.9 billion, operating profit dropped to KRW 369 million and the controlling-shareholder result was a loss of KRW 274 million, marking the trough. Q2 2026 revenue was KRW 40.6 billion, operating profit KRW 1.15 billion and controlling-shareholder net income KRW 347 million.

Q3 2026 revenue was KRW 48 billion, up 18.3% QoQ and 19.2% YoY, operating profit was KRW 1.18 billion, up 1.9% QoQ, and controlling-shareholder net income was KRW 702 million, up 102.4% QoQ.

Net income attributable to controlling shareholders over the latest four quarters totaled KRW 1.05 billion, and the 1.9% QoQ rise in Q3 operating profit trailed the revenue increase.

Industry analysis

The apparel and textile sector is sensitive to consumption in major export markets such as the United States and to trade policy. fnguide's summary of the nine months to Q3 2025 shows garment sales rising while profitability weakened under trade policy changes and tariff shocks.

The case shows revenue growth and margin pressure occurring together, so a recovery in revenue and in margins does not necessarily move at the same pace. In 2026, quarterly revenue rose from KRW 34.9 billion in Q1, the trough, to KRW 40.6 billion in Q2 and KRW 48 billion in Q3.

Company disclosures alone do not separate how much of the increase comes from industry recovery versus volume recovery. Won strength also weighed on export profitability, a point raised in defense-sector coverage that reported the Q3 2026 average won-dollar rate fell 5.6% from the prior quarter.

Within the sector, large OEM exporters such as Youngone and Hansae differ in scale and order structure, while Taihan Textile's spinning and weaving base makes it sensitive to utilization and cost changes.

Competition from lower-cost producing countries such as China remains a constant variable in both fabrics and apparel. Where the industry sits in its cycle is hard to pin down, and more quarters of results will build the evidence base.
