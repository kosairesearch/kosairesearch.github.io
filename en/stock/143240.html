Quarterly profit recovers on AI services, hiring-market weakness remains the test

02

Business structure

Saramin operates the Saramin job portal and runs three business lines: career platform, recruiting consulting, and outsourcing. The career platform charges employers fees and advertising for posting job openings and matching them with job seekers, so weaker hiring directly reduces job postings and platform revenue.

In the first quarter of 2024, platform revenue was KRW 19.0 billion, outsourcing revenue KRW 9.2 billion, and recruiting consulting revenue KRW 2.6 billion. On a cumulative basis for 2025, the career platform generated about KRW 49.9 billion, more than 50% of total revenue.

Recruiting consulting provides end-to-end hiring solutions mainly to public institutions and state-run companies, while outsourcing places staff with employers for flexible workforce needs.

In the first half of 2026, career platform revenue rose to KRW 36.0 billion from KRW 32.5 billion a year earlier, and the company credits a lock-in effect from expanded AI services.

AX business revenue, which includes career education, freelancer matching and an AX platform, reached KRW 4.0 billion in the first half of 2026, up 25.0% from KRW 3.2 billion. Outside recruiting, the company operates a consumer dating app based on personality analysis and a non-face-to-face fortune-telling service.

Its main competitors are other job portals and HR platform operators, and during hiring slowdowns these players have diversified into non-recruiting businesses.

04

Earnings analysis

Annual results show revenue shrinking and profitability falling at the same time. Revenue went from KRW 148.9 billion in 2022 to KRW 131.5 billion in 2023 (down 11.7% year on year), KRW 128.4 billion in 2024 (down 2.4%) and KRW 121.2 billion in 2025 (down 5.6%), declining for three consecutive years from 2023 to 2025.

Operating profit fell from KRW 40.6 billion in 2022 to KRW 16.8 billion in 2025 and declined three years in a row from 2023 to 2025; 2022 was the largest year and 2025 the smallest in the 2022 to 2025 period. The operating margin moved from 27.3% in 2022 to 19.2% in 2023, 16.6% in 2024 and 13.8% in 2025.

When it reported 2024 annual results, the company said lower job postings from employers pushed platform revenue down, and it said cost efficiency lifted fourth-quarter 2024 operating profit slightly year on year.

Operating cash flow fell from KRW 37.5 billion in 2022 to KRW 20.9 billion in 2023, KRW 22.2 billion in 2024 and KRW 12.4 billion in 2025. The debt ratio rose from 16.5% in 2024 to 22.4% in 2025, with total liabilities at KRW 59.7 billion and total equity at KRW 267 billion at year-end 2025.

Net profit attributable to controlling shareholders was KRW 91.9 billion in 2025, up 644.6% and the largest year in the 2022 to 2025 period, while operating profit was only KRW 16.8 billion and total net profit including non-controlling interests was KRW 91.8 billion, which points to non-operating items driving the increase.

Press reports said Saramin disposed of its entire stake in Remember & Company in 2025 and secured roughly KRW 160 billion in cash, about twice the original investment. Quarterly results bottomed in the fourth quarter of 2025, when operating profit was KRW 4.06 billion, down 29.7% from the prior quarter.

Operating profit then rose to KRW 4.27 billion in the first quarter of 2026, KRW 6.44 billion in the second and KRW 6.92 billion in the third, a three-quarter run of increases. Third-quarter 2026 revenue was KRW 33 billion, up 7.0% year on year.

Net profit attributable to controlling shareholders jumped to KRW 79.3 billion in the fourth quarter of 2025 and fell 93.7% to KRW 5.02 billion in the first quarter of 2026, with KRW 6.58 billion in the third quarter.

The last four quarters total KRW 97.7 billion, and the fourth-quarter 2025 gain accounts for most of that sum. The company attributed the first-quarter 2026 improvement to company-wide AI adoption and cost efficiency, and press reports put the first-quarter operating margin at 15.1% versus 7% a year earlier.

05

Industry analysis

Saramin's hiring market is highly sensitive to how many new positions employers open. In the third quarter of 2025, HR platform companies broadly struggled with a hiring slump, and press reports said Saramin's revenue fell 6% year on year while operating profit dropped more than 10%.

Over the same period, one peer HR platform reported first-quarter 2025 revenue down 11.2% year on year with a widening operating loss.

Since then the sector has expanded into generative AI and non-recruiting services, and in the second quarter of 2026 several HR platform operators posted strong results on the back of AI businesses. Saramin has run an internal AI lab since 2014, which industry observers cite as the basis for its AI service expansion.

Where the hiring cycle stands is still unclear, and the company's revenue gains appear to come mainly from AI services that raise customer retention and per-customer spend rather than from a confirmed rebound in job postings.

In competitive terms, Saramin combines a career platform that generates most revenue with recruiting consulting and outsourcing, while its job-posting monetization model is similar to rival portals.

Its differentiators are cited as its job-seeker data, its employer customer base and the speed of its AI feature development. Profitability of its non-recruiting businesses remains small and is not yet separately visible in filed figures.

06

Outlook

The company said it will keep focusing on expanding AI services through the second half of 2026. When it reported second-quarter 2026 results, a Saramin official said the company would use its AI technology to extend services and sustain growth.

In the first half it launched an AI job-preparation product for job seekers and expanded AI services for employers. In its third-quarter 2026 filing, the company said platform revenue and consulting income both rose on AI-driven service upgrades.

For the first quarter of 2026 it cited company-wide AI adoption, productivity gains and structural cost efficiency, and said it would keep raising platform competitiveness through further AI development.

Press reports in 2025 said the company planned to put the cash from its Remember & Company stake sale into AI technology and future growth strategies. In non-recruiting services, the company previewed a personality-based dating app, and it said 100,000 people joined within two weeks of the preview.

No numerical revenue or operating profit guidance from the company was confirmed in the available sources. The next periodic filing, the 2026 annual report, will be the first disclosure of fourth-quarter 2026 revenue, operating profit and cost structure.

The key items to verify there are how the fourth-quarter 2025 gain was accounted for and whether the operating margin holds near third-quarter 2026 levels. Any recovery in job postings may appear first in industry indicators before it shows up in filed results.

08

Bull factors

Three straight quarters of operating profit growth

Operating profit rose from KRW 4.27 billion in the first quarter of 2026 to KRW 6.44 billion in the second and KRW 6.92 billion in the third. Third-quarter operating profit was up 20.0% year on year. The recovery follows a low of KRW 4.06 billion in the fourth quarter of 2025. The company attributes the margin improvement to cost efficiency and AI adoption together.

AI services lift career platform revenue

Career platform revenue in the first half of 2026 reached KRW 36.0 billion, up from KRW 32.5 billion a year earlier. The company credits a lock-in effect from expanded AI services. AX business revenue rose 25.0% to KRW 4.0 billion. The shift in direction for the core platform business is cited as evidence of a turnaround.

Financial base and cash capacity

Total equity at year-end 2025 was KRW 267 billion, with total liabilities of KRW 59.7 billion and a debt ratio of 22.4%. Press reports said the Remember & Company stake disposal generated roughly KRW 160 billion in cash. The cash was reported to be earmarked for AI technology and future growth strategies. Financial capacity that can fund business investment and non-recruiting expansion is a bullish factor.

Bear factors

Revenue down three years in a row

Revenue declined from KRW 148.9 billion in 2022 to KRW 121.2 billion in 2025, falling three years in a row from 2023 to 2025. The company has attributed the decline to fewer job postings from employers. First-quarter 2026 revenue was KRW 28.3 billion, down 8.3% from the prior quarter. Whether the revenue recovery is tied to a recovery in hiring has not yet been confirmed.

Lower operating margin and shrinking operating cash flow

The operating margin fell from 27.3% in 2022 to 13.8% in 2025. Operating cash flow dropped from KRW 37.5 billion in 2022 to KRW 12.4 billion in 2025. Quarterly operating profit rose in 2026, but confirming an annual recovery must wait for the annual report. The debt ratio also rose from 16.5% in 2024 to 22.4% in 2025.

Earnings volatility and reliance on one-off gains

Net profit attributable to controlling shareholders of KRW 91.9 billion in 2025 is far above operating profit of KRW 16.8 billion. After KRW 79.3 billion in the fourth quarter of 2025, the figure fell 93.7% to KRW 5.02 billion in the first quarter of 2026.

Because earnings depend heavily on non-operating items, the durability of quarterly net profit is limited. Judging underlying earning power requires looking at the operating profit trend alongside it.

10

Risk factors

Hiring cycle risk

Saramin's revenue is directly tied to how many positions employers open and how many postings they place. Press reports said third-quarter 2025 revenue and operating profit fell year on year amid a hiring slump. A prolonged economic slowdown could push career platform revenue down again. This risk exists regardless of how AI services perform.

Diversification execution risk

Non-recruiting businesses such as the dating app and fortune-telling service do not yet show identifiable scale in filed figures. If cash spent on new businesses does not pay off, the pace of margin recovery could slow.

With competition in AI services intensifying, whether feature differentiation holds against rivals also needs confirmation. If new business results do not offset weakness in core recruiting, the revenue mix may not change.

Earnings interpretation risk

The KRW 79.3 billion net profit attributable to controlling shareholders in the fourth quarter of 2025 appears to coincide with the stake disposal, but its accounting and details must be verified in periodic filings.

This gain accounts for most of the KRW 97.7 billion total over the last four quarters, which complicates earnings-based metrics. Some quarters show net profit in the single-digit billions of won, so volatility is high. Underlying earnings excluding these items should be read through operating profit.

12

Overall view

Saramin has been an HR platform whose revenue and operating profit fell from 2022, and in 2026 its direction is changing as quarterly operating profit has risen for three straight quarters.

Net profit attributable to controlling shareholders rose to KRW 91.9 billion in 2025, but operating profit fell to KRW 16.8 billion, so the increase appears to rely heavily on non-operating items.

Bullish factors are the career platform recovery driven by expanded AI services, growth in AX business, a better operating margin from cost efficiency, and a financial base with total equity of KRW 267 billion.

Bearish factors are revenue declines for three consecutive years, a 13.8% operating margin in 2025, lower operating cash flow, a higher debt ratio and high quarterly earnings volatility. Whether the 2026 quarterly recovery extends to a recovery in hiring demand has not yet been confirmed.

The next checkpoints are the 2026 annual report, due around late March 2027, and the first-quarter 2027 report. This report is for information only and does not include a buy or sell opinion or a target price.
