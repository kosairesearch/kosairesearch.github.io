02

Business structure

Lithium-for-earth was founded in 1998 as a semiconductor test-equipment maker and diversified its business by merging with content manufacturer Withmobile in 2019. The core of its ongoing operations remains the manufacture and sale of IP-licensed content products and mobile phone accessories.

In November 2022 the company added a business purpose for manufacturing and selling lithium materials for secondary batteries, and in May 2023 changed its name from Urban Lithium to its current name, repositioning itself as a battery materials company.

However, the lithium materials business generated only KRW8 million in revenue in 2025, about 0.1% of total sales.

The company spent roughly KRW17.0 billion to buy land for a lithium materials plant in the Saemangeum National Industrial Complex in North Jeolla Province and held a groundbreaking ceremony while announcing plans for a further KRW200.0 billion of investment.

More recently, the company has been expanding its existing IP licensing and OEM/ODM business, which has worked with properties such as Kakao Friends, Samsung Electronics, Mercedes-Benz, and BTS characters, into global game platforms such as Roblox.

Through subsidiary LMK it is also building a PFAS-free semiconductor component cleaning and regeneration business and is reviewing a possible merger.

The largest shareholder group, including CEO Byun Jae-seok and related parties, held a combined 55.53% stake as of an August 2025 disclosure, reflecting a governance restructuring following a past management-control dispute.

04

Earnings analysis

Consolidated revenue fell for three straight years, from KRW20.7 billion in 2022 to KRW15.6 billion in 2023 (down 24.6% year on year), KRW15.2 billion in 2024 (down 3.0%), and KRW10.7 billion in 2025 (down 29.7%), the smallest among the four disclosed years.

Over the same period the operating loss widened and then narrowed: KRW3.75 billion in 2022, KRW6.81 billion in 2023 (loss continued, widened), KRW5.22 billion in 2024 (loss continued, narrowed), and KRW4.48 billion in 2025 (loss continued, narrowed), a fourth consecutive year of operating losses.

Controlling-interest net income was a profit of KRW89.6 billion in 2022 before swinging to a net loss of KRW182.8 billion in 2023, followed by net losses of KRW102.2 billion in 2024 (narrowed) and KRW13.4 billion in 2025 (narrowed), three straight years of losses.

The company has previously disclosed that valuation losses on convertible bonds linked to Hydro Lithium Inc. and another affiliated lithium venture, along with derivative valuation losses, were large, suggesting non-operating items played a significant role in the heavy net losses of 2023-2024.

The operating margin worsened from -18.1% in 2022 to -43.6% in 2023, improved to -34.4% in 2024, then slipped back to -42.1% in 2025.

On a quarterly basis, revenue rose from KRW2.58 billion in the second quarter of 2025 to KRW2.67 billion in the third quarter (up 3.4%), KRW3.06 billion in the fourth quarter (up 14.6%), then eased to KRW2.93 billion in the first quarter of 2026 (down 4.4%) before jumping to KRW4.88 billion in the second quarter of 2026, up 66.6% quarter on quarter and 88.8% year on year.

The quarterly operating loss narrowed from KRW1.38 billion in the second quarter of 2025 to KRW841 million in the third quarter, then widened to KRW1.1 billion in the fourth quarter and KRW1.9 billion in the first quarter of 2026, before narrowing again to KRW1.33 billion in the second quarter of 2026 versus both the prior quarter and the year-ago period, though losses continued for a fifth straight quarter.

Controlling-interest net loss widened from KRW1.83 billion in the second quarter of 2025 to KRW10.3 billion in the fourth quarter, then narrowed to a KRW1.06 billion loss in the first quarter of 2026, before turning into a KRW972 million profit in the second quarter of 2026, a turnaround versus both the prior quarter and the year-ago period.

Still, with the operating loss persisting while only net income turned positive, non-operating items appear to have had a substantial effect on quarterly results, and the cumulative controlling-interest net loss over the last four quarters (third quarter of 2025 through second quarter of 2026) reached KRW10.9 billion.

05

Industry analysis

The lithium materials market for secondary batteries has gone through a period in which slowing growth in EV demand combined with increased lithium mine supply to push raw material prices lower, raising concerns about the profitability of related companies.

Against this backdrop, the second quarter of 2026 brought some signs of a cyclical rebound, with reports that Korea's three major battery makers all swung to operating profit, helped by a rebound in the European EV market and a surge in demand for energy storage systems (ESS) from US data centers.

POSCO Holdings' subsidiary POSCO Argentina said it posted its first quarterly profit in the second quarter of 2026, helped by stabilized operations at its first brine lithium plant. Ecopro also said it maintained a profit in the second quarter of 2026 as higher lithium selling prices were reflected in its pricing.

While lithium and battery-materials peers in the sector have begun reflecting price pass-through effects from actual production and sales volumes in their results, Lithium-for-earth generated virtually no lithium materials revenue over the same period, leaving it structurally unable to benefit from the industry recovery.

The market views the medium- to long-term growth outlook as intact given rising EV and ESS demand, but sees near-term share-price volatility among lithium-related names widening on raw material price and supply-demand swings, with names such as POSCO Future M, Lithium-for-earth, and Hydro Lithium Inc. increasingly moving on individual supply-demand factors rather than in unison.

Lithium-for-earth's share price has repeatedly swung in response to lithium-related news despite having almost no actual lithium revenue.

06

Outlook

The company has been revising its business plan for the lithium materials plant site in the Saemangeum National Industrial Complex, citing difficulty in securing external financing, and has disclosed that it recognized an impairment on part of the asset under construction as the risk of a tenancy-contract termination clause being triggered increased.

As of the end of 2025, it recognized an impairment of KRW9.1 billion on the Saemangeum plant's land and facilities along with a KRW1.1 billion loan-loss allowance, and set aside a provision of roughly KRW600 million for restoration costs in case the tenancy contract is terminated due to construction delays.

On the capital structure front, a capital reduction (a 5-for-1 reverse stock split) was approved at the March 2026 annual general meeting, followed by a change of listing for the new shares and a resumption of trading.

In October the company decided to retire 8,361 treasury shares to reduce the number of shares outstanding, a shareholder-value measure it said, like the earlier capital reduction, was aimed at improving its financial structure and capital efficiency.

CEO Byun Jae-seok said in a shareholder letter that the company expects to meet the pre-tax continuing-operations-loss requirement and resolve the administrative-issue designation by the time the fiscal 2026 audit report is submitted in March 2027.

In the same letter the company also disclosed the status of major debt recovery efforts.

On the business portfolio side, the company is pursuing new growth drivers by expanding its IP licensing and OEM/ODM business into global platforms such as Roblox and by building out its semiconductor-parts cleaning subsidiary LMK, for which it is also reviewing a possible merger.

Risk factors

Administrative-Issue Designation and Listing Risk

The company has been designated as an administrative-issue stock after its pre-tax loss from continuing operations exceeded 50% of equity in two of the last three fiscal years.

The company has said it expects to resolve this requirement by the time the fiscal 2026 audit report is submitted in March 2027, but this is the company's own forecast, not a confirmed outcome.

Contingent Liability and Contract Risk at the Saemangeum Plant

Because construction delays could lead to the termination of the Saemangeum industrial complex tenancy contract, the company has set aside a provision of roughly KRW600 million for restoration costs.

With the business plan still being revised due to difficulty in external financing, the possibility of further asset impairment or costs from a contract termination cannot be ruled out.

Theme-Driven Share Price Volatility and Non-Operating Earnings Swings

Despite having minimal lithium-related revenue, the share price has repeatedly swung in response to lithium-related news regardless of actual results.

Convertible-bond and derivative valuation gains or losses tied to affiliates have significantly swung net income in the past, and such non-operating volatility could continue to complicate the interpretation of future results.

Overall view

Despite its name, most of Lithium-for-earth's revenue comes from mobile phone accessories and IP licensing, while the lithium materials business for secondary batteries has yet to reach a full-scale production and sales stage, having generated only KRW8 million in revenue in 2025.

Annual results showed revenue declining for three straight years and operating losses continuing for four straight years from 2022 through 2025, though the size of the losses narrowed somewhat in 2024-2025.

In the second quarter of 2026, revenue rose 66.6% quarter on quarter and controlling-interest net income turned positive at KRW972 million, but the operating loss of KRW1.33 billion persisted in the same quarter, so whether core profitability is actually improving needs to be reconfirmed in subsequent quarters.

The capital structure has improved, with the debt-to-equity ratio falling to the single digits and concerns about capital impairment easing after a reverse stock split and capital restructuring, but the company remains designated as an administrative-issue stock, with the requirement's resolution to be determined at the March 2027 audit report.

The Saemangeum lithium plant is undergoing a business-plan revision due to financing difficulties and has already recognized a large impairment, leaving it unclear how the originally announced large-scale investment plan will ultimately unfold.

Investors need to distinguish between share-price volatility driven by the lithium theme and the company's actual business performance, with the next quarterly filing and the resolution of the administrative-issue designation standing out as the key items to watch.
