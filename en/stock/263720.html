IP-driven CP faces test as quarterly profits slow

02

Business structure

D&C Media plans, produces, and distributes intellectual property (IP) from web novels and webtoons, operating as a content provider (CP). In 2025 consolidated revenue, webtoon e-books made up about 47%, the largest share, followed by web novel e-books at about 23%.

Paper books were small, with web novels at about 4% and webtoons at about 3%, while products and other revenue accounted for about 23%. These shares are the figures cited in a Korean IR association report reported in the first half of 2026.

The company secures original web novel IP, adapts it into webtoons, and extends it into print and screen adaptations, a multi-layer structure that analysts have pointed to as its key differentiator. As of the end of 2025 it had three consolidated subsidiaries covering webtoon production, animation, and digital content.

Its flagship title Solo Leveling had reached a cumulative 14.3 billion views globally as reported in 2024, and it has been developed into games and animation. In Japan, a joint venture formed in 2022 with a Japanese platform company handles local artist sourcing and new title production.

In competition, most webtoon CPs are small, and few hold large IP, according to a 2024 research note. Distribution relies heavily on major platforms, so changes in commission structures or contract terms can affect profitability.

04

Earnings analysis

Annual results show a clear recovery since the 2023 low. Revenue fell from KRW 61.2 billion in 2022 to KRW 60.4 billion in 2023, down 1.4% year on year, then rose to KRW 83.5 billion in 2024, up 38.3%, and to KRW 85.5 billion in 2025, up 2.3%, marking two straight years of growth.

Operating profit dropped to KRW 3.49 billion in 2023, down 40.5%, then grew to KRW 10.3 billion in 2024, up 194.8%, and to KRW 17.3 billion in 2025, up 68.7%, also two straight years of increases.

Operating margin moved from 9.6% in 2022 and 5.8% in 2023 to 12.3% in 2024 and 20.3% in 2025, the highest of the four years. In 2025 operating profit grew far faster than revenue, which points to an improved cost structure.

Net income attributable to controlling shareholders was KRW 6.14 billion in 2022, KRW 3.96 billion in 2023, KRW 11.1 billion in 2024, the peak of the period, and KRW 7.97 billion in 2025, down 28.5%. Net income including non-controlling interests in 2025 was KRW 7.98 billion, close to the attributable figure.

Operating cash flow rose from KRW 5.44 billion in 2022 to KRW 14.1 billion in 2024 and KRW 15.1 billion in 2025, moving in step with earnings.

At the end of 2025, the debt ratio was 22.8%, down from 30.8% in 2024, with total liabilities of KRW 21.8 billion, total equity of KRW 95.7 billion, and equity attributable to controlling shareholders of KRW 95.1 billion. Quarterly results turned after the third quarter of 2025.

Revenue fell from KRW 27.7 billion in the third quarter of 2025 to KRW 15.4 billion in the fourth quarter, down 44.5% from the prior quarter, rebounded to KRW 17.4 billion in the first quarter of 2026, up 13.0%, and then declined for two straight quarters to KRW 15.9 billion and KRW 14.9 billion.

Third-quarter 2026 revenue was down 46.0% year on year. Operating profit fell for four straight quarters, from KRW 6.5 billion in the third quarter of 2025 to KRW 4.11 billion, KRW 3.49 billion, KRW 2.25 billion, and KRW 1.46 billion in the third quarter of 2026, down 77.5% year on year.

Because operating profit fell faster than revenue, margins also compressed. Attributable net income swung to a KRW 802 million net loss in the fourth quarter of 2025 and back to a KRW 2.13 billion profit in the first quarter of 2026, then KRW 2.15 billion in the second quarter and KRW 786 million in the third quarter.

The fourth-quarter 2025 net loss came despite positive operating profit of KRW 4.11 billion, which points to a large non-operating impact in that quarter. Attributable net income for the last four quarters totals KRW 4.26 billion.

05

Industry analysis

The webtoon and web novel industry is shifting its weight from a platform-centered structure toward an IP-centered one. A report carried in the first half of 2026 cites a multi-layer model, in which IP is adapted into webtoons, print books, and video, as a beneficiary of this shift.

Domestic webtoon market size was cited at about KRW 1 trillion in a 2020 report, and overseas demand for Korean webtoons grew as titles were exported through foreign platforms. Analysts in the early 2020s repeatedly pointed to rising value in secondary rights as webtoon-based dramas and adaptations increased.

Compared with Studio Dragon, a drama producer in the same content sector, D&C Media is described as owning a large share of its original web novel and webtoon IP. On the other hand, most webtoon CPs are small, so securing writers and production staff is a competitive factor.

Japan is the country the company identifies as the largest share of its overseas business, and a joint venture set up in 2022 supports local new-title production.

On distribution, commission structures and contract terms with major platforms can change depending on platform bargaining power, which directly affects CP profitability. For this reason, strengthening proprietary IP and expanding collaborations with multiple platforms are both cited as ongoing tasks.

08

Bull factors

Track record of secondary IP monetization

Solo Leveling, which reported cumulative global views of 14.3 billion in 2024, has been monetized through animation and games after its webtoon run. The company extends its own IP into secondary works, so one title creates several revenue paths.

A multi-layer structure that moves original web novel IP into webtoons, print, and video is cited as a factor broadening earnings contribution.

Profit recovery since the 2023 low

Operating margin rose from 5.8% in 2023 to 20.3% in 2025, and operating profit grew for two straight years to KRW 17.3 billion in 2025. Operating cash flow also increased alongside earnings, to KRW 14.1 billion in 2024 and KRW 15.1 billion in 2025. The improvement in profit structure since the 2023 low is the basis of the bullish case.

Low debt burden

At the end of 2025 the debt ratio was 22.8%, with total liabilities of KRW 21.8 billion and total equity of KRW 95.7 billion. Equity attributable to controlling shareholders accounts for most of that, at KRW 95.1 billion.

With a relatively low debt burden, the company has a comparatively large financial cushion, which is cited as a bullish factor.

Bear factors

Consecutive quarterly slowdown

Operating profit has declined for four straight quarters since the fourth quarter of 2025. Because the drop in operating profit exceeded the drop in revenue, margin pressure appeared at the same time.

Dependence on major platforms

A report carried in the first half of 2026 noted that high dependence on major distribution channels means profitability can be affected by changes in commission structures or contract terms. Webtoon e-books made up about 47% of 2025 revenue, so a large share depends on specific distribution routes. If platform bargaining power shifts, the company's share of the same revenue could shrink.

Volatility in net income

Net income attributable to controlling shareholders fell 28.5% from KRW 11.1 billion in 2024 to KRW 7.97 billion in 2025, and the fourth quarter of 2025 swung to a KRW 802 million net loss. Attributable net income for the last four quarters totals KRW 4.26 billion.

The gap between operating profit and net income is wide, so non-operating items can heavily influence the net income trend.

Risk factors

Platform and contract risk

Because distribution depends heavily on major platforms, changes in commission rates or contract terms could shake both revenue mix and margins. A report carried in the first half of 2026 also cited distribution dependence as a variable.

Broadening collaborations across platforms is offered as an alternative, but restructuring contracts takes time and cost.

Continuity of IP hits

Because secondary monetization of flagship IP has driven earnings gains, a lack of follow-up hits could weigh on quarterly profit. A 2024 report described the company as holding multiple hit IPs, but the performance of new titles has to be tracked through quarterly revenue trends. The consecutive quarterly revenue declines in 2026 are an indicator of whether this risk is materializing.

Overseas operations and non-operating items

Overseas businesses, including the Japan joint venture, are affected by local platform share, exchange rates, and the structure of the joint venture.

As the fourth quarter of 2025 showed, a quarter with positive operating profit can still post a net loss for controlling shareholders due to non-operating factors, which makes net income volatile. Details of overseas performance and non-operating items can be traced in the notes to the annual report.

Overall view

D&C Media is a content provider that owns webtoon and web novel IP and extends it into secondary businesses. After the 2023 low, annual revenue and operating profit rose for two straight years in 2024 and 2025, restoring earnings strength. The 2025 operating margin of 20.3% is the highest of the four reported years.

However, quarterly results changed direction in 2026. Third-quarter 2026 revenue was KRW 14.9 billion, down 46.0% year on year, and operating profit was KRW 1.46 billion, down 77.5%, with operating profit declining for four straight quarters since the fourth quarter of 2025.

Attributable net income swung to a net loss in the fourth quarter of 2025 and back to profit in the first quarter of 2026, but fell to KRW 786 million in the third quarter.

The bullish factors are the track record of secondary IP monetization and a low debt ratio of 22.8%, while the bearish factors are the consecutive quarterly declines, dependence on major distribution platforms, and volatile net income.

On valuation, the share price trades below book value attributable to controlling shareholders, and a small earnings base of KRW 4.26 billion over the last four quarters makes multiples highly sensitive.

The next checkpoint is the 2026 annual report, followed by whether operating profit declines stop in the 2027 first- and second-quarter results.
