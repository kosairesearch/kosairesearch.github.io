02

Business structure

Sungchang Enterprise Holdings is a pure holding company that owns shares in its subsidiaries, directs their business and provides management guidance and consulting, and it runs no factory or business division of its own. Its fee income comes from management consulting and guarantee fees charged to subsidiaries.

Standalone revenue for 2025 was KRW 6.678 billion, small next to consolidated revenue of KRW 127.2 billion. Operations sit with the manufacturing subsidiaries, whose businesses have been organized around plywood, particleboard (PB), wood chips and imported wood-product distribution.

The plywood subsidiary makes general plywood, TEGO plywood and formalin, an adhesive raw material, and sells mainly through dealers and wholesalers.

The particleboard subsidiary recycles waste wood into eco-friendly particleboard, while the wood-chip subsidiary processes waste wood into chips used as particleboard feedstock and as fuel for cogeneration plants.

The import and distribution subsidiary has brought plywood and particleboard in from overseas for domestic sale, and a stake transaction in April 2026 was followed by a small-scale absorption merger in July 2026.

Key raw materials are plywood semi-finished products, veneer and methanol, and the group relies heavily on imports, which makes it sensitive to international demand and business cycles. Within the group, waste-wood processing feeds particleboard production in a vertically linked structure.

After a five-for-one share consolidation effective April 10, 2026, common shares outstanding stood at 13,950,320.

04

Earnings analysis

Consolidated revenue fell from KRW 227.5 billion in 2022 to KRW 166.3 billion in 2023 (down 26.9% year on year), KRW 143.1 billion in 2024 (down 13.9%) and KRW 127.2 billion in 2025 (down 11.1%). Revenue declined three years in a row from 2023, with 2022 the largest year and 2025 the smallest in the 2022–2025 period.

The operating loss widened from KRW 11.5 billion in 2022 to KRW 16.7 billion in 2023, then narrowed to KRW 7.29 billion in 2024 and KRW 6.93 billion in 2025, and the company has posted operating losses in four consecutive years from 2022 to 2025.

The operating margin was -5.0% in 2022, -10.0% in 2023, -5.1% in 2024 and -5.4% in 2025, staying near -5% outside 2023. The net loss attributable to controlling shareholders was KRW 13.2 billion in 2022, KRW 24 billion in 2023, KRW 3.53 billion in 2024 and KRW 20.6 billion in 2025.

The 2025 net loss including non-controlling interests was also KRW 20.6 billion, the same as the controlling-shareholder figure. The 2025 controlling-shareholder loss of KRW 20.6 billion is far larger than the KRW 6.93 billion operating loss, which suggests that items below the operating line weighed heavily that year.

Quarterly, the operating loss grew from KRW 1.14 billion in Q3 2025 to KRW 2.15 billion in Q4, narrowed to KRW 1.62 billion in Q1 2026, and turned into an operating profit of KRW 391 million in Q2 2026.

Q3 2026 returned to an operating loss of KRW 788 million, while revenue of KRW 30.6 billion fell 11.9% from the prior quarter.

The controlling-shareholder result was a loss of KRW 14.9 billion in Q4 2025, the largest quarterly loss in the series, followed by a profit of KRW 951 million in Q2 2026 and a loss of KRW 1.06 billion in Q3 2026.

Over the last four quarters the controlling-shareholder net loss totals KRW 16.5 billion, with Q4 2025 accounting for the largest part. Operating cash flow moved from negative KRW 26.5 billion in 2022 to KRW 12 billion in 2023, then stayed positive at KRW 98.04 million in 2024 and KRW 1.53 billion in 2025.

05

Industry analysis

Demand for the company's products is concentrated in construction and furniture. A slowdown in construction activity or housing starts, or weaker furniture demand, hits product volumes and selling prices directly.

The plywood business is weak because of a construction downturn and competition, but a corporate summary on a securities information site describes improving profit structure through cost reduction and a reorganized production system.

The same summary notes that rising use of KS-certified plywood for concrete formwork is lifting demand for KS plywood. The particleboard business is under pressure from low-priced imported particleboard. Because raw materials depend heavily on imports, the group is sensitive to international demand and business cycles.

Within the holding-company group, unlike a holding company such as SK Square whose value centers on stakes in semiconductor companies, the operating results of the wood-products subsidiaries feed directly into Sungchang's consolidated earnings.

The most direct signal of where the cycle stands is the quarterly revenue and profit trend, with quarterly revenue rising to KRW 34.7 billion in Q2 2026 before falling to KRW 30.6 billion in Q3.

Plywood is already weak amid the construction downturn. Whether the decline to KRW 30.6 billion in Q3 2026 revenue continues is the point to watch.

Import dependence and import competition

Particleboard competes with low-priced imports, which can make defending selling prices difficult. How far cost reduction shows up in profit is a key question.

Governance and free float
