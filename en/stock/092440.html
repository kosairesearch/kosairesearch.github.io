Kishin: Three Years of Falling Sales and a Swing to Operating Loss

Kishin's revenue has declined for three straight fiscal years, and the company swung to an operating loss in the fiscal year ended March 2026.

  1. 1

    Sales in the fiscal year ended March 2026 were KRW 119.3 billion, down 9.2% year on year, extending a decline that has run three years since the fiscal year ended March 2024.

  2. 2

    Operating loss was KRW 3.71 billion in the fiscal year ended March 2026, a swing from operating profit, with an operating margin of -3.1%.

  3. 3

    The Q2 2026 operating loss narrowed to KRW 869 million from a year earlier, but the company has posted operating losses for five straight quarters since Q2 2025.

  4. 4

    Total equity stood at KRW 246.5 billion at the end of March 2026 against total liabilities of KRW 13.2 billion, a debt ratio of 5.4%.

  5. 5

02

Business structure

Kishin manufactures and sells mold bases, the basic frames of injection molds, as its core business. According to its business report, production sites are in Incheon, covering the headquarters and first plant, a second plant, and a steel plant.

In December 2021 the company acquired a large mold plant to secure large-mold production capacity. Based on Q1 2026 sales, the segment mix was mold base 74%, plate 10%, other 6%, forged steel 5%, and press 5% (Butler aggregation).

In the segment table for the fiscal year ended March 2025, mold base was the largest at 82.77%, followed by plate at 14.00%, forged steel at 8.55%, and press at 3.73% (FnGuide).

Automotive and home appliance industries are the main sources of mold demand, and WISEReport cited tariff-driven weakness in North American mold competitiveness, softer automotive and appliance demand, and lower US exports due to development delays as reasons for the 2025 decline.

A board resolution on December 23, 2025 approved a shutdown of a subsidiary citing a deteriorating business environment and continuing losses, and the shutdown amount equaled 7.37% of recent consolidated sales.

In 2023 the company announced plans for new businesses in lithium-ion battery molds and motor core die sets, tied to renewable energy and electric vehicles.

Competition in mold bases involves domestic and overseas mold component makers, and the company's structure and scale differ widely from the large equipment makers in the machinery and equipment sector.

Earnings analysis

Kishin's results show three years of declining sales followed by an operating loss in the fiscal year ended March 2026.

Sales peaked at KRW 144.4 billion in the fiscal year ended March 2023 and then fell to KRW 137.9 billion in the fiscal year ended March 2024 (down 4.6% year on year), KRW 131.3 billion in the fiscal year ended March 2025 (down 4.7%), and KRW 119.3 billion in the fiscal year ended March 2026 (down 9.2%).

Operating profit fell from KRW 6.42 billion in the fiscal year ended March 2023 to KRW 2.5 billion in the fiscal year ended March 2024 (down 61.1%) and KRW 1.02 billion in the fiscal year ended March 2025 (down 59.2%), before the fiscal year ended March 2026 recorded an operating loss of KRW 3.71 billion.

The operating margin moved from 4.4% to 1.8% to 0.8% and then to -3.1%.

Profit attributable to owners of the parent was KRW 4.38 billion, KRW 1.72 billion, and KRW 3.11 billion in the fiscal years ended March 2023, 2024, and 2025 (up 81.5% in the last), before a net loss attributable to owners of KRW 775 million in the fiscal year ended March 2026.

Because the fiscal year ended March 2025 profit attributable to owners exceeded its operating profit, non-operating items likely played a large role, and the composition of that year's earnings is a point to check in the full-year report.

On a quarterly basis, sales peaked at KRW 31.2 billion in Q4 2025 and then fell to KRW 28.4 billion in Q1 2026 and KRW 26.7 billion in Q2 2026, down 10.5% year on year in Q2.

Operating losses were KRW 1.37 billion in Q2 2025, KRW 713 million in Q3 2025, KRW 140 million in Q4 2025, KRW 1.49 billion in Q1 2026, and KRW 869 million in Q2 2026, marking five straight loss quarters, with the loss widening in Q1 2026.

Profit attributable to owners turned to a KRW 165 million profit in Q4 2025 before posting a KRW 12.1 million loss in Q1 2026 and a KRW 307 million loss in Q2 2026. Losses attributable to owners totaled KRW 424 million over the last four quarters.

Operating cash flow was KRW -5.54 billion in the fiscal year ended March 2023, then KRW 9.87 billion, KRW 13 billion, and KRW 10 billion in the following three years, so cash generation continued even during operating losses.

05

Industry analysis

The mold material and component market Kishin serves moves with production and capital spending in finished-goods industries such as automotive and home appliances.

WISEReport's cited drivers of the 2025 decline, namely North American tariffs and weaker automotive and appliance demand, show how demand-side factors fed directly into results.

The business report describes the mold material industry as one where precision and quality improvements and volume production are central challenges. In 2023 the company outlined a push into renewable energy and electric vehicles, with lithium-ion battery molds and motor core die sets as flagship items.

However, the company's disclosures do not present a figure for how much these new businesses contributed to 2026 sales, so the mold base business still appears to drive results. The cycle position is still weak: Q2 2026 sales fell 10.5% year on year, and signs of demand recovery are not yet visible in the numbers.

On competition, domestic and overseas mold component makers coexist, and with mold bases making up roughly three-quarters of sales, the product mix is concentrated. Large equipment makers in the same machinery and equipment sector rely on project-based businesses, which limits direct comparison.

The core industry question is when demand for automotive and appliance components bottoms and whether new businesses can reduce reliance on mold bases.

The shutdown date was set for January 30, 2026, and a corporate dissolution process was disclosed as following. Whether the subsidiary's exit actually reduced operating losses will become visible in the Q3 2026 quarterly report, covering July to September, with a filing deadline around November 14, 2026.

The fiscal year ended March 2026 annual shareholders' meeting was held on June 19, 2026, followed by a disclosure on the appointment of an outside director. The new-business plan announced in 2023 for lithium-ion battery molds and motor core die sets remains an open item.

The company has not disclosed quantified annual sales or profit targets that can be checked. Three variables will shape the outlook: when demand for mold bases recovers, whether exports to the United States resume under the tariff environment, and how much fixed cost falls after the subsidiary exit.

The Q4 2026 quarterly report is due around mid-February 2027, and the business report for the fiscal year ending March 2027 is due by late June 2027. The next checkpoint at that point is whether the annual operating margin moves out of negative territory.

Bull factors

Operating cash flow has been positive since the fiscal year ended March 2024, reaching KRW 10 billion in the fiscal year ended March 2026. This points to financial capacity to get through a recovery period.

Narrowing losses and a past profit quarter

The Q2 2026 operating loss narrowed to KRW 869 million from a year earlier. Q4 2025 produced a KRW 165 million profit attributable to owners, a quarter in which the company returned to profit. The company's disclosure says the subsidiary shutdown reduces consolidated sales but can narrow operating losses. Whether this narrowing continues is central to the bullish case.

Low price against book value and new-business optionality

The share price trades at a large discount to book value. In 2023 the company said it would pursue new businesses in renewable energy and electric vehicles, including lithium-ion battery molds and motor core die sets. Bulls argue these could reduce reliance on mold bases. Actual results from these businesses have not been shown in the disclosed figures.

Bear factors

Persistent sales decline

Sales fell from KRW 144.4 billion in the fiscal year ended March 2023 to KRW 119.3 billion in the fiscal year ended March 2026. Sales declined in Q1 and Q2 2026, and Q2 2026 sales were down 10.5% year on year. There is not yet a sign that the pace of decline is easing. Continued sales decline would weaken the company's ability to absorb fixed costs.

Five straight quarters of operating losses

The company has posted operating losses for five straight quarters from Q2 2025 through Q2 2026. The Q1 2026 operating loss widened to KRW 1.49 billion. The accumulated quarterly losses carried the full fiscal year into a loss. When losses repeat quarter after quarter, the timing of any earnings recovery becomes uncertain.

Export conditions and subsidiary exit uncertainty

WISEReport attributed part of the 2025 decline to tariff-driven weakness in North American competitiveness and lower US exports. The subsidiary shutdown reduces consolidated sales, and the shutdown amount equaled 7.37% of recent consolidated sales.

How much the exit will reduce losses has not yet shown up in quarterly results, which forms the core of the bearish case.

Risk factors

Customer and export concentration

Mold bases account for 74% of sales based on Q1 2026, which concentrates the product portfolio in a single line. Demand is tied to automotive and appliance customers, so cuts in capital spending by finished-goods makers feed through to orders and sales.

North American tariffs and lower US exports were cited as drivers of the 2025 decline. Whether this concentration falls is a key item to monitor.

Subsidiary exit and financial impact

The board approved a subsidiary shutdown on December 23, 2025, and a corporate dissolution process was disclosed as following. The process could bring one-off costs or profit swings. The company expects narrower operating losses, but the size of that effect must be checked in quarterly reports. The profit and loss impact of the exit is an item to review in upcoming quarterly reports.

New-business execution and delayed recovery

The new businesses the company outlined in 2023, including lithium-ion battery molds and motor core die sets, do not show disclosed contributions in reported figures. If they fail to offset declines in mold bases, the earnings recovery could be delayed.

With three years of falling sales and five straight quarters of operating losses, execution speed is the key variable. Whether these businesses contribute to sales should be checked in the annual business report.
