Mold-base demand slump pushes Kishin into losses

Kishin's mold-base business has seen revenue fall for three consecutive fiscal years, and the company has posted an operating loss in each of the last five quarters.

02

Business structure

Kishin, listed on KOSPI in October 2007, is a mold-component maker whose core business is mold bases, the standard frames on which injection molds are built. By business mix, mold bases were 74% of revenue, plates 10%, other 6%, forged steel 5% and press parts 5% (Q1 2026 basis).

Plates are precision-machined parts used as mold core blocks and as plates for factory automation equipment. The company also runs large mold bases, forged steel for molds and mold marshalling systems.

In March 2023 it said it was pursuing new businesses in lithium-ion battery molds and motor core die sets, aimed at renewable energy and electric vehicles. Its end markets are automotive and appliance manufacturers that use molds, and demand from those industries drives mold-base sales.

In competition, low-priced Chinese products are cited as pressure on profitability. The company has an overseas unit, KISHIN VIETNAM COMPANY LIMITED. Its subsidiary Wonjin Precision (WONJIN PRECISION CO., LTD.), a mold-base maker, had operations suspended following a December 23, 2025 board decision.

Earnings analysis

Revenue for the fiscal year ended March 2026 was KRW 119.3 billion, down for a third consecutive year since the fiscal year ended March 2024, and the fiscal year ended March 2023 at KRW 144.4 billion was the largest year in the period.

Operating profit fell from KRW 6.42 billion in the fiscal year ended March 2023 to KRW 2.5 billion in the fiscal year ended March 2024 (down 61.1% year on year) and KRW 1.02 billion in the fiscal year ended March 2025 (down 59.2%), before the fiscal year ended March 2026 recorded an operating loss of KRW 3.71 billion.

The operating margin moved from 4.4% to 1.8% and 0.8%, reaching -3.1% in the fiscal year ended March 2026.

Net income attributable to controlling shareholders fell from KRW 4.38 billion to KRW 1.72 billion (down 60.8%), recovered to KRW 3.11 billion in the fiscal year ended March 2025 (up 81.5%), and then turned into a net loss attributable to controlling shareholders of KRW 775 million in the fiscal year ended March 2026.

In the fiscal year ended March 2025, operating profit declined while net income rose, which suggests non-operating items drove the direction of net income; the detail should be checked in the income statement of the annual report.

The debt ratio stayed in a 5.4% to 5.8% range without large swings, and total liabilities were KRW 13.2 billion against total equity of KRW 246.5 billion at the end of March 2026.

Operating cash flow went from -KRW 5.54 billion in the fiscal year ended March 2023 to KRW 9.87 billion, KRW 13 billion and KRW 10 billion in the following three fiscal years.

Positive operating cash flow alongside an operating loss in the fiscal year ended March 2026 needs to be read against the size of non-cash charges such as depreciation.

Quarterly, the operating loss went from KRW 713 million in Q3 2025 to KRW 140 million in Q4 2025, KRW 1.49 billion in Q1 2026, KRW 869 million in Q2 2026 and KRW 2.13 billion in Q3 2026.

Q3 2026 revenue of KRW 27.2 billion was down 8.9% year on year, and the net loss attributable to controlling shareholders widened to KRW 2.16 billion in the same quarter. In Q4 2025 the company posted an operating loss of KRW 140 million but a net profit attributable to controlling shareholders of KRW 165 million.

The operating suspension amount at subsidiary Wonjin Precision was KRW 9.68 billion on its 2024 standalone revenue basis, equal to 7.37% of the parent's consolidated revenue.

05

Industry analysis

Mold bases are the basic frames of injection molds used to make automotive parts and appliances, so demand moves with equipment investment and new-model schedules at finished-goods makers.

A company-results summary published in June 2026 said North American tariffs weakened mold competitiveness, and that softer automotive and appliance demand reduced exports to the United States and mold-base sales. The same summary pointed to low-priced Chinese competition as a drag on profitability.

An alternative reading holds that mold investment is maintained during downturns, as new-product mold demand rises and production shifts to small-lot, multi-model output. Demand swings and price competition therefore operate together.

A three-year run of revenue declines since the fiscal year ended March 2024 is consistent with a gradual slowdown in mold orders, but confirming the cycle position requires industry-wide order data.

In the company's customer structure, a precision plate announcement described mold makers as the main customers, so end-market demand from automotive and appliance producers reaches Kishin through mold makers.

Within the machinery and equipment sector, the company's exposure to mold-part demand differs from peers tied to construction equipment such as Doosan Bobcat or HD Construction Equipment. New businesses in lithium-ion battery molds and motor core die sets target electric vehicle and renewable energy demand.

Bull factors

Low leverage and a large equity base

Total liabilities were KRW 13.2 billion and total equity was KRW 246.5 billion at the end of March 2026, with the debt ratio at 5.4% for the fiscal year ended March 2026. Equity attributable to controlling shareholders makes up the full equity balance, which keeps financing dependence low. The debt ratio stayed between 5.4% and 5.8% across the four fiscal years shown.

Operating cash flow positive despite losses

Operating cash flow stayed positive, at KRW 9.87 billion in the fiscal year ended March 2024, KRW 13 billion in the fiscal year ended March 2025 and KRW 10 billion in the fiscal year ended March 2026.

The fiscal year ended March 2026 recorded an operating loss of KRW 3.71 billion even though operating cash flow was KRW 10 billion. The size of the non-cash items behind this gap should be checked in the cash flow statement of the annual report.

Expected loss reduction from subsidiary suspension

In its December 23, 2025 disclosure, the company said that suspending subsidiary Wonjin Precision would reduce consolidated revenue but was expected to shrink operating losses and improve the financial structure. The company cited a worsening business environment and continuing losses as the reasons for the suspension.

In Q4 2025, the company swung to a net profit attributable to controlling shareholders of KRW 165 million despite an operating loss of KRW 140 million.

Five straight quarters of operating losses

Operating losses have continued for five straight quarters, starting with KRW 713 million in Q3 2025. The loss was KRW 1.49 billion in Q1 2026, KRW 869 million in Q2 2026 and KRW 2.13 billion in Q3 2026, the widest in the period. The operating margin was -3.1% for the fiscal year ended March 2026.

Chinese low-price competition and North American tariffs

A company-results summary attributed deteriorating profitability to low-priced Chinese products. North American tariffs weakened mold competitiveness, and lower exports to the United States fed into lower mold-base sales. Because price pressure and volume loss act together, these factors are unlikely to reverse quickly.

Risk factors

Customer and demand concentration

Mold bases, which make up 74% of revenue, track equipment investment in industries that use molds, including automotive and appliances. If North American tariffs and lower exports to the United States persist, volume declines could last longer. Quarterly revenue trends will be the main gauge of when demand recovers.

Execution of subsidiary suspension

The suspension of Wonjin Precision and its dissolution procedures carry both a consolidated revenue decline and a smaller operating loss. The suspended amount, KRW 9.68 billion on its 2024 standalone revenue basis, equals 7.37% of the parent's consolidated revenue.

The size of the actual earnings effect stays uncertain until the completion timing and settlement amounts are disclosed.

Dividend decided amid a net loss

The total dividend of KRW 1.752 billion was decided for a fiscal year that ended with a net loss attributable to controlling shareholders of KRW 775 million. The company said the dividend content could change depending on the external audit or the shareholders meeting. Whether the dividend is maintained independently of earnings is a point to check in the next annual results.
