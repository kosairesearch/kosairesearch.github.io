Memory module expansion amid three straight quarterly losses

02

Business structure

Dreamtech makes parts for mobile and IT devices, camera modules, and bio, healthcare and convergence products. Its business divisions are IT and Mobile Communication (IMC), Bio, Healthcare and Convergence (BHC), and Camera Module (CCM).

According to a Valuefinder report, the first-half 2026 revenue mix was 47.3% IMC, 36.9% CCM and 15.8% BHC. Its main customers are smartphone and IT device makers, including Samsung Electronics.

The memory module business entered through an Indian production base that Samsung Electronics added as a new site while diversifying its production locations. The product lineup has expanded to server RDIMM, PC UDIMM and notebook SoDIMM modules.

The company plans to grow memory modules into a core medium-to-long-term revenue pillar. On the medical side, subsidiaries AIMATICS, an AI-based video recognition company, and Cardiac Insight, a US ECG analysis company, are leading new businesses, and both reduced their losses in 2025. In camera modules, Dreamtech competes with large parts makers such as Samsung Electro-Mechanics and LG Innotek.

04

Earnings analysis

Annual revenue fell from KRW 1.37 trillion in 2022 to KRW 1.03 trillion in 2023, down 24.7% from the prior year, marking the low point. It then rose to KRW 1.17 trillion in 2024, up 13.8%, and to KRW 1.23 trillion in 2025, up 4.9%.

The company attributed the 2025 increase to shipment expansion driven by early US-bound orders from customers and described pull-in demand ahead of tariff application that continued through the year.

Operating income fell from KRW 96.7 billion in 2022 to KRW 34 billion in 2023, down 64.8%, and to KRW 23.6 billion in 2024, down 30.8%, before recovering to KRW 31.4 billion in 2025, up 33.2%. The operating margin was 7.1% in 2022, 3.3% in 2023, 2.0% in 2024 and 2.5% in 2025.

Attributable net income declined from KRW 85 billion in 2022 to KRW 15.3 billion in 2023, KRW 3.89 billion in 2024 and KRW 92.22 million in 2025, a decline for three consecutive years.

Net income including non-controlling interests was KRW 85 billion in 2022, KRW 27 billion in 2023, KRW 12.7 billion in 2024 and KRW 13.2 billion in 2025. The debt-to-equity ratio rose from 56.6% in 2023 to 76.3% in 2024 and 78.3% in 2025.

Quarterly revenue went from KRW 322.5 billion in the third quarter of 2025 to KRW 275.5 billion in the fourth quarter, down 14.6% from the prior quarter, then KRW 260.7 billion in the first quarter of 2026 and KRW 257.3 billion in the second quarter, before rising to KRW 325.9 billion in the third quarter, up 26.6% from the prior quarter.

Operating income fell to KRW 2.05 billion in the fourth quarter of 2025, KRW 413 million in the first quarter of 2026 and KRW 105 million in the second quarter, then rose to KRW 13.5 billion in the third quarter.

Attributable net income turned from a KRW 1.24 billion profit in the fourth quarter of 2025 to an attributable net loss of KRW 6.32 billion in the first quarter of 2026, followed by a KRW 5.5 billion loss in the second quarter and a KRW 8.51 billion loss in the third quarter.

In the third quarter operating income increased while the net loss widened, so the two measures moved in different directions. The non-operating items and tax factors behind that gap can be checked in the notes to the 2026 annual report.

05

Industry analysis

Memory modules are assembled DRAM products for server and PC use, and demand has risen as global memory supply has remained tight.

Valuefinder analysed that both end customers and Samsung Electronics have an urgent need to secure production capacity, which moved the expansion of Dreamtech's Indian plant from 2027 forward to September 2026.

The company is supplying Indian and Chinese server makers first to build a quality track record, then pursuing approvals from North American big-tech customers in sequence. Its path in memory modules therefore depends on the pace of North American approvals.

On the demand side, IMC and CCM are tied to smartphone demand and customers' inventory cycles. Early US-bound orders tied to tariff concerns boosted 2025 revenue, and whether that effect carries into 2026 depends on customers' US inventory flows.

In camera modules, the company competes with large parts makers such as Samsung Electro-Mechanics and LG Innotek, so changes in its share there are a key point to monitor.

In memory modules, which is a new business competing with larger module makers, customer approvals and production stability in the early stage will shape its position.

Outlook

The company has said it expects its major new businesses to begin contributing to earnings in earnest from 2026. Its memory module plan calls for full-scale supply of server RDIMM modules to global big-tech customers during 2026.

Its 2026 disclosure of progress under its corporate value-enhancement plan also listed the start of DRAM memory module mass production as a financial-area target.

Revenue at the Indian subsidiary rose 49.4% from KRW 9.1 billion in the first quarter of 2026 to KRW 13.6 billion in the second quarter, according to Valuefinder.

The same report estimated memory module revenue of at least KRW 100 billion in 2026 and a scenario of about KRW 400 billion in 2027 once the expanded lines run at full capacity. These are independent research estimates, not company guidance.

With qualification testing reportedly completed at two of six North American big-tech customers, approvals at the remaining four and whether mass production starts in the fourth quarter of 2026 are the near-term items to watch.

A first shipment of memory modules for North American AI data centers has also been reported. Because net losses have continued through the third quarter, whether the new businesses translate into operating margin improvement is the central question for the outlook.

Bull factors

Widening memory module customer base

After being selected as a vendor for Samsung Electronics' DS division, Dreamtech obtained approvals for key products such as server RDIMM and PC UDIMM modules in sequence. Valuefinder reported that qualification testing has been completed at two of six North American big-tech customers.

The same report estimated 2026 memory module revenue at KRW 100 billion or more. This is an independent research estimate, not company guidance.

Operating income recovery

Operating income for 2025 was KRW 31.4 billion, up 33.2% from the prior year, moving away from the 2024 low. The operating margin also rose from 2.0% in 2024 to 2.5% in 2025. Operating income in the third quarter of 2026 was KRW 13.5 billion, up 28.1% from a year earlier.

The company cited reduced subsidiary losses and visible results from new businesses as factors behind the improvement.

Stronger shareholder return policy

After the cancellation deadline for treasury shares was written into the articles of incorporation, an actual cancellation was carried out in May 2026. A KRW 5 billion treasury share purchase trust agreement signed in July 2026 had 89.8% executed as of August 26, 2026. Shareholder return measures are continuing through both disclosure and execution.

Bear factors

Three consecutive quarters of net losses

Attributable net losses were KRW 6.32 billion in the first quarter of 2026, KRW 5.5 billion in the second quarter and KRW 8.51 billion in the third quarter, continuing for three quarters. The cumulative attributable net loss over the last four quarters is KRW 19.1 billion.

Operating income rose to KRW 13.5 billion in the third quarter, yet the net loss widened, so the operating recovery has not yet carried through to net income.

Thin operating margin and volatile quarterly revenue

Operating income in the second quarter of 2026 was KRW 105 million, the lowest quarterly level in the period shown. First-quarter 2026 revenue of KRW 260.7 billion was down 23.6% from the same quarter a year earlier, according to FnGuide data. The operating margin was 2.0% in 2024 and 2.5% in 2025, which is thin. Even with a large revenue base, the thin margin leaves quarterly results volatile.

Rising financial leverage

The debt-to-equity ratio has trended upward since 2023, adding to balance-sheet pressure. Total liabilities at the end of 2025 were KRW 379 billion, close to total equity of KRW 484.2 billion.

If shareholder returns and capital spending continue while net losses persist, changes in financial headroom will need to be watched. Operating cash flow recovered to KRW 95.9 billion in 2025 but remained below the KRW 143.4 billion recorded in 2022.

Risk factors

Customer demand and tariff factors

Revenue growth in 2025 relied substantially on early orders ahead of US tariffs and pull-in demand. Whether that demand continues in 2026 depends on customer inventories and US policy. Quarterly revenue fell in the first two quarters of 2026 before rebounding in the third, which shows how large demand swings can be. Revenue can depend on the timing of orders from particular customers.

Memory module approvals and price swings

Qualification testing has reportedly been completed at two of six North American big-tech customers, and the pace of approvals at the others still needs to be confirmed. If the Indian plant expansion does not run on schedule, the benefit of added capacity will be delayed.

Memory price movements can affect both customer orders and product margins at the same time. If memory modules do not grow as expected, the new business will contribute less to revenue.

Financial structure and return capacity

Attributable net losses over the last four quarters total KRW 19.1 billion, which weakens the company's ability to generate funds from earnings. The debt-to-equity ratio of 78.3% in 2025 is higher than in 2023. As treasury share cancellations and trust purchases continue, the burden on shareholder returns may grow. The pace of operating cash flow recovery and borrowing capacity are both items to watch.

Overall view

Dreamtech's revenue has grown for two consecutive years since the 2023 low, and operating income for 2025 recovered to KRW 31.4 billion, up 33.2%. The operating margin of 2.5%, however, remains below the 7.1% of 2022.

Attributable net losses have continued from the first quarter through the third quarter of 2026, and the cumulative loss over the last four quarters is KRW 19.1 billion.

In memory modules, the company has made progress through its selection as a Samsung Electronics vendor, mass production starting in November 2025, and reported completion of qualification testing at two North American big-tech customers.

Revenue estimates for the segment, however, rest on independent research projections. Shareholder returns have been strengthened mainly through treasury share cancellations, while the debt-to-equity ratio has risen to 78.3%.

The items to confirm next are whether mass production starts in the fourth quarter, additional North American approvals, and the net income structure in the 2026 annual report and its notes.
