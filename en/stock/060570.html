02

Business structure

Dreamus Company operates the music streaming platform FLO and runs a 'music IP 360' structure that links planning, distribution, live performance, and merchandise (MD) around music intellectual property.

The company said the IP business accounted for about 65% of revenue in 2025 and about 62% in the first half of 2026, when music distribution, MD, and performances were the main sources.

Media reports put first-half 2025 album-related content distribution at KRW 58.8 billion and performance-related product sales at KRW 46.1 billion. In 2025 the company liquidated Studio Dolphin, sold its device business unit, and divested its iriver business unit, refocusing on music and entertainment.

The overseas share of revenue expanded to more than one-quarter in 2025, led by Japan performance at its subsidiary Life Design Company. It partnered with the Japanese music distributor RecoChoku to secure a J-pop distribution network and opened the global fan store FLO Shop in January 2026.

The domestic music streaming market is described as saturated, with the company competing against Melon, Genie Music, YouTube Music, and Spotify. The company has framed its direction as a fandom-based music platform linking listening, fan communities, merchandise purchases, and concert ticketing.

Spun off from iriver, a music subsidiary of SK Telecom, in 2018, the company's largest shareholder changed from SK Square to a fandom business company in 2025.

04

Earnings analysis

On an annual basis, revenue went from KRW 274.4 billion in 2022 to KRW 270.3 billion in 2023 (down 1.5% year on year), KRW 225.1 billion in 2024 (down 16.7% year on year), and KRW 225 billion in 2025 (no change year on year), with revenue declining for three consecutive years from 2023 through 2025.

Among 2022 to 2025, 2022 was the largest year for revenue and 2025 was the smallest. Operating losses were KRW 8.67 billion in 2022, KRW 3.49 billion in 2023, and KRW 3.15 billion in 2024, a continued loss run, before operating profit of KRW 593 million in 2025 marked a turnaround to profit.

The operating margin moved from -3.2% in 2022 to -1.3% in 2023 and -1.4% in 2024, before reaching 0.3% in 2025.

Net income attributable to controlling shareholders was KRW 27.3 billion in 2022, then a net loss of KRW 2.9 billion in 2023 (a turn to loss), and a deeper net loss of KRW 17.8 billion in 2024, before a KRW 2.6 billion profit in 2025 marked a turnaround.

Net income including non-controlling interests was also KRW 2.6 billion in 2025. The company attributed the first-half 2025 improvement to the exit from non-core businesses, and said a rise in the value of its stake in Titan Contents was also reflected in net income.

At the end of 2025, total equity was KRW 143.9 billion, all attributable to controlling shareholders, and total liabilities were KRW 78.8 billion, with the debt ratio falling from 68.6% in 2024 to 54.8% in 2025.

Operating cash flow, however, moved from an inflow of KRW 32 billion in 2024 to an outflow of KRW 16.5 billion in 2025.

Quarterly revenue peaked at KRW 61 billion in the fourth quarter of 2025, up 3.4% from the prior quarter, and that quarter swung to an operating loss of KRW 66.38 million and a net loss attributable to controlling shareholders of KRW 1.2 billion.

The first quarter of 2026 brought revenue of KRW 48.6 billion (down 20.4% quarter on quarter) and an operating profit of KRW 145 million, while the second quarter brought revenue of KRW 42.8 billion (down 12.0%), an operating profit of KRW 890 million (up 513.9%), and a net income attributable to controlling shareholders of KRW 1.37 billion.

The third quarter of 2026 brought revenue of KRW 38.1 billion (down 10.9% quarter on quarter and down 35.4% year on year), an operating profit of KRW 76.8 million (down 91.4% quarter on quarter), and a net loss attributable to controlling shareholders of KRW 525 million, a turn to loss.

Revenue has declined for three consecutive quarters from the first quarter to the third quarter of 2026, and the cumulative net loss attributable to controlling shareholders over the last four quarters is KRW 252 million.

05

Industry analysis

The domestic music streaming market is described as saturated, and service differentiation has become harder amid competition among large players including Melon, Genie Music, YouTube Music, and Spotify.

Music streaming has been cited as a reason the company ran losses for several years, given subscriber fatigue and a low revenue-sharing structure.

Some in the industry argue that per-stream economics leave limited room for streaming revenue and that fandom platform models, which can add merchandise, content, and live-event revenue, offer a way to diversify income.

Under pressure from global music platforms, the question for domestic players is how they design the fan experience after listening. The company is widening its work with K-pop agencies by combining music distribution, live performances, and merchandise, and it is pursuing Japanese J-pop IP overseas.

Compared with large entertainment groups that build artist IP directly, Dreamus's position is closer to providing distribution, platform, and merchandise functions.

Business models differ across peers in overseas revenue share and platform economics, so comparisons are more meaningful at the level of business structure.

Outlook

The company plans to expand FLO into a fandom business platform that connects music services with marketing. After opening the global fan store FLO Shop in January 2026, it plans to introduce integrated bstage login within FLO and add global platform linkage features for artists and fans in sequence.

Overseas, it is securing more J-pop IP and plans to expand into Japan, the United States, and Southeast Asia in that order within 2026. The company reported first-half 2026 revenue of KRW 91.4 billion and operating profit of KRW 1.0 billion, its second consecutive year of profit, in its semiannual report.

However, operating profit fell to KRW 76.8 million in the third quarter of 2026 and the company swung to a net loss attributable to controlling shareholders of KRW 525 million, so whether the first-half profit run continued into the second half will be confirmed in the next periodic report.

Revenue has declined for three consecutive quarters from the fourth quarter of 2025 peak through the third quarter of 2026, and whether this reverses through the fandom platform shift is the key variable.

The company's stated goal of growing revenue and improving profit at the same time has not yet been shown in disclosed results. The next checkpoint is the annual revenue and operating profit trend in the 2026 annual report, along with whether the overseas revenue share rises above one-quarter.

The second-quarter 2026 operating profit of KRW 890 million, up 513.9% quarter on quarter, is read as a sign of earnings recovery.

Overseas expansion and fandom platform shift

The fourth quarter of 2025 had an operating loss of KRW 66.38 million and a net loss attributable to controlling shareholders of KRW 1.2 billion. With a cumulative net loss of KRW 252 million over the last four quarters, the profit run has not been stable at the quarterly level.

Risk factors

Platform competition and revenue model

The domestic streaming market is saturated, and the company competes with Melon, Genie Music, YouTube Music, and Spotify. Subscriber fatigue and a low revenue share in streaming contributed to losses over several years.

If the fandom platform shift does not generate secondary revenue as expected, the revenue decline could continue.

Ownership change and financial base

The largest shareholder changed from SK Square to a fandom business company in 2025, moving the company toward an independent management structure. A 2025 media report noted the acquirer's asset base was smaller than the company it acquired.

Total liabilities were KRW 78.8 billion at the end of 2025, and whether the company can fund overseas expansion and platform investment within that financial base is a risk factor.

Dependence on overseas expansion

The overseas share of revenue exceeded one-quarter in 2025 and depends heavily on the Japanese subsidiary. Expansion into the United States and Southeast Asia has been reported as a plan for 2026. Overseas revenue may not grow as expected depending on local IP acquisition and partner performance.

Overall view

Dreamus Company's revenue fell from KRW 274.4 billion in 2022 to KRW 225 billion in 2025, yet the company turned profitable in 2025 with operating profit of KRW 593 million and net income attributable to controlling shareholders of KRW 2.6 billion.

Its music IP business accounts for more than 60% of revenue, and the overseas share is growing, led by Japan.

However, the company swung to a net loss attributable to controlling shareholders of KRW 525 million in the third quarter of 2026 despite positive operating profit of KRW 76.8 million, and the cumulative net loss over the last four quarters is KRW 252 million.

Operating cash flow was an outflow of KRW 16.5 billion in 2025. The domestic streaming market is saturated and competitive, so whether the fandom platform shift can offset revenue declines is the central variable.

Price-to-book sits below book value while the price-to-earnings ratio cannot be calculated, which makes earnings stability the reference point for the next assessment.

Strengths include the return to profit and overseas and fandom expansion, while weaknesses include the revenue decline, the quarterly swing to loss, and cash outflows.
