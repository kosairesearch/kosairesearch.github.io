Report overview

Q3 Loss Puts Fuel Costs and Maintenance in Focus

Korea District Heating restored profit in 2025, with operating profit up 61.5%, but a KRW 7.92 billion operating loss in the third quarter of 2026 has put fuel costs and generator maintenance at the center of the earnings outlook.

02

Business structure

Korea District Heating runs a district heat business that supplies heat to regions and a power business that sells electricity generated at its combined heat and power (CHP) plants. Because CHP plants produce heat and power from the same fuel, earnings from both businesses are tied to plant utilization.

According to BizTribune, cumulative heat business revenue for January to September 2026 was KRW 1.23 trillion, down 2.8% year on year, and heat business operating profit for the same period was KRW 141.6 billion, down 14.6%.

A report on Q1 2025 results said heat sales volume rose 6.1% and power sales volume rose 29.5%, while heat unit prices fell 10.0% and power unit prices fell 13.1%. In other words, volume growth had to offset lower unit prices for profits to rise.

Power business margins move with the mix of LNG costs, the system marginal price (SMP), and sales volume. BizTribune reported that in Q3 2026 rising LNG prices, a lower SMP and weaker sales volume together compressed electricity sales margins.

A May 2025 report by Eugene Investment & Securities said that after completion of the Daegu and Cheongju plants, the plants moved up to about the top ten in dispatch order and an individual tariff scheme widened energy margins.

On the heat side, recovery of receivables is linked to the earnings structure, and the same report said heat business receivables had turned to a decline, falling to KRW 536.5 billion.

Because district heating is supplied within regional networks, the main competition is with other generators in the power market over sales prices and volumes.

04

Earnings analysis

Annual revenue went from KRW 4.17 trillion in 2022 to KRW 3.95 trillion in 2023, down 5.3%, and KRW 3.57 trillion in 2024, down 9.7%, before recovering to KRW 4 trillion in 2025, up 12.0%. Among 2022 to 2025, 2022 was the largest year for revenue and 2024 the smallest.

Operating performance moved from an operating loss of KRW 403.9 billion in 2022 to operating profit of KRW 314.7 billion in 2023, a swing to profit.

Operating profit then rose to KRW 327.9 billion in 2024, up 4.2%, and to KRW 529.6 billion in 2025, up 61.5%, so operating profit increased for two consecutive years after the 2023 low. The operating margin moved from -9.7% in 2022 to 8.0% in 2023, 9.2% in 2024 and 13.2% in 2025.

Controlling-shareholder net income swung from a loss of KRW 183.9 billion in 2022 to net income of KRW 199.4 billion in 2023, then rose 5.4% to KRW 210.2 billion in 2024 and 61.2% to KRW 338.9 billion in 2025, again increasing for two consecutive years after the 2023 low.

Net income including non-controlling interests was KRW 209.9 billion in 2024 and KRW 338.9 billion in 2025, slightly different from controlling-shareholder net income in 2024. Operating cash flow went from negative KRW 141.8 billion in 2023 to KRW 416.7 billion in 2024 and KRW 677.3 billion in 2025.

The debt ratio fell from 348.6% in 2022 to 257.6% in 2025, yet total liabilities of KRW 5.93 trillion at end-2025 remained above total equity of KRW 2.3 trillion. On a quarterly basis, Q3 2025 revenue was KRW 758.2 billion with operating profit of KRW 89.4 billion.

Q4 2025 revenue rose 37.2% quarter on quarter to KRW 1.04 trillion, and operating profit rose 41.0% to KRW 126 billion. Q1 2026 revenue reached KRW 1.43 trillion with operating profit of KRW 320.2 billion, the peak of the period.

Q2 2026 revenue fell 54.5% quarter on quarter to KRW 653.1 billion and operating profit fell 89.7% to KRW 33.1 billion. Q3 2026 revenue was KRW 606.2 billion, down 20.0% year on year, and the company posted an operating loss of KRW 7.92 billion. Revenue declined for two consecutive quarters after the Q1 2026 peak.

Controlling-shareholder net income for the last four quarters, from Q4 2025 to Q3 2026, totaled KRW 288 billion. Reports attributed the Q3 loss to concentrated generator maintenance and to narrower power sales margins from higher LNG costs. The reports did not mention a specific one-off item.

05

Industry analysis

In the power market, the Q3 2026 profit squeeze reflects rising LNG prices and a lower system marginal price (SMP) at the same time. iM Securities expects profitability to decline because LNG cost increases are not fully reflected in the SMP. Fuel costs are also seen as the largest burden inside the company.

According to Asia Today, the CEO told staff at the end of Q3 that September fuel costs were up 39% year on year and urged a proactive response to a tougher second-half business environment. The same report also cited interest rate increases among the second-half conditions.

Power earnings depend on generator utilization, dispatch order and wholesale electricity prices, so maintenance schedules strongly influence quarterly results.

Several 2025 broker reports named the company the top utility pick in its sector on the strength of power business improvement, though those views are from 2025. Hana Securities at the time described the stability of regulated businesses and the upside of non-regulated businesses as the company's combined strengths.

The heat business operates in regional supply areas under tariff and settlement rules, so it is less directly exposed to wholesale price swings, but the timing of settlements can change how earnings are recognized.

The company combines regional heat supply with electricity sales, which makes its exposure to power prices and fuel costs distinct from that of other energy companies.

06

Outlook

The company told staff that the second-half 2026 business environment is not easy. Broker views are split. On November 11, 2026, iM Securities lowered its target price from KRW 135,000 to KRW 110,000 while keeping its rating, and BizTribune reported that brokers had lowered target prices to reflect earnings revisions.

By contrast, 2025 broker reports pointed to growth led by the power business and to receivable recovery. Replacement and tariff timing are also key checks. A 2025 report said the 512MW Hwaseong CHP plant could move to an individual tariff scheme from 2027.

The Suwon facility is set to convert from heavy-oil-based equipment to a 141MW LNG CHP plant, with Hana Securities citing 2028 and Eugene Investment & Securities citing 2029. Because these timelines come from 2025 sources, they should be checked against the latest disclosures.

Q4 2026 results have not yet been released in a periodic report. The next periodic report is the 2026 annual report, where the full-year operating margin and fuel cost burden will be the key checks. Until then, the path of LNG prices and the SMP after September will determine quarterly margins.

08

Bull factors

Power plant upgrades

A 2025 report said completion of the Daegu and Cheongju plants moved them up the dispatch order to about the top ten, and that the individual tariff scheme widened energy margins.

The Hwaseong CHP plant was reported to be able to move to that scheme from 2027, and the Suwon facility is set to be replaced with an LNG CHP plant, so the power asset base may keep improving.

Two years of earnings growth and higher margins

Operating profit increased for two consecutive years after the 2023 low, and the operating margin rose from 8.0% in 2023 to 13.2% in 2025. Controlling-shareholder net income also increased for two consecutive years over the same span. Operating cash flow reached KRW 677.3 billion in 2025, a sharp improvement from the 2023 outflow.

Receivable recovery and dividend capacity

A May 2025 Eugene Investment & Securities report said heat business receivables had turned to a decline, falling to KRW 536.5 billion. Hana Securities at the time said receivable recovery could expand dividend funding and raise the payout ratio. The debt ratio falling from 348.6% in 2022 to 257.6% in 2025 is also cited as evidence of financial room.

09

Bear factors

Q3 loss and maintenance clustering

Q3 2026 produced an operating loss of KRW 7.92 billion and a controlling-shareholder net loss of KRW 16.7 billion, reversing the profit recorded in Q3 2025. Major generator maintenance clustered in one quarter, lowering CHP plant utilization.

Revenue declined in both Q2 and Q3 2026, and operating profit fell to KRW 33.1 billion in Q2 before turning to a loss in Q3.

Fuel cost increases and the SMP gap

iM Securities cut its target price, arguing that LNG cost increases are not fully passed through to the SMP and that profitability will decline. If fuel costs cannot be passed through to power sales prices, the margin squeeze becomes a structural weakness.

Target cuts and interest rate pressure

BizTribune reported that brokers lowered target prices to reflect earnings revisions. Asia Today cited interest rate increases as a second-half burden. Total liabilities of KRW 5.93 trillion at end-2025, above total equity, could translate into higher interest costs in a rising-rate environment.

10

Risk factors

Fuel costs and power prices

When LNG prices rise and the SMP falls, electricity sales margins narrow, as Q3 2026 results showed. How quickly fuel cost changes can be reflected in power sales prices will determine the size of quarterly earnings swings.

Plant utilization and maintenance timing

When major generator maintenance clusters in one quarter, utilization falls and earnings drop sharply. Replacement of the Hwaseong and Suwon facilities has been reported for dates between 2027 and 2029, which vary by source. Construction delays would postpone the benefits of these upgrades.

Interest rates and financial structure

Total liabilities at end-2025 were KRW 5.93 trillion, above total equity of KRW 2.3 trillion. If interest rates keep rising, financing costs could weigh on net income. A delay in receivable collection would also postpone cash inflows.

Overall view

Korea District Heating moved to profit in 2023, and operating profit and controlling-shareholder net income rose for two consecutive years in 2024 and 2025, with the operating margin reaching 13.2% in 2025.

However, a Q3 2026 operating loss of KRW 7.92 billion and a controlling-shareholder net loss of KRW 16.7 billion reversed that trend. Reports attributed the loss to concentrated generator maintenance and narrower power sales margins from higher LNG costs.

The CEO said September fuel costs were up 39% year on year and that the second-half environment is difficult.

Broker views are split. iM Securities lowered its target price to KRW 110,000 on November 11, 2026 while keeping its rating, and 2025 broker reports were positive on receivable recovery and power asset upgrades.

On the financial side, the debt ratio fell to 257.6% in 2025, but total liabilities still exceed total equity. The next checkpoints are the Q4 2026 preliminary results and the operating margin and fuel cost burden in the 2026 annual report.
