02

Business structure

Korean Reinsurance Company was founded in 1963 and is the only dedicated reinsurance company in Korea.

Reinsurance transfers and spreads part of the insurance risk that primary insurers take on, and the company's income divides into domestic business with Korean primary insurers and overseas business, known as foreign reinsurance acceptance, with overseas primary insurers.

The overseas share of insurance revenue rose to 44% in 2025 from 41% in 2024, while the domestic share fell 3 percentage points to 56%. The overseas acceptance share roughly doubled from 22% in 2014 to 41% in 2024.

As of the end of the third quarter of 2025, overseas operations comprised 4 subsidiaries, 5 branches and 3 representative offices, 12 locations in total.

The combined profit of the 4 overseas subsidiaries in Hong Kong, the United Kingdom, Switzerland and the United States was KRW 131 billion at that point, more than triple the level at the end of the prior year.

In November 2025 the company obtained approval from the India International Financial Services Centres Authority to open a reinsurance branch. Its customers are domestic and overseas primary insurers, and volumes depend on their underwriting policies and cession levels.

AM Best ranked the company eighth among global reinsurers on an IFRS 17 basis in its August 2026 list, but the scale gap with leaders such as Munich Re, Swiss Re and Hannover Re remains wide based on 2025 reinsurance revenue.

Domestically, the company is widening co-developed long-term non-life and life products and co-reinsurance acceptance to grow insurance revenue.

04

Earnings analysis

Insurance revenue moved from KRW 5.76 trillion in 2022 to KRW 5.17 trillion in 2023, KRW 5.14 trillion in 2024 and KRW 4.98 trillion in 2025.

The 2023 figure was down 10.3% year on year, the 2024 figure down 0.6% and the 2025 figure down 3.1%, so insurance revenue declined for three consecutive years from 2023 through 2025. Among the years from 2022 to 2025, 2022 was the largest and 2025 the smallest.

Over the same period operating profit was KRW 352.4 billion, KRW 363.7 billion, KRW 405.4 billion and KRW 470.9 billion, rising for three consecutive years from 2023 through 2025, with 2025 the largest year in the period.

Profit attributable to owners rose from KRW 278.5 billion to KRW 322 billion and increased for three consecutive years from 2023 through 2025. Insurance revenue fell while operating profit rose because operating profit as a share of insurance revenue climbed from about 6.1% in 2022 to about 9.5% in 2025.

The debt-to-equity ratio fell from 305.7% in 2022 to 270.9% in 2023, then rose to 281.1% in 2024 and 287.1% in 2025. Total equity at the end of 2025 was KRW 3.68 trillion and total liabilities were KRW 10.6 trillion.

Operating cash flow went from KRW 1.31 trillion in 2022 to KRW 638.6 billion in 2023, then recovered to KRW 1.15 trillion in 2024 and KRW 1.67 trillion in 2025.

Quarterly insurance revenue rose from KRW 1.23 trillion in the third quarter of 2025 to KRW 1.29 trillion in the fourth quarter, up 4.8% from the prior quarter, fell 10.5% to KRW 1.15 trillion in the first quarter of 2026, then rebounded 14.8% to KRW 1.32 trillion in the second quarter.

The third quarter of 2026 came in at KRW 1.27 trillion, down 4.2% from the prior quarter but up 3.2% year on year.

Operating profit fell from KRW 153.9 billion in the third quarter of 2025 to KRW 55.6 billion in the fourth quarter, down 63.8% from the prior quarter, then peaked at KRW 291.2 billion in the first quarter of 2026, up 423.4% from the prior quarter.

It then declined for two consecutive quarters to KRW 175 billion in the second quarter and KRW 68.4 billion in the third quarter, the latter down 55.5% year on year.

In the third quarter of 2026, insurance revenue rose year on year while operating profit fell, so operating profit as a share of insurance revenue dropped from about 12.5% in the third quarter of 2025 to about 5.4%.

Profit attributable to owners peaked at KRW 214.6 billion in the first quarter of 2026, then declined for two consecutive quarters to KRW 132.6 billion and KRW 51.5 billion. The first quarter accounts for nearly half of the KRW 438.2 billion total over the latest four quarters.

In its preliminary disclosure dated November 13, 2026, the company attributed higher revenue to growth in overseas insurance revenue and to larger overseas bond valuations from a stronger exchange rate, and attributed higher net profit to a profitability-focused portfolio adjustment and fewer large claims.

The same preliminary disclosure showed third-quarter revenue excluding foreign exchange effects at KRW 1.44 trillion, down 5.2% year on year (preliminary).

05

Industry analysis

Korean Reinsurance Company is the only dedicated reinsurance company in Korea and has maintained a majority share of the domestic reinsurance market. However, a Korea Insurance News report dated September 21, 2026 said its domestic market share fell 12.4 percentage points over three years.

In global comparison, AM Best's 2025 reinsurance revenue figures show Munich Re at about USD 35.4 billion, Swiss Re in the USD 34 billion range and Hannover Re in the USD 31 billion range, leaving a wide scale gap with the top three.

The same report cited an outside view that Asian specialist reinsurers struggle to secure scale and profitability at the same time as large global players.

In response, the company said it is not shrinking but going through a restructuring of its business, with profitability-focused selective underwriting and an adjusted operating environment.

Reinsurance demand depends on primary insurers' underwriting capacity, cession strategy and whether major catastrophes occur, and reinsurance pricing generally rises after large losses and eases in quieter periods.

Domestic non-life insurers such as Samsung Fire & Marine Insurance, DB Insurance and HYUNDAI MARINE&FIRE INSURANCE CO are primary-insurer clients whose underwriting volumes influence domestic cession volumes.

The company cited fewer large claims in the third quarter of 2026 as a driver of higher net profit, which points to portfolio effects rather than the market cycle. Exchange rates are a shared industry variable, and the company said currency hedging offsets related costs to minimize the effect on net profit.

06

Outlook

The company has outlined plans to expand overseas reinsurance acceptance centered on Europe and North America, based on 2026 reports. It obtained approval for a reinsurance branch in India and, according to a January 2026 report, planned to start operations there in April 2026.

An earlier target of reaching a 50% overseas acceptance share by 2030 was reset to a Smart Growth approach, which an executive said reflects a view that the original goal was too aggressive.

The same explanation pointed to stronger exposure checks through natural catastrophe modeling and tighter regional accumulation limits. The company has also said it will use AX and DX to raise efficiency across underwriting and investment operations.

The third quarter of 2026 saw overseas insurance revenue growth cited as a driver, yet operating profit fell 55.5% year on year, so whether overseas expansion translates into margin recovery is the key point to watch.

Whether currency hedging continues to limit the exchange-rate effect on net profit is another item to track. In the 2026 annual report, the next periodic filing, the full-year overseas acceptance share and operating margin can be checked against 44% and about 9.5% for 2025.

The fourth-quarter 2026 preliminary result can be compared with the third-quarter operating profit of KRW 68.4 billion.

Bull factors

Three years of rising annual profit

Operating profit rose from KRW 352.4 billion in 2022 to KRW 470.9 billion in 2025 and increased for three consecutive years from 2023 through 2025. Profit attributable to owners also rose for three consecutive years to KRW 322 billion in 2025.

The first quarter of 2026 marked the quarterly peak, with operating profit of KRW 291.2 billion and profit attributable to owners of KRW 214.6 billion. The combination of falling insurance revenue and rising profit is cited as a bullish point.

Overseas portfolio shift

The overseas share of insurance revenue rose from 41% in 2024 to 44% in 2025, while the domestic share fell to 56%. Combined profit of the four overseas subsidiaries reached KRW 131 billion at the end of the third quarter of 2025, more than triple the prior year-end level. The company presents overseas expansion as a means of diversifying earnings.

Cash generation and portfolio adjustment

Operating cash flow rebounded from KRW 638.6 billion in 2023 to KRW 1.15 trillion in 2024 and KRW 1.67 trillion in 2025. In its preliminary disclosure dated November 13, 2026, the company said a profitability-focused portfolio adjustment and fewer large claims supported net profit. The company also said currency hedging offsets related costs to limit the impact on net profit.

Bear factors

Softening quarterly earnings

Operating profit fell for two consecutive quarters to KRW 175 billion in the second quarter and KRW 68.4 billion in the third quarter of 2026. The third-quarter figure was down 55.5% year on year, and profit attributable to owners of KRW 51.5 billion declined 36.3%.

Insurance revenue rose 3.2% year on year, but the steeper profit decline lowered operating profit as a share of insurance revenue. Because the first quarter of 2026 makes up a large part of the latest four-quarter total, the trend after that quarter is a bearish point.

Scale and market share

Insurance revenue declined for three consecutive years from 2023 through 2025. A Korea Insurance News report said domestic reinsurance market share fell 12.4 percentage points over three years. The company ranks eighth globally on AM Best's list, but the scale gap with leaders such as Munich Re and Swiss Re is wide. Third-quarter 2026 revenue excluding foreign exchange effects fell 5.2% year on year (preliminary).

Balance sheet leverage

The debt-to-equity ratio rose from 270.9% in 2023 to 281.1% in 2024 and 287.1% in 2025. Total liabilities at the end of 2025 were KRW 10.6 trillion against total equity of KRW 3.68 trillion.

With a larger debt load relative to equity, changes in interest rates, exchange rates and investment asset valuations can have a bigger effect on capital.

Risk factors

Large losses and natural catastrophes

Reinsurance results depend heavily on the frequency of major catastrophes and large losses. The company cited fewer large claims as a driver of higher net profit in the third quarter of 2026, which means the same item can reverse direction from quarter to quarter.

The jump in profit attributable to owners to KRW 214.6 billion in the first quarter of 2026, followed by declines, shows that this volatility appears directly in quarterly results.

Exchange rates and revenue measures

Exchange-rate changes flow into revenue, but the company says currency hedging offsets related costs to limit the effect on net profit. Third-quarter 2026 revenue excluding foreign exchange effects fell 5.2% year on year in the preliminary figures, so revenue and profit measures can move in different directions.

As the overseas acceptance share rises, managing foreign-currency assets and liabilities becomes more important.

Execution of overseas expansion

The 2030 target of a 50% overseas acceptance share has been reset to a Smart Growth approach. The profit contribution of new locations such as the India branch can only be measured in detail once periodic reports are published.

Outside commentary has questioned whether Asian specialist reinsurers can secure both scale and profitability against large global players, so proving the profitability of overseas expansion remains a task.

Overall view

Korean Reinsurance Company's insurance revenue fell for three consecutive years from 2023, yet operating profit and profit attributable to owners rose over the same period, showing an improving annual earnings pattern.

The overseas acceptance share rose to 44% in 2025 and overseas subsidiary profit increased, which the company cites as evidence of its portfolio shift.

On the other side, operating profit and profit attributable to owners declined for two consecutive quarters in the second and third quarters of 2026, so the quarterly trend has weakened since the first-quarter peak.

A 12.4 percentage point fall in domestic market share over three years and a debt-to-equity ratio that has risen since 2023 are also bearish factors.

Market capitalization sitting below total equity at the end of 2025 is a factual comparison against net asset value, and given the volatility of reinsurance earnings its meaning should be read alongside the earnings trend.

The next checkpoints are the preliminary fourth-quarter 2026 results, the full-year insurance revenue and operating profit in the 2026 annual report, and first-quarter 2027 results. This report offers no view on share price direction or investment rating and presents bullish and bearish factors with equal weight.
