Finetek Returns to Q3 Profit; Order Conversion in Focus

Finetek swung to an operating profit of KRW 2.17 billion in Q3 2026 after an annual operating loss in 2025, and how far Samsung Display orders convert into revenue in later quarters is the next checkpoint.

  1. 1

    Q3 2026 revenue reached KRW 24.6 billion, up 68.1% year on year, and operating profit turned positive at KRW 2.17 billion.

  2. 2

  3. 3

  4. 4

  5. 5

02

Business structure

Finetek was founded in 2008 to make display parts and equipment as well as secondary-battery manufacturing equipment, and listed on KOSDAQ in 2015. Its business divides into parts and equipment, with parts at 56% and equipment at 44% of revenue in Q2 2026 according to Butler company data.

The core is display manufacturing equipment, supplied to Samsung-affiliated and Chinese display makers, according to a securities firm company profile.

In secondary-battery equipment, the company won orders on the strength of its existing design and manufacturing capability, and it produces Cap-Assy equipment and is registered as a Samsung SDI supplier, according to a 2025 securities research note. The business scope has been widening.

Its articles of incorporation added secondary-battery, process-control and welding businesses in 2025, followed by intelligent-robot and robot-parts businesses in 2026. The company says it is accelerating commercialization of new businesses, including food-tech robots.

Its consolidated group includes five unlisted subsidiaries, one of which is classified as a major subsidiary. In display equipment, Samsung Display is a prominent customer. Secondary-battery equipment orders vary sharply with customers' facility investment timing. Because the company is small, a single customer's investment decision feeds directly into revenue swings.

04

Earnings analysis

On a disclosed basis, annual revenue fell from KRW 81.4 billion in 2022 to KRW 44.8 billion in 2023, a 45.0% decline, recovered to KRW 71 billion in 2024 (up 58.7%), and then dropped to KRW 46.3 billion in 2025 (down 34.7%). 2022 was the largest year and 2023 the smallest within 2022 to 2025.

Profit swung more sharply than revenue. Operating profit of KRW 6.67 billion in 2022 turned into an operating loss of KRW 4.23 billion in 2023, returned to an operating profit of KRW 1.87 billion in 2024, and turned into an operating loss of KRW 2.93 billion in 2025.

The operating margin moved from 8.2% in 2022 to -9.4% in 2023, 2.6% in 2024 and -6.3% in 2025. The company attributed the 2025 swing to a loss to fewer orders amid a downturn in its secondary-battery end markets.

Profit attributable to controlling shareholders followed the same pattern: KRW 5.97 billion in 2022, a net loss of KRW 7.59 billion in 2023, KRW 2.78 billion in 2024, and a net loss of KRW 7.33 billion in 2025.

Net income, which includes non-controlling interests, is a separate figure from profit attributable to controlling shareholders, and the 2025 net loss was KRW 7.99 billion.

Because the 2025 net loss was larger than the operating loss, items below operating profit and non-controlling interests should be checked in the periodic report. Quarterly, Q3 2025 revenue was KRW 14.6 billion with an operating loss of KRW 1.56 billion.

Q4 2025 revenue was KRW 13.2 billion, down 9.8% from the prior quarter, and the operating loss narrowed to KRW 273 million. Q1 2026 revenue fell 25.0% quarter on quarter to KRW 9.9 billion and the operating loss widened to KRW 2.66 billion.

Q2 2026 revenue was KRW 6.89 billion with an operating loss of KRW 2.35 billion. Q3 2026 revenue rose 257.1% quarter on quarter to KRW 24.6 billion, up 68.1% year on year, and operating profit turned positive at KRW 2.17 billion.

The company attributed the turnaround mainly to wider display equipment orders and reported cumulative nine-month revenue of KRW 41.4 billion. Operating cash flow went from KRW 6.78 billion in 2022 and KRW 12.4 billion in 2023 to KRW 272 million in 2024 and negative KRW 3.5 billion in 2025.

At year-end 2025, total liabilities were KRW 53.3 billion and total equity KRW 27.3 billion, and the debt-to-equity ratio rose to 195.4% from 120.6% in 2024.

05

Industry analysis

Display equipment is sensitive to panel makers' facility investment cycles. A 2025 securities note cited panel makers' reduced investment and a non-investment phase as reasons display equipment sales fell sharply in the first nine months.

Secondary-battery equipment was hit as customers postponed investment amid slower electric-vehicle demand. A securities summary expected OLED adoption to expand from smartphones to tablets, notebooks and automobiles, and expected battery makers' investment to resume as EV sales grow.

These are forecasts, not confirmed demand. On competition, one analysis said Finetek's revenue growth in Q4 2025 was low relative to peers. In Q3 2026, growth picked up again as display equipment orders converted into revenue.

Because the portfolio spans display and secondary-battery equipment, results can swing widely when the two end markets move in different directions.

The 56% parts and 44% equipment mix suggests parts revenue can partly offset equipment order gaps, though the segment-level profitability of parts is not disclosed in the figures used here.

06

Outlook

The company said it won about KRW 35.2 billion in equipment orders from Samsung Display over four months starting July 2026, that the orders are now being reflected in revenue, and that growth should continue into Q4. It also said it expects enterprise value to improve further next year.

On new businesses, intelligent-robot and robot-parts operations were added to the articles of incorporation in 2026, and the company said it is accelerating commercialization of food-tech robots. These businesses appear to be at an early stage and are not yet visible in disclosed results.

Three points are worth tracking. First, whether Q4 2026 revenue reflects the Samsung Display orders as the company describes. Second, whether the 2026 full-year operating margin moves from the -6.3% recorded in 2025 into positive territory.

Third, whether the Q3 2026 operating profit continues into the following quarters. Q4 2026 results will be confirmed in the 2026 annual report. The timing of any recovery in secondary-battery equipment orders depends on customers' investment plans. New order disclosures should be checked for both amount and customer.

Bull factors

Quarterly turnaround

Profit attributable to controlling shareholders was also positive at KRW 705 million in Q3 2026. The swing from an operating loss of KRW 2.35 billion in the prior quarter suggests revenue recovery is feeding into profitability, although this is a single quarter.

Large order visibility

The company said it won about KRW 35.2 billion in equipment orders from Samsung Display over four months starting July 2026. It said the orders are being reflected in revenue and that growth should continue into Q4.

The company also reported cumulative nine-month revenue of KRW 41.4 billion, close to the full-year 2025 revenue of KRW 46.3 billion.

Wider business scope

Secondary-battery, process-control and welding businesses were added to the articles of incorporation in 2025, and intelligent-robot and robot-parts businesses in 2026. The contribution of these businesses to results is not yet visible in disclosures, so execution is the next thing to watch.

09

Bear factors

Recurring annual losses

Annual operating losses occurred in 2023 and 2025, and the 2025 net loss attributable to controlling shareholders was KRW 7.33 billion after a profit in 2024. The 2025 operating margin was -6.3%. Profit years and loss years have alternated, so earnings have not yet built up steadily. The Q3 2026 profit is so far a single quarter.

Secondary-battery order gap

The company attributed its 2025 deterioration to fewer orders amid a downturn in secondary-battery end markets. A securities summary described postponed battery-maker investment due to slower EV demand.

No confirmed timetable for a recovery in secondary-battery orders is available, and reliance on display orders could rise.

Financial strain

At the end of 2025, total liabilities were KRW 53.3 billion and total equity KRW 27.3 billion, and the debt-to-equity ratio rose from 120.6% in 2024 to 195.4%. Operating cash flow turned negative at KRW 3.5 billion in 2025.

The cumulative net loss attributable to controlling shareholders over the last four quarters is KRW 5.42 billion. Whether the Q3 profit turns into cash generation has not yet been confirmed.

10

Risk factors

Customer concentration

The company's recent display equipment orders are concentrated in Samsung Display. A change in that customer's facility investment plans could swing quarterly revenue sharply, as happened when panel-maker investment cuts reduced sales in the first nine months of 2025. Progress in diversifying customers should be watched.

Balance sheet

The debt-to-equity ratio reached 195.4% at the end of 2025, and operating cash flow was negative KRW 3.5 billion in 2025. Total equity fell to KRW 27.3 billion from its 2024 level. Whether borrowing burden and cash generation improve after the Q3 turnaround should be checked in the periodic reports.

New-business execution and liquidity

New businesses such as robotics and food-tech are at the stage of articles amendments and commercialization announcements, and their revenue contribution is not yet visible in disclosures. As a small-cap stock, trading volume and price volatility can be large. If the new businesses do not convert into revenue as expected, diversification benefits may stay limited.

Overall view

Finetek moved to a quarterly profit in Q3 2026, with revenue of KRW 24.6 billion and operating profit of KRW 2.17 billion, after an annual loss in 2025. The company said it won about KRW 35.2 billion in equipment orders from Samsung Display over four months starting July 2026, and said growth should continue into Q4.

On the other side, annual losses occurred in 2023 and 2025, the cumulative net loss attributable to controlling shareholders over the last four quarters is KRW 5.42 billion, and the debt-to-equity ratio stood at 195.4% at the end of 2025.

No confirmed timetable for a recovery in secondary-battery orders is available, and the revenue contribution of robotics and food-tech businesses has not yet appeared in disclosures. The business is split 56% parts and 44% equipment, with high customer concentration in display equipment.

Whether the earnings recovery lasts will be checked against the Q4 2026 results, the 2026 annual report and the Q1 2027 quarterly report.
