02

Business structure

Iteyes was founded in 2012 as a financial IT company, starting with IT compliance for financial institutions and extending into cloud-based digital platforms.

The company now positions three core platforms, digital finance (STO), energy trading and AI bio-health (precision medicine), as its growth axes and aims to raise the share of platform revenue. Its cloud platform GURMWI is offered to healthcare, agri-bio and public institutions.

A 2024 press report put the revenue mix at about 76% service, about 18% products and about 5% cloud. That mix comes from a 2024 snapshot and may differ from the current split.

Financial institutions remain the core customer base, and Iteyes has run maintenance of the Korea Power Exchange's power market operating system since 2019. Its scope in power has widened to include operation of core power market systems.

In security, it presented zero-trust solutions for finance with Illumio at FISCON 2026. The company plans to extend zero-trust business into bio-health, energy and public finance beyond banking.

Among IT services companies, it ranked 98th by market capitalization out of 100 in December 2025 data, a far smaller scale than large SI groups such as Samsung SDS and LG CNS.

04

Earnings analysis

On a filed basis, annual revenue rose from KRW 54.6 billion in 2022 to KRW 74.9 billion in 2023 (up 37.3% year on year) and KRW 79.6 billion in 2024 (up 6.2%), the largest year of 2022 to 2025. It eased to KRW 78.1 billion in 2025 (down 1.9%). Profitability moved along a different path.

The operating loss narrowed from KRW 11.3 billion in 2022 to KRW 6.39 billion in 2023, turned into an operating profit of KRW 167 million in 2024, and returned to an operating loss of KRW 5.39 billion in 2025. Operating margin was -20.8% in 2022, -8.5% in 2023, 0.2% in 2024 and -6.9% in 2025.

The 2025 deterioration was attributed to lower sales in the core finance solution and AI/big data solution segments together with higher cost burdens.

On a controlling-shareholder basis, net income of KRW 1.43 billion in 2024 gave way to a net loss of KRW 9.52 billion in 2025, while net loss including non-controlling interests was KRW 10.3 billion in 2025.

Operating cash flow was negative in 2022 (-KRW 6.03 billion), 2023 (-KRW 3.99 billion) and 2025 (-KRW 7.98 billion), and positive at KRW 9.16 billion in 2024.

At end-2025, total equity was KRW 8.41 billion, controlling-shareholder equity was KRW 8.99 billion and total liabilities were KRW 25.1 billion, with the debt ratio rising from 65.8% in 2022 to 299.0% in 2025.

Quarterly revenue went from KRW 20.1 billion in Q3 2025 to KRW 16.2 billion in Q4 (down 19.2% quarter on quarter), KRW 18.7 billion in Q1 2026 (up 15.4%), KRW 12.9 billion in Q2 (down 30.8%) and KRW 14.2 billion in Q3 (up 9.6% quarter on quarter, down 29.3% year on year).

The operating loss widened from KRW 296 million in Q3 2025 to KRW 4.32 billion in Q4, then KRW 2.77 billion in Q1 2026, KRW 3 billion in Q2 and KRW 1 billion in Q3, a narrower loss than in Q2 but wider than in Q3 2025.

Losses have been reported in each of the five quarters from Q3 2025 through Q3 2026, and the controlling-shareholder net loss over the last four quarters totaled KRW 9.3 billion.

05

Industry analysis

Iteyes operates where financial IT services, public and energy system operations and security demand overlap. Recent hacking incidents at financial institutions have raised the importance of protecting internal systems and core data, a backdrop the company cited when presenting its zero-trust offering.

Zero trust continuously verifies access by users and devices. In energy, operation and maintenance of the power market system is the core contract business, and Iteyes has run it since 2019 while widening its scope. In bio-health, a cooperation on AI cancer diagnosis development has been reported.

Against larger IT services peers such as Samsung SDS, LG CNS and Hyundai Autoever, Iteyes is far smaller, ranking 98th by market capitalization out of 100 IT services companies in December 2025 data. Because of that scale, a single large project can weigh relatively heavily on revenue.

In security, competition from established vendors such as Ahnlab means the Illumio partnership's commercial traction is an open question.

06

Outlook

The company said it secured about KRW 60 billion in new projects in July 2026 on a total project basis, of which roughly KRW 32 billion is reflected as its own revenue. The package includes the Korea Power Exchange project and two projects of about KRW 20 billion each with a credit guarantee foundation.

Reporting differs on the power exchange contract's scale: one outlet cites about KRW 32 billion in total, while the exchange's filing shows a confirmed amount of about KRW 14.4 billion for Iteyes' 50% share in a three-company consortium.

The contract runs from July 16, 2026 to July 15, 2029, with payment on progress billing and no advance or down payment. The company said it expects full-scale revenue from August, improved results in the second half of 2026 and record profit in 2027.

However, the Q3 2026 filed operating loss of KRW 1 billion fell short of the earlier company view that Q3 could mark a visible return to profit. Timing and size of revenue recognition can be checked against the 2026 annual report and 2027 quarterly filings.

Bull factors

Large contracts widen the revenue base

In July 2026 the company said it secured about KRW 60 billion in projects on a total basis. The Korea Power Exchange maintenance contract runs for about three years through July 2029. Two credit guarantee foundation projects are also included, and the company argues the backlog can smooth quarterly swings.

Progress in the platform shift

The company is developing digital finance (STO), energy trading and AI bio-health as three core platforms. Its related-article list includes news of a preliminary licence for a KDX-led STO exchange consortium that Iteyes participates in. In November 2026 it also presented zero-trust security with Illumio for financial clients.

CEO share purchases

In May 2026 CEO Lee Sung-nam disclosed open-market purchases of about 40,000 shares over the month. At that time the company pointed to a second-half contract pipeline as the basis for a possible return to profit from the third quarter. The purchase is confirmed by the filing, and its meaning should be read alongside later results.

Bear factors

Consecutive losses and falling sales

The losses have continued for a fifth straight quarter, which adds to the pressure. Q3 2026 revenue of KRW 14.2 billion fell 29.3% year on year. After the 2025 operating loss of KRW 5.39 billion, no operating profit has been reported since. If the return to profit is delayed, the profitability of the new contracts becomes more important.

Balance sheet pressure

The debt ratio reached 299.0% at end-2025, up from 65.8% in 2022. Total equity was KRW 8.41 billion and controlling-shareholder equity was KRW 8.99 billion. Operating cash flow was -KRW 7.98 billion in 2025. Payment on progress billing can raise working capital needs while a project is under way.

Potential dilution and cost pressure

Related-article headlines include a KRW 4.1 billion convertible bond issuance to fund new businesses. In March 2026 stock options for 58,000 common shares were granted to six executives. Rising cost burdens were cited alongside lower sales as causes of the 2025 deterioration.

Risk factors

Contract execution and cash flow

The power exchange contract pays on progress billing, with no advance or down payment. Cost control over a three-year term at a 50% consortium stake could shape reported results. Operating cash flow was -KRW 7.98 billion in 2025.

Balance sheet and capital capacity

The debt ratio was 299.0% at end-2025, with total equity of KRW 8.41 billion. Continued losses could erode capital capacity and may require external funding. Financing news such as the convertible bond should be read against this balance sheet.

Gap between new-business promises and results

12

Overall view

Iteyes has disclosed a series of large contracts since July 2026, including the Korea Power Exchange maintenance deal, as it prepares to widen its revenue base. Its filed results, however, show losses in each of the five quarters from Q3 2025 through Q3 2026, and Q3 2026 revenue fell 29.3% year on year.

The debt ratio was 299.0% at end-2025 and total equity was KRW 8.41 billion, so the balance sheet remains a burden. Strengths include the contract backlog, the platform strategy and the CEO's share purchases. Weaknesses include consecutive losses, falling sales, negative operating cash flow and potential dilution.

The company expects record profit in 2027, but that is its own outlook. The next checkpoints are how much second-half contract revenue appears in the 2026 annual report and whether the operating loss persists, followed by whether the first quarter of 2027 shows a sustained return to profit.
