02

Business structure

Daelim Trading manufactures and exports building materials and household fixtures centered on bathroom and residential products.

According to FnGuide, the building-materials division accounted for 62.07% of revenue at the end of 2024, followed by other items such as sanitary ware at 20.48%, Western tableware at 10.90%, other kitchenware at 3.66%, and faucet fittings at 2.90%.

The main brand is DOBIDOS, and its core product lines are faucet fittings, bidets and sanitary ware. The company said building-materials revenue in the first half of 2026 reached KRW 57.1 billion, up 20.7% year on year, while domestic sales rose 27.7% to KRW 44.5 billion.

The SPEC supply to large construction firms was presented as a pipeline of 45 domestic projects covering about 55,000 households, worth roughly KRW 45.5 billion in total.

New categories such as shower booths are also expanding, and cumulative shower-booth sales through July 2026 were reported at about KRW 3.54 billion, or 174.2% of the business plan.

Overseas, the long-term supply relationship with Japan's TAKAGI is central, and the company describes TAKAGI-related revenue at about KRW 15.6 billion a year. A 2014 industry report grouped the company with Daelim Bath as the top two players in the domestic bathroom market.

04

Earnings analysis

On a disclosed basis, annual revenue fell from KRW 174.1 billion in 2022 to KRW 145.7 billion in 2023 (down 16.3% year on year), KRW 137 billion in 2024 (down 6.0%), and KRW 126.2 billion in 2025 (down 7.9%).

Revenue declined for three consecutive years from 2023 through 2025, with 2022 the largest year and 2025 the smallest in the 2022 to 2025 window.

Operating profit of KRW 6.95 billion in 2022 turned into an operating loss of KRW 4.93 billion in 2023, followed by an operating loss of KRW 3.41 billion in 2024 (loss narrowed) and KRW 9.57 billion in 2025 (loss widened). Operating margin moved from 4.0% in 2022 to -3.4% in 2023, -2.5% in 2024 and -7.6% in 2025.

Net profit attributable to controlling shareholders was KRW 3.79 billion in 2022, then turned into a net loss of KRW 7.97 billion in 2023, widening to KRW 10.9 billion in 2024 and KRW 16.9 billion in 2025.

Net income including non-controlling interests was a net loss of KRW 17 billion in 2025, a slightly different figure from the controlling-shareholder measure.

Operating cash flow went from KRW 6.59 billion in 2022 to negative KRW 3.17 billion in 2023 and negative KRW 7.74 billion in 2024, before turning to KRW 450 million in 2025.

The debt-to-equity ratio rose from 113.9% in 2022 to 305.4% in 2025, when total equity was KRW 38.2 billion and total liabilities were KRW 116.6 billion.

Quarterly revenue peaked at KRW 35.4 billion in the fourth quarter of 2025 and then declined to KRW 34.4 billion, KRW 34 billion and KRW 30.5 billion in the first three quarters of 2026.

Operating loss narrowed from KRW 3.68 billion in the third quarter of 2025 to KRW 2.02 billion in the fourth quarter and KRW 612 million in the first quarter of 2026, then widened to KRW 1.94 billion in the second quarter and KRW 3.36 billion in the third quarter.

The third-quarter 2026 net loss attributable to controlling shareholders of KRW 6.18 billion reflects a wider loss than a year earlier. The company's own announcement, separate from disclosed results, said first-half 2026 operating cash flow turned positive from a negative figure a year earlier.

05

Industry analysis

Daelim Trading's end markets are new housing construction and the remodeling market. In the first half of 2026 the company attributed revenue growth to meeting remodeling and replacement demand, and said wider sales of its DOBIDOS brand lifted domestic bathroom and faucet-fitting sales.

SPEC supply to large construction firms builds up project by project, so project timing tends to shape quarterly revenue. On the export side, supply to Japan's TAKAGI is establishing itself as a higher-value product line. In competition, Daelim Bath is frequently cited as a leading rival in the same bathroom market.

Daelim Bath's third-quarter 2026 revenue rose 4.5% year on year, while its operating profit fell 15.0%. In the same quarter Daelim Trading's revenue fell 3.2% year on year and its operating loss narrowed from KRW 3.68 billion to KRW 3.36 billion, so the two companies' profitability trends moved in different directions. The comparison starts from the fact that company-level results within the bathroom segment are diverging.

Outlook

At its first-half 2026 results announcement on August 18, 2026, the company said it plans to keep growing sales and improving profitability in the second half through a stronger DOBIDOS brand, expanded SPEC projects and higher-value overseas supply, including to Japan.

The SPEC pipeline was presented as 45 domestic projects worth about KRW 45.5 billion. Shower-booth sales through July 2026 were reported above the business plan. For Japan, the company planned first shipments of a new TAKAGI product in October 2026 and expects annual revenue of about KRW 1.5 billion from it.

Press reports said an order for a PHS water-purifier faucet and a China-bound FL449E product was confirmed, with meaningful revenue expected from late 2026. A plan to monetize KRW 134.1 billion in owned assets was reported on August 28, 2026.

The bullish question is whether these orders and overseas shipments show up in quarterly revenue, while the bearish question is whether the 10.5% quarter-on-quarter revenue decline in the third quarter of 2026 continues. The second-half growth plan has not yet appeared in reported quarterly results.

Bull factors

Building-materials growth and wider DOBIDOS sales

The company said on August 18, 2026 that first-half revenue reached KRW 68.4 billion, up 15.2% year on year. Building-materials revenue rose 20.7% and domestic sales rose 27.7%, with wider sales of DOBIDOS-centered bathroom and faucet products cited as the driver.

The disclosed first- and second-quarter 2026 revenue of KRW 34.4 billion and KRW 34 billion sums to the company's first-half figure.

SPEC pipeline and shower-booth expansion

The company said it has secured a SPEC pipeline of 45 domestic projects covering about 55,000 households, worth roughly KRW 45.5 billion in total. Shower-booth sales reached KRW 3.54 billion cumulative through July 2026, or 174.2% of plan, according to press reports. Large-builder volume and new B2C categories are presented as widening the revenue base.

TAKAGI supply in Japan and cash-flow turnaround

Based on its long-term relationship with TAKAGI, the company said it was selected as a supplier of the JY series next-generation water-purifier faucet and has entered mass production. TAKAGI-related revenue is described at about KRW 15.6 billion a year.

First-half 2026 operating cash flow was positive at KRW 1.9 billion, turning from negative KRW 2.5 billion a year earlier, according to the company's announcement.

Bear factors

Continued revenue decline

On a disclosed basis, revenue fell for three consecutive years from 2023 through 2025. Third-quarter 2026 revenue of KRW 30.5 billion was down 10.5% from the prior quarter and down 3.2% from a year earlier. Since peaking in the fourth quarter of 2025, quarterly revenue has not yet rebounded.

Persistent losses and wider losses

Third-quarter 2026 operating loss was KRW 3.36 billion and the net loss attributable to controlling shareholders was KRW 6.18 billion, both larger than in the first quarter of 2026.

The four-quarter total of controlling-shareholder net losses is KRW 15.4 billion, approaching the KRW 16.9 billion full-year loss for 2025. Operating losses have continued for three consecutive years since 2023.

Shrinking equity and high leverage

The debt-to-equity ratio climbed sharply from 113.9% in 2022 to 305.4% in 2025. At the end of 2025, total equity was KRW 38.2 billion and total liabilities were KRW 116.6 billion. With equity shrinking against a large debt base, financial burden could constrain any earnings recovery.

Risk factors

Financial structure and asset monetization

A plan to monetize KRW 134.1 billion in owned assets was reported on August 28, 2026. The effect on the debt ratio and non-operating income will depend on the assets involved, the terms, and how the proceeds are used. The detailed terms have to be checked in follow-up disclosures.

Project timing and customer concentration

The SPEC pipeline of 45 projects worth about KRW 45.5 billion was presented, but supply timing and revenue recognition can shift with construction schedules. Overseas revenue relies in part on volume from specific customers such as TAKAGI. A delay in a major project could directly move quarterly revenue.

Changes in largest shareholder's stake

The largest shareholder group's common-share stake fell from 68.51% in the report dated March 27, 2026 to 65.03% in the report dated May 21, 2026. Off-market block trades were disclosed as the cause of the reduction.

Whether the change signals a shift in management direction cannot be settled from the filing alone, so later shareholding reports should be read alongside it.

Overall view

Daelim Trading's first-half 2026 revenue rose year on year, driven by building-materials growth and wider DOBIDOS sales. On a disclosed basis, however, revenue has fallen for three consecutive years from 2023, and the third quarter of 2026 combined a third straight quarterly decline with a wider operating loss.

Operating losses have continued for three consecutive years since 2023, and the controlling-shareholder net loss over the last four quarters totals KRW 15.4 billion. On the financial side, a debt-to-equity ratio of 305.4% and total equity of KRW 38.2 billion at the end of 2025 are burdens.

Set against this, the SPEC pipeline worth about KRW 45.5 billion, expanded TAKAGI supply and new categories such as shower booths are factors the company presents as broadening its revenue base.

The planned monetization of KRW 134.1 billion in owned assets could both add financial room and shrink the asset base, so its terms should be checked in follow-up disclosures.

The valuation picture combines a PER that cannot be calculated and a price-to-book ratio below 1x, which some read as a discount to net assets and others as reflecting capital erosion and continuing losses.

The next checkpoints are the operating margin in the 2026 annual report and the quarterly revenue trend in the first quarter of 2027.
