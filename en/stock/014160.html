02

Business structure

Daeyoung Packaging, founded in 1979, is a corrugated board manufacturer that produces corrugated sheet in its own plants and supplies it to its box production lines, forming a vertically integrated structure. The sheet is used for internal box production and is also sold to outside box makers.

The company says this structure supports stable raw material supply and cost competitiveness. Sales come mostly from corrugated box sales, followed by sheet sales.

A business classification on a corporate information aggregator shows roughly 89% of sales from box manufacturing, about 10% from merchandise, and about 1% from waste sheet and other items, a figure that depends on that site's classification and should be checked against the business report's segment data.

The company held a 7.33% market share in 2023, placing it among the top players in the industry. On the product side, it has focused on specialized double-wall and triple-wall board products to expand demand beyond agricultural boxes into general industrial goods.

Its main end markets are described as e-commerce, agricultural and marine products, and processed foods. Competitively, large producers have held up through high-volume, low-margin pricing, while smaller producers have lost ground on price competitiveness, according to an industry source.

At the March 2026 annual general meeting, the articles of incorporation were amended to add solar power generation and electricity sales to the business purpose.

04

Earnings analysis

Annual sales were KRW 302.6 billion in 2022, then fell to KRW 283.5 billion in 2023 (down 6.3% year on year) and KRW 280.8 billion in 2024 (down 0.9%), before edging up to KRW 283.7 billion in 2025 (up 1.0%). Within 2022 to 2025, 2022 was the largest year and 2024 the smallest.

Operating profit went from KRW 11.9 billion in 2022 to KRW 11.1 billion in 2023 (down 7.0%), KRW 923 million in 2024 (down 91.7%) and KRW 882 million in 2025 (down 4.5%), a decline in each of the three years from 2023 to 2025. The operating margin fell from 3.9% in both 2022 and 2023 to 0.3% in both 2024 and 2025.

The company attributed the 2025 operating profit decline mainly to lower selling prices, even though sales volume rose.

Profit attributable to controlling shareholders fell from KRW 12.9 billion in 2022 to KRW 7.93 billion in 2023 (down 38.3%), swung to a net loss of KRW 31.97 million in 2024, and returned to KRW 1.5 billion in 2025.

Net income including non-controlling interests followed the same direction, with the 2024 loss of KRW 31.97 million and the 2025 figure of KRW 1.5 billion.

On a quarterly basis, the third quarter of 2025 showed sales of KRW 74.6 billion and operating profit of KRW 1.57 billion, followed by an operating loss of KRW 2.44 billion in the fourth quarter, an operating loss of KRW 2.25 billion in the first quarter of 2026 (a smaller loss), and a return to operating profit of KRW 2.53 billion in the second quarter.

Third-quarter 2026 sales were KRW 78.6 billion (down 1.8% from the prior quarter, up 5.3% year on year), and operating profit was KRW 4.49 billion (up 77.5% from the prior quarter and up 185.1% year on year).

Profit attributable to controlling shareholders reached KRW 3.52 billion in the third quarter, up 79.9% from the prior quarter, and the last four quarters total KRW 1.29 billion, close to the KRW 1.5 billion reported for 2025 as a whole.

At the end of 2025, total equity was KRW 192.3 billion, all attributable to controlling shareholders, total liabilities were KRW 69 billion, and the debt-to-equity ratio was 35.9% (28.3% in 2024).

Operating cash flow fell from KRW 26.7 billion in 2022 to KRW 266 million in 2024 and recovered to KRW 13.3 billion in 2025.

05

Industry analysis

The corrugated board industry is strongly exposed to economic conditions. An industry source said that even after paper prices rose, producers could not pass the increase on to box prices and instead lowered prices to protect volume.

Large producers have held on through high-volume, low-margin selling, while smaller producers have lost ground on price. Corrugated packaging makers' results have been unsettled across the board since 2024, after the pandemic-era boom ended.

Daeyoung Packaging held a 7.33% market share in 2023, among the top players, but commentary at the time pointed to a need for more active measures as sales and operating profit declined and share slipped.

In April 2026, amid expectations of petrochemical raw material supply disruption tied to the Middle East conflict, paper-based corrugated packaging drew attention as a possible substitute for plastic and vinyl packaging that depends heavily on naphtha, and the share price rose.

Because this was a theme-driven price response, whether substitution demand turned into sales growth should be checked against the trend in second-quarter 2026 sales of KRW 80 billion and third-quarter sales of KRW 78.6 billion.

Within the corrugated industry, the company ranks among the leaders by scale and share, while its profitability metrics sit below the industry average.

Which of these the company actually adopts will be confirmed through later disclosures. On earnings, the next question is whether the third-quarter 2026 operating profit of KRW 4.49 billion holds in the fourth quarter and beyond.

The 2026 annual business report will fix full-year operating results and fourth-quarter profit, and whether the 2026 operating margin rises above the 0.3% recorded in both 2024 and 2025 will serve as the baseline for that report.

Operating profit declined in each of the three years from 2023 to 2025. A margin near 0.3% means that changes in sales pass through to profit in a magnified way.

Daeyoung Packaging recorded operating losses in the fourth quarter of 2025 and the first quarter of 2026. If the industry recovery is delayed, the quarterly profit rebound could reverse, which is the bearish point.

Listing maintenance and governance burden

The stock was designated as an administrative issue on August 12, 2026 due to its low share price, and on August 20 it was also designated as an investment caution issue due to concentrated buying by a small number of accounts. The largest shareholder holds a high 50.79% stake.

Risk factors

Listing maintenance risk

If the reason for the administrative designation is not resolved, delisting procedures can begin. According to press reports, the resolution window is 90 trading days, with a requirement of 45 consecutive trading days meeting the criteria. Whether the share price requirement holds after the consolidation is central. Trading conditions and the shareholder base could change significantly if delisting procedures begin.

Raw material and pricing risk

If changes in paper prices cannot be passed on to box prices, margins shrink directly. In 2025, lower selling prices were cited as the reason operating profit declined. Substitution demand linked to naphtha supply concerns is theme-driven, so its durability is uncertain.

In a structure where the gap between raw material costs and selling prices is narrow, small changes carry large effects on profit.

Earnings volatility and governance

Quarterly operating profit has swung widely, from KRW 1.57 billion in the third quarter of 2025 to an operating loss of KRW 2.44 billion in the fourth quarter and KRW 4.49 billion in the third quarter of 2026.

Profit attributable to controlling shareholders over the last four quarters totals KRW 1.29 billion, so quarter-to-quarter swings can easily change the annual profit trend. With the largest shareholder holding 50.79%, changes in the shareholder base and governance carry significant weight.

Overall view

Daeyoung Packaging is a vertically integrated corrugated company that makes its own board and converts it into boxes, and its sales have moved in the KRW 280 billion range since 2023. Operating profit fell from KRW 11.9 billion in 2022 to KRW 882 million in 2025, and the operating margin dropped to 0.3%.

The clearest recent change is the return to operating profit in the second quarter of 2026 and the rise to KRW 4.49 billion in the third quarter. However, this is still a single quarter of results, and the losses in the fourth quarter of 2025 and the first quarter of 2026 show how volatile quarterly profit has been.

The financial structure looks relatively stable, with a debt-to-equity ratio of 35.9% and total equity of KRW 192.3 billion. The August 2026 administrative designation, the September 2026 share consolidation, and the 50.79% largest shareholder stake are variables affecting listing status and the shareholder base.

Expectations for substitution demand linked to naphtha supply concerns appeared as a theme-driven share price response, and whether they translate into results should be checked against the quarterly sales trend.

The most important checkpoint is when the 2026 annual business report fixes the full-year operating margin and fourth-quarter profit.
