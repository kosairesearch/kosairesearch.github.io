02

Business structure

KC Green Holdings is a holding company listed on the KOSPI since 1994 that converted to a holding structure through a 2010 corporate split. In 2024 it created KC Biochip, KC Green Energy and KC Construction through a spin-off, and restructuring has continued since then.

Its former core subsidiary KC Glass is a glass bottle maker founded in 1971 that operates eco-friendly manufacturing based on recycling waste glass. As of year-end 2024 KC Green Holdings was the largest shareholder of KC Glass with a 97.09% stake.

KC Glass received a court-supervised reorganization start decision in July 2025, and Opus PE was named final preferred acquirer in February 2026. The reorganization plan was approved at a creditors' meeting on June 16, 2026, and after losing control KC Glass was reclassified as another related party.

Environmental equipment subsidiary KC Cotrel reached a 76.01% stake for KC Green Holdings after a May 2025 debt-to-equity conversion, and it entered a workout process at about the same time.

KC Construction was sold in 2025, and in September 2026 the company disclosed the sale of its entire stake in KC Safety Technology, a high-pressure gas inspection firm, for KRW 22.33 billion.

Because the set of consolidated subsidiaries keeps changing through sales and reorganizations, reported revenue has moved sharply with changes in consolidation scope. The holding company's own business is shifting toward managing subsidiaries and adjusting the value of its stakes.

04

Earnings analysis

Annual revenue fell from KRW 711.2 billion in 2022 to KRW 698.5 billion in 2023 (down 1.8% year on year), KRW 421.1 billion in 2024 (down 39.7%), and KRW 86 billion in 2025 (down 79.6%).

Revenue declined for three consecutive years from 2023 through 2025, with 2022 the largest year and 2025 the smallest in the 2022 to 2025 range.

Operating profit of KRW 13.6 billion in 2022 turned into an operating loss of KRW 29.9 billion in 2023, widened to an operating loss of KRW 65.2 billion in 2024, and narrowed to an operating loss of KRW 1.57 billion in 2025.

The operating margin moved from 1.9% in 2022 to -4.3% in 2023, -15.5% in 2024 and -1.8% in 2025. Net loss attributable to controlling shareholders was KRW 3.03 billion in 2022 and KRW 22.5 billion in 2023, turned into a profit of KRW 36.4 billion in 2024, and returned to a loss of KRW 40.4 billion in 2025.

Net loss including non-controlling interests was KRW 17 billion in 2022, KRW 55.9 billion in 2023, KRW 4.98 billion in 2024 and KRW 40.8 billion in 2025. The gap between the two figures comes from non-controlling interests, so in 2024 the controlling shareholders' line was positive while the total was still a loss.

The company attributed the 2025 changes to shifts in the scope of consolidated subsidiaries and higher other losses, which is the stated background for a net result turning negative even as the operating loss narrowed.

Operating cash flow was -KRW 116.1 billion in 2022, -KRW 89.7 billion in 2023, -KRW 25.5 billion in 2024 and -KRW 665 million in 2025, so the cash burn narrowed sharply. The debt-to-equity ratio fell from 326.0% in 2023 to 67.2% in 2024 and 51.0% in 2025.

At year-end 2025 total equity was KRW 114.1 billion, of which KRW 104.3 billion belonged to controlling shareholders, and total liabilities were KRW 58.2 billion. On a quarterly basis, the Q4 2025 net loss attributable to controlling shareholders of KRW 41.2 billion was the largest in the series.

Q1 2026 returned to a profit of KRW 233 million, followed by KRW 3.92 billion in Q2, up 1,580.0% from the prior quarter, and KRW 5.91 billion in Q3, up 50.9%, marking two consecutive quarterly increases.

Q3 2026 revenue was KRW 2.12 billion, down 89.6% from the prior quarter and 86.8% from a year earlier, and the operating loss was KRW 606 million. An operating loss alongside a profit attributable to controlling shareholders in the same quarter suggests non-operating items had a large effect. The net loss attributable to controlling shareholders over the last four quarters totals KRW 31.2 billion.

Industry analysis

Holding companies move with the results and asset values of their subsidiaries, so subsidiary sales and restructuring are the central variables for this group.

KC Green Holdings has most of its subsidiaries in reorganization or sale processes, which makes its situation closer to a balance-sheet clean-up than a typical holding company comparison.

Among its subsidiaries, environmental equipment (KC Cotrel) and glass bottle manufacturing (KC Glass) have separate demand structures, and their results are combined at the holding level.

A June 2026 report cited a low-PBR cutoff of 0.3 times for the bottom 20% of listed companies and described pressure on about 170 listed companies.

An August 2026 report said the number of companies at risk of delisting for reasons other than low share prices or market capitalization shortfalls had grown, and KC Green Holdings was mentioned as one example.

Larger holding companies in the sector such as SK Square and Meritz Financial Group hold assets concentrated in listed stakes or financial affiliates, so their subsidiary mix and business profile differ from KC Green Holdings. Sector-average multiples should therefore be read with those structural differences in mind.

The sector-wide low-PBR pressure applies to holding companies too, but for KC Green Holdings the listing review is the more immediate company-specific factor.
