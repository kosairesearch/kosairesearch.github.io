02

Business structure

HD Korea Shipbuilding & Offshore Engineering serves as the intermediate holding company of the HD Hyundai shipbuilding group, and most of the actual construction capacity sits in its consolidated subsidiaries.

The core units are HD Hyundai Heavy Industries, which operates domestic yards, and a Samho-based shipbuilding subsidiary, while the Asian holding company that controls yards in Vietnam and the Philippines handles overseas production.

The annual order target set in early 2026 is USD 23.31 billion, compared with 133 vessels and USD 18.16 billion of orders won in 2025. Press reports said the 2025 target of USD 18.05 billion was exceeded by 100.6%.

Container ships were the largest category, but the company said it broadened the mix toward tankers and LNG carriers from the second half of 2025. The first LNG carrier order of 2026 came early in the year, and LNG carriers are positioned as a higher-value category.

The special-vessel business is run by the special-vessel unit of HD Hyundai Heavy Industries, which set a 2026 order target of USD 3 billion, roughly KRW 4.3 trillion.

Competition centers on price with Chinese yards, and domestic builders are responding by selecting higher-margin vessels rather than competing on low prices. The earnings structure relies heavily on subsidiary results and the dividends they pay to the holding company.

04

Earnings analysis

On a disclosed basis, annual revenue rose from KRW 17.3 trillion in 2022 to KRW 21.3 trillion in 2023 (up 23.1% year on year), KRW 25.5 trillion in 2024 (up 19.9%), and KRW 29.9 trillion in 2025 (up 17.2%).

Revenue has increased for three consecutive years from 2023 through 2025, and 2025 is the largest year in the 2022 to 2025 window.

Operating profit swung from an operating loss of KRW 355.6 billion in 2022 to operating profit of KRW 282.3 billion in 2023, then rose to KRW 1.43 trillion in 2024 (up 408.1%) and KRW 3.9 trillion in 2025 (up 172.3%), increasing for two consecutive years.

Operating profit grew far faster than revenue, so margin expansion rather than volume drove most of the improvement. The operating margin moved from -2.1% to 1.3%, 5.6%, and 13.0%.

Net income attributable to controlling shareholders went from a net loss of KRW 216.9 billion in 2022 to KRW 221.7 billion in 2023, then KRW 1.17 trillion in 2024 and KRW 2.17 trillion in 2025, rising for two consecutive years.

Net income including non-controlling interests was KRW 2.93 trillion in 2025, higher than the figure attributable to controlling shareholders, reflecting the share held by non-controlling holders.

On a quarterly basis, operating profit was KRW 1.05 trillion in 3Q 2025 and KRW 1.04 trillion in 4Q 2025, down 1.5% from the prior quarter.

It then reached KRW 1.36 trillion in 1Q 2026 (up 30.6% from the prior quarter), KRW 1.65 trillion in 2Q 2026 (up 21.3%), and KRW 1.45 trillion in 3Q 2026 (down 12.0% from the prior quarter but up 37.3% year on year).

Net income attributable to controlling shareholders peaked at KRW 1.29 trillion in 2Q 2026 before falling to KRW 623.5 billion in 3Q 2026, and the last four quarters total KRW 3.44 trillion.

In 3Q 2026 the decline in controlling-shareholder net income was much larger than the decline in operating profit, so whether non-operating or one-off items played a role is a point to verify in the notes to the periodic report.

Total liabilities at year-end 2025 were KRW 22.6 trillion, and the debt-to-equity ratio fell from 142.7% in 2022 to 133.9% in 2025. Operating cash flow rose from KRW 462.2 billion in 2022 to KRW 4.42 trillion in 2025, which strengthened the cash backing for reported profits.

05

Industry analysis

Shipbuilding demand tracks newbuild ordering and the vessel mix. The company said that global newbuild orders in 2026 were tallied at more than 20% below the prior year. The Korea Eximbank overseas economic research unit projected 2026 global newbuild ordering at 35 million CGT, a 14.6% decline from the current year.

Management noted that volumes still exceed 100 million GT, which it described as evidence that the newbuild market remains at a high level. Container-ship orders, which had accounted for a large share of the commercial business, are expected to weaken in 2026.

LNG carriers, by contrast, are expected to see stronger demand as US LNG export capacity expands, and the company has expressed confidence in its LNG order flow.

On the basis of global commercial order backlog, one analysis put China's share in 2026 at 62% in CGT terms and Korea's at 20%, compared with roughly 31% and 27% during the 2007 supercycle.

Domestic yards are responding by shifting toward higher-value categories such as LNG carriers and tankers, along with special vessels. Management also expects that replacement demand for aging ships, delayed by weak freight markets, could support newbuild orders once freight rates decline.

06

Outlook

The 2026 order target is USD 23.31 billion. Through mid-July 2026, press reports put cumulative orders at 142 vessels worth USD 16.39 billion, or 70.3% of the target. Whether the remaining months close that gap is the central question for the year.

The company set the target alongside its early-2026 disclosures and said it will keep a selective strategy aimed at protecting vessel prices. LNG carriers are the category most often cited as decisive for reaching the target.

The special-vessel plan also includes follow-on work for the Philippine navy within the 2026 targets. The company said revenue from a Peru project would begin to be recognized from 4Q 2026 and contribute more fully from 2027.

On overseas production, one broker estimated that building at Vietnamese and Philippine yards could cut costs materially over the medium term, but expanding offshore yards brings quality and labor-management risks that should be tracked through subsidiary productivity data.

Timing for the US naval vessel business depends on the progress of related legislation. The 4Q 2026 and full-year 2026 results are likely to be announced around early February 2027, which will show how much of the order target was met and how large the LNG share became.

Bull factors

Margin recovery has changed the earnings base

The operating margin moved from -2.1% in 2022 to 13.0% in 2025, and operating profit turned from a loss into KRW 3.9 trillion over the same period. Operating cash flow grew from KRW 462.2 billion to KRW 4.42 trillion, adding cash support to reported profits. Quarterly operating profit stayed above KRW 1 trillion in each of the three disclosed quarters of 2026.

Order backlog cited as covering several years

Press coverage from July 2026 cited Korean builders as holding at least three years of work and having moved away from low-price bidding contests. The company's cumulative 2026 orders were reported at 70.3% of its target. Selective bidding focused on higher-margin vessels is cited as the basis for holding vessel prices.

Shareholder returns funded by subsidiary dividends

The holding company funds its shareholder returns using dividends received from subsidiaries. In May 2025, Korea Investment & Securities argued that new investment results matter more than the dividend flow itself. A structure in which dividend capacity grows with subsidiary earnings is cited as a strength.

Bear factors

Slowing commercial newbuild orders

The decline in global newbuild orders weighs directly on the company's order environment. The Korea Eximbank research unit projected 2026 volumes at 35 million CGT, down 14.6%. Weaker container-ship demand, a major pillar of the commercial order book, adds pressure to the order target.

Softer profit trend in 3Q 2026

Operating profit in 3Q 2026 was KRW 1.45 trillion, down 12.0% from the prior quarter. Net income attributable to controlling shareholders fell to KRW 623.5 billion, a 51.6% drop from the prior quarter and sharply below the KRW 1.29 trillion recorded in 2Q 2026. The detailed causes will need to be checked in the notes to the periodic report.

Share gap with Chinese yards

One analysis put China's share of the global commercial order backlog at 62% in 2026 and Korea's at 20%. In the 2007 supercycle the two countries were close, at roughly 31% and 27%. Competition with lower-priced Chinese yards could pressure vessel prices and margins.

Risk factors

Order cycle and vessel-mix concentration

Reaching the 2026 order target leans heavily on higher-value categories such as LNG carriers. A concentration in any one vessel type could leave gaps if its ordering slows. The company has said it will adjust its portfolio flexibly to avoid dependence on a single category.

Exchange rates and subsidiary equity values

A weaker won against the dollar has been cited as a factor that reduces consolidated operating profit. In May 2025, Korea Investment & Securities lowered its target price citing reduced equity values at shipbuilding subsidiaries. The holding company's results and valuation are sensitive to changes in subsidiary values.

Policy and project timing

Analysts note that changes in US policy and LNG demand could shift ordering timelines. The US naval vessel business depends on related legislation, so its timing is uncertain.

Follow-on work for the Philippine navy and the start of Peru project revenue recognition are timelines the company has stated, and their actual progress needs to be tracked.

Overall view

On a disclosed basis, HD Korea Shipbuilding & Offshore Engineering swung from an operating loss in 2022 to KRW 3.9 trillion of operating profit in 2025, and its operating margin reached 13.0%.

All three disclosed quarters of 2026 stayed above KRW 1 trillion in operating profit, but 3Q 2026 was lower than the prior quarter and net income attributable to controlling shareholders fell sharply.

Progress toward the USD 23.31 billion 2026 order target depends heavily on higher-value categories such as LNG carriers. Strengths include the margin recovery, a multi-year order backlog, and a dividend structure funded by subsidiaries.

Weaknesses include the slowdown in global commercial newbuild ordering and the share gap with Chinese yards. The price-to-book multiple sits below its past year-end range, so the durability of earnings is the central question.

The next checkpoints are whether the 2026 order target is met, the 4Q and full-year 2026 results expected around early February 2027, and the 2026 annual report. This material is provided for information only and contains no investment opinion.
