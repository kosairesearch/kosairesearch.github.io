Report overview

Submarine fuel-cell hopes meet a run of quarterly operating losses

Bumhan Fuel Cell has grown revenue through submarine fuel-cell spare-module orders and liquefied hydrogen charging-station contracts, but operating losses have continued for four straight quarters, so profitability has not yet recovered.

02

Business structure

Bumhan Fuel Cell is a specialist maker of hydrogen fuel cells that also builds hydrogen charging stations, and it is a KOSDAQ-listed company headquartered in Changwon, South Gyeongsang Province.

Its business is divided into submarine fuel cells, hydrogen charging-station construction and building-type fuel cells, and the station business covers both gaseous and liquefied hydrogen stations.

The submarine unit has supplied fuel-cell modules for the Jang Bogo-III class submarines on an exclusive basis since 2018, with four modules installed per submarine and build periods of roughly three to four years, which analysts say supports medium-term revenue.

A spare-module supply contract for three Jang Bogo-III submarines, disclosed on July 20, 2026, is worth about KRW 7.08 billion, equal to 14.8% of revenue in the latest fiscal year, with delivery by July 2028.

In the hydrogen-station segment, the company had won 39 stations as of the end of the third quarter of 2025, including 30 gaseous and 9 liquefied-hydrogen stations, and had completed 27 of them, with 12 under construction, according to a February 2026 report by the Korea IR Council's research center.

Cumulative order value at that point was KRW 95.2 billion, up sharply from KRW 24.6 billion at the end of 2024. In 2025 the company signed an eight-station liquefied-hydrogen contract with an SK group affiliate worth KRW 67.6 billion, which the company said was its largest single contract to date.

In March 2026 it disclosed a KRW 3.6 billion contract to build equipment for a hydrogen station in Hanam. Analysts cite its status as the only domestic supplier of fuel cells for submarines as a competitive advantage, and Doosan Fuel Cell is the usual comparison in the fuel-cell sector. Reported R&D spending was KRW 8.3 billion in 2022, KRW 6.0 billion in 2023 and KRW 5.4 billion in 2024.

04

Earnings analysis

Annual revenue fell 39.8% from KRW 50.7 billion in 2022 to KRW 30.5 billion in 2023, then rose 18.6% to KRW 36.2 billion in 2024 and 19.8% to KRW 43.4 billion in 2025, so revenue has grown for two consecutive years from 2024 through 2025. Revenue was largest in 2022 and smallest in 2023 across the 2022–2025 period.

Operating profit went from KRW 1.31 billion in 2022 to an operating loss of KRW 5.24 billion in 2023, which marked a turn into the red, then returned to KRW 2.41 billion in 2024, a turn back to profit, before falling 64.4% to KRW 858 million in 2025.

The operating margin was 2.6% in 2022, -17.2% in 2023, 6.7% in 2024 and 2.0% in 2025, so in 2025 the margin fell below its 2024 level even as revenue grew.

Net income attributable to controlling shareholders was KRW 2.26 billion in 2022, a net loss of KRW 2.38 billion in 2023, KRW 835 million in 2024 and KRW 1.59 billion in 2025, up 90.5% year on year. Net income including non-controlling interests, a different figure, was KRW 1.18 billion in 2025.

Analysts attribute the swings in revenue to a structure dependent on large projects, and they note that delays in new orders could leave gaps in revenue. Operating cash flow moved from -KRW 12.4 billion in 2022 and -KRW 18.9 billion in 2023 to KRW 8.91 billion in 2024 and KRW 21.7 billion in 2025, a turn to positive.

The debt-to-equity ratio rose from 21.9% in 2022 to 88.4% in 2024 and 116.6% in 2025, when total liabilities were KRW 189.7 billion and total equity was KRW 162.7 billion, of which KRW 161.3 billion belonged to controlling shareholders.

On a quarterly basis, revenue of KRW 10.4 billion and operating profit of KRW 842 million in the third quarter of 2025 gave way to revenue of KRW 15.5 billion in the fourth quarter (up 48.6% from the prior quarter) and an operating loss of KRW 821 million, even though net income attributable to controlling shareholders that quarter was KRW 1.63 billion.

In the first quarter of 2026 revenue fell 67.3% from the prior quarter to KRW 5.05 billion and the operating loss widened to KRW 2.15 billion. In the second quarter revenue rose 252.7% to KRW 17.8 billion, but the operating loss widened further to KRW 3.23 billion.

In the third quarter revenue reached KRW 26.1 billion, up 150.9% year on year, and the operating loss narrowed to KRW 67.63 million from the prior quarter.

Over the most recent four quarters, from the fourth quarter of 2025 through the third quarter of 2026, net losses attributable to controlling shareholders totaled KRW 5.92 billion.

05

Industry analysis

The hydrogen fuel-cell market rests on three overlapping sources of demand: defense orders for submarines, infrastructure for liquefied hydrogen charging stations, and power demand from data centers.

In defense, the key variable was the Canadian Patrol Submarine Project (CPSP), and a July 7, 2026 report said the final award to the Team Korea bid had fallen through, weakening hopes for overseas submarine exports. Earlier in 2026, analysts had described the project as a final competition against a German bidder.

In September 2026, Doosan Fuel Cell surged on expectations of a large order from U.S. AI data centers, and fuel-cell stocks including Bumhan Fuel Cell moved up together, while a sharp rise in the U.S. fuel-cell maker Bloom Energy also reportedly lifted investor sentiment.

Reports attributed that move to sentiment across the sector, not to any direct order announcement by Bumhan Fuel Cell. The hydrogen-station market is shifting from gaseous stations toward large liquefied-hydrogen orders.

The company is viewed as a player that entered the liquefied-hydrogen market on the basis of its earlier gaseous-station construction experience. Supply of submarine fuel cells is effectively limited to a small number of domestic suppliers with actual delivery records.

Doosan Fuel Cell, the usual sector comparison, is centered on large stationary power projects and is much bigger by revenue, but the business models differ, which limits direct comparison.

The current cycle combines a phase of rising hydrogen-station orders with a phase in which expectations for submarine exports have weakened.

06

Outlook

The outlook rests on specific contracts and project schedules. One spare fuel-cell module for the Jang Bogo-III submarines is due for delivery by July 21, 2028, and analysts in early 2026 expected revenue recognition from Batch-II boats 2 and 3 to continue through 2026 as construction progresses.

A project to replace German-made fuel cells on the Jang Bogo-II class with domestic units, along with additional auxiliary fuel-cell modules and maintenance work, was also scheduled, according to the same analyst summary.

Following the Canadian loss, overseas submarine opportunities now depend on other projects, and the company is pursuing participation in submarine procurement programs in countries including Saudi Arabia.

Analysts' medium-term revenue estimate of at least KRW 360 billion was conditional on winning the Canadian project, and that condition has now fallen away.

In hydrogen stations, 12 stations were under construction as of the end of the third quarter of 2025, and the larger order book points to revenue recognition continuing after 2026.

In January 2026 the company decided on an exchangeable bond issue of KRW 7.03 billion, with treasury shares of 178,500 (2.04% of total issued shares) as the exchange subject, and the exchange period runs from February 3, 2026 to December 27, 2030.

Whether the operating loss, which narrowed to KRW 67.63 million in the third quarter of 2026, keeps narrowing is the next checkpoint. The next points to confirm are the operating margin and order backlog in the preliminary fourth-quarter 2026 results and the 2026 annual report.

Bull factors

Exclusive position in submarine fuel cells

Bumhan Fuel Cell has supplied fuel-cell modules for the Jang Bogo-III submarines on an exclusive basis since 2018, with four modules per submarine and build periods that analysts say support medium-term revenue.

The spare-module contract disclosed on July 20, 2026, worth about KRW 7.08 billion, runs through delivery by July 2028, and the limited number of domestic suppliers with delivery records is also cited as a foundation.

Hydrogen-station orders and revenue recovery

Cumulative order value was KRW 95.2 billion at the end of the third quarter of 2025, up sharply from KRW 24.6 billion at the end of 2024, and the KRW 67.6 billion eight-station liquefied-hydrogen contract is described by the company as its largest single contract to date.

Third-quarter 2026 revenue of KRW 26.1 billion, up 150.9% year on year, shows a clear revenue recovery. A KRW 3.6 billion Hanam hydrogen-station contract was added in March 2026.

Positive cash flow and narrowing losses

Operating cash flow was positive at KRW 8.91 billion in 2024 and KRW 21.7 billion in 2025.

The operating loss in the third quarter of 2026 narrowed to KRW 67.63 million from the prior quarter, and the net loss attributable to controlling shareholders also narrowed to KRW 2.12 billion from KRW 3.79 billion in the second quarter. Whether the narrowing continues into the next quarter is the central point for the bullish case.

Bear factors

Weaker hopes for overseas submarine exports

After the final award for the Canadian CPSP project fell through, the stock hit the daily limit down on July 7, 2026. Analysts had cited the project as a key medium-term growth driver, and reports said the loss weakened the export momentum. Medium-term revenue estimates that assumed a win in Canada no longer have that premise.

Continuing losses and their scale

Operating losses have run for four consecutive quarters from the fourth quarter of 2025 through the third quarter of 2026, and net losses attributable to controlling shareholders totaled KRW 5.92 billion over the same period.

In the second quarter of 2026 revenue rose to KRW 17.8 billion, yet the operating loss of KRW 3.23 billion was the largest in that stretch. Operating profit for 2025 also fell 64.4% to KRW 858 million.

Rising debt and an exchangeable bond issue

The debt-to-equity ratio rose from 88.4% in 2024 to 116.6% in 2025, and total liabilities at the end of 2025 were KRW 189.7 billion. In January 2026 the company decided on a KRW 7.03 billion exchangeable bond issue, with 178,500 treasury shares as the exchange subject.

Because revenue depends on large project orders, a gap in orders could become a source of change in financial capacity.

Risk factors

Order concentration and revenue volatility

Because revenue depends on large submarine and similar projects, delays in new orders could open gaps in revenue and widen earnings volatility, according to analyst commentary. After the Canadian loss, overseas submarine projects are seen as still lacking visible order track records.

Quarterly revenue swung sharply too, from KRW 5.05 billion in the first quarter of 2026 to KRW 17.8 billion in the second.

Profitability and working-capital needs

Operating losses have continued for four quarters, so how the turn back to profit appears in the next regular report is the key issue. The exchangeable bond proceeds were planned for raw-material purchases for liquefied-hydrogen station construction, with the largest outlay scheduled for the second quarter of 2026. With the debt-to-equity ratio at 116.6%, a rise in working-capital needs could add to financial strain.

Theme-driven volatility and share dilution

Fuel-cell stocks have swung between daily limit-up and limit-down moves on sector news, such as Doosan Fuel Cell's order developments and the Canadian bid outcome. The 178,500 shares subject to exchange equal 2.04% of total issued shares, and an exchange would increase the number of shares outstanding. The stock is sensitive to policy and overseas order news, which can produce wide price swings.

Overall view

Bumhan Fuel Cell rests on two pillars: an exclusive supply record in submarine fuel cells and a growing order book for liquefied-hydrogen charging stations. Annual revenue reached KRW 43.4 billion in 2025 and has grown in both 2024 and 2025 after the 2023 low, but operating profit fell 64.4% to KRW 858 million in 2025.

Third-quarter 2026 revenue of KRW 26.1 billion has risen for two consecutive quarters since the first-quarter 2026 low, but operating losses have continued for four quarters starting in the fourth quarter of 2025.

Net losses attributable to controlling shareholders totaled KRW 5.92 billion over the most recent four quarters, and the loss narrowed in the third quarter of 2026 from the prior quarter.

Positive factors include the submarine spare-module contract, the cumulative hydrogen-station order value and the turn to positive operating cash flow, while negative factors include the failed Canadian CPSP bid, continuing operating losses and a higher debt-to-equity ratio.

At the sector level, fuel-cell theme trading tied to AI data-center demand moves the stock, while the direct earnings basis remains limited to hydrogen-station and defense contracts.
