02

Business structure

Ewon Comfortech is a commercial-vehicle seat manufacturer headquartered in Jeongeup, North Jeolla Province, producing seats and interior parts for trucks, buses, construction equipment, rail cars, and vessels.

The company positions itself as the leading player by market share in Korea's commercial-vehicle seat market and has supplied seats to assembly plants of its main customer, Hyundai Motor.

Hyundai Motor's Jeonju plant in particular is a core customer site, and changes to that plant's production schedule directly affect Ewon Comfortech's quarterly results.

The company is moving away from its traditional OEM-centered commercial-vehicle seat structure and expanding into specialty-vehicle segments such as heavy equipment and agricultural machinery through its proprietary NEXSIS brand.

The NEXSIS brand is considered a relatively higher-margin business and has maintained revenue at or above prior-year levels even when companywide sales were disrupted by external factors.

Beyond its core business, the company holds stakes in Nasdaq-listed biotech firm Liminatus Pharma and domestic biotech firm Innovation Bio, both legacies of a past management team's biotech diversification push.

The company has stated it is not involved in the management of these investee companies and remains focused on its core commercial-vehicle seat business.

Separately, the company approved a share-consolidation agenda at its March 2026 annual general meeting and underwent a trading halt in May 2026 ahead of the listing of newly consolidated shares.

04

Earnings analysis

Annual revenue declined from KRW 47.8 billion in 2022 to KRW 47.1 billion in 2023 (down 1.4% year-on-year), then rose for two consecutive years to KRW 52.5 billion in 2024 (up 11.4%) and KRW 56.6 billion in 2025 (up 7.8%).

Operating profit fell from KRW 1.7 billion in 2022 to KRW 880 million in 2023 (down 48.4%), surged to KRW 2.47 billion in 2024 (up 180.9%), then contracted again to KRW 1.57 billion in 2025 (down 36.3%).

The operating margin fluctuated between 3.6% in 2022, 1.9% in 2023, 4.7% in 2024, and 2.8% in 2025, failing to settle on a stable trajectory.

Controlling-interest net loss narrowed from KRW 9.64 billion in 2022 to KRW 2.4 billion in 2023, widened again to KRW 21 billion in 2024, and narrowed to KRW 4.1 billion in 2025 — marking four consecutive years of losses from 2022 through 2025.

Operating cash flow fell from KRW 8.46 billion in 2022 to -KRW 56.55 million in 2023 and -KRW 961 million in 2024, remaining negative for two straight years before turning positive at KRW 2.74 billion in 2025.

On a quarterly basis, revenue fell from KRW 16.6 billion in the second quarter of 2025 to KRW 15.6 billion in the third quarter (down 6.2% quarter-on-quarter), contracted further to KRW 8.55 billion in the first quarter of 2026, then recovered to KRW 15.5 billion in the second quarter of 2026 (up 81.5% quarter-on-quarter, down 6.5% year-on-year).

Operating profit swung from a KRW 859 million loss in the first quarter of 2026 to a KRW 1.34 billion profit in the second quarter, up 101.5% year-on-year as well.

Controlling-interest net profit likewise turned from a KRW 1.54 billion loss in the first quarter of 2026 to a KRW 1.06 billion profit in the second quarter, up 1,238.8% year-on-year.

Still, the combined controlling-interest net loss across the latest four quarters (first through fourth quarters of 2025) totaled KRW 4.1 billion, meaning that despite sharp quarterly swings, the company has yet to escape losses on an annual basis.

05

Industry analysis

The commercial-vehicle seat industry in which Ewon Comfortech operates is centered on an original equipment manufacturing (OEM) structure directly tied to automakers' production schedules, meaning changes in a customer plant's utilization rate feed straight through to revenue.

In the first quarter of 2026, for instance, reduced production at Hyundai Motor's Jeonju plant due to new production line construction caused both revenue and operating profit to contract simultaneously.

Korea's commercial-vehicle seat market is understood to be an oligopoly among a small number of suppliers, and the company presents itself as the market-share leader.

By contrast, seats for specialty vehicles such as heavy equipment and agricultural machinery carry relatively higher entry barriers and profitability, which the company says gives it stronger margin defense than standard OEM commercial-vehicle seats.

Commercial-vehicle production is also sensitive to macro variables such as construction activity, logistics demand, and emissions-regulation transitions, contributing to quarter-to-quarter volatility common across the industry.

In terms of competitive positioning, maintaining Tier-1 supplier status with automakers is critical, and the degree of customer diversification serves as a gauge of medium- to long-term stability.

Bull factors

Second-Quarter Earnings Turnaround

Second-quarter 2026 revenue reached KRW 15.5 billion with operating profit of KRW 1.34 billion, both swinging to profit quarter-on-quarter, while controlling-interest net profit also turned positive at KRW 1.06 billion, up 1,238.8% year-on-year.

This is interpreted as reflecting the easing of the Hyundai Motor Jeonju plant construction disruption and the shipment of delayed backlog. Regaining quarterly profitability after a difficult stretch is a positive signal.

NEXSIS Brand and Specialty-Vehicle Portfolio Expansion

The higher-margin proprietary NEXSIS brand maintained revenue at or above prior-year levels even when companywide sales were disrupted by external factors, contributing to overall results defense.

The company is pursuing a strategy of broadening its portfolio into specialty-vehicle segments such as heavy equipment and agricultural machinery, moving away from its traditional OEM-centered seat business. This has the potential to reduce single-customer dependence while improving margin structure.

Balance-Sheet Repair Efforts and Controlling-Shareholder Purchases

Operating cash flow turned positive at KRW 2.74 billion in 2025 after two negative years, and recognition of the non-cash Liminatus Pharma shares on the balance sheet had a financial-structure-improving effect.

The controlling shareholder purchased 325,000 treasury-market shares in 2026, signaling a commitment to responsible management. These moves are linked to the broader effort to resolve the capital-impairment issue.

Bear factors

Four Consecutive Years of Controlling-Interest Net Losses

Controlling-interest net losses continued from 2022 through 2025, and even in 2022 and 2024 when operating profit was positive, the bottom line failed to escape losses. Non-operating items have amplified earnings volatility.

The combined net loss over the latest four quarters also reached KRW 4.1 billion, showing that the loss-making trend has not been easily resolved.

Capital Impairment and Administrative Issue Risk

The debt ratio jumped from 60.3% in 2023 to 148.7% in 2025, and total equity stood at only KRW 9.53 billion at the end of 2025. In a May 2026 statement, the company directly referenced a capital-impairment-related administrative issue designation and emphasized efforts to resolve it.

Until the normalization of the financial structure is confirmed, this remains a variable investors should watch closely.

Single-Customer and Single-Plant Dependence

As shown by the case where changes to Hyundai Motor's Jeonju plant production schedule pulled down both revenue and operating profit in the first quarter of 2026, dependence on a specific customer and plant is high.

If the customer's production line construction or demand changes recur, quarterly earnings volatility could widen again. Progress on customer diversification warrants continued monitoring.

Risk factors

Balance-Sheet Risk

The rise in the debt ratio combined with a shrinking equity base has resulted in the capital-impairment-related administrative issue designation being officially referenced in first-half 2026 disclosures.

The timing and method of resolution (rights offering, asset sale, retained-earnings accumulation, etc.) could have differing effects on shareholder value. Confirmation through the next periodic disclosure is needed.

Customer Concentration Risk

A significant portion of revenue is tied to commercial-vehicle seat supply for Hyundai Motor, so changes in the customer's production schedule or order volume directly affect results. The first-quarter 2026 slowdown is a representative example. If customer diversification proceeds slowly, this volatility could recur.

Non-Core Financial Asset Volatility

The company holds stakes, including in Nasdaq-listed Liminatus Pharma, as a legacy of its past biotech diversification, and changes in their valuation can affect reported earnings. Given the inherent share-price volatility of the biotech sector, the direction of valuation gains or losses is difficult to predict. Future disclosures of valuation gain/loss line items warrant continued attention.

Overall view

Ewon Comfortech sustained revenue growth in its core commercial-vehicle seat business through 2024 and 2025, but operating profit and controlling-interest net profit have fluctuated sharply, meaning a stable profit trajectory has yet to be fully established.

Results that fell sharply in the first quarter of 2026 due to the Hyundai Motor Jeonju plant construction disruption recovered in the second quarter, with revenue of KRW 15.5 billion, operating profit of KRW 1.34 billion, and controlling-interest net profit of KRW 1.06 billion.

However, four consecutive years of controlling-interest net losses, a debt ratio that climbed to 148.7% by the end of 2025, and the capital-impairment-related administrative issue risk the company itself referenced remain unresolved challenges.

The high-margin proprietary NEXSIS brand and specialty-vehicle portfolio expansion have the potential to reduce single-customer dependence, but whether they can stably support companywide results requires confirmation over additional quarters.

Valuation changes in non-core financial assets, including the Nasdaq-listed Liminatus Pharma stake, also remain a variable that could affect future earnings. It will be important to track the progress of balance-sheet normalization through the next quarterly report and any administrative-issue-related disclosures.
