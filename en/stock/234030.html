02

Business structure

Synic Solution is a system-semiconductor design house founded in 2005 that supports chip development through mass production, and it listed on KOSDAQ in July 2025.

Its core business is application-specific integrated circuit (ASIC) design services and mass-production support, and a report on 2025 results put ASIC at 91% of revenue.

Main product families are power management ICs (PMIC), CMOS image sensors (CIS) and display driver ICs (DDI), and the company says its revenue base rests on mass-production volumes linked to foundries. A 2026 report puts exports at 93% of total revenue.

The customer count was above 220 at the IPO stage, while a 2026 report cited about 170, so figures differ across sources. The top seven customers account for about 43% of sales.

The most important structural feature is that the company is the sole domestic design house for SK hynix System IC, so results track that foundry's utilization and process competitiveness. It is widening its foundry portfolio with EPISIL in Taiwan and AMF in Singapore, though those contribute a limited share of sales.

New businesses include MEMS microphones, MEMS ultrasonic sensors and SWIR image sensors, and ultrasonic sensor samples have gone to a medical device maker. The company also says it has been registered as a partner of Hyundai Mobis for automotive chips.

At the IPO, the company used overseas peers such as Taiwan's Alchip Technologies, since many domestic listed design houses were loss-making.

04

Earnings analysis

Annual results slowed clearly from 2024 to 2025. Revenue fell 7.7% to KRW 154.6 billion in 2025 from KRW 167.4 billion in 2024, and operating profit dropped 52.3% to KRW 2.51 billion from KRW 5.27 billion.

Profit attributable to controlling shareholders fell 68.8% to KRW 1.73 billion from KRW 5.53 billion, while net profit including non-controlling interests was KRW 1.77 billion in 2025 versus KRW 5.52 billion in 2024.

The company attributed the operating profit decline to higher selling and administrative expenses, including bad-debt provisions, and the net profit decline to higher financial costs from foreign-currency translation losses. The operating margin fell from 3.1% to 1.6%.

The balance sheet improved, with the debt-to-equity ratio down to 74.4% from 150.2%. At year-end 2025, total equity was KRW 36.5 billion, all attributable to controlling shareholders, and total liabilities were KRW 27.2 billion. Operating cash flow moved from KRW 710 million in 2024 to -KRW 2.38 billion in 2025.

Quarterly results recovered in 2026.

Revenue was KRW 42 billion in the fourth quarter of 2025 and KRW 40.4 billion in the first quarter of 2026, down 4.0% from the prior quarter, then rose to KRW 50 billion in the second quarter (up 23.8%) and KRW 65.9 billion in the third quarter (up 31.8%), the second straight quarterly increase.

Operating profit went from an operating loss of KRW 31.7 million in the first quarter to KRW 753 million in the second quarter and KRW 1.32 billion in the third, up 75.2% on the quarter.

Profit attributable to controlling shareholders was KRW 1.56 billion in the first quarter and KRW 1.05 billion in the second, then turned into a net loss of KRW 1.94 billion in the third quarter.

Because operating profit and controlling-shareholder profit moved in opposite directions in the third quarter, the composition of non-operating items should be checked in the 2026 annual report. Profit attributable to controlling shareholders over the last four quarters totals KRW 1.69 billion.

05

Industry analysis

The 8-inch foundry segment sits at the center of the current supply realignment. TrendForce estimates that the average 8-inch utilization rate of the top 10 global foundries recovered to about 88% in 2026 and expects it to reach 90% in the second half.

Reports say Samsung Electronics and TSMC have reduced 8-inch capacity, which has put legacy-process design houses in a bridging role. Foundry price increases could also lift 8-inch selling prices.

The company uses the 8-inch foundry network of SK hynix System IC in China, and some market observers see fabless chip volumes for PMIC and CIS moving to that production base. Some analysts argue that 8-inch processes face limited regulatory exposure compared with leading-edge nodes targeted by US China-related rules.

The company said customers seeking to relocate production or diversify supply chains are increasing, which it expects to lift demand for new design and verification projects. On competition, the company said most domestic listed design houses were loss-making, a comment made at the IPO stage.

On the other side, a concentration on 8-inch processes leaves the company sensitive to foundry capacity policy and pricing changes.

06

Outlook

During its 2025 IPO preparation, the company set a 2027 revenue target of KRW 210 billion. It named the expansion into 12-inch foundry business and sensor businesses as the means to reach it, and said it has secured two 12-inch customers.

The 12-inch foundry business is being developed with partners including PSMC in Taiwan and UMC.

The MEMS microphone has samples with companies in the United States and China for product-by-product certification, and its commercialization timeline has slipped from an originally expected late 2026 because of delays in sample production and validation.

In an interview reported in November 2026, the CEO said revenue is expected to begin and good news is expected during the first half of 2027. The ultrasonic sensor has begun sample supply to a medical device maker, and the SWIR sensor is being co-developed with an AI-based infrared technology company.

The company is building a sensor process on its own cost in a domestic 6-inch production line and is discussing a dedicated facility with local governments for sensor R&D through early mass production.

The points to confirm are therefore whether MEMS microphone revenue actually starts in the first half of 2027, whether more 12-inch customers are added, and whether annual revenue in 2027 approaches the KRW 210 billion target.

08

Bull factors

8-inch utilization recovery and a quarterly revenue rebound

Revenue rose in the second and third quarters of 2026, and the company disclosed record quarterly revenue for the third quarter. Higher 8-inch foundry utilization and larger PMIC-focused mass-production volumes were cited as drivers. TrendForce projected that the 8-inch utilization recovery would continue into the second half.

Wider customer contact in sensor businesses

MEMS microphone samples have gone to companies in the United States and China for product certification. Ultrasonic sensor samples have started going to a medical device maker. Registration as a Hyundai Mobis partner was cited as a basis for expansion into automotive.

Possible gains from foundry realignment

The company says customers are increasing as Samsung Electronics and TSMC reduce 8-inch capacity and as customers relocate production or diversify supply chains. The company said it has two 12-inch customers and is working with PSMC and UMC in Taiwan. Expectations for new design and verification demand were also cited.

09

Bear factors

Thin margins and falling operating profit

Operating profit fell 52.3% to KRW 2.51 billion in 2025, with an operating margin of 1.6%. Securities analysts said results fell short of the company's 2025 business plan of KRW 170 billion in revenue and KRW 5 billion in operating profit.

In the third quarter of 2026, a controlling-shareholder net loss of KRW 1.94 billion followed an operating profit.

Delays in sensor commercialization

The commercialization timeline for the MEMS microphone, originally expected by late 2026, was pushed back during sample production and validation. The mass-production start the company planned for the second half of 2025 at its listing was also adjusted later. The first-half 2027 revenue expectation is a company forecast and has not yet been realized.

Concentration in customers, regions and a partner

Exports account for 93% of revenue and the top seven customers for about 43%. A report said about 90% of overseas sales in 2024 were concentrated in China and Taiwan. Results are tied to the utilization and process competitiveness of SK hynix System IC, which makes partner dependence high.

10

Risk factors

Partner foundry dependence and geopolitical risk

Results depend on the utilization and process competitiveness of a SK hynix group foundry. Concentration in China and Taiwan makes the company sensitive to US-China tensions, export controls and tariff changes.

The company is broadening its foundry portfolio with EPISIL in Taiwan and AMF in Singapore, but their share of sales is limited.

Working capital and cash flow

Operating cash flow turned to -KRW 2.38 billion in 2025 as receivables and inventory rose together. Short-term borrowings were KRW 11 billion, up slightly from the prior year. Because revenue is recognized after delivery in a small-lot, many-product model, working capital burden is cited as large.

Exchange-rate swings and share supply

Foreign-currency translation losses from exchange-rate moves were cited as a factor in the 2025 net profit decline. Financial investors sold part of their stakes around listing and at the lock-up expiry, and a report said major volumes have been absorbed. The report noted that short-term overhang pressure existed.

12

Overall view

Synic Solution posted consecutive quarterly revenue gains in the second and third quarters of 2026, helped by a recovery in 8-inch foundry utilization, and returned to operating profit in the third quarter.

On the other side, annual operating profit fell 52.3% to KRW 2.51 billion in 2025, and the operating margin stayed at 1.6%. The controlling-shareholder net loss of KRW 1.94 billion in the third quarter of 2026 moved in the opposite direction from operating profit, so the quality of earnings needs separate checking.

Operating cash flow turned to a net outflow in 2025, and the structural limit of depending on a specific foundry and a small set of customers remains. Sensor businesses have advanced with MEMS microphone and ultrasonic sensor samples, but the commercialization timeline has moved to the first half of 2027.

The debt-to-equity ratio fell to 74.4% in 2025 from 150.2% in 2024, and total equity reached KRW 36.5 billion. Bullish factors are the foundry realignment and the sensor shift, while bearish factors are thin margins and delayed new businesses, and the weight of each will become clearer at the next checkpoints.

The next checkpoints are the non-operating items in the 2026 annual report and whether MEMS microphone revenue starts in the first half of 2027.
