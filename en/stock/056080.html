Sales Recovering, Losses Persisting in a Transition Phase

Yujin Robot has restructured around autonomous logistics and factory automation, and its Q3 2026 revenue rose 123.8% year on year. However, it has posted net losses attributable to controlling shareholders for five consecutive quarters since Q3 2025, so whether profitability recovers is the central issue.

02

Business structure

Yujin Robot is a KOSDAQ-listed machinery and equipment company focused on autonomous logistics robots and smart factory automation systems. It operates two business divisions.

The Smart Factory (SAS) division supplies factory automation equipment to automotive parts, semiconductor and heavy industry sites, offering integrated systems that combine FMS, MES, WMS and digital twin technology.

The Autonomous Mobility (AMS) division is built on the GoCart autonomous logistics robot, along with the company's in-house 3D LiDAR sensors and SLAM technology. The company ended its domestic robot vacuum business at the end of 2023 and refocused on factory automation.

The CEO has stated that more than half of total revenue in 2025 came from overseas operations. The company established a European subsidiary in Germany in 2025, and in a March 2026 statement it said it plans to set up a U.S. subsidiary in 2027.

At the AW 2026 exhibition it showed a wafer transfer automation solution for semiconductor fabs and a high-payload autonomous mobile robot capable of carrying up to 2 tons. Its customers range from large manufacturers such as global automotive parts makers to hospitals and logistics sites.

Competition is taking shape less around standalone robot hardware and more around robotization solutions that combine autonomous platforms, collaborative robots, sensors and software.

04

Earnings analysis

On a disclosed annual basis, revenue fell from KRW 49.6 billion in 2022 to KRW 30.4 billion in 2023 (down 38.7% year on year) and KRW 26 billion in 2024 (down 14.4%), before rising to KRW 28.2 billion in 2025 (up 8.3%). 2022 was the largest year and 2024 the smallest among 2022 to 2025.

Operating profit of KRW 3.57 billion in 2022 turned into an operating loss of KRW 4.68 billion in 2023, narrowed to KRW 4.22 billion in 2024, and widened to KRW 5.03 billion in 2025, marking three consecutive years of operating losses.

The operating margin moved from 7.2% in 2022 to -15.4% in 2023, -16.2% in 2024 and -17.9% in 2025. Margins also declined during 2023 and 2024, when revenue was falling.

One securities data site attributed the wider 2025 operating loss to losses from ending the robot vacuum and joint technology development businesses, along with higher administrative costs.

Net income attributable to controlling shareholders went from KRW 4.88 billion in 2022 to a net loss of KRW 5.18 billion in 2023, a narrower loss of KRW 3.19 billion in 2024, and a wider loss of KRW 6.98 billion in 2025. Net income including non-controlling interests was reported at the same amounts in each year.

Operating cash flow alternated between inflows and outflows: KRW 3.13 billion in 2022, -KRW 5.69 billion in 2023, KRW 1.92 billion in 2024 and -KRW 5.39 billion in 2025. On a quarterly basis, Q3 2025 revenue was KRW 3.48 billion with an operating loss of KRW 1.69 billion.

Q4 2025 revenue jumped to KRW 20.4 billion (up 487.0% from the prior quarter) and the company posted an operating profit of KRW 1.97 billion, a swing to profit. Q1 2026 revenue fell 75.5% to KRW 5.01 billion, with an operating loss of KRW 3.98 billion.

Q2 2026 revenue was KRW 8.2 billion with an operating loss of KRW 2.95 billion. Q3 2026 revenue was KRW 7.78 billion, up 123.8% year on year, with an operating loss of KRW 2.04 billion, which was a wider loss than a year earlier. Total equity at end-2025 was KRW 28.7 billion, total liabilities KRW 25.1 billion, and the debt ratio 87.3%.

05

Industry analysis

The robotics sector drew attention in 2026 from both policy and themes. The government has said it plans to channel about KRW 32 trillion of the KRW 150 trillion National Growth Fund, to be raised over five years, into AI and robotics. Some analysts view 2026 as the first year of mass production for humanoid robots.

Robots and physical AI drew attention at CES 2026, and capital flowed into related stocks. Yujin Robot was added to the KOSDAQ 150 index and was included in a humanoid robot ETF listed in January 2026.

These flow-driven factors can move separately from earnings, so industry cycles and share price moves should be viewed apart. The company is regarded as one of Korea's first-generation robot makers and promotes a robotization strategy that combines autonomous platforms with factory automation equipment.

Compared with peers grouped with it, such as Rainbow Robotics, Yujin Robot has a larger weight in autonomous logistics and factory system integration. Having exited the consumer robot vacuum business to focus on B2B, it does not directly capture growth in consumer markets.

Whether broader demand for robots converts into factory automation orders, and how fast that shift happens, is what separates its position within the sector.

06

Outlook

The 2026 targets come from a January 2026 interview with the CEO. The CEO set a goal of more than doubling AMS revenue and growing SAS revenue by more than 50% in 2026. In the same interview he said product costs had been cut by about 15% the previous year and that the target is to extend the reduction to 30%.

These figures are company targets, and they can be checked against actual results when the annual report is disclosed. The company also said it planned full-scale mass production of special-purpose robots, including for defense applications, starting in the second half of 2026.

It presented an autonomous mobile manipulator that combines a mobile base with collaborative robots as a product in development. At AW 2026 in March, it showed a wafer transfer solution and a high-payload AMR.

The third-party share issuance of about KRW 17.3 billion announced in May 2026 is said to fund R&D, expansion of mass production facilities and overseas growth. A supply contract of KRW 8.99 billion for display assembly lines, running from April 2025 to January 2026, was also corrected in a disclosure.

The counterparty and sales region are scheduled to be disclosed after a confidentiality period ends. On overseas plans, a U.S. subsidiary is positioned after the European one, with its establishment planned for 2027.

The CEO said the company aims to achieve visible sales growth and an improved earnings structure based on technology validated in Europe. The next checkpoints are whether the 2026 annual report shows AMS and SAS growth in line with targets, and whether operating losses keep narrowing along the 2026 quarterly trend.

Bull factors

Overseas Mix and Subsidiary Expansion

After a European subsidiary established in 2025, a U.S. subsidiary is planned for 2027. The company intends to extend solutions validated at domestic semiconductor and battery sites into Europe and the United States.

Stated Growth Targets for 2026

The CEO set a target of more than doubling AMS revenue and growing SAS by more than 50% in 2026. The cost reduction goal was also raised from about 15% the previous year to 30%. Whether these targets were met can be checked against segment results in the 2026 annual report.

Funding and Policy Tailwinds

Proceeds from the about KRW 17.3 billion third-party share issuance announced in May 2026 are said to fund R&D and expansion of mass production facilities. The government plans to put about KRW 32 trillion of the National Growth Fund into AI and robotics. Yujin Robot is also cited in flow terms for its inclusion in the KOSDAQ 150 index and a humanoid robot ETF.

09

Bear factors

Continued Losses and Cumulative Deficits

The 2025 operating loss was KRW 5.03 billion and the net loss attributable to controlling shareholders was KRW 6.98 billion, both wider than a year earlier. After an operating loss of KRW 3.98 billion in Q1 2026, losses narrowed to KRW 2.95 billion in Q2 and KRW 2.04 billion in Q3, but they have continued. The net loss attributable to controlling shareholders over the last four quarters totals KRW 9.16 billion.

Quarterly Revenue Volatility

Q4 2025 revenue of KRW 20.4 billion was the largest quarter in the disclosed series, but it fell 75.5% to KRW 5.01 billion in Q1 2026. Such large swings reduce the predictability of earnings. Operating cash flow in 2025 was also negative at -KRW 5.39 billion.

Rising Financial Leverage

The debt ratio at end-2025 was 87.3%, more than double the 38.8% recorded in 2022. Total equity was KRW 28.7 billion and total liabilities KRW 25.1 billion. Capital is being strengthened through a third-party issuance to the largest shareholder.

10

Risk factors

Funding and Share Count Changes

The third-party issuance targets the largest shareholder and is about KRW 17.3 billion. New shares will increase the share count and change per-share metrics. The stated uses are R&D, mass production facilities and overseas expansion, and how the funds are spent will need to be confirmed through future disclosures.

Theme Flows and Share Price Volatility

The share price has reacted sharply to robot theme buying and to index and ETF inclusion. Theme-driven buying can reverse regardless of earnings. Because the stock is often traded alongside other robotics names, it is also exposed to sector-wide flow changes.

Dependence on Large Individual Contracts

The KRW 8.99 billion display assembly line supply contract, running from April 2025 to January 2026, was disclosed with an unlisted counterparty kept confidential. The counterparty will be disclosed after the confidentiality period ends. A high reliance on single large contracts increases the swings in quarterly revenue.
