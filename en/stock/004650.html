Changhae Ethanol: Checking the Quarterly Earnings Trend

02

Business structure

Changhae Ethanol's core business is producing spirits-grade ethanol, with energy, engineering, trading and feed manufacturing as secondary operations.

In its quarterly report as of March 31, 2025, fermented spirits accounted for 43.77% of sales, refined spirits for 43.41% and other items for 1.21%, so the spirits unit makes up most of revenue.

A corporate monitoring report notes that more than 90% of the spirits business is used as raw material for diluted soju, which links demand closely to the soju market. The energy segment supplies steam fuel and represented 4.84% of sales in the same quarter.

The engineering segment handles mechanical facility construction and accounted for 12.86%. The trading segment, CHANGHAE VIETNAM JSC, collects tapioca chips, an ethanol feedstock, in Vietnam and represented 0.95%.

In the first half of 2026, the energy segment's aging incinerator boiler and operating losses in the engineering and trading segments were cited as drags on results.

According to the Korea Alcohol Industry Association's 2024 survey, the company held a 19.8% share of the spirits market, the largest in the industry, 3.4 percentage points ahead of the runner-up at 16.4%.

The company's quarterly report says its in-house R&D institute holds leading domestic technology in second-generation bioethanol component and demonstration work.

04

Earnings analysis

On an annual basis, revenue rose from KRW 109.4 billion in 2022 to KRW 116.4 billion in 2023 (up 6.4% year on year) and KRW 137.5 billion in 2024 (up 18.1%), then fell to KRW 122.3 billion in 2025 (down 11.0%). Among 2022 to 2025, 2024 was the largest year and 2022 the smallest.

Operating profit rose from KRW 9.77 billion in 2022 to KRW 14.6 billion in 2023 (up 49.3%) and KRW 17.4 billion in 2024 (up 19.2%), then declined to KRW 15.9 billion in 2025 (down 8.6%).

The operating margin moved from 8.9% in 2022 to 12.5% in 2023, 12.6% in 2024 and 13.0% in 2025, so margins improved even in 2025 when revenue fell.

Net income attributable to controlling shareholders was KRW 77.56 million in 2022, swung to a net loss of KRW 3.84 billion in 2023, returned to a profit of KRW 12.5 billion in 2024 and reached KRW 13.3 billion in 2025 (up 6.0%).

Net income including non-controlling interests was KRW 13.1 billion in 2025, a different figure from the controlling-shareholder number. Operating cash flow grew from KRW 3.12 billion in 2022 to KRW 20.3 billion in 2025.

On a quarterly basis, revenue of KRW 32.4 billion in the fourth quarter of 2025 (up 15.8% from the prior quarter) was the highest of the last five quarters, while third-quarter 2026 revenue of KRW 27.7 billion fell 11.0% from the prior quarter and 1.0% from a year earlier.

Operating profit peaked at KRW 4.43 billion in the second quarter of 2026, then dropped to KRW 2.43 billion in the third quarter (down 45.1% from the prior quarter and 39.0% from a year earlier).

Net income attributable to controlling shareholders peaked at KRW 3.47 billion in the first quarter of 2026 and then declined to KRW 3.42 billion in the second quarter (down 1.4%) and KRW 2.11 billion in the third quarter (down 38.1%).

A corporate monitoring report, as of September 3, 2026, put first-half 2026 consolidated revenue down 4.2%, operating profit down 7.5% and net income down 8.7% from a year earlier. IB Tomato separately attributed the rise in third-quarter 2025 operating cash flow to lower inventory rather than sales growth.

Industry analysis

Changhae Ethanol's spirits market depends on demand for diluted soju raw material. A corporate monitoring report notes that more than 90% of the spirits business goes into diluted soju and stresses the link to the soju market. Weaker liquor consumption is weighing on the sector as a whole.

IB Tomato reported that in the third quarter of 2025, peers earned profits outside spirits while Changhae Ethanol was weak in both spirits and other businesses. Lower raw material prices were a favorable backdrop.

The same report noted that the third-quarter 2025 cost ratio fell to 76.19% from 78.37% a year earlier, but a larger drop in sales volume kept profits from being protected.

According to the Korea Alcohol Industry Association's 2024 survey, the company's 19.8% share ranks first, while the other three companies in the top four hold 16.4%, 9.7% and 9.5%. The company's website describes it as a bioethanol producer with leading domestic ethanol production technology.

A corporate monitoring report sees Vietnam's bioethanol blending mandate and rising ethanol plant investment in Southeast Asia as possible growth drivers. Because end demand is tied to soju production, liquor consumption trends pass directly into spirits-unit results.

Risk factors

Raw materials and cost

Margins are sensitive to cost ratio movements. The third-quarter 2025 cost ratio of 76.19% was below 78.37% a year earlier, but a rebound in raw material prices could pressure margins again. The trading unit's tapioca collection is also exposed to local supply conditions in Vietnam.

Demand and liquor consumption

Spirits demand is tied to soju production, so it is sensitive to liquor consumption trends. When consumption weakens, quarterly revenue is affected, and third-quarter 2026 revenue of KRW 27.7 billion fell 11.0% from the prior quarter. With the non-spirits units contributing little to profit, there is limited cushion against a demand slowdown.

Governance and subsidiary factors

The largest shareholder changed to Lim Woo-seok through a gift on September 30, 2026, and a new joint report covering the remaining holders is planned. Aging equipment in the energy segment and operating losses in the engineering and trading units are sources of consolidated earnings volatility. The effect of the governance change on operations can be checked in the next periodic report.
