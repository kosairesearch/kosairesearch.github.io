02

Business structure

Biotoxtech is a non-clinical CRO (contract research organization) that evaluates the safety and efficacy of drug candidates through animal studies. The company handles toxicity and efficacy evaluation in rodents and non-rodents, while primate safety and efficacy testing is carried out by its subsidiary.

Based on the 2025 annual report, non-clinical testing revenue split 83.18% domestic and 16.82% export, and a March 2026 press report said the export share rose 2.5 percentage points from 2024.

The subsidiary generated about KRW 14 billion in 2025 revenue, roughly 30% of consolidated sales, and the company said about 50% of its total orders come from customers in the United States and Europe.

Clients include domestic and foreign pharmaceutical and biotech companies, and testing scope has widened from synthetic drugs to ADCs, ASOs, peptides and next-generation vaccines. The company says it is smaller than multinational CROs but competes on regulatory response capability and quality rather than price.

A one-stop service covering toxicity testing, biosample analysis and data packages for FDA submissions is cited as a driver of overseas orders.

Raw materials include test animals, reagents, feed, bedding and test consumables; in the 2021 annual report, test animal purchases accounted for nearly half of raw material purchases.

According to the company, its Osong facility in Chungbuk, built in 2022, began full operation and has supported more GLP orders from overseas biotech clients.

04

Earnings analysis

Annual revenue fell from KRW 37.3 billion in 2022 to KRW 32.6 billion in 2023, a 12.5% decline, then rose 26.7% to KRW 41.3 billion in 2024 and 13.7% to KRW 47 billion in 2025. After the 2023 low, revenue grew in both 2024 and 2025, and 2025 was the largest year of 2022 to 2025.

Press coverage tied the 2023 drop to a weak domestic biotech market and cuts in pharma and biotech research spending. Operating profit moved from KRW 800 million in 2022 to an operating loss of KRW 8.19 billion in 2023, widened to KRW 8.88 billion in 2024, and narrowed to KRW 4.81 billion in 2025.

Operating margin went from 2.1% in 2022 to -25.1% in 2023, -21.5% in 2024 and -10.2% in 2025, and the company attributed the 2025 improvement to fixed-cost absorption from higher sales.

Profit attributable to owners turned positive at KRW 1.16 billion in 2025, while net loss stood at KRW 1.57 billion, so the non-controlling interest share pulled the two figures in opposite directions.

Total equity of KRW 3.19 billion at end-2025 was below the KRW 4.02 billion owner stake, which implies a negative non-controlling interest balance.

Quarterly, Q3 2025 showed revenue of KRW 11.9 billion, an operating loss of KRW 1.36 billion and owner profit of KRW 5.51 billion; Q4 2025 shifted to an operating loss of KRW 97.59 million, narrower than before, and an owner net loss of KRW 1.83 billion.

Q1 2026 revenue was KRW 11.9 billion, down 16.7% quarter on quarter, with an operating loss of KRW 1.82 billion. Q2 2026 revenue rose 21.9% to KRW 14.5 billion and the operating loss narrowed to KRW 182 million, yet the owner net loss widened to KRW 5.81 billion.

Q3 2026 revenue reached KRW 16.1 billion, up 11.1% quarter on quarter and 35.0% year on year, with operating profit of KRW 579 million and an owner net loss narrowed to KRW 797 million.

The gap between operating profit and owner net loss appears to reflect non-operating items and the allocation to non-controlling interests, which the next annual report should clarify.

Operating cash flow was negative for three straight years, at KRW -1.31 billion in 2022, KRW -16.2 billion in 2023 and KRW -7.12 billion in 2024, before turning to KRW 6.64 billion in 2025.

05

Industry analysis

Demand for non-clinical CROs moves with the R&D spending of pharmaceutical and biotech companies. In 2023, a weak domestic biotech industry and cuts to drug pipeline research weighed on the sector, and the company's revenue fell that year.

A March 2026 press report noted that domestic pharma and biotech licensing deals exceeded KRW 20 trillion for the first time in 2025, which was cited as a driver of non-clinical demand. Government-backed drug research projects tied to biotech industry promotion policy are also cited as a demand factor.

A company official said it has secured clients in ADC and obesity-drug programs, and said demand for primate CRO services exceeds supply, leading key clients to place advance orders.

On competition, a financial news outlet described the company as the domestic leader in toxicity-testing non-clinical CROs, but the company itself says it is smaller than multinational CROs.

The company emphasizes regulatory response capability, including FDA-related support, over price competition as it pursues overseas clients. Celltrion's stake of about 10.22%, as reported in February 2026, is viewed by analysts as a relationship that could help capture biopharma-driven non-clinical demand.

However, the 2023 downturn showed that CRO demand is sensitive to biotech funding conditions and clinical pipeline investment.

06

Outlook

The company said its KRW 60.7 billion consolidated order backlog at the end of the first half of 2026 will be recognized as revenue sequentially from the second half, according to its August 2026 announcement.

The company also said profit growth would widen from Q3 and that a full-year operating profit is likely, but this is management's view, and with operating losses reported for Q1 and Q2, the full-year outcome must be confirmed in the 2026 annual report.

A March 2026 press report projected double-digit revenue growth and better profitability. In a February 2026 shareholder letter, the company declared 2026 the first year of an earnings turnaround and outlined share buybacks, cancellations and expanded investor relations.

The same letter put the end-2025 backlog at KRW 41.5 billion, while a March 2026 report cited KRW 52 billion, so the two figures likely reflect different definitions that should be checked. Press coverage said the primate subsidiary's Q1 2026 orders were about KRW 15 billion, roughly 75% of its full-year 2025 total.

The subsidiary reported Q2 2026 revenue of KRW 5.8 billion and operating profit of KRW 400 million, its first quarterly profit, according to the company. The subsidiary is preparing for an IPO, though the timing and whether it will list have not been set, as of a February 2026 report.

As overseas orders grow, the effect of exchange rates and overseas contract terms on revenue recognition is the next item to track.

08

Bull factors

Revenue growth backed by orders

Q3 2026 revenue reached KRW 16.1 billion, up 35.0% year on year, extending the sales growth that began in 2024. The company reported a consolidated order backlog of KRW 60.7 billion at mid-2026 and said it will be recognized sequentially from the second half.

Because orders take time to convert into revenue, how much of this backlog shows up in revenue after Q4 2026 is the point to watch for this strength case.

Improvement at the operating level

The 2025 operating loss narrowed to KRW 4.81 billion, and Q3 2026 operating profit of KRW 579 million marked a quarterly turn to profit. Operating margin improved from -25.1% in 2023 to -10.2% in 2025. The company's explanation of fixed-cost absorption as sales rise is consistent with the direction of the recovery.

Overseas orders and subsidiary growth

According to the company, its primate-focused subsidiary posted its first quarterly profit in Q2 2026, with revenue of KRW 5.8 billion and operating profit of KRW 400 million. The company said overseas clients made up more than 60% of the KRW 17.2 billion in new contracts signed in the first half of 2026.

Growth in drug pipelines at domestic and foreign pharma and biotech firms, along with Celltrion's shareholding, is cited as background for demand and credibility.

Bear factors

Four straight quarters of owner net losses

Losses attributable to owners have run for four straight quarters from Q4 2025 through Q3 2026, totaling KRW 10.3 billion over the last four quarters. The KRW 5.81 billion owner net loss in Q2 2026 was the largest of the four.

Even with Q3 2026 operating profit, an owner net loss of KRW 797 million remained, so operating profit did not carry through to net income.

Rising debt burden

The debt-to-equity ratio rose every year, from 183.3% in 2022 to 204.8% in 2023, 294.5% in 2024 and 312.2% in 2025. Total liabilities of KRW 99.7 billion at end-2025 were more than three times total equity of KRW 3.19 billion.

Because total equity is below the owner stake, the non-controlling interest balance is negative, and accumulated losses may have reduced financial capacity.

Three years of operating losses and cash outflow

The company recorded operating losses for three consecutive years from 2023 through 2025, and 2023 revenue fell 12.5%. Operating cash flow was KRW -16.2 billion in 2023 and KRW -7.12 billion in 2024, two straight years of outflows, and turned positive at KRW 6.64 billion only in 2025.

How far that single year of inflow offsets the earlier outflows needs to be confirmed in the 2026 annual report.

10

Risk factors

Regulation and policy (changes to animal testing standards)

The U.S. Food and Drug Administration is reported to have announced a roadmap in April 2025 aimed at reducing reliance on animal testing. The subsidiary's primate testing demand is heavily tied to overseas clients, so a change in foreign regulatory standards could reshape that demand. Faster adoption of alternative testing methods in Korea could also affect the test mix of non-clinical CROs.

Subsidiary listing and financing

The subsidiary has begun preparatory work for an IPO, according to the company's February 2026 shareholder letter. A listing would change the subsidiary's ownership structure and how consolidated profit is allocated, while a delay could push back the company's plans to re-rate its value. Depending on how funds are raised, existing shareholders' stakes could also be diluted.

Exchange rates and overseas client concentration

Export revenue accounted for 16.82% of non-clinical testing revenue in 2025, and about half of the subsidiary's total orders come from overseas clients. Movements in the won affect the won value and profitability of overseas orders directly.

If overseas clients cancel contracts or reschedule in bulk, the pace at which the backlog converts into revenue could also shift.

Overall view

Biotoxtech's revenue grew in 2024 and 2025 in consecutive years, and Q3 2026 revenue of KRW 16.1 billion rose 35.0% year on year. The KRW 60.7 billion consolidated order backlog at mid-2026 is a company-disclosed figure that frames how much revenue may follow from the second half.

On an owner basis, however, net losses have persisted over the last four quarters, totaling KRW 10.3 billion. The debt-to-equity ratio reached 312.2% at end-2025, up from 2022, and total equity is smaller than the owner stake.

The strength factors are order-driven revenue growth and the subsidiary's first quarterly profits, while the weakness factors are continuing net losses and a heavy debt load. Which factor shows up first in annual results will be determined by the 2026 full-year figures and the 2026 annual report. Multiples move with the share price, so they should be compared with the filed figures at each point in time.
