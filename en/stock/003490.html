Defense Mix Grows, Passenger Volatility Remains

02

Business structure

Korean AIR Lines operates as an airline and a defense contractor, with passenger, cargo and aerospace (defense) as its core businesses.

In preliminary third-quarter 2026 results released on October 15, 2026 (standalone basis), passenger revenue was KRW 2.92 trillion and cargo revenue was KRW 1.43 trillion; these preliminary figures use a different basis from the disclosed financials.

The passenger business is sensitive to oil prices and the won-dollar exchange rate. The company said fuel surcharge burdens and a weaker won dampened demand from Korea, while inbound demand to Korea and transfer traffic at Incheon rose.

The cargo business has been growing on steady demand for high-value AI and semiconductor-related freight. The Aerospace division handles military aircraft maintenance, repair and upgrade (MROU), medium-altitude unmanned aerial vehicles (MUAV), and aircraft system development.

In the first quarter of 2026, the division posted revenue of KRW 252.2 billion and operating profit of KRW 19.8 billion, as reported in May 2026. Order backlog at the end of the first quarter of 2026 was KRW 4.7 trillion, of which military aircraft MROU and drones totaled KRW 2.69 trillion, or 57%.

Major large projects include the UH-60 Black Hawk helicopter performance upgrade (KRW 830.2 billion) and an electronic warfare system development project (reported at KRW 1.56 trillion).

Its customers center on defense authorities, and it also participates in a U.S. military regional maintenance hub program covering F-16, F-15 and A-10 aircraft.

On the passenger side, competition with domestic airlines on routes is intensifying; on the defense side, it is compared with Korea Aerospace Industries and Hanwha Aerospace.

04

Earnings analysis

On the disclosed annual basis, revenue rose from KRW 14.1 trillion in 2022 to KRW 16.1 trillion in 2023 (up 14.3% year on year), KRW 17.9 trillion in 2024 (up 10.9%), and KRW 25.2 trillion in 2025 (up 41.2%).

Over the same period, operating profit fell from KRW 2.83 trillion in 2022 to KRW 1.79 trillion in 2023 (down 36.8%), recovered to KRW 2.11 trillion in 2024 (up 17.9%), and then fell to KRW 1.11 trillion in 2025 (down 47.2%).

The operating margin declined from 20.1% in 2022, to 11.1% in 2023 and 11.8% in 2024, to 4.4% in 2025. 2025 was the largest year of the four in revenue and the lowest in operating margin, so scale grew while the conversion into operating profit weakened.

Net profit attributable to owners fell from KRW 1.73 trillion in 2022 to KRW 779.7 billion in 2025, while net profit including non-controlling interests was KRW 647.3 billion in 2025. The debt-to-equity ratio rose from 209.6% in 2023 to 328.8% in 2024 and 339.9% in 2025.

At the end of 2025, total equity was KRW 11.5 trillion and total liabilities were KRW 38.9 trillion. Operating cash flow declined from KRW 5.57 trillion in 2022 to KRW 4.08 trillion in 2025 but stayed in the KRW 4 trillion range.

Quarterly revenue rose from KRW 6.03 trillion in the third quarter of 2025 to KRW 7.32 trillion in the third quarter of 2026, with four consecutive quarters of growth from the fourth quarter of 2025.

Operating profit was KRW 517.4 billion in the first quarter of 2026, up 234.0% from the prior quarter, then an operating loss of KRW 207.1 billion in the second quarter, and KRW 349.2 billion in the third quarter, a return to profit.

The company cited fuel surcharge burdens and a high exchange rate for the second-quarter weakness (preliminary results released October 15, 2026).

Net profit attributable to owners moved from a KRW 119.3 billion loss in the third quarter of 2025 to KRW 129.4 billion profit in the fourth quarter, KRW 121.8 billion profit in the first quarter of 2026, a KRW 387.9 billion loss in the second quarter, and KRW 1.1 trillion profit in the third quarter. The sum for the last four quarters was KRW 963 billion.

05

Industry analysis

The end markets move in two directions. On the defense side, a forecast published in March 2026 by DB Securities projected record Korean defense exports of USD 37.7 billion for 2026.

In the same forecast, expected order volumes were KRW 23.3 trillion for Hanwha Aerospace, KRW 23.2 trillion for Hyundai-Rotem, and KRW 6.5 trillion for Korea Aerospace Industries.

As Polish orders declined, Korea Aerospace Industries reported third-quarter 2026 revenue up 51.6% but net profit down 51.1%, according to a November 2026 report.

Within Korean AIR Lines, military aircraft MROU backlog rose 22% from KRW 1.54 trillion in the fourth quarter of 2025 to KRW 1.87 trillion in the first quarter of 2026, and drone orders climbed from KRW 34.3 billion in 2022 to KRW 949.9 billion in 2024.

The company said that conflicts in Russia and the Middle East are expanding the global defense market. The passenger market faces opposite pressure: route competition among airlines is intensifying and passenger profitability is slowing, while the prolonged Middle East war has raised fuel costs.

On the cycle, defense is in a phase of backlog accumulation, while passenger earnings swing sharply with oil prices and exchange rates.

Relative to peers, the combination of airline and defense operations is distinctive, but the defense share of revenue is still a fraction of the total, so passenger exposure remains large.

06

Outlook

The company expects fuel cost pressure from the prolonged Middle East war to continue into the fourth quarter of 2026. Its passenger business aims to grow results and profitability centered on inbound demand to Korea and transfer traffic through Korea.

Its cargo business plans to adjust capacity flexibly around demand for AI and semiconductor products and the year-end air cargo peak season. The combined airline with Asiana Airlines launches on December 17, 2026, and the company is focusing on re-checking safety systems and raising service quality ahead of the launch.

In defense, the first medium-altitude unmanned aerial vehicle (MUAV) for mass production was delivered in April 2026, and after Air Force acceptance testing, deliveries are scheduled to begin in sequence from 2027. The mass production program is reported at roughly KRW 980 billion.

According to a report by Mirae Asset Securities, revenue could rise sharply from the early 2030s, when the UH-60 upgrade, the airborne control aircraft second phase and the electronic warfare program enter production.

The same report described plans to invest about KRW 200 billion by 2029 in a new multipurpose plant covering about 11,000 pyeong. iM Securities, in a March 2026 report, said aerospace growth could take off from 2026 and that annual revenue of KRW 1.2 trillion is achievable.

These views are analyst and press assessments rather than company guidance and may change with program schedules and order volumes.

08

Bull factors

Defense backlog shifts the mix

Of the KRW 4.7 trillion backlog at the end of the first quarter of 2026, military aircraft MROU and drones account for 57%. The military MROU backlog rose 22% from the fourth quarter of 2025 to the first quarter of 2026.

The Aerospace division posted first-quarter 2026 revenue of KRW 252.2 billion and operating profit of KRW 19.8 billion, per May 2026 reporting. Brokerage observers note that a larger defense share could partly cushion swings in passenger earnings tied to oil and exchange rates.

Passenger and cargo demand mix

Revenue rose for four consecutive quarters from the fourth quarter of 2025 through the third quarter of 2026. The company said passenger demand gained from inbound travel to Korea and transfer traffic at Incheon in the third quarter of 2026.

In cargo, high-value AI and semiconductor-related freight showed steady demand. 2025 revenue rose 41.2% to KRW 25.2 trillion. If demand holds, the conversion of top-line growth into profit has room to improve.

Return to quarterly profit

Net profit attributable to owners swung from a KRW 387.9 billion loss in the second quarter of 2026 to KRW 1.1 trillion profit in the third. Operating profit moved from a KRW 207.1 billion loss in the second quarter to KRW 349.2 billion in the third.

Operating profit also recovered in 2024, rising 17.9% to KRW 2.11 trillion. With the combined airline launching shortly, the integration effect will be visible in upcoming quarterly profit.

Bear factors

Operating margin decline

The operating margin fell from 20.1% in 2022 to 4.4% in 2025. Operating profit fell 47.2% to KRW 1.11 trillion in 2025, and net profit attributable to owners fell 40.8% to KRW 779.7 billion. The second quarter of 2026 brought an operating loss of KRW 207.1 billion. The bear case rests on the point that when costs rise alongside revenue, margin recovery is limited.

Exposure to oil and the exchange rate

The company cited fuel surcharge burdens and a high exchange rate for weakness in the second quarter of 2026. It expects fuel costs from the prolonged Middle East war to remain a burden in the fourth quarter. Route competition among airlines is intensifying in passenger markets.

If oil and exchange rates move against the company at the same time, quarterly profit could slip back into loss.

High debt leverage

The debt-to-equity ratio jumped from 209.6% in 2023 to 328.8% in 2024 and reached 339.9% in 2025. At the end of 2025, total liabilities of KRW 38.9 trillion far exceeded total equity of KRW 11.5 trillion. Operating cash flow stayed in the KRW 4 trillion range but declined from KRW 5.57 trillion in 2022. When earnings fall, interest and repayment burdens amplify swings in net profit.

Risk factors

Governance and defense regulation

In the contest for control of the parent holding company, the Hoban group holds 20.69% and a related party of the chairman holds 20.58%, a gap of 0.11 percentage points. The parent holds 26.13% of Korean AIR Lines' common shares.

Under the Defense Industry Act, a change in management control of a defense contractor requires approval from the Minister of Trade, Industry and Energy. A bill to add the Minister of National Defense to the prior consultation list has been introduced in the National Assembly.

Integration and contract transfer

The combined airline with Asiana Airlines launches on December 17, 2026. The company is focusing on re-checking safety systems and service quality ahead of the launch.

Separating the defense business would require a new entity to take over backlog of roughly KRW 2.6 trillion, and whether contracts with each customer allow the performing party to change is the key question.

The memorandum of understanding for the Busan aerospace plant investment also needs review if the investing entity changes.

Timing lag in defense profits

The Aerospace division posted losses from 2020 through 2024 after turning to deficit that year. A return to profit was expected in 2025, but the full revenue ramp of large programs has been described as arriving in the early 2030s. Deliveries of mass-produced drones start in 2027.

Because facilities and R&D spending come before defense revenue, the expansion may weigh on near-term profit even as orders grow.

12

Overall view

Korean AIR Lines grew 2025 revenue 41.2% to KRW 25.2 trillion, but operating profit fell to KRW 1.11 trillion and the operating margin dropped to 4.4%. In 2026, revenue rose for four consecutive quarters, and net profit attributable to owners returned to profit in the third quarter at KRW 1.1 trillion.

In defense, 57% of the KRW 4.7 trillion backlog at the end of the first quarter of 2026 consisted of military aircraft MROU and drones, so the business mix is shifting.

Meanwhile, passenger and cargo earnings remain highly sensitive to oil, exchange rates and fuel costs from the Middle East war, and the debt-to-equity ratio stands at 339.9%.

The combined airline launching on December 17, 2026, changes in control at the parent holding company, and defense regulation bills will shape the direction ahead. Because the full ramp of defense profit has been placed in the early 2030s, passenger profitability is likely to drive overall earnings for now.

The next checkpoints are the fourth-quarter 2026 preliminary results and the operating margin in the 2026 annual business report.
