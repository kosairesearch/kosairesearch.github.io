Shrinking Sales and Five Straight Loss-Making Quarters; Listing Status in Focus

Carry's revenue fell from KRW 52.3 billion in 2022 to KRW 7.48 billion in 2025, and it has posted operating losses for five straight quarters through the third quarter of 2026. Both the shrinking sales base and the listing-related disclosures need close tracking.

02

Business structure

Carry was founded in 2003 to manufacture and sell power conversion equipment and is a power electronics company.

Its main products are induction heating inverters used in electric pressure cookers, power conversion units for marine ballast water treatment, power units for the instant heating and cooling of direct-feed water purifiers, and battery packs for telecom use.

According to a company profile on WiseReport, the induction heating inverter is supplied exclusively to an electric pressure cooker maker, and the company is said to hold the top market share in temperature-control inverters.

The marine ballast unit reflects tighter international environmental rules, while the water purifier unit is promoted on instant cooling and heating technology. Customers are mainly manufacturers in a business-to-business model, and the company aims to build long-term relationships through custom design and quality.

In November 2024 it spun off its renewable energy business into a subsidiary, and after a 2025 rights offering the company became an associate of that unit.

In July 2025 the company said it would expand into solar power, energy storage systems (ESS), virtual power plants (VPP), hydrogen fuel cell power plants, and transmission line design and construction. It is pursuing rooftop solar installation for buildings in cooperation with major construction firms.

Segment revenue shares and product-level sales have no confirmed figures yet, so they remain items to check in the notes to the periodic reports.

04

Earnings analysis

On an annual basis, revenue fell from KRW 52.3 billion in 2022 to KRW 17 billion in 2023 (down 67.5% year on year), KRW 8.17 billion in 2024 (down 51.9%), and KRW 7.48 billion in 2025 (down 8.5%).

Revenue declined three straight years from 2023, with 2022 the largest year and 2025 the smallest in the 2022 to 2025 range. The 2025 annual commentary cited weaker demand for the core products as the reason for the deterioration.

Operating losses were KRW 12.7 billion in 2022, KRW 13.6 billion in 2023, KRW 4.02 billion in 2024, and KRW 4.87 billion in 2025, a loss streak of four straight years.

Operating margins were -24.3% in 2022, -79.9% in 2023, -49.2% in 2024, and -65.1% in 2025, so the loss rate was deepest in 2023, narrowed in 2024, and widened again in 2025.

Net loss attributable to controlling shareholders was KRW 13.9 billion in 2022, KRW 17.6 billion in 2023, KRW 13 billion in 2024, and KRW 8.41 billion in 2025. Total net loss including non-controlling interests was KRW 13.9 billion, KRW 17.6 billion, KRW 13 billion, and KRW 8.4 billion in the same years.

At the end of 2025, total equity was KRW 21.4 billion, of which KRW 21.3 billion belonged to controlling shareholders, and total liabilities were KRW 31.3 billion. The debt ratio rose from 55.1% in 2022 to 104.1% in 2023, 110.6% in 2024, and 146.6% in 2025.

Operating cash flow was -KRW 7.57 billion in 2022, -KRW 5.13 billion in 2023, -KRW 4.05 billion in 2024, and -KRW 3.81 billion in 2025, so the cash outflow narrowed each year.

Quarterly revenue was KRW 1.75 billion in Q3 2025 and KRW 1.83 billion in Q4 2025 (up 4.6% from the prior quarter), then KRW 521 million in Q1 2026 (down 71.6%) and KRW 23.37 million in Q2 2026 (down 95.5%).

Operating losses were KRW 1.24 billion in Q3 2025, KRW 1.09 billion in Q4 2025, KRW 696 million in Q1 2026, KRW 528 million in Q2 2026, and KRW 633 million in Q3 2026. Operating loss stayed at KRW 528 million even when quarterly revenue was only KRW 23.37 million, which points to a cost base large relative to sales.

The last four quarters together show a net loss attributable to controlling shareholders of KRW 6.88 billion. The effect of one-off items cannot be separated from the disclosed figures alone and should be checked in the notes to the periodic reports.

05

Industry analysis

Carry operates in the power conversion (inverter) market, which splits into temperature-control equipment for home appliances and power conversion for renewable generation and energy storage.

In appliances, the induction heating inverter for electric pressure cookers is the flagship product, and the company profile promotes a top share in temperature-control inverters. In marine applications, tighter international environmental rules create demand for ballast water treatment units.

In renewables, a July 2025 press report introduced the company as a leading domestic solar inverter maker and said it is pursuing building-integrated solar, ESS, and VPP markets.

Data on appliance demand, marine orders, the size of the solar and ESS markets, and where the cycle stands are not yet available to use as evidence here. The top-share claim also rests on company-provided information rather than independently verified figures.

Industry classification also differs by source: the user classification is machinery and equipment, while WiseReport places the company in the KOSDAQ electrical and electronics sector under IT appliances.

For now, the industry question is mainly which markets the company actually sits in, and peer comparisons of sales and share will need the periodic report figures first.
