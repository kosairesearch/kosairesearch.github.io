02

Business structure

JMT is an EMS (electronics manufacturing services) company that provides production and delivery of electronic products using its own manufacturing equipment. Its core business is PBA (printed board assembly), a key component for TFT-LCD panels, and it has also begun supplying PBA for LED.

Its main revenue source is FPBA products for OLED panels used in mobile phones, and manufacturing is carried out mainly through its Vietnamese subsidiary. The EMS business is shaped by a trend in which OEM companies outsource production to improve inventory efficiency and competitiveness.

Strong mobile phone sales in the second half of 2025 expanded FPBA supply volumes, and growth in new PBA product lines lifted sales and profitability in the electronic components segment.

Automotive interior parts are adding production capacity through expanded investment, and household products are growing as supply to new OEM customers gets underway.

The 2026 value-up plan includes expanding North American automotive parts and nurturing health-and-living ODM business, which points to broader product axes. On Alphasquare's classification of 90 stocks in the display equipment and parts sector, JMT ranked 23rd by 2025 annual revenue and 22nd by operating profit. Because volumes follow OEM order schedules, results are sensitive to customer production plans.

04

Earnings analysis

Based on disclosed results, annual revenue grew from KRW 90.6 billion in 2022 to KRW 122.7 billion in 2023 (up 35.5% year on year), KRW 164 billion in 2024 (up 33.6%) and KRW 200.2 billion in 2025 (up 22.1%).

Revenue has risen for three consecutive years from 2023 through 2025, and 2025 was the largest year of 2022 to 2025. Operating profit fell from KRW 20 billion in 2022 to KRW 14.8 billion in 2023 (down 25.9%) and KRW 5.28 billion in 2024 (down 64.4%), then recovered to KRW 13.1 billion in 2025 (up 148.7%).

The operating margin was 22.1% in 2022, 12.1% in 2023, 3.2% in 2024 and 6.6% in 2025, falling sharply in 2023 and 2024 even as revenue grew. In Q3 2024, higher labor, transport and consumables costs were reported to have lowered operating profit despite higher sales.

Controlling-shareholder net income was KRW 29.4 billion in 2022, KRW 22.5 billion in 2023 (down 23.4%), KRW 8.91 billion in 2024 (down 60.5%) and KRW 14.3 billion in 2025 (up 60.1%), with 2022 the largest year.

Net income including non-controlling interests, at KRW 15.9 billion in 2025, is a separate figure from controlling-shareholder net income of KRW 14.3 billion.

Operating cash flow was KRW 21.9 billion in 2022, KRW 43.7 billion in 2023, -KRW 3.86 billion in 2024 and KRW 17.3 billion in 2025, with 2024 the only negative year.

Quarterly revenue rose from KRW 53.4 billion in Q3 2025 to KRW 62.2 billion in Q4 2025 (up 16.4% quarter on quarter) and KRW 68 billion in Q1 2026 (up 9.3%), dipped to KRW 60.7 billion in Q2 2026 (down 10.8%), and reached KRW 67.2 billion in Q3 2026 (up 10.8% quarter on quarter and 25.8% year on year).

Over the same span, operating profit went from KRW 5.62 billion in Q3 2025 and KRW 6.06 billion in Q4 2025 to KRW 3.43 billion in Q1 2026 (down 43.3% quarter on quarter), KRW 3.29 billion in Q2 2026 and KRW 4.27 billion in Q3 2026 (down 24.0% year on year).

Controlling-shareholder net income declined from KRW 6.55 billion in Q3 2025 to KRW 5.77 billion in Q4 2025, KRW 4.72 billion in Q1 2026 and KRW 3.3 billion in Q2 2026, then swung to a KRW 5.49 billion net loss in Q3 2026.

Operating profit stayed positive in Q3 2026, so the gap to the controlling-shareholder net loss should be traced through non-operating items and taxes in the quarterly report, and the scale of any one-off items should be checked in the same filing.

05

Industry analysis

JMT's volumes span two demand axes: FPBA for mobile OLED and PBA for TFT-LCD. The strong mobile phone sales in the second half of 2025 that expanded FPBA volumes show how volumes follow new-model cycles.

The drop in operating margin to 3.2% in 2024, during a period of weaker demand, shows how profit swings with volume and cost structure. TFT-LCD parts are tied to large-display production for laptops, monitors and TVs, so they run on a different cycle from mobile.

In EMS, the trend of OEM companies outsourcing production continues, which supports demand for contract manufacturers. On Alphasquare's classification of 90 stocks in the display equipment and parts sector, JMT ranked 23rd by 2025 annual revenue and 22nd by operating profit.

On cycle position, the recovery in mobile volumes since the second half of 2025 overlaps with Q3 2026 revenue growth, but the fall in Q3 2026 operating profit suggests margin pressure remains. Growth in automotive interior parts investment and new OEM household supply is widening the product base beyond mobile. The 2025 operating margin of 6.6% has recovered from the 2022 level of 22.1%, but remains well below it.

Outlook

The tasks set out in the 2026 value-up plan are advancing OLED display module manufacturing technology, expanding North American automotive parts, nurturing health-and-living ODM business, optimizing global production sites, and making subsidiary dividends regular. The shareholder return target is a 10% ratio by 2029.

Automotive interior parts are at a stage where investment is adding production capacity, and the timing at which those capacity gains show up in revenue is the next checkpoint.

Household products are at a stage where supply to new OEM customers has started in earnest, so how those customer volumes appear in quarterly sales is the key issue.

According to a financial information site's aggregated data, first-half 2026 revenue rose 52.0% year on year, a direction consistent with the sum of the disclosed quarterly revenue.

In Q3 2026, revenue growth did not carry through to operating profit, which suggests cost of sales or selling and administrative expenses grew faster than sales, and the detailed cost structure should be confirmed in the periodic report.

The 2026 annual report will finalize full-year and Q4 results and will also show how far the shareholder return plan has been executed. Whether mobile volumes and the automotive and household product axes convert into profit is the central variable for the outlook.

Bull factors

Sustained revenue growth

Recovering operating margin

The operating margin recovered from 3.2% in 2024 to 6.6% in 2025. Operating profit rose 148.7% to KRW 13.1 billion in 2025. If Q3 2026 revenue growth is matched by cost structure gains, the durability of the margin recovery can be tested.

Shareholder return target and cash generation

Whether the shareholder return ratio target is carried out is a point to check in future dividend-related filings. Making subsidiary dividends regular is also part of the plan. Operating cash flow recovered from -KRW 3.86 billion in 2024 to KRW 17.3 billion in 2025.

Bear factors

Q3 divergence between revenue and profit

Q3 2026 revenue rose 25.8% year on year, but operating profit fell 24.0% to KRW 4.27 billion. The controlling-shareholder net loss of KRW 5.49 billion marks a swing into loss year on year. Revenue growth not reaching profit points to a possible rise in cost burden.

Earnings volatility

The operating margin fell from 22.1% in 2022 to 3.2% in 2024. Controlling-shareholder net income declined in both 2023 and 2024 even as revenue grew. Whether the 2025 recovery has become a trend cannot be confirmed from one year of data.

Rising financial burden

The debt ratio rose from 33.6% in 2022 to 46.8% in 2025. At year-end 2025, total liabilities were KRW 86.1 billion and total equity was KRW 183.9 billion. Over the same period, liabilities grew faster than equity.

Risk factors

Mobile demand and volume swings

FPBA volumes depend heavily on mobile phone sales. The operating margin fell to 3.2% in 2024 during a period of weaker demand. Whether new PBA product lines and household OEM volumes can offset such swings is the key issue.

Concentration of overseas production

Manufacturing is carried out mainly through the Vietnamese subsidiary, so the business is exposed to local labor costs, exchange rates and trade conditions. Global site optimization in the 2026 value-up plan signals that the production structure is changing. The costs and effects of any site restructuring should be checked in the periodic reports.

Balance sheet and cash flow

The debt ratio rose from 33.6% in 2022 to 46.8% in 2025. Operating cash flow was negative at -KRW 3.86 billion in 2024. With the Q3 2026 swing to a net loss, how well the company sustains internally generated cash will be a key check.

Overall view

JMT is an EMS company centered on TFT-LCD PBA and mobile OLED FPBA, and its annual revenue grew from KRW 90.6 billion in 2022 to KRW 200.2 billion in 2025. The operating margin fell from 22.1% in 2022 to 3.2% in 2024 and recovered to 6.6% in 2025.

Q3 2026 revenue reached KRW 67.2 billion, up 25.8% year on year, but operating profit fell 24.0% to KRW 4.27 billion and the company swung to a controlling-shareholder net loss of KRW 5.49 billion.

Strengths include revenue growth, the 2025 profit recovery, the 10% shareholder return target for 2029, and new axes in automotive interior parts and household products. Weaknesses include the divergence between Q3 revenue and profit, earnings volatility since 2022, and a debt ratio that rose to 46.8%.

On valuation, a share price low relative to net assets coexists with large swings in earnings-based multiples. The next checkpoints are the detailed profit and loss items in the 2026 annual report and the year-on-year trend in the Q1 2027 quarterly report.
