02

Business structure

Nice D&B centers its operations on corporate credit information services and also runs SME business support and specialized research and survey businesses.

According to the company's own overview, its business areas include global corporate information services, VAT refund agency services, and automotive and financial research.

The global corporate information unit operates as the exclusive domestic partner of the US credit rating company D&B, supplying overseas corporate databases and supporting export-related government projects.

A 2023 research note from a brokerage put the revenue of the private, public and ESG evaluation segment at KRW 17.2 billion in 2020, KRW 16.9 billion in 2021 and KRW 18.9 billion in 2022, and expected the recovery to continue into 2023.

A wholly owned subsidiary holds 100% of its shares and handles bookkeeping automation services as well as market and opinion research. As of end-March 2026, the company reported 341 employees.

In competition, the domestic corporate credit information market is compared with peers whose results move with demand for technology credit evaluation, as the 2023 downturn showed. The business mix can be gauged from the order of its business areas and the subsidiary structure.

The two pillars of the revenue model are therefore domestic evaluation demand and the overseas data partnership.

04

Earnings analysis

On an annual basis, revenue fell from KRW 92.2 billion in 2022 to KRW 87.9 billion in 2023, a decline of 4.6%, before rising to KRW 89.7 billion in 2024 (up 2.0%) and KRW 91.4 billion in 2025 (up 1.9%). Revenue has risen for two consecutive years from 2024 through 2025.

The largest year of 2022 to 2025 was 2022, and the smallest was 2023. Operating profit was KRW 15.5 billion in 2022, KRW 14.2 billion in 2023 (down 8.4%), KRW 15.8 billion in 2024 (up 11.2%) and KRW 15.4 billion in 2025 (down 2.2%), with 2024 the largest year.

For the 2023 decline, a brokerage research note pointed to a slump in the technology credit evaluation market, when domestic peers all shrank.

Profit attributable to owners of the parent fell from KRW 13.7 billion in 2022 to KRW 10.4 billion in 2023 (down 24.2%), rose to KRW 13.7 billion in 2024 (up 31.7%) and reached KRW 12.9 billion in 2025 (down 5.4%). Net income including non-controlling interests, a separate figure, was KRW 12.9 billion in 2025.

The operating margin stayed between 16% and 18%, at 16.8% in 2022, 16.1% in 2023, 17.6% in 2024 and 16.9% in 2025. Operating cash flow rose from KRW 16.8 billion in 2022 to KRW 17.7 billion in 2025, while the debt ratio fell from 27.0% to 19.7% over the same period.

Quarterly, revenue of KRW 22.4 billion and operating profit of KRW 3.47 billion in Q3 2025 were followed by Q4 2025 revenue of KRW 21.2 billion (down 5.4% from the prior quarter), operating profit of KRW 1.3 billion (down 62.4%) and profit attributable to owners of the parent of KRW 805 million (down 77.4%).

Q1 2026 revenue of KRW 18.3 billion and operating profit of KRW 1.26 billion gave way to Q2 2026 revenue of KRW 32 billion (up 74.4% from the prior quarter) and operating profit of KRW 11.1 billion (up 779.7%).

Q3 2026 revenue of KRW 21.8 billion (down 32.0%) and operating profit of KRW 3.5 billion (down 68.4%) followed, with operating profit up 0.9% from a year earlier. Profit attributable to owners of the parent over the latest four quarters totaled KRW 13.4 billion.

Whether the Q2 2026 surge was one-off or recurring cannot be separated from the disclosed figures alone, so the business-segment explanation in the next periodic report is the point to check.

05

Industry analysis

Nice D&B's results move with demand for corporate credit information and evaluation services. In 2023, revenue fell 4.6% amid a slump in technology credit evaluation, and a brokerage research note noted that domestic peers all shrank.

Revenue recovered with 2% growth in 2024, and the same research note said demand for corporate information keeps expanding. In 2025, however, operating profit edged lower, so the recovery was limited on the profit side.

The unevenness of quarterly results in 2026 makes it hard to pin down where the company sits in the industry cycle. On competition, it shares domestic industry variables with local peers, while its overseas corporate information unit gains differentiation from the D&B partnership.

The business is not directly tied to the interest rate or trading flows that drive the broader financial sector, so its relationship with sector movements depends more on company-specific demand factors.

Bull factors

Financial capacity and low leverage

The debt ratio fell from 27.0% in 2022 to 23.8% in 2023 and 23.4% in 2024. Operating cash flow of KRW 17.7 billion in 2025 exceeded the KRW 16.8 billion of 2022, showing cash generation from core operations has grown.

Profit surge in Q2 2026

Q2 2026 revenue of KRW 32 billion rose 74.4% from the prior quarter, and operating profit of KRW 11.1 billion jumped 779.7%. Against operating profit of KRW 1.26 billion in Q1 2026, quarterly profit reached a markedly higher level.

Compared with profit attributable to owners of the parent of KRW 805 million in Q4 2025, the quarterly rebound was large.

Shareholder return policy formalized

The value-up plan disclosed on March 25, 2026 set a cyclical dividend policy to enhance shareholder value as a target. The 2025 payout ratio was 26.9%, with the 2025 total dividend amount disclosed at KRW 3.48 billion. That is an increase from the KRW 3.16 billion of 2024, which supports reading the policy as dividend expansion.

Bear factors

Stalled top line

Revenue of KRW 91.4 billion in 2025 sat below the KRW 92.2 billion of 2022. It fell to KRW 87.9 billion in 2023 and recovered in 2024 and 2025 without regaining the 2022 level. With the top line not growing visibly, room for profit growth is limited.

Sharp swings in quarterly profit

Operating profit in Q4 2025 was KRW 1.3 billion, down 62.4% from the prior quarter, and profit attributable to owners of the parent fell 77.4% to KRW 805 million. Operating profit in Q3 2026 was KRW 3.5 billion, down 68.4% from KRW 11.1 billion in Q2.

With quarterly profit swinging this much, it becomes hard to tell which quarter carries the KRW 13.4 billion four-quarter total.

Lower profit in 2025

Operating profit in 2025 was KRW 15.4 billion, down 2.2% from the prior year, and profit attributable to owners of the parent fell 5.4% to KRW 12.9 billion. The operating margin slipped from 17.6% in 2024 to 16.9%. The 2024 recovery did not carry into 2025, which remains a weak spot in the profit trend.

Risk factors

Industry cycle

In 2023, revenue fell 4.6% amid a slump in technology credit evaluation, and domestic peers shrank alongside it. The 2023 case shows that when evaluation demand weakens with the financial environment, the company's revenue can move in the same direction. A renewed downturn could weaken its earnings base.

Earnings volatility and one-off factors

Operating profit of KRW 11.1 billion in Q2 2026 stands out against KRW 1.26 billion in Q1 and KRW 3.5 billion in Q3. The disclosed figures alone do not separate a one-off surge from a recurring one. If one-off factors were large, reading the 2026 profit trend on an annual basis could overstate it.

Dependence on an overseas partner

The global corporate information unit relies on its position as the exclusive domestic partner of the US credit rating company D&B. A change in contract terms or in the partnership could directly affect the revenue structure of the overseas data business.

The conditions under which this relationship continues are a matter to confirm in the business description of the annual report.

Overall view

Nice D&B is a corporate information services company whose revenue has stayed near KRW 90 billion and whose operating profit has moved within roughly KRW 14 billion to KRW 16 billion since 2022. A debt ratio that fell to 19.7% at end-2025 leaves substantial financial capacity.

Annual profit recovered in 2024 and edged lower in 2025, so the earnings base has not changed much.

Quarterly results in 2026 rose sharply to KRW 32 billion of revenue and KRW 11.1 billion of operating profit in Q2, then came back to KRW 21.8 billion and KRW 3.5 billion in Q3, which makes the unevenness the defining feature.

The value-up plan disclosed in March 2026 and its dividend element are strengths on the shareholder-return side. On the other side, the top line has not regained its 2022 level and quarterly profit swings widely.

The valuation sits below the five-year average price-to-earnings ratio, but because the earnings base shifts each quarter, the range of interpretation is wide. The next points to confirm are the 2026 annual report, expected around mid-March 2027, and Q1 2027 results.
