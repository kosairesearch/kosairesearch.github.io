02

Business structure

Sangsin Brake, founded in August 1975, makes and sells friction materials for automobiles, with brake pads as its core product. Its business is divided into a braking segment and an equipment segment.

The braking segment supplies brake pads and other braking products for passenger cars and trucks to original equipment manufacturers (OE) and the replacement market (RE).

The equipment segment develops and supplies high value-added machines used in brake pad production, including automatic pad assembly lines, molding machines and wear testers, and it is widening its equipment lineup to add revenue sources.

Its main customers are domestic automakers such as Hyundai Motor and KIA, and a Korean news report described the company as a primary Tier 1 supplier to Hyundai Motor. The company claims the number one position in the domestic brake pad market and cites a broad distribution network.

Production bases are in China, India and Mexico, with a U.S. sales company and a Middle East branch, and overseas subsidiaries also operate in Poland. On technology, the company points to NVH tuning that improves brake feel and eco-friendly friction materials aimed at North American and European environmental rules.

A financial data site summary as of June 2026 indicates that about 3.6% of sales goes to R&D. The company is also developing a non-contact braking assist device for the electric vehicle era.

04

Earnings analysis

Annual revenue rose from KRW 489 billion in 2022 to KRW 575.5 billion in 2023, up 17.7% year on year, and to KRW 578.6 billion in 2024, up 0.5%, before falling to KRW 542.3 billion in 2025, down 6.3%. Among 2022 to 2025, 2024 was the largest year and 2022 the smallest.

Operating profit jumped to KRW 27.5 billion in 2023, up 95.6%, then fell to KRW 20.6 billion in 2024, down 25.0%, and in 2025 the company posted an operating loss of KRW 1.36 billion, turning from profit. The operating margin declined from 4.8% in 2023 to 3.6% in 2024 and -0.3% in 2025.

Net income attributable to controlling shareholders rose to KRW 14.1 billion in 2023, up 1,859.0%, and to KRW 16.3 billion in 2024, up 15.7%, before turning into a net loss of KRW 11.9 billion in 2025.

Net income including non-controlling interests was a net loss of KRW 12 billion in 2025, a different figure from the loss attributable to controlling shareholders and should be read separately. The company attributed the 2025 change mainly to lower sales and higher costs that reduced profitability.

Quarterly, an operating loss of KRW 2.99 billion in the third quarter of 2025 gave way to operating profit of KRW 2.88 billion in the fourth quarter, then KRW 2.22 billion in the first quarter of 2026 and KRW 918 million in the second, before rising to KRW 9.74 billion in the third quarter.

Net income attributable to controlling shareholders was KRW 4.12 billion in the fourth quarter of 2025, KRW 2.58 billion in the first quarter of 2026 and KRW 560 million in the second, and in the third quarter it became a net loss of KRW 5.41 billion, with the deficit widening.

A 2025 first-quarter news report cited overseas subsidiary losses, foreign currency translation losses, high income tax expense and finance costs as factors weighing on net income.

Operating cash flow went from -KRW 7.16 billion in 2022 to KRW 27 billion in 2023, KRW 25.1 billion in 2024 and KRW 38.4 billion in 2025, and the debt ratio fell from 175.1% in 2022 to 154.5% in 2025.

Industry analysis

The auto industry contracted in 2025 as exports weakened amid a worse external environment, and Sangsin Brake's core braking segment saw sales decline with lower vehicle production.

A Korean financial data site expects 2026 auto exports to turn to growth as tariff uncertainty eases and eco-friendly vehicle exports gain momentum. Whether this outlook reaches parts suppliers' volumes will show up in automaker production and export results.

Being the domestic leader in brake friction materials is a strength, but its main customers are domestic automakers, so it is exposed to the vehicle production cycle.

Listed peers in the same sector include Hyundai Mobis, HL Mando and Hankook Tire & Technology, and in brake systems the company competes with large parts makers. In sector statistics for auto parts, operating margins were in negative territory alongside the sector average for the most recent annual period.

The company's development of a non-contact braking assist device for the electric vehicle era can be read as a response to changing product structure. Its eco-friendly friction material capabilities, aimed at tightening environmental rules in North America and Europe, are cited as an advantage in overseas orders.

An industry source has said that improving fixed cost structures at overseas subsidiaries and strengthening currency risk management are key to restoring profitability.

Bull factors

High value-added equipment and diversification

The equipment segment develops and supplies high value-added machines such as automatic pad assembly lines, molding machines and wear testers, widening revenue sources beyond braking. Measuring equipment such as dynamometer testers is also part of the diversification. Equipment sales can partly offset braking revenue that moves with vehicle production.

Export recovery outlook and domestic leadership

A financial data site expects 2026 auto exports to turn to growth as tariff uncertainty eases and eco-friendly vehicle exports rise. The company holds the number one position in domestic brake pads and an OEM supply base with Hyundai Motor and KIA. It is also expanding overseas supply through local plants in China, India and Mexico and a U.S. sales company.

Overseas losses and financial burden

Risk factors

Automaker production and exports

Its main customers are domestic automakers such as Hyundai Motor and KIA, so changes in their production flow directly into sales. In 2025, weaker exports and lower vehicle output reduced sales in the braking segment. Renewed tariff changes could again disrupt export volumes and widen earnings swings.

Overseas subsidiaries and currency

The fixed cost structure of overseas subsidiaries in China, Mexico and Poland has been identified as a key swing factor for profit and loss. Currency moves can flow into other income and expense items such as foreign currency translation losses and shake net income.

Improving fixed cost structures abroad and strengthening currency risk management are cited as conditions for restoring profitability.

Financial burden and continued losses

At the end of 2025, total liabilities were KRW 338.7 billion against total equity of KRW 219.1 billion, a debt ratio of 154.5%. The net loss attributable to controlling shareholders widened to KRW 5.41 billion in the third quarter of 2026. If finance costs continue to weigh on net income, financial flexibility could be constrained.

Overall view

Sangsin Brake is the domestic leader in brake friction materials and supplies braking products to domestic automakers including Hyundai Motor and KIA. Annual results for 2025 turned into an operating loss and a net loss attributable to controlling shareholders, and revenue fell 6.3% year on year.

After operating profit turned positive in the fourth quarter of 2025, third-quarter 2026 operating profit rose to KRW 9.74 billion, a sign of recovery at the operating line.

Yet the net loss attributable to controlling shareholders widened to KRW 5.41 billion in the same quarter, so operating profit did not carry through to net income. Over the last four quarters, net income attributable to controlling shareholders totaled KRW 1.85 billion.

Strengths include the swing to operating profit, diversification into equipment and the export recovery outlook, while weaknesses include continued losses, overseas subsidiary results, a 154.5% debt ratio and finance costs.
