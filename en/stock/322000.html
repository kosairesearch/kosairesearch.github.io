US Sales Growth Drives Earnings Recovery; Quarterly Swings Remain a Test

02

Business structure

HD Hyundai Energy Solutions sells mainly solar modules and inverters. In the second quarter of 2026, module revenue reached KRW 136.3 billion, up 35.5% year on year and about 83% of total revenue, while solution-segment revenue fell 15.3% to KRW 27.6 billion.

In 2025 the company attributed its revenue growth to higher module and inverter sales. US revenue in the second quarter of 2026 was KRW 65.8 billion, 4.2 times the year-earlier level, lifting the US share of total revenue from 12% to 40%.

The company linked this to installation demand that continued ahead of a reduction in US solar tax incentives. A notable contract is a KRW 127.8 billion module supply deal disclosed in late March 2026 with Hillsboro Solar Project LLC, covering US-market supply from March 30, 2026 to March 17, 2027.

The company is also unveiling its G12R TOPCon Multi-Cut module, rated at 24.4% efficiency, to North American buyers for the first time. Its domestic integrated cell and module production is positioned as a response to US foreign-entity supply chain rules.

In one external PV module manufacturer ranking, the company ranked first in financial soundness among 64 companies.

04

Earnings analysis

On an annual basis, 2022 was the largest year of 2022 to 2025, with revenue of KRW 984.8 billion and operating profit of KRW 90.2 billion, while 2024 was the smallest, with revenue of KRW 422.4 billion and operating profit of KRW 3.5 billion.

Revenue fell 44.5% to KRW 546.1 billion in 2023 and 22.7% to KRW 422.4 billion in 2024, then rose 16.6% to KRW 492.7 billion in 2025. The operating margin declined from 9.2% in 2022 to 3.2% in 2023 and 0.8% in 2024, then recovered to 8.4% in 2025.

Operating profit of KRW 41.2 billion in 2025 rose 1,076.9% from the prior year, and the company said higher module and inverter sales lifted both revenue and profitability.

Profit attributable to owners of the parent went from KRW 60.6 billion in 2022 to a net loss of KRW 2.86 billion in 2023, a turn back to profit of KRW 114 million in 2024, and KRW 41.7 billion in 2025. Net income including non-controlling interests was KRW 41.7 billion in 2025.

Quarterly operating profit held at KRW 14.7 billion in the third quarter of 2025 and KRW 14.5 billion in the fourth quarter, then jumped to KRW 29 billion in the first quarter of 2026.

Press coverage described second-quarter 2026 operating profit as the largest quarterly figure on record, with the margin rising 10.6 percentage points from a year earlier.

The company cited higher module prices at home and abroad, larger overseas volumes and revenue recognition from the third batch of an Angola project. Third-quarter operating profit was KRW 20.4 billion, down 43.4% from the prior quarter, and revenue was KRW 150.8 billion, down 8.7%.

Operating cash flow went from negative KRW 34.6 billion in 2022 to KRW 75.8 billion in 2023 and KRW 83.4 billion in 2024, then fell to KRW 17.7 billion in 2025. Profit attributable to owners of the parent over the latest four quarters totals KRW 83.6 billion.

05

Industry analysis

Solar module demand is moving on two fronts, the United States and South Korea. The U.S. Energy Information Administration (EIA) reported that US solar generation rose 18.3% year on year in January through August 2026.

More than 16% of large solar capacity built through September 2026 was installed alongside battery storage, which links solar demand to grid-connected storage.

In Korea, the 12th Basic Plan for Electricity Supply and Demand is designed around renewables, and a revision easing setback rules for solar installations took effect in September 2026, which is expected to gradually expand domestic module demand.

A researcher at NICE Investors Service said US FEOC rules are tightening constraints on Chinese supply chains, which could benefit companies with non-Chinese supply.

On the Chinese side, a February 2026 report said Beijing planned to end VAT export rebates for solar exporters from April, a change expected to ease price competition and possibly lift module prices.

The company's operating margin rose from 0.8% in 2024 to 8.4% in 2025 and 21.9% in the second quarter of 2026, which places it in a phase where higher module prices show up in earnings. Its domestic integrated cell and module production is seen as an advantage under US supply chain rules.

Whether module price gains persist depends on Chinese supply conditions and should be checked against quarterly prices and margins.

08

Bull factors

Structural Expansion of US Revenue Share

US revenue in the second quarter of 2026 reached KRW 65.8 billion, 4.2 times the year-earlier level, and the US share of total revenue rose from 12% to 40%. The company attributed this partly to installation demand ahead of a reduction in US solar tax incentives. Growing co-installation of solar and storage in US grids also broadens the North American revenue base.

Shift in Profitability Structure

The operating margin rose from 0.8% in 2024 to 8.4% in 2025 and was reported at 21.9% in the second quarter of 2026. The company credited higher module prices at home and abroad and larger overseas volumes. Operating profit of KRW 41.2 billion in 2025 rose 1,076.9% from the prior year.

Financial Stability and External Recognition

At the end of 2025, total liabilities were KRW 112.6 billion and total equity was KRW 417.8 billion, with a debt ratio of 27.0%. The ratio had been 35.1% in 2023 and 22.7% in 2024, so it edged up in 2025. An external PV module manufacturer ranking placed the company first in financial soundness among 64 companies.

09

Bear factors

Large Swings in Quarterly Profit

Third-quarter 2026 operating profit was KRW 20.4 billion, down 43.4% from the prior quarter, and revenue fell 8.7% to KRW 150.8 billion. Quarterly operating profit has ranged from KRW 14.7 billion in the third quarter of 2025 to KRW 36.1 billion in the second quarter of 2026. Given the 0.8% margin recorded in 2024, earnings volatility remains high.

Possible Pull-Forward Effect in US Demand

The company said installation demand ahead of a reduction in US solar tax incentives helped lift second-quarter US revenue. If installations were pulled forward before the policy change, later quarters may not grow at the same pace. Whether this demand holds after the incentive reduction should be checked against upcoming US revenue.

Slower Cash Generation

Operating cash flow fell from KRW 83.4 billion in 2024 to KRW 17.7 billion in 2025, while profit attributable to owners rose to KRW 41.7 billion, widening the gap between earnings and cash.

Solution-segment revenue fell 15.3% to KRW 27.6 billion in the second quarter of 2026, so the revenue base leans heavily on modules.

Risk factors

Policy and regulation

Changes to US FEOC supply chain rules or solar tax credit conditions could reshape the US revenue mix. The company says its domestic integrated cell and module production is its response. In Korea, renewable policy direction and the effect of the setback-rule revision will determine the pace of demand.

Customer and project concentration

The KRW 127.8 billion supply contract disclosed in late March 2026 equals 30.3% of recent revenue. Because a single contract carries this much weight, volumes could drop off after its term ends on March 17, 2027. The timing of one-off large projects, such as the Angola project, can also swing quarterly results.

Pricing and supply chain

Higher module prices at home and abroad lifted margins, but a shift in Chinese module pricing could reverse those gains. China's planned end to export VAT rebates was cited as a price-raising factor, but actual price moves must be confirmed in quarterly results. If margins are highly sensitive to module prices, their stability may be limited.

12

Overall view

HD Hyundai Energy Solutions recovered from a 0.8% operating margin in 2024 to 8.4% in 2025, and quarterly operating profit rose to KRW 29 billion in the first quarter of 2026 and KRW 36.1 billion in the second.

The recovery rests on a larger US revenue share, which reached 40% in the second quarter, and on higher module prices, with US demand also shaped by installations ahead of tax incentive cuts.

On the other side, third-quarter operating profit fell to KRW 20.4 billion from the prior quarter, and operating cash flow of KRW 17.7 billion in 2025 trailed profit attributable to owners of KRW 41.7 billion.

The debt ratio stood at 27.0% in 2025, and an external ranking placed the company first in financial soundness among 64 module makers. The relationship between market value and equity reflects expectations for the earnings recovery, but earnings-based multiples will shift with quarterly results.

The next checkpoints are the fourth-quarter and full-year 2026 preliminary results, the 2026 annual report, and the end of the Hillsboro contract term on March 17, 2027. Whether the recovery persists will show up in whether quarterly operating profit and operating cash flow rise together.
