Export-led profit recovery, but quarterly sales are slipping

02

Business structure

Essen Tech was founded in 1985 and listed on KOSDAQ in 2000 as a metal processor specializing in copper and copper alloys. It produces brass forged parts, ball valves, distributors and fittings, supplying housing, electronics, automotive, shipbuilding and plant applications.

The company runs an integrated process from materials to assembly and sells on a made-to-order basis. Its center of gravity is shifting toward refrigeration and air-conditioning valves.

Refrigerant ball valves are the main product, and its pressure-relief integrated valves for CO2 refrigerant systems are seeing rising demand in North America and Europe, according to the company.

Based on UL certification in the United States, it supplies this product exclusively to one global customer and recently secured a new order from a UK global company. In April 2026 it agreed to add hose fittings and core removal tools to the ball valves and welding-free fittings already supplied to a major U.S.

HVAC-R distributor. The export share rose from 34.8% in 2024 to 43% in 2025, and exports accounted for KRW 14 billion, or 43%, of KRW 32.5 billion in first-half 2026 sales, per press reports.

The average price of its main raw material, brass forging bar, rose from KRW 10,495 in 2025 to KRW 13,629 in the first half of 2026, adding to cost pressure. The largest shareholder is a Daechang group affiliate, and the largest shareholder and related parties held 78.69% as of September 2, 2026.

04

Earnings analysis

On a DART-filed annual basis, revenue fell 23.9% from KRW 62.6 billion in 2022 to KRW 47.6 billion in 2023, then rose 14.1% to KRW 54.4 billion in 2024 and 6.5% to KRW 57.9 billion in 2025, increasing for two consecutive years from 2024 to 2025. 2022 was the largest year in the 2022 to 2025 span, and 2023 was the smallest.

Profit moved far more than revenue. Operating profit swung from KRW 1.97 billion in 2022 to an operating loss of KRW 7.22 billion in 2023, and the operating loss narrowed to KRW 383 million in 2024. Operating profit returned to KRW 1.97 billion in 2025, for an operating margin of 3.4%.

The company attributes the turn to restructuring and operational improvements, lower cost ratios, expense cuts and a rising share of higher-margin export volume.

Attributable net income was KRW 370 million in 2022, an attributable net loss of KRW 9.55 billion in 2023 and KRW 2.94 billion in 2024, before returning to KRW 378 million in 2025. Net income including non-controlling interests was also reported at KRW 378 million for 2025.

The debt ratio fell from 247.6% in 2022 to 159.4% in 2025. However, operating cash flow declined from KRW 3.48 billion in 2024 to KRW 1.14 billion in 2025, so the return to profit did not translate directly into cash generation.

Quarterly revenue rose from KRW 13.1 billion in Q3 2025 to a peak of KRW 17.3 billion in Q4 2025, a 31.4% sequential increase, then declined to KRW 16.3 billion in Q1 2026, KRW 16.2 billion in Q2 and KRW 15.5 billion in Q3, three consecutive quarterly declines. Q3 2026 revenue was still up 18.1% year on year.

Operating profit was KRW 1.08 billion in Q4 2025, KRW 823 million in Q1 2026, KRW 1.05 billion in Q2 and KRW 691 million in Q3. Q3 2025 operating profit was only KRW 1.49 million, so the 46,255.9% year-on-year increase in Q3 2026 should be read against that very small base.

Attributable net income turned to KRW 581 million in Q4 2025, then KRW 804 million in Q1 2026 and KRW 822 million in Q2. In Q3 2026 it swung to an attributable net loss of KRW 555 million, and the loss widened year on year. Attributable net income for the latest four quarters totals KRW 1.65 billion.

Because the company returned to an attributable net loss in Q3 even with operating profit of KRW 691 million, the items between operating profit and net income, including non-operating items and taxes, are the next thing to examine.

According to press reports based on the half-year filing, operating cash flow was a net outflow of KRW 2.3 billion in the first half of 2026, and inventory rose from KRW 11.2 billion at end-2025 to KRW 16.4 billion at end-June.

05

Industry analysis

The core trend in refrigeration and air-conditioning is the shift to eco-friendly refrigerants. The company is widening its valve and fitting lineup for refrigerants with lower global warming potential and says tightening global environmental regulation supports this direction.

CO2 natural-refrigerant systems are seeing rising demand in North America and Europe on safety grounds, and the company's pressure-relief integrated products sit squarely in that demand.

The company profile cites a recovering domestic construction market and a global economic recovery as growth foundations for the industry. Because its roots lie in housing and construction components, it is also exposed to domestic construction activity.

On raw materials, rising brass forging bar prices keep pressure on working capital. Its competitive position rests on a metal-processing base in Korea, plus UL certification and a U.S. distribution channel that together give it a route into overseas valve markets.

Whether exclusive supply deals and certification act as barriers to entry will be judged by new certifications and order wins.

Although it sits in the same machinery and equipment sector as robotics and construction equipment companies, it is a materials-processing parts maker, so sector-level indicators say little about its business.

On the cycle, revenue recovered in 2024 and 2025 after the 2023 trough, but quarterly sales have declined for three consecutive quarters in 2026, so the recovery has been uneven. Whether the expansion of overseas channels that the company describes changes this pattern will determine its position in the industry.

06

Outlook

The company said its first-half 2026 results met the guidance it had set in June, according to an August 14, 2026 announcement. In the same announcement it attributed the profit gains to higher overseas sales and cost efficiency.

Its second-half plan focuses on eco-friendly refrigerant products and expanding the overseas customer base. It intends to keep its partnership with its existing U.S. distributor while raising the export share through new global customers.

Beyond its existing UL certification, it is pursuing additional global certifications to accelerate entry into overseas markets. Having secured a new order from a UK global company, it expects its global supply chain to become more solid, according to the company.

When it reported 2025 results, the company said it would keep the profit trend going in 2026 and pursue sound growth.

On share structure, trading resumed on November 6, 2026 after the listing of the consolidated shares, and the company said the purpose of the consolidation was to maintain an appropriate free float and stabilize the share price.

The next checkpoint is whether quarterly revenue in Q4 2026 breaks the run of three consecutive declines. Also worth watching are whether the operating margin holds near the 3.4% level of 2025 and how the inventory build shows up in operating cash flow. Audited figures for 2026 will be confirmed in the 2026 annual report.

Bull factors

Rising export share and margins

The export share rose from 34.8% in 2024 to 43% in 2025 and held at 43% in the first half of 2026. The company says higher-margin export volume has improved profitability. Added product supply to a U.S. distributor is cited as evidence of expanding overseas sales.

Sustained operating profit and margin gains

Operating profit returned to KRW 1.97 billion in 2025, for an operating margin of 3.4%. Operating profit was KRW 823 million in Q1 2026 and KRW 1.05 billion in Q2, staying near KRW 1 billion per quarter. After alternating losses and profits in earlier years, the company is building a run of profitable quarters.

Eco-refrigerant demand and certification base

The company says its pressure-relief integrated valves for CO2 systems are seeing rising demand in North America and Europe. It supplies one global customer exclusively under its U.S. UL certification and has secured a new order from a UK global company. Pursuit of additional global certifications supports its overseas expansion case.

Bear factors

Q3 loss and three quarters of declining sales

Attributable net loss reached KRW 555 million in Q3 2026, a swing into losses with a wider loss year on year. Sales peaked at KRW 17.3 billion in Q4 2025 and then fell to KRW 16.3 billion, KRW 16.2 billion and KRW 15.5 billion over the next three quarters. Net income failing to follow operating profit is a weak point.

Weak cash flow and inventory build

Operating cash flow fell to KRW 1.14 billion in 2025 from KRW 3.48 billion in 2024. Press reports say it was a net outflow of KRW 2.3 billion in the first half of 2026, while inventory rose from KRW 11.2 billion to KRW 16.4 billion. Higher raw-material prices add to working-capital strain.

Borrowing burden and leverage

Total liabilities were KRW 41.3 billion at end-2025 against total equity of KRW 25.9 billion, a 159.4% debt ratio. In May 2026 the company decided to raise short-term borrowings by KRW 5 billion for operating funds, lifting the total to KRW 28.4 billion. Whether cash flow improves alongside rising borrowings is a key variable.

Risk factors

Share liquidity and listing-status risk

The largest shareholder and related parties hold 78.69%, leaving a 21.31% free float. Two share consolidations were carried out in 2026, and a disclosure in August 2026 flagged possible designation as a managed issue after closing prices stayed below KRW 1,000. Repeated share-count changes could thin trading liquidity.

Raw-material prices and cost pressure

The average price of brass forging bar, its main raw material, rose from KRW 10,495 in 2025 to KRW 13,629 in the first half of 2026. The direction of operating margin depends on how much of that increase can be passed on to customers. With a 2025 operating margin of 3.4%, cost swings can have a large effect.

Overseas customer concentration and certification dependence

Export sales depend heavily on a U.S. distributor and on one global customer under exclusive supply. With the export share at 43%, changes in trading terms or certification requirements could show up quickly in results. Whether new certifications and customers can reduce this concentration is the key test.

Overall view

Essen Tech returned to annual operating profit in 2025 and lifted its export share to 43%, building overseas business on eco-friendly refrigerant products and certifications.

The bullish case rests on a rising overseas share, a run of profitable operating quarters, a lower debt ratio, and exclusive supply and new orders.

The bearish case rests on the swing to an attributable net loss in Q3 2026, three consecutive quarters of falling sales, weak operating cash flow with rising inventory, and higher short-term borrowings.

The largest shareholder's 78.69% stake, the small free float and two share consolidations are features of its trading structure that should be read together. With no investment opinions issued in the last three months, outside views are thin.

On valuation, the multiples are sensitive to earnings, since attributable net income over the latest four quarters totals KRW 1.65 billion. The next checkpoints are preliminary full-year 2026 results around January 2027, the 2026 annual report by late March 2027, and Q1 2027 results around late April 2027.
