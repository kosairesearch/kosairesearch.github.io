Essen Tech: Brass Valve Turnaround Meets Second Reverse Split

Essen Tech returned to operating profit in 2025 and has posted five consecutive quarters of operating profit through the second quarter of 2026, even as two separate reverse stock splits this year have sharply reshaped its share count and trading structure.

02

Business structure

Essen Tech is a brass and copper-alloy metal processing specialist founded in 1985 and listed on KOSDAQ in 2000.

The company manufactures brass forged parts, ball valves, distributors, and fittings, supplying essential components to industries ranging from housing and electronics to automotive, shipbuilding, and plants.

Its core products include refrigeration ball valves, LPG valves, service valves, and gas cylinder valves, and it has developed a new connection fitting product called 'SB1' for refrigerant piping aimed at the US and Japanese markets.

The company is an affiliate of Daechang Group, a non-ferrous metal specialist in the copper-alloy value chain, and sources much of its core brass rod material from Daechang in a complementary relationship.

Controlling shareholder Daechang Co., Ltd. held a 50.0% stake as of August 2026, sitting at the top of the de facto governance structure.

Essen Tech is expanding its North American and European supply base centered on refrigeration ball valves backed by US UL certification, with plans to pursue additional certifications and new customer accounts.

As a metal-processing business, it is directly exposed to the business cycle of its downstream industries and to fluctuations in copper and zinc prices, operating on a made-to-order basis tailored to each customer.

Domestically it competes against a number of small and mid-sized brass component makers, with vertical integration within Daechang Group serving as a cost-competitiveness foundation.

04

Earnings analysis

On an annual basis, revenue fell 23.9% from KRW 62.6 billion in 2022 to KRW 47.6 billion in 2023, then rose for two consecutive years to KRW 54.4 billion in 2024 (up 14.1%) and KRW 57.9 billion in 2025 (up 6.5%).

Operating profit swung from KRW 1.97 billion in 2022 to an operating loss of KRW 7.22 billion in 2023, and the loss continued but narrowed to KRW 383 million in 2024, before the company swung back to an operating profit of KRW 1.97 billion in 2025.

Net profit attributable to controlling shareholders followed the same pattern, moving from a net loss of KRW 9.55 billion in 2023 to a narrower net loss of KRW 2.94 billion in 2024, then back to net profit of KRW 378 million in 2025.

The operating margin fell from 3.1% in 2022 to -15.2% in 2023 before recovering to 3.4% in 2025.

The quarterly pattern is even more pronounced: operating profit dropped from KRW 348 million in the second quarter of 2025 to just KRW 1.49 million in the third quarter, before jumping to KRW 1.08 billion in the fourth quarter and continuing to KRW 823 million in the first quarter of 2026 and KRW 1.05 billion in the second quarter, up 201.0% year-on-year.

Net profit attributable to controlling shareholders rose for two consecutive quarters from KRW 581 million in the fourth quarter of 2025 to KRW 804 million in the first quarter of 2026 and KRW 822 million in the second quarter, with the trailing four quarters (Q3 2025 through Q2 2026) totaling KRW 1.83 billion.

Operating cash flow swung from negative KRW 3.98 billion in 2023 to positive KRW 3.48 billion in 2024, but the surplus narrowed to KRW 1.14 billion in 2025, suggesting the earnings recovery has not fully translated into cash generation.

05

Industry analysis

The brass component industry supplies essential parts used across most downstream sectors, including housing, electronics, electricals, petrochemicals, automotive, shipbuilding, plants, construction, and industrial machinery, making it directly exposed to downstream industry cycles and reliant on made-to-order manufacturing tailored to each customer.

Refrigeration and air-conditioning parts performance depends on construction and facility investment, seasonal demand, and factory utilization, while automotive parts revenue is affected by automaker production plans, customer orders, and unit pricing.

Fluctuations in copper and zinc raw material prices affect profitability with a lag before being reflected in selling prices and cost ratios. The ongoing shift toward eco-friendly refrigerants is creating new demand, prompting the company to expand its refrigerant piping product lineup in response.

On the export side, the company is gradually expanding its North American and European supply base backed by US UL certification, with a favorable won-dollar exchange rate environment reported to be supporting export competitiveness.

The domestic brass rod market is dominated by a small number of players including Daechang, and Essen Tech's position within this affiliated supply chain provides relatively stable access to raw materials on the cost side.

The competitive landscape is fragmented, with numerous small and mid-sized brass and non-ferrous metal processors, while product certifications and track records with customers act as barriers to entry.

Outlook

In a June 2026 fair-disclosure filing, the company projected first-half revenue of KRW 33.0 billion and operating profit of KRW 1.8 billion, representing year-on-year growth of 20.2% in revenue and 103.3% in operating profit, with the operating profit figure nearing the full-year 2025 total of KRW 1.97 billion.

The actual disclosed results for the first and second quarters of 2026 (revenue of KRW 16.3 billion and KRW 16.2 billion, operating profit of KRW 823 million and KRW 1.05 billion) broadly tracked that guidance.

The company has said it is diversifying its revenue mix from a domestic-centered structure toward greater North American and European exposure by expanding its overseas supply base centered on refrigerant ball valves.

When announcing first-quarter results, management cited cooperation with a US global distributor and a new order win from a UK company as evidence of a strengthening global supply chain.

The CEO has framed the restructuring and operational-efficiency effects as translating into core business performance, identifying stronger global competitiveness as the next challenge.

These statements represent company-provided expectations, however, and may differ from confirmed figures in subsequent semi-annual and quarterly reports, warranting comparison against later disclosures.

On the capital expenditure side, the company has maintained a recurring, modest level of machinery investment designated for maintenance purposes.

Bull factors

Five Straight Quarters of Operating Profit

The company posted operating profit for five consecutive quarters from the second quarter of 2025 through the second quarter of 2026, with second-quarter 2026 operating profit of KRW 1.05 billion up 201.0% year-on-year, the highest level in the recent quarterly stretch.

Net profit attributable to controlling shareholders also rose for two consecutive quarters following the fourth quarter of 2025, with profitability improvement accompanying revenue growth. The restructuring and cost-ratio improvement appear to be a cumulative quarterly trend rather than a one-time event.

Export Diversification and New Product Lineup

The company is expanding its North American and European supply base centered on refrigeration ball valves backed by US UL certification, while pursuing the US and Japanese markets with its new 'SB1' connection fitting.

In the first quarter of 2026, the export segment reportedly grew 31% year-on-year, driving the earnings improvement. The industry-wide shift toward eco-friendly refrigerants is creating new demand that could present an opportunity for lineup expansion.

Balance Sheet Support From Group Affiliation

Controlling shareholder Daechang Co., Ltd. has a history of supporting Essen Tech's balance sheet improvement, including through participation in a prior third-party allotment capital increase.

The debt-to-equity ratio has steadily declined from 247.6% in 2022 to 159.4% in 2025, and raw material procurement is handled stably through the affiliate. This intra-group support structure can serve as a liquidity buffer in situations where independent financing would be difficult.

Bear factors

Repeated Reverse Splits and Share Price Instability

Essen Tech carried out two reverse stock splits in a single year in 2026 alone, cutting shares outstanding to roughly one-tenth of the level at the start of the year, from 95 million to 9.5 million.

Even after the first split in May, the share price fell back below KRW 1,000, repeatedly raising concerns about designation as an administrative issue stock. Despite improvement in core business performance, the lack of sustained price stability is a burden for investors to weigh.

Two Straight Quarters of Revenue Decline

Quarterly revenue has declined for two consecutive quarters since peaking at KRW 17.3 billion in the fourth quarter of 2025, falling to KRW 16.3 billion in the first quarter of 2026 and KRW 16.2 billion in the second quarter.

While operating profit and net profit have risen, the shrinking top line suggests the profitability improvement may be relying more heavily on cost control than on volume growth. Sustaining the earnings trend through margin improvement alone, without top-line growth, could have limits.

Elevated Debt Ratio and Weaker Cash Generation

The debt-to-equity ratio stood at 159.4% at the end of 2025, lower than in prior years but still elevated, with total liabilities of KRW 41.3 billion against total equity of KRW 25.9 billion.

Operating cash flow narrowed from KRW 3.48 billion in 2024 to KRW 1.14 billion in 2025, indicating that the income-statement recovery has not fully carried through to cash generation. With borrowing pressure persisting, the quality of earnings warrants continued monitoring.

Risk factors

Administrative Issue and Delisting-Related Risk

Concerns about designation as an administrative issue stock have repeatedly arisen as the share price fell below KRW 1,000, prompting two reverse stock splits in 2026 alone to address the issue. If the price falls again after consolidation, similar measures cannot be ruled out.

Investors should also factor in the limited liquidity during the trading suspension periods that accompany each split.

Raw Material Price and Downstream Industry Volatility Risk

Fluctuations in copper and zinc raw material prices are reflected in selling prices and cost ratios with a time lag, which can increase quarterly profitability volatility. A slowdown in downstream industries such as automotive, shipbuilding, or construction could negatively affect both order volumes and unit pricing.

Given the made-to-order nature of the business, exposure to changes in orders from specific customers also persists.

Controlling Shareholder Dependence and Related-Party Transaction Risk

With controlling shareholder Daechang Co., Ltd. holding a 50.0% stake and supplying a significant portion of core raw materials, changes in intra-group transaction terms or in Daechang's own business situation could affect Essen Tech.

While the history of capital support through a prior third-party allotment is a positive, it also implies potential constraints on independent management decisions or negotiating leverage on transaction terms.

The high proportion of related-party transactions within the group is a structural factor investors should continue to monitor.

Overall view

Essen Tech, after a sizable loss in 2023, achieved a turnaround to profit across revenue, operating profit, and net profit in 2025 on the back of restructuring and cost-ratio improvement, and this trend continued into the first and second quarters of 2026 with net profit attributable to controlling shareholders rising for two consecutive quarters.

However, quarterly revenue has declined for two straight quarters since peaking in the fourth quarter of 2025, suggesting cost efficiency rather than top-line growth has been driving the earnings improvement.

The balance sheet has improved as the debt-to-equity ratio has steadily declined, but total liabilities still substantially exceed total equity, and the operating cash flow surplus narrowed compared with 2024.

Two reverse stock splits within 2026 alone have sharply reset the share count and per-share metric basis in a short period, a move aimed at price stabilization that also complicates direct comparison with historical figures for investors.

The raw-material and capital ties with controlling shareholder Daechang Co., Ltd. present both a stability benefit and a dependency risk.

Going forward, the share price behavior after the November share listing, revenue and profit trends in the third-quarter report, and the pace of execution on the export diversification strategy are likely to form the basis for the next assessment.
