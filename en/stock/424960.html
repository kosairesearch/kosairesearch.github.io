02

Business structure

Smart Radar System was founded in October 2017 and develops 4D imaging radar. The company describes its sensors as measuring distance, direction and speed with electromagnetic waves, and as adding height information that conventional 3D radar does not provide.

It develops radars for mobility uses such as autonomous vehicles, drones and special-purpose vehicles, and for non-mobility uses such as smart cities, healthcare and security, and it sells mainly to corporate customers on a B2B basis.

Its core technologies are an irregular antenna design built on commercial radar chips and a high-speed radar signal processing algorithm, and the company says these enabled the world's first high-resolution radar release.

Revenue comes mainly from contracts for radar sales for autonomous vehicles and drones, while R&D and production costs are the main expense items.

According to a Newspim report dated March 12, 2026, radar sensor sales for special-purpose vehicles grew about 3.7 times in 2025 and led company growth, while radar sales for ITS rose about 1.45 times.

Jobkorea's company profile, as of March 2026, also lists autonomous driving, special-purpose vehicles and drones as the main business areas.

On the customer side, the company has won a radar development contract for autonomous vehicles from Hyundai Mobis and others, and reportedly supplies up to six radar sensors per drone. The company's profile says it works with US, European and Japanese companies in markets such as autonomous robots and farm machinery.

Among competitors, domestic 4D imaging radar and LiDAR start-ups serve as the comparison group, and the company's differentiators are described as its own antenna design and mass-production experience in special-purpose vehicles and drones.

04

Earnings analysis

Annual revenue was KRW 4.02 billion in 2022, KRW 4.08 billion in 2023 (up 1.3% year on year), KRW 4.74 billion in 2024 (up 16.2%) and KRW 9.4 billion in 2025 (up 98.4%), rising three years in a row from 2023 through 2025, with 2025 the largest year of 2022 to 2025.

Operating losses were KRW 5.52 billion in 2022, KRW 5.37 billion in 2023, KRW 6.76 billion in 2024 and KRW 5.27 billion in 2025, a loss in each of the four years from 2022 through 2025. The operating margin moved from -137.0% in 2022 and -131.8% in 2023 to -142.6% in 2024 before improving to -56.1% in 2025.

Net loss attributable to controlling shareholders was KRW 7.74 billion in 2022, KRW 5.1 billion in 2023, KRW 6.46 billion in 2024 and KRW 2.97 billion in 2025, and net loss including non-controlling interests was reported at the same amounts in each year.

Newspim, reporting the company's account, attributed the 2025 operating loss improvement to the start of mass production of existing developed products, expanded customers and higher gains on derivative valuation.

Derivative valuation gains are generally recorded as non-operating items, so the nature of the operating loss improvement should be read separately.

At the end of 2025, total equity was KRW 19.1 billion, all of it attributable to controlling shareholders, total liabilities were KRW 14.6 billion, and the debt ratio fell from 99.2% in 2024 to 76.2% in 2025.

Operating cash flow was -KRW 6.21 billion in 2022, -KRW 4.69 billion in 2023, -KRW 5.52 billion in 2024 and -KRW 3.88 billion in 2025, negative in all four years.

Quarterly revenue rose from KRW 1.83 billion in Q3 2025 to KRW 2.91 billion in Q4 2025 (up 59.4% quarter on quarter), then fell to KRW 1.46 billion in Q1 2026 (down 49.7%), recovered to KRW 1.94 billion in Q2 2026 (up 32.8%), and dropped to KRW 931 million in Q3 2026 (down 52.1% quarter on quarter and down 49.0% year on year).

Quarterly operating losses were KRW 964 million in Q3 2025, KRW 602 million in Q4 2025, KRW 1.47 billion in Q1 2026, KRW 1.8 billion in Q2 2026 and KRW 1.86 billion in Q3 2026, a loss in each of the five quarters from Q3 2025 through Q3 2026, with the Q3 2026 loss widening year on year.

Net loss attributable to controlling shareholders reached KRW 5.12 billion in Q2 2026, the largest of the last five quarters, before narrowing to KRW 2.64 billion in Q3 2026. The four quarters from Q4 2025 through Q3 2026 total KRW 9.79 billion in such losses.

On an annual basis, revenue growth and a smaller loss occurred together, but the 2026 quarterly trend shows losses widening while revenue has declined.

05

Industry analysis

4D imaging radar is described as a core sensor enabling autonomous driving at Level 3 and above, so the pace of commercialization in autonomous driving sets the basic demand condition. The company positions its 4D technology, which adds height information, and its irregular antenna design as differentiators.

Demand in the front market splits between autonomous vehicles and special-purpose vehicles used for industrial accident prevention, drones, intelligent transport systems (ITS) and smart cities.

According to a Newspim report, exports of radar sensors for special-purpose vehicles are expanding to the United States and Europe, and a high-resolution ITS radar being co-developed with an overseas partner is scheduled for release in 2027.

On the cycle, the company says it has entered an early stage in which profit structure improvement is beginning in earnest alongside revenue growth.

It also says that since the strategic investment by LG Innotek, which is a shareholder, its entry into global OEM supply chains has increased mass-production opportunities.

In competition, domestic 4D imaging radar and LiDAR start-ups are the comparison group, and against large electronic components companies its revenue scale is small.

Autonomous driving demand depends on automakers' development schedules and regulation, so the speed of market growth will track commercialization timelines across the industry.

Bull factors

Revenue growth trajectory

Revenue rose for three years in a row from 2023 through 2025, reaching KRW 9.4 billion in 2025, up 98.4% year on year. According to a Newspim report, radar sensor sales for special-purpose vehicles grew about 3.7 times and led growth, while ITS radar sales rose about 1.45 times.

Among the last five quarters on the disclosed basis, the highest was Q4 2025 at KRW 2.91 billion, and Q2 2026 at KRW 1.94 billion was also relatively strong.

Technology and customer base

The company says its high-resolution radar is built on an irregular antenna design and a high-speed signal processing algorithm.

It has won a radar development contract for autonomous vehicles from Hyundai Mobis and others, and reports say that since LG Innotek's strategic investment, its entry into global OEM supply chains has expanded. The supply of up to six sensors per drone is cited as an example of diversified demand.

Narrowing annual losses

The annual operating loss narrowed to KRW 5.27 billion in 2025 from KRW 6.76 billion in 2024, and net loss attributable to controlling shareholders shrank from KRW 6.46 billion to KRW 2.97 billion.

The company attributes part of the improvement to the start of mass production of existing products and an expanded customer base. The operating cash flow deficit also narrowed to -KRW 3.88 billion in 2025 from -KRW 5.52 billion in 2024.

Bear factors

Widening quarterly losses

The Q3 2026 operating loss reached KRW 1.86 billion, wider than a year earlier. The Q2 2026 net loss attributable to controlling shareholders of KRW 5.12 billion was the largest in the last five quarters, and the four-quarter total reached KRW 9.79 billion.

Q3 2026 revenue of KRW 931 million fell 49.0% year on year, so the annual trend of narrowing losses has weakened on a quarterly basis.

Limited cash generation

Operating cash flow was negative in every year from 2022 through 2025, at -KRW 3.88 billion in 2025. Total equity of KRW 19.1 billion at the end of 2025 leaves a cushion that is hard to call wide against a four-quarter net loss of KRW 9.79 billion.

Total liabilities of KRW 14.6 billion and a 76.2% debt ratio increase the possibility that more funding will be needed.

Possible reliance on non-operating gains

Derivative valuation gains, which the company cited among drivers of the 2025 operating loss improvement, are generally non-operating and may not recur. Revenue growth has not yet produced an operating profit, which suggests cost and R&D burdens remain high. The operating loss pattern that has run from 2022 through 2025 has not yet been resolved.

Risk factors

Commercialization risk

Revenue timing for 4D imaging radar can slip with autonomous driving production schedules and automakers' qualification procedures. If the 2027 release of the high-resolution ITS radar is delayed, the timing of non-mobility revenue growth would also be delayed. Intensifying competition with domestic 4D radar and LiDAR start-ups is another burden.

Financing risk

Operating cash flow has been negative since 2022, and total equity at the end of 2025 was KRW 19.1 billion. If additional funding is required, a rights offering or similar issuance could dilute existing shareholders' stakes.

The debt ratio fell from 99.2% in 2024 to 76.2% in 2025, but if quarterly losses continue, the burden could rise again.

Revenue concentration and volatility

Revenue growth relies on a few areas, including radar for special-purpose vehicles and ITS, so it is exposed to order swings from those customers. Quarterly revenue in 2026 moved sharply, at KRW 1.46 billion, KRW 1.94 billion and KRW 931 million, so timing differences in orders flow directly into results. As exports grow, exchange rates and changes in local regulation also become variables for results.

Overall view

Smart Radar System develops 4D imaging radar for autonomous vehicles, special-purpose vehicles, drones and intelligent transport systems (ITS). Revenue rose three years in a row to KRW 9.4 billion in 2025, up 98.4% year on year.

The operating loss narrowed to KRW 5.27 billion in the same year, and net loss attributable to controlling shareholders shrank to KRW 2.97 billion. However, losses have continued for five consecutive quarters from Q3 2025 through Q3 2026, and Q3 2026 revenue of KRW 931 million fell 49.0% year on year.

The four-quarter net loss attributable to controlling shareholders totals KRW 9.79 billion, about half of the KRW 19.1 billion total equity at the end of 2025. Operating cash flow has been negative in every year from 2022 through 2025.

Strengths include three years of revenue growth, growth in radar for special-purpose vehicles, and the opportunity to enter global OEM supply chains following LG Innotek's investment. Weaknesses include widening quarterly losses, limited cash generation and possible reliance on non-operating gains.

The next points to check are the 2026 full-year preliminary results (around mid-March 2027), the 2026 annual report (filing deadline at the end of March 2027), and whether the ITS radar is released in 2027.
