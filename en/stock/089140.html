02

Business structure

NEXTURN & ROLL KOREA is a precision machinery and construction equipment parts maker centered on CNC automatic lathes and heavy equipment parts.

It was founded in 2000 to make and sell CNC automatic lathes, listed on KOSDAQ in 2006, and supplies products to industries including automotive, telecom equipment, medical parts and consumer electronics.

In the first nine months of 2026 on a standalone basis, the heavy equipment segment accounted for KRW 14.25 billion, or 76.7%, of sales, followed by the CNC segment at KRW 2.67 billion (14.4%) and the parts business at KRW 1.65 billion (8.9%), which began booking sales in April 2026.

On a consolidated basis, its main subsidiary is a semiconductor equipment maker, in which the company became the largest shareholder with a 40.4% stake. In May 2026 it also announced entry into semiconductor injection-molded and mold parts, aiming for vertical integration.

Its EV materials subsidiary returned to profit, and the company is strengthening cooperation with Lumens after agreeing to acquire management control. In medical robotics, a cardiovascular catheter-assist robot project completed stage one and received a stage-two go-ahead. Sales track customers' capital spending and order flow, which is the core feature of this parts-supply model.

04

Earnings analysis

Annual revenue rose from KRW 29 billion in 2022 to KRW 41.3 billion in 2023, up 42.3% year on year, fell to KRW 31.6 billion in 2024, down 23.3%, and then climbed to KRW 70.4 billion in 2025, up 122.6%. Among 2022 to 2025, 2025 was the largest year and 2022 the smallest.

Operating margin moved from -11.9% in 2022 to 3.1% in 2023, -3.9% in 2024 and 3.2% in 2025, when operating profit of KRW 2.28 billion turned positive.

Shareholder net income swung from KRW 9.68 billion in 2023 to a KRW 13 billion shareholder net loss in 2024 and a KRW 9.69 billion loss in 2025, which narrowed the deficit.

Net income including non-controlling interests was a KRW 2.35 billion loss in 2025, so the gap versus the shareholder figure reflects the share attributable to non-controlling interests. Operating cash flow was negative in each year from 2022 to 2025, and in 2025 it was KRW -4.72 billion, the largest outflow.

At end-2025, total equity was KRW 222.5 billion, of which KRW 135.1 billion belonged to shareholders, total liabilities were KRW 118.4 billion, and the debt-to-equity ratio had fallen from 85.0% in 2022 to 53.2%.

Quarterly revenue bottomed at KRW 21.7 billion in the fourth quarter of 2025, then rose to KRW 25.5 billion, KRW 37.1 billion and KRW 48 billion across the first three quarters of 2026.

Operating profit went from KRW 3.85 billion in the third quarter of 2025 to an operating loss of KRW 2.04 billion in the fourth quarter, then recovered to KRW 3.55 billion, KRW 8.21 billion and KRW 5.77 billion in 2026.

Shareholder results were weaker, with a KRW 19.6 billion net loss in the fourth quarter of 2025 and a KRW 439 million net loss in the third quarter of 2026. The latest four quarters total a shareholder net loss of KRW 15.5 billion.

One-off items also matter: the company reported a KRW 15.1 billion valuation gain through its subsidiary and KRW 84 billion of cash from selling a property, and both should be read apart from operating trends.

05

Industry analysis

In the CNC lathe business, demand is rising as customers favor unmanned automation, and in heavy equipment parts, the company reported higher orders from major customers tied to expanding global infrastructure investment and the spread of electric forklifts.

Standalone results in 2025 were hurt by US tariff policy and external trade uncertainty that kept capital spending in key end markets from recovering, according to the company. On the cycle, the direction of standalone sales depends on whether heavy equipment and factory capital spending recover.

Consolidated growth has been driven mainly by the semiconductor equipment subsidiary, where the company holds a 40.4% stake and is the largest shareholder.

Entry into semiconductor injection and mold parts widens exposure to chip equipment demand, and the company describes it as vertical integration of its parts manufacturing with the subsidiary's technology. In medical robotics, a cardiovascular catheter-assist robot project is moving toward clinical application.

Peer growth comparisons only make sense after stripping out the consolidation effect. Because heavy equipment parts make up most standalone sales, global infrastructure spending and electric forklift demand are the key variables. Tariff and export-environment policy risks remain a source of uncertainty for the sector.

06

Outlook

The company plans to improve the profitability of its standalone business in order to return it to operating profit. It has also set a target of clear standalone revenue growth and an operating turnaround in 2027.

To get there it cites expanding high-value semiconductor parts and cost innovation, along with cooperation with Lumens. On a consolidated basis, further stake purchases and group cooperation will change both the consolidation scope and funding needs.

A market purchase of Lumens-group shares worth about KRW 1.5 billion is scheduled from December 17, 2026 to January 15, 2027. The consolidated debt ratio rose to 67.2% at end-Q3 2026, while the standalone ratio fell to 28.5%.

The pace of medical robot clinical application after stage two will also affect medium-term growth. The company says it will continue transparent disclosure and shareholder return efforts. The next checkpoint is the operating margin and standalone segment profit in the 2026 annual report.

08

Bull factors

Sales Momentum

Q3 2026 revenue of KRW 48 billion extended three straight quarters of growth, and 2025 revenue of KRW 70.4 billion is more than double the KRW 29 billion of 2022. The company attributes the expansion to subsidiary consolidation and the parts business. Quarterly sales kept rising through 2026.

Operating Profit Holds Positive

Operating profit turned positive at KRW 2.28 billion in 2025 and stayed positive through 2026, at KRW 3.55 billion, KRW 8.21 billion and KRW 5.77 billion across the first three quarters. The operating margin improved from -3.9% in 2024 to 3.2% in 2025. Operating-level improvement is the main bullish evidence on earnings recovery.

Balance Sheet Repair

Sale of the Asan property brought in KRW 84 billion of cash, and the standalone debt ratio fell from 69.9% to 28.5%. The consolidated debt ratio had already improved from 85.0% in 2022 to 53.2% in 2025. Financial room from asset sales could support business restructuring.

09

Bear factors

Standalone Losses Persist

On a standalone basis, the nine-month 2026 operating loss was KRW 3.51 billion and the net loss KRW 2.4 billion. The company itself lists returning standalone operating profit as a core medium-term task. In 2025 it blamed tariff policy and external factors for weak standalone demand.

Repeated Shareholder Losses

Shareholder net losses were recorded in 2024 and 2025, a second consecutive loss year, and the third quarter of 2026 swung back to a KRW 439 million net loss. The latest four quarters total a KRW 15.5 billion shareholder net loss.

Losses at the shareholder level despite positive operating profit point to non-operating items that need separate review.

Leverage and Cash Flow Strain

The consolidated debt ratio rose 14.0 percentage points from 53.2% at end-2025 to 67.2% at end-Q3 2026. Operating cash flow was negative in every year from 2022 to 2025, reaching KRW -4.72 billion in 2025. A planned KRW 1.5 billion purchase of Lumens-group shares adds to funding needs.

10

Risk factors

Subsidiary Dependence and Valuation Gains

Consolidated sales and profit depend heavily on subsidiary results and fair-value gains. The KRW 15.1 billion valuation gain is one-off in nature, so it cannot be assumed to recur. A slowdown at the subsidiary would change the direction of consolidated profit.

Demand and Policy Risk

Tariff policy and export uncertainty are constraining capital spending in key end markets. Heavy equipment accounts for 76.7% of standalone sales, so this exposure is large. A cut in customer orders would quickly affect parts revenue.

Financial and Liquidity Risk

The consolidated debt ratio rose to 67.2%, and operating cash outflow widened in 2025. Group share purchases such as the KRW 1.5 billion Lumens-group buy add to funding needs. A prolonged loss period could also affect financing conditions.

12

Overall view

NEXTURN & ROLL KOREA has grown both sales and operating profit, with a 2025 annual turn to profit and three straight quarters of revenue growth through Q3 2026. Consolidated revenue expanded from KRW 29 billion in 2022 to KRW 70.4 billion in 2025, centered on subsidiary consolidation.

Bullish factors include positive quarterly operating profit, cash and a lower standalone debt ratio from the property sale, and entry into semiconductor parts.

Bearish factors include persistent standalone operating losses, repeated shareholder-level losses with a KRW 15.5 billion net loss over the latest four quarters, and a consolidated debt ratio that rose to 67.2%.

The Q3 2026 swing to a shareholder net loss despite positive operating profit means non-operating items and the non-controlling interest structure need to be read alongside operations. One-off gains from valuation and asset sales are mixed into results, so trend analysis should center on operating figures.

The next checkpoints are the 2026 annual report, the outcome of the Lumens-group share purchase, and whether standalone operating profit turns positive in 2027. This report does not present an investment opinion or target price.
