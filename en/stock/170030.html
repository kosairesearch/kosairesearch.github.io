Q3 2026 Profit Plunges as Auto Output Slows

02

Business structure

Hyundai Industrial was founded in 1969 as Hyundai Industrial Co., converted into a corporation in 1978, and listed on KOSDAQ in 2013. It operates as a tier supplier to Hyundai Motor and specializes in interior seating components for automobiles.

Its core products are automotive seat pads, armrests, headrests and side pads, produced on the basis of seat-pad foaming technology. As of December 2025, headrests accounted for 28.16% of sales, seat pads for 26.46%, armrests for 24.19% and other items such as side pads for 21.19% (FnGuide).

However, the 'other side pads' share jumped from 6.36% in 2024 to 21.19% in 2025 and the back board line shows 0%, so whether the product classification changed needs to be checked in the quarterly report. On the automaker side, Hyundai Motor and KIA are the confirmed customers.

Production sites include the Ulsan headquarters, the Asan plant, a plant in the United States and plants in China, and the Asan No. 2 plant was established in June 2023. The company holds two China production subsidiaries as consolidated entities, and it sold one Chinese subsidiary in May 2024. R&D spending was KRW 3,189 million in 2024, or 0.98% of sales. Headcount stood at 142 as of September 2026.

04

Earnings analysis

On the basis of disclosed results, annual revenue rose from KRW 278.5 billion in 2022 to KRW 307.5 billion in 2023 (up 10.4%), KRW 326.9 billion in 2024 (up 6.3%) and KRW 367.9 billion in 2025 (up 12.5%).

Revenue has increased for three consecutive years from 2023 through 2025, and 2025 was the largest year in the 2022 to 2025 range.

Operating profit was KRW 15.1 billion in 2022 and KRW 15.4 billion in 2023 (up 2.3%), making 2023 the largest year, before falling to KRW 10.9 billion in 2024 (down 29.3%) and recovering to KRW 12.3 billion in 2025 (up 13.2%).

The operating margin declined from 5.4% in 2022 to 5.0% in 2023, 3.3% in 2024 and 3.3% in 2025. Even as revenue grew 12.5% in 2025, the margin stayed at the 2024 level.

Net income attributable to controlling shareholders reached KRW 11.4 billion in 2022 and peaked at KRW 13.1 billion in 2023 (up 15.0%), then declined to KRW 12.9 billion in 2024 (down 1.8%) and KRW 11.8 billion in 2025 (down 8.0%), a two-year decline.

Net income including non-controlling interests was reported at the same amounts in each year, so no gap appears between the two measures in this period.

Cash flow weakened sharply: operating cash flow went from KRW 19.8 billion in 2022 and KRW 34 billion in 2023 to negative KRW 230 million in 2024 and negative KRW 8.98 billion in 2025.

The debt-to-equity ratio was 51.3% in 2022, 59.5% in 2023, 46.2% in 2024 and 60.1% in 2025, and at the end of 2025 total equity stood at KRW 162 billion with total liabilities at KRW 97.4 billion.

Quarterly revenue peaked at KRW 98.8 billion in Q4 2025 (up 14.4% from the prior quarter) and then fell to KRW 91.2 billion in Q1 2026 (down 7.8%), KRW 85.5 billion in Q2 2026 (down 6.2%) and KRW 78.3 billion in Q3 2026 (down 8.5%), a three-quarter decline.

Operating profit fell to KRW 1.07 billion in Q4 2025, recovered to KRW 5.51 billion in Q1 2026 (up 417.7% from the prior quarter) and KRW 6.26 billion in Q2 2026 (up 13.6%), then dropped to KRW 374 million in Q3 2026 (down 94.0%).

Net income attributable to controlling shareholders climbed to KRW 10.6 billion in Q2 2026, the largest of the last five quarters, but turned into a net loss of KRW 3.54 billion in Q3 2026. An aggregator, Google Finance, shows Q2 2026 non-operating income at KRW 24.38 million?

Correction: KRW 2.438 billion, up from KRW 317 million in the prior quarter, but the cause of the gap between Q2 operating profit and net income, and the reason Q3 posted operating profit yet a controlling-shareholder net loss, have not been confirmed and should be checked in the quarterly reports' non-operating and tax notes.

05

Industry analysis

The end market for Hyundai Industrial is Hyundai Motor and KIA vehicle output and sales. Hyundai Motor's global wholesale sales in Q3 2026 were 920,710 units, down 11.3% from a year earlier, which the automaker attributed to fewer operating days around the Chuseok holiday combined with production disruptions.

Domestic sales fell 27.2% amid the effects of a labor strike. Over the same quarter, Hyundai Motor's automotive segment revenue fell 10.0% to KRW 33.0058 trillion, while automotive operating profit rose 24.8% to KRW 2.135 trillion.

Even as volumes fell, hybrid sales rose year on year to 18.2% of total sales, while total electrified vehicle sales declined 2.7%. Whether this mix shift changes seat-component volumes or unit prices per vehicle cannot be confirmed from the available data.

The average KRW/USD rate in Q3 2026 was 1,423 won, up 2.8% from a year earlier, and Hyundai Motor's cost of sales ratio rose 1.5 percentage points to 83.8% on higher raw-material prices. How automaker cost pressure feeds into supplier price negotiations was not confirmed in public materials.

Among listed interior-parts peers, Seoyon E-Hwa operates in a similar area, but no comparable earnings figures were obtained in this review. Hyundai Industrial's volumes are tied directly to the production and sales cycle at Hyundai Motor and KIA, so automaker supply conditions are the main industry variable.

06

Outlook

No formal company guidance for revenue or operating profit targets was found in this review. The confirmed moves are on the asset and structure side. The Asan No. 2 plant was established in June 2023, and the company sold one Chinese subsidiary in May 2024.

In its performance commentary, the company described rising output at Hyundai Motor and KIA and sales from its Georgia plant in the United States as lifting North American revenue and improving results. That explanation does not address the cause of the Q3 2026 revenue decline.

Q3 2026 revenue of KRW 78.3 billion marks a third straight quarterly decline, so whether Q4 2026 revenue turns up again is the near-term checkpoint. Because Hyundai Motor faced domestic production disruptions in Q3, its Q4 sales and output trend can serve as a volume signal for Hyundai Industrial.

Whether the Asan No. 2 plant ramp and the China subsidiary sale affected 2026 results needs to be confirmed in the 2026 annual report. How long the North American volume lasted also needs to be checked against the quarterly revenue mix.

Bull factors

Customer output and North American plant

Revenue reached KRW 367.9 billion in 2025, up 12.5% from a year earlier. The company's performance commentary attributes higher North American sales to rising output at Hyundai Motor and KIA and volumes from its Georgia plant.

Because its customers are concentrated in the automaker group, a recovery in customer output could feed quickly into volumes.

Quarterly profit recovery

Operating profit recovered from KRW 1.07 billion in Q4 2025 to KRW 5.51 billion in Q1 2026 and KRW 6.26 billion in Q2 2026. Net income attributable to controlling shareholders of KRW 10.6 billion in Q2 2026 was the largest of the last five quarters. The two-quarter recovery shows the earnings capacity before the sharp Q3 drop.

Balance sheet base and dividend record

Total equity was KRW 162 billion at the end of 2025 and total liabilities were KRW 97.4 billion. The company recorded cash dividends every year from 2018 through 2022, and cash dividends are also recorded on a 2025 year-end basis. The equity base is large relative to annual revenue, which provides a capital cushion for short-term losses.

Bear factors

Sharp Q3 profit decline

Operating profit fell to KRW 374 million in Q3 2026, down 91.5% from a year earlier. Net income attributable to controlling shareholders swung from a profit of KRW 6.29 billion in Q3 2025 to a net loss of KRW 3.54 billion in Q3 2026. Revenue has declined for three consecutive quarters, and Q3 2026 revenue was down 9.4% year on year.

Margin erosion and cost burden

The operating margin declined from 5.4% in 2022 to 3.3% in both 2024 and 2025. The cost of sales ratio also rose from 90.78% in 2022 to 91.38% in 2025 (FnGuide). Even as 2025 revenue grew 12.5%, net income attributable to controlling shareholders fell 8.0%.

Cash outflow and rising debt ratio

Operating cash flow was negative KRW 230 million in 2024 and negative KRW 8.98 billion in 2025, two consecutive years of outflows. The debt-to-equity ratio rose from 46.2% in 2024 to 60.1% in 2025. If operating cash continues not to come in, reliance on borrowing could grow, which is a bearish factor.

Risk factors

Customer output concentration

Revenue is tied to output and sales at Hyundai Motor and KIA. Hyundai Motor's global wholesale sales fell 11.3% year on year in Q3 2026, and a domestic labor strike was one factor in production disruption.

The timing overlaps with the company's Q3 revenue decline, which may point to customer output affecting volumes, but the causal link needs confirmation in the quarterly report.

Cost and margin pressure

The cost of sales ratio rose from 90.78% in 2022 to 91.38% in 2025, and the operating margin stayed at 3.3% in both 2024 and 2025. Public disclosures do not show how much cost increases are passed through to automaker prices.

At a low margin, falling revenue makes it harder to absorb fixed costs, so operating leverage can work in reverse.

Cash flow and financial capacity

Operating cash flow was negative in both 2024 and 2025, and total liabilities stood at KRW 97.4 billion at the end of 2025. The debt-to-equity ratio rose to 60.1% in 2025 from 46.2% in 2024. Whether the Q3 net loss has slowed the pace of earnings accumulation will be visible in upcoming quarterly disclosures.

Overall view

Hyundai Industrial is a KOSDAQ-listed maker of automotive interior seating components whose main customers are Hyundai Motor and KIA.

Annual revenue rose for three consecutive years from 2023 through 2025, reaching KRW 367.9 billion in 2025, the largest figure in the period, but the operating margin has stayed at 3.3% since 2024.

Net income attributable to controlling shareholders fell for two straight years to KRW 11.8 billion in 2025, and operating cash flow was negative in both 2024 and 2025.

Operating profit recovered in Q1 and Q2 2026, but in Q3 2026 revenue fell to KRW 78.3 billion and operating profit to KRW 374 million, and the company posted a net loss of KRW 3.54 billion.

The bullish factors are the structure that passes through volumes quickly when customer output recovers, the North American plant effect, and a market capitalization below controlling-shareholder equity.

The bearish factors are three consecutive quarters of revenue decline, a rising cost of sales ratio, and negative cash flow alongside a higher debt ratio.

Both sets of factors should be weighed equally, and the next checkpoints are whether Q4 revenue recovers, what the quarterly report says about the gap between Q3 operating profit and net loss, and the cash flow in the 2026 annual report.
