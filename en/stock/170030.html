Sales Growth, 3% Margins and a Q3 2026 Net Loss

02

Business structure

Hyundai Industrial was founded in 1969, converted to a corporation in 1978 and listed on KOSDAQ in 2013 as an automotive interior seating parts maker. As one of the oldest suppliers to Hyundai Motor, it develops and produces seat pads, armrests, headrests and side pads and supplies them to Hyundai Motor and KIA.

Its core products are seat components built on polyurethane foam technology, and its production bases are split among the Ulsan headquarters, the Asan plant, a U.S. plant and a China plant. It set up a second Asan plant in June 2023 and holds two China production subsidiaries as consolidated entities.

Because production sites are split between Korea and overseas, exchange rates and local sales trends also feed into consolidated results. Product mix at the end of 2025 was headrests at 28.16%, seat pads at 26.46%, armrests at 24.19% and side pads and other items at 21.19%.

The share of side pads and other items rose sharply from 6.36% at the end of 2024, which points to a shift away from the three core products.

The same table shows 'Other' and 'Back board' at 0.00% at the end of 2025, suggesting a possible change in classification, so the detailed tables in the annual report should be checked. Customers are concentrated on Hyundai Motor and KIA, so production schedules and model mix at these two groups directly drive sales.

On competition, a technical analysis report published in 2020 put the company's share of Korean automotive new seat manufacturing shipments in 2018 at 4.6%.

04

Earnings analysis

Annual sales rose from KRW 278.5 billion in 2022 to KRW 307.5 billion in 2023 (up 10.4% year on year), KRW 326.9 billion in 2024 (up 6.3%) and KRW 367.9 billion in 2025 (up 12.5%), marking three straight years of growth from 2023 through 2025, with 2025 the largest year in the 2022–2025 period.

Operating profit went from KRW 15.1 billion in 2022 to KRW 15.4 billion in 2023 (up 2.3%), then fell to KRW 10.9 billion in 2024 (down 29.3%) before recovering to KRW 12.3 billion in 2025 (up 13.2%); 2023 was the largest year and 2024 the smallest in the 2022–2025 period.

Operating margin slid from 5.4% in 2022 to 5.0% in 2023, 3.3% in 2024 and 3.3% in 2025, and in 2023 operating profit rose only 2.3% while sales grew 10.4%, which narrowed the margin.

Net income attributable to controlling shareholders went from KRW 11.4 billion in 2022 to KRW 13.1 billion in 2023 (up 15.0%), the peak year, then to KRW 12.9 billion in 2024 (down 1.8%) and KRW 11.8 billion in 2025 (down 8.0%), a two-year decline; net income including non-controlling interests is reported at KRW 11.4 billion, KRW 13.1 billion, KRW 12.9 billion and KRW 11.8 billion for the same years, identical to the attributable figures in the table.

Operating cash flow rose from KRW 19.8 billion in 2022 to KRW 34 billion in 2023, then turned negative at -KRW 230 million in 2024 and -KRW 8.98 billion in 2025.

At the end of 2025, total equity was KRW 162 billion, of which KRW 162 billion was attributable to controlling shareholders, and total liabilities were KRW 97.4 billion; the debt ratio rose from 46.2% in 2024 to 60.1% in 2025.

By quarter, sales peaked at KRW 98.8 billion in Q4 2025 (up 14.4% from the prior quarter) and then fell to KRW 91.2 billion in Q1 2026, KRW 85.5 billion in Q2 2026 and KRW 78.3 billion in Q3 2026, three consecutive quarterly declines.

Operating profit dropped to KRW 1.07 billion in Q4 2025 (down 75.8% from the prior quarter) before recovering to KRW 5.51 billion in Q1 2026 (up 417.7%) and KRW 6.26 billion in Q2 2026 (up 13.6%).

Q3 2026 operating profit fell to KRW 374 million (down 94.0% from the prior quarter and down 91.5% from a year earlier), and the company recorded a net loss attributable to controlling shareholders of KRW 3.54 billion, a swing into deficit from a year earlier.

Net income attributable to controlling shareholders was KRW 6.29 billion in Q3 2025, KRW 1.14 billion in Q4 2025, KRW 5.04 billion in Q1 2026 and KRW 10.6 billion in Q2 2026; the Q2 2026 figure far exceeded operating profit, which aggregated market data suggest reflects higher non-operating income, while the Q3 2026 loss despite positive operating profit points to non-operating or tax items.

Whether these non-operating items were one-off in nature should be confirmed in the non-operating profit and loss breakdown of the annual report.

05

Industry analysis

The company's results are tied to production and sales at Hyundai Motor and KIA, so changes in OEM demand flow directly into its revenue. According to Hyundai Motor's figures, global wholesale sales in Q3 2026 fell 11.3% from a year earlier to 920,710 units.

Fewer operating days because of the Chuseok holiday and production disruptions compounded the drop, and domestic sales fell 27.2% amid strike-related disruptions. Over the same period, hybrid electric vehicle (HEV) sales rose while electric vehicle sales declined, leaving total electrified vehicle sales down 2.7%.

Hyundai Motor's automotive segment operating profit rose 24.8% to KRW 2.135 trillion in Q3 2026, helped partly by exchange-rate effects and cost-cutting activities. Hyundai Motor's cost of sales ratio rose 1.5 percentage points to 83.8% in the same quarter, while its SG&A ratio fell 2.0 points to 10.3%.

How the shift in model mix toward HEVs affects demand for seat components by item is a point to check against model-level sales data. In North America, a company performance comment cited higher output at the Georgia plant as a driver of higher sales for Hyundai Industrial.

In competition, Hyundai Industrial is one of Korea's automotive seat component makers, and its share of 2018 domestic automotive new seat shipments was 4.6%.

The second Asan plant expanded domestic production capacity, which improves the ability to respond when model volumes rise.

With sales down for three consecutive quarters through Q3 2026 and operating profit at KRW 374 million, whether revenue recovers after Q4 is the first variable that will determine whether the operating margin recovers.

Higher raw material prices and currency swings pushed up cost of sales at Hyundai Motor in Q3 2026, so the same pressure could weigh on supplier margins. The 2025 annual report was filed on March 20, 2026, and the next periodic report is the 2026 annual report.

The 2026 annual report should be checked first for the causes of the Q3 deficit, the composition of non-operating items and any change in product classification. The contribution from the two China production subsidiaries can be confirmed from the subsidiary information in the annual report.

Dividend decisions are handled as an agenda item at the regular shareholders' meeting in March 2027, so the direction of dividends after the Q3 deficit can be checked in the same material.

Bull factors

Volume contribution from North American plants

A company performance comment credited higher output at Hyundai Motor and KIA and operations at the Georgia plant in the United States with lifting North American sales and overall results. Annual sales reached KRW 367.9 billion in 2025, up 12.5%, the fastest growth rate since 2023. A North American production base can absorb volume when vehicle sales recover.

Operating profit recovery in H1 2026

Operating profit rebounded from KRW 1.07 billion in Q4 2025 to KRW 5.51 billion in Q1 2026 and KRW 6.26 billion in Q2 2026. Profit rose over this stretch even as sales declined, which shows the company held on to earnings while volumes fell.

Net income attributable to controlling shareholders of KRW 10.6 billion in Q2 2026 was the highest of the last five quarters.

Equity exceeds liabilities

Total equity was KRW 162 billion at the end of 2025 against total liabilities of KRW 97.4 billion, so equity exceeds liabilities. The debt ratio rose from 46.2% in 2024 to 60.1% in 2025, but the equity base remains larger than liabilities.

Bulls argue that this capital base can absorb earnings swings even while operating margin stays in the 3% range.

Overall view

Hyundai Industrial's sales grew from KRW 278.5 billion in 2022 to KRW 367.9 billion in 2025, with three straight years of increases from 2023, so the scale of the business expanded.

Operating margin, however, declined from 5.4% in 2022 to 3.3% in both 2024 and 2025, and net income attributable to controlling shareholders fell for two years after peaking at KRW 13.1 billion in 2023.

Sales declined for three consecutive quarters through 2026, and Q3 2026 showed operating profit of KRW 374 million and a net loss attributable to controlling shareholders of KRW 3.54 billion.

Operating profit recovered to KRW 5.51 billion in Q1 2026 and KRW 6.26 billion in Q2 2026, which also shows how much quarterly earnings can swing. Strengths include the contribution from North American production, the earnings recovery in the first half of 2026 and an equity base larger than total liabilities.

Weaknesses include sales declines tied to customer concentration, an operating margin in the 3% range and negative operating cash flow. Valuation sits with the share price well below net assets, but the interpretation depends on whether earnings and cash flow recover.

The next checkpoints are the non-operating items and product classification in the 2026 annual report and whether operating profit recovers in the Q1 2027 quarterly report.
