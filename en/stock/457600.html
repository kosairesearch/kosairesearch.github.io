Report overview

Robotics pivot under way, losses and funding remain the variables

Vect is pursuing a shift from a digital signage business toward a robotics and physical AI platform company, but it has posted losses for five consecutive quarters from the third quarter of 2025 through the third quarter of 2026, so both the results of this pivot and its funding remain open questions.

02

Business structure

Vect was founded on March 30, 2006 and is listed on KOSDAQ. Its legacy business centers on digital signage and electronic whiteboards for education ICT. According to company profile data, digital signage accounts for 99.3% of the business segment mix.

At the September 2026 extraordinary shareholders meeting, Vect changed its name to Vect Robotics and added to its business purposes the R&D, manufacturing and sales of physical AI-based robot systems for manufacturing, defense and special-purpose use, along with system integration of robotic automation equipment.

The new purposes also include robot control software and operating platforms, digital twin-based engineering software, retrieval-augmented generation (RAG) systems and cloud software services. The company plans to combine robot hardware and software by leveraging the customer base built in its signage business.

Its core product is ROXI, a platform that integrates robot perception, decision-making and control. ROXI and related robots, including an upper-body humanoid and a mobile dual-arm robot, were shown at ROBOWORLD 2026 at KINTEX in Goyang from November 4 to 7, 2026.

The company plans to extend the applications from welding to parts assembly, logistics transfer and humanoid control. Its target customers are small and mid-sized manufacturers running high-mix, low-volume production.

The CEO has described Vect as a latecomer that will focus on a software platform spanning robots from multiple manufacturers rather than competing on hardware price.

04

Earnings analysis

Annual revenue fell from KRW 68.9 billion in 2023 to KRW 64.7 billion in 2024, down 6.1% year on year, and then to KRW 44.5 billion in 2025, down 31.1%. Revenue declined in both 2024 and 2025, and 2023 was the largest year and 2025 the smallest in the 2023 to 2025 period.

Operating profit of KRW 4.02 billion in 2023 turned into an operating loss of KRW 1.28 billion in 2024, and the loss widened to KRW 3.43 billion in 2025. The operating margin moved from 5.8% in 2023 to -2.0% in 2024 and -7.7% in 2025.

Net income attributable to controlling shareholders of KRW 1.96 billion in 2023 turned into a net loss of KRW 1.8 billion in 2024, and the loss widened to KRW 4.6 billion in 2025.

Net income including non-controlling interests moved from KRW 2.26 billion in 2023 to a net loss of KRW 2.28 billion in 2024 and KRW 5.22 billion in 2025, a different series from the controlling-shareholder figure.

Operating cash flow improved from -KRW 400 million in 2023 and -KRW 4.58 billion in 2024 to KRW 4.5 billion in 2025, turning positive. The debt-to-equity ratio fell from 220.2% in 2023 to 117.9% in 2024 and 116.3% in 2025.

At the end of 2025, total equity was KRW 18.5 billion, of which KRW 18 billion belonged to controlling shareholders, and total liabilities were KRW 21.5 billion.

Quarterly revenue was KRW 8.39 billion in the third quarter of 2025, KRW 8.61 billion in the fourth quarter, KRW 15.7 billion in the first quarter of 2026 (up 82.3% quarter on quarter), KRW 6.06 billion in the second quarter (down 61.4%) and KRW 10 billion in the third quarter (up 65.6% quarter on quarter and up 19.6% year on year).

Operating losses were KRW 963 million, KRW 992 million, KRW 645 million, KRW 1.71 billion and KRW 1.1 billion across the five quarters from the third quarter of 2025 to the third quarter of 2026, which makes five consecutive loss-making quarters.

Net loss attributable to controlling shareholders narrowed to KRW 917 million in the third quarter of 2026 from a year earlier, and the four-quarter total from the fourth quarter of 2025 through the third quarter of 2026 was KRW 4.83 billion.

The filings alone do not show whether the large quarterly swings came from one-off contracts or recurring sales.

05

Industry analysis

The market Vect targets is manufacturing sites running high-mix, low-volume production, especially small and mid-sized companies. Rather than building robot hardware itself, the company says it will combine robots from several manufacturers into one software platform and train them on work intelligence.

This approach reads as an attempt to establish a position in the perception, decision and control layer rather than compete head-on with robot makers.

In welding, the first application, the company plans to verify simplified robot teaching based on work instructions for cutting and welding steel beams, jig position recognition and AI-based quality uniformity.

Reducing dependence on skilled workers in preparing and setting up welding work is presented as the core value. Demand from the downstream market depends on manufacturers seeking to automate work that has relied on skilled labor.

On competition, the company acknowledges that it is a latecomer, and in a setting where robot makers and software firms are both promoting physical AI platforms, its differentiation must be proven on actual factory floors.

Its existing digital signage and electronic whiteboard business serves education and public procurement customers, and whether this base can serve as a customer entry point for the robotics business is a point to watch.

06

Outlook

The company is rapidly hiring robot software specialists and plans to build an 'AI skill library' that accumulates process-specific work skills by learning from design drawings and work environment data. It has filed four core patent applications related to its platform technology.

The welding proof of concept is scheduled to begin in early 2027, and the company is preparing a robotic welding automation system for a fitness equipment manufacturer. The roadmap then extends process-specific AI skills to parts assembly, logistics transfer and humanoid control.

The CEO said the company aims to become a general-purpose physical AI platform company connecting robots and processes across industrial sites in Korea and abroad, starting from the welding PoC.

On funding, reports said a rights offering of about KRW 8.3 billion to Blackstone Holdings was scheduled for September 2026, with proceeds to be used as operating funds including raw material purchases for new businesses. Amended rights offering filings followed on October 30 and November 10, 2026.

On the ownership side, Uni Holdings Investment Fund No. 1 agreed to acquire 2 million shares from the former largest shareholder side for KRW 5 billion, and the governance structure is expected to become clearer once that transaction is completed. Whether the PoC leads to actual orders and revenue is the next point to confirm.

The price-to-book ratio is therefore the more commonly referenced measure, and it sits above 1x, which reads as a premium to book net assets.

That premium appears to reflect expectations for the business shift toward a robotics platform and the existing signage customer base rather than the value of book assets. The company does not pay a dividend, so its dividend yield is below the industry average.

Its market capitalization falls in the small-cap segment on KOSDAQ. On the balance sheet, the debt-to-equity ratio fell from 220.2% in 2023 to 116.3% in 2025, but controlling shareholders' equity is a small share of total equity, and while operating cash flow turned positive, cash and cash equivalents declined.

Bull factors

Clearer business direction

At a September 2026 extraordinary shareholders meeting, Vect changed its name and business purposes toward robotics and AI, and it unveiled the ROXI platform at ROBOWORLD 2026. The company has set out a strategy centered on a software platform spanning robots from multiple makers rather than hardware. Welding was chosen as the first application, with a proof of concept planned to begin in early 2027.

Signs of quarterly revenue recovery

Revenue in the third quarter of 2026 was KRW 10 billion, up 19.6% from a year earlier. Quarterly revenue of KRW 15.7 billion in the first quarter of 2026 and KRW 10 billion in the third quarter compares with KRW 8.39 billion and KRW 8.61 billion in the third and fourth quarters of 2025. Whether this level repeats in later quarters remains to be confirmed.

Improvement in financial structure and cash flow

The debt-to-equity ratio fell from 220.2% in 2023 to 116.3% in 2025. Operating cash flow turned from -KRW 4.58 billion in 2024 to KRW 4.5 billion in 2025, a positive figure. The net loss attributable to controlling shareholders narrowed to KRW 917 million in the third quarter of 2026 from a year earlier.

09

Bear factors

Consecutive losses and weaker profitability

Vect has recorded losses for five consecutive quarters from the third quarter of 2025 through the third quarter of 2026. The operating margin moved from 5.8% in 2023 to -2.0% in 2024 and -7.7% in 2025. Revenue for 2025 was KRW 44.5 billion, down 31.1% year on year. The operating loss of KRW 1.1 billion in the third quarter of 2026 was wider than a year earlier.

Funding burden

Amended rights offering filings were submitted repeatedly, including on October 30 and November 10, 2026. Press reports cite cash and cash equivalents of KRW 4.82 billion, down 34.6% from the prior period.

A large part of the funds used by the new largest shareholder to acquire its stake came from borrowing from Blackstone Holdings.

Reliance on the legacy business and an unproven pivot

Digital signage accounts for 99.3% of the business segment mix. The robotics business is at the stage of starting a welding proof of concept in early 2027, and no robotics revenue is visible in the reported figures.

Until customer wins and order sizes for the robot platform are disclosed, the effect of the pivot will not appear in the reported numbers.

10

Risk factors

Platform patent risk

Four core patent applications are still pending, so their registration status and scope of protection remain undetermined.

Because ROXI's differentiation depends on combining robots from multiple manufacturers into one software layer, a delay in registration or narrower claims could weaken the company's position as a latecomer.

If compatibility with other manufacturers' robot control structures is not sufficiently verified in the field, the platform expansion plan could also be delayed.

Talent and roadmap risk

The company is rapidly hiring robot software specialists, and building the AI skill library by learning from design drawings and work environment data depends on this talent. If hiring falls behind plan, the accumulation of process-specific AI skills could be delayed.

This could affect the timing of the entire sequential roadmap extending from welding to parts assembly, logistics transfer and humanoid control.

Governance and audit risk

The largest shareholder changed to KBN Holdings following an August 2026 agreement, and further share transactions are scheduled. Several filings related to management changes have been reported. The auditor issued an unqualified opinion, and revenue recognition timing was presented as a key audit matter.

12

Overall view

In September 2026, Vect changed its name and business purposes toward robotics and physical AI, and at ROBOWORLD 2026 it unveiled the ROXI platform and welding AI solutions. The business direction has become clearer, but the financials still reflect the structure before the pivot.

Revenue for 2025 was KRW 44.5 billion, down 31.1% year on year, and the operating loss widened to KRW 3.43 billion. Revenue in the third quarter of 2026 was KRW 10 billion, up 19.6% year on year, yet the operating loss of KRW 1.1 billion extended a run of five consecutive loss-making quarters.

The financial structure improved, with the debt-to-equity ratio falling from 220.2% in 2023 to 116.3% in 2025 and operating cash flow turning positive. On the other side, repeated amendments to the rights offering and declining cash make funding a near-term variable.

Because the robotics business is only at the stage of starting a welding proof of concept in early 2027, whether it converts into orders and revenue has not yet been established, and this is what separates the bullish and bearish readings.

The next checkpoints are the operating margin in the 2026 annual report, the first-quarter 2027 results, and whether customers adopt the platform after the proof of concept.
