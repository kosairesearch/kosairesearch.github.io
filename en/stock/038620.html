WIZ: Shrinking Quarterly Sales, Swinging Profits

WIZ runs fuel stations, academy education and highway rest stops. Quarterly revenue has declined for four consecutive quarters since the fourth quarter of 2025, and the company swung to an operating loss in the third quarter of 2026.

  2. 2

  3. 3

  4. 4

02

Business structure

WIZ was founded in 1995 as a developer of information system software, but it now operates mainly fuel stations, academy education and highway rest stops.

By segment share of second-quarter 2026 revenue, fuel station merchandise sales accounted for 55%, academy education for 32%, rest stop merchandise for 12%, and rest stop rental income for 1%, according to Butler data.

The fuel station business sells diesel, gasoline and LPG, so oil product prices and sales volume drive its revenue scale. The education business centers on tuition fees and also runs online content and after-school programs. Subsidiaries include Wiznetwork, Olympiad Education and Dreamone Systems.

The rest stop business expanded with the Namhangang rest stop in May 2024, following the Jeongeup, Juam and Suncheon stops on the Honam Expressway, and its main revenue comes from food products such as walnut cakes and udon as well as convenience store and coffee outlets.

According to WISEreport, the opening of the Namhangang rest stop and new fuel stations in 2024 lifted sales, but initial operating costs and the burden of adopting advanced technology reduced operating profit.

In fuel stations WIZ competes with refiner-affiliated and independent stations, and in education it competes with local academies and tutoring providers. Rest stop operations depend on the terms under which the company runs highway routes.

04

Earnings analysis

On a reported basis, annual revenue rose from KRW 41.2 billion in 2022 to KRW 56.1 billion in 2023 (up 36.0% year on year), KRW 104.7 billion in 2024 (up 86.7%), and KRW 112.6 billion in 2025 (up 7.6%). Most of the expansion came in 2024, and growth slowed in 2025.

Operating profit fell from KRW 1.45 billion in 2022 to KRW 648 million in 2023 (down 55.2%), recovered to KRW 2.79 billion in 2024 (up 330.8%), and was KRW 2.49 billion in 2025 (down 10.8%).

Operating margin was 3.5% in 2022, 1.2% in 2023, 2.7% in 2024 and 2.2% in 2025, remaining low even as revenue grew about 2.7 times over the period. For 2025, the company attributed the operating profit decline to a higher cost ratio.

Net income attributable to controlling shareholders was KRW 2.85 billion in 2022, KRW 1.43 billion in 2023, KRW 345 million in 2024 and KRW 4.29 billion in 2025, the largest of the four years.

Net income including non-controlling interests was KRW 4.67 billion in 2025, and the company said higher valuation gains on financial assets at fair value through profit or loss supported the increase, which suggests non-operating valuation gains lay behind net income exceeding operating profit.

Quarterly revenue fell from KRW 30.1 billion in the third quarter of 2025 to KRW 25.6 billion in the third quarter of 2026, down 14.8% year on year in the latest quarter.

Operating profit recovered to KRW 1.5 billion in the first quarter of 2026 (up 258.3% quarter on quarter) and KRW 2.22 billion in the second quarter (up 48.6%), then the third quarter posted an operating loss of KRW 906 million.

Net income attributable to controlling shareholders peaked at KRW 7.66 billion in the second quarter of 2026 before a net loss of KRW 801 million in the third quarter, and the last four quarters total KRW 11.2 billion. Revenue declined while operating profit swung sharply, indicating high margin volatility by quarter.

05

Industry analysis

WIZ's revenue mixes three demand drivers: petroleum products, education services and food sales at highway rest stops. The fuel station business depends on domestic oil prices and driving demand, and margins come under pressure when price pass-through lags.

The education business relies on tuition and after-school demand and is affected by the school-age population and local competition. Rest stop sales depend on highway traffic and store location.

In an Alphasquare comparison of 17 companies in other industries on a 2025 annual basis, WIZ's operating margin of 2.21% slightly exceeded the industry average of 1.90%. In the same comparison, its debt ratio of 18.80% was well below the industry average of 88.58%.

Its revenue and operating profit scale are both smaller than the industry average. WIZ therefore has a smaller top line but a margin and balance sheet position comparable to or better than the industry average.

Fuel station merchandise, at 55% of the mix, is the largest segment, so oil market price swings are a key factor in its position within the industry.

Earnings recovery

Operating profit then increased for two straight quarters, to KRW 1.5 billion in the first quarter of 2026 and KRW 2.22 billion in the second, showing recovery at the operating level before the third-quarter swing to a loss.

Operating cash flow was positive in each of the four years from 2022 to 2025, at KRW 1.01 billion, KRW 2.34 billion, KRW 7.26 billion and KRW 5.57 billion.

Reliance on non-operating gains

Net income attributable to controlling shareholders of KRW 4.29 billion in 2025 exceeded operating profit of KRW 2.49 billion, and the company cited higher valuation gains on financial assets.

In the second quarter of 2026, net income attributable to controlling shareholders of KRW 7.66 billion also exceeded operating profit of KRW 2.22 billion. A structure where non-operating gains drive net income is considered a weakness.

Risk factors

Cost and fuel price pressure

The company attributed the 2025 operating profit decline to a higher cost ratio. Fuel station merchandise accounted for 55% of second-quarter 2026 revenue, so oil price swings feed directly into profitability. If price pass-through is limited, a margin of around 2.2% could narrow further.

Volatility in valuation gains

Valuation gains on financial assets at fair value through profit or loss supported net income in 2025, and such gains can turn into losses depending on market conditions.

The third-quarter 2026 net loss attributable to controlling shareholders of KRW 801 million appeared in the same quarter as the operating loss of KRW 906 million. Depending on the direction of valuation gains, net income can diverge sharply from operating trends.

Rest stop investment burden

After the 2024 openings of the Namhangang rest stop and new fuel stations, initial operating costs and the cost of adopting advanced technology were cited as factors weighing on profit. If new stores do not reach stable sales, the cost burden could delay the earnings recovery. The pace of profitability improvement at rest stops is a variable that shapes the annual operating margin.

Overall view

WIZ is a consumer distribution company operating fuel stations, academy education and highway rest stops. Its revenue rose from KRW 41.2 billion in 2022 to KRW 112.6 billion in 2025, but its operating margin remained at a low 2.2% in 2025.

Net income attributable to controlling shareholders recovered to KRW 4.29 billion in 2025, though this included contributions from non-operating factors such as valuation gains.

Quarterly revenue has declined for four consecutive quarters in 2026, and the third quarter swung to an operating loss of KRW 906 million and a net loss attributable to controlling shareholders of KRW 801 million.

On the other hand, the debt ratio was a low 18.8% at the end of 2025, and operating cash flow stayed positive every year since 2022. Compared with the industry, the operating margin and debt structure are on par with or somewhat better than average, while earnings-based multiples are recorded well below the average.

Strengths are the profit recovery and financial stability, while weaknesses are the shrinking top line, the third-quarter swing to a loss and reliance on non-operating gains. The next checkpoints are the 2026 annual results and the segment cost ratios and operating margin in the 2026 annual business report.
