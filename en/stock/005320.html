02

Business structure

Ontide is a knit apparel manufacturer and seller founded in 1967, focused on OEM and ODM (original equipment and design manufacturing) for overseas brands. Apparel is the core of the business, and its main revenue comes from apparel exports.

Export accounted for 91.55% of product sales, with domestic sales at 8.45%, as of December 2025. Major customers named in public materials include Fanatics, Carhartt and H&M, and OEM contracts with these buyers form the order base.

Production runs through subsidiaries in Indonesia and Mexico, while a U.S. subsidiary handles sales. Affiliates include Kukdong Apparel, Inc. in the United States and PT. Semarang Garment in Indonesia, and the company also describes bio-related ventures through a subsidiary as diversification.

On 2025 annual sales, the company ranked around 27th among 71 listed apparel and fashion accessories companies. Comparing production footprints and customer mix with Youngone, Hansae and F&F shows how the business models differ. A high export share means overseas demand and local production costs feed directly into profit and loss.

04

Earnings analysis

Annual sales fell from KRW 329.9 billion in 2022 to KRW 224.4 billion in 2023, a 32.0% decline, then recovered to KRW 265.9 billion in 2024, up 18.5%, before dropping to KRW 231.5 billion in 2025, down 12.9%. Among 2022 to 2025, 2022 was the largest year and 2023 the smallest.

Operating profit of KRW 17 billion in 2022 turned into an operating loss of KRW 3.58 billion in 2023. The loss widened to KRW 4.35 billion in 2024 and narrowed to KRW 1.86 billion in 2025, and the company has posted losses for three consecutive years from 2023 to 2025.

Operating margin moved from 5.2% in 2022 to -1.6% in 2023, -1.6% in 2024 and -0.8% in 2025. The 2025 narrowing is attributed to cost reductions at the Indonesian and Mexican production subsidiaries, which offset lower sales from weaker global demand and fewer orders from key customers.

Net profit attributable to controlling shareholders moved from KRW 1.39 billion in 2022 to a loss of KRW 8.51 billion in 2023, a loss of KRW 3.79 billion in 2024, and a loss of KRW 10.7 billion in 2025. Quarterly sales rose from KRW 51.4 billion in 3Q 2025 to KRW 70 billion in 2Q 2026 and KRW 63.5 billion in 3Q 2026.

The operating loss of KRW 5.52 billion in 1Q 2026 is linked in a securities information site's commentary to losses at the Indonesian and Mexican apparel units and weak sales at the U.S. unit.

Operating profit of KRW 6.56 billion in 2Q 2026 and KRW 3.45 billion in 3Q 2026 followed, yet 3Q 2026 still recorded a net loss attributable to controlling shareholders of KRW 2.87 billion. At the end of 2025, total equity was KRW 105 billion, total liabilities were KRW 59 billion, and the debt ratio was 56.2%. Operating cash flow moved from negative KRW 4.04 billion in 2024 to KRW 5.81 billion in 2025.

Industry analysis

Ontide operates in an OEM apparel market where sales move with order volumes from overseas brands. Exports accounted for 91.55% of 2025 product sales, which means heavy exposure to changes in overseas consumption and order flow.

The 2025 explanation cited weaker global demand and fewer orders from key customers as the reasons for lower sales. Over the same period, cost reductions at the Indonesian and Mexican production subsidiaries improved profitability.

The position in the industry cycle can be read only from the trend in reported results, with sales falling from KRW 329.9 billion in 2022 through 2023 to 2025. Quarterly sales recovered to KRW 70 billion in 2Q 2026 and KRW 63.5 billion in 3Q 2026.

Whether this reflects a broader rebound or changes at specific customers cannot be separated from filings alone. On an annual basis for 2025, Alphasquare's apparel and fashion accessories sector data shows an operating margin of -0.80% for Ontide against a sector average of 2.22%.

Comparing with Youngone, Hansae and F&F highlights differences in scale and customer mix. Within the sector, the overseas cost structure is a variable linked to pricing power with buyers.

Bull factors

Return to operating profit in 2026

Operating profit of KRW 6.56 billion in 2Q 2026 marked a turn to profit, and KRW 3.45 billion in 3Q 2026 was also a turn from the prior-year quarter. The move from a KRW 1.86 billion operating loss in 2025 to operating profit in 2026 points in the direction of cost improvement.

Sales of KRW 63.5 billion in 3Q 2026 rose 23.6% from a year earlier, confirming a recovery in top-line volume as well.

Cost savings at production bases

The 2025 annual explanation pointed to cost reductions at the Indonesian and Mexican production subsidiaries as a factor behind better profitability. The operating loss narrowed even as sales declined, which indicates that efficiency gains at the production bases worked in part.

The cost structure of overseas production remains an area where further improvement is possible even after the 1Q 2026 loss.

Lower leverage and a larger controlling stake

Total equity was KRW 105 billion at the end of 2025 and the debt ratio fell to 56.2% from 60.5% in 2022. The lower leverage can be read as added capacity to absorb losses.

The de facto controlling shareholder raised its common-share stake to 54.12% as of November 3, 2026 through on-market purchases in October, according to the disclosures.

Overall view

Ontide's sales fell from KRW 329.9 billion in 2022 to KRW 231.5 billion in 2025, and the company has posted losses for three consecutive years from 2023 to 2025. The operating loss narrowed to KRW 1.86 billion in 2025, and operating profit returned in 2Q and 3Q 2026.

However, a net loss attributable to controlling shareholders of KRW 2.87 billion in 3Q 2026 and a cumulative KRW 7.73 billion over the last four quarters show that the link from operating profit to net profit remains weak.

Positive factors include the return to operating profit, a base of overseas customers, and a financial structure with total equity of KRW 105 billion and a debt ratio of 56.2%.

Negative factors include exposure to overseas demand with exports at 91.55% of product sales, earnings volatility at the Indonesian, Mexican and U.S. units, and year-to-year swings in operating cash flow.

The rise in the de facto controlling shareholder's common-share stake to 54.12% as of November 3, 2026 is also relevant for the trading structure and free float.

Because losses leave no earnings-based multiple, the core question is how much profit the asset base generates, with the market valuing the company below its total equity.

The next checkpoints are the 2026 annual report and 1Q 2027 results, and whether operating profit carries through to net profit attributable to controlling shareholders.
