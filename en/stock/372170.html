02

Business structure

Yunsung F&C is a specialist maker of secondary battery mixing equipment, founded in 1999 and listed on KOSDAQ in November 2022. Its core products are mixing equipment and systems that blend powder or liquid raw materials in battery manufacturing, supplied on a turn-key basis with customized solutions.

It also makes equipment for pharmaceutical, bio and food manufacturing, but the core business is secondary battery equipment. As of December 2025, the export share of the secondary battery segment was 89.23%, indicating high dependence on overseas customers, while the bio-pharmaceutical segment was 95.62% domestic.

Its flagship products are the large-capacity PD Mixer and the Powder Handling System (PHS), which the company says it developed in-house. Overseas subsidiaries in the United States, Hungary and China handle local customers. R&D spending equaled 8.11% of 2025 revenue.

Customers are domestic and overseas secondary battery makers, and the timing of orders and revenue recognition depends on the equipment investment cycle. Technical understanding and turn-key capability are cited as the core competitive strengths. In 2025 the company also began operating a solar power plant, a modest step beyond battery equipment.

04

Earnings analysis

On a disclosed annual basis, revenue rose from KRW 210.3 billion in 2022 to KRW 312.9 billion in 2023, a 48.8% increase, and then declined 13.3% to KRW 271.3 billion in 2024 and 63.1% to KRW 100.2 billion in 2025.

Revenue fell in both 2024 and 2025, and 2023 was the largest year and 2025 the smallest in the 2022 to 2025 period.

Operating profit was KRW 32.1 billion in 2022, KRW 26.5 billion in 2023 (down 17.5%) and KRW 31.9 billion in 2024 (up 20.5%), before an operating loss of KRW 31.6 billion in 2025 turned the year into a loss. The operating margin moved from 15.3% in 2022 to 8.5% in 2023, 11.8% in 2024 and -31.6% in 2025.

Net profit attributable to controlling shareholders reached KRW 37.1 billion in 2024 (up 51.3%) before a net loss of KRW 19.2 billion in 2025, and net income including non-controlling interests showed the same KRW 19.2 billion loss.

The company attributed the 2025 deterioration to one-off bad debt and inventory valuation losses related to Geumyang and a delay in revenue recognition from shipment delays on some projects.

Preliminary 2025 figures of KRW 92.6 billion in revenue and an operating loss of KRW 32.5 billion were corrected in March 2026 after audit adjustments. Operating cash flow moved from KRW negative 7.8 billion in 2022 and KRW negative 5.18 billion in 2023 to KRW 24.1 billion in 2024 and KRW 4.66 billion in 2025.

Quarterly, the operating loss deepened from KRW 10.9 billion in Q3 2025 to KRW 14.6 billion in Q4, narrowed to KRW 1.48 billion in Q1 2026, turned into operating profit of KRW 17.1 billion in Q2 2026 on revenue of KRW 59.8 billion, and returned to an operating loss of KRW 12.5 billion in Q3 2026.

The sharp swings in quarterly revenue reflect the equipment sector's pattern of recognizing sales when systems are delivered.

05

Industry analysis

Demand for secondary battery equipment has weakened as electric vehicle demand slowed and battery makers adjusted equipment investment. Factors cited include the EV slowdown, high interest rates and reduced subsidies.

On the other side, expanded local production in North America and Europe, rising demand for energy storage systems and the resumption of next-generation battery equipment investment are cited as grounds for a gradual recovery.

The company has been developing mixing systems for all-solid-state batteries, and in May 2022 it confirmed delivery of a mixer for all-solid-state battery use. Its technical understanding in powder and liquid mixing and its turn-key supply model are cited as supports for its position in the industry.

Mirae Asset Securities projected a rebound in 2026 earnings and praised the company's position in mixing equipment. However, Q1 2026 revenue declined from a year earlier, and although order signals improved after Q2, the Q3 operating loss means the pace of recovery is not yet clear.

Because overseas sales are a large share, exchange rates also affect profitability, and the October 2026 US contract was converted at KRW 1,358.50 per US dollar. Signs of demand recovery and contraction coexist, so the position of the industry cycle is hard to pin down.

06

Outlook

The confirmed order flow was concentrated in October 2026. Following a KRW 48.2 billion contract disclosed on October 7, a supply contract for a secondary battery mixing system for a US customer, worth US$9.9 million (about KRW 13.4 billion), was disclosed on October 8.

The latter runs from October 7, 2026 to January 27, 2028, with payment split 80% advance and 20% balance. The sale is self-produced, not outsourced. The counterparty's identity has been withheld until January 27, 2028 at the customer's request for trade secret reasons.

Because a large share of payment is an advance, the timing of cash receipts could affect cash flow. Revenue recognition may spread over the contract period, and the actual timing should be confirmed in quarterly results.

The solar power plant that began operating in April 2025 is a non-battery revenue source whose contribution remains to be seen. For the all-solid-state mixing system, the question is whether development leads to commercial orders. How the overseas subsidiaries' local customer support links to new orders also matters.

With an operating loss in Q3 2026, the speed at which order backlog converts into quarterly profit is the key variable for upcoming results.

Bull factors

Large supply contracts disclosed

Two supply contracts were disclosed in October 2026, KRW 48.2 billion on October 7 and about KRW 13.4 billion on October 8. The second equals 13.42% of 2025 revenue, and 80% of its payment is an advance. Order disclosures during a period of weak battery equipment demand are cited as a demand-side positive.

Return to profit in Q2 2026

Q2 2026 revenue was KRW 59.8 billion with operating profit of KRW 17.1 billion, and net profit attributable to controlling shareholders reached KRW 16.0 billion. Q1 2026 had already turned to a net profit of KRW 976 million, so the pattern shows a move out of the loss phase.

Improved financial structure

The debt-to-equity ratio moved from 101.5% in 2022 and 114.6% in 2023 down to 52.5% in 2024 and 50.9% in 2025. Total equity at year-end 2025 was KRW 177.2 billion against total liabilities of KRW 90.2 billion. Operating cash flow was positive in both 2024 and 2025, at KRW 24.1 billion and KRW 4.66 billion.

Bear factors

Sharp 2025 revenue drop and swing to losses

Revenue fell 63.1% to KRW 100.2 billion in 2025, with an operating loss of KRW 31.6 billion and a net loss attributable to controlling shareholders of KRW 19.2 billion. The company cited one-off Geumyang-related losses and shipment delays.

The bear case centers on how quickly a revenue base that declined for two years after the 2023 peak can recover.

Renewed Q3 losses and quarterly volatility

Q3 2026 revenue fell to KRW 16.8 billion from KRW 59.8 billion in Q2, and the quarter returned to an operating loss of KRW 12.5 billion and a net loss attributable to controlling shareholders of KRW 16.7 billion. Quarterly revenue swings are large, so one or two profitable quarters are not enough to establish a trend.

Industry slowdown and reliance on overseas sales

Export sales accounted for 89.23% of the secondary battery segment as of December 2025, leaving the company exposed to overseas demand and exchange rates.

The EV slowdown and battery makers' equipment investment adjustments have weighed on demand, and Q1 2026 revenue declined year on year, showing that the industry recovery could be delayed.

Risk factors

Order execution and revenue timing

The October 2026 contracts run until January 27, 2028, so revenue recognition may span several years. The counterparty is undisclosed until January 2028, which makes customer concentration hard to gauge from outside. Shipment delays led to revenue recognition delays in 2025, so schedule management matters.

Exchange rates and overseas concentration

A high overseas share means won-denominated results move with exchange rates. The October 2026 US contract was converted at KRW 1,358.50 per US dollar, and currency conditions in collections and overseas subsidiaries could affect profit and cash flow.

Restatement history and earnings volatility

Preliminary 2025 figures were corrected in March 2026 after audit adjustments. The 2025 audit opinion was unqualified, but the correction history keeps earnings reliability on the review list. Quarterly operating results have alternated between losses and profits, so earnings volatility is high.

Overall view

Yunsung F&C is a secondary battery mixing equipment maker whose revenue fell from KRW 312.9 billion in 2023 to KRW 100.2 billion in 2025, with an operating loss of KRW 31.6 billion that turned the year into a loss.

Q2 2026 returned to operating profit on revenue of KRW 59.8 billion, but Q3 2026 posted an operating loss of KRW 12.5 billion again. Two supply contracts disclosed in October 2026, worth KRW 48.2 billion and about KRW 13.4 billion, show order-side signs of recovery.

The net loss over the last four quarters narrowed to KRW 1.9 billion, but quarterly results remain volatile. The EV slowdown and equipment investment adjustments have not fully reversed.

The next checkpoints are the operating margin in the 2026 annual report and whether order backlog converts into profit in Q1 2027 results.
