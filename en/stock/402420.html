A Diagnostics Maker Shrunk by the Post-COVID Slump

Calth is a KONEX-listed in-vitro diagnostics company whose revenue has contracted sharply since the COVID-19 boom while operating losses persist, so the balance between technology development and financial stability is the central issue.

02

Business structure

Calth was founded in March 2018 and is headquartered in Seongnam, Gyeonggi Province. It was listed on the KONEX market on November 30, 2021. Kiwoom Securities was the designated adviser at listing.

Market data labels the sector as electrical equipment, but the business is point-of-care in-vitro diagnostics, so direct comparison with electrical equipment names such as LS Electric is of limited use.

According to the company's own description, it holds manufacturing approvals for 18 point-of-care reagents, including three for high-risk viral infectious diseases. From the second quarter of 2020, sales expanded around COVID-19 antibody, antigen, and self-test products.

Its core technology is ion concentration polarization (ICP)-based sample pretreatment, which underpins its saliva-based diagnostic system under development.

Other core technologies cited include a smartphone AI diagnostic analysis system, a cell-membrane pretreatment system, and exosome diagnostics, with early-detection kits for stroke and liver cancer in development.

No verified revenue breakdown by product line was found, so segment mix is described only qualitatively, and peers named in a startup database include Interroid, Care4U, and Zbiotech.

03

Recent trends

On reported figures, revenue was KRW 1,686.12 million in 2025, KRW 1,943.33 million in 2024, KRW 2,989.46 million in 2023, and KRW 12,546.51 million in 2022. Operating loss was KRW 4,858.31 million in 2023, KRW 4,800.87 million in 2024, and KRW 3,999.71 million in 2025.

The 2025 operating loss was narrower than in the prior two years, but the decline in revenue continued. Net loss for 2025 was KRW 4,533.35 million. At the end of 2025, on a standalone basis, total assets were about KRW 6,779 million, total liabilities KRW 6,518 million, and total equity KRW 260 million.

Paid-in capital at the same date was about KRW 2.1 billion, so total equity sits far below capital, which indicates partial capital impairment. Press reports said the company issued roughly KRW 2 billion of convertible bonds on July 25, 2025, and converted a KRW 300 million shareholder loan into equity.

At the annual general meeting on March 26, 2026, all agenda items, including approval of the eighth-term financial statements and an inside director appointment, were passed.

The share price closed at KRW 1,475 on March 26, and on October 27, 2026 it stood at KRW 1,480, unchanged from the previous session, with zero trading value.

04

Outlook

The near-term watch points are whether revenue recovers and whether the balance sheet improves. On policy, the Korea Disease Control and Prevention Agency expanded support for confirmatory testing of hepatitis C antibody-positive 56-year-olds (born 1970) to general and tertiary hospitals from 2026.

Among 537,726 56-year-olds screened in 2025, the antibody positivity rate was 0.8%. Press coverage reported that Calth views the inclusion of hepatitis C antibody testing in national screening as a business opportunity.

However, filings and press reports do not confirm that the screening program has translated into actual product supply or sales for the company.

The same study found that only 44.9% of antibody-positive individuals underwent confirmatory testing and only 18.0% of estimated confirmed cases started treatment, pointing to gaps in follow-up care.

In a March 2024 report, the company said it would pursue a transfer from KONEX to KOSDAQ, yet as of October 27, 2026 the stock remains on KONEX. On R&D, the company carried out national R&D projects in 2025 related to prostate cancer, urinary exosomes, and artificial intelligence.

The timing for converting these projects into commercial products and revenue has not been confirmed, so upcoming periodic filings should be tracked for sales trends and financing details.

05

Bull factors

Hepatitis C national screening backdrop

The Korea Disease Control and Prevention Agency added hepatitis C antibody testing for 56-year-olds to the national health screening program in 2025. In 2026, support for confirmatory test costs was extended to general hospitals and above.

Calth is described as holding anti-HCV diagnostic reagents, so a wider diagnostic demand base is a supportive backdrop for its business. Evidence that this translates into Calth's own sales has not yet been confirmed.

Proprietary technology pipeline

Calth has developed a saliva-based diagnostic system using ICP-based pretreatment technology. It adds a smartphone AI diagnostic analysis system and exosome diagnostics. In 2025 it carried out national R&D projects on prostate cancer, urinary exosomes, and artificial intelligence. If development leads to commercialization, it could help reduce the company's reliance on COVID-19 products.

Narrower operating loss and funding track record

Operating loss narrowed from KRW 4,800.87 million in 2024 to KRW 3,999.71 million in 2025. The company issued roughly KRW 2 billion of convertible bonds on July 25, 2025, with investors including a fund operated by the National Pension Service. Total equity remained positive at KRW 260 million at the end of 2025. Continued access to outside funding can be read as supportive of business continuity.

06

Bear factors

Persistent revenue decline

Revenue fell each year, from KRW 12,546.51 million in 2022 to KRW 2,989.46 million in 2023, KRW 1,943.33 million in 2024, and KRW 1,686.12 million in 2025. Press coverage attributed the first revenue contraction to declining demand for COVID-19 diagnostic kits.

There is no confirmed evidence yet that non-COVID products have grown enough to replace those sales. The small revenue base makes it hard to absorb fixed costs.

Fragile balance sheet

At the end of 2025, total equity of KRW 260 million compared with total liabilities of KRW 6,518 million. A third-party data aggregator put the debt ratio at 2,503.99% at that date. The 2025 operating loss was about KRW 4.0 billion and net loss about KRW 4,533 million. If losses continue, it is difficult to rule out equity turning negative.

Extremely thin liquidity and small scale

Market capitalization is KRW 3.5 billion and trading value on October 27, 2026 was zero. KONEX trading is limited to a narrow set of participants, so gaps between quotes and executions can be wide. The reference price at listing in 2021 was KRW 6,670.

The difference from the current KRW 1,480 reflects the combined effect of a weaker business environment and deteriorating finances.

07

Risk factors

Policy and market

Demand from hepatitis C screening depends on the eligible age cohort and the confirmatory-care pathway. In 2025, only 44.9% of antibody-positive individuals completed confirmatory testing and only 18.0% started treatment.

Antibody screening is also a contested market, and there is no assurance that national screening volume flows to Calth's products. A further risk is that no new revenue source emerges after COVID-19 diagnostic demand normalized.

Financial and funding

At the end of 2025, total equity of KRW 260 million was very small relative to total liabilities of KRW 6,518 million. Cash flow from operating activities was also negative for 2025.

The company relies on outside funding such as convertible bonds to cover operating losses, so delayed or unfavorable financing could add liquidity pressure. Conversion of convertible bonds into shares could dilute existing shareholders.

Listing and liquidity

In 2024 the company said it would pursue a move to KOSDAQ, keeping open options such as the technology special-listing route, a SPAC merger, and the KONEX fast track. As of October 27, 2026, however, the stock remains on KONEX and no disclosure confirming a transfer was found.

Capital impairment and continuing operating losses can weigh on listing-maintenance and transfer reviews. Liquidity is low enough that sessions with zero trading value occur, which limits the ability to sell shares.

08

Overall view

Calth is a diagnostics company whose revenue base has shrunk sharply since the COVID-19 boom and whose operating losses continue. Market data labels the sector as electrical equipment, but the real business is medical diagnostic devices, so electrical equipment sector metrics are a poor guide.

On reported 2025 figures, revenue was KRW 1,686.12 million, operating loss KRW 3,999.71 million, and net loss KRW 4,533.35 million, while total equity was a thin KRW 260 million.

On the positive side, the introduction of national hepatitis C screening, proprietary ICP and exosome technologies, and national R&D participation support the business backdrop and pipeline. Evidence that these have converted into actual sales has not yet been confirmed.

Liquidity conditions, with a market capitalization of KRW 3.5 billion and zero trading value, also need to be weighed. Key items to track in upcoming periodic filings are the revenue trend, the method of capital replenishment, and any progress on a market transfer.
