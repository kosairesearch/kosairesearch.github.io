Qurient: Loss-Making Biotech Tied to Trials and Funding

Qurient has posted operating losses for four years in a row, released Phase 1 data for mocaciclib, and is heading into a rights offering of about KRW 105.8 billion, with payment scheduled for December 2026.

  1. 1

    On reported figures, 2025 revenue of KRW 6.98 billion was the smallest of 2022-2025, and the operating loss of KRW 29.8 billion extended a four-year run of losses since 2022.

  2. 2

    The third-quarter 2026 operating loss narrowed to KRW 6.97 billion from KRW 12.8 billion in the prior quarter, but the net loss attributable to owners was KRW 6.71 billion, wider than KRW 6.37 billion a year earlier.

  3. 3

    Mocaciclib Phase 1 data shown at ESMO in October 2026 reported 2 partial responses and 17 cases of stable disease among 55 evaluable patients.

  4. 4

    The rights offering runs on a fixed timetable: existing-shareholder subscription on November 25-26, 2026, payment on December 3, new-share listing on December 17, with the final issue price set on November 20.

  5. 5

    The debt ratio rose to 58.2% at the end of 2025 from 10.2% a year earlier, and first-half 2026 losses reached a high share of equity, making capital replenishment a central issue.

02

Business structure

Qurient is a drug developer rather than a revenue-driven pharma company: it advances candidates through the clinic and aims to earn money from licensing and co-development deals.

One industry review notes that because the model monetizes efficacy once it is shown in patients, valuing the company on product-sales growth alone is difficult. A segment revenue breakdown could not be confirmed from searches, so revenue is described only qualitatively.

The core oncology assets are mocaciclib (Q901), a CDK7 inhibitor, and adrixetinib (Q702), an Axl/Mer/CSF1R triple inhibitor. Q702 is an oral small molecule that targets the same CSF1R as the intravenous antibody Niktimvo while also inhibiting AXL and MER.

In AML, a triple-combination trial adding Q702 to azacitidine and venetoclax is under way with MD Anderson Cancer Center in the United States. In infectious disease, telacebec was out-licensed to TB Alliance, and TB Alliance holds the full development rights.

Because the counterparty is a non-profit, Qurient received no upfront payment, but it retained the right to a priority review voucher (PRV) and is watching for it.

The next growth leg is QP101, a dual-payload ADC in preclinical development, along with a proteasome-inhibitor payload at its German subsidiary QLi5 Therapeutics. Dongkoo Bio & Pharma is the largest shareholder.

04

Earnings analysis

Annual revenue rose from KRW 8.47 billion in 2022 to KRW 9.03 billion in 2023 (up 6.7%) and KRW 9.18 billion in 2024 (up 1.6%), then fell 23.9% to KRW 6.98 billion in 2025. Within 2022-2025, 2024 was the largest year and 2025 the smallest.

The operating loss was KRW 27.5 billion in 2022, KRW 23.2 billion in 2023, KRW 27.5 billion in 2024 and KRW 29.8 billion in 2025, a four-year run of losses, and the 2025 operating margin was -426.3%.

The net loss attributable to owners widened to KRW 30.1 billion in 2025 from KRW 24.1 billion in 2024, while the net loss including non-controlling interests was KRW 31.3 billion. Operating cash flow was -KRW 28.7 billion in 2025, a cash outflow of similar size to that year's operating loss.

By quarter, revenue peaked at KRW 1.94 billion in the first quarter of 2026, then fell to KRW 1.91 billion in the second and KRW 1.75 billion in the third, two straight quarterly declines, although third-quarter revenue was up 3.7% from a year earlier.

The operating loss was KRW 9.26 billion in the fourth quarter of 2025, KRW 7.67 billion in the first quarter of 2026, KRW 12.8 billion in the second and KRW 6.97 billion in the third, five consecutive quarters of losses with large swings.

Medipharm Health News noted that the wider operating loss in the second quarter of 2026 reflects a cost structure in which R&D spending grows as trials advance.

The third-quarter net loss attributable to owners was KRW 6.71 billion, down from KRW 12.7 billion in the prior quarter but above the KRW 6.37 billion of a year earlier, and the sum for the last four quarters (fourth quarter of 2025 through third quarter of 2026) was KRW 38.7 billion, larger than the KRW 30.1 billion for full-year 2025.

05

Industry analysis

Drug-discovery biotechs are valued on clinical data and licensing outcomes rather than revenue. Unlike large revenue-generating names such as Samsung Biologics and Celltrion, Qurient is judged mainly on the clinical results of its pipeline.

Dual-payload ADCs, where Qurient is increasing its investment, are a field in which competition is intensifying. In an October 2025 report, Samsung Securities said CDK7 inhibition could offer a new approach to overcoming resistance to Topo I inhibitors.

In chronic graft-versus-host disease, the indication targeted by Q702, an analyst argued that FDA approval of Niktimvo, the CSF1R antibody co-developed by Incyte and Syndax, strengthened the rationale for Q702. That shows the target has been validated by another drug, but it does not guarantee success for Q702 itself.

In infectious disease, TB Alliance runs development of telacebec, so Qurient's direct cost burden there is lighter than in oncology. Across the sector, funding needs grow as trials advance, so access to capital and the ability to close partnerships separate the positions of small biotechs.

06

Outlook

The nearest item to verify is the rights offering. In a corrected filing, the first provisional issue price changed from KRW 14,310 to KRW 14,900, and the expected proceeds were adjusted from about KRW 101.6 billion to about KRW 105.8 billion.

The company plans to put KRW 66.7 billion of this into R&D on key pipelines, with KRW 27.3 billion allocated to QP101 and KRW 21.9 billion to Q702. It plans to file an IND for QP101 with the US FDA and aims to move Q702 into a registrational trial.

Mocaciclib completed its Phase 1 dose-escalation study in January 2026 and fixed its Phase 2 recommended dose. The company is pursuing several combination trials, starting with dosing in a Phase 2 study of mocaciclib plus fulvestrant in HR+/HER2- breast cancer.

For telacebec, an expanded trial enrolling 80 additional patients with the same design is under way after the initial 40-patient cohort, and its results bear on the filing timeline and the timing of any PRV. The end date of the expanded trial and the filing date could not be confirmed from searches.

08

Bull factors

Mocaciclib Phase 1 shows safety and early efficacy signals

Among 55 evaluable patients as of September 30, 2026, 2 had partial responses and 17 had stable disease. Neutropenia, a main side effect of CDK inhibitors, was not reported.

A single-agent response was seen in a patient with CDK4/6-inhibitor-resistant breast cancer, the Phase 2 target population, but that was only one patient, which limits interpretation.

Potential PRV from telacebec

According to the company, all 40 patients in the initial Australian Buruli ulcer cohort were tallied as cured. TB Alliance plans to file for conditional approval after the Phase 2b trial, which has drawn attention to the PRV that Qurient would receive. The contract carried no upfront payment, but an approval could create separate value.

Large fundraising supports continuity of development

Sisa Journal-e reported that the offering pre-secures three years of operating funds along with R&D money. Once payment is made, pressure from a debt ratio that reached 58.2% at the end of 2025 and from reduced equity could ease.

By choosing a rights offering over convertible bonds, the company says it aimed to avoid the potential overhang from mezzanine issuance such as convertible bonds.

Bear factors

Widening losses and continuing cash burn

The 2025 operating loss of KRW 29.8 billion and operating cash flow of -KRW 28.7 billion were both worse than in 2024. The last four quarters' net loss attributable to owners of KRW 38.7 billion exceeds the KRW 30.1 billion for full-year 2025.

Quarterly operating losses swung from KRW 12.8 billion in the second quarter of 2026 to KRW 6.97 billion in the third, which makes the annual cost run-rate hard to gauge.

Dilution and uncertainty over proceeds

The 7.1 million new shares equal about 18.7% of the existing common shares. The figure is based on the provisional issue price, so final proceeds can change with the share price, and a lower issue price would also reduce the equity-building effect. A 25% discount rate is applied in setting the issue price.

Expectations concentrated on trial results, not revenue

Revenue of KRW 6.98 billion in 2025 was the smallest of 2022-2025, and third-quarter 2026 revenue was only KRW 1.75 billion. With valuation resting on trial results and licensing, there is little commercial cushion if results fall short of expectations.

A report said that after uncertainty over pipeline development spread, differences arose with some investors on terms and financing costs. This points to friction in the second-half 2026 funding process.

10

Risk factors

Financial and listing status

Consolidated first-half 2026 pre-tax loss from continuing operations was KRW 21.079 billion, equal to 96.7% of equity of KRW 21.803 billion. One report interpreted this as meaning that failing to raise equity within the year would leave a risk of designation as an issue under administrative management. Whether the offering is paid in as planned determines whether that pressure eases.

Clinical and development risk

The mocaciclib Phase 1 data are early and based on a small number of patients, and only one breast cancer patient from the Phase 2 target group was enrolled. Efficacy data for Q702 in AML combination and in chronic graft-versus-host disease are not yet established.

For telacebec, the approval timeline and the timing of any PRV depend on expanded-trial results and regulatory decisions.

Licensing and monetization risk

The company's route to revenue depends on licensing and co-development, and one review warned that continuing long-term in-house development without external cash inflows could again raise the financial burden.

The telacebec deal carried no upfront payment, so its cash contribution depends on later events such as the PRV. The timing and size of deals rest on partners' decisions and are hard for the company to control.

12

Overall view

Qurient has posted operating losses for four years in a row since 2022, and its 2025 revenue of KRW 6.98 billion was the smallest of 2022-2025. The third-quarter 2026 operating loss narrowed to KRW 6.97 billion, but the net loss attributable to owners was larger than a year earlier, so the improvement is limited.

The company's value centers on clinical results and licensing for its pipeline, including mocaciclib, adrixetinib, QP101 and telacebec. Strengths are the safety and early efficacy signals from the mocaciclib Phase 1 data shown at ESMO in October 2026 and the PRV right tied to telacebec.

Weaknesses are continuing losses and cash outflow, a high loss ratio against equity, and dilution from the rights offering. The next items to check are the timetable from the final issue price on November 20, 2026 to the new-share listing on December 17, and the 2027 development milestones.
