Report overview

Holding Company Rebounds on Consolidation, Profit Still Volatile

Hansol Holdings grew revenue and operating profit through consolidation and equity-method gains, but posted a net loss attributable to controlling shareholders in the third quarter of 2026, so the durability of its earnings remains an open question.

  1. 1

    Operating profit for 2025 was KRW 22.6 billion, up 202.3% from the prior year. The company attributed the rise to newly consolidated subsidiaries and higher equity-method gains from its main affiliates.

  2. 2

    Third-quarter 2026 revenue reached KRW 633 billion, up 128.5% year on year. The company posted a net loss attributable to controlling shareholders of KRW 1.24 billion, a swing into deficit, while operating profit of KRW 19.6 billion was up 166.7% year on year.

  3. 3

    Net income attributable to controlling shareholders reached KRW 51.3 billion in the second quarter of 2026, the largest of the last five quarters. Operating profit for the same quarter was KRW 19.9 billion.

  4. 4

    Subsidiary Hansol Paper reported a consolidated operating loss of KRW 6.4 billion for the third quarter of 2026 in a provisional release dated October 30, 2026. Its operating profit in the year-earlier quarter was KRW 2.55 billion.

  5. 5

    Reports in September 2026 said Hansol Technics also improved its profitability in the second quarter. The subsidiary now adds to consolidated revenue and to the range of businesses the holding company covers.

02

Business structure

Hansol Holdings is a pure holding company that does not run a business of its own and controls its group companies through its shareholdings in subsidiaries. Its main income comes from trademark royalties for use of the Hansol brand, management consulting fees, and dividends from subsidiaries.

Based on segment revenue in the 2024 annual report (sum of segments), Hansol Logistics, which runs integrated logistics, accounted for 42.7%, the paper-distribution and IT services subsidiary for 38.6%, the corrugated-board subsidiary for 15.0%, and the holding company itself for 3.7%.

In 2024 the corrugated-board subsidiary recorded a segment operating loss. The key affiliates are Hansol Paper, which makes printing paper, containerboard and specialty paper, and Hansol Technics, which makes power modules, mobile phone parts, LED materials and solar power equipment.

In the first half of 2026 the company acquired more shares in Hansol Technics and consolidated it, raising the number of consolidated subsidiaries to 31 and adding power module, automotive electronics and EMS businesses.

Hansol Logistics has expanded volume in the United States and Europe on the strength of its secondary-battery logistics capability.

Reports from the first half of 2026 said the holding company would put up to KRW 61.7 billion into a KRW 90 billion rights offering at Hansol Technics during an acquisition process, lifting its stake from about 20% to about 38%. Customer mix differs by subsidiary, so it is hard to group them as a single customer base at the holding level.

04

Earnings analysis

On an annual basis, revenue went from KRW 467.2 billion in 2022 to KRW 443.1 billion in 2023, a decline of 5.2%, then rose to KRW 791.6 billion in 2024 (up 78.6%) and KRW 1.1 trillion in 2025 (up 38.7%).

After the 2023 low, revenue rose for two consecutive years from 2024 through 2025, and 2025 was the largest year of the 2022 to 2025 period.

Operating profit fell from KRW 19.1 billion in 2022 to KRW 4 billion in 2023 (down 79.1%), then recovered to KRW 7.48 billion in 2024 (up 87.1%) and KRW 22.6 billion in 2025 (up 202.3%).

Operating margin moved from 4.1% in 2022 to 0.9% in 2023 and 0.9% in 2024, before reaching 2.1% in 2025, so margins stayed thin even as revenue grew. The company linked the 2025 improvement to newly consolidated subsidiaries and higher equity-method gains.

Net income attributable to controlling shareholders fell from KRW 48.1 billion in 2022 to KRW 7.43 billion in 2023 (down 84.5%) and KRW 2.02 billion in 2024 (down 72.8%), then turned into a net loss of KRW 607 million in 2025.

Net income including non-controlling interests was KRW 8.9 billion in 2025, which means a large share of profit belonged to non-controlling interests.

The debt ratio was 21.1% in 2022 and 21.2% in 2023, rose to 38.6% in 2024 and was 37.8% in 2025, when total equity was KRW 645.2 billion, KRW 556.9 billion of it attributable to controlling shareholders, against total liabilities of KRW 243.8 billion.

Operating cash flow was KRW 43.4 billion in 2022, KRW 21.6 billion in 2023, KRW 34.9 billion in 2024 and KRW 53.1 billion in 2025.

Quarterly revenue fell 3.9% to KRW 266.1 billion in the fourth quarter of 2025 and then rose for three consecutive quarters, reaching KRW 269.6 billion, KRW 416.7 billion and KRW 633 billion in the first three quarters of 2026, the last up 128.5% year on year.

Operating profit moved from a loss of KRW 449 million in the fourth quarter of 2025 to KRW 7.94 billion, KRW 19.9 billion and KRW 19.6 billion in the first three quarters of 2026.

Net income attributable to controlling shareholders was KRW 2.57 billion in the third quarter of 2025, a net loss of KRW 19.5 billion in the fourth quarter, KRW 9.48 billion in the first quarter of 2026, KRW 51.3 billion in the second and a net loss of KRW 1.24 billion in the third, and the last four quarters total KRW 40 billion.

In the third quarter of 2026 operating profit was positive while attributable net income was negative, and the items behind that gap, such as non-operating and equity-method results, are to be traced in the quarterly report.

05

Industry analysis

Hansol Holdings earnings depend on the business cycles of its subsidiaries.

In paper, Hansol Paper was reported to have posted second-quarter 2026 operating profit of KRW 50.8 billion, up about 163% year on year, driven by strong demand for high-margin industrial and thermal paper, but it swung to a consolidated operating loss of KRW 6.4 billion in the third quarter.

Whether demand or cost factors drove that third-quarter swing is a point to trace in the affiliate's periodic filings.

In logistics, Hansol Logistics is adding volume in the United States and Europe on the strength of its secondary-battery logistics capability, and that volume follows customers' battery production and capital spending.

In electronic components, Hansol Technics was reported to have improved profitability in the second quarter of 2026. Among holding companies, such as SK Square and Hanjinkal, the way subsidiary results are reflected and the ownership structure are the central variables.

One investment bank official said the group would keep pursuing M&A to shift toward semiconductors and that profit gains from acquisitions may not appear right away. Analysts have also noted that Hansol IONES, acquired in 2022, has not yet shown clear synergies.

06

Outlook

The business direction the company has set out comes down to three points: consolidating subsidiaries, expanding secondary-battery logistics, and increasing investment related to semiconductors.

Reports from the first half of 2026 said the stake in Hansol Technics would rise to about 38% after the company joins the rights offering. In a 2025 report, a credit rating agency flagged a possible increase in new capital spending and funding needs at Hansol Technics as an item to monitor.

The same assessment cited the variety of the holding company's cash-flow sources, including dividend income, trademark royalties and management fee income, as a positive.

Because Hansol Technics has been consolidated since the first half of 2026, quarterly revenue now reflects a different scale, and whether that scale holds in later quarters will shape how the earnings trend reads.

Volume growth in the United States and Europe for Hansol Logistics was cited as a driver of the first-half 2026 improvement. Paper demand was strong in the second quarter, so whether the third-quarter loss was temporary or structural is a point for the affiliate's business report.

The company has not released a set of full-year revenue or profit targets in material that can be cited here. The next periodic report, the 2026 annual business report, will fix segment revenue and operating results for the full year, and that table is the starting point for the outlook.

Bull factors

Larger consolidated scale

Consolidating Hansol Technics in the first half of 2026 added power module, automotive electronics and EMS businesses, and the company cited that consolidation together with secondary-battery logistics growth as drivers of the first-half improvement.

The larger revenue base came with operating profit of KRW 7.94 billion, KRW 19.9 billion and KRW 19.6 billion across the first three quarters of 2026.

Recovery in operating profit

Operating profit for 2025 was KRW 22.6 billion, up 202.3% from the prior year. After the 2023 low, operating profit rose in 2024 and 2025. Second-quarter 2026 operating profit was KRW 19.9 billion, up 150.1% from the prior quarter. Operating margin improved from 0.9% in 2023 and 0.9% in 2024 to 2.1% in 2025.

Financial capacity and credit standing

Total equity at the end of 2025 was KRW 645.2 billion, and total liabilities were KRW 243.8 billion. As of the end of June 2025, the double-leverage ratio was 81.6%, which the rating agency described as low, and the company was in a net cash position. The agency assigned an A2- rating, citing its diversified subsidiary structure and low financial burden.

Bear factors

Volatility in profit attributable to controlling shareholders

Net income attributable to controlling shareholders fell from KRW 48.1 billion in 2022 to KRW 7.43 billion in 2023 and KRW 2.02 billion in 2024, then turned into a net loss of KRW 607 million in 2025.

After a KRW 19.5 billion net loss in the fourth quarter of 2025, the company posted KRW 51.3 billion in the second quarter of 2026, then a net loss of KRW 1.24 billion in the third quarter. Swings of this size from quarter to quarter make it hard to read a single direction for the trend.

Thin operating margin

Operating margin was 0.9% in 2023, 0.9% in 2024 and 2.1% in 2025, which is low for the revenue base. Revenue reached KRW 1.1 trillion in 2025, but the margin remained in the low single digits. The fact that revenue growth has not translated directly into margin improvement is a bearish point.

Dependence on affiliate industry cycles

Hansol Paper swung to a consolidated operating loss of KRW 6.4 billion in the third quarter of 2026, from operating profit of KRW 2.55 billion in the year-earlier quarter. In the 2024 annual report, the corrugated-board subsidiary recorded a segment operating loss.

Holding-company results that rely on the industry cycles and equity-method results of affiliates form the basis of the bearish case.

Risk factors

Financial and investment burden

Taking part in the Hansol Technics rights offering and raising the stake could add to the holding company's funding needs. The debt ratio rose from 21.2% in 2023 to 38.6% in 2024.

A credit rating agency flagged possible growth in capital spending and funding needs at Hansol Technics as an item to monitor, according to a 2025 report.

Gap between consolidated and attributable profit

Net income including non-controlling interests was KRW 8.9 billion in 2025, while profit attributable to controlling shareholders was a net loss of KRW 607 million.

A larger share of profit going to non-controlling interests reduces what belongs to controlling shareholders, so the reading depends on which profit measure is used. The gap between third-quarter 2026 operating profit of KRW 19.6 billion and the attributable net loss of KRW 1.24 billion is a similar item to trace.

Reliance on specific industry volumes

Growth at Hansol Logistics relies on secondary-battery logistics volumes. If secondary-battery demand slows or customers adjust capacity plans, both volumes and profit could be affected. The paper business is sensitive to swings in demand for industrial and thermal paper.

12

Overall view

Hansol Holdings is a pure holding company whose consolidated revenue and profit change with the results and ownership structure of subsidiaries such as Hansol Paper, Hansol Technics and Hansol Logistics.

In the disclosed results, revenue rose for two consecutive years from 2024 through 2025 after the 2023 low, and operating profit recovered to KRW 22.6 billion in 2025.

In 2026, the consolidation of Hansol Technics added to quarterly revenue, which has risen for three consecutive quarters since the fourth quarter of 2025, and attributable net income reached KRW 51.3 billion in the second quarter.

Operating margin remained at 2.1% in 2025, so it did not rise as fast as revenue, and the third quarter of 2026 swung to an attributable net loss of KRW 1.24 billion.

Strengths include the wider business scope from consolidation, expansion in secondary-battery logistics and electronic components, and financial stability reflected in a net cash position and an A2- rating.

Weaknesses include quarterly volatility in profit, a thin operating margin, dependence on affiliate industry cycles such as paper, and the funding burden of stake purchases.

Both sides carry comparable weight, and the attributable net income and operating margin in the next periodic report are the most important points to confirm.
