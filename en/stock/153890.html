A Motion-Control Newcomer Below Its IPO Price

Justek is shifting its mix toward semiconductors, but its first-half results sit far from the full-year estimates given at listing, and the shares trade below the IPO price.

02

Business structure

Justek is a precision motion-control specialist founded in 1999. It started under a KIST venture-startup support program and was the first in Korea to develop and commercialize a linear motor. Its vertically integrated structure covers motors, sensors, drivers and controllers.

The business is split into semiconductor, display and industrial-automation motion systems, plus a space segment.

Order backlog at the end of the first quarter of 2026 was KRW 16.265 billion: KRW 2.681 billion in semiconductors, KRW 11.879 billion in displays, KRW 1.405 billion in industrial automation and KRW 0.3 billion in space. By backlog, displays are therefore still the largest segment. By revenue, however, semiconductor motion systems have been reported as the largest revenue source.

In semiconductors, Justek is the exclusive motion-system supplier for a domestic laser maker's dicing and grooving tool and for another maker's laser annealing tool. The growing use of lasers in place of blades in wafer dicing is viewed as an opportunity for the company.

Among overseas competitors, MKS Inc., a U.S. supplier of precision motion solutions, is named as a peer. The company says it can cut lead times by three to four months and price more than 30% below leading overseas suppliers. That is a company claim and has not been independently verified.

Sales to Japan were about KRW 3.9 billion in 2025, roughly 18% of the total. In space, it has supplied attitude-control motors for a launched satellite and is eyeing the low-earth-orbit inter-satellite laser communication market.

03

Recent trends

On a DART-disclosed basis, first-half 2026 (cumulative) revenue was KRW 5.04 billion, down 54.5% year on year. Operating profit was KRW 1.1 billion, down 40.0%, and net income was KRW 1.16 billion, down 32.4%. Revenue fell by more than half, yet the company stayed profitable at both the operating and net levels. Profit declined by less than revenue did, which is a notable feature of the margin picture.

In 2025 the company reported revenue of KRW 21.343 billion, an operating loss of KRW 0.992 billion and a net loss of KRW 1.62 billion. A payment dispute with a customer was cited as a factor behind the operating loss.

During the listing process, the company presented 2026 estimates of KRW 33.4 billion in revenue and KRW 4.3 billion in operating profit. The first-half cumulative results are far from those estimates, so second-half orders and the pace of revenue recognition are the points to watch.

The share price has moved sharply since listing. The IPO price was set at KRW 12,500, the top of the range, after demand forecasting. The first-day opening price was KRW 35,350, 182.8% above the IPO price, and the 52-week high is KRW 42,500. The close on August 28 was KRW 11,750.

The close on September 29 was KRW 10,030. The close on October 27 was KRW 6,610 (-2.51% from the previous day), and trading value that day was KRW 0.4 billion, a thin level of turnover.

04

Outlook

The company's outlook centers on semiconductors. The registration statement projects semiconductor revenue of KRW 8.9 billion in 2025, KRW 12.8 billion in 2026, KRW 24.6 billion in 2027 and KRW 37.4 billion in 2028. The semiconductor segment's compound annual growth rate through 2028 was projected at about 61%.

All of these are estimates the company gave during listing, not confirmed results. Total operating profit estimates are KRW 9.4 billion for 2027 and KRW 15.7 billion for 2028. Given the first-half cumulative results, meeting these estimates depends heavily on converting second-half orders.

Semiconductor and display orders take an average of under three months from order to delivery, and most orders taken in a year are recognized as revenue that year. Under this structure, the order flow from the third quarter onward feeds into results relatively quickly.

Of roughly KRW 19.3 billion in net IPO proceeds, KRW 9.947 billion goes to a new plant and clean room for semiconductor motion systems and space equipment. Once the facility is running, production capacity will rise, but fixed costs will rise with it.

Expectations of expanded IT OLED investment are cited as an order opportunity for display equipment makers. The timing and scale of that investment, however, rest with panel makers. The third-quarter earnings disclosure is the next checkpoint for the post-half-year trend.

05

Bull factors

Exposure to back-end semiconductor demand

Justek is the exclusive motion-system supplier for a domestic laser maker's dicing, grooving and annealing tools. The spread of lasers in wafer dicing is a favorable factor for the company. It has also been explained that precision transfer technology gains importance as HBM stacking becomes more complex. With semiconductors now the largest revenue source, this exposure has begun to show up in results.

Profit fell less than revenue

First-half (cumulative) revenue fell 54.5%, but operating profit fell 40.0% and net income 32.4%. The company kept its profit, with operating profit of KRW 1.1 billion and net income of KRW 1.16 billion. Compared with the operating loss in 2025, this suggests a change in the earnings structure. It should still be read as profit earned during a revenue decline.

Fresh capital and limited float overhang

The offering was entirely new shares, with 1.6 million shares issued. Net proceeds of about KRW 19.3 billion go to facilities and working capital. CEO Kim Yong-il and his spouse committed to holding all of their shares for at least two years. With the largest shareholder's stake locked up for a long period, overhang pressure from that block is limited.

06

Bear factors

Gap to estimates given at listing

The 2026 estimates presented during listing are revenue of KRW 33.4 billion and operating profit of KRW 4.3 billion. First-half (cumulative) revenue is KRW 5.04 billion and operating profit is KRW 1.1 billion. Meeting the full-year estimates would require second-half results to far exceed the first half. A shortfall could weigh on confidence in the broader growth outlook.

High customer concentration

In 2025 the largest customer accounted for 25.5% of sales and the top five customers for about 69%. A change in a key customer's investment schedule or shipment volume can swing revenue sharply. A payment dispute with a customer was cited as a factor behind the 2025 operating loss. Timing shifts in customer orders may also be part of the background to the sharp drop in first-half revenue.

Cost burden and space-segment uncertainty

KB Securities assessed that, with space-sector order volatility, higher labor costs from expanding R&D staff could weigh on profitability. Once the new plant and clean room are running, fixed costs such as depreciation will also rise.

Space-segment backlog was still small at KRW 0.3 billion at the end of the first quarter of 2026. If new-business investment takes longer to turn into revenue, margins could be affected.

07

Risk factors

Macro

Components for semiconductor and display equipment depend directly on customers' capital-spending schedules. Delays in the memory cycle or in panel makers' investment decisions could push orders back. Expanded IT OLED investment is still described at the expectation stage. Shifts in sentiment toward small-cap KOSDAQ technology stocks are also a source of share price volatility.

Business

Revenue is recognized on average within three months of an order, so quarterly results can swing widely. With the top five customers at about 69% of sales, order changes from a few clients show up strongly in results. Overseas, the company competes with large players such as MKS Inc. If utilization is low after the new capacity comes online, the fixed-cost burden could grow.

Market and liquidity

Major financial investors such as Winvest Venture Partners had a one-month lock-up. Winvest Venture Partners reported selling 121,482 shares on the market on the listing day. Even one year after listing, only about 48.7% of shares were expected to be freely tradable.

As lock-ups expire in stages, the pool of shares available for sale could grow. With trading value at just KRW 0.4 billion on October 27, even small trades can move the price sharply.

08

Overall view

Justek is a precision motion-control company spanning semiconductor back-end, display and space, and it is at an early stage of a shift toward a semiconductor-weighted business.

DART first-half (cumulative) results show revenue of KRW 5.04 billion, operating profit of KRW 1.1 billion and net income of KRW 1.16 billion, all lower than a year earlier. Even so, the company remained profitable at both the operating and net levels.

The gap to the 2026 estimates given at listing, revenue of KRW 33.4 billion and operating profit of KRW 4.3 billion, is large, so second-half orders and revenue recognition are the key items to verify.

Customer concentration, the history of a payment dispute and higher fixed costs from new capacity are bearish factors, while the exclusive supply positions and growing back-end semiconductor demand are bullish ones.

The share price has moved from a first-day opening price of KRW 35,350 to KRW 6,610, below the KRW 12,500 IPO price. This report is for information only, and investment decisions and their consequences rest with the reader.
