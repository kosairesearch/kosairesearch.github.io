02

Business structure

Seoyon Topmetal is a mid-sized automotive mold and parts maker founded in 1987 and based in Namdong-gu, Incheon.

According to JobKorea company data as of March 2026, its sales mix is 56% injection molds, 27% CABIN products, 16% press molds and 1% other, so injection molds and CABIN products together account for more than 80%.

Wisereport's domestic and export breakdown as of December 2025 shows injection molds at 99.15% domestic, while press molds have a relatively higher export share of 29.6%.

The company entered Mexico in 2013 to produce auto parts, later decided to return to Korea, and was designated a reshoring company by the Ministry of Trade, Industry and Energy in June 2022.

In September 2024 it sold its stake in the Chinese joint venture Huahua Seoyon Topmetal Automotive Parts Co., simplifying its overseas holdings. Its demand base is the automaker and auto parts value chain, with molds ordered early in new model development and parts supplied during mass production.

It competes between large mold and parts makers and smaller mold shops, with delivery responsiveness and mold-making technology central to winning orders. Its revenue scale is small compared with large parts makers. The largest shareholder is Seoyon, which held 55.62% as of October 19, 2026.

04

Earnings analysis

Annual results show revenue growth and margin improvement moving together. Revenue went from KRW 164.2 billion in 2022 to KRW 173.1 billion in 2023 (up 5.4% year on year), KRW 182.4 billion in 2024 (up 5.4%), and KRW 207.1 billion in 2025 (up 13.5%), rising for three consecutive years from 2023 through 2025.

Operating profit went from KRW 2.81 billion in 2022 to KRW 6.95 billion in 2023 (up 147.6%), KRW 9.26 billion in 2024 (up 33.2%) and KRW 11.9 billion in 2025 (up 28.7%), also increasing for three consecutive years. The operating margin rose from 1.7% in 2022 to 4.0% in 2023, 5.1% in 2024 and 5.8% in 2025.

Net income attributable to controlling shareholders jumped to KRW 8.47 billion in 2023 (up 203.3%), peaked at KRW 9.38 billion in 2024 (up 10.7%), and edged down to KRW 9.3 billion in 2025 (down 0.9%).

Net income including non-controlling interests was KRW 9.3 billion in 2025, roughly flat against KRW 9.31 billion in 2024, so net profit stalled even as operating profit rose 28.7%.

Wisereport's financial table indicates that income tax expense rose sharply in 2025 versus 2024, which appears to have limited the translation of operating gains into net income.

Operating cash flow was KRW 11.7 billion in 2022, KRW 6.32 billion in 2023, KRW 19.7 billion in 2024 and KRW 14 billion in 2025, with notable swings. The debt ratio fell from 72.0% in 2022 to 67.3% in 2023 and 62.4% in 2024 before jumping to 81.4% in 2025, when total liabilities stood at KRW 82 billion.

Quarterly, operating profit was KRW 3.71 billion in the third quarter of 2025 on revenue of KRW 49.5 billion, then in the fourth quarter revenue rose 8.6% quarter on quarter to KRW 53.7 billion while operating profit fell 97.7% to KRW 86 million.

Operating profit recovered to KRW 3.29 billion in the first quarter of 2026 (up 3,730.1% quarter on quarter) and KRW 5.09 billion in the second quarter (up 54.4%).

Third-quarter 2026 revenue reached KRW 61.1 billion (up 11.7% quarter on quarter and 23.4% year on year), the highest of the last five quarters, but operating profit fell to KRW 2.73 billion (down 46.4% quarter on quarter and 26.6% year on year).

Net income attributable to controlling shareholders over the last four quarters (fourth quarter 2025 to third quarter 2026) totaled KRW 9.09 billion. Whether cost or product mix changes are holding back margins in the third quarter will need to be separated out in the following quarters' results.

05

Industry analysis

Seoyon Topmetal makes automotive molds and parts, so demand depends heavily on automaker production plans and new model development cycles. Molds are ordered early in model development and parts are supplied during mass production, so model change cycles feed into revenue with a lag.

Domestic vehicle output, export volumes and exchange rates are the main variables for the sector. As the shift to electric vehicles reshapes part composition, demand may move differently among suppliers between combustion-focused parts and new parts.

The company is a mid-sized supplier that is small relative to large parts makers, combining mold making with press parts. Competition plays out between large parts makers and smaller mold shops, where delivery speed and quality responsiveness are key to winning orders.

Roughly 30% of press mold sales were exports as of December 2025, so overseas plant utilization, tariffs and exchange rates directly affect that segment.

Having entered Mexico in 2013 and later been designated a reshoring company in 2022, the company's domestic production base will shape its cost and delivery outcomes as the sector evolves.

Risk factors

With a high export share in press molds, tariff and exchange rate changes could affect profitability. Quarterly cost trends need to be read alongside these swings.
