02

Business structure

Founded in 1987 and listed on KOSDAQ in 2011, Seoyon Topmetal specializes in molds and heavy equipment parts, producing automotive press and injection molds alongside excavator cabins and exterior cover parts for construction equipment.

According to disclosed sales mix data as of March 2026, injection molds account for roughly 57% of sales, CABIN-type products about 25%, press molds about 17%, and other items about 1%.

Press molds are used for mass production of auto bodies while injection molds serve interior parts production, with both mold lines supplied mainly to Seoyon E-Hwa, a tier-1 supplier to Hyundai and Kia.

The company also supplies door trim interior parts to Seoyon E-Hwa's Chinese subsidiary, a related party, providing a relatively stable revenue base.

In the construction equipment segment, the company develops and mass-produces excavator cabins for HD Hyundai Infracore (formerly Doosan Infracore) excavators and loaders, as well as for Volvo Construction Equipment excavators.

The company was selected as a firm returning to Korea after withdrawing its Mexican subsidiary, investing in the Incheon Namdong National Industrial Complex and building an automated painting line for excavator cabins.

Its controlling shareholder is parent company Seoyon, and the Seoyon Group's affiliates include Seoyon E-Hwa, Seoyon CNF, Seoyon Intech, and Seoyon Autovision alongside Seoyon Topmetal. Within Korea's mold and die manufacturing industry, the company is classified as a mid-sized firm ranked among the sector leaders.

04

Earnings analysis

Revenue rose from KRW 164.2 billion in 2022 to KRW 173.1 billion in 2023 (up 5.4%), KRW 182.4 billion in 2024 (up 5.4%), and KRW 207.1 billion in 2025 (up 13.5%), marking three consecutive years of growth from 2023 through 2025 with 2025 as the largest year in the period.

Operating profit also grew for three straight years, from KRW 2.81 billion in 2022 to KRW 6.95 billion in 2023 (up 147.6%), KRW 9.26 billion in 2024 (up 33.2%), and KRW 11.9 billion in 2025 (up 28.7%), with the operating margin steadily improving from 1.7% to 4.0% to 5.1% to 5.8%.

Net profit attributable to owners, however, climbed from KRW 2.79 billion in 2022 to KRW 8.47 billion in 2023 (up 203.3%) and KRW 9.38 billion in 2024 (up 10.7%) before slipping 0.9% to KRW 9.3 billion in 2025, diverging from the operating profit trend.

On a quarterly basis, operating profit fell sharply from KRW 4.92 billion in Q2 2025 to KRW 3.71 billion in Q3 2025 (down 24.4%) and KRW 86 million in Q4 2025 (down 97.7%), then recovered for two consecutive quarters to KRW 3.29 billion in Q1 2026 and KRW 5.09 billion in Q2 2026.

Net profit attributable to owners likewise bottomed at KRW 228 million in Q4 2025 before rising for two straight quarters to KRW 2.98 billion in Q1 2026 and KRW 4.21 billion in Q2 2026.

Q2 2026 revenue of KRW 54.7 billion edged down 0.8% from the prior quarter but rose 20.8% year over year, while net profit attributable to owners of KRW 4.21 billion increased 33.8% year over year. Net profit attributable to owners over the trailing four quarters (Q3 2025 through Q2 2026) totaled KRW 10.7 billion.

Operating cash flow stayed positive every year, at KRW 11.7 billion in 2022, KRW 6.32 billion in 2023, KRW 19.7 billion in 2024, and KRW 14 billion in 2025, though quarter-to-quarter volatility in earnings is a notable feature of the business.

05

Industry analysis

Seoyon Topmetal operates across two business axes, automotive molds and construction equipment parts, whose downstream cycles move somewhat independently.

The automotive mold segment depends heavily on orders from Seoyon E-Hwa, which are tied to Hyundai and Kia's production volumes and model changeover cycles, making automaker capital investment pace a key variable.

The construction equipment segment is linked to excavator production volumes at global heavy equipment makers including HD Hyundai Infracore and Volvo Construction Equipment, and the January 2026 merger of HD Hyundai Infracore into the newly formed HD Hyundai Construction Equipment entity is a point where order patterns could shift.

Construction-equipment-linked parts makers have previously drawn market attention tied to overseas reconstruction demand themes, reflecting a sector characteristic of sensitivity to shifts in heavy equipment demand.

Mold manufacturing carries heavy capital investment requirements and quality certification barriers such as ISO 9001 and ISO 14001, which tend to concentrate orders among top-tier suppliers. Fluctuations in steel and resin input costs are a direct variable affecting mold production costs.

Both downstream industries, automotive and construction equipment, are sensitive to interest rates and global demand trends, which is considered a factor behind the relatively high quarter-to-quarter earnings volatility in this sector.

Bull factors

Multi-Year Profit Improvement Trend

From 2022 through 2025, both revenue and operating profit rose for three consecutive years, with the operating margin steadily improving from 1.7% to 5.8%. Operating profit and net profit attributable to owners also recovered for two straight quarters in Q1 and Q2 2026, reconfirming the improving earnings trend. Q2 2026 revenue grew 20.8% year over year, accompanying this profit recovery with top-line growth.

Stable Cash Generation

Operating cash flow remained positive every year from 2022 through 2025, reaching KRW 19.7 billion in 2024. Despite volatility in reported earnings, the cash flow foundation of the business has held up. This could provide a positive basis for securing funds for future investment or dividends.

Diversified Customer Base Across Two Segments

The automotive mold segment is connected to the Hyundai and Kia supply chain via Seoyon E-Hwa, while the construction equipment segment is linked to HD Hyundai Infracore and Volvo Construction Equipment, giving relatively diversified exposure rather than dependence on a single downstream industry. When the demand cycles of the two segments move differently, weakness in one can be partly offset by the other.

Bear factors

Quarter-to-Quarter Earnings Volatility

Operating profit plunged 97.7% quarter over quarter to KRW 86 million in Q4 2025, and net profit attributable to owners fell to KRW 228 million. Because revenue recognition for mold and cabin products can be concentrated in specific quarters, a similar sharp drop cannot be ruled out in the future. When reviewing quarterly results, it is necessary to check whether one-off factors are involved.

Rising Debt Ratio

The debt-to-equity ratio rose from 62.4% in 2024 to 81.4% in 2025, reflecting a growing debt burden relative to equity. Total liabilities at the end of 2025 reached KRW 82 billion, approaching the KRW 100.7 billion in total equity. It will be worth watching whether this ratio rises further in upcoming quarterly reports.

Customer Concentration

Automotive mold revenue depends heavily on related party Seoyon E-Hwa, and the construction equipment segment is also concentrated among a small number of global heavy equipment makers.

Because key customer HD Hyundai Infracore merged with another entity in January 2026, there is a possibility that order structures could change, making the continuity of the business relationship worth monitoring.

A structure with high dependence on specific customers or affiliates can act as a constraint on bargaining power or pricing terms.

Risk factors

Customer and Affiliate Concentration Risk

Seoyon Topmetal's automotive mold revenue depends mostly on affiliated company Seoyon E-Hwa. The construction equipment segment is also concentrated among a small number of customers including HD Hyundai Infracore and Volvo Construction Equipment. Changes in key customers' ordering policies or sourcing strategies could directly affect revenue.

Raw Material Price Volatility Risk

Steel and resin are key raw material inputs for mold and heavy equipment parts production, and price fluctuations in these materials directly affect manufacturing costs. Given the order-based nature of the business, there can be periods when cost changes are not immediately reflected in selling prices. A sharp rise in raw material prices could increase volatility in quarterly operating margins.

Downstream Industry Cycle Risk

Both automotive production and construction equipment demand are sectors sensitive to interest rates and global economic trends. A slowdown in automaker new-model investment pace or a contraction in construction equipment demand could negatively affect orders and revenue.

With the debt ratio already elevated, a simultaneous cyclical slowdown could increase financial strain, a possibility that cannot be ruled out.
