02

Business structure

SG operates in three segments: apparel exports on an OEM basis, a fashion business selling its own brands, and other operations.

According to a stock information site's aggregation, apparel exports account for about 37.5 percent of sales, the fashion business for about 61.0 percent, and other businesses for about 1.5 percent.

The apparel export segment supplies women's wear, casual wear and children's wear to large US buyers on an OEM basis, with production at a factory in Vietnam. Customers commonly cited include GAP, Target and Macy's.

According to a company profile summary, this segment maintains buyer trust through quality and on-time delivery and is evolving from simple OEM toward an ODM model. The fashion segment sells five brands through department stores and outlets across the country, and it is the largest contributor to sales.

It operates in a domestic market with low entry barriers, diversifying distribution channels, and rising competition from global fashion companies entering Korea. The company also earns rental income from its headquarters building and logistics centers.

A profile also highlights luxury leather goods as a key product line, adding a higher-margin category to the portfolio. The company holds 100 percent of its consolidated subsidiaries, so the 2025 net income carries no non-controlling interest share, according to its disclosure.

Because overseas sales rely heavily on a single US buyer base, the company is directly exposed to tariffs and US consumer demand.

04

Earnings analysis

Annual revenue fell from KRW 161.3 billion in 2022 to KRW 143.1 billion in 2023 (down 11.3 percent year on year), KRW 138.8 billion in 2024 (down 3.0 percent) and KRW 132.3 billion in 2025 (down 4.6 percent), declining for three consecutive years.

The company attributed the 2025 decline to US tariff effects and an economic downturn, and said selling and administrative cost cuts narrowed the operating loss to KRW 1.52 billion (disclosed February 6, 2026).

Operating profit fell from KRW 7.2 billion in 2022 to KRW 1.84 billion in 2023 (down 74.5 percent), then turned into an operating loss of KRW 2.76 billion in 2024, and the loss continued in 2025.

The operating margin declined from 4.5 percent in 2022 to -1.1 percent in 2025, while operating cash flow of KRW 3.36 billion in 2025 was the largest of 2022 to 2025.

Net income attributable to controlling shareholders went from KRW 1.77 billion in 2022 to a net loss of KRW 1.03 billion in 2023, back to KRW 5.36 billion in 2024, and then KRW 884 million in 2025 (down 83.5 percent).

In 2024 the business posted an operating loss while net income was positive, which suggests non-operating items carried significant weight.

Quarterly, the company moved from an operating loss of KRW 617 million in Q3 2025 to an operating profit of KRW 347 million in Q4 2025, then back to an operating loss of KRW 1.87 billion in Q1 2026.

Q1 2026 net income attributable to controlling shareholders of KRW 3.01 billion reflected a KRW 5.2 billion gain on investment property disposal, as the company stated (preliminary, May 8, 2026).

An operating loss of KRW 464 million in Q2 2026 was followed by KRW 1.22 billion in Q3 2026, marking three consecutive loss-making quarters.

The preliminary nine-month operating loss of KRW 3.55 billion was wider than KRW 1.86 billion a year earlier (announced November 6, 2026), and the nine-month net income of KRW 3.33 billion included the KRW 5.2 billion property disposal gain and an equity-method gain of about KRW 3.1 billion.

05

Industry analysis

SG's core US OEM exports are directly exposed to US consumer demand and tariff policy. The company's explanation for the 2025 revenue decline, citing US tariff effects and an economic downturn, reflects this structure.

OEM order volumes move with buyers' inventory adjustments and order timing, so the three years of declining sales from 2023 are consistent with that volatility.

The company attributes the decline in its fashion segment to low domestic entry barriers, more diverse distribution channels and rising entry by global fashion companies into Korea. The same profile describes the domestic luxury market as growing as demand broadens to Generation Z and younger consumers.

The company says apparel exports are moving beyond simple OEM toward ODM, which could change the margin structure. Production in Vietnam brings a cost base advantage but also carries US tariff exposure.

At KRW 132.3 billion in 2025 revenue, SG is small next to large Korean fashion and retail groups, which implies relatively high dependence on a few buyers and channels. Its differentiators are delivery reliability, quality control and a diversified mix of own brands and rental assets.

Sales declined for three years before rebounding in Q3 2026, so one quarter is not enough to call the direction of the cycle.

06

Outlook

In March 2026 the company received shareholder approval for a 10-to-1 share consolidation, reducing common shares from 202,424,960 to 20,242,496. How the share count change affects the trading structure will be visible through later trading and disclosures.

Q3 2026 revenue rose 4.5 percent year on year to KRW 36.8 billion, a sign of rebound on the top line. However, nine-month revenue was KRW 91.9 billion, down 6.1 percent, so whether the quarterly recovery carries into the full year will be confirmed by Q4 results.

Nine-month net income of KRW 3.33 billion was supported by a KRW 5.2 billion property disposal gain and an equity-method gain of about KRW 3.1 billion, while the operating result remains a loss.

Whether operating profit turns positive excluding these one-off items is the key checkpoint for any change in earnings quality. The company posted an operating profit of KRW 347 million in Q4 2025, so quarterly swings are large.

Based on the timing of last year's annual disclosure (February 6, 2026), the 2026 full-year preliminary results would likely appear in early February 2027. US tariff policy and buyer order flow remain external variables the company cannot control directly.

08

Bull factors

Financial capacity and low leverage

Total equity at year-end 2025 was KRW 261.1 billion against total liabilities of KRW 33.1 billion. The debt-to-equity ratio fell from 23.8 percent in 2022 to 12.7 percent in 2025. Operating cash flow of KRW 3.36 billion in 2025 was the largest of 2022 to 2025. With a thick equity base, financial burden stays low even during a period of shrinking sales.

Track record of narrowing operating losses

The operating loss narrowed from KRW 2.76 billion in 2024 to KRW 1.52 billion in 2025, which the company attributed to selling and administrative cost reductions. Q4 2025 also produced an operating profit of KRW 347 million.

Cost control translating into a smaller loss is an observed pattern, and it forms the basis of the bullish case.

Q3 2026 revenue rebound

Q3 2026 revenue reached KRW 36.8 billion, up 35.0 percent from the prior quarter and 4.5 percent from a year earlier. Compared with KRW 27.7 billion in Q1 and KRW 27.3 billion in Q2 2026, quarterly sales scale recovered.

Nine-month revenue, however, was 6.1 percent below the prior year, so whether the rebound lasts remains to be confirmed.

Bear factors

Operating losses continue and widen

The company has posted three consecutive operating losses from Q1 to Q3 2026. The Q3 operating loss of KRW 1.22 billion was wider than KRW 617 million a year earlier. The preliminary nine-month operating loss of KRW 3.55 billion widened from KRW 1.86 billion a year earlier (announced November 6, 2026).

Three years of falling sales and tariff exposure

Revenue declined for three straight years from 2023 through 2025, and the company cited US tariffs and an economic downturn as causes. Because US buyer OEM work is a large share of the mix, tariffs and softer consumption pass through directly. The domestic fashion segment also faces low entry barriers and intensifying competition from global brands.

Reliance on one-off gains

Q1 2026 net income attributable to controlling shareholders of KRW 3.01 billion included a KRW 5.2 billion property disposal gain. Nine-month net income also leaned on an equity-method gain of about KRW 3.1 billion.

Q3 swung to a net loss of KRW 1.02 billion attributable to controlling shareholders, showing how sharply quarterly results depend on such items.

Risk factors

US tariffs and buyer concentration

The core of SG's exports sits with large US buyers such as GAP, Target and Macy's. If tariff costs pass into buyer pricing, margins could shrink, and concentrated orders mean any change in buyer purchasing hits revenue directly. The company itself cited tariff effects as a driver of the 2025 revenue decline.

Domestic fashion competition and slow recovery

The branded fashion segment faces intensifying competition from low entry barriers, more distribution channels and global brands entering Korea. With nine-month revenue below the prior year, a slow domestic recovery could delay any narrowing of losses.

Dependence on one-off gains and asset disposals

Q1 2026 and cumulative net income include a KRW 5.2 billion gain on investment property disposal and an equity-method gain of about KRW 3.1 billion. Such items are unlikely to recur regularly, and if operating losses persist, net income could slip back toward losses.
