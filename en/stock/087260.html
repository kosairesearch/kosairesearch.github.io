02

Business structure

Mobile Appliance, founded in 2004, makes automotive electronics and listed on KOSDAQ in 2017. Its core products are dash cameras, head-up displays (HUD), advanced driver assistance systems (ADAS) and navigation units, and it also holds a subsidiary, Hyundai Pontus.

Its customers are automakers in Korea and overseas, so revenue is sensitive to the automotive cycle and to order flow. In March 2026 the company said it had moved beyond its black box and navigation focus into core autonomous driving sensors and AI-based automotive electronics solutions.

Management also said it has resumed supply for new models at European premium carmakers including Mercedes-Benz and Audi. The company says it is strengthening its technology barriers against rivals by combining several product technologies.

At the March 2026 shareholder meeting, it proposed a charter amendment adding business purposes such as aircraft and spacecraft manufacturing, satellite communications, AI and robotics equipment sales, data center construction and renewable power generation. Revenue by product line is not presented here as numbers.

04

Earnings analysis

On the disclosed annual figures, revenue fell from KRW 52.5 billion in 2022 to KRW 49.8 billion in 2023 (down 5.1% year on year), KRW 40.5 billion in 2024 (down 18.7%) and KRW 34.9 billion in 2025 (down 13.7%).

Revenue declined for three consecutive years from 2023 to 2025, with 2022 the largest year and 2025 the smallest in the 2022 to 2025 span. Operating results swung from an operating loss of KRW 717 million in 2022 to an operating profit of KRW 452 million in 2023, which the company reported as a turn to profit.

Operating profit then reached KRW 972 million in 2024, up 114.9% year on year, before the company returned to an operating loss of KRW 896 million in 2025. Operating margins were -1.4% in 2022, 0.9% in 2023, 2.4% in 2024 and -2.6% in 2025.

Net income attributable to controlling shareholders was KRW 2.11 billion in both 2022 and 2023, fell to KRW 1.4 billion in 2024 (down 33.6%), and turned into a net loss of KRW 565 million in 2025.

Quarterly, revenue went from KRW 7.88 billion with an operating loss of KRW 560 million in 2025 Q3 to KRW 4.46 billion with an operating loss of KRW 2.87 billion in 2026 Q1. In 2026 Q2 revenue rose to KRW 8.67 billion, up 94.6% from the prior quarter, while the operating loss stayed at KRW 2.81 billion.

In 2026 Q3, revenue was KRW 4.62 billion, down 41.4% year on year, and the operating loss was KRW 1.67 billion.

In its May 15, 2026 announcement, the company attributed the first-quarter loss to one-time M&A advisory and post-merger integration costs, higher R&D and infrastructure spending for new businesses, and severance payments.

The company also pointed to lower orders at home and abroad and higher raw material costs from a weaker won as drivers of the revenue decline.

05

Industry analysis

Mobile Appliance operates in in-vehicle infotainment and ADAS, where demand follows automaker production and order flow. A 2021 technical analysis report noted that the automotive industry slowed amid semiconductor supply problems, showing how closely results track the cycle.

The same report also described IT companies entering the infotainment market as connectivity advances and expectations rise for commercial autonomous driving. That expansion widens the opportunity but also increases competition.

Among same-sector listed peers are Hyundai Autoever, LG CNS and Samsung SDS, though their revenue scale and business models differ widely, which limits direct comparison.

On the new-business side, AI demand is driving interest in data centers and GPU-based infrastructure, and the company says it is exploring entry into that market. Satellite communications remains at an early stage, so it may take time before it contributes to results.

Because the core business depends on automaker orders, how management divides resources between the core and new businesses is a key variable.

06

Outlook

In its May 15, 2026 announcement, the company said it expects synergies from the acquisition to appear from the second half of 2026 and to make revenue visible. It also said it is counting on revenue growth and better profitability from new product launches.

In the core business, resumed supply to European premium carmakers, including Mercedes-Benz and Audi, is cited as a positive. The company has started company-wide cost reductions and efficiency measures and says it aims to deliver visible results from new businesses quickly.

In data infrastructure, it is considering small distributed edge data centers using idle assets combined with power and renewable energy. In satellites, it says it is taking part in global infrastructure cooperation talks under a phased low-Earth-orbit roadmap.

These projects remain at the plan or review stage and are not yet reflected in reported results. Revenue for 2026 Q3 fell 41.4% year on year and the operating loss was KRW 1.67 billion, so whether the second-half expectations show up in reported results is the most direct checkpoint.

Quarterly revenue has also varied widely, with 2026 Q2 at KRW 8.67 billion, so swings should be watched alongside trend. The next thing to confirm is how quickly new-business investment and integration costs decline.

08

Bull factors

Balance sheet improvement

The debt ratio fell from 56.4% in 2022 to 24.3% in 2025. At the end of 2025, total liabilities were KRW 11.9 billion and total equity was KRW 49.1 billion, which gives relatively wide financial room.

Operating cash flow stayed positive, at KRW 7.33 billion in 2023, KRW 5.06 billion in 2024 and KRW 4.67 billion in 2025, even during the loss years.

Resumed supply to European premium automakers

The company says it has stepped up supply to European premium new-model lineups, including Mercedes-Benz and Audi, and presents this as evidence of a core business recovery. Whether new-model supply turns into revenue should show up in later quarterly trends. The rise in 2026 Q2 revenue to KRW 8.67 billion may be one early signal.

Expansion into new business platforms

In March 2026 the company proposed a charter amendment adding business purposes including aircraft and spacecraft manufacturing, satellite communications, AI and robotics equipment sales and data center construction. It says it is reviewing a small distributed edge data center model using idle assets.

It is also exploring entry into GPU-based infrastructure as AI demand grows. These efforts have not yet shown up in reported results.

09

Bear factors

Consecutive losses and loss size

The company has posted losses for five consecutive quarters from 2025 Q3 through 2026 Q3. Operating losses were KRW 2.87 billion in 2026 Q1, KRW 2.81 billion in Q2 and KRW 1.67 billion in Q3.

The cumulative controlling-shareholder net loss over the last four quarters is KRW 11.4 billion, a heavy burden relative to revenue of this size.

Three years of declining revenue

Revenue declined from KRW 49.8 billion in 2023 to KRW 40.5 billion in 2024 and KRW 34.9 billion in 2025. The company cited lower orders at home and abroad and higher raw material costs from a weaker won. Until order recovery is confirmed, the core revenue base can be read as weak.

Unproven profitability of new businesses

The new businesses span aerospace, satellites and data centers, and many are still at the review stage. The company said R&D and hiring costs rose to help new businesses take hold quickly. Investment costs can hit reported results before revenue arrives, which may pressure short-term profit. Continued investment while core profitability has not recovered is a bearish factor.

10

Risk factors

Governance and legal risk

Media reports cited a company disclosure that it would pursue criminal complaints for breach of trust and embezzlement against its former CEO and largest shareholder, with the amount reported at KRW 6.4 billion. Reports also described a change in the largest shareholder.

Governance uncertainty can affect financing and investor confidence, so progress in the case should be followed.

Financing burden

Reports said the company planned to raise a total of KRW 19 billion through a third-party rights offering of KRW 9 billion and convertible bonds of KRW 10 billion. A payment schedule originally set for late April was pushed back to late June.

If losses continue, more funding may be needed, and conversion of bonds or new shares would dilute existing holders.

Concentration and order volatility

The core business depends heavily on automaker orders, so lower orders or shifts in supply timing feed directly into revenue. Quarterly revenue moved from KRW 4.46 billion in 2026 Q1 to KRW 8.67 billion in Q2, showing wide swings. Until new businesses are large enough to offset this volatility, earnings stability will likely remain limited.

Overall view

Mobile Appliance's revenue fell from KRW 52.5 billion in 2022 to KRW 34.9 billion in 2025, and the company has posted losses for five consecutive quarters from 2025 Q3 through 2026 Q3. Its balance sheet looks relatively stable, with a debt ratio of 24.3% and total equity of KRW 49.1 billion at the end of 2025.

Resumed supply to European premium automakers is the main positive in the core business, but falling revenue and wide quarterly swings remain bearish factors. New businesses in satellites, data centers and AI infrastructure are still at the review or planning stage and are not yet reflected in results.

Changes in the largest shareholder, legal complaints and a KRW 19 billion financing plan leave governance and funding uncertainty. Asset-based valuation measures sit below peer averages, while earnings-based measures cannot be calculated.

The next checkpoint is the 2026 annual business report, and whether operating results narrow losses or move to profit will be the test for both the core business and new ventures.
