Report overview

Losses Persist as Focus AI Shifts to an SI Model

Focus AI is moving its business from hardware distribution toward an AI solution and system integration (SI) model. Operating losses have continued for five straight quarters through the third quarter of 2026 on a disclosed basis, so whether profitability recovers in reported results remains the central question.

  1. 1

    On a disclosed basis, revenue fell from KRW 61.6 billion in 2022 to KRW 45.3 billion in 2025, and the operating loss widened to KRW 10.2 billion in 2025.

  2. 2

    In its August 14, 2026 announcement, the company said the first-half gross margin rose to 11.5%, about double the prior-year level, while selling and administrative expenses fell 21%. The same release put the first-half operating loss at KRW 3.65 billion, down roughly 40%.

  3. 3

    The debt-to-equity ratio stood at 120.7% at the end of 2025, down from 195.0% in 2024. The company said it secured more than KRW 5 billion in cash after the first half of 2026 through rights offering payments and disposal of fixed assets.

  4. 4

    At the March 31, 2026 annual general meeting, the C-suite was replaced, including CEO Koo Hyung-mo. The company acknowledged that continued adverse opinions on internal control over financial reporting remain a governance priority.

  5. 5

    Events in the fourth quarter of 2026 included a mass-production agreement for NPU-based AI servers with DEEPX (October 30, 2026) and an award at the Republic of Korea Marine Corps AI competition (November 10, 2026).

02

Business structure

Focus AI supplies physical security and industrial safety solutions built on AI video analytics, and is listed on KOSDAQ. Its business falls into three lines.

The first is distribution of video equipment such as CCTV, network video recorders (NVRs) and DVRs, which the company describes as low-margin device sales that it has been reducing in 2026.

The second is system integration (SI), which bundles design, construction, operation and maintenance; the company says operation and maintenance revenue continues after installation, creating a recurring revenue base.

The third is industrial safety solutions, led by an AI forklift human-detection system and a smart industrial safety platform, with the company stating it has secured references at large domestic corporate sites.

Public-sector revenue grew from roughly KRW 2 billion to 3 billion in 2023 to the KRW 7 billion range, and the 2026 target is KRW 10 billion. The company also offers face recognition and intelligent security server products, which drew interest from Middle East vendors at a January 2026 exhibition.

Competition overlaps with video security equipment makers and large SI providers, although the company is far smaller than the large SI players in revenue scale.

04

Earnings analysis

On a disclosed basis, annual revenue was KRW 61.6 billion in 2022, KRW 59.5 billion in 2023 (down 3.5% year on year), KRW 58.2 billion in 2024 (down 2.1%) and KRW 45.3 billion in 2025 (down 22.1%).

Revenue has declined for three consecutive years from 2023 through 2025, with 2022 the largest year and 2025 the smallest in the 2022–2025 period.

Operating profit was KRW 2.66 billion in 2022 and KRW 2.65 billion in 2023 (down 0.7%), before turning to an operating loss of KRW 5.09 billion in 2024 and widening to KRW 10.2 billion in 2025. The operating margin moved from 4.3% in 2022 and 4.5% in 2023 to -8.7% in 2024 and -22.5% in 2025.

Net income attributable to controlling shareholders was KRW 2.68 billion in 2022 and KRW 2.47 billion in 2023 (down 8.1%), then a net loss of KRW 11 billion in 2024 and a narrower net loss of KRW 10.3 billion in 2025. Net income including non-controlling interests was reported at the same levels for these years.

Operating cash flow went from KRW 2.12 billion in 2022 and KRW 5.98 billion in 2023 to -KRW 3.42 billion in 2024 and -KRW 7.8 billion in 2025.

Quarterly revenue was KRW 13.5 billion in Q3 2025, KRW 10.8 billion in Q4 2025 (down 20.2% quarter on quarter), KRW 7.78 billion in Q1 2026 (down 28.0%), KRW 10.6 billion in Q2 2026 (up 36.2%) and KRW 5.48 billion in Q3 2026 (down 48.3% quarter on quarter and down 59.6% year on year).

Operating losses over the same five quarters were KRW 1.34 billion, KRW 2.79 billion, KRW 2.48 billion, KRW 1.17 billion and KRW 2.17 billion, five consecutive quarterly losses. The drivers of the Q3 2026 revenue drop cannot be pinned down from the disclosed figures alone.

The company attributed the first-half improvement to reduced low-margin product sales and lower selling and administrative costs.

05

Industry analysis

Focus AI's main end markets are industrial safety, public and smart-city security, and defense surveillance. The company's starting point is the demand for workplace safety management that grew after the Serious Accidents Punishment Act took effect.

At a July 2026 exhibition, the company said it was seeking new customers in shipbuilding, construction and manufacturing. It also said demand for AI-based security solutions is growing alongside smart-city projects.

In defense, media reports note that the industry is watching the move of surveillance equipment toward integrated detection, tracking and equipment control. On the semiconductor side, DEEPX has said the application of domestic NPUs in actual products is expanding.

Video equipment distribution has the characteristics of a mature, low-margin market, while AI solutions and SI offer recurring revenue and higher-value structures.

On the macro side, a Mirae Asset-type research chief-level concern about tightening funding for AI infrastructure investment was raised in October 2026, with high rates and oil prices weighing on AI customers' capacity to invest.

Compared with large listed SI providers in the same IT and software sector, Focus AI is far behind in scale and reference base.

06

Outlook

The company has laid out several concrete plans. Its 2026 public-sector revenue target is KRW 10 billion. The DEEPX agreement was described as covering algorithm porting, validation, system integration, chip supply for mass production and field trials.

In announcing it, CEO Koo Hyung-mo said domestic AI servers would be supplied to the market from December, while a news headline reported the first product launch for January 2027. Because these two timelines differ, the actual shipment date is a checkpoint.

The company said it would complete a unified AI server that combines an AI box with NVR functions within 2026 and expand its 36-channel and 64-channel embedded recorder lineup.

The autonomous surveillance-equipment model shown at the Marine Corps competition is at the stage of being adapted to military operating conditions and possibly linked to defense AI demonstration projects or follow-on R&D.

In the Middle East, the company said in January 2026 that it was considering bids for large projects. On the financial side, the company said the auditor's half-year review opinion no longer carries an uncertainty note, so removal from the market warning designation looks likely in 2026.

For the second half of 2026, the company said it aims to pursue revenue growth and profitability improvement together.

08

Bull factors

Early signs of margin improvement

The company said the first-half 2026 gross margin rose to 11.5%, about double the prior-year level, while selling and administrative expenses fell 21%. It attributed this to cutting low-margin NVR distribution and expanding AI cameras, solutions and SI revenue.

Because the operating loss widened again in the third quarter of 2026, whether this improvement holds on a quarterly basis is the point to watch.

Recurring revenue base and public and safety references

The SI model is the recurring revenue structure the company emphasizes, since operation and maintenance revenue follows system installation. Public-sector revenue expanded from the KRW 2 billion to 3 billion range in 2023 to the KRW 7 billion range, with a KRW 10 billion target for 2026.

The company also said it has secured references from supplying AI forklift safety solutions to large domestic corporate sites.

Entry into AI servers and defense

On October 30, 2026, the company signed a mass-production agreement with DEEPX for NPU-based AI servers, with its own video analytics algorithms optimized for DEEPX chips. On November 10, 2026, it won an award at the Republic of Korea Marine Corps AI competition for an automated surveillance-equipment model.

The company is considering military demonstrations and linked R&D, and the revenue contribution of these new lines does not yet appear in disclosed figures.

09

Bear factors

Consecutive losses and revenue decline

Operating losses have run for five consecutive quarters from the third quarter of 2025 through the third quarter of 2026. Third-quarter 2026 revenue of KRW 5.48 billion fell 59.6% from a year earlier. Net losses attributable to controlling shareholders over the latest four quarters total KRW 11.7 billion.

Revenue decline and continued losses are happening at the same time, so the effect of the business shift has not yet fully shown up in quarterly results.

Financial and governance issues

Adverse opinions on internal control over financial reporting have persisted, and the company is rebuilding its external audit response system. Of the KRW 5.8 billion second-series convertible bond, the early redemption right covers about KRW 2.8 billion, which the company says it can cover with cash on hand.

Busan Digital Asset Exchange was reclassified from a consolidated subsidiary to an associate in the first quarter of 2026 and accounted for under the equity method.

Customer concentration and uncertain timelines

The company acknowledged the limits of depending on specific customers and a hardware-centered structure, and named customer diversification a core task for 2026. Timing for the AI server launch differs between the CEO's remark (supply from December) and press reports (first product in January 2027).

Market concern also exists that weaker funding conditions for customers could delay AI solution investment and orders.

10

Risk factors

Cash flow and reliance on capital raising

Operating cash flow was -KRW 3.42 billion in 2024 and -KRW 7.8 billion in 2025. Total liabilities stood at KRW 25.5 billion at the end of 2025. Capital growth in the first half of 2026 relied on rights offerings and capital support from the largest shareholder, so further funding needs matter. Until operating cash generation is confirmed, dependence on capital raising remains.

Accounting, internal controls and market confidence

Continued adverse internal control opinions led the company to build a collaboration system with a large accounting firm and a law firm. The company says removal from the market warning designation is likely in 2026, but that remains a company expectation rather than a settled outcome.

The reclassification of Busan Digital Asset Exchange followed a review of control requirements, and future classification changes could alter reported profit and equity presentation.

Execution of the business shift and demand volatility

The shift from hardware distribution to SI and solutions has been accompanied by lower revenue. Third-quarter 2026 revenue dropped sharply, and quarterly revenue has been volatile.

Even if demand for industrial safety, public-sector and defense applications expands, revenue recognition may be delayed by the timing of orders and field trial periods. High interest rates and concern over AI customers' investment capacity could slow execution.

What to watch next

  1. December 2026

    Whether supply of the DEEPX-partnered AI server actually begins. The CEO said supply starts in December, while press reports put the first product launch in January 2027. Which timeline is met is the first test of the business shift.

  2. By year-end 2026

    Completion of the unified AI server combining AI box and NVR functions, and expansion of the 36- and 64-channel recorder lineup. Completion is a precondition for a larger share of SI and solution revenue.

  3. End of December 2026

    Whether public-sector revenue reaches the KRW 10 billion target. Public revenue has grown from roughly KRW 2–3 billion in 2023, and reaching the target is an interim gauge of the recurring revenue structure.

  4. Within 2026

    Whether the market warning designation is lifted. The company says the auditor's half-year review no longer carries an uncertainty note and that removal looks likely in 2026. The outcome is tied to progress on internal controls.

  5. End of March 2027

    Filing of the 2026 annual report, the next periodic report. It confirms full-year revenue, operating results and the internal control audit opinion, and will show whether the fourth-quarter 2026 results and quarterly losses continued.

12

Overall view

Focus AI is shifting its business from hardware distribution toward AI video analytics solutions and SI. On a disclosed basis, revenue declined from KRW 61.6 billion in 2022 to KRW 45.3 billion in 2025, and the operating loss widened to KRW 10.2 billion in 2025.

In the first half of 2026, the company announced an 11.5% gross margin, a 21% cut in selling and administrative expenses and a roughly 40% smaller operating loss. However, third-quarter 2026 revenue fell 59.6% from a year earlier and the operating loss widened, extending the losses to five consecutive quarters.

On the financial side, the debt-to-equity ratio fell to 120.7% in 2025, but much of the balance-sheet improvement relies on capital raising, and adverse internal control opinions and the market warning designation remain open issues.

New lines such as AI servers, unified recorders and defense AI are at a stage where timing and revenue contribution are not yet visible.

The next points to check are the start of AI server supply in December 2026, completion of product development within 2026, progress toward the public-sector revenue target, and the operating loss trend in the 2026 annual report.
