02

Business structure

Iljin Electric runs two segments: cables and heavy electrical equipment. The cable business makes power transmission and distribution cables, while the heavy electrical business supplies transformers and circuit breakers that control voltage and power flow at power plants and substations.

In the first half of 2026, cable sales were KRW 814.9 billion, about 71% of the total, and heavy electrical sales were KRW 327.2 billion, about 29%. The contribution to profit runs the other way: heavy electrical operating profit was KRW 82.5 billion in the same period, about 67% of total operating profit.

Its main production base is the Hongseong plant in Chungcheongnam-do, which has made extra-high-voltage transformers and circuit breakers since 2013. A second-plant expansion completed in October 2024 raised transformer capacity to about KRW 430 billion, and the plant produces units up to the 765kV class.

Orders are concentrated overseas, with overseas accounting for 72% of the backlog at end-September 2026. A long-term supply contract worth KRW 431.8 billion with an energy company in the eastern United States is scheduled for delivery in stages from 2026 to 2030.

On the cable side, the company won two orders totaling KRW 271.6 billion in 2026 for extra-high-voltage underground transmission lines linked to a UK offshore wind grid project.

In Korea, a November 2025 Hankyung report grouped Iljin Electric with Hyosung Heavy Industries, HD Hyundai Electric and LS Electric as leading transformer makers.

04

Earnings analysis

On a reported basis, annual revenue rose from KRW 1.16 trillion in 2022 to KRW 1.25 trillion in 2023 (up 7.0%), KRW 1.58 trillion in 2024 (up 26.5%) and KRW 2.04 trillion in 2025 (up 29.6%), extending three straight years of growth from 2023 to 2025.

Operating profit increased every year, from KRW 31.5 billion in 2022 to KRW 60.8 billion, KRW 79.7 billion and KRW 151.2 billion in 2025, which was the largest year of 2022–2025. Operating profit grew faster than revenue, lifting the operating margin from 2.7% in 2022 to 4.9%, 5.1% and 7.4% in 2025.

Net profit attributable to controlling shareholders rose from KRW 24.2 billion in 2022 to KRW 34.5 billion, KRW 46.2 billion and KRW 103.9 billion in 2025 (up 124.9%), and net profit including non-controlling interests was also KRW 103.9 billion in 2025.

The main driver of the margin gain is the growing weight of the heavy electrical business. In March 2026, Yuanta Securities cited the faster rise in heavy electrical revenue as evidence of a structural improvement in earnings.

Quarterly revenue climbed from KRW 450.2 billion in the third quarter of 2025 to KRW 614.2 billion in the fourth quarter (up 36.4% quarter on quarter), fell 17.6% to KRW 506.1 billion in the first quarter of 2026, then rose to KRW 637.4 billion in the second quarter (up 25.9%) and KRW 637.6 billion in the third quarter, unchanged from the prior quarter.

Quarterly operating profit went from KRW 35.3 billion to KRW 44.2 billion, KRW 50.8 billion, KRW 72 billion and KRW 70 billion, with the third quarter down 2.7% from the second.

According to the company's November 11, 2026 disclosure coverage, the third-quarter operating margin was about 11.0%, against 7.8% a year earlier.

Net profit attributable to controlling shareholders bottomed at KRW 27.2 billion in the fourth quarter of 2025 and then rose for three straight quarters to KRW 35.9 billion, KRW 49.9 billion and KRW 51.4 billion, with the last four quarters totaling KRW 164.5 billion.

Cash flow told a different story: operating cash flow went from KRW 50.6 billion in 2022 and KRW 49.2 billion in 2023 to KRW 105.7 billion in 2024, then fell to KRW 86.9 billion in 2025.

Over the same period the debt ratio climbed from 143.4% in 2022 to 159.2% at end-2025, with total liabilities of KRW 937.3 billion and total equity of KRW 588.7 billion, all of it attributable to controlling shareholders.

05

Industry analysis

Demand for power equipment is rising as aging grid assets are replaced and new transmission investment picks up. In its November 11, 2026 results announcement, Iljin Electric said that the replacement of aging US grids and the expansion of renewable generation in Europe supported demand.

The industry describes this as a power supercycle driven by global grid investment. A past report in Electimes said about 70% of large transformers installed in the United States are more than 25 years old, which suggests replacement demand will continue given their 30–40 year design lives.

Tighter US data-center regulation is a possible headwind, but the company sees its extra-high-voltage transformers, which are used in large grid builds, as only limitedly exposed.

In competition, Iljin Electric is grouped with Hyosung Heavy Industries, HD Hyundai Electric and LS Electric among the leading transformer makers. A March 2026 brokerage report said the company can produce up to 525kV units and that recent orders centre on 345kV and 525kV.

On the production side, extra-high-voltage transformers involve heavy manual work, so skilled labour is a constraint on expansion; the company says training technicians takes at least six to seven years.

Domestically, the company is taking part in a Ministry of Trade, Industry and Energy-backed development project for 500kV-class HVDC converter transformers, which is aimed at the national energy highway programme.

On the cycle, both the company and brokers describe order books that already fill a large share of planned production slots.

06

Outlook

The company said it expects a solid fourth quarter of 2026, as shipments from existing orders continue (November 11, 2026 disclosure coverage). It also said large projects where orders are expected in the fourth quarter leave room for additional wins.

The backlog at end-September 2026 was about KRW 3.05 trillion, with heavy electrical products at 69% and cables at 31%. In cables, the company won orders for a UK offshore wind grid project in 2026 and announced a KRW 84.5 billion UK high-voltage transmission order in July (Job-Post, September 2026 report).

In March 2026, Yuanta Securities said the company had secured much of its 2028 and part of its 2029 volume. Heavy electrical capacity was expanded through the Hongseong expansion to about KRW 430 billion in transformer capacity.

A 2025 report put second-plant utilisation at about 80%, with the company aiming for about 95% in the following year. Domestically, the national energy highway programme and HVDC converter transformer development are cited as possible new demand sources.

The next periodic report is the 2026 annual report, which will disclose full-year results, year-end backlog and segment sales. Whether faster growth in overseas cable orders shows up in quarterly revenue is the next point to track.

08

Bull factors

Backlog fills production slots

The backlog at end-September 2026 was about KRW 3.05 trillion, roughly 16% above end-June. Overseas orders make up 72% of it. This means a large part of the production schedule from 2028 onward is already filled by secured volume.

Heavy electrical profit contribution and margin gains

The heavy electrical business's share of operating profit is far larger than its share of sales, so earnings tilt toward it. The third-quarter 2026 operating margin was about 11.0%, against 7.8% a year earlier. The annual operating margin rose from 2.7% in 2022 to 7.4% in 2025.

Capacity expansion and large project wins

Transformer capacity at the Hongseong plant was expanded to about KRW 430 billion. In 2026 the company won two orders totaling KRW 271.6 billion for UK offshore wind grid cables, and in July it announced a KRW 84.5 billion UK transmission order. The company said large projects where orders are expected remain in the fourth quarter.

09

Bear factors

High multiple to book value and a low dividend

The share price sits at a high multiple of equity, meaning a large premium to net asset value. The dividend yield is low, so cash income from dividends is modest. The multiple is sensitive to any change in earnings expectations.

Balance sheet strain and softer cash flow

The debt ratio rose from 143.4% in 2022 to 159.2% in 2025, with total liabilities of KRW 937.3 billion. Operating cash flow fell from KRW 105.7 billion in 2024 to KRW 86.9 billion in 2025. Cash generation did not rise alongside earnings, which is a point to re-check in the next periodic report.

Quarterly volatility

First-quarter 2026 revenue fell 17.6% from the prior quarter, and third-quarter operating profit fell 2.7% from the second quarter. Because large orders ship on specific schedules, quarterly revenue can swing. Third-quarter revenue stayed at the level of the prior quarter.

10

Risk factors

Trade and tariffs

In a March 2025 interview the company said it had been affected for eight years by US anti-dumping duties. It believes its US market share would hold even if additional tariffs are imposed. The effect of tariffs on order pricing and profitability needs to be tracked through quarterly disclosures.

Skilled labour and production concentration

Extra-high-voltage transformers involve heavy manual work, and the company says training skilled technicians takes at least six to seven years. Adding capacity does not raise output immediately if the workforce lags. With long-term contracts delivering from 2026 to 2030, any production delay could shift revenue timing.

Demand and policy shifts

Tighter US data-center regulation could change power demand patterns, although the company sees only limited direct impact. With 72% of the backlog overseas, policy changes in the US and Europe or project rescheduling could affect order flow. If the pace at which orders convert into revenue slows, the pace of earnings improvement would change as well.

Overall view

Iljin Electric's reported results for 2022–2025 show revenue rising from KRW 1.16 trillion to KRW 2.04 trillion and operating profit rising from KRW 31.5 billion to KRW 151.2 billion. Third-quarter 2026 revenue of KRW 637.6 billion and operating profit of KRW 70 billion show the trend continuing.

The backlog grew to about KRW 3.05 trillion at end-September 2026, and with 72% overseas, the company depends heavily on US and European grid investment. The margin gain came from the heavy electrical business, which generated about 67% of first-half 2026 operating profit.

Strengths include order visibility, margin improvement and expanded capacity; weaknesses include a high multiple to book value, a low dividend, a higher debt ratio and lower operating cash flow in 2025.

Quarterly results show uneven stretches, such as the first-quarter revenue decline and the small third-quarter operating profit dip, so the pace at which orders convert into revenue is the key variable.

The next checkpoint is the 2026 annual report due by late March 2027, which will disclose fourth-quarter 2026 results and year-end backlog. This report includes no investment rating or target price, and figures and dates are drawn from disclosures and press coverage.
