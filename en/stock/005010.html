02

Business structure

Husteel is a steel pipe manufacturer founded in 1967 that produces pipes for piping, oil country tubular goods (OCTG), civil and structural construction, and electrical conduit. Its production base consists of the Dangjin and Daebul plants for carbon steel pipe and the Daegu plant for stainless steel pipe.

The Gunsan plant, completed in June 2025, added large-diameter production, and reports say it can make pipe up to 9 m in outer diameter and 120 mm in wall thickness. The company has said the large-diameter expansion added annual capacity of 165,000 tons.

The U.S. plant near Houston, Texas is reported to have an energy pipe capacity of 72,000 tons. By region, reports indicate that 54%, or KRW 213.2 billion, of Husteel's KRW 390 billion total sales in the first half of 2026 came from the United States.

Its core oil country tubular goods sales depend on U.S. oil and gas drilling activity. Overseas, the company has been registered as a supplier to Shell and approved as a supplier to Bechtel, Williams, and Syncrude.

In Korea, it has been grouped with Hyundai Steel Company and SeAH Steel as one of the three major steel pipe makers, but until the Gunsan plant it was the only one of the three without large-diameter LSAW capacity.

04

Earnings analysis

On a reported basis, revenue fell from KRW 1.03 trillion in 2022 to KRW 764.8 billion in 2023 (down 25.8% year on year), KRW 723.1 billion in 2024 (down 5.5%), and KRW 612.5 billion in 2025 (down 15.3%), a three-year decline. 2022 was the largest year in the 2022–2025 range for revenue, operating profit, and net profit attributable to controlling shareholders.

The operating margin stepped down from 28.1% in 2022 to 16.1% in 2023, 2.4% in 2024, and 0.6% in 2025. Net profit attributable to controlling shareholders fell from KRW 72.2 billion in 2023 to KRW 22.3 billion in 2024, then turned into a net loss of KRW 15 billion in 2025.

Total net income for 2025 was also a loss of KRW 15 billion. The company attributed the revenue decline to weaker domestic and North American sales, and the profit deterioration to stronger U.S. protectionist measures, including a 50% tariff on steel products.

Operating cash flow dropped from KRW 206.3 billion in 2022 to KRW 6.44 billion in 2025.

Quarterly results show a sharp shift: the third quarter of 2025 posted revenue of KRW 162.1 billion and operating profit of KRW 11.5 billion, while the fourth quarter brought revenue of KRW 140.9 billion and an operating loss of KRW 4.78 billion. The first quarter of 2026 widened the operating loss to KRW 5.25 billion.

The second quarter lifted revenue to KRW 242 billion, up 63.6% from the prior quarter, while the operating loss narrowed to KRW 806 million. The third quarter revenue of KRW 228.2 billion rose 40.8% year on year, but the operating loss widened to KRW 32.6 billion.

Net loss attributable to controlling shareholders over the latest four quarters totals KRW 96.7 billion. Coverage of the first half of 2026 attributed the pressure to depreciation on new plants being recognized before output reached installed capacity. Revenue recovery and profit recovery are therefore not moving at the same pace.

05

Industry analysis

Demand for Husteel's core steel pipe products depends heavily on North American energy markets. Its oil country tubular goods sales track U.S. oil and gas drilling activity, and reports say the number of U.S. oil drilling rigs in the first half of 2026 rose about 10% from a year earlier.

Volatility in oil prices tied to the Middle East conflict that broke out in early 2026 could cloud the outlook, and steel industry sources have taken a cautious view on sustained pipe demand growth.

Across Korea's pipe industry, capacity additions have created oversupply and weak demand, which has intensified competition. In the U.S. market, protectionist measures such as a 50% tariff on steel products directly affect the profitability of export channels.

On the cycle, North American energy demand shows signs of improvement, but Korean oversupply and tariff costs are weighing at the same time. In offshore wind, demand is rising for large-diameter, thick-wall pipe used to build transition pieces for wind turbine foundations.

Before the Gunsan plant came online, the company said it lacked large-diameter LSAW capacity, which limited its market reach compared with Hyundai Steel Company and SeAH Steel.

Market commentary has described the pipe theme as showing short-term strength on hopes for global energy infrastructure investment, while noting that raw material prices and global economic volatility also need to be weighed.

06

Outlook

Three lines of confirmed business activity frame the near-term outlook.

First, the Gunsan large-diameter plant, completed in June 2025, is in its production stabilization phase, and the company said on October 12, 2026 that it had completed supply of about 3,000 tons of large-diameter, thick-wall pipe for an overseas offshore wind project.

The company says it has won successive offshore wind projects in Korea and abroad and plans to build its supply record in renewable energy markets. Second, the U.S. plant near Houston was reported to have completed its investment in the first half of 2026.

Reports indicate it generated no revenue and a net loss of KRW 300 million in the first half of 2026, and that depreciation will be fully reflected in consolidated results once commercial production gets underway in the second half of 2026 and beyond.

Third, recovering the investment requires raising output to absorb fixed costs, a challenge the company and the industry both recognize. The company is also pursuing entry into green energy and diversification of export markets, and U.S. trade conditions will continue to shape export volumes and pricing.

In the next annual report for fiscal 2026, the operating margin relative to 0.6% in 2025 will be the key checkpoint. Whether utilization at the Gunsan and U.S. plants can return quarterly operating results to profit will become clearer in results for the first half of 2027.

Bull factors

Large-diameter and offshore wind demand base

The Gunsan large-diameter plant is described as capable of producing pipe up to 9 m in outer diameter. The company said on October 12, 2026 that it had completed supply of about 3,000 tons of large-diameter pipe to an overseas offshore wind project and that it has been winning additional projects.

Higher utilization of the new capacity would give room to absorb fixed costs, which is the main bull-side argument.

Revenue rebound in the second quarter of 2026

Second-quarter 2026 revenue reached KRW 242 billion, up 63.6% from the fourth quarter of 2025, when it had bottomed at KRW 140.9 billion. The operating loss narrowed to KRW 806 million from KRW 5.25 billion in the prior quarter. The combination of a larger top line and a smaller loss can be read as an early sign of normalization.

Share price low relative to book assets

Total equity at the end of 2025 was KRW 1.06 trillion, all attributable to controlling shareholders, against total liabilities of KRW 315 billion. The price-to-book ratio sits well below 1x, so the market values the asset base at a low level. If earnings recover, whether book value converts into profit is the core of the bull case.

Bear factors

Four consecutive quarters of operating losses

Operating losses have continued for four straight quarters, from the fourth quarter of 2025 through the third quarter of 2026. The third-quarter 2026 operating loss of KRW 32.6 billion was the largest of the period, appearing even though revenue rose from a year earlier.

Long-term erosion of profitability and cash generation

The operating margin fell from 28.1% in 2022 to 0.6% in 2025. Operating cash flow also dropped from KRW 206.3 billion in 2022 to KRW 6.44 billion in 2025. With revenue declining for three straight years and fixed costs rising, the earnings structure has weakened, which forms the core of the bear case.

Trade barriers and demand concentration

Reports indicate that 54% of first-half 2026 sales came from the United States. The company attributed the 2025 profit deterioration to tougher North American protectionist measures, including a 50% tariff on steel products.

Because oil country tubular goods demand is tied to U.S. drilling and oil prices, the company remains exposed to trade conditions and energy price swings.

Risk factors

Fixed-cost and utilization risk

Depreciation on new plants is hitting the income statement first, so operating losses can persist if output stays below installed capacity. Reports say the U.S. plant recorded no revenue and a net loss of KRW 300 million in the first half of 2026.

The timing of full commercial production and the pace of utilization gains will determine when earnings recover.

Trade and tariff risk

Protectionist measures in North America and the 50% tariff on steel products directly reduce profitability in U.S. export channels. Reports indicate that more than half of sales come from the United States, so changes in trade policy have a large effect on earnings. Without changes in tariff conditions, expanding export volume faces limits.

Raw material and oil price volatility

Pipe demand is sensitive to oil price swings and raw material costs. Reports point to rising oil price volatility tied to the Middle East conflict that broke out in early 2026.

Market commentators also say raw material prices and global economic volatility should be considered together, so both costs and demand carry uncertainty.

Overall view

Husteel's revenue has declined from KRW 1.03 trillion in 2022 to KRW 612.5 billion in 2025, and its operating margin has fallen from 28.1% to 0.6%, a structural weakening.

The company swung to a net loss attributable to controlling shareholders of KRW 15 billion in 2025, and operating losses have continued for four straight quarters from the fourth quarter of 2025 through the third quarter of 2026.

Revenue recovered to KRW 242 billion in the second quarter of 2026 and the loss narrowed, but the third-quarter operating loss widened again to KRW 32.6 billion.

On the bull side, the Gunsan large-diameter plant and the U.S. plant are entering operation, offshore wind pipe supply is building a track record, and the share price sits low relative to book assets.

On the bear side, depreciation from new plants, trade risk from a U.S. sales base above half of revenue, and oil and raw material volatility could delay any recovery.

The next checkpoints are the operating margin in the 2026 annual report, the commercial ramp-up and utilization of the U.S. plant, and whether quarterly operating results return to profit in the first quarter of 2027. This report is for information only and does not include a buy or sell opinion.
