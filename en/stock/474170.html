Lumir: Losses Widen Again After 2025 Turn to Profit

Lumir returned to a net profit attributable to controlling shareholders in 2025, but has posted losses in each of the first three quarters of 2026, and quarterly results swing widely with satellite project timing and revenue recognition.

  3. 3

02

Business structure

Lumir develops synthetic aperture radar (SAR) Earth-observation satellites and payloads, and also runs a civil device business that makes solenoids for ship engines.

Satellite manufacturing accounted for 76.6% of 2023 revenue and civil devices for 23.1%, according to data included in the pre-listing registration statement. For Q3 2025, satellite manufacturing and services were 73% and civil devices 27%, as reported by Daily Invest.

A company-data aggregator puts the Q2 2026 split at 66% space and 34% civil devices, so the mix shifts sharply from quarter to quarter. The satellite business relies on national satellite programs, and in 2025 Lumir won the SAR payload development contract for the Next-Generation Medium Satellite 5.

The civil business centers on ship-engine solenoids, and according to NH Investment & Securities' December 2025 report, the company expanded its production facility in early 2025 to meet higher ship-engine demand. KB Securities' March 2026 report described only five companies globally as able to provide SAR data.

Its own LumirX satellite is a 0.3m-class high-resolution SAR satellite, and a successful launch could create new satellite service revenue. The largest shareholder held 46.4% at listing.

Because the two businesses' revenue shares change from quarter to quarter, results depend heavily on when revenue from one business is recognized.

04

Earnings analysis

Annual revenue was KRW 12.1 billion in 2023, KRW 14.3 billion in 2024 (up 17.7% year over year), and KRW 10.6 billion in 2025 (down 26.0% year over year). Among 2023–2025, the largest year was 2024 and the smallest year was 2025.

The operating loss narrowed from KRW 3.02 billion in 2023 to KRW 1.2 billion in 2024 and KRW 0.59 billion in 2025, and the operating margin improved from -24.9% to -8.4% and -5.6%.

The company has posted an operating loss for three consecutive years, from 2023 through 2025, and the 2025 net income attributable to controlling shareholders of KRW 45.66 million marks a turnaround to profit from the previous year.

As the reasons for the revenue decline in 2025, the company cited delays in the schedule of the national satellite project and certification procedures accompanying the expansion of its production facilities.

KB Securities assessed that the narrowing of the operating loss in the same year was supported by strong performance in the shipbuilding business segment.

Cash flow from operating activities showed a large outflow of KRW 4.98 billion in 2023 and KRW -26.3 billion in 2024, then turned to an inflow of KRW 16.4 billion in 2025.

On a quarterly basis, the company returned to profit in the fourth quarter of 2025 with revenue of KRW 3.72 billion and operating profit of KRW 0.24 billion, but revenue in 2026 fluctuated widely, at KRW 1.9 billion in the first quarter, KRW 4.24 billion in the second quarter, and KRW 0.8 billion in the third quarter.

The operating loss widened to KRW 1.06 billion in the second quarter of 2026 and KRW 1.74 billion in the third quarter, and the net loss attributable to controlling shareholders for the same periods was KRW 0.88 billion in the second quarter and KRW 1.47 billion in the third quarter.

The structure in which quarterly profit and loss respond sensitively to the scale of revenue appears to be the core of this trend.

05

Industry analysis

The end market is shaped by national space budgets and satellite procurement schedules. NH Investment & Securities' December 2025 report said the 2026 Korea Aerospace Administration budget rose 16.1% year on year to KRW 1.1201 trillion and expected higher results in the space business.

The same report said eco-friendly ship-engine demand is broadening demand for marine solenoids. In cycle terms, delays in national satellite programs were the largest swing factor for results, and their effect was most visible in 2025.

Under the 2024 plan, LumirX was to launch on an external rocket, so its schedule also depends on launch-vehicle availability. Unlike larger defense and systems companies in the same sector, such as Korea Aerospace Industries and Hanwha Systems, Lumir focuses on satellite payloads and small SAR satellites.

Compared with the satellite operator Satrec Initiative, Lumir is distinguished by pursuing both SAR payloads and its own satellite operations. The civil device business is tied to shipbuilding demand and cushions results while ship-engine demand holds.

06

Outlook

The outlook rests on three threads. First, the LumirX-1 technology demonstration satellite. KB Securities' March 2026 report described a launch in the second half of 2026, followed by one satellite in 2027 and four in 2028, for a constellation of 18 by 2031.

A Hana Securities report from November 2024 targeted 18 satellites by 2030, so completion dates differ across reports. Second, the expected benefit from a larger space budget.

NH Investment & Securities' December 2025 report expected results to recover after 2026 as the company launches its own satellite and government budgets expand. Third, rising demand for civil devices.

Quarterly revenue rebounded to KRW 4.24 billion in Q2 2026, up 123.2% from the prior quarter, then fell to KRW 796 million in Q3, down 81.2% quarter on quarter. Whether the outlook materializes therefore depends on whether quarterly revenue stabilizes at a sustainable level.

The next periodic report is the 2026 annual report, which will confirm full-year 2026 revenue, operating results and Q4 figures. After that, the next checkpoint is the Q1 2027 quarterly report on the continuity of civil-device revenue and the timing of satellite revenue recognition.

08

Bull factors

Possible satellite service revenue

The LumirX-1 technology demonstration satellite is planned for launch in the second half of 2026, and a successful launch could create new satellite service revenue, according to brokerage reports.

If the planned addition of one satellite in 2027 and four in 2028 proceeds, the business mix could broaden from satellite manufacturing toward services. A 2024 Hana Securities report also presented this service revenue as a growth pillar.

Larger space budget and contract opportunities

In a December 2025 report, NH Investment & Securities expected higher results in the space business, citing the increase in the 2026 Korea Aerospace Administration budget. The larger budget was cited as a basis for contract opportunities. If national satellite program schedules normalize, the delay effects seen in 2025 could ease.

Civil device recovery and return to profit

NH Investment & Securities said the civil device business returned to normal earnings levels in Q2 and Q3 2025 after the production facility expansion. Q4 2025 posted an operating profit of KRW 236 million, a turn to profit.

The 2025 operating loss narrowed from KRW 1.2 billion in 2024 to KRW 594 million, and KB Securities assessed that strength in shipbuilding supported the improvement.

09

Bear factors

Quarterly losses widen in 2026

The operating loss widened to KRW 1.06 billion in Q2 2026 and KRW 1.74 billion in Q3 2026. The net loss attributable to controlling shareholders grew to KRW 880 million and KRW 1.47 billion over the same quarters.

Q3 revenue of KRW 796 million was 81.2% lower than Q2's KRW 4.24 billion, which suggests quarterly results have not yet settled into a clear direction.

Dependence on national satellite schedules

According to a December 2025 news report, delays in national satellite programs cut cumulative nine-month 2025 revenue to KRW 6.9 billion, down 42.5% year on year. The company also cited certification procedures tied to its production facility expansion. The same pattern of procurement timing driving results appears in the quarterly revenue swings of 2026.

Track record of launch delays

A 2024 Hana Securities report projected a LumirX launch within Q1 2026. KB Securities' March 2026 report placed the technology demonstration launch in the second half of 2026 and listed launch-plan delays as a key risk. Satellite revenue timing could also slip with external launch schedules.

10

Risk factors

Follow-on launch plan revision

Follow-on launch plans could be revised depending on the outcome of the LumirX-1 technology demonstration satellite.

Completion dates for the 18-satellite constellation also differ across brokerage reports, at 2030 in a Hana Securities report from November 2024 and 2031 in KB Securities' March 2026 report, so the baseline for outlooks is divided.

If the technology demonstration falls short of expectations, the constellation build-out could take longer and the start of satellite service revenue could be pushed back.

Weaker civil-device buffer

The civil device business is tied to shipbuilding conditions and ship-engine demand, and it cushions results in quarters when satellite revenue is thin. KB Securities assessed that strength in the shipbuilding segment supported the narrowing of the 2025 operating loss.

If demand for eco-friendly ship engines slows or shipbuilding conditions turn down, this cushion would weaken and swings in satellite revenue could pass more directly into quarterly profit and loss.

Financial capacity and cash flow

Operating cash flow was negative KRW 26.3 billion in 2024 before turning to an inflow of KRW 16.4 billion in 2025. If quarterly losses continue through 2026, the sustainability of cash generation will be tested again. Total equity of KRW 61.4 billion and a 13.0% debt-to-equity ratio indicate financial cushion.

12

Overall view

On an annual basis, Lumir's net profit attributable to controlling shareholders turned positive in 2025, and the operating loss narrowed for three consecutive years through 2025.

However, operating losses have continued in each quarter from Q1 to Q3 2026, and in Q3 revenue of KRW 796 million and an operating loss of KRW 1.74 billion widened the deficit.

The business is split between national satellite programs and ship-engine solenoids, and the timing of revenue recognition in each drives quarterly results. Bullish factors include potential satellite service revenue after the LumirX launch, a larger 2026 space budget, and recovery in civil-device profitability.

Bearish factors include repeated launch delays, dependence on national program schedules, and continued quarterly losses in 2026. Because the last four quarters are net losses, no earnings-based multiple is calculated, and on an asset basis market capitalization is above total equity.

The next checkpoints are whether LumirX-1 launches in the second half of 2026 and the full-year results in the 2026 annual report.
