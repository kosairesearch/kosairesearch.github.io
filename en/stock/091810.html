02

Business structure

Trinity Airways is an airline that operates passenger and cargo services on domestic and international routes. The company was founded in 2003 as a offshoot of the low-cost carrier Chungcheong Air, and in 2026 it changed its name from the company's former name to Trinity Airways.

Its controlling shareholder group, including related parties, holds 55.41%, and after a change in the largest shareholder in 2025 the company became part of the Sono group. Passenger transport accounts for about 91% of revenue (per Valueline's aggregation), with cargo and ancillary services making up the rest.

As of the end of March 2026 the fleet comprised 48 aircraft, including A330, B777 and B737 models, serving 51 cities at home and abroad. In route mix, short-haul international routes to Japan and China are growing in weight.

In the third quarter of 2026, Japan routes accounted for 28.3% of revenue, up 8.7 percentage points year on year, and China routes rose to 9.0%, up 4.5 percentage points. Over the same period, the Europe share fell from 24% to 15.7%.

Cargo revenue for the nine months through September 2026 reached KRW 68.1 billion, up 53.4% year on year. The hub is Daegu Airport, and the company is reported to run one of the largest catering centers among Korean LCCs.

Competition overlaps with domestic low-cost carriers such as Jin Air, Jejuair and Air Busan on some routes, and with larger carriers such as Korean AIR Lines and Asiana Airlines.

Fuel is the largest cost item for airlines, and reports say the company bears a relatively heavier cost burden from exchange-rate and oil-price swings than LCCs focused on short-haul routes.

04

Earnings analysis

Annual revenue rose from KRW 525.8 billion in 2022 to KRW 1.35 trillion in 2023 (up 156.5% year on year), then to KRW 1.54 trillion in 2024 (up 13.9%) and KRW 1.8 trillion in 2025 (up 17.0%). Revenue has risen for three consecutive years from 2023 through 2025, and 2025 was the largest year in the 2022-2025 period.

Operating results moved from an operating loss of KRW 103.9 billion in 2022 to an operating profit of KRW 139.4 billion in 2023, a swing to profit from a year earlier.

It then fell back to an operating loss of KRW 12.3 billion in 2024 and widened to an operating loss of KRW 265.5 billion in 2025, marking two straight years of losses. Operating margins were -19.8% in 2022, 10.3% in 2023, -0.8% in 2024 and -14.8% in 2025.

Net income attributable to controlling shareholders was KRW 99.1 billion in 2023, a return to profit, but the company posted net losses of KRW 65.9 billion in 2024 and KRW 338.3 billion in 2025.

At the end of 2025, total liabilities were KRW 1.82 trillion against total equity of KRW 52 billion, giving a debt-to-equity ratio of 3,498.6%. Operating cash flow was positive in all four years, at KRW 166 billion in 2022, KRW 415.3 billion in 2023, KRW 272.4 billion in 2024 and KRW 171.1 billion in 2025.

On a quarterly basis, revenue was KRW 449.8 billion in the third quarter of 2025 with an operating loss of KRW 95.5 billion, then KRW 523.9 billion with a narrower operating loss of KRW 56.2 billion in the fourth quarter.

Revenue reached KRW 612.1 billion in the first quarter of 2026 with an operating profit of KRW 19.2 billion, a swing to profit, and press reports described it as the first profit in eight quarters.

In the second quarter of 2026, revenue was KRW 469.7 billion and the operating loss widened to KRW 182 billion; reports attributed this to high fuel prices linked to the Middle East conflict, with fuel costs rising to KRW 287.5 billion.

In the third quarter of 2026, revenue was KRW 533.4 billion, up 18.6% year on year, and the operating loss narrowed to KRW 41.8 billion. Fuel costs fell by KRW 49.6 billion to KRW 237.9 billion even as scheduled international flights rose from 13,992 to 16,607.

Lease costs fell 55.5% from KRW 79.2 billion to KRW 35.3 billion. Net losses were also narrowed by a foreign currency translation gain of KRW 51.7 billion, so operating improvement and this one-off type of gain should be considered separately.

Net losses attributable to controlling shareholders have persisted for five consecutive quarters from the third quarter of 2025 through the third quarter of 2026, with a four-quarter total of KRW 320 billion.

05

Industry analysis

The Korean low-cost carrier market is in a phase where airlines are reorganizing routes while absorbing high fuel costs and currency pressure.

According to press reports, Jin Air and Jejuair both moved from losses to operating profit in the third quarter of 2026, while Trinity Airways cut its operating loss by more than half.

Over the same period, aviation fuel costs rose year on year at all three companies: KRW 148.5 billion at Jin Air, KRW 178.9 billion at Jejuair and KRW 237.9 billion at Trinity Airways.

For the first three quarters of 2026, Trinity Airways posted the largest cumulative operating loss among the three LCCs, at KRW 204.6 billion. Cost pressure from medium- and long-haul route operations and aircraft investment also remains, according to commentary.

Trinity Airways has reduced the weight of European routes and expanded short-haul international routes to Japan and China. On demand, third-quarter 2026 international passengers reached 1.93 million, up 11.8% year on year, which points to steady demand on short-haul international routes.

Because the company relies more on short-haul routes than some peers, it is more exposed to exchange-rate and oil-price swings, which places it among the more cost-sensitive operators in the sector.

It is difficult to pin down where the industry sits in its cycle, and aviation fuel prices and exchange rates remain the variables that drive profit and loss. Among the three LCCs, Trinity Airways has the largest operating loss, which is the clearest fact describing its current position.

06

Outlook

The company has said it will continue to improve profitability in the fourth quarter of 2026 through route efficiency and aircraft replacement. Reallocating capacity after reviewing profitability by route is the basic approach for fourth-quarter operations.

New service from Incheon to Kobe and from Incheon to Nagoya is scheduled for December, and the company plans to strengthen its network around short-haul routes with steady demand.

Over the medium to long term, it is pursuing an SSC strategy that combines tourism infrastructure with aviation, and press reports say it is also accelerating aircraft replacement and investment in service infrastructure.

To strengthen maintenance capability, the company is building a new hangar within the Incheon International Airport advanced complex, targeting completion at the end of 2027. Plans to introduce more fuel-efficient aircraft have also been reported.

According to the company, the easing of cost pressure seen in the third quarter is expected to continue into the fourth quarter as a positive factor for results. No numerical earnings guidance from the company has been identified. Fourth-quarter results will be reflected in the 2026 annual report on a quarterly basis.

Oil prices and exchange rates remain the largest variables, and whether the third-quarter decline in fuel costs continues into the fourth quarter is the next point to check.

08

Bull factors

Passenger demand and route mix improving

Third-quarter 2026 revenue reached KRW 533.4 billion, up 18.6% year on year. International passengers rose 11.8% to 1.93 million, and the Japan route share of revenue increased to 28.3%. The company said load factors and yields both improved.

Lower fuel costs and cost restructuring

Third-quarter fuel costs fell to KRW 237.9 billion from KRW 287.5 billion in the second quarter, even as international flights rose from 13,992 to 16,607. Lease costs also dropped 55.5%, from KRW 79.2 billion to KRW 35.3 billion. These factors helped explain why the operating loss more than halved from a year earlier.

Experience of a quarterly return to profit

The first quarter of 2026 posted an operating profit of KRW 19.2 billion, a return to profit. Press reports describe it as the first profit in eight quarters. The quarterly profit shows that route efficiency measures can appear in reported results.

Bear factors

Fuel exposure and wider second-quarter loss

The second-quarter 2026 operating loss of KRW 182 billion was the largest quarterly loss in the period, with fuel costs rising to KRW 287.5 billion in the same quarter. The third-quarter operating loss of KRW 41.8 billion remains.

Reports say the company bears a relatively larger cost burden from exchange-rate and oil-price swings than LCCs focused on short-haul routes.

Financial structure and equity base

At the end of 2025, total liabilities were KRW 1.82 trillion and total equity was KRW 52 billion, giving a debt-to-equity ratio of 3,498.6%. Reports put total liabilities at KRW 2.09 trillion, equity at KRW 88.7 billion and the ratio at about 2,358% at the end of September 2026. Debt remains large relative to equity.

One-off factors behind the net loss improvement

The third-quarter net loss narrowed partly because of a foreign currency translation gain of KRW 51.7 billion. A translation loss of KRW 12.6 billion was recorded in the same period a year earlier.

The operating loss of KRW 41.8 billion remains, so the net loss improvement cannot be read entirely as operating improvement.

Risk factors

Fuel and currency cost risk

Fuel costs surged to KRW 287.5 billion in the second quarter, widening the operating loss to KRW 182 billion. Costs eased to KRW 237.9 billion in the third quarter but still form a large share of expenses.

Foreign currency translation results move net income substantially, so exchange-rate swings widen quarterly volatility.

Financial leverage and equity erosion concerns

Total liabilities are many times larger than total equity, so changes in profit and loss feed directly into the equity base. Equity was KRW 52 billion at the end of 2025 and reported at KRW 88.7 billion at the end of September 2026, a small base. If losses continue, financial resilience could weaken further.

Ownership structure and share liquidity

The largest shareholder and related parties hold 55.41%, leaving a free float ratio of 42.94% as of August 5, 2026 according to aggregated data. A tally shows institutions net sold 1.3 million shares in the 20 days before the first-quarter 2026 results announcement. Share price volatility tied to ownership structure and flows is a risk factor.

Overall view

Trinity Airways is a company whose revenue grew for three consecutive years from 2023 through 2025, but its results were in loss for two consecutive years in 2024 and 2025. In the third quarter of 2026, revenue reached KRW 533.4 billion, up 18.6% year on year, and the operating loss narrowed to KRW 41.8 billion.

Reports attribute the main improvement to fuel costs falling from KRW 287.5 billion in the second quarter to KRW 237.9 billion in the third. The net loss narrowing also reflected a foreign currency translation gain of KRW 51.7 billion, so it should be distinguished from operating improvement.

The four-quarter total of net losses attributable to controlling shareholders is KRW 320 billion, and the debt-to-equity ratio at the end of September 2026 was about 2,358%, so financial burden remains.

Strengths include expansion on Japan and China routes, growth in cargo and ancillary services, and the experience of a first-quarter 2026 return to operating profit.

Weaknesses include exposure to fuel price swings, a cumulative operating loss of KRW 204.6 billion for the first three quarters of 2026, and a small equity base.

The next points to check are the operating margin and debt-to-equity ratio in the 2026 annual report, and the fuel cost trend in the first-quarter 2027 quarterly report.
