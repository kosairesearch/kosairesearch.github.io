02

Business structure

KCTC is a diversified logistics company that runs container terminals, port stevedoring, container transport, warehousing (CY and CFS), heavy-cargo and parcel services. According to the company profile, it was founded in 1973 and listed in 1978.

Its core asset is Busan New Port Pier 5 Container Terminal (BNCT), which the company describes as having four 50,000-ton berths and annual handling capacity of 2.6 million TEU.

Its transport arm handles about 400,000 TEU of export and import containers a year through its national network and about 450,000 TEU of transshipment cargo at Busan Port.

Under the holding structure, the Pier 5 business is reached through a 50% joint venture, I&K Shinhang Port, and a 12% associate, BNCT, so the stake percentages alone do not show the consolidated revenue contribution.

Inland, CY and CFS facilities at Uiwang ICD (4,000 TEU capacity) and Yangsan ICD (4,400 TEU) handle consolidation, storage and gate-in and gate-out for import and export cargo.

In April 2024 the company signed a private-investment agreement for a Busan New Port grain pier and set up a grain-terminal subsidiary in December 2024. On diversification, the company is pursuing stronger third-party logistics, a fulfillment business expansion and transport of offshore wind equipment.

Its competitive overlap includes CJ Logistics, HANJIN Logistics and SUN Kwang in port stevedoring, warehousing and logistics.

04

Earnings analysis

Annual revenue fell from KRW 920.4 billion in 2022 to KRW 823.1 billion in 2023, down 10.6% year over year, then rose 7.2% to KRW 882.1 billion in 2024 and 11.9% to KRW 986.7 billion in 2025, a two-year run of gains.

Operating profit moved from KRW 37.5 billion in 2022 to KRW 36.8 billion in 2023 (down 1.9%), KRW 39 billion in 2024 (up 5.9%) and KRW 38.8 billion in 2025 (down 0.4%), with 2024 the largest year of the period.

The operating margin was 4.1% in 2022, 4.5% in 2023, 4.4% in 2024 and 3.9% in 2025, the lowest of the four years. In 2025 revenue grew the most but operating profit slipped slightly, so top-line growth did not translate into a higher margin.

Net profit attributable to controlling shareholders was KRW 1.81 billion in 2022, KRW 1.76 billion in 2023 (down 2.7%), KRW 25.7 billion in 2024 (up 1,361.6%) and KRW 21.2 billion in 2025 (down 17.7%).

Net profit including non-controlling interests was KRW 28.1 billion, KRW 28.6 billion, KRW 27.6 billion and KRW 23.3 billion over the same years, and the gap between the two measures changed sharply from year to year.

The jump in attributable profit in 2024 is not explained by the 5.9% operating profit gain alone, so non-operating items and the non-controlling share should be read alongside it. Operating cash flow fell every year, from KRW 68.7 billion in 2022 to KRW 59.6 billion, KRW 52.1 billion and KRW 42.8 billion in 2025.

Total liabilities stood at KRW 457.3 billion and total equity at KRW 334.4 billion at the end of 2025, with the debt-to-equity ratio easing from 149.6% in 2024 to 136.8% in 2025.

Quarterly revenue bottomed at KRW 244.6 billion in Q4 2025, down 5.0% from the prior quarter, and then rose to KRW 265.7 billion, KRW 290.9 billion and KRW 306.2 billion across Q1 to Q3 2026, three straight quarterly increases.

Quarterly operating profit was KRW 12.9 billion in Q3 2025, KRW 4.39 billion in Q4 2025 (down 66.1% quarter over quarter), KRW 11.7 billion in Q1 2026, KRW 15.6 billion in Q2 2026 and KRW 9.56 billion in Q3 2026.

Q3 2026 net profit attributable to controlling shareholders was KRW 1.81 billion, down 80.8% year over year, and the last four quarters total KRW 20.9 billion. Revenue growth without matching profit growth is the central feature of the latest quarters.

Industry analysis

Container volumes move with export-import activity and transshipment demand, and terminal throughput depends heavily on how shipping lines deploy their services.

According to the company's website, Busan New Port Pier 5 has annual handling capacity of 2.6 million TEU, but that figure shows equipment scale rather than actual throughput, which depends on carrier volumes.

Port stevedoring, inland transport and warehousing move as one package, so large integrated logistics firms and regional carriers compete side by side. KCTC overlaps with CJ Logistics, HANJIN Logistics and SUN Kwang, and the use of port-adjacent land and warehouse assets shapes its long-term earnings base.

Fulfillment and third-party logistics are tied to changes in retail channels. A February 10, 2026 report said that the approval of early-morning delivery by large supermarkets lifted logistics-related themes in the stock market.

Offshore wind equipment transport depends on the order and installation schedules of domestic projects, so volumes are timing-sensitive. The Q1 2026 quarterly report discloses sales and purchases with related parties, which is worth reading alongside the share of external revenue.

Volatile profit attributable to shareholders

Net profit attributable to controlling shareholders fell 17.7% to KRW 21.2 billion in 2025, and Q3 2026 came in at KRW 1.81 billion, down 80.8% year over year. Following KRW 979 million in Q4 2025, quarterly profit has been weak twice.

Against KRW 25.7 billion in 2024, the year-to-year spread is wide, which makes the durability of earnings hard to judge.

Balance-sheet load and shrinking cash flow

Total liabilities of KRW 457.3 billion exceeded total equity of KRW 334.4 billion at the end of 2025, leaving a debt-to-equity ratio of 136.8%. Cash coming in from operations has been trending down, so cash generation is weakening, and together with the large debt load this could constrain room for new investment.
