From AI vision inspection to physical AI, expansion under losses

02

Business structure

Twim is a machine vision company whose core business is deep-learning-based AI vision inspection solutions.

Its 2026 first-quarter report describes the development and supply of equipment control software and vision software as its main businesses, with the MOAI deep-learning vision inspection solution and the T-MEGA AI inspection machine as flagship products.

A 2023 NICE D&B report said the company had built inspection systems across metal parts, electrical and electronics, secondary batteries and food and beverage manufacturing, giving it what the report called the largest reference base in Korea.

In 2026 the company has been extending into physical AI and robot automation. It said it secured the G1 embodied AI robot and the S1 heavy-duty robot lineup as the official Korean distributor of GALBOT.

It also operates RoAX, its in-house AI platform for recognition, appearance inspection and robot behavior learning, and T-Move, a platform for orchestrating and monitoring general-purpose robots, and it describes an end-to-end physical AI offering that links the two.

Logistics automation is at the proof-of-concept stage. At the March 31, 2026 annual general meeting, the company added the manufacture and sale of electronic signboards and electronic components to its articles of incorporation. It was also named a 2026 second-half Innovative IT Award winner.

04

Earnings analysis

On an annual basis, the company moved from an operating loss of KRW 6.46 billion in 2022 to operating profit of KRW 2.01 billion in 2023, a swing to profit. Operating profit then fell 70.1% to KRW 602 million in 2024, and 2025 brought an operating loss of KRW 4.08 billion, a swing to loss.

Revenue was KRW 19.6 billion in 2022 and KRW 34.5 billion in 2023, up 76.3% and the largest year of 2022 through 2025. It then fell 15.1% to KRW 29.3 billion in 2024 and 28.1% to KRW 21 billion in 2025, declining for two straight years.

Operating margins were -33.0% in 2022, 5.8% in 2023, 2.1% in 2024 and -19.4% in 2025. For the 2022 loss, a 2023 NICE D&B report pointed to one-time charges including bad-debt and inventory valuation provisions.

The company attributed the 2025 swing to a loss mainly to delayed and reduced investment in downstream industries amid a weaker economy.

Net profit attributable to controlling shareholders moved to KRW 2.98 billion in 2023, a swing to profit, then to KRW 1.63 billion in 2024, down 45.4%, and to a net loss of KRW 3.1 billion in 2025, a swing to loss.

By quarter, the third quarter of 2025 showed revenue of KRW 5.02 billion and an operating loss of KRW 1.09 billion. The fourth quarter brought revenue of KRW 6.2 billion, up 23.6% from the prior quarter, and a wider operating loss of KRW 1.39 billion.

The first quarter of 2026 marked the revenue low at KRW 2.93 billion, down 52.8% from the prior quarter, with an operating loss of KRW 1.66 billion.

In the second quarter, revenue recovered to KRW 4.67 billion, up 59.3%, the operating loss narrowed to KRW 1.21 billion, and net profit attributable to controlling shareholders was KRW 1.41 billion, a swing to profit.

In the third quarter, revenue was KRW 4.67 billion, up 0.1% from the prior quarter and down 6.9% from a year earlier, the operating loss narrowed to KRW 864 million, but the net loss attributable to controlling shareholders was KRW 2.53 billion, a swing back to loss.

Operating and net-to-controlling-shareholder results moved in different directions across quarters, so non-operating items should be read alongside the operating figures. The net loss attributable to controlling shareholders for the last four quarters totaled KRW 2.36 billion.

At the end of 2025, total equity was KRW 62.8 billion and total liabilities were KRW 6.47 billion, a debt ratio of 10.3%. Operating cash flow moved from an inflow of KRW 3.28 billion in 2024 to an outflow of KRW 7.05 billion in 2025.

05

Industry analysis

Twim's core market is machine vision, which automates quality inspection in manufacturing, and demand moves with capital spending in downstream industries.

The company attributed the 2025 revenue decline to delayed and reduced investment in downstream industries, which shows how closely its sales track equipment investment cycles.

After the first-quarter low in 2026, quarterly revenue recovered to about KRW 4.67 billion in both the second and third quarters, yet third-quarter revenue remained 6.9% below the year-earlier level.

The industrial robot market is shifting from repetitive automation toward physical AI, in which machines recognize their surroundings and learn and execute tasks. Twim is responding by linking its AI vision recognition to robot judgment and motion.

Among IT and software peers, NAVER, Samsung SDS and LG CNS are large SI and platform companies, but their scale and product structures differ greatly from Twim's.

Twim combines inspection equipment with software for factory floors, and its robot business partly depends on an external supply chain through its GALBOT distributorship. The key industry question is whether recovery in downstream equipment investment or growth in physical AI demand reaches revenue first.

06

Outlook

The most concrete order the company has disclosed is a KRW 6 billion supply contract signed on July 16, 2026. It was disclosed on July 20, 2026, equal to 28.52% of recent revenue, and runs from July 16, 2026 to July 25, 2029.

The counterparty stays undisclosed until the contract ends, at the company's request to protect trade secrets. How much of this contract lands in quarterly revenue depends on the delivery schedule, so its contribution will need to be checked in upcoming periodic reports.

On the physical AI side, the company demonstrated the G1 humanoid robot at the 2026 World Smart City Expo on September 17, 2026. Logistics automation remains at the proof-of-concept stage, and the company said it will use field trial results to improve recognition range and the grasping of irregular products.

Company officials said it will expand a general-purpose physical AI business through digital twin integration validation and a policy library built on robot learning.

Whether the electronic signboard and electronic component business added to the articles of incorporation becomes an actual business has not yet been separated in the reported figures. The revenue contribution of the physical AI and signboard businesses is an area that still needs confirmation. The next periodic report may show how robot-related revenue is classified and how the order backlog changes.

Bull factors

Physical AI business secured

The company says it secured the G1 and S1 robot lineup as the official Korean distributor of GALBOT, and it offers end-to-end physical AI technology linking its RoAX and T-Move platforms.

The expansion path is technically continuous because recognition technology built in inspection equipment is connected to robot judgment and motion. Whether this axis shows up as revenue will be checked in later reports.

Orders and quarterly revenue recovery

The KRW 6 billion contract signed in July 2026 equals 28.52% of recent revenue and runs through July 25, 2029. Quarterly revenue bottomed at KRW 2.93 billion in the first quarter of 2026, then rose to KRW 4.67 billion in each of the second and third quarters. The operating loss also narrowed to KRW 1.21 billion in the second quarter and KRW 864 million in the third.

Financial capacity and low debt burden

At the end of 2025, total equity was KRW 62.8 billion and total liabilities were KRW 6.47 billion, a debt ratio of 10.3%. The debt ratio fell from 19.0% in 2023 to 9.3% in 2024 and stayed near 10.3% in 2025. With a small borrowing burden relative to equity, the company has financial room to absorb losses.

Bear factors

Five consecutive quarters of operating losses

Operating losses have continued for five consecutive quarters from the third quarter of 2025 through the third quarter of 2026.

The net loss attributable to controlling shareholders totaled KRW 2.36 billion over the last four quarters, and the third-quarter 2026 figure of KRW 2.53 billion was a larger loss than a year earlier.

Even with a narrower operating loss, profitability recovery is not yet visible until net results stabilize in the black.

Limits to revenue recovery

Revenue peaked at KRW 34.5 billion in 2023 and then declined for two straight years in 2024 and 2025. Third-quarter 2026 revenue was KRW 4.67 billion, down 6.9% from a year earlier.

The company cited delayed and reduced downstream investment as the main cause of the 2025 swing to a loss, showing that sales still depend on outside equipment investment decisions.

Reversal in operating cash flow

Operating cash flow changed direction from an inflow to an outflow in 2025. Three of the four years from 2022 through 2025 saw outflows. With earnings in the red, continued cash outflows could increase the burden of funding operations, so whether cash flow improves is an important point to check.

Risk factors

Demand cycle

Revenue depends heavily on the equipment investment cycle of downstream industries. The investment delays the company cited for the 2025 decline could recur. Quarterly revenue fell to KRW 2.93 billion in the first quarter of 2026, showing how sharply demand can swing within a single quarter.

Monetization of new businesses

The physical AI and robot business is at the proof-of-concept stage in logistics automation. Because it relies on a distributorship for external robot products, margins could vary with supply terms and revenue sharing.

The reported figures do not break out physical AI revenue as a separate line, which makes it hard to gauge how quickly the expansion will show up in results.

Governance and shareholder meeting items

At the March 31, 2026 annual general meeting, the auditor re-election and the director compensation limit were rejected for lack of a quorum. The auditor item was affected by voting restrictions on shares above 3%, and the compensation item by voting restrictions on specially interested parties.

Re-election of inside directors and the articles amendment passed, and the case shows how the voting structure can affect agenda outcomes.

Overall view

Twim is a machine vision company built around AI vision inspection equipment and software, and in 2026 it has been widening into physical AI and robot businesses.

Revenue peaked at KRW 34.5 billion in 2023 and then declined for two straight years in 2024 and 2025, when the company swung to an operating loss of KRW 4.08 billion.

In the second and third quarters of 2026, revenue recovered to KRW 4.67 billion each and the operating loss narrowed, but third-quarter revenue was still down 6.9% from a year earlier and the net loss attributable to controlling shareholders widened to KRW 2.53 billion.

Positive factors include the physical AI axis built on the GALBOT distributorship and in-house platforms, the KRW 6 billion supply contract, and total equity of KRW 62.8 billion with a low debt ratio.

Negative factors include five consecutive quarters of operating losses, a net loss attributable to controlling shareholders of KRW 2.36 billion over the last four quarters, the 2025 operating cash outflow, and dependence on downstream equipment investment.

With no PER currently calculable, valuation can be described against net assets, which sit at a low level relative to book value, but earnings-based comparisons are limited.

The next checkpoints are the 2026 annual results disclosure, the annual general meeting and annual report around the end of March 2027, and the first-quarter 2027 report.
