Brake Pad Maker Pledges Dividends and PBR Gains Amid Losses

02

Business structure

Saeronautomotive Corporration was founded in 1999 and listed on the KOSPI in 2005; it manufactures automotive brake friction materials. Its main products are Brake Pads, Brake Linings and Rotor Facing.

Based on second-quarter 2026 sales, Brake Pad accounted for 94%, Rotor Facing 4%, other products 2% and Brake Lining 1%. Most sales come from Brake Pads sold by the friction division. According to WiseReport's company monitor, domestic sales made up 99.78% of Brake Pad sales in 2025.

Key customers include Hyundai Motor, KIA, Shanghai Volkswagen, Changchun Volkswagen, Suzuki and Toyota, along with parts makers such as HL Mando, Hyundai Mobis and Hanon Systems.

Overseas, the company operates Chinese subsidiaries including Saeron (Beijing) Brake Parts, Saeron (Yantai) Brake Parts and Nisshinbo Saeron (Shangyu) Brake Parts. A merger of the Chinese subsidiaries, in which Saeron (Yantai) absorbs Saeron (Beijing), had its effective date revised to May 29, 2026.

The company said the merger aims to improve operating efficiency and synergies in China through integrating human and physical resources. R&D spending was 9.39% of sales in 2025, and the company is developing copper-free friction materials to respond to U.S. EPA regulation.

As of September 30, 2026, Nisshinbo Industries and related parties held 65.01%, and HL Mando held 5.42% as a major shareholder.

04

Earnings analysis

Annual revenue rose from KRW 138 billion in 2022 to KRW 140.3 billion in 2023 (up 1.6% year on year) and KRW 142 billion in 2024 (up 1.2%). Revenue then fell to KRW 138.7 billion in 2025, down 2.3% year on year. Among 2022 to 2025, 2024 was the largest year for revenue and 2022 the smallest.

Operating losses were KRW 3.1 billion in 2022, KRW 7.62 billion in 2023, KRW 5.78 billion in 2024 and KRW 739 million in 2025. The company has been in operating loss for four consecutive years from 2022 to 2025; the loss widened in 2023 and narrowed in 2024 and 2025.

Operating margins were -2.2% in 2022, -5.4% in 2023, -4.1% in 2024 and -0.5% in 2025. Net income attributable to controlling shareholders turned from a KRW 5.72 billion loss in 2022 to KRW 3.02 billion profit in 2023, rose to KRW 9.69 billion in 2024 (up 221.1%), and fell to KRW 2.77 billion in 2025 (down 71.4%).

Total net income including non-controlling interests was a KRW 515 million loss in 2025, a swing to loss. The company cited a decline in non-operating income as the main reason for the 2025 change in net income.

At the end of 2025, total equity was KRW 244.9 billion and total liabilities KRW 30.2 billion, with a debt ratio of 12.3%. Operating cash flow was KRW 5.73 billion in 2022, KRW 5.66 billion in 2023, KRW 15.3 billion in 2024 and KRW 9.13 billion in 2025.

Quarterly operating losses were KRW 530 million in the third quarter of 2025, KRW 111 million in the fourth quarter, KRW 1.73 billion in the first quarter of 2026, KRW 639 million in the second quarter and KRW 1.29 billion in the third quarter, five consecutive quarters of losses.

For the first quarter of 2026, the company attributed the larger loss to one-off costs from an early retirement program, and the second-quarter loss narrowed as those costs fell away.

Net income attributable to controlling shareholders reached KRW 11.7 billion in the second quarter of 2026 before the third quarter posted a net loss of KRW 19.8 billion.

The sum of net losses attributable to controlling shareholders over the last four quarters (fourth quarter 2025 to third quarter 2026) was KRW 1.19 billion.

05

Industry analysis

Brake friction materials are supplied under an OEM structure, where volumes depend on automaker production and model-specific program awards. The company's sales rest on supply to domestic automakers and to Chinese joint-venture automakers, so they track automaker production trends.

Several sources describe 2026 auto market conditions as uncertain amid trade friction and high interest rates. Rising raw material and energy prices were also cited as cost pressures for parts makers.

According to WiseReport's company monitor, OEM supply for Brake Pads kept sales rising, but profitability weakened as raw and subsidiary materials imported from abroad raised costs.

In Alphasquare's ranking of 138 companies in the automotive parts industry, Saeronautomotive Corporration ranked 84th by market capitalization, 100th by revenue and 114th by operating margin. Its debt ratio was far below the industry average in the same comparison.

On environmental regulation, copper-free friction material development is cited as a competitive factor, and the company is pursuing a response to U.S. EPA rules. Listed peers in the same industry include HL Mando, Hanon Systems and Hyundai Mobis, which operate within the same automaker supply chains.

06

Outlook

In its corporate value-up plan decided on October 21, 2026 and disclosed on October 22, the company set a target of keeping dividends at the existing level in both 2026 and 2027. The plan also covers strengthening core business competitiveness, improving profitability and measures to raise PBR.

The implementation stages are listed as building a foundation, making results visible, and then taking off and settling. The plan does not set quantitative targets; a company official said this reflects unpredictable factors such as the 2027 wage negotiations.

On earnings volatility, the company pointed to one-off costs and said the improvement in second-quarter 2026 operating loss supports its efforts to improve core profitability. For the Chinese operations, the company plans to integrate human and physical resources centered on Saeron (Yantai).

On products, it is pursuing copper-free friction materials. In March 2026 the co-CEO lineup changed, with Jung Seok-tae newly appointed and Kumakawa Tetsuya retained. No revenue or profit guidance was found in the disclosures.

The next periodic report, the 2026 annual report, will confirm fourth-quarter and full-year 2026 results, with a filing deadline of March 31, 2027, and will also show the dividend decision and progress on the value-up plan.

Bull factors

Equity base and low leverage

Total equity at the end of 2025 was KRW 244.9 billion against total liabilities of KRW 30.2 billion, a debt ratio of 12.3%. The ratio rose slightly from 11.4% in 2022 but remains low. The balance sheet has relatively wide room relative to liabilities. Earnings power relative to the equity base remains limited by operating losses.

Dividend continuity and value-up plan

In its value-up plan decided on October 21, 2026, the company set a goal of keeping dividends stable in 2026 and 2027. The plan includes measures to raise PBR, with implementation split into foundation-building, visible results, and take-off and settling stages.

The disclosure also notes that cash dividends at the same level were paid for three consecutive years. Whether the dividend policy holds despite operating losses is the next item to confirm.

Narrowing losses and cash generation

The 2025 operating loss narrowed to KRW 739 million from KRW 5.78 billion in 2024, with an operating margin of -0.5%. The second-quarter 2026 operating loss was KRW 639 million, smaller than the first quarter's KRW 1.73 billion, which the company attributed to the absence of one-off costs.

Operating cash flow was KRW 9.13 billion in 2025. Whether this trend continues after the fourth quarter of 2026 is the next point to confirm.

Bear factors

Continued quarterly operating losses and lower sales

The company has posted operating losses for five consecutive quarters from the third quarter of 2025 through the third quarter of 2026. The third-quarter 2026 operating loss was KRW 1.29 billion, wider than KRW 639 million in the second quarter.

Sales that quarter fell to KRW 33 billion, down 12.1% from the prior quarter and 3.4% from a year earlier. The absence of simultaneous sales recovery and loss reduction is a bearish factor.

Cost pressure and weaker profitability

WiseReport's company monitor attributes weaker profitability to higher costs from reliance on imported raw and subsidiary materials. Industry sources cite rising raw material and energy prices as cost pressures for parts makers.

Operating margins were -2.2%, -5.4%, -4.1% and -0.5% from 2022 to 2025 and have not reached positive territory. If the cost structure does not improve, a recovery in core profitability may be limited.

Net income volatility and thin earnings

The third-quarter 2026 net loss attributable to controlling shareholders was KRW 19.8 billion, far larger than the KRW 1.29 billion operating loss. The sum of such net losses over the last four quarters was KRW 1.19 billion, so no price-to-earnings ratio is calculated.

Net income attributable to controlling shareholders rose to KRW 11.7 billion in the second quarter of 2026 before swinging to a loss in the third quarter, a large swing. The gap between operating and net results makes earnings-based assessment difficult.

Risk factors

Customer and product concentration

Brake Pads account for 94% of sales, so the product mix is concentrated. Major customers are automakers such as Hyundai Motor and KIA, making the company directly exposed to their production plans and model changes. Shifts in program awards for specific models could feed through to sales. Progress on product diversification or new customers will determine how much this risk eases.

Cost and raw material structure

Raw and subsidiary materials depend partly on imports, so swings in raw material and energy prices pass directly into costs. WiseReport's company monitor identified this cost burden as a factor weakening profitability. If automakers press for lower unit prices, passing costs through could be difficult. The level at which the operating margin improves is the checkpoint for this risk.

Labor negotiations and overseas operations

The company cited the 2027 wage negotiations among the unpredictable factors behind its lack of quantitative targets. Wage outcomes could change labor costs and affect earnings.

The Chinese subsidiaries are being integrated through a merger that changes their operating structure, so costs and synergies during integration could differ from expectations. Both factors are variables for the 2027 earnings path.

Overall view

Operating losses persisted from 2022 through 2025, but the 2025 loss narrowed to KRW 739 million from 2024, and operating cash flow was KRW 9.13 billion. At the end of 2025 the balance sheet showed total equity of KRW 244.9 billion and a debt ratio of 12.3%.
