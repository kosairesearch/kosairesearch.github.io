02

Business structure

Pulmuone Corporate is a holding company under the Fair Trade Act, with six subsidiaries including Pulmuone Foods and 30 consolidated subsidiaries as of June 1, 2026.

Its segments are holding, domestic food manufacturing and distribution, food service distribution, health care manufacturing and distribution, and overseas food manufacturing and distribution.

The domestic segment centers on tofu, namul vegetables and fresh noodles, and brands such as Jigu Sikdan, and it generated KRW 380.5 billion in the first quarter of 2026, or 44.7% of total revenue, up 2.3% year on year.

Food service distribution, driven by new contract catering wins from industrial sites and military units and by airport and rest-stop concessions, reported first-quarter 2026 revenue of KRW 254.0 billion, up 10.6%, and operating profit of KRW 6.1 billion, up 28.3%.

The overseas segment sells tofu and Asian noodles in the United States, China and Japan and posted first-quarter 2026 revenue of KRW 189.8 billion, up 13.8%, while its operating result recovered from a loss of KRW 5.3 billion a year earlier to around breakeven.

For January to September 2026, cumulative segment revenue was KRW 1.2152 trillion for domestic manufacturing (45.7% of the total), KRW 783.3 billion for food service and KRW 579.9 billion for overseas.

The company competes with domestic and foreign food makers in processed foods and with contract caterers in food service. As food service and overseas units grow in weight, changes in raw material and logistics costs affect group profit more.

04

Earnings analysis

Annual revenue rose from KRW 2.84 trillion in 2022 to KRW 2.99 trillion in 2023, up 5.5% year on year; KRW 3.21 trillion in 2024, up 7.4%; and KRW 3.38 trillion in 2025, up 5.2%.

Revenue increased for three consecutive years from 2023 to 2025, and 2025 was the largest year in the 2022 to 2025 span while 2022 was the smallest.

Operating profit went from KRW 26.3 billion in 2022 to KRW 62 billion in 2023, up 135.4%, then KRW 91.8 billion in 2024, up 48.2%, and KRW 93.2 billion in 2025, up 1.5%, rising for three consecutive years from 2023 to 2025.

The operating margin was 0.9% in 2022, 2.1% in 2023, 2.9% in 2024 and 2.8% in 2025, so it has stayed near 3% since 2023.

Profit attributable to owners of the parent moved from a net loss of KRW 18.6 billion in 2022 to a profit of KRW 19.5 billion in 2023, rose to KRW 35.4 billion in 2024, and fell 48.9% to KRW 18.1 billion in 2025, so operating profit and owner profit moved in opposite directions in 2025.

Quarterly, the third quarter of 2025 posted revenue of KRW 888.4 billion, operating profit of KRW 38.1 billion and owner profit of KRW 21.8 billion.

The fourth quarter of 2025 fell to revenue of KRW 859.2 billion (down 3.3% quarter on quarter), operating profit of KRW 24.3 billion (down 36.3%) and owner profit of KRW 4.83 billion (down 77.8%).

The first quarter of 2026 showed revenue of KRW 850.4 billion (down 1.0%), operating profit of KRW 19 billion (down 21.6%) and owner profit of KRW 6.51 billion.

The second quarter of 2026 rose to revenue of KRW 893.6 billion (up 5.1%), operating profit of KRW 24.6 billion (up 29.5%) and owner profit of KRW 10.6 billion (up 63.1%), and revenue has grown in the two quarters since.

The third quarter of 2026 reached revenue of KRW 915.1 billion, up 3.0% year on year, but operating profit fell to KRW 3.98 billion, down 83.8% from the prior quarter and 89.6% from a year earlier, and the owner result turned to a net loss of KRW 17.6 billion.

Cost pressures in the third quarter included higher raw material costs, one-off costs including severance pay from the August voluntary retirement program, and logistics costs of KRW 77.6 billion, up 24.2% from KRW 62.5 billion a year earlier, according to the third-quarter reporting. Overseas, higher US freight rates and early-stage costs at a new US plant widened losses.

05

Industry analysis

The food sector environment looks like one where raw material and logistics costs are weighing on profit. The company named higher raw material costs as the first driver of the weaker domestic profitability in the third quarter of 2026, and third-quarter logistics costs rose 24.2% year on year.

On demand, fresh and health-oriented products such as tofu, namul and fresh noodles, along with HMR-centered domestic manufacturing, are cited as growth drivers in company profile information as of June 2026.

Food service distribution grew on new contract catering wins from industrial sites and military units and on concession demand at airports and rest stops.

Overseas, the US, China and Japan units all grew; in the first quarter of 2026 the US unit extended profitability to three consecutive quarters and the Japan unit cut its loss by more than 40%.

In the third quarter, however, higher US freight rates and stabilization costs at a new plant meant profitability improved less than expected.

Compared with listed food peers, Pulmuone operated with a 2.8% operating margin in 2025, a thin margin structure that depends on expanding revenue, so higher cost and logistics expenses can cut profit quickly even as sales grow.

Industry-wide raw material price trends were not confirmed in the sources, so the analysis rests on cost changes shown in the company's own results.

06

Outlook

The plans the company has stated are qualitative. Alongside its third-quarter 2026 disclosure, Pulmuone said it would pursue company-wide efficiency measures, including improving operating efficiency and raising overseas profitability, to build a stable growth base and strengthen profit-focused management.

The August voluntary retirement program covered regular employees with five or more years of service, offering severance of 12 to 24 months of base salary, and it was reflected as a one-off cost in the third quarter.

The size of that one-off cost was not disclosed in the reporting, so whether costs fall in the fourth quarter onward is the first test of profit recovery.

In food service, new industrial and military catering contracts and higher concession sales drove first-quarter growth, and whether those wins continue will support revenue expansion. Overseas, the new US plant is still in its early stabilization phase, so the timing of efficiency gains there is a key variable.

The China unit reportedly grew sales and operating profit on strong noodle and frozen product sales and a wider membership channel. Whether domestic raw material and logistics costs ease, and whether overseas losses do not widen again, are the two pillars of profit recovery. The 2026 annual report will show whether the full-year operating margin exceeds the 2.8% recorded in 2025.

08

Bull factors

Overseas units moving toward profitability

In the first quarter of 2026 the US unit extended profitability to three consecutive quarters after turning a profit in the second half of 2025, and the Japan unit cut its loss by more than 40%. The China unit grew sales and operating profit on strong noodle and frozen product sales.

Overseas operating results returned to around breakeven in the first quarter of 2026. Stable overseas earnings can limit downside to group profit in periods when domestic costs rise, as they did in the third quarter.

Food service wins and concession growth

Food service distribution posted first-quarter 2026 revenue of KRW 254.0 billion, up 10.6%, and operating profit of KRW 6.1 billion, up 28.3%. New wins in core channels such as industrial sites and military catering expanded, and airport lounge and rest-stop concession sales rose 17.7%. Continued contract wins would support group revenue growth.

Signs of profit recovery in 2026

Operating profit rose from KRW 19 billion in the first quarter of 2026 to KRW 24.6 billion in the second, up 29.5%, showing a recovery trend. Operating profit increased for three consecutive years from 2023 to 2025.

The company attributed the third-quarter decline partly to one-off costs, and if those prove non-recurring, profit could return closer to earlier levels. Easing cost pressure is the key condition.

09

Bear factors

Sharp drop in third-quarter profitability

Third-quarter 2026 operating profit was KRW 3.98 billion, down 89.6% year on year and 83.8% from the prior quarter. Profit attributable to owners of the parent swung to a net loss of KRW 17.6 billion from a profit of KRW 21.8 billion a year earlier. Revenue rose to KRW 915.1 billion, but profit did not follow. Owner profit totaled only KRW 4.38 billion over the last four quarters, showing a thin earnings base.

Persistent cost and logistics pressure

The company pointed to higher raw and packaging material costs as a driver of weaker domestic profitability in the third quarter of 2026. Logistics costs reached KRW 77.6 billion, up 24.2% from KRW 62.5 billion a year earlier.

When material and logistics costs rise faster than revenue, sales growth does not carry through to profit. Whether this pressure persists after the fourth quarter is central to the bear case.

Slower overseas margin gains and earnings decline

In the third quarter, overseas revenue grew but profitability improved less than expected. The US unit's losses widened on higher local freight rates and early-stage costs at a new plant.

Profit attributable to owners of the parent fell 48.9% to KRW 18.1 billion in 2025, while operating profit rose 1.5% to KRW 93.2 billion. If overseas profitability weakens again, group profit recovery could be delayed.

Risk factors

Cost and logistics

Rising raw material and logistics costs could repeat the pattern of revenue growth without profit growth. Third-quarter logistics costs rose 24.2% year on year. Raw material and overseas freight movements are difficult for the company to control, and margins depend on whether and when price increases pass through.

One-off costs and restructuring

Severance costs from the August voluntary retirement program were reflected as one-off costs in the third quarter, and their total size was not confirmed in the reporting. Additional efficiency costs or delayed stabilization at overseas plants could push back the timing of margin recovery. Whether such costs recur will be visible in results after the fourth quarter.

Balance sheet and earnings base

At the end of 2025, total liabilities were KRW 1.73 trillion against total equity of KRW 617.7 billion, a debt-to-equity ratio of 280.1%. Operating cash flow rose from KRW 94.8 billion in 2022 to KRW 163.7 billion in 2025 but declined after 2024.

With owner profit totaling only KRW 4.38 billion over the last four quarters, the earnings base is thin against a large debt load. Higher interest or repayment needs could limit financial flexibility.

Overall view

Pulmuone's third-quarter 2026 revenue rose 3.0% year on year to KRW 915.1 billion, but operating profit fell 89.6% to KRW 3.98 billion and profit attributable to owners of the parent swung to a net loss of KRW 17.6 billion.

On an annual basis, revenue and operating profit both rose for three consecutive years from 2023 to 2025, and the operating margin was 2.8% in 2025. Profit attributable to owners of the parent fell 48.9% to KRW 18.1 billion in 2025, a gap with operating profit trends.

Bullish points include overseas units moving toward profitability, growing food service contract wins, and operating profit recovering from the first to the second quarter of 2026.

Bearish points include raw material and logistics costs, one-off restructuring charges, and slower stabilization at a new overseas plant, which together weigh on the third quarter.

Owner profit totaled only KRW 4.38 billion over the last four quarters, so the earnings base is thin and the share price trades at a high multiple against it, making the pace of profit recovery the key variable.

The next checkpoints are the fourth-quarter 2026 preliminary results, the 2026 annual report and first-quarter 2027 results, where the recovery of the operating margin and the end of one-off costs will determine the direction of profitability.
