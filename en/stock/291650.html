02

Business structure

Choolip & Sciences, which was spun off from POSTECH and formerly traded as Aptamer Sciences, builds its business around aptamers and a lung cancer early-detection kit as its flagship product.

The company describes an integrated platform running from aptamer discovery to applied product development, and lists custom aptamer discovery and manufacturing services among its business purposes.

In diagnostics, it markets a lung cancer kit built on its AptoMIA multi-marker technology, which it presents as the world's first lung cancer early-detection kit of its kind.

It argues that X-ray and CT imaging carry high false-positive rates, and that a blood-based, non-invasive test used as an adjunct could raise accuracy by more than 20%, a claim the company itself makes.

On the therapeutics side, it focuses on anticancer pipelines that attach small-molecule drugs to aptamers in an Aptamer Drug Conjugate (ApDC) platform. The company is also developing chimeric aptamers combined with antibodies, peptides and oligonucleotides.

It positions this against antibody-drug conjugates such as Trodelvy and Enhertu, arguing that aptamer-based ApDCs could offer superior properties. A contract research (CRO) service is part of its stated plan to diversify revenue.

The company also describes a BBB shuttle aptamer designed to carry antibodies, peptides and siRNA across the blood-brain barrier. Its own profile says it is in discussions with several global pharmaceutical companies on joint development and early technology transfer.

Several small Korean diagnostics companies are grouped with it as similar companies on third-party profile sites.

04

Earnings analysis

Annual sales moved in two directions. Revenue fell from KRW 358 million in 2022 to KRW 243 million in 2023, a 32.1% decline, then rose to KRW 1.08 billion in 2024 (up 345.3%) and KRW 3.5 billion in 2025 (up 223.7%), growing for two consecutive years.

Of the 2022 to 2025 period, 2025 was the largest year and 2023 the smallest. Operating losses were KRW 8.32 billion in 2022, KRW 12.2 billion in 2023, KRW 7.05 billion in 2024 and KRW 7.8 billion in 2025, four consecutive years of operating losses in which the loss narrowed in 2024 and widened again in 2025.

The operating margin moved from -2,322.3% in 2022 and -5,019.5% in 2023 to -651.2% in 2024 and -222.6% in 2025. That improvement reflects sales growing faster than losses, not a turn to profit.

Attributable net losses were KRW 7.12 billion in 2022, KRW 13.6 billion in 2023, KRW 8.7 billion in 2024 and KRW 8.62 billion in 2025, and the net loss including non-controlling interests was disclosed at the same levels.

Operating cash outflows were KRW 5.78 billion in 2022, KRW 11.2 billion in 2023, KRW 5.9 billion in 2024 and KRW 9.67 billion in 2025. At the end of 2025, total equity was KRW 28.2 billion, all of it attributable to controlling shareholders, against total liabilities of KRW 12.9 billion.

The debt ratio went from 56.2% in 2022 to 110.2% in 2023, 25.7% in 2024 and 45.6% in 2025. Quarterly sales were KRW 611 million in Q2 2025, KRW 544 million in Q3, KRW 1.76 billion in Q4, KRW 707 million in Q1 2026 and KRW 2.39 billion in Q2 2026, changing direction sharply from quarter to quarter.

The Q2 2026 operating loss of KRW 3.08 billion widened from both the prior quarter and the year-earlier quarter, and the attributable net loss of KRW 4.26 billion was the largest of the last five quarters.

Sales rising while the loss widened means costs grew faster than revenue; which cost lines drove this and whether one-offs were involved belongs to the notes of the quarterly report.

Attributable net losses over the last four quarters total KRW 11.7 billion, and operating cash outflows every year point to a recurring reliance on outside funding.

05

Industry analysis

Aptamers are nucleic-acid or peptide-type molecules that bind target proteins, and the company describes them as a platform for both diagnostic kits and therapeutics.

In anticancer work, antibody-drug conjugates such as Trodelvy and Enhertu have drawn clinical attention, and the company argues that its aptamer-based ApDC could show better properties than those products.

That competitive claim still rests more on technical argument than on clinical results, so proof of real advantage in the conjugate market is at an early stage. In diagnostics, the company's logic is that imaging has a high false-positive problem, which creates demand for blood-based adjunct testing.

Korea has many small diagnostics companies making infectious disease and cancer test kits, and the similar-company lists on third-party profile sites group several of them under bio and medical diagnostics.

Figures on the size, share and supply-demand of the company's end markets are not presented in its disclosure tables, so the cycle position can only be judged in qualitative terms. The company's position rests more on its development pipeline and technology platform than on commercial manufacturing scale.

Sales grew in 2025 and in Q2 2026, but the product or contract amounts behind that growth are not broken out in the disclosure tables, so reading it as a change in market share would be premature.

Sector-wide funding conditions can also affect the terms on which small development-stage companies raise equity and convertible bonds, so industry factors and company-specific factors need to be separated.

Overall view

Choolip & Sciences is a small biotech that combines a lung cancer diagnostic kit, an anticancer drug pipeline and contract research services on an aptamer platform. On the disclosed figures, sales rose in both 2024 and 2025 to KRW 3.5 billion, and Q2 2026 sales of KRW 2.39 billion were up 291.5% year on year.

Operating losses, however, have run for four consecutive years from 2022 through 2025, and the company has posted losses for five straight quarters through Q2 2026. The widening of the Q2 2026 operating loss to KRW 3.08 billion shows that sales growth alone has not yet changed the loss structure.
