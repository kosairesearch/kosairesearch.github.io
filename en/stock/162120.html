Shifting weight from display test gear to chip test parts

02

Business structure

LUKEN Technologies sells LCD and OLED panel inspection equipment, inspection parts, and semiconductor inspection parts as its core lines. In 2024, inspection equipment generated KRW 11.0 billion, or 52.6% of revenue, while inspection parts brought in KRW 6.3 billion (30.0%) and services KRW 2.3 billion (10.7%).

Semiconductor parts contributed KRW 1.4 billion, or 6.7%. Equipment sales fell 65.1% from KRW 31.5 billion in 2023, making them the largest driver of earnings swings. Inspection parts are consumables that recur as customer lines run, and the company is pursuing a plan to lift their share above 50% over the long term.

Its semiconductor push has rested on HBM test socket and vertical probe card development, a 2024 supplier registration with Samsung Electronics, and the 2024 acquisition of the TIU business from ISC.

On competition, the company points to its in-house unmanned automatic inspection algorithms, which it applies in mass production. Its revenue scale is, however, small compared with several inspection equipment peers.

Export sales reached KRW 15.3 billion in 2024, or 73.2% of the total, which leaves it exposed to overseas customers' investment cycles. At a 2025 investor briefing, the CEO said display accounted for 80% to 90% of the prior revenue base.

03

Recent trends

On a disclosed-results basis, 2023 revenue was KRW 38.5 billion with an operating profit of KRW 1.7 billion.

Revenue in 2024 fell 45.5% to KRW 21.0 billion, and the operating loss was reported in a range of KRW 3.3 billion to 3.4 billion, depending on the source. 2025 revenue was about KRW 17.3 billion, down 17.5% from the prior year, while the operating loss narrowed to about KRW 1.2 billion.

That loss was smaller than the KRW 1.8 billion forecast that Korea IR Consortium research gave in October 2025. Some data sources put the 2025 net loss at about KRW 2.6 billion. The 2024 decline is mainly attributed to delayed capital spending at major display customers and postponed investment by Chinese panel makers.

The share price closed at KRW 3,890 on October 8, 2026, within a 52-week range of KRW 2,260 to KRW 4,590. As of October 2026, the one-year share price change was reported at 33.22%. Daily trading value of roughly KRW 1 million indicates thin liquidity.

04

Outlook

The outlook depends on two variables: whether display capital spending recovers and how quickly semiconductor inspection parts move into mass production. At a 2025 investor briefing, the company said it expects its interface boards and test sockets to be standard equipment in a major test-equipment maker's systems.

The CEO said annual revenue of KRW 200.0 billion would be possible if supply of vertical probe cards and HBM test sockets is secured, though this is a management expectation. The company has set a target of trading on the KOSDAQ market in May to June 2027.

Korea IR Consortium research flagged the possibility that the pace of the semiconductor transition could be somewhat slower than planned. The narrowing of the 2025 operating loss to about KRW 1.2 billion is read as an early sign of improved financial capacity.

Whether HBM inspection-part orders convert into firm revenue is the next checkpoint. Whether the TIU business shifts to mass-production models is also cited as a variable for revenue improvement.

05

Bull factors

Progress in customer qualification for chip test parts

The company was registered as a supplier to Samsung Electronics in 2024, and the TIU business it acquired from ISC was reported to have generated about KRW 1.5 billion in sales after the deal. Customer registration news from China followed in 2025.

Korea IR Consortium research expects the HBM3E vertical probe card to contribute revenue once customer evaluation is complete. Semiconductor sales remain small, but the stacking of qualification steps is cited as a bullish factor.

Possible cost advantage from vertical integration

The company says it has secured the technology to produce test sockets and probe cards in-house. Korea IR Consortium research points to the possibility that vertical integration could strengthen cost competitiveness and customer responsiveness.

Because it supplies both equipment and parts, its room to tailor offerings to customers is considered significant. The report notes, however, that these effects are emerging gradually.

Narrower losses and in-house technology differentiation

The 2025 operating loss of about KRW 1.2 billion was smaller than the KRW 3.3 to 3.4 billion loss in 2024. It was also below the KRW 1.8 billion loss that Korea IR Consortium research had estimated for 2025.

The company highlights its in-house unmanned inspection algorithms, applied in mass production, as a point of differentiation. Inspection parts sales rose 16.6% in 2024 to KRW 6.3 billion from KRW 5.4 billion a year earlier.

06

Bear factors

High sensitivity to display capital spending swings

Inspection equipment sales in 2024 were KRW 11.0 billion, down 65.1% from KRW 31.5 billion a year earlier. Delayed capacity investment by major customers and postponed spending by Chinese panel makers were cited as causes. At a 2025 investor briefing, the CEO said display made up 80% to 90% of the prior revenue base. Display market conditions still determine earnings more directly than the semiconductor transition does.

Semiconductors still a small share of revenue

Semiconductor parts sales were KRW 1.4 billion in 2024, or 6.7% of revenue. Korea IR Consortium research flagged a risk that the semiconductor transition could take longer than originally planned. The CEO projected roughly KRW 3.0 billion in semiconductor-related sales for 2025, but this remains a company estimate. With revenue mid-transition, earnings volatility persists.

Limited financial capacity and thin liquidity

Morningstar data show a current ratio of 0.49 and a negative interest coverage ratio. Daily trading value of roughly KRW 1 million indicates thin share liquidity. Financial stability scores from a financial rating service also rank relatively low.

07

Risk factors

Macro

Display and semiconductor capital spending depends on global demand and panel makers' expansion plans. Exports made up 73.2% of 2024 revenue, so the company is highly exposed to exchange rates and overseas customer investment flows. Postponed spending by Chinese panel makers has previously hit results directly.

Business

If customer qualification and mass-production transfer for semiconductor inspection parts are delayed, the benefit of the business shift will also slip. Korea IR Consortium research mentioned possible HBM inspection-part orders, but these are not yet firm revenue. Whether the cooperation with a major test-equipment maker turns into actual supply sales is a key variable.

Financial & liquidity

The company remained loss-making in 2025, and normalized ROE was -21.52%. A current ratio of 0.49 indicates limited room to cover short-term obligations. Low trading value could make it harder to execute share trades smoothly.

08

Overall view

LUKEN Technologies is in a transition from a display-equipment-centered structure toward one centered on semiconductor inspection parts. The 2025 operating loss narrowed to about KRW 1.2 billion from 2024, but a return to profit remains some distance away.

Bullish points include the Samsung Electronics supplier registration, HBM-related product development, and in-house inspection algorithms.

Bearish points include high sensitivity to display capital spending, the still-small semiconductor share of revenue, and limited financial capacity reflected in a current ratio of 0.49.

Korea IR Consortium research said the transition could build a differentiated competitive position if it converts into results, while also flagging the risk of a slower pace. The company has set targets of expanding semiconductor inspection-part sales and pursuing a KOSDAQ listing transfer in 2027.

The outcome of both paths is likely to depend on whether semiconductor orders move into mass production and whether display customers resume investment. The pace of the business transition and financial capacity are therefore the two main lenses for this company.
