Report overview

Top Deck-Plate Maker Eyes Rebound via US Orders

Dukshinepc, a leading Korean deck-plate maker, is expanding orders tied to semiconductor construction sites, but its operating loss in 2025 and a return to loss in the second quarter of 2026 show profitability has not yet been confirmed to recover.

  4. 4

02

Business structure

Dukshinepc was established in 1990 and operates primarily in the manufacturing and installation of deck plates, a key construction material. Its products are divided into integrated deck plates, form decks, and other components, with integrated deck plates accounting for the large majority of revenue.

Within that category, its flagship 'Speed Deck' product, which simultaneously serves as formwork and rebar support, forms the core of sales, supplemented by the detachable 'Eco Deck' and insulated 'Insu Deck' lines.

Production is centered on its plant in Cheonan, South Chungcheong Province, and a second plant in Gunsan, with overseas footholds including a Vietnam facility and a secured production site near Atlanta, Georgia in the United States.

Company materials state that it holds the No.1 market share position in Korea's deck-plate market.

Major clients include large construction and engineering firms such as Samsung C&T, Samsung Electronics, and Samsung E&A, and its customer base recently expanded to include Amkor Technology, the world's second-largest OSAT (outsourced semiconductor assembly and test) company, at its Arizona plant.

Competition centers on quality and technical differentiation, including automated production lines and overseas certifications such as the US UL certification, within a market populated by numerous domestic deck-plate makers.

More recently, the company broadened its articles of incorporation to add new business areas such as real estate development and golf course construction through a subsidiary, beyond its core deck-plate operations.

04

Earnings analysis

Consolidated revenue rose from KRW 201.6 billion in 2022 to a peak of KRW 218.1 billion in 2023 (up 8.2%), then declined for two consecutive years to KRW 146.6 billion in 2024 (down 32.8%) and KRW 106.5 billion in 2025 (down 27.4%).

Operating profit followed a similar pattern, rising from KRW 20.2 billion in 2022 to KRW 28.9 billion in 2023 (up 43.3%), then falling to KRW 10.7 billion in 2024 (down 62.9%) before swinging to an operating loss of KRW 104 million in 2025.

Net income attributable to controlling shareholders also peaked at KRW 24.1 billion in 2023 (up 137.2%) before falling for two straight years to KRW 11.3 billion in 2024 (down 53.2%) and KRW 1.55 billion in 2025 (down 86.3%).

The operating margin held in double digits at 10.0% in 2022 and 13.3% in 2023, then dropped to 7.3% in 2024 and turned negative at -0.1% in 2025, reflecting a marked deterioration in profitability.

On a quarterly basis, the operating loss widened from KRW 69.38 million in the second quarter of 2025 to KRW 1.34 billion in the third quarter, before narrowing to KRW 803 million in the fourth quarter.

The company returned to an operating profit of KRW 19.57 million in the first quarter of 2026, but swung back to an operating loss of KRW 371 million in the second quarter, a wider loss than the same period a year earlier.

Net income attributable to controlling shareholders turned positive at KRW 1.26 billion in the fourth quarter of 2025, fell sharply to KRW 68.49 million in the first quarter of 2026, and reverted to a net loss of KRW 118 million in the second quarter.

Over the most recent four quarters (Q3 2025 to Q2 2026), cumulative net losses attributable to controlling shareholders totaled KRW 515 million, while operating cash flow turned negative at -KRW 17.5 billion in 2025, a reversal from KRW 25 billion in 2024 that points to increased working-capital pressure.

05

Industry analysis

Deck plates serve the dual function of formwork and rebar support at construction sites, contributing to shorter construction periods and lower costs, and the segment is closely tied to housing and commercial construction activity in Korea.

The revenue decline following the 2023 peak can be interpreted as a reflection of a broader slowdown in domestic construction orders and starts.

More recently, however, advanced facility construction such as semiconductor fabs and data centers has emerged as a new demand driver, with large-scale projects such as Samsung Electronics' Pyeongtaek P5 campus supporting deck-plate demand.

Overseas, US policy to strengthen the domestic semiconductor supply chain has spurred related facility investment, and Amkor Technology reportedly expanded its planned investment at its Peoria, Arizona production complex from an initial $2 billion to $7 billion.

Korea's deck-plate market is populated by numerous small and mid-sized manufacturers, and Dukshinepc positions its automated production lines and its status as the sole domestic holder of US UL certification as key competitive advantages.

The pace of a broader construction-market recovery, alongside the semiconductor and industrial-facility investment cycle, is likely to remain a key variable differentiating performance among industry players going forward.

06

Outlook

The company has been expanding semiconductor-related construction orders through 2026.

In February it signed a supply contract worth roughly KRW 12.9 billion with Samsung E&A for the complex building at the Pyeongtaek P5 campus, and in March it secured an additional installation contract worth roughly KRW 15.6 billion with Samsung C&T for the P5 FAB building, broadening its participation in the P5 project.

Overseas, in February it signed a contract to supply roughly 70,460 square meters of Speed Deck to Amkor Technology's semiconductor plant in Arizona, and in June it began main construction at that site, which it said would take approximately ten months to complete.

The company has said that, building on this track record, it is in discussions for additional orders tied to semiconductor and industrial-facility projects in the United States, and it is also preparing to break ground on a local plant using the production site it secured near Atlanta, Georgia.

Separately from its core business, however, capital is being deployed into real estate development and golf course construction through a subsidiary via equity investment and loans, making the timing of monetization for these new ventures, and its balance against financial burden, a point worth watching.

With quarterly results swinging between profit and loss through the second quarter of 2026, whether semiconductor-related orders begin contributing meaningfully to revenue in the latter part of the year is likely to be a key determinant of the earnings trajectory.

Bull factors

New Exposure to North American Semiconductor Construction

The company secured its first track record in the North American semiconductor construction market by supplying deck plates and beginning main construction at Amkor Technology's Arizona semiconductor plant.

As the client is expanding the scale of this large project, there is potential for additional order opportunities in subsequent phases. Being the first in the domestic industry to obtain US UL certification underpins its overseas order expansion.

Expanding Participation in Samsung's Pyeongtaek P5 Project

Through successive contracts with Samsung E&A and Samsung C&T, the company has secured participation in both the complex building and FAB building phases of the Pyeongtaek P5 project. The company has said it expects additional orders as further construction phases at the site are scheduled to be put out for bid.

Expanding exposure to large semiconductor investment projects is a positive factor for securing a more stable revenue base.

Valuation Below Net Asset Value

The price-to-book ratio is below 1x and sits toward the lower end of the historical trading band, meaning the shares trade at a discount to book value. Total equity at the end of 2025 stood at KRW 125.2 billion, well above the current market capitalization, indicating the net asset base itself remains intact. This comparison, however, does not presuppose an earnings recovery and requires independent judgment.

Bear factors

Continued Profitability Deterioration

Following a full-year operating loss in 2025, quarterly results in 2026 have continued to swing between profit and loss, without a clear sign of a sustained earnings recovery.

The operating loss in the second quarter of 2026 widened even compared with the same period a year earlier, showing that revenue growth has not translated directly into improved profitability. If cost burdens or structural cost issues remain unresolved, earnings volatility could persist.

Financial Strain from Capital Allocation Outside the Core Business

Capital is being deployed via equity investment and loans into real estate development and golf course construction through a subsidiary, and according to related reporting, borrowings rose sharply from KRW 15.1 billion at the prior year-end to KRW 66.2 billion.

The consolidated debt-to-equity ratio also rose from 27.9% to 64.6% in 2025. With the monetization timeline for the new businesses uncertain, there is a risk that financial burden expands first.

Operating Cash Flow Turned Negative

Operating cash flow turned negative at -KRW 17.5 billion in 2025, a reversal from KRW 25 billion in 2024. A combination of lower revenue and changes in working capital, such as receivables and inventory, may have contributed to this shift. A continued inability to generate cash from operations could increase reliance on external financing.

Risk factors

Sensitivity to the Construction Cycle

Deck-plate revenue is directly tied to the volume of domestic building and construction starts, so a continued slowdown in the residential and commercial construction cycle could delay a revenue recovery. The two consecutive years of revenue decline following the 2023 peak illustrate this sensitivity. If semiconductor and industrial-facility orders fail to offset this, overall revenue could contract further.

Execution Risk in Overseas Business

Main construction at the Arizona site is expected to take about ten months, and as an early large-scale overseas project, any delays or quality and safety issues could affect the company's ability to secure follow-on orders.

Breaking ground on a local plant using the Georgia production site remains at an in-progress stage, raising the possibility of a longer investment payback period until completion and operation. Foreign-exchange fluctuations and local permitting are among the variables inherent to overseas operations.

Risk from Diversification into New Businesses

Diversification into real estate development and golf course construction, areas unrelated to the core deck-plate business, could dilute the focus of capital allocation.

Because the related business purposes were only recently added through a 2026 amendment to the articles of incorporation, the monetization model and payback timeline have not yet been sufficiently tested in the market.

If the scale of equity investment and loans to the subsidiary continues to grow, it could constrain the parent company's financial flexibility.

Overall view

Dukshinepc has built a long track record in Korea's deck-plate market and has recently broadened its order base to large-scale semiconductor construction sites, including Amkor Technology's US plant and Samsung Electronics' Pyeongtaek P5 project.

However, revenue and profit declined for two consecutive years after peaking in 2023, the company swung to a full-year operating loss in 2025, and results in 2026 have continued to alternate between profit and loss on a quarterly basis.

Its move into real estate and golf course development through a subsidiary could become a new growth driver, but it comes with a corresponding rise in borrowings and financial burden.

A 5-for-1 stock consolidation completed in August 2026 changed the share count and per-share price level, limiting simple comparison with historical share prices.

The price-to-book ratio trades toward the lower end of its historical range, but with four consecutive quarters of net losses attributable to controlling shareholders, a conventional earnings-based assessment remains difficult.

Whether the expansion in North American orders and the semiconductor construction cycle translate into meaningful revenue contribution, and how well the financial burden from new business lines is managed, appear to be the key variables for gauging future performance and financial structure.
