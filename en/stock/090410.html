02

Business structure

Dukshinepc is a building materials maker whose core business is integrated deck plates for construction. A deck plate serves as a floor slab in buildings and also acts as formwork when concrete is poured.

Based on the sales mix compiled by AlphaSquare, integrated deck products accounted for 97.32%, merchandise for 1.00%, foam deck products for 0.66%, raw materials for 0.63%, and other items for 0.38%. Its main product is Speedeck, and it also sells the eco-friendly EcoDeck.

Production bases are the Cheonan plant and the Gunsan No. 2 plant, and a Vietnam plant was set up in 2015. Overseas subsidiaries were established in the United States in 2023, the Philippines in 2024, and Türkiye in November 2024. The company said it became the first and only domestic deck plate maker to obtain U.S.

UL certification. In February 2026, a contract to supply 70,460 square meters of Speedeck to an Amkor semiconductor plant was disclosed. A domestic order is a KRW 13.398 billion Sejong deck works contract with Samsung E&A, disclosed in late October 2026, equal to 12.6% of consolidated sales at the end of 2025.

A corporate information platform describes the company as holding the number one domestic market share.

In March 2026 the representative structure changed to joint CEOs Yoo Hee-sung and Oh Jong-nyeong, and at the same shareholder meeting the articles of incorporation added business purposes such as real estate development and sales and sports facilities.

04

Earnings analysis

Annual results peaked in 2023 and turned down afterward. Sales rose from KRW 201.6 billion in 2022 to KRW 218.1 billion in 2023, an 8.2% increase, but then fell 32.8% to KRW 146.6 billion in 2024 and 27.4% to KRW 106.5 billion in 2025, a decline in both 2024 and 2025.

Operating profit was KRW 28.9 billion in 2023, a 43.3% increase and the largest year in the period, before dropping 62.9% to KRW 10.7 billion in 2024 and turning into an operating loss of KRW 104 million in 2025. The operating margin ran at 10.0% in 2022, 13.3% in 2023, 7.3% in 2024, and -0.1% in 2025.

Net profit attributable to controlling shareholders went from KRW 24.1 billion in 2023, up 137.2%, to KRW 11.3 billion in 2024, down 53.2%, and KRW 1.55 billion in 2025, down 86.3%.

Net income including non-controlling interests was KRW 1.4 billion in 2025, a different figure from profit attributable to controlling shareholders. Operating cash flow fell from KRW 40.9 billion in 2023 and KRW 25 billion in 2024 to -KRW 17.5 billion in 2025. The debt ratio rose from 27.9% in 2024 to 64.6% in 2025.

According to the disclosure approved at the March 2026 shareholder meeting, consolidated total liabilities were KRW 34.9 billion at the end of 2024, and at the end of 2025 total liabilities were KRW 80.8 billion, total equity was KRW 125.2 billion, and equity attributable to controlling shareholders was KRW 125.4 billion.

Quarterly results began with Q3 2025 sales of KRW 24.9 billion, an operating loss of KRW 1.34 billion, and a loss attributable to controlling shareholders of KRW 1.73 billion.

Q4 2025 sales were KRW 27.4 billion, up 10.1% from the prior quarter, with an operating loss of KRW 803 million that narrowed, and a controlling-shareholder profit of KRW 1.26 billion that turned positive.

Q1 2026 sales of KRW 25.7 billion, down 6.2% quarter on quarter, marked the low point, with operating profit of KRW 19.57 million. Q2 2026 sales rose 26.6% to KRW 32.5 billion, but the company posted an operating loss of KRW 371 million and a controlling-shareholder net loss of KRW 118 million.

Q3 2026 sales reached KRW 40.6 billion, up 25.0% quarter on quarter and 63.5% year on year, with operating profit of KRW 479 million, yet a controlling-shareholder net loss of KRW 39.17 million remained, indicating that items below operating profit weighed on net results.

The company has posted losses for two consecutive quarters, from Q2 to Q3 2026. Net profit attributable to controlling shareholders for the last four quarters, from Q4 2025 to Q3 2026, totals KRW 1.17 billion. Sales rose for two consecutive quarters, but operating profit was positive only in Q1 and Q3 2026.

05

Industry analysis

Deck plates are a floor slab material, so results track the construction cycle closely. An equity information site summarized that the slump in construction activity is continuing in the short term. The same source noted that the trend toward taller new buildings could expand adoption of integrated deck plates.

The company expects the development of EcoDeck, a non-removal deck plate, to create new demand. Reports in the industry described weak demand and falling selling prices as the reason peer listed deck makers posted poor results in Q3 2025.

Overseas, according to the company, rising U.S. construction material and labor costs are increasing demand for construction efficiency and safety management. The company names semiconductor back-end plants and data centers as key high-value facility markets.

On competition, the company highlights that it is the first and only domestic deck plate maker to hold U.S. UL certification. Whether that certification converts into orders depends on how the order disclosures accumulate.

One semiconductor facility supply contract has been disclosed, and the share of North American revenue has not been confirmed in the filings reviewed. In the domestic market, public and private construction orders continue to drive results.

06

Outlook

The confirmed schedule is concentrated in the U.S. semiconductor project and large domestic construction work. The main construction phase of the first Amkor supply order in Arizona is expected to take about 10 months, and it was reported as started in late June 2026.

The company said that by completing this work successfully it will build local credibility and gradually expand its participation in follow-on projects.

CEO Yoo Hee-sung said in February 2026 that talks are under way on a number of additional U.S. construction projects and that the company plans to widen order opportunities step by step based on verified construction track records.

Executives from a U.S. builder and a steel supplier visited the Korean plant to check production equipment and quality control, as noted in the company's disclosure.

The production site near Atlanta, Georgia was secured in 2023, and the factory design was reported as nearly complete with preparations for construction under way, although the construction start date has not been confirmed.

A domestic order is the Sejong deck works contract disclosed in late October 2026, running through June 30, 2027.

The March 2026 charter amendment broadened business purposes to real estate development and sales, golf courses, and sports facilities, and the contribution of these businesses to reported results has not yet been confirmed in the filings.

Depending on whether future orders concentrate on North American semiconductor work or domestic construction volume, the revenue mix could shift. The 2026 annual sales and operating margin will be confirmed in the next annual report.
