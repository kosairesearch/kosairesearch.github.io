Report overview

Liquor Growth Cushions Beverage and Overseas Earnings Slump

Lotte Chilsung Beverage's third-quarter 2026 operating profit fell 37.9% year on year as beverage and overseas margins weakened, while fast-growing RTD liquor partly cushioned the decline.

02

Business structure

Lotte Chilsung Beverage operates across beverages, liquor and overseas subsidiaries. Standalone third-quarter 2026 revenue was KRW 513.3 billion for beverages and KRW 198.2 billion for liquor, with operating profit of KRW 29 billion and KRW 17.2 billion respectively.

The overseas segment, which groups foreign subsidiaries, posted revenue of KRW 380.6 billion and operating profit of KRW 10.7 billion in the same quarter. Segment figures are on a standalone or entity basis and do not sum to consolidated revenue of KRW 1.05 trillion.

The beverage portfolio centers on carbonated drinks such as Chilsung Cider and Pepsi, plus coffee, sports drinks and water, while exports of Milkis and Rex-branded drinks grew 6.1%.

In liquor, the Soonhari Jin fruit-flavored sparkling line drove RTD sales up 119.6% in the third quarter, with the company citing an expanded lineup including yuja, sangria and unsweetened clear lemon variants. Soju sales rose 2.2% on Cheoeum Cheoreom and Saeroo, and cheongju rose 1.9% on Suboku One Cup.

In October 2026 the company relaunched Captain Q for the first time in 11 years after discontinuing it in 2015. Domestic beverage competition intensified, and sales declined across carbonated, coffee and sports drink categories.

04

Earnings analysis

Annual revenue rose from KRW 2.84 trillion in 2022 to KRW 3.22 trillion in 2023 (up 13.5%) and KRW 4.02 trillion in 2024 (up 24.8%), then fell to KRW 3.97 trillion in 2025 (down 1.3%). Of the 2022 to 2025 years, 2024 was the largest.

Operating profit went from KRW 222.9 billion in 2022 to KRW 210.7 billion in 2023 (down 5.5%), KRW 184.9 billion in 2024 (down 12.2%) and KRW 167.2 billion in 2025 (down 9.6%), a decline in each of the three years from 2023 to 2025.

Revenue grew while profit shrank, so the operating margin fell from 7.8% in 2022 to 6.5%, 4.6% and 4.2% in 2025.

Profit attributable to controlling shareholders peaked at KRW 165.9 billion in 2023 (up 26.5%), then fell to KRW 59.1 billion in 2024 (down 64.4%) and KRW 47.2 billion in 2025 (down 20.1%), a two-year decline.

Net income including non-controlling interests was KRW 51.2 billion in 2025, a different figure from profit attributable to controlling shareholders.

At end-2025, total equity was KRW 1.6 trillion, of which KRW 1.52 trillion belonged to controlling shareholders, and total liabilities were KRW 2.68 trillion, a debt ratio of 167.7%. The debt ratio rose from 161.7% in 2022 to 177.0% in both 2023 and 2024 before easing in 2025.

Operating cash flow was KRW 268.3 billion in 2022, KRW 267.7 billion in 2023, KRW 325.2 billion in 2024 and KRW 353.6 billion in 2025, rising after 2024.

Quarterly, operating profit of KRW 91.8 billion in the third quarter of 2025 was followed by an operating loss of KRW 12 billion in the fourth quarter, then a profit of KRW 47.8 billion in the first quarter of 2026, KRW 55.8 billion in the second and KRW 57 billion in the third.

The third-quarter 2026 figure was down 37.9% year on year, and first-to-third-quarter 2026 cumulative operating profit of KRW 160.6 billion was down 10.4%, per the preliminary announcement.

05

Industry analysis

The beverage market is facing weak domestic consumption and intensifying domestic competition at the same time. Third-quarter 2026 beverage declines spanned carbonated drinks, coffee and sports drinks, and domestic beverage sales fell 5.8% year on year to KRW 469.6 billion.

Water sales dropped 10.5%, juice 8.5% and coffee 9.8%. Raw material and logistics costs added pressure, and the company cited higher oil prices tied to Middle East conflict and surging sea freight costs. Beverage exports, however, rose 6.1% to KRW 40 billion, supported by demand for Milkis and Rex in overseas markets.

Liquor has a different trajectory: RTD growth was strong on the expanded fruit-flavored sparkling lineup, while soju rose 2.2% and cheongju 1.9%. Beverage demand has not yet shown a confirmed domestic recovery, whereas liquor remains in a growth phase led by RTD. Given beverages' weight in group results, this divergence is the key variable for consolidated earnings.

06

Outlook

The company set 2026 full-year guidance at KRW 4.08 trillion in revenue and KRW 185 billion in operating profit, lowering the earlier outlook by KRW 20 billion and KRW 15 billion respectively. The operating profit target is 10.7% above 2025 results of KRW 167.2 billion.

With first-to-third-quarter cumulative operating profit of KRW 160.6 billion, the fourth quarter needs roughly KRW 24.4 billion or more to meet the target.

In beverages, the company plans a fourth-quarter rebound from new products launched in the third quarter, including Chilsung Cider RICH and Pepsi Prebiotic Zero Sugar Yogurt. It is also pursuing domestic restructuring and cost efficiency through a innovation task force set up in September 2026.

In liquor, the company expects Soonhari Jin's 2026 sales to exceed twice its full-year 2025 sales. Captain Q, relaunched in October 2026, sold out its limited 12,000-set treasure box package within a day of leaving the factory, and the company expects it to contribute to fourth-quarter liquor growth.

These are company projections rather than externally verified figures. Whether guidance is met depends on the sales pace of fourth-quarter beverage launches and the results of cost efficiency measures.

08

Bull factors

Rapid RTD liquor growth

RTD sales rose 119.6% year on year in the third quarter of 2026. The company attributes this to relaunching its fruit-flavored sparkling brand Soonhari Jin and widening the lineup. It expects Soonhari Jin's 2026 sales to exceed twice its full-year 2025 sales. Standalone liquor operating profit rose 24.1% to KRW 17.2 billion, partly offsetting the beverage slump.

A broad liquor portfolio beyond RTD

Soju sales rose 2.2% on Cheoeum Cheoreom and Saeroo, and cheongju sales grew 1.9% led by Suboku One Cup. Captain Q, relaunched in October 2026, sold out its limited package within a day, according to the company. Growth from categories beyond RTD is a supporting factor on the bull side.

Cash generation and export momentum

Operating cash flow rose from KRW 268.3 billion in 2022 to KRW 353.6 billion in 2025. Cash generation expanded even as profit declined, which is a financial strength. Beverage export sales grew 6.1% to KRW 40 billion, confirming overseas demand for Milkis and Rex.

09

Bear factors

Weak domestic beverage demand

Standalone beverage revenue in the third quarter of 2026 fell 4.7% to KRW 513.3 billion, and operating profit fell 47.4% to KRW 29 billion. Sales declined across carbonated, coffee and sports drink categories, and domestic beverage sales were down 5.8%. The company cited beverage and overseas weakness as the main reasons for the consolidated profit decline.

Overseas subsidiaries under margin pressure

The overseas segment's third-quarter 2026 revenue fell 0.9% to KRW 380.6 billion, and its operating profit fell 48.1% to KRW 10.7 billion. The company said higher raw material prices and logistics costs tied to high oil prices and geopolitical uncertainty weighed on overseas subsidiaries. Beverage exports grew, but overseas entity profitability deteriorated.

Multi-year profit decline and guidance cut

Operating profit declined three years in a row from 2023 to 2025, and profit attributable to controlling shareholders fell for two consecutive years in 2024 and 2025. The 2026 operating profit guidance was cut by KRW 15 billion. First-to-third-quarter cumulative operating profit of KRW 160.6 billion was down 10.4% year on year.

10

Risk factors

Raw materials, logistics and oil prices

Higher oil prices tied to Middle East conflict pushed up raw material costs and sea freight rates together. The company attributed weaker beverage and overseas profitability to rising input prices. How much of the cost increase is passed through to product prices will show up in later quarterly results.

Domestic demand and competition

A prolonged domestic consumption slowdown led to sales declines across all beverage categories. Intensifying competition in the domestic beverage market creates a structure in which sales battles weigh on profitability.

The company set up an innovation task force in September 2026 to improve its domestic business, but results have not yet shown the effect.

Concentration on RTD and restructuring

Much of the liquor growth depends on RTD lines such as Soonhari Jin. High dependence on a single brand family can make results sensitive to shifts in consumer trends or competing launches.

Money Today reported that the company is pairing product diversification with cost efficiency, including voluntary retirement programs run for two consecutive years.

Overall view

Lotte Chilsung Beverage's third quarter of 2026 showed a split between liquor and beverages. Consolidated operating profit fell 37.9% to KRW 57 billion, with the beverage slump and weaker overseas subsidiaries cited as the main causes. Liquor operating profit rose 24.1% on RTD sales growth of 119.6%.

On an annual basis, operating profit has declined for three straight years from 2023, and profit attributable to controlling shareholders has fallen for two years since 2024. Quarterly operating profit has risen for two consecutive quarters after the first-quarter 2026 return to profit, a change in direction.

Still, reaching the KRW 185 billion full-year guidance requires fourth-quarter operating profit of roughly KRW 24.4 billion or more. The share price sits below book value, but the thin recent earnings base means earnings multiples are highly sensitive to profit changes.

The next checkpoints are the preliminary 2026 full-year results and the operating margin in the 2026 annual report.
