02

Business structure

HRS is a specialty silicone materials company founded in 1981 that compounds and processes silicone rubber into compounds and finished products. It purchases GUM as its raw material and processes it in-house, with high consistency silicone rubber (HCR) and liquid silicone rubber (LSR) as the core product lines.

Other products include room-temperature vulcanizing (RTV) silicone, silicone sheets, silicone fire-stop products, dental impression materials, pressure-sensitive adhesives (PSA), and cosmetic and household materials. Its customers span electronics, IT, home appliances, automotive and medical applications.

The company is competitive in a high-mix, low-volume production model, where it can respond to customer-specific requirements rather than only standard products.

Its history includes localizing silicone products that had previously been imported, which has positioned it as a leader in domestic specialty silicone manufacturing. It has entered the North American and European markets and is diversifying into nuclear power, electric vehicles and high-function cosmetic ingredients.

Its production base is moving toward a new Pyeongtaek plant, where KRW 29.5 billion was spent on land and buildings, with design work in 2026 and operations planned for around 2028.

Being classified as a small and medium-sized enterprise, it differs in structure from large petrochemical groups and targets niches within specialty silicone applications.

04

Earnings analysis

Annual revenue fell from KRW 86.1 billion in 2022 to KRW 78.2 billion in 2023, down 9.2% year on year, then rose to KRW 80.2 billion in 2024, up 2.6%, before slipping to KRW 78.9 billion in 2025, down 1.7%. Among 2022–2025, 2022 was the largest revenue year and 2023 the smallest.

Operating profit was KRW 11.4 billion in 2022, KRW 12.5 billion in 2023 (up 9.5%), KRW 14.9 billion in 2024 (up 19.3%) and KRW 13.4 billion in 2025 (down 10.2%), with 2024 the largest year. Because revenue stayed in a narrow band, the swings in operating profit mainly reflected margin changes.

The operating margin was 13.3% in 2022, 16.0% in 2023, 18.6% in 2024 and 17.0% in 2025, peaking in 2024 and easing slightly afterward.

Profit attributable to controlling shareholders was KRW 10 billion in 2022, KRW 12.1 billion in 2023 (up 20.7%), KRW 15.1 billion in 2024 (up 24.8%) and KRW 13.5 billion in 2025 (down 10.5%).

Net income including non-controlling interests was reported at KRW 10 billion, KRW 12.1 billion, KRW 15.1 billion and KRW 13.5 billion for the same years. Operating cash flow was KRW 14.9 billion in 2022, KRW 20.4 billion in 2023, KRW 14.2 billion in 2024 and KRW 15.7 billion in 2025.

On a quarterly basis, the third quarter of 2025 posted revenue of KRW 20.8 billion, operating profit of KRW 3.94 billion and profit attributable to controlling shareholders of KRW 3.72 billion.

The fourth quarter of 2025 fell to revenue of KRW 19 billion, down 8.4% from the prior quarter, and operating profit of KRW 2.39 billion, down 39.4%. The first quarter of 2026 recorded revenue of KRW 20.9 billion and operating profit of KRW 3.07 billion.

The second quarter of 2026 rose to revenue of KRW 24.7 billion, up 18.5% from the prior quarter, operating profit of KRW 4.38 billion, up 42.8%, and profit attributable to controlling shareholders of KRW 6.92 billion, up 130.5%.

The third quarter of 2026 came in at revenue of KRW 21.1 billion, down 14.6% from the prior quarter but up 1.6% year on year, operating profit of KRW 3.2 billion, down 26.9% quarter on quarter and down 18.8% year on year, and profit attributable to controlling shareholders of KRW 1.54 billion, down 77.8% quarter on quarter and down 58.7% year on year.

Because second-quarter profit attributable to controlling shareholders exceeded operating profit, non-operating items or taxes may have contributed. Profit attributable to controlling shareholders over the latest four quarters totaled KRW 14.2 billion.

05

Industry analysis

Silicone materials are used in areas such as thermal management and thermal runaway prevention inside electric vehicle battery packs, so end demand spans electric vehicles, electronics and medical applications.

However, HRS's revenue fell 1.7% in 2025, and third-quarter 2026 revenue rose only 1.6% year on year, so the data does not show a clear acceleration in end demand.

The company's earnings commentary notes that the prolonged war, high interest rates and high inflation have disrupted supply chains and raised raw material price volatility, limiting revenue and profitability. Because raw material prices drive margins, cost swings flow directly into quarterly profit.

In the 2025 year-end industry comparison on wisereport, HRS's dividend yield was above the average of the KOSDAQ chemicals sector, while its PER was below the sector average.

Peers in the same chemicals industry such as KCC, Kumho Petro Chemical and Lotte Chemical differ in scale and product mix, which limits direct comparison. HRS concentrates on silicone product lines, so its results tend to move with specific customer groups and demand cycles.

Its entry into North America and Europe adds regional diversification. Whether the business is in a recovery or slowdown phase is best read from the quarterly revenue path, which peaked at KRW 24.7 billion in the second quarter of 2026 and fell to KRW 21.1 billion in the third quarter.

Revenue stayed in the KRW 78–86 billion range over the same period, so the margin gain points to a stronger earnings structure rather than revenue expansion. Near debt-free balance sheet

The debt ratio at the end of 2025 was 9.9%, with total liabilities of KRW 12.9 billion. Total equity of KRW 129.6 billion is entirely attributable to controlling shareholders, which gives the company relatively wide financial room. This structure means limited borrowing pressure even when raw material prices are volatile.

New plant and overseas expansion

The Pyeongtaek plant involved KRW 29.5 billion in land and building purchases, with design work in 2026 and operations planned for around 2028. Entry into North America and Europe is broadening the portfolio toward nuclear power, electric vehicles and high-function cosmetic ingredients.

Profit attributable to controlling shareholders fell 58.7% to KRW 1.54 billion, so revenue growth did not carry through to earnings. Stagnant and shrinking revenue

Revenue declined from KRW 86.1 billion in 2022 to KRW 78.2 billion in 2023, recovered to KRW 80.2 billion in 2024, and then fell 1.7% to KRW 78.9 billion in 2025. No year since 2022 has shown a clear revenue expansion. Profit growth has depended on margin improvement, so the drivers of top-line growth remain limited.

Cost pressure and investment burden

The prolonged war, high interest rates and high inflation have caused supply chain disruptions and raw material price volatility. Because HRS purchases and processes raw materials, cost increases pass through quickly to margins. The KRW 29.5 billion Pyeongtaek investment could also weigh on results before the plant begins operating.

Risk factors

Macro and raw materials

The prolonged war, high interest rates and high inflation are affecting supply chains and raw material prices. Higher GUM prices could compress processing margins, and rising rates could reduce customers' capital spending. These are external variables that the company cannot easily control.

Expansion execution

The Pyeongtaek plant targets operations around 2028 after design work in 2026. Delays in start-up or weaker-than-expected demand would postpone the return on investment. Until the plant starts, results will depend on existing facilities.

Earnings volatility

Second-quarter 2026 profit attributable to controlling shareholders of KRW 6.92 billion exceeded operating profit of KRW 4.38 billion, then fell to KRW 1.54 billion in the third quarter. If non-operating or tax items drive much of the swing, quarterly results are harder to read as underlying earnings power. Dividend capacity could also shift with earnings volatility.
