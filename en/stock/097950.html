02

Business structure

CJ Cheiljedang reports its business in lifestyle food, core materials and technical materials segments. In the preliminary Q3 2026 results, lifestyle food sales were KRW 2.57 trillion, up 3.8% year on year, and segment operating profit was KRW 118.7 billion, flat from a year earlier.

Domestic food sales were KRW 1.07 trillion, up 3.9%, with Hetbahn rice and Sobaba noodles among the growth drivers and Bibigo grilled fish sales up 69%. Overseas food sales were KRW 1.51 trillion, up 3.7%, with sales in Europe up 26%, Vietnam up 14%, China up 8% and the Americas up 3%.

Hetbahn rice sales in the Americas rose 46% from Q3 2025. Core materials sales were KRW 1.42 trillion, up 7.0%, and operating profit was KRW 108.5 billion, up 36.0%. The bio business centers on feed amino acids such as lysine and tryptophan, where Chinese supply is the main competitive reference point.

Technical materials sales rose 20.2% to KRW 232.6 billion, but the unit posted a KRW 24.2 billion operating loss.

04

Earnings analysis

Annual sales were KRW 30.1 trillion in 2022, the largest year in the disclosed 2022 to 2025 window, and KRW 27.3 trillion in 2025, the smallest. Sales moved from KRW 29 trillion in 2023 (down 3.5% year on year) to KRW 29.4 trillion in 2024 (up 1.2%) before falling 6.9% in 2025.

Operating profit fell from KRW 1.66 trillion in 2022 to KRW 1.29 trillion in 2023 (down 22.4%), recovered to KRW 1.55 trillion in 2024 (up 20.2%), and then dropped to KRW 1.23 trillion in 2025 (down 20.6%). The operating margin was 5.5% in 2022, 4.5% in 2023, 5.3% in 2024 and 4.5% in 2025.

Controlling-shareholder net income fell from KRW 802.7 billion in 2022 to KRW 385.9 billion in 2023 (down 51.9%) and KRW 148.2 billion in 2024 (down 61.6%), and then turned into a net loss of KRW 571.5 billion in 2025. Net income including non-controlling interests was a net loss of KRW 417 billion in 2025.

The debt ratio rose from 146.3% in 2024 to 157.5% in 2025, with total liabilities of KRW 18.2 trillion and total equity of KRW 11.6 trillion at year-end 2025, of which controlling-shareholder equity was KRW 7.06 trillion.

Operating cash flow stayed at KRW 2.26 trillion or higher from 2023 through 2025, reaching KRW 2.48 trillion in 2025.

Quarterly operating profit fell from KRW 346.5 billion in Q3 2025 to KRW 204.8 billion in Q4 2025 (down 40.9% quarter on quarter), then rose to KRW 238.1 billion in Q1 2026, KRW 257.6 billion in Q2 and KRW 309 billion in Q3, a run of three straight quarterly increases.

Controlling-shareholder net income swung from a net loss of KRW 820.6 billion in Q4 2025 to a profit of KRW 81.8 billion in Q1 2026, returned to a loss of KRW 13.5 billion in Q2, and was KRW 19.2 billion in Q3.

On a cumulative basis through the third quarter of 2026, operating profit fell 21.8% and controlling-shareholder net income fell 64.9% year on year.

Consolidated operating profit declined including the logistics subsidiary, while the figure excluding it rose; net income was pressured by foreign-exchange valuation losses tied to a weaker dollar.

05

Industry analysis

For the bio business, the amino acid market cycle is the key variable. Hana Securities said Chinese lysine is facing stronger scrutiny and that the CJ Cheiljedang-ADM joint venture is an extension of the same trend.

The same report argued that the introduction of US anti-dumping duties on Chinese lysine could lift both prices and volumes. In Europe, it expected market stability to continue after a confirmed tariff adjustment in 2027.

Kiwoom Securities, in an April 2026 report, said methionine supply disruptions added a tailwind from March 2026 as Middle East geopolitical risk widened. Among feed amino acids, tryptophan was seen recovering in selling prices after a trough in Q3 2026. In food, overseas demand for Korean processed foods is expanding.

Overseas food sales rose 26% in Europe and 3% in the Americas in Q3 2026, and Hetbahn rice sales in the Americas grew 46%. The company said it defended profitability through wider sales channels and cost efficiency despite persistent raw material and logistics cost pressure.

06

Outlook

In its Q3 2026 results release, the company said it expects Q4 2026 consolidated sales excluding the logistics subsidiary to grow by a low-single-digit percentage year on year, with an operating margin in the 5% range (as reported by Investing.com).

In food, the company plans to widen overseas sales of global strategic products such as Hetbahn rice and dumplings. For core materials, where operating profit rose 36.0% in Q3 2026, the company plans to keep improving profitability through sales expansion (as reported by Seoul Ilbo).

Among brokers, Hana Securities estimated that operating profit excluding the logistics subsidiary could reach roughly KRW 800 billion in 2027, citing base effects in domestic and overseas food and an improving bio market.

For bio, the key question is whether the lysine joint venture and anti-dumping duties translate into actual sales. The starch sweeteners sale remains at the press-report stage, with no confirmed completion timing or final price. Hana Securities also expects food price increases to show up more fully in 2027 results.

08

Bull factors

Shifts in the lysine market

The bio business is operating amid stronger scrutiny of Chinese lysine. Hana Securities said US anti-dumping duties on Chinese lysine and the CJ Cheiljedang-ADM joint venture could support both prices and volumes. Tryptophan selling prices are seen recovering from a Q3 2026 trough. Whether this shows up in results will be visible in bio operating profit for Q1 2027.

Overseas food expansion

Overseas food sales reached KRW 1.51 trillion in Q3 2026, up 3.7% on a preliminary basis. In the Americas, sales grew even though the weaker dollar reduced won-translated revenue, according to the company. NH Investment & Securities noted that dumplings are driving visible growth in Europe. This expansion came despite persistent raw material and logistics cost pressure.

Consecutive quarterly profit recovery

Quarterly operating profit bottomed at KRW 204.8 billion in Q4 2025 and then rose to KRW 238.1 billion in Q1 2026, KRW 257.6 billion in Q2 and KRW 309 billion in Q3. Core materials operating profit reached KRW 108.5 billion in Q3, up 36.0%, on a preliminary basis.

The quarterly recovery is linked to improving bio market conditions. Whether the gains carry through to net income is the next checkpoint.

09

Bear factors

Net loss in 2025 and accumulated losses

Controlling-shareholder net income turned into a net loss of KRW 571.5 billion in 2025, and net loss including non-controlling interests was KRW 417 billion. The KRW 820.6 billion controlling-shareholder net loss in Q4 2025 was the largest quarterly loss among the last five quarters.

Even after profits returned in Q1 2026, the controlling-shareholder net loss total over the last four quarters remains KRW 733.2 billion.

Rising debt ratio and capital structure

The debt ratio rose from 146.3% in 2024 to 157.5% in 2025. Total liabilities at year-end 2025 stood at KRW 18.2 trillion, above total equity of KRW 11.6 trillion. Controlling-shareholder equity accounted for KRW 7.06 trillion of that total. Profit decline and the swing into net loss occurred in the same year, which increased financial strain.

Q3 profit decline and technical materials losses

Consolidated operating profit including the logistics subsidiary fell 10.8% to KRW 309 billion in Q3 2026. Controlling-shareholder net income was KRW 19.2 billion, down 73.7% year on year.

The technical materials unit's operating loss of KRW 24.2 billion was larger than the KRW 8.6 billion loss in Q3 2025, on a preliminary basis. Net income excluding the logistics subsidiary fell 64.3% as foreign-exchange valuation losses followed a weaker dollar.

10

Risk factors

Raw materials, logistics costs and Middle East tensions

The food business still faced raw material and logistics cost pressure in Q3 2026, according to the company. If Middle East tensions persist, oil and raw material prices could rise again and add cost pressure to overseas food.

In contrast, the bio business benefited from methionine supply disruptions, so the effect of regional tensions differs by segment.

Portfolio restructuring and divestment

The starch sweeteners unit sale is being pursued according to press reports, with a price discussed at around KRW 200 billion. If completed, the unit's sales and profit would leave the reported results. Whether the proceeds will be used to improve the financial structure, and when, has not been determined.

Trade policy and tariffs

US anti-dumping duties and the European tariff adjustment directly affect lysine prices and volumes. If Europe's confirmed tariff rate differs from expectations, the pace of the bio market recovery could change. A delay in the joint venture could also narrow the benefit from restrictions on Chinese supply.

What to watch next

  2. At the Q1 2027 earnings release

    Bio segment operating profit, lysine sales volumes and whether anti-dumping duty effects are reflected. Results will be compared with core materials operating profit of KRW 108.5 billion in Q3 2026 on a preliminary basis.

  3. By end-March 2027

    The 2026 annual report on DART. The items to check are the full-year operating margin, whether the Q4 operating margin excluding the logistics subsidiary matches the 5% range guided, whether controlling-shareholder net income returns to profit, and the debt ratio.

12

Overall view

On an annual basis, CJ Cheiljedang's operating profit and controlling-shareholder net income both declined in 2025, and controlling-shareholder net income swung to a net loss. In 2026, operating profit rose to KRW 238.1 billion in Q1, KRW 257.6 billion in Q2 and KRW 309 billion in Q3, showing a recovery trend.

In food, wider overseas distribution and sales of strategic products such as Hetbahn rice and dumplings are supporting results. The bio business depends on policy factors including restrictions on Chinese lysine, anti-dumping duties and the joint venture.

Technical materials remain loss-making, and in Q3 2026 foreign-exchange valuation losses after a weaker dollar weighed on net income, leaving controlling-shareholder net income at KRW 19.2 billion. The debt ratio rose to 157.5% in 2025.

Controlling-shareholder net losses over the last four quarters total KRW 733.2 billion, so the earnings recovery has not yet offset accumulated losses. Brokerage target prices differ widely, and they are the brokers' own views.
