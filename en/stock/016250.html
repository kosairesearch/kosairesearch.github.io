02

Business structure

SGC E&C is an engineering, procurement and construction (EPC) company founded in 1967 from the technology division of OCI (formerly Dongyang Chemical), and it changed its name from SGC E&C's predecessor name.

Building on in-house engineering capability, it has expanded from chemical and industrial plants into semiconductor, pharmaceutical and bio, and environmental facilities.

Plant revenue in 2025 was KRW 950.3 billion, up 40.9% from KRW 674.3 billion a year earlier, and overseas plant revenue rose from KRW 77.1 billion to KRW 419.6 billion, an increase of 444%. Construction segment revenue in the first quarter of 2026 was KRW 87.8 billion, down 3.5% year on year.

Overseas plant work is centered on Saudi Arabia and Malaysia, and new overseas orders in 2024 were about USD 1.3 billion, ranking sixth among Korean construction companies for overseas new orders.

The company continues discussions with global project owners including Aramco and SABIC, and it is exploring new projects in Malaysia with OCI Terrace, an affiliate of OCI Holdings.

Domestic projects include a finished-dose pharmaceutical plant for Celltrion (KRW 118.3 billion), a semiconductor back-end plant expansion (KRW 62.6 billion) and a hangar for Korean Air military aircraft.

Public projects include the LH Siheung Geomo apartment package 5 (KRW 80.3 billion), a Gangnam Station rainwater tunnel (KRW 80 billion) and a Yongsan International Business District complex (KRW 52 billion).

According to the Ministry of Land, Infrastructure and Transport's 2025 ranking, the company placed 10th in industrial and environmental facility construction, and its capacity-assessment rank in that category improved from 20th in 2024 to 15th in 2026.

Rather than competing with large general contractors on scale, it positions plant EPC expertise and semiconductor construction experience as its differentiators.

04

Earnings analysis

Annual results began in 2022 with revenue of KRW 1.52 trillion, operating profit of KRW 33.2 billion and net profit attributable to controlling shareholders of KRW 52.3 billion. 2023 was the largest year in the 2022 to 2025 window, with revenue of KRW 1.86 trillion (up 22.3%), yet operating profit turned into an operating loss of KRW 21.6 billion.

Net loss attributable to controlling shareholders was KRW 33.8 billion that year, a swing into the red. In 2024, revenue fell to KRW 1.21 trillion (down 35.3%), making it the smallest year in the window, and operating profit returned to KRW 10.6 billion.

Net loss attributable to controlling shareholders widened to KRW 41.8 billion that year.

In 2025, revenue rose to KRW 1.34 trillion and operating profit to KRW 52.7 billion, a 3.9% operating margin, but the net loss attributable to controlling shareholders was KRW 64.5 billion and the net loss including minority interests was KRW 74.8 billion, extending losses for three years from 2023.

Greened Economy Daily reported that bad receivables from past projects and financing costs are widening net losses. Operating cash flow was KRW -189.6 billion in 2023, KRW -118.5 billion in 2024 and KRW -89.8 billion in 2025.

The debt ratio rose to 310.7% in 2025 from 295.2% in 2022, and total liabilities at the end of 2025 were KRW 1.03 trillion. On a quarterly basis, revenue peaked at KRW 350.2 billion in the fourth quarter of 2025 and fell to KRW 265.6 billion in the third quarter of 2026.

Operating profit peaked at KRW 17.9 billion in the first quarter of 2026 and fell to KRW 11 billion in the third quarter of 2026.

Net income attributable to controlling shareholders moved from a KRW 50 billion loss in the fourth quarter of 2025 to KRW 8.26 billion in the first quarter of 2026, then declined to KRW 6 billion and KRW 2.31 billion in the following two quarters.

Cumulative revenue for January to September 2026 was KRW 862.1 billion, down 13.08% from the same period a year earlier, according to a disclosure-based report.

05

Industry analysis

Domestic construction activity remains weak, and rising construction costs add pressure. The company said it is keeping a selective bidding approach that prioritizes profitability over expanding volume, as the domestic construction market contracts and construction costs continue to rise.

In a late-October 2026 comparison of third-quarter results, Samsung C&T's construction division and Daewoo Engineering & Construction more than doubled operating profit from a year earlier, while GS Engineering & Construction saw both revenue and operating profit decline.

The same report attributed the gap to project volume on large jobs and cost management.

Because plant work makes up a larger share of SGC E&C's business than housing does for residential-focused contractors, its results depend more on overseas mega-project progress and semiconductor investment flows than on the domestic housing cycle.

Domestic plant revenue in the first quarter of 2026 was KRW 88.6 billion, down 53.4% from KRW 190.2 billion a year earlier, which shows domestic plant orders as a pressure point.

Overseas plant revenue in the same quarter was KRW 137.2 billion, up 175.9%, and it offset the domestic decline and supported operating profit. In domestic semiconductor work, expansions of back-end plants and new packaging and test facilities are cited as order opportunities.

Ranking around 15th in industrial and environmental facility capacity assessment indicates a scale gap versus large general contractors.

06

Outlook

The company's 2026 targets are revenue of KRW 1.4428 trillion, operating profit of KRW 50.9 billion and new orders of KRW 1.92 trillion, according to June 2026 reporting. The plant new-order target is KRW 1.1 trillion, of which KRW 1 trillion is for overseas plants.

The company said that by the end of the first quarter of 2026 it had achieved 22% of its revenue target, 35% of its operating profit target and 26% of its new-order target.

Cumulative revenue for January to September 2026 was KRW 862.1 billion, so meeting the annual revenue target depends on filling roughly KRW 580.7 billion in the fourth quarter. Several high-margin overseas projects are scheduled to end in 2026, and the company is pursuing new overseas contracts to replace them.

Domestically, it is pursuing additional orders tied to the Incheon semiconductor back-end plant expansion, semiconductor packaging and test plants, and a semiconductor component plant in Gwangju.

In the public segment, it plans to raise the share of urban redevelopment and mixed-use urban projects and expand supply under its THE LIV housing brand. The company says it aims to generate operating profit of at least KRW 50 billion a year to create room for repaying borrowings and improving financing terms.

Whether the annual order and operating profit targets are met after the fourth quarter of 2026 is the next checkpoint.

08

Bull factors

Overseas plants drive operating profit recovery

Overseas plant revenue in 2025 reached KRW 419.6 billion, up sharply from KRW 77.1 billion a year earlier. Operating profit rose 399.7% to KRW 52.7 billion, and the operating margin improved from 0.9% in 2024 to 3.9% in 2025.

Reports attributed the shift to large projects in Saudi Arabia and Malaysia entering full construction stages, which raised the share of higher-margin overseas revenue.

Plant order targets and overseas discussions

The company set a 2026 plant new-order target of KRW 1.1 trillion, including KRW 1 trillion for overseas plants. At the end of the first quarter of 2026, it had reached 26% of the annual order target. Ongoing discussions with global project owners such as Aramco and SABIC are cited as evidence of the order pipeline.

High-value semiconductor and pharmaceutical orders

High-value industrial orders include a Celltrion finished-dose pharmaceutical plant (KRW 118.3 billion) and a semiconductor back-end plant expansion (KRW 62.6 billion). The company's capacity-assessment rank in industrial and environmental facilities moved from 20th in 2024 to 18th in 2025 and 15th in 2026. Its semiconductor construction experience is cited as a differentiating factor.

09

Bear factors

Net losses persist and earnings quality is questioned

Net loss attributable to controlling shareholders was recorded in each year from 2023 through 2025, widening to KRW 64.5 billion in 2025. Greened Economy Daily reported that impairments on receivables from past projects and financing costs keep operating gains from reaching the bottom line.

Balance sheet and cash flow burdens

The debt ratio rose to 310.7% in 2025 from 295.2% in 2022. Operating cash flow was KRW -189.6 billion in 2023, KRW -118.5 billion in 2024 and KRW -89.8 billion in 2025.

Reporting pointed to liabilities of several trillion won tied to completion guarantees on logistics center projects as adding to financial strain, a point that the company's own disclosures do not isolate.

Revenue decline and shrinking domestic plant sales

Revenue peaked at KRW 350.2 billion in the fourth quarter of 2025 and declined to KRW 265.6 billion in the third quarter of 2026. Domestic plant revenue fell 53.4% year on year to KRW 88.6 billion in the first quarter of 2026.

Operating profit has also declined from KRW 17.9 billion in the first quarter of 2026 to KRW 14.3 billion and KRW 11 billion in the two quarters after.

10

Risk factors

Project execution risk

Overseas plant revenue growth depends on construction progress on large projects in Saudi Arabia and Malaysia. Delays or cost increases could narrow the operating profit gains. The past impairments on receivables from earlier projects also make project management quality a point to monitor.

Financial and liquidity risk

A 310.7% debt ratio and negative operating cash flow in three straight years point to heavy reliance on borrowing. The company plans to repay debt from operating profit of at least KRW 50 billion a year, but financing costs still weigh on net results. If completion-guarantee obligations materialize, funding pressure could rise further.

Domestic order gap risk

With the domestic construction market contracting and construction costs rising, the company is keeping its bidding selective. Several overseas projects are set to end in 2026, so a revenue gap could emerge if large new contracts do not follow. Revenue declines over three consecutive quarters through the third quarter of 2026 reinforce this concern.

12

Overall view

SGC E&C recorded operating profit of KRW 52.7 billion in 2025, up 399.7%, on higher overseas plant revenue, showing a recovery at the operating level.

However, net loss attributable to controlling shareholders was KRW 64.5 billion that year, extending losses for a third straight year, and the cumulative net loss over the last four quarters is KRW 33.4 billion.

A 310.7% debt ratio and negative operating cash flow from 2023 through 2025 highlight the strain on the balance sheet. Quarterly results show operating profit peaking in the first quarter of 2026 and falling to KRW 11 billion in the third quarter, with revenue declining for three consecutive quarters.

The company has set a 2026 plant new-order target of KRW 1.1 trillion and an annual operating profit target of KRW 50.9 billion.

Bullish factors are the overseas plant order pipeline and the improved operating margin, while bearish factors are persistent net losses, financial burden and the shrinking domestic plant business.

The next checkpoints are whether the order and earnings targets are met by the end of December 2026 and the 2026 annual report due by March 31, 2027.
