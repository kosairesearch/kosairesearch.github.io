02

Business structure

Abco Electronics was founded in 1973 as an electronic components maker. The parent makes passive components such as inductors and resistors, while its PCB subsidiary makes printed circuit boards for automotive electronics, home appliances, smartphones and telecom equipment.

The company expanded into PCBs by acquiring its PCB subsidiary in September 2018 while it was in court-supervised rehabilitation.

According to JobKorea company data, PCBs (DS, MLB, PKG and others) are the largest segment at roughly 38 to 39 percent of sales, signal and metal inductors about 32 percent, semiconductor packaging subcontracting about 13 percent, and chip and lead resistors about 12 percent, with other items at 4 to 5 percent.

The two listings use different year bases, so the shares are shown as ranges. The company has supplied inductors to Samsung Electronics smartphones since 2010. A 2024 brokerage analysis said semiconductor-related sales typically account for 30 percent or more of sales on a standalone basis.

The company describes capacity investment in its power inductor (PWL) business. Its Vietnamese subsidiary, ABCO Electronics Vina Co., Ltd., contributed to the 2025 improvement through stronger inductor sales.

In terms of peers, passive components are compared with large makers such as Samsung Electro-Mechanics, and PCBs with players such as Korea Circuit.

04

Earnings analysis

Annual revenue fell from KRW 164.7 billion in 2022 to KRW 125 billion in 2023 (down 24.1% year on year) and KRW 120.9 billion in 2024 (down 3.3%), then rose to KRW 133.9 billion in 2025 (up 10.7%). Revenue was largest in 2022 and smallest in 2024 across 2022 to 2025.

Operating profit went from KRW 11.3 billion in 2022 to an operating loss of KRW 4.71 billion in 2023, which marked a swing into loss. The operating loss widened to KRW 4.88 billion in 2024 before operating profit of KRW 8.31 billion in 2025 marked a return to profit.

The operating margin was 6.9 percent in 2022, -3.8 percent in 2023, -4.0 percent in 2024 and 6.2 percent in 2025. Owner net income was KRW 9.36 billion in 2022, an owner net loss of KRW 2.37 billion in 2023, an owner net loss of KRW 5.82 billion in 2024, and owner net income of KRW 9.52 billion in 2025.

Net income including non-controlling interests is recorded in the same years at the same values. The 2025 turnaround has been attributed to stronger inductor sales at the Vietnamese subsidiary, according to a disclosure summary.

The same commentary also cites higher demand for high-frequency inductors and automotive PCB substrates. The debt ratio was 26.0 percent in 2022, 28.1 percent in 2023, 32.9 percent in 2024 and 31.1 percent in 2025.

At the end of 2025, total equity was KRW 116.7 billion, all attributable to owners, and total liabilities were KRW 36.3 billion. Operating cash flow was KRW 10.6 billion in 2022, KRW 3.2 billion in 2023, KRW 8.61 billion in 2024 and KRW 11.9 billion in 2025.

Quarterly revenue went from KRW 34.7 billion in Q3 2025 to a low of KRW 34.2 billion in Q4 2025 (down 1.6 percent quarter on quarter), then rose to KRW 35.3 billion, KRW 37.3 billion and KRW 42.1 billion in Q1 to Q3 2026, three straight quarters of increases. Third-quarter revenue was up 21.2 percent year on year.

Operating profit moved from KRW 2.42 billion in Q3 2025 to KRW 2.12 billion in Q4 and KRW 1.95 billion in Q1 2026, then rose to KRW 4.03 billion in Q2 (up 106.9 percent quarter on quarter) and KRW 4.1 billion in Q3 (up 69.5 percent year on year).

Owner net income peaked at KRW 4.48 billion in Q2 2026 and was KRW 4.11 billion in Q3, down 8.2 percent quarter on quarter. Owner net income over the last four quarters totaled KRW 13.6 billion.

The specific drivers behind the sharp Q2 2026 operating profit increase are a point to verify in the detailed items of the quarterly report.

05

Industry analysis

A April 2026 report drew attention to views that the passive components market is passing its trough. The argument is that component demand is gradually improving as downstream recovery follows a period of weak IT demand and inventory adjustment.

Passive components are used in nearly every electronic product, including smartphones, automobiles, industrial equipment and telecom gear. Because the end-market is broad, profit improvement can be larger during an economic recovery.

Korea IR Council analysts said in the same report that Abco has a rising chance of a turnaround, supported by its resistor and inductor product competitiveness. Automotive electrification and the expansion of 5G telecom infrastructure are cited as medium-term growth drivers.

A 2025 earnings commentary on a financial information site said electric and autonomous vehicles increased demand for automotive electronics and raised demand for high-frequency inductors. On the other hand, price competition and industry volatility are key variables in passive components.

Intensifying price competition with global rivals could weigh on profitability. Abco competes with large passive component makers on one side and PCB specialists such as Korea Circuit on the other, given its combined passive components and PCB structure.

A 2024 brokerage analysis cited customer production cuts and weak demand for set makers in mobile, home appliances and TVs.

Outlook

The latest confirmed results are for Q3 2026. The third-quarter quarterly report was filed on November 16, showing consolidated revenue of KRW 42.1 billion and operating profit of KRW 4.1 billion.

Revenue rose 12.8 percent quarter on quarter, while owner net income of KRW 4.11 billion was down 8.2 percent from the prior quarter. The company's records show a cash capital injection of USD 4,500,000 into its subsidiary ABCO Electronics Vina Co., Ltd. in July 2026.

Because Vietnamese inductor sales were one pillar of the 2025 turnaround, whether the injection translates into higher capacity or sales is a checkpoint.

The PCB segment is described as gaining new customers after the completion of capacity investment in multilayer boards of 12 layers or more, so it is worth tracking how much of that customer revenue appears each quarter.

On labor matters, in intensive bargaining on March 27, 2026, management said it would allocate 10 percent of operating profit to bonuses, the same level as a competitor. The union, citing information from its members, has described management's earlier proposal differently.

Because a larger profit pool means a larger bonus pool, whether the bonus rule becomes institutionalized is a cost item to verify. Annual numeric targets and new product timelines are not covered in this report. The next checkpoint is how the operating margin and debt ratio move in the 2026 annual report.

Bull factors

From losses to earnings recovery

Operating profit returned to KRW 8.31 billion in 2025, and owner net income was KRW 9.52 billion. Revenue has risen for three straight quarters through Q3 2026, and operating profit has increased for two straight quarters.

Owner net income totaled KRW 13.6 billion over the last four quarters, which supports the view of a recovering earnings base.

Medium-term demand from automotive and 5G

Automotive electrification and 5G infrastructure expansion are viewed as medium-term growth drivers. Analysts point to rising demand for automotive electronics and high-frequency inductors as electric and autonomous vehicles expand. Abco has added new customers for automotive PCBs in its PCB segment, aligning with this trend.

Financial capacity and equity base

The debt ratio was 31.1% at the end of 2025, with total liabilities of KRW 36.3 billion and total equity of KRW 116.7 billion. Operating cash flow rose to KRW 11.9 billion in 2025 from KRW 8.61 billion in 2024. Equity scale and cash generation are improving together as profits recover.

Bear factors

Pricing pressure in passive components

Price competition and industry volatility are key variables in passive components. Intensified pricing competition with global rivals could weigh on profitability. Earnings may swing more if downstream demand changes, so higher-value products and technology competitiveness are cited as ongoing tasks.

Customer mix and the semiconductor cycle

A 2024 brokerage analysis said semiconductor-related sales typically account for 30 percent or more of standalone sales. The same analysis pointed to production cuts by the largest customer. Weak supply to set makers in mobile, home appliances and TVs also shows high exposure to demand swings tied to customer mix.

Volatility in quarterly profits

Operating profit fell to KRW 2.12 billion in Q4 2025 and KRW 1.95 billion in Q1 2026, two consecutive quarterly declines. Owner net income in Q3 2026 was down 8.2 percent from the prior quarter. Because operating losses ran through 2023 and 2024, quarterly profit can swing sharply within a short period.

10

Risk factors

Labor relations and bonus costs

At intensive bargaining on March 27, 2026, management said it would allocate 10 percent of operating profit to bonuses, in line with a competitor. The union has described management's earlier proposal differently.

Because the bonus pool grows with profit, the way the rule is institutionalized could weigh on the operating margin.

Reliance on the overseas subsidiary

Stronger inductor sales at the Vietnamese subsidiary were cited as a driver of the 2025 turnaround. A cash capital injection into the same subsidiary was recorded in July 2026.

If earnings and investment concentrate in an overseas production base, local demand or operating factors can quickly affect consolidated results.

PCB cycle and customer investment cycles

The PCB business is the largest segment by sales, so it is strongly exposed to PCB market conditions and customers' capital spending cycles. New customers are being added after capacity investment in multilayer boards of 12 layers or more, but volume shifts at a single customer could move quarterly revenue.
