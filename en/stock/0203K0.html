02

Business structure

Songwoo Information & Technology, founded in 2010, is a system software company supplying intelligent transportation system (ITS) solutions based on video analytics technology.

Its business is organized around traffic control systems, video surveillance devices, and license plate recognition (LPR) products, built on proprietary LPR core technology and deep-learning-based video analytics.

Its flagship Smart Intersection product is a data-driven signal control system that analyzes traffic volume by direction and vehicle type to compute optimal signal cycles.

The company's customer base centers on public institutions and local governments, exemplified by its contract with the Korea Land and Housing Corporation (LH) for the 'Neulbom A-Eye System Phase 2 Construction Service,' running from August 6, 2026 to October 4, 2027.

This LH contract was revised to approximately KRW 3.79 billion, excluding VAT, on a company-share basis. The company was also selected for a Ministry of Science and ICT program to build an AI-based forest fire monitoring system in the fire-affected areas of Sancheong and Hadong.

Management has indicated a direction of expanding its accumulated traffic-safety experience and data into the broader smart city domain. The company newly listed on the KONEX market on July 21, 2026, with IBK Investment & Securities as its designated advisor.

Compared to larger security and control-system providers such as S-1 and POSCO DX Company, Songwoo operates at a smaller scale with relatively higher reliance on specific public-sector demand.

04

Earnings analysis

Songwoo's 2025 revenue came to KRW 9.79 billion, down 2.7% from KRW 10.1 billion in 2024. Operating profit, however, rose 10.6% from KRW 1.07 billion in 2024 to KRW 1.18 billion in 2025, with the operating margin improving from 10.6% to 12.1% over the same period.

The combination of declining revenue and rising operating margin suggests efficiency gains in cost or SG&A management.

Net income attributable to controlling shareholders grew more sharply, up 61.7% from KRW 1.33 billion in 2024 to KRW 2.15 billion in 2025, and total net income including non-controlling interests matched this figure at KRW 2.15 billion.

The sum of controlling-shareholder net income across the last four quarters (Q1-Q4 2025) also totaled KRW 2.15 billion, consistent with the annual figure. Cash generation improved as well, with operating cash flow nearly doubling from KRW 1.15 billion in 2024 to KRW 2.21 billion in 2025.

On the balance sheet, total equity stood at KRW 14 billion at year-end 2025 (entirely attributable to controlling shareholders), with total liabilities of KRW 4.05 billion; the debt-to-equity ratio rose from 16.5% in 2024 to 28.8% in 2025 but remains at a low absolute level.

Quarterly results for periods after 2026 have not yet been disclosed through regular periodic reports and are therefore not reflected in this analysis.

Industry analysis

The KONEX market where Songwoo trades has shown an overall contraction in liquidity. No company transferred from KONEX to KOSDAQ during 2026, and only three companies, including Songwoo, newly listed on KONEX, prompting assessments that the market's role as a 'growth ladder' has weakened.

Average daily trading value also declined from the prior year as capital concentrated in larger stocks. In this environment, small ITS and video-analytics companies like Songwoo are structurally exposed to sharp price reactions around individual disclosures or contract-win events.

Operationally, the traffic control, video surveillance, and LPR markets are classic public-procurement-driven businesses tied to the budget cycles of local governments and public institutions.

In terms of competitive positioning, large security and control-system operators such as S-1 and POSCO DX Company hold an advantage in capital and sales networks, while Songwoo operates in a narrower segment built on its proprietary LPR technology and regional reference projects.

Bull factors

Operating cash flow also increased substantially to KRW 2.21 billion, indicating improved earnings quality as well.

Stable Financial Structure

The debt-to-equity ratio rose to 28.8% at year-end 2025 but remains low in absolute terms, with total equity of KRW 14 billion entirely attributable to controlling shareholders. This conservative financial structure could provide financial flexibility for future investment or business expansion.

Public-Sector Reference Accumulation

The company is accumulating a track record of public-institution projects, including the LH Neulbom A-Eye Phase 2 contract and the Sancheong-Hadong AI forest fire monitoring system. Such references could positively factor into future bids for similar projects from local governments and public corporations.

Bear factors

KONEX Market Liquidity Constraints

No companies transferred from KONEX to KOSDAQ in 2026, and only three new listings occurred, reflecting an overall slowdown in market vitality. Given KONEX's limited trading characteristics, investors may face constraints in order execution and face greater price volatility.

Stagnant Revenue Growth

2025 revenue declined 2.7% year-on-year to KRW 9.79 billion. If profit improvement stems mainly from cost efficiency rather than revenue growth, the sustainability of further margin gains could be questioned absent renewed top-line momentum.

Dependence on Public Procurement

Revenue is concentrated in orders from public institutions such as LH and local governments, making performance sensitive to shifts in budget timing and scale. Delays or reductions in a specific project could increase single-quarter earnings volatility.

Risk factors

Liquidity Risk

KONEX-listed stocks have low trading volume, which can lead to significant price impact upon transactions. Prices can react sharply to sudden supply-demand shifts beyond the scope of the designated advisor IBK Investment & Securities.

Uncertainty over a potential KOSDAQ transfer listing also makes it difficult to gauge when liquidity might improve.

Business Concentration Risk

With business concentrated in specific video-analytics fields such as traffic control, surveillance, and LPR, the company may have limited buffer against technological change or intensifying competition. Heavy reliance on specific clients such as LH or specific regional projects could increase revenue volatility.

Disclosure and Information Access Risk

KONEX-listed companies have more limited disclosure obligations and analyst coverage compared to KOSDAQ, which can make it harder for investors to track up-to-date management information.

Detailed quarterly financials or order backlog data are disclosed only on the periodic reporting cycle, which can create information asymmetry.

Overall view

Songwoo Information & Technology is a small-cap ITS solutions company that saw a marked improvement in operating margin and controlling-shareholder net income in 2025 despite a modest revenue decline.

Rising operating cash flow and a low debt-to-equity ratio are factors that could be viewed positively in terms of financial stability. However, limited top-line growth momentum and a business model dependent on public-sector orders such as LH imply that performance could be tied to the progress of specific projects.

Even after its July 2026 KONEX listing, the broader slowdown in KONEX market liquidity appears to be continuing, meaning trading-related constraints may persist for some time.

Key points to monitor going forward include the completion timing of the LH contract, any additional public-sector contract wins, and the specific figures in the full-year 2026 periodic report expected in the first half of 2027.

Investors should weigh the KONEX market's characteristically low liquidity alongside the company's reliance on public procurement before forming a judgment.
