02

Business structure

Doosan Fuel Cell manufactures and supplies fuel cell systems that generate electricity and heat from hydrogen or city gas.

Its core product is the phosphoric acid fuel cell (PAFC) for power generation, while solid oxide fuel cell (SOFC) stacks began shipping to Germany's Reverion and are being co-developed with UK-based Ceres Power. Revenue splits into core equipment sales and after-sales service.

According to a securities firm report, third-quarter 2026 core equipment revenue was KRW 119.3 billion, up 75% year on year, and service revenue was KRW 26.0 billion, an improvement.

Domestic demand centers on bids under the Clean Hydrogen Power Portfolio Standard (CHPS); this year's bid volume was reported at 125MW, about 70% of last year's level, though the company's share is expected to stay high.

Overseas, PAFC units for North American AI data-center on-site power are supplied through Hyaxiom, a U.S. subsidiary of Doosan. In its third-quarter 2026 results release, the company said it would restart existing production lines to secure up to 390MW of capacity.

In SOFC, Bloom Energy of the United States is cited as a main rival. The center of gravity is shifting from policy-driven domestic volume toward large overseas orders.

04

Earnings analysis

Annual revenue went from KRW 312.1 billion in 2022 to KRW 260.9 billion in 2023, down 16.4%, before rising to KRW 411.8 billion in 2024 (up 57.8%) and KRW 454.8 billion in 2025 (up 10.4%), marking two consecutive years of growth. Of the 2022–2025 period, 2025 was the largest year.

Operating profit fell from KRW 7.22 billion in 2022 to KRW 1.64 billion in 2023 (down 77.3%), and the company swung to an operating loss of KRW 1.73 billion in 2024. The operating loss widened to KRW 105.7 billion in 2025, extending losses for a second consecutive year.

The operating margin moved from 2.3% in 2022 to 0.6% in 2023 and -0.4% in 2024, reaching -23.2% in 2025. Net income attributable to controlling shareholders turned to a loss of KRW 8.5 billion in 2023, then KRW 10.5 billion in 2024 and KRW 132.8 billion in 2025, three consecutive loss years.

Net income including non-controlling interests is reported with the same loss values in each year. Operating cash flow went from KRW 101.1 billion in 2024 to -KRW 56.5 billion in 2025.

Quarterly, revenue in the fourth quarter of 2025 reached KRW 135.8 billion, up 49.5% from the prior quarter, but the operating loss widened to KRW 76.6 billion.

In the second quarter of 2026, revenue fell 69.4% to KRW 44.3 billion and the operating loss grew to KRW 50.9 billion; the company said delayed domestic PAFC deliveries resumed in the third quarter.

Third-quarter 2026 revenue was KRW 145.2 billion, up 59.9% year on year, and the operating loss of KRW 17 billion was KRW 33.9 billion narrower than the prior quarter. Gross loss on a consolidated basis shrank from KRW 40.6 billion to KRW 6.9 billion quarter on quarter. Net losses attributable to controlling shareholders over the last four quarters total KRW 199.4 billion.

05

Industry analysis

The main demand driver is AI data-center construction in the United States. As on-site power, where facilities install their own generation next to the data center, expands, fuel cells are cited as a leading option because installation is quick and modular capacity can be added in stages.

In its September 15, 2026 disclosure, the company said this trend is now leading to actual purchase orders. North American demand, however, is delivered through Hyaxiom, a U.S. subsidiary of Doosan, which means some reliance on the group channel.

In Korea, the market is centered on CHPS bids; Hana Securities estimated that the company's share would stay high even though this year's bid volume fell. On technology, PAFC is viewed as less efficient at power generation than SOFC, but coverage noted that large North American orders were won regardless.

In SOFC, Bloom Energy of the United States is cited as a main rival, and the company works with Ceres Power of the UK. Hana Securities expected SOFC stack volumes for Reverion in Germany to be recognized mainly in 2027.

The cycle position can be summarized as orders building quickly while revenue recognition and cost improvement follow later.

06

Outlook

The company has stated three concrete plans. First, it will restart existing production lines to secure up to 390MW of capacity and is discussing supply volumes and schedules with key partners through 2028. Second, it is working on quality improvements aimed at reaching a 90% yield on a core SOFC production process.

Third, PAFC deliveries to North America through Hyaxiom will begin in 2028 and continue in stages.

Hana Securities reported that third-quarter cumulative new orders were 66MW and projected that annual volume could exceed 137MW, taking into account existing CHPS awards and overseas contracts signed in the fourth quarter. The same report expected SOFC stack supply to Germany to be recognized mainly next year.

Some press coverage noted that large order volumes will start to ship from next year, drawing attention to the possibility of a return to profit in 2027.

Hana Securities presented a target price of KRW 60,000 in a late October 2026 report, and DS Investment & Securities presented KRW 47,000 in a report at the same time. The key questions are how much revenue recognized from these deliveries absorbs cost burdens, and whether capacity expansion proceeds on schedule.

08

Bull factors

Order growth tied to on-site power demand

The company said it signed two PAFC supply contracts with Hyaxiom in September 2026, worth KRW 501.4 billion and KRW 322.2 billion, bringing the cumulative total to KRW 823.6 billion.

The units serve on-site power installations near AI data centers, a demand driver the company linked to short installation periods and modular expansion. Including domestic equipment contracts, the company reported about KRW 1.11 trillion in orders for 2026.

Quarterly loss narrowing and cost improvement

Third-quarter 2026 operating loss was KRW 17 billion, narrowing by KRW 33.9 billion from KRW 50.9 billion in the prior quarter.

Consolidated gross loss shrank from KRW 40.6 billion to KRW 6.9 billion, which the company linked to higher factory utilization from expanded SOFC stack production and a temporary decline in service costs.

Hana Securities attributed the improvement to recovering core equipment sales, which eased fixed-cost pressure, and lower stack replacement costs.

Diversification into SOFC and overseas projects

In August 2026, the company signed an SOFC stack supply contract worth KRW 108.7 billion with Reverion of Germany. It is co-developing SOFC technology with Ceres Power of the UK, and marine fuel cell development is also continuing. Hana Securities projected that recognition of the German SOFC stack volumes would concentrate in 2027.

09

Bear factors

Persistent losses and no return to profit

The 2025 operating loss was KRW 105.7 billion, with an operating margin of -23.2%. Cumulative operating losses for the first three quarters of 2026 reached about KRW 69.3 billion, wider than KRW 29.1 billion in the same period a year earlier. Even with third-quarter revenue up 59.9% year on year, the company remained in operating deficit.

Balance sheet and cash flow pressure

The debt ratio rose from 96.3% in 2022 to 226.1% in 2025. Total liabilities at the end of 2025 were KRW 830.9 billion, exceeding total equity of KRW 367.5 billion. Operating cash flow moved from KRW 101.1 billion in 2024 to -KRW 56.5 billion in 2025.

Volatile quarterly revenue

Second-quarter 2026 revenue fell 69.4% from the prior quarter to KRW 44.3 billion. The company said delayed domestic PAFC deliveries resumed in the third quarter, lifting revenue. Because recognition depends on project delivery timing, quarterly swings are large.

10

Risk factors

Production and delivery schedule

Restarting production lines to reach up to 390MW and the 90% yield target for a core SOFC process are still in the planning stage. The company said it is discussing supply volumes through 2028 with key partners to limit delivery disruption. Delays in capacity expansion could push back revenue recognition on the order backlog.

Concentration of demand

Domestic CHPS bid volume fell to about 70% of last year's level, at 125MW this year. Overseas orders are concentrated in North American data-center procurement channelled through Hyaxiom. A change in specific customers or procurement timing could carry through to earnings volatility.

Possible revisions to preliminary results

Third-quarter 2026 results were released as preliminary figures before completion of the external auditor's review. The company said the numbers could change depending on the review outcome. They remain preliminary until confirmed in the periodic report.

12

Overall view

Doosan Fuel Cell's cumulative 2026 orders reached about KRW 1.11 trillion on demand for on-site power at AI data centers. The two Hyaxiom contracts alone total KRW 823.6 billion, with deliveries to North America starting in stages from 2028. Results have not yet caught up with orders.

The operating margin was -23.2% in 2025, and cumulative operating losses for the first three quarters of 2026 widened from the prior year. Third-quarter 2026 revenue rose 59.9% year on year to KRW 145.2 billion and the operating loss narrowed from the prior quarter, but the company has not returned to profit.

Financially, the debt ratio rose to 226.1% at the end of 2025 from 96.3% in 2022, with total liabilities of KRW 830.9 billion exceeding total equity of KRW 367.5 billion.

The key variables for earnings recovery are the timing of revenue recognition and production costs, and the next checkpoints are the 2026 annual report and progress on capacity restart and SOFC yield during 2027.
