Report overview

Ad-Tech Profit Rebound, Debt Burden Remains

Wisebirds has sustained a profit recovery since 2024 on the back of its AdEfficiency acquisition and AI ad-automation platform Nest Ads Manager, but the elevated debt ratio stemming from acquisition financing remains a balance-sheet challenge.

02

Business structure

Founded in 2013, Wisebirds is a digital marketing company centered on online ad agency services and ad-efficiency optimization built on RTB (real-time bidding) media such as Meta and Google.

Its proprietary RTB optimization platform, Adwitt, became the first in Korea to earn a Facebook Preferred Marketing Developer badge in 2013 and later secured Instagram Partner and Google Premier Partner status. The company listed on KOSDAQ via a SPAC merger in 2020 and became part of the Daou Kiwoom Group.

In 2023 it acquired a 95% stake in search-ad agency AdEfficiency for KRW 74 billion, folding it into a wholly owned subsidiary in 2024 to build an integrated agency covering display, search, and app advertising.

The business is broadly split between traditional performance ad-agency operations and a technology division led by the AI-based ad-automation platform Nest Ads Manager.

Nest Ads Manager lets publishers hand over the entire ad-operation process to AI via a simple SDK installation with no separate infrastructure, in a revenue-share structure now deployed across 16 major domestic media properties.

The company became the first TikTok Marketing Partners agency in Korea and launched a retail media unit supporting TikTok Shop operations in the United States and Southeast Asia.

Its client base spans global brands such as LVMH and Ralph Lauren alongside major domestic advertisers including Shinsegae International, Amorepacific, and Kolon Sport, while the competitive landscape ranges from large ad groups like Cheil Worldwide and Innocean to small- and mid-cap digital agencies such as Mobidays and Playd.

04

Earnings analysis

Consolidated revenue in 2025 came to KRW 45.52 billion, up sharply from KRW 30.79 billion in 2024, while operating profit rose to KRW 7.58 billion (16.6% margin) from KRW 2.31 billion (7.5%) the prior year.

This follows a weak 2023, when revenue was KRW 13.86 billion and operating profit just KRW 0.22 billion (1.6% margin), before the AdEfficiency acquisition effect took hold.

According to disclosed results, first-half 2026 consolidated revenue was KRW 24.54 billion and operating profit KRW 5.77 billion, up 25.2% and 125.4% year-on-year respectively, with the operating margin rising 10.4 percentage points to 23.5%.

The center of the improvement is the technology division: revenue from the AI-based ad-automation platform Nest Ads Manager grew 135.9% year-on-year to KRW 1.25 billion in the first half, and the segment turned operating-profit positive in the second quarter. Quarterly net income, however, has been volatile.

Per the provided quarterly data, the second quarter of 2025 posted an operating profit of KRW 1.92 billion yet a net loss attributable to owners of KRW 1.51 billion, while the fourth quarter of 2025 recorded revenue of KRW 15.49 billion, operating profit of KRW 2.92 billion, and owners' net income of KRW 3.70 billion, the year's peak, suggesting sizable one-off items in both directions.

The first and second quarters of 2026 delivered steadier operating profits of KRW 2.91 billion and KRW 2.86 billion respectively, with owners' net income of KRW 1.23 billion and KRW 0.99 billion maintaining a profitable trend.

On the cash-flow side, operating cash flow turned negative at KRW -0.28 billion in 2025 despite the earnings improvement, a contrast to positive figures of KRW 6.09 billion in 2024 and KRW 4.17 billion in 2022.

On the balance sheet, financing the AdEfficiency acquisition through a KRW 28 billion bank loan and KRW 36 billion in convertible bonds pushed the debt ratio from 64.6% in 2022 to 113.0% in 2023 and then to an elevated 281.4% in 2024 and 280.1% in 2025.

05

Industry analysis

The digital advertising industry is in a phase where AI-driven automation is raising operational efficiency, with generative AI expected to grow at roughly 27% annually over the next decade and drive overall ad-market growth.

As digital media consumption becomes routine and mobile-centric short-form video expands, growth is also anticipated in vertical full-screen ad formats and connected TV (CTV) advertising.

Within this backdrop, revenue-share models like Wisebirds' Nest Ads Manager, which avoid publisher infrastructure burden, have drawn comparisons in the industry to the business model of Nasdaq-listed AppLovin.

Competitively, the market includes large ad groups such as Cheil Worldwide and Innocean, media rep companies like Incross and Nasmedia, and small- to mid-cap performance agencies such as Mobidays and Playd.

Wisebirds is distinguished by holding multiple premier-tier partnerships with major platforms including Meta, Google, TikTok, and Naver.

That said, given the industry's heavy reliance on large platforms for revenue, sensitivity to platform policy or fee changes and to advertising-cycle conditions remains a common variable across the sector.

06

Outlook

Management has said that first-half results already reached 76% of last year's full-year operating profit, and that continued momentum in the second half should support a new annual record.

Wisebirds has a seasonal pattern of stronger second-half results: in 2024, a first-half operating loss of KRW 0.64 billion turned into a second-half profit of KRW 2.94 billion, and in 2025 first-half operating profit of KRW 2.56 billion nearly doubled to KRW 5.02 billion in the second half.

The company has stated plans to accelerate the overseas expansion of Nest Ads Manager based on the recovery of its core business and growth in its new venture. It also intends to place the creative AI solution Ad-Visor at the forefront of second-half growth to speed up new revenue generation.

On shareholder returns, the company carried out its first-ever share buyback, cancellation, and share consolidation, and has stated plans to make shareholder return policy a regular practice as the company grows.

New share certificates from the 5-for-1 consolidation are scheduled to be re-listed on September 9, 2026, meaning the reduction in outstanding shares is set to be reflected in the market imminently.

08

Bull factors

High-Margin Turn in the AI Technology Division

Nest Ads Manager operates on a revenue-share model with minimal incremental infrastructure cost as more publishers join, and the segment turned operating-profit positive for the first time in four years in the second quarter.

First-half revenue growth of 135.9% year-on-year shows that expanding publisher adoption is a genuine growth driver. Adding the creative AI solution Ad-Visor could further expand the technology division's revenue contribution.

Pronounced Second-Half Seasonality

Wisebirds has repeated a pattern of second-half operating profit far exceeding first-half profit in both 2024 and 2025. In 2025, first-half operating profit of KRW 2.56 billion nearly doubled to KRW 5.02 billion in the second half.

Management itself has characterized this trend as structural profitability improvement, making second-half results an important point to monitor.

First-Ever Shareholder Return Initiative

For the first time since founding, the company purchased KRW 2 billion of treasury shares, canceled KRW 1.5 billion of them, and carried out a 5-for-1 share consolidation in parallel. Management has stated its intent to make shareholder returns a regular practice going forward as growth continues. The combination of earnings improvement with a change in capital policy marks a departure from prior practice.

09

Bear factors

Persistently High Debt Ratio

A large portion of the KRW 74 billion AdEfficiency acquisition price was financed through bank borrowing and convertible bonds, pushing the debt ratio from 64.6% in 2022 to 280.1% in 2025. This level is nearly unchanged from 2024 (281.4%), indicating limited progress on balance-sheet deleveraging. Financial costs such as interest expense could continue to weigh on net income volatility.

Significant Quarterly Net Income Volatility

In the second quarter of 2025, an operating profit of KRW 1.92 billion coexisted with a net loss attributable to owners of KRW 1.51 billion, while the fourth quarter saw net income rise to KRW 3.70 billion, reflecting large quarter-to-quarter swings.

This volatility suggests one-off non-operating items materially affect results, which can lower the reliability of forward earnings expectations.

Dependence on Platforms and Large Advertisers

Revenue is heavily dependent on partnerships with a handful of major platforms such as Meta, Google, TikTok, and Naver, as well as on winning new large-scale campaigns. Changes in platform fee structures or policies, or the loss of a major advertiser, could have an immediate impact on results.

10

Risk factors

Financial / Capital Structure

The KRW 28 billion bank loan and KRW 36 billion in convertible bonds raised to fund the AdEfficiency acquisition continue to keep the debt ratio elevated. Whether the convertible bonds are converted or redeemed at maturity is a variable that could affect future capital structure and share dilution. The negative turn in operating cash flow in 2025 also warrants continued attention.

Business / Competitive

The digital ad-agency business is structurally exposed to the policies of a handful of global platforms. Changes in fee structures or algorithms at Meta, Google, TikTok, or Naver, as well as competitors expanding their own partnerships, could affect revenue and profitability. Renewal of contracts with large advertisers is also an important factor for earnings stability.

Market / Liquidity

As a small-cap KOSDAQ stock, both earnings and share price can be affected by macroeconomic shifts or a slowdown in advertising spending. The trading halt and re-listing process tied to the 5-for-1 share consolidation could generate short-term liquidity and price volatility.

12

Overall view

Having passed through the earnings trough of 2023, Wisebirds has sustained a profit recovery through 2024–2025 driven by synergies from the AdEfficiency acquisition and growth in the AI ad-automation platform Nest Ads Manager.

Revenue and operating profit both grew sharply year-on-year in the first half of 2026, continuing the earnings-improvement trend, and management expects a new record year based on the company's pronounced second-half seasonality.

That said, the debt ratio stemming from financing the AdEfficiency acquisition remains elevated, and quarterly net income has shown large swings tied to one-off items.

The first-ever share buyback, cancellation, and share consolidation can be read as a signal of strengthened shareholder returns, and the process by which the reduced share count is reflected in the market following the September 9 re-listing warrants continued observation.

Overall, the company sits in a phase where business growth and financial burden coexist, with third-quarter results, the technology division's overseas expansion, and changes in debt structure standing out as key items to watch.
