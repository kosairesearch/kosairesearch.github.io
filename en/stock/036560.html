02

Business structure

KZ Precision manufactures pumps and valves. Although the stock is classified under the automotive sector, its core business is industrial pump and valve manufacturing.

Revenue is generated from customer orders, so quarterly sales swing sharply, and the company says comparing cumulative results is more meaningful than comparing single quarters. Its main demand source is petrochemical plant investment, so sales and earnings structure move with that investment.

The company says investment contracted after the IMF crisis, competition intensified, and it has focused on expanding exports. Overseas market growth was cited as a driver of sales growth on a cumulative basis through the third quarter of 2025.

On governance, its largest shareholder group, including related parties, held 70.30% as of the end of December 2025. Its chairman is the uncle of the chairman of another listed group that is in a management-control dispute with a related company.

KZ Precision holds 3.76% of a related listed company and in March 2026 submitted shareholder proposals on governance and capital return. Dividend income from equity holdings is recorded apart from operating revenue and lifts net profit. A filing dated September 30, 2026 amended a main-shareholder change tied to a share collateral agreement.

04

Earnings analysis

Annual revenue rose 55.7% from KRW 89.1 billion in 2022 to KRW 138.7 billion in 2023, the largest year in the 2022 to 2025 span. It then fell 18.7% to KRW 112.7 billion in 2024 and 2.0% to KRW 110.5 billion in 2025, a two-year decline from 2024 through 2025.

Operating profit rose from KRW 12 billion in 2022 to a peak of KRW 20.8 billion in 2023, which was up 73.9%. It then fell 26.9% to KRW 15.2 billion in 2024 and 33.0% to KRW 10.2 billion in 2025, the smallest year in the span.

The operating margin moved from 13.4% in 2022 to 15.0% in 2023, 13.5% in 2024, and 9.2% in 2025. Net profit attributable to shareholders reached KRW 29.2 billion in 2023, above operating profit, and then fell 51.5% to KRW 9.3 billion in 2025.

The 2023 gap between net profit and operating profit reads as the effect of non-operating income. Dividend income rose from about KRW 3.1 billion in the first nine months of 2025 to about KRW 12.5 billion in the first nine months of 2026.

Equity at the end of 2025 was KRW 581.2 billion, all of it attributable to shareholders, while the debt ratio climbed from 21.0% in 2022 to 26.3% in 2025. Operating cash flow was KRW 20.3 billion in 2023 and KRW 22.2 billion in 2024, then KRW 12.2 billion in 2025.

In quarterly terms, net profit attributable to shareholders peaked at KRW 8.52 billion in the first quarter of 2026, then fell to KRW 6.86 billion and KRW 2.78 billion over the next two quarters.

Operating profit reached KRW 6.98 billion in the second quarter of 2026, up 282.3% from the prior quarter, then fell 67.2% to KRW 2.29 billion in the third quarter. Third-quarter 2026 revenue fell 45.7% from the prior quarter to KRW 18.6 billion.

Net profit attributable to shareholders over the last four quarters, from the fourth quarter of 2025 to the third quarter of 2026, totaled KRW 18.5 billion.

Cumulative revenue for January to September 2026 was KRW 75.85 billion, down 7.3% year on year, and cumulative operating profit was KRW 11.09 billion, up 26.0% (preliminary figures disclosed on November 5, 2026).

05

Industry analysis

KZ Precision's demand is tied to the petrochemical plant investment cycle in Korea and overseas. In an environment where competition intensified after investment contracted, the company has focused on expanding exports.

On a cumulative basis through the third quarter of 2025, overseas market expansion was cited in its filing as a driver of sales growth. This means revenue tends to rise when plant orders cluster and to fall when orders slow.

Quarterly revenue in 2026 moved sharply, at KRW 23 billion in the first quarter, KRW 34.3 billion in the second, and KRW 18.6 billion in the third. The pattern reflects the gap between order timing and revenue recognition, which feeds directly into quarterly results.

Where petrochemical investment is made, and how fast, is the variable that sets the direction of demand. In the competitive setting, the company has chosen to widen its overseas order share, and the results of that choice will appear in the revenue mix in its annual report.

The industry backdrop, in the company's own description, is a competitive phase that followed a period of weaker investment.

06

Outlook

Order intake is the most direct leading indicator the company discloses. Second-quarter 2026 new orders were about KRW 23 billion, up 19.2% from roughly KRW 19.3 billion in the first quarter, and third-quarter order intake totaled about KRW 28.9 billion, up 25.7% from the prior quarter.

How far the two consecutive increases convert into revenue will show up in the next annual report. The shareholder return plan the company has announced centers on improving the dividend procedure and maintaining a payout ratio of at least 40%.

At the March 2026 shareholder meeting, articles of incorporation were amended so that the dividend amount is fixed first and the record date is set afterward. The total year-end dividend for fiscal 2025 was KRW 12.6 billion, and the company presented a market dividend yield of 5%.

The company said it will maintain a sustainable dividend policy on the premise of securing stable cash flow. Dividend capacity depends on the size of operating cash flow and dividend income from its equity holdings.

With cumulative dividend income for January to September 2026 at about KRW 12.5 billion, whether that income continues is the next point to watch. The quantitative target the company has made public centers on keeping the payout ratio at 40% or higher.

Bull factors

Dividend procedure and policy overhaul

At the March 2026 shareholder meeting, the company amended its articles so that the dividend amount is fixed first and the record date is set afterward. It also outlined a plan to keep the payout ratio at 40% or higher and to maintain a sustainable dividend policy supported by stable cash flow.

The total dividend for fiscal 2025 was KRW 12.6 billion, and the company described itself as a high-dividend company under the Restriction of Special Taxation Act.

Improving order intake

Second-quarter 2026 new orders were about KRW 23 billion, up 19.2% from KRW 19.3 billion in the first quarter. Third-quarter order intake totaled about KRW 28.9 billion, up 25.7% from the prior quarter, marking two consecutive quarters of growth.

Second-quarter 2026 revenue of KRW 34.3 billion rose 49.2% from the prior quarter, showing orders converting into sales in that period.

Financial capacity

At the end of 2025, total liabilities were KRW 152.8 billion and equity was KRW 581.2 billion, for a debt ratio of 26.3%. All equity is attributable to shareholders, and the debt ratio rose from 21.0% in 2022 to 25.3% in 2024 and 26.3% in 2025. Operating cash flow was KRW 12.2 billion in 2025, close to the KRW 12.7 billion recorded in 2022.

Slowing operating profitability

The operating margin fell from 15.0% in 2023 to 9.2% in 2025. Third-quarter 2026 operating profit was KRW 2.29 billion, down 20.8% year on year.

Net profit rose while operating profit fell, which reflects the contribution of dividend income, so whether the core business recovers is decided by the operating profit trend.

Revenue volatility

Third-quarter 2026 revenue of KRW 18.6 billion fell 45.7% from KRW 34.3 billion in the second quarter. Cumulative revenue for January to September 2026 was KRW 75.85 billion, down 7.3% year on year (preliminary figures disclosed on November 5, 2026).

The company says its revenue is generated from orders, so cumulative comparisons are more meaningful, but quarter-to-quarter swings remain large.

Reliance on dividend income and governance

Cumulative dividend income for January to September 2026 was about KRW 12.5 billion, more than the cumulative operating profit of KRW 11.09 billion for the same period.

The largest shareholder group holds 70.30%, and a filing dated September 30, 2026 amended a main-shareholder change tied to a share collateral agreement. Governance issues tied to intergroup disputes could affect the direction of shareholder returns, and that uncertainty remains.

Risk factors

Governance and group dispute

The company is one side of an intergroup dispute, having submitted shareholder proposals to a related listed company that include a request for share buybacks and cancellation. That company has asked for further explanation on some items and has taken a cautious stance.

Whether the items are put to a vote depends on the related company's board review, and the outcome could change the direction of shareholder returns and the ownership structure.

Earnings volatility

Revenue swings sharply from quarter to quarter because of the order-based structure, and the operating margin fell from 15.0% in 2023 to 9.2% in 2025. Net profit depends on the size of dividend income, so it can diverge widely from operating performance. If petrochemical plant investment shrinks and orders fall, downside pressure on results could increase.

Insider holding changes

Director Kim Sung-il sold a total of 2,400 shares on the open market between March 3 and April 20, 2026, according to a April 27 filing. Executive director Park Chan-hee raised his holding to 1,358 shares through open-market purchases on January 30 and February 4, 2026. Director Choi Jong-tae added 1,000 shares through open-market purchases on the same two dates.

Overall view

KZ Precision's sales and operating profit peaked in 2023 and then declined for two consecutive years through 2025, with the operating margin falling to 9.2% in 2025.

Quarterly results in 2026 have swung sharply on revenue, and net profit attributable to shareholders peaked at KRW 8.52 billion in the first quarter before falling over the next two quarters.

On the other side, order intake rose in the second and third quarters of 2026, and the company disclosed third-quarter order intake of about KRW 28.9 billion.

Dividend income now makes up a large share of net profit, and of the KRW 18.16 billion in cumulative net profit for January to September 2026, about KRW 12.5 billion came from dividend income.

The company has disclosed a payout ratio of at least 40% and an improved dividend procedure, while the largest shareholder group's 70.30% stake means governance and intergroup disputes could influence the direction of shareholder returns.
