02

Business structure

Hurum is a specialist company centered on health functional foods. Its core health functional food business is being run with an emphasis on widening sales channels and products, and a securities firm report described it as holding up relatively well even though the health functional food market is weak.

Its consolidated subsidiaries include a filler subsidiary, and a recent securities firm report highlighted that filler sales are growing rapidly. A manufacturing affiliate is also cited as a contributor to revenue.

In its corporate value enhancement plan disclosed on March 26, 2026, the company set out business diversification through new businesses and overseas exports, along with better production efficiency and integrated logistics.

It also said affiliates would open their domestic and overseas distribution channels to each other to pursue shelf placement and joint promotions.

At an extraordinary shareholders meeting on October 7, 2026, five additional business purposes were approved, including sales of bio-material products, medical device wholesale and retail, warehousing and logistics agency, marketing agency, and software development.

The company said the purpose is to expand business areas and secure new growth drivers. The filler market is seeing rising demand while treatment prices are falling, and some analysts expect growth to be led by emerging markets in Asia and Eastern Europe.

04

Earnings analysis

Per the DART periodic reports, annual sales were KRW 66.9 billion in 2022, KRW 88.4 billion in 2023 (up 32.1% year on year), KRW 98.2 billion in 2024 (up 11.0%), and KRW 99 billion in 2025 (up 0.9%).

Sales have grown for three consecutive years from 2023 through 2025, and 2025 is the largest year in the 2022 to 2025 span. Growth slowed to 0.9% in 2025.

Operating results moved from an operating loss of KRW 1.7 billion in 2022 to operating profit of KRW 3.76 billion in 2023, a turn to profit, then fell 72.0% to KRW 1.05 billion in 2024 before recovering 78.6% to KRW 1.87 billion in 2025.

The operating margin was -2.5% in 2022, 4.2% in 2023, 1.1% in 2024, and 1.9% in 2025, so margins have stayed low since the 2023 peak.

Net profit attributable to controlling shareholders went from a net loss of KRW 1.32 billion in 2022 to KRW 3.27 billion in 2023, a turn to profit, then fell 46.3% to KRW 1.76 billion in 2024 and 33.9% to KRW 1.16 billion in 2025, two consecutive years of decline.

Consolidated net income including non-controlling interests was KRW 958 million in 2025, a different figure from the controlling-shareholder number. At year-end 2025, total equity was KRW 46.3 billion, of which KRW 42.7 billion belonged to controlling shareholders, and total liabilities were KRW 52.7 billion.

The debt ratio rose from 63.6% in 2022 to 84.0% in 2023, 96.6% in 2024, and 113.8% in 2025. Operating cash flow was KRW 2.83 billion in 2023 and KRW 3.31 billion in 2024, then fell to KRW 75.86 million in 2025.

Quarterly, sales were KRW 26.3 billion in the third quarter of 2025 with operating profit of KRW 7.04 million and a net loss attributable to controlling shareholders of KRW 540 million.

In the fourth quarter of 2025, sales were KRW 23.5 billion, operating profit KRW 1.04 billion, and net profit attributable to controlling shareholders KRW 114 million, a turn to profit.

In the first quarter of 2026, sales were KRW 23 billion with an operating loss of KRW 219 million, and in the second quarter sales were KRW 22 billion with an operating loss of KRW 813 million.

Third-quarter 2026 sales were KRW 23.7 billion, up 8.1% from the prior quarter, and operating profit turned positive at KRW 93.39 million, though the net loss attributable to controlling shareholders stayed at KRW 178 million.

Securities firm reports cited growth at the filler subsidiary and sales contributions from a manufacturing affiliate as the main growth drivers.

05

Industry analysis

Hurum operates in the health functional food market, which securities firm reports describe as weak. Within that backdrop, Hurum has been described as holding up relatively well in its industry on the back of channel expansion and product diversification.

This assessment is a qualitative description from securities firm reports, and no numerical peer comparison is included here. One analysis points to rising demand in the filler market alongside falling treatment prices.

In that setting, a key variable is whether the filler subsidiary can offset lower prices with higher volumes. Growth expected in emerging markets such as Asia and Eastern Europe also aligns with the direction of the subsidiary's overseas sales.

The 2026 corporate value enhancement plan lists building a global marketing base and identifying export hub countries as tasks. How much the export share grows while the domestic health functional food market is weak will shape the company's position.

Within the food and beverage sector, Hurum's sales scale is small compared with large listed peers. Whether shared channels between affiliates translate into higher sales is therefore the starting point for assessing its position.

Outlook

The corporate value enhancement plan the company disclosed in March 2026 sets out revenue expansion, profit-focused management, and shareholder value as directions, without quantitative targets.

Its execution tasks are finding export hub countries, improving production efficiency, integrating logistics, and opening distribution channels between affiliates. Whether production and logistics gains show up in the operating margin is the next checkpoint.

The new business purposes approved at the October 2026 extraordinary shareholders meeting cover bio-materials, medical device distribution, logistics agency, marketing agency, and software development, which provide a basis for widening the business scope.

Whether the operating profit turn in the third quarter of 2026 carries into subsequent quarters matters. On November 11, 2026, KRW 1 billion of the second convertible bond was repurchased before maturity, and on November 18, 2026, KRW 12 billion of the third convertible bond was acquired over the counter.

The company reported zero remaining principal on the third bond, and the purchase was funded with own cash and borrowings. Fewer convertible bonds outstanding eases dilution concerns, but higher borrowings could add to financial burden.

The Korea Exchange said market capitalization had been below KRW 20 billion for 25 consecutive trading days as of November 12, 2026, and that a managed issue designation is possible if the condition continues for five more trading days. A 5-for-1 share consolidation took effect in April 2026, when trading was suspended.

Risk factors

Financial structure

The debt ratio rose from 63.6% in 2022 to 113.8% in 2025. Because part of the convertible bond repurchase was funded with borrowings, interest burden and liquidity management are challenges. The drop in operating cash flow to KRW 75.86 million in 2025 also needs monitoring as a gauge of funding capacity.

Listing maintenance criteria

Under the Korea Exchange rule, a market capitalization below KRW 20 billion sustained for 30 consecutive trading days leads to designation as a managed issue. The exchange reported 25 consecutive trading days as of November 12, 2026.

Whether a designation follows should be checked in exchange disclosures, and designation could affect trading and financing conditions.

Business environment and overseas expansion

Securities firm reports describe the health functional food market as weak, and filler treatment prices are falling. If overseas export expansion and new business execution in the corporate value enhancement plan proceed slower than expected, top-line growth could be limited.

Whether the effects of production efficiency and logistics integration show up in the operating margin also needs checking.
