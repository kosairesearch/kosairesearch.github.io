02

Business structure

Forcs was founded in July 2008 as a software company that develops electronic document generation and reporting solutions for web and mobile environments, supplying public agencies, financial institutions and corporates.

Its business is split into e-document, report and cloud segments, and it also distributes Apple devices to businesses as an Apple authorized reseller. By segment, e-documents accounted for 48% of revenue, reports 31%, cloud 14% and other 8% as of March 2026.

Company information sites present OZ e-Form and OZ Report as holding the top share of the domestic market. The eformsign e-contract service moved to a subscription (SaaS) model and has widened its base from small firms to large corporates.

Overseas, Japan and Southeast Asia are the focus; in Japan the company built a bundled product with Fujitsu to get past entry barriers, and in Southeast Asia it strengthened technical support and sales through local partners.

The domestic e-document market has shifted from foreign-software dominance toward domestic reporting tools. Customers announced in 2022 centered on securities firms' digital counters, card and insurance non-face-to-face systems, and public projects.

04

Earnings analysis

On an annual basis, revenue fell from KRW 33.2 billion in the fiscal year ended June 2023 to KRW 31.3 billion in the fiscal year ended June 2024, down 5.9% year on year, before rising to KRW 32.9 billion in the fiscal year ended June 2025 (up 5.1%) and KRW 35.5 billion in the fiscal year ended June 2026 (up 8.0%).

Operating profit went from KRW 5.16 billion to KRW 4.52 billion (down 12.4%) and then KRW 4.4 billion (down 2.6%) before recovering to KRW 5.78 billion in the fiscal year ended June 2026 (up 31.3%). The operating margin moved from 15.5% to 14.4% and 13.4%, then up to 16.3% in the fiscal year ended June 2026.

Because operating profit grew faster than revenue, margin recovery drove the profit rebound. Net income attributable to controlling shareholders reached KRW 7.4 billion in the fiscal year ended June 2026, up 62.7% year on year, and net income including non-controlling interests was also reported as KRW 7.4 billion.

In a 2025 interview, the CEO said AI and talent investment had reduced the margin compared with the past, while the company aimed to keep it in a 15–20% range. Operating cash flow ran at KRW 5.31 billion, KRW 7.17 billion, KRW 6.65 billion and then KRW 7.62 billion across the four fiscal years.

Quarterly profit clustered in specific periods. Q4 2025 revenue of KRW 12.6 billion (up 67.1% from the prior quarter) and operating profit of KRW 4 billion were the largest values among the last five quarters, compared with operating profit of KRW 97.88 million in Q3 2025.

Q1 2026 revenue of KRW 6.04 billion (down 52.1% quarter on quarter) came with an operating loss of KRW 49.56 million, a swing into loss, and Q2 2026 returned to operating profit of KRW 1.73 billion on revenue of KRW 9.32 billion.

Q3 2026 revenue of KRW 7.63 billion rose 1.0% year on year, while operating profit of KRW 749 million fell 56.7% from the prior quarter and rose 664.7% from a year earlier.

Net income attributable to controlling shareholders was KRW 4.64 billion in Q4 2025, KRW 1.48 billion in Q1 2026, KRW 968 million in Q2 2026 and KRW 1.42 billion in Q3 2026, totaling KRW 8.5 billion over the last four quarters.

The gap between operating profit and net income in some quarters is a point to verify in the quarterly reports' non-operating items.

An outside report noted that when customers' on-premise solution deployments concentrate in certain years, revenue rises in that year and the comparison base becomes higher the following year.

05

Industry analysis

Demand for e-document and e-contract software is being driven by the spread of AI and cloud, the settling of non-face-to-face work, and rising digital transformation demand, as the company's own performance commentary describes. The company started with reporting solutions and is widening its focus to cloud and AI.

In the financial sector, demand for e-documents was lifted by AI digital counter systems and non-face-to-face contract platforms, according to a June 2026 report. On-premise solution revenue depends on customers' deployment schedules, contract size and revenue recognition timing, so annual results can vary.

An outside research house, the Korea IR Council, forecast a decline in Forcs' results in a recent report, yet the disclosed results for the fiscal year ended June 2026 showed both revenue and operating profit rising.

The gap between the outside forecast and disclosed results will be clarified in upcoming periodic reports. An industry tally based on the fiscal year ended June 2025 showed Forcs' operating margin well above the industry average.

Overseas, the company is expanding from Japan into Southeast Asia, and it expects medical-sector adoption in Japan. The company also said recruiting support staff in Japan is difficult.

06

Outlook

The company is pursuing overseas markets while using its cloud business to widen its customer base. Its AI-based automation tool analyzes documents and proposes fields and formulas, is in trial service, and is currently offered free to existing customers.

A 2025 press report said the company planned to launch a paid model in 2026. The company said the tool also fits large corporates that run on-premise versions, so it sees use in both packaged and cloud formats.

On costs, the company acknowledged that investment has grown faster than revenue, and it has expanded its lower-cost development team in Vietnam from 2 people to 6 to raise the share of development work.

The fiscal year ended June 2026 operating margin of 16.3% sits within the 15–20% range the company said it aims to maintain. The Korea IR Council suggested that shareholder-value plans and the business structure should be reviewed alongside results.

The same outside report framed the key question as whether the expected decline reflects a temporary deployment cycle or a change in growth by business line. Upcoming periodic reports should show the share of on-premise revenue and cloud growth.

08

Bull factors

Two Years of Revenue Growth and Profit Recovery

Revenue rose from KRW 32.9 billion in the fiscal year ended June 2025 to KRW 35.5 billion in the fiscal year ended June 2026, a second straight year of growth. Operating profit grew 31.3% to KRW 5.78 billion, and net income attributable to controlling shareholders rose 62.7% to KRW 7.4 billion. Profit growth outpaced revenue growth, which points to an improving earnings structure.

Margin Recovery Within a Stated Range

The operating margin recovered from 13.4% in the fiscal year ended June 2025 to 16.3% in the fiscal year ended June 2026. The CEO said in a 2025 press report that the company aims to keep the margin at 15–20%, and the latest figure falls within that range. Margin recovery while investment continues is read as a bullish point.

Financial Stability and External Rating

Total equity was KRW 81 billion at the end of June 2026, with a 6.2% debt ratio. Operating cash flow was KRW 7.62 billion in the fiscal year ended June 2026, up from KRW 5.31 billion in the fiscal year ended June 2023. A corporate information service rated the company's stability in the top 3% of its industry peers.

09

Bear factors

High Volatility in Quarterly Profit

Q1 2026 revenue of KRW 6.04 billion fell 52.1% from the prior quarter and the company posted an operating loss of KRW 49.56 million. Q3 2026 operating profit of KRW 749 million fell 56.7% from the prior quarter. Even with annual improvement, large quarterly swings make earnings harder to forecast.

Rising Investment Burden

In a 2025 press report, the company said investment has grown faster than revenue and that it is working on ways to cut costs. Headcount has expanded to about 200, and the cloud business requires continued hiring. Whether margin recovery can absorb this investment load is the key question.

Deployment Cycle and Outside Forecast

The Korea IR Council cited a high base effect from customers' on-premise deployment cycles in forecasting a decline in results in a recent report. The disclosed results for the fiscal year ended June 2026 showed growth, so the gap between forecast and actuals is a point to verify. Concentrated on-premise deals in one year still raise volatility the following year.

10

Risk factors

Earnings volatility

Quarterly revenue moves sharply with customers' deployment schedules and revenue recognition timing. The gap between KRW 12.6 billion in Q4 2025 and KRW 6.04 billion in Q1 2026 illustrates this.

Even with strong annual numbers, quarterly losses can appear, so quarterly disclosures should be read alongside annual figures.

Investment and business transition

Cloud, SaaS and AI automation require up-front investment. A 2025 press report described a plan to introduce paid AI features, but their actual revenue contribution will need to be confirmed in later disclosures.

Overseas growth relies on the Fujitsu partnership in Japan and local partners in Southeast Asia, which is also a risk factor.

Ownership concentration and liquidity

As of an October 2026 tally, the largest shareholder group held 51.86% and treasury shares stood at 3.99%. A free-float ratio of around 44% can limit trading liquidity. Foreign ownership is low at 1.50%.

12

Overall view

Forcs is a software company built on electronic document and reporting solutions, and its disclosed results for the fiscal year ended June 2026 show revenue of KRW 35.5 billion and operating profit of KRW 5.78 billion.

Revenue has risen for two straight years, and the operating margin recovered to 16.3% from 13.4% in the fiscal year ended June 2025. Total equity of KRW 81 billion and a 6.2% debt ratio indicate a fairly stable financial structure.

On the other side, Q1 2026 posted an operating loss, and Q3 2026 operating profit fell 56.7% from the prior quarter, so quarterly swings are large. The company is continuing investment in cloud, AI automation and overseas expansion, and it has described investment growing faster than revenue.

An outside report's forecast of a decline did not match the disclosed results. A largest shareholder stake of 51.86% is also a factor to watch. The next checkpoints are the operating margin in the half-year report due in mid-February 2027, Q4 2026 results, and whether the AI feature moves to paid pricing.
