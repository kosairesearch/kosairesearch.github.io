Forcs: Profit Recovery Amid Quarterly Swings

Forcs posted higher sales and operating profit in the fiscal year ended June 2026, but quarterly earnings remain volatile and the largest shareholder holds a concentrated stake.

02

Business structure

Forcs is a software company whose core business is electronic document and reporting solutions. Its main products are OZ e-Form, a document development solution, OZ Report, a reporting solution, and eformsign, a cloud-based e-contract service that the company is using to expand its subscription business.

According to Alphasquare's company page, sales are split roughly 52% electronic documents, 46% reporting, 1.3% eformsign and 1.3% other. A JobKorea breakdown shows 48% electronic documents, 31% reporting, 14% cloud and 8% other, so the mix depends on how segments are classified.

The customer base centers on finance and the public sector. In finance, the company has built digital counter systems for securities firms, a tablet branch system for a card company, and video-based non-face-to-face contract and consultation systems for banks and insurers.

In the public sector, its company press release lists projects for the Ministry of the Interior and Safety, the Ministry of Foreign Affairs, the Ministry of Justice, the Ministry of National Defense, the Supreme Court and KEPCO.

In manufacturing, distribution and services, the same release cites work for Samsung Electronics, POSCO, Hyundai E&C, CJ Logistics and BGF Retail. The eformsign service has added references including Hyundai Motor, SK Telecom, LG Chem and GS Caltex.

The company says it has more than 5,000 customer references and works with about 100 partners across more than 20 countries, centered on Korea, Japan and Southeast Asia.

Competition comes from large system integrators such as Samsung SDS, LG CNS and Hyundai Autoever, plus cloud platform providers moving into document workflows. Forcs is also preparing to commercialize an AI assistant built on a large language model.

04

Earnings analysis

Annual sales were KRW 33.2 billion in the fiscal year ended June 2023, KRW 31.3 billion in the year ended June 2024 (down 5.9% year on year), KRW 32.9 billion in the year ended June 2025 (up 5.1%) and KRW 35.5 billion in the year ended June 2026 (up 8.0%).

Sales rose for two consecutive fiscal years, from the year ended June 2025 through the year ended June 2026, and the year ended June 2026 was the largest of the four.

Operating profit fell from KRW 5.16 billion to KRW 4.52 billion (down 12.4%) and then to KRW 4.4 billion (down 2.6%), before rising to KRW 5.78 billion (up 31.3%) in the year ended June 2026. Operating margin moved from 15.5% and 14.4% to 13.4%, then recovered to 16.3%, above the fiscal 2023 level.

Operating profit grew faster than sales in the latest year, which means costs rose more slowly than revenue; the cost line items will be checked in the next annual report. Profit attributable to owners of the parent reached KRW 7.4 billion in the year ended June 2026, up 62.7% and the highest of the four years.

Net income including non-controlling interests was the same KRW 7.4 billion. Operating cash flow rose from KRW 5.31 billion in the year ended June 2023 to KRW 7.62 billion in the year ended June 2026. Quarterly results are uneven.

Operating profit of KRW 97.88 million in the quarter ended September 2025 fell 86.0% from the prior quarter, while KRW 4 billion in the quarter ended December 2025 rose 3,983.1%.

Sales peaked at KRW 12.6 billion that quarter, then fell 52.1% to KRW 6.04 billion in the quarter ended March 2026, when an operating loss of KRW 49.56 million appeared.

In the quarter ended June 2026, sales of KRW 9.32 billion (up 31.3% year on year) and operating profit of KRW 1.73 billion (up 146.6%) returned the company to profit.

Profit attributable to owners of the parent fell for two consecutive quarters after the KRW 4.64 billion peak in the quarter ended December 2025, to KRW 1.48 billion and then KRW 968 million. Because quarterly figures swing widely, the annual trend is the more reliable reference point.

05

Industry analysis

The electronic document and e-contract market is growing on the back of AI and cloud adoption, the settling of remote work practices and demand for digital transformation. Electronic documents and reporting, Forcs' core areas, generate steady demand as companies move paperwork online.

However, the Korea IR Council pointed to a lag effect tied to the installation cycle of on-premise solutions, which can make revenue lumpy when deployments cluster. The disclosed fiscal year ended June 2026 showed sales of KRW 35.5 billion, up 8.0%, the opposite direction from that projection.

The reporting segment is reportedly shifting faster toward cloud-based BI solutions as demand grows. Because the business mixes on-premise and subscription revenue, the balance between those two lines will shape earnings stability.

On competition, Forcs is far smaller than large system integrators such as Samsung SDS, LG CNS and Hyundai Autoever, but it has built a distinct position in specialized electronic document software.

The company says it holds a number one share in the public sector and cites more than 20 domestic and overseas patents and awards as evidence; this is the company's own claim and should be weighed against independent market share data.

Internationally, the company is building partner networks in Japan and Southeast Asia. A medium-term variable is the spread of generative AI document features from large platform companies. If those features are bundled or offered free, pricing power for specialized solutions could weaken.

06

Outlook

The disclosed fiscal year ended June 2026 becomes the baseline for the next annual comparison: sales of KRW 35.5 billion, operating profit of KRW 5.78 billion and profit attributable to owners of the parent of KRW 7.4 billion.

Operating profit recovered sharply in the quarter ended June 2026, at KRW 1.73 billion on sales of KRW 9.32 billion, but profit attributable to owners of the parent is still below its December 2025 quarter peak.

The quarter ended September 2026 has a quarterly report filing deadline of November 14, 2026, and will be the first quarter after the disclosed period to appear in a filing.

The AI assistant and the large language model-based service are being prepared for commercialization, and their revenue contribution has not yet appeared in figures. Whether eformsign's growing references among large enterprises raise the share of subscription revenue is a key point to watch.

Overseas, the company is expanding through partner networks in Japan and Southeast Asia. A shareholder-value plan has been cited as a new point of focus, but its timing and scale should be checked against upcoming disclosures.

Whether the installation-cycle effect flagged by the Korea IR Council shows up again in future filings is another question. Judgments should rest on disclosed results and periodic reports, since the company's numerical guidance was not part of the material reviewed.

Bull factors

Wider eformsign references

The company says eformsign has added large references including Hyundai Motor, SK Telecom, LG Chem and GS Caltex. Continued demand for remote contracts could raise the share of subscription revenue. The electronic document market is growing on digital transformation demand.

Profit recovery and cash generation

Operating profit in the year ended June 2026 was KRW 5.78 billion, up 31.3%, and operating margin of 16.3% exceeded the fiscal 2023 level. Operating cash flow of KRW 7.62 billion was also above the KRW 5.31 billion of the year ended June 2023. Profit and cash rising together is evidence of a recovering earnings base.

Financial capacity and shareholder-return plans

Total equity at the end of June 2026 was KRW 81 billion, liabilities were KRW 5.03 billion and the debt ratio was 6.2%. Pinpoint News reported that a shareholder-value plan has become a new point of focus. Timing and scale still need to be confirmed through disclosures.

Bear factors

Sharp quarterly volatility

After KRW 4 billion of operating profit in the quarter ended December 2025, the company posted an operating loss of KRW 49.56 million in the quarter ended March 2026. Profit attributable to owners of the parent fell for two consecutive quarters, from KRW 4.64 billion to KRW 1.48 billion and then KRW 968 million. Large quarterly swings make the annual trend harder to read.

Installation-cycle risk

The Korea IR Council projected that both sales and operating profit would fall in 2026 due to a lag effect from customers' installation cycles. The disclosed fiscal year ended June 2026 showed growth, but the timing of on-premise deployments can still move results. Whether the effect reappears in future filings needs checking.

Concentrated ownership and liquidity

The largest shareholder group holds about 51.86% of shares, and the free float ratio has been reported at about 44.04%. Foreign ownership is also low, at about 1.63%. A limited float can affect trading liquidity and price volatility.

Risk factors

Customer and project concentration

Sales depend heavily on finance and public-sector projects. When large deliveries cluster in particular quarters, earnings swing more. A change in public procurement timing or in financial firms' system replacement cycles could move quarterly revenue.

Technology substitution and platform competition

If large platform companies bundle generative AI document features, pricing power for specialized solutions could weaken. Large system integrators could also expand into electronic documents. The company is responding by integrating large-scale AI, but commercial results have not yet appeared in figures.

Small-cap liquidity and scale

With a small market capitalization and a low free float, trading can be thin. The size gap with large system integrators could weigh on competition for big projects. As a small-cap, the share price may react sharply to external shocks.

Overall view

In the fiscal year ended June 2026, Forcs posted sales of KRW 35.5 billion, operating profit of KRW 5.78 billion and profit attributable to owners of the parent of KRW 7.4 billion, the largest year in the four-year span. An operating margin of 16.3% confirms the earnings recovery in disclosed figures.

On the balance sheet, a debt ratio of 6.2% and total equity of KRW 81 billion leave room. Quarterly results are less steady: the company returned to operating profit in the quarter ended June 2026 after a loss in the March quarter, yet profit attributable to owners of the parent has fallen for two consecutive quarters.

The installation-cycle effect flagged by the Korea IR Council and the spread of generative AI document features from large platforms remain variables to track. A largest-shareholder stake of about 51.86% also limits the free float. Valuation reads best alongside both the earnings recovery and the quarterly swings.

The next checkpoints are the quarterly report for the quarter ending September 2026 and the one for the quarter ending March 2027, which will show whether quarterly profit is holding.
