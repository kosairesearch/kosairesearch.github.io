02

Business structure

Sambo Motors supplies automatic transmission parts, fuel-system pipes and plastic exterior parts such as bumpers and spoilers to Hyundai Motor, KIA and other automakers. According to a company overview, it produces transmission parts and pipe products, while its subsidiaries manufacture plastic products and molds.

In Q2 2026 sales, plastic parts made up 70%, Pipe Parts 17%, Auto Parts 8% and molds 4%, according to a corporate information service. Hyundai Motor and KIA hold more than 80% share of the plastic parts business, but competitive bidding and global sourcing by automakers keep profitability limited even as sales grow.

In a 2024 press report, the company was described as supplying 60% to 70% of Hyundai Motor's steel pipes. Its customer base is concentrated in the Hyundai Motor Group, so automaker sales flow directly into volumes.

The Mexico subsidiary began supplying plastic injection and painted parts for the group in around August 2024. A 2025 report said a contract with Hyundai Mobis runs through December 2031 for 15 part families, with a total value of KRW 145.4 billion.

The India subsidiary, established in 2024, is at the initial volume-securing stage. Product lines are expanding into high-voltage busbars for EVs and parts for hybrid vehicles. Urban air mobility and AMR initiatives have been presented as new businesses, but no sales figures showing their scale have been disclosed.

04

Earnings analysis

Annual revenue rose from KRW 1.2 trillion in 2022 to KRW 1.44 trillion in 2023 (up 20.1% year on year), KRW 1.57 trillion in 2024 (up 9.0%) and KRW 1.68 trillion in 2025 (up 7.3%), with revenue increasing for three consecutive years from 2023 through 2025. The largest year in 2022–2025 was 2025.

Operating profit climbed from KRW 22.9 billion in 2022 to KRW 51.6 billion in 2023 (up 125.4%), then KRW 54.3 billion in 2024 (up 5.1%) and KRW 61.7 billion in 2025 (up 13.7%), also increasing for three consecutive years from 2023 through 2025.

Operating margin was 1.9% in 2022, 3.6% in 2023, 3.5% in 2024 and 3.7% in 2025, staying in the mid-3% range since 2023. Margin gains have been modest relative to revenue growth.

Net income attributable to controlling shareholders was KRW 21.4 billion in 2022, KRW 29.8 billion in 2023 (up 39.5%) and KRW 43.8 billion in 2024 (up 47.0%), the largest year in the period, before falling 53.8% to KRW 20.3 billion in 2025.

Net income including non-controlling interests was also KRW 20.3 billion in 2025. The company attributed the 2025 decline to higher derivative valuation losses from a rise in the year-end share price, despite higher customer volumes and hybrid vehicle sales.

By quarter, the company moved from a controlling-shareholder profit of KRW 9.91 billion in Q3 2025 to a loss of KRW 13.7 billion in Q4 2025, returned to a profit of KRW 7.7 billion in Q1 2026, posted KRW 5.31 billion in Q2 2026 (down 31.0% from the prior quarter), and fell back to a loss of KRW 20.4 billion in Q3 2026.

Q3 2026 operating profit of KRW 22.6 billion rose 319.1% year on year, yet the same quarter was a net loss. A 2025 report said the Mexico subsidiary recognized about KRW 14.0 billion in losses from early defect rates in 2024.

Operating cash flow was KRW 71.7 billion in 2022, KRW 138 billion in 2023, KRW 90 billion in 2024 and KRW 88.4 billion in 2025. Total liabilities at year-end 2025 were KRW 918.5 billion against total equity of KRW 419.4 billion.

05

Industry analysis

Sambo Motors' results depend heavily on automaker sales. A 2024 press report cited Hyundai Motor Group's strong vehicle sales and new orders as drivers of growth. The same report flagged a prolonged slowdown in EV demand as a variable for the payback on new eco-friendly car parts investments.

The company's parts serve internal combustion, EV and hybrid vehicles, so the mix of orders may shift with the pace of electrification. In 2025, the company cited higher hybrid vehicle sales as a factor in its results. Competitive bidding and global sourcing by automakers add pricing pressure on plastic parts.

According to a 2026 analysis by ThinkPool covering Q1 2026, Sambo Motors' operating margin was among the lowest of its peers. Listed companies in the same sector include Hyundai Mobis, Hanon Systems, Sungwoo Hitech, Seoyon E-Hwa and Myoung Shin Industry.

Sambo Motors is characterized by its status as a Tier 1 supplier to automakers and its concentration in plastic parts. Where the sector sits in its cycle is difficult to determine, and the period calls for monitoring automaker production and sales data alongside EV demand trends.

06

Outlook

The 2026 corporate value enhancement plan, approved by the board on October 19, 2026 and disclosed on October 22, sets medium-term targets. It uses the 3.7% operating margin of 2025 as its starting point and targets 4.5% or higher for 2027.

To get there, the company plans to win more eco-friendly car orders for motor oil coolers, busbars, HEV rotor assemblies and battery management systems, and to raise the share of higher-value eco-friendly parts and overseas customer volumes. Smart factory and AI transformation are cited as cost-reduction tools.

Reporting noted that these figures are company targets, and whether they are achieved will be confirmed through future orders and business results. Overseas, the long-term Hyundai Mobis supply contract underpins volumes.

The Mexico subsidiary set stabilizing its early defect rate as a goal, and the India subsidiary is at the initial volume-securing stage. Hybrid urban air mobility and AMR parts are in development, but no revenue timing has been disclosed.

The company also presented PBR targets of 0.22 times for 2026, 0.3 times or higher for 2027 and 0.45 times for 2029. The next checkpoints are whether the 2027 operating margin tracks the target path, whether eco-friendly orders show up in the revenue mix, and whether overseas defect rates are reflected in results.

Bull factors

Three years of operating profit growth

Operating profit rose from KRW 22.9 billion in 2022 to KRW 61.7 billion in 2025, increasing for three consecutive years from 2023 through 2025. Q3 2026 operating profit of KRW 22.6 billion was up 319.1% year on year, and net income attributable to controlling shareholders was positive in Q1 and Q2 2026. Recovery in operating-level profitability is the core argument on the bullish side.

Overseas footprint and eco-friendly orders

The Mexico subsidiary began supplying plastic injection and painted parts to the Hyundai Motor Group around August 2024, and a report said it signed a contract with Hyundai Mobis to supply 15 part families through December 2031.

The company targets an operating margin of 4.5% or higher in 2027 and plans to expand orders for motor oil coolers, busbars, HEV rotor assemblies and BMS. If long-term contracts and electrified parts expand as planned, the revenue mix could change.

Disclosed PBR targets in value enhancement plan

The value enhancement plan approved by the board in October 2026 set a PBR target of 0.45 times for 2029. Total equity at year-end 2025 was KRW 419.4 billion, with the same amount attributable to controlling shareholders. With market capitalization well below equity, the company's public PBR targets are cited as a bullish argument.

Bear factors

Gap between operating and net profit

The bearish view focuses on the gap between operating profit and net profit. Derivative valuation losses were cited for the 53.8% decline in 2025 controlling-shareholder net income, so the extent to which earnings swings reflect factors outside core operations needs checking. In Q3 2026, operating profit rose while the quarter still recorded a net loss.

Thin margins and pricing pressure

Operating margin improved from 1.9% in 2022 to 3.7% in 2025 but remains in the low single digits. Hyundai Motor and KIA hold more than 80% share of the plastic parts business, yet competitive bidding and global sourcing put pricing pressure on it. ThinkPool's Q1 2026 comparison placed the company's operating margin among the lowest of its peers.

Financial burden and convertible bonds

Total liabilities at year-end 2025 were KRW 918.5 billion against total equity of KRW 419.4 billion, leaving a debt-to-equity ratio of 219.0%. A report based on Q1 2025 data pointed to a high share of short-term debt in total borrowings.

A conversion price adjustment was disclosed on July 29, 2026, and concerns about put options on convertible bonds have been raised.

Risk factors

Customer concentration and automaker demand

Most revenue is tied to Hyundai Motor Group vehicle sales, so slower sales or shifts in model mix pass directly into volumes. A 2024 report noted a prolonged slowdown in EV demand, which could affect the pace of the eco-friendly parts expansion. The company cited hybrid sales as a driver, but demand swings linked to model mix remain.

Overseas subsidiary quality and payback

A report said the Mexico subsidiary recognized about KRW 14.0 billion in losses from early defect rates in 2024. If defect-rate stabilization is delayed, the expected contribution from North American operations could also slip.

The India subsidiary is still securing initial volumes, and the pace of overseas payback underpins the operating margin target.

Capital structure and management change

The debt-to-equity ratio eased from 227.7% in 2022 to 219.0% in 2025 but remains above 200%. A change of CEO was disclosed on July 9, 2026, according to a disclosure list. With convertible bond disclosures ongoing, continuity of management direction and funding plans remains a point to watch.

Overall view

Sambo Motors grew revenue from KRW 1.2 trillion in 2022 to KRW 1.68 trillion in 2025 on a customer base centered on Hyundai Motor and KIA, and operating profit reached KRW 61.7 billion in 2025, rising for three consecutive years from 2023.

The operating margin stayed in the 3% range at 3.7% in 2025, so margin gains have lagged revenue growth. Net income attributable to controlling shareholders fell to KRW 20.3 billion in 2025, and the company attributed the decline to higher derivative valuation losses.

Controlling-shareholder net income was positive in Q1 and Q2 2026, but Q3 2026 returned to a loss of KRW 20.4 billion, and the four-quarter total of losses is KRW 21.2 billion. Q3 2026 operating profit of KRW 22.6 billion was up 319.1% year on year, so the recovery at the operating level is clear.

Bullish factors include Mexico and India subsidiaries, the expansion of eco-friendly parts orders and the long-term Hyundai Mobis contract. Bearish factors include repeated net losses, a debt-to-equity ratio of 219.0% and a high share of short-term debt.

The company has set 2027 and 2029 targets for operating margin and PBR, but these remain company targets.

The next checkpoints are the 2026 annual business report, the Q1 2027 results and the 2027 half-year report, where the direction of operating margin and controlling-shareholder net income will show whether they align.
