02

Business structure

Onejoon runs two core businesses: heat-treatment kilns used to make battery cathode and anode materials, and process-equipment engineering that designs, builds and commissions material production lines.

A company profile summary says the firm is also pursuing new efforts in heat-treatment solutions for all-solid-state and carbon-fiber materials and in battery recycling solutions. By revenue type in 2024, product sales (mainly RHK kilns and similar equipment) were 56.3% exports and 43.7% domestic.

Engineering services in the same year were 100% domestic, while other revenue such as paid after-sales service and rental income was 73.2% domestic.

The RHK is a roller hearth kiln used to sinter multilayer ceramic capacitors (MLCCs), and press reports describe Onejoon as the first domestic company to localize this equipment. The same reports describe the company as a key supplier to Samsung Electro-Mechanics.

A 2023 brokerage report named a large cathode-materials maker as its main customer at the time. Because orders depend on customers' plant expansion schedules, quarterly revenue can swing sharply with the timing of equipment orders.

As of June 2025, consolidated subsidiaries were two companies, one in real estate leasing and one in investment. The new businesses draw on demand separate from battery plant expansion, so whether they can offset the slowdown in core orders is the central structural question.

04

Earnings analysis

On a reported basis, annual sales went from KRW 143.2 billion in 2022 to KRW 137.3 billion in 2023, a 4.2% decline from the prior year, then rose to KRW 149.9 billion in 2024, a 9.2% increase, and eased to KRW 149.1 billion in 2025, a 0.5% decline.

Across 2022 to 2025, 2024 was the largest year for sales and 2023 the smallest. Operating profit fell from KRW 10.9 billion in 2022 to KRW 4.64 billion in 2023, a 57.4% decline, recovered to KRW 12.4 billion in 2024, a 167.8% increase, and dropped again to KRW 6.98 billion in 2025, a 43.9% decline.

The operating margin was 7.6% in 2022, 3.4% in 2023, 8.3% in 2024 and 4.7% in 2025. Net income attributable to shareholders declined from KRW 15.2 billion in 2022 to KRW 5.59 billion in 2023 and KRW 3.74 billion in 2024, then turned into a net loss of KRW 13.1 billion in 2025.

Net income including non-controlling interests also showed a net loss of KRW 13.1 billion in 2025, the same direction.

Quarterly, the fourth quarter of 2025 posted sales of KRW 15 billion and operating profit of KRW 2.05 billion, a swing to profit from the prior quarter, but first-quarter 2026 sales fell to KRW 608 million, down 95.9% from the prior quarter, with an operating loss of KRW 4.48 billion.

In the second quarter of 2026, sales of KRW 3.46 billion, up 469.7% from the prior quarter, coincided with an operating loss of KRW 14.2 billion, the largest loss relative to sales in the period.

In the third quarter of 2026, sales were KRW 693 million, down 94.7% from a year earlier, and the operating loss narrowed to KRW 1.11 billion from the prior quarter and from a year earlier. The third-quarter net loss attributable to shareholders was KRW 2.47 billion, a larger loss than a year earlier.

Net losses attributable to shareholders have run for five consecutive quarters from the third quarter of 2025 through the third quarter of 2026, and the net loss attributable to shareholders over the last four quarters totaled KRW 32.6 billion.

On the balance sheet, the debt ratio went from 41.2% in 2022 and 68.1% in 2023 to 106.7% in 2024 before falling to 64.3% in 2025, with total equity of KRW 105.9 billion at the end of 2025, all of it attributable to shareholders, and total liabilities of KRW 68.1 billion.

Operating cash flow was -KRW 9.06 billion in 2022, KRW 10.4 billion in 2023, -KRW 1.29 billion in 2024 and KRW 32.6 billion in 2025, so 2025 posted a positive figure well above operating profit despite the net loss. The reason should be checked in the cash flow statement notes of the annual report.

Industry analysis

Demand for battery-materials heat-treatment equipment is tied to electric vehicle demand and to customers' plant expansion schedules.

A corporate profile data provider (WISEreport, data as of September 6, 2026) reports that first-half 2026 sales fell 96.6% from a year earlier, attributing the drop to slower global electric vehicle growth and delayed capacity expansions by battery-materials makers, which reduced orders for heat-treatment and process equipment.

In an early 2024 press report, a company official said the order backlog was close to KRW 300 billion, yet quarterly sales later fell from KRW 15 billion in the fourth quarter of 2025 to KRW 693 million in the third quarter of 2026.

Capacity-expansion announcements and supply-contract disclosures from customers are the most direct signals of where the cycle stands.

In competition, press reports describe Onejoon as the first domestic company to localize the RHK kiln for MLCC sintering, while in battery-materials heat treatment its order share shifts with customer investment levels.

On the MLCC side, a report noted that Samsung Electro-Mechanics' investment in its MLCC business could link to demand for the RHK.

The three new business lines (all-solid-state materials heat treatment, carbon-fiber heat treatment and battery recycling) draw on demand separate from battery plant expansion, so how far they offset the slowdown in core orders is the central question. Press reports describe the U.S.

LFP supply-chain partnership as aimed at expanding U.S.-based production to reduce dependence on China. Supporting factors include demand for non-Chinese materials equipment from supply-chain realignment, while weighing against them are slower electric vehicle demand and delayed expansions. The balance between the two depends on when customer orders resume.

Heat treatment for all-solid-state and carbon-fiber materials and battery recycling solutions are disclosed as new initiatives. The U.S. LFP partnership connects with domestic U.S. production demand aimed at reducing reliance on China. Whether these projects translate into revenue will be visible in periodic reports.

Precedent for a swing to profit

In the fourth quarter of 2025, sales of KRW 15 billion and operating profit of KRW 2.05 billion marked a swing to profit from the prior quarter. The third-quarter 2026 operating loss of KRW 1.11 billion was far smaller than the KRW 14.2 billion loss in the second quarter. A quarterly profit has occurred before, so order recovery could move earnings considerably.

Bear factors

Consecutive losses

Net losses attributable to shareholders have run for five consecutive quarters from the third quarter of 2025 through the third quarter of 2026. The last four quarters total KRW 32.6 billion, a larger figure than the KRW 13.1 billion annual net loss for 2025. The third-quarter 2026 net loss of KRW 2.47 billion was wider than a year earlier.

Dependence on industry and customer capex

Press and data-provider reports cite slower global electric vehicle growth and delayed capacity expansions as reasons for lower orders. The operating margin was 3.4% in 2023 and 4.7% in 2025, with low levels recurring, suggesting margins are sensitive to customer investment timing.

Risk factors

Customer investment timing

Revenue is tied to the expansion schedules of a small number of materials customers, so delayed orders can swing quarterly results sharply. Quarterly results in 2026 illustrate this. Progress on customer diversification is a point to check in the customer notes of the next periodic report.

Capital and cash flow

If operating and net losses continue, total equity will decline, and it stood at KRW 105.9 billion at the end of 2025. Whether the KRW 32.6 billion operating cash inflow of 2025 continues in 2026 needs separate confirmation. The debt ratio fell to 64.3% in 2025, but it could rise again if losses accumulate.

Overseas projects and trade environment

The U.S. LFP partnership is structured to expand through pilot and commercial production, and press reports do not specify its phases. With product sales 56.3% export-oriented in 2024, the company is exposed to overseas policy and trade changes. Delays or cancellations of overseas projects could widen revenue gaps.

Overall view

Onejoon's reported annual sales ranged from about KRW 137 billion to KRW 150 billion over 2022 to 2024, then the company swung to a net loss attributable to shareholders of KRW 13.1 billion in 2025.

Quarterly sales fell to between KRW 608 million and KRW 3.46 billion in the first three quarters of 2026, and net losses attributable to shareholders have run for five consecutive quarters.

In the third quarter of 2026 the operating loss narrowed from a year earlier while the net loss widened, so the direction of earnings was mixed. On the financial side, a debt ratio of 64.3% and total equity of KRW 105.9 billion at the end of 2025 read as capacity to absorb losses.

On the business side, press reports attribute the sales drop to weaker demand for battery-materials equipment, while the all-solid-state, carbon-fiber and battery-recycling efforts and the U.S. LFP partnership are presented as alternative lines.

Valuation is read more through assets, since the loss-making results mean the price-to-earnings ratio cannot be calculated and the price-to-book ratio is below 1x. The strengths of financial capacity and new businesses and the weaknesses of the order gap and consecutive losses can be weighed on equal footing.

The next checkpoints are the 2026 annual report, due by the end of March 2027, and the first-quarter 2027 quarterly report. This report is for information only and does not include a buy or sell opinion or a target price.
