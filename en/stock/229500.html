Cyclo-Z Expansion Against Mounting Losses

NovMetaPharma is widening the indications for its oral candidate Cyclo-Z, but it remains a clinical-stage company with almost no revenue and continuing losses.

02

Business structure

NovMetaPharma was founded in 2010 and was listed on the KONEX market on October 28, 2015. The company describes itself as a developer of innovative drugs with differentiated mechanisms in metabolic disease.

Its business has two segments, new drug development and health functional foods, the latter covering products related to blood sugar and prostate health. The business model is to out-license pipeline assets and collect milestones and royalties.

The lead candidate, Cyclo-Z, is an oral combination of cyclo-his-pro (CHP) and zinc gluconate that acts as a CLIC1 conformational modulator.

According to a Korea Investor Relations Service report, Cyclo-Z has completed a US Phase 1, a Phase 2a in obese type 2 diabetes, a Phase 2b in type 2 diabetes and an Australian Phase 1, with no serious drug-related adverse events reported.

The indications have broadened beyond diabetes and obesity, and Cyclo-Z is being developed as a candidate for FSHD, diabetic kidney disease (DKD) and idiopathic pulmonary fibrosis (IPF).

On competition, most FSHD therapies in development are RNA or gene therapies that directly suppress DUX4, so their approach differs from that of Cyclo-Z. Current revenue is extremely small relative to R&D activity, so the focus of the business is clinical progress and whether a licensing deal materializes.

04

Outlook

In the near term, the key item to watch is the actual start and pace of the FSHD Phase 2 trial.

The FSHD Canada Foundation provides funding for clinical development in exchange for a revenue-sharing interest in future FSHD proceeds, while NovMetaPharma retains ownership of Cyclo-Z and global commercialization rights across all indications.

As the company has stated, it plans to widen commercialization beyond FSHD to GLP-1-related muscle loss and DKD.

However, a Korea Investor Relations Service analyst noted that human efficacy validation for Cyclo-Z indications is effectively at a first stage and that improvements in patient indicators should be monitored.

For the domestic DKD Phase 2, a 2024 report described a 24-week study in 120 patients, and we could not confirm its current status. Financially, total equity of only KRW 3,593 million and continuing operating losses make the method of any further fundraising an important variable.

On the sector backdrop, first-half 2026 results showed deepening polarization by scale and business model, and the second half was expected to diverge further depending on whether R&D results are commercialized. A listing question also remains.

The company tried several times in the past to transfer to KOSDAQ without success, and we found no confirmed plan for a new attempt.

Bull factors

Externally Funded Clinical Development

The FSHD Canada Foundation is providing funding for clinical development. According to the company, the agreement provides non-dilutive capital to develop a new therapeutic area. The foundation decided to collaborate after about two years of review covering nonclinical efficacy, safety and commercial potential.

The ability of a small KONEX company to run trials while lightening its own funding burden is cited as a strength.

Safety Data and Indication Expansion

According to the company, Cyclo-Z has been through multiple clinical trials with no serious adverse events directly related to the drug reported. These data are viewed as a foundation for developing new indications.

The company is also developing a potential co-therapy intended to protect lean muscle mass in patients treated with GLP-1-based therapies. A notable feature is that indications ranging from diabetes and obesity to muscle and kidney disease are explained by a single mechanism.

Positive External Recognition

In 2026 NovMetaPharma won the top technology award at the 13th Korea KONEX Awards. In addition, a Korea Investor Relations Service analyst report on the company was selected as the KONEX-category best report for the first half of 2026.

These evaluations are examples of an external view on the company's technological potential. However, awards do not guarantee clinical success or a licensing deal.

Bear factors

No Meaningful Revenue and Larger Losses

Per reported results, 2025 standalone revenue was only KRW 35 million, against an operating loss of KRW 5,367 million. Net loss was KRW 10,447 million, nearly double the roughly KRW 5.6 billion net loss of 2024.

With total equity of KRW 3,593 million, the structure requires continued external funding to cover clinical costs. The company experienced full capital impairment in 2022, with total equity of negative KRW 4.2 billion.

Unproven Efficacy in Humans

A Korea Investor Relations Service analyst advised that investors should monitor whether positive preclinical results in animal models translate into meaningful indicator improvements in patients. A 2024 report noted that more than ten drug candidates are in development but none has completed Phase 3.

In a 2020 report, the absence of past licensing deals and low recognition in the global pharmaceutical market were cited as reasons for a failed listing attempt. Whether a licensing deal can be concluded remains unproven.

Share-Price Decline and Thin Trading

The share price fell from KRW 13,470 on January 16, 2026 to KRW 3,295 on October 27. According to Hankyung Korea Market data as of October 1, the 3-month change was -31.37%, the 6-month change was -57.57% and the 12-month change was -66.19%.

Trading value on October 27 was KRW 52,566,965, a small amount that points to limited liquidity. The direct cause of the decline was not confirmed.

Risk factors

Macro and Sector

The biotech sector is sensitive to investor sentiment, interest rates and funding-market conditions. In a first-half 2026 analysis, 30 of 75 major pharmaceutical and biotech companies recorded operating losses.

Within the sector, results are concentrated in large biotechs and high-margin companies, producing polarization. A pre-revenue KONEX drug developer may be affected relatively strongly by such shifts.

Clinical and Regulatory

According to the company, Cyclo-Z is investigational and has not been approved by any regulatory authority. Timelines for new-indication studies such as the FSHD Phase 2 can change depending on trial design, patient enrollment and regulatory discussions.

If trial results differ from expectations, the assessment of pipeline value could change significantly. Licensing negotiations also depend on clinical data and the counterparty's judgment.

Financial and Liquidity

With almost no revenue and continuing operating losses, further fundraising may be unavoidable. An earlier report pointed out that dilution from frequent fundraising and repeated listing setbacks weighed on market capitalization. Shares from the January 2026 rights offering are subject to a one-year lock-up. Thin trading on KONEX can increase price volatility when shares are bought or sold.

08

Overall view

NovMetaPharma is a KONEX-listed drug developer widening indications around a single compound, Cyclo-Z. The June 2026 collaboration with the FSHD Canada Foundation and the KONEX award are confirmed business progress.

On the other hand, per reported results, 2025 revenue was only KRW 35 million and net loss widened to KRW 10,447 million. As a Korea Investor Relations Service analyst noted, human efficacy validation is only beginning, and no licensing deal has been confirmed.

The share price has fallen sharply since January 2026 and closed at KRW 3,295 on October 27, and we could not confirm a direct cause for the decline. The clinical timeline, the method of fundraising and the status of licensing talks are the key facts to follow. This report is for information only and is not investment advice.
