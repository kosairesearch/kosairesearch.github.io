02

Business structure

WINS Technet is a cybersecurity company that combines security appliance sales with security services. Its core products are intrusion prevention systems (IPS) and firewalls, sold as security appliances that embed its own security engine in servers and network interface cards.

The product line includes Sniper IPS, Sniper ONE, Sniper APTX, Sniper NGFW and others. The company holds the leading share of the domestic IPS market and is the sole supplier of 100G-class security equipment to a Japanese mobile carrier.

Its service business covers security monitoring, security consulting and cloud infrastructure security, and since 2020 it has operated as an MSP handling cloud adoption from design through operation.

Public institutions, telecom operators and financial institutions account for more than 80% of revenue, a customer base with low sensitivity to the economic cycle but with sales concentrated around budget execution. Since 2024 the company has expanded into zero-trust and launched the AI security solution Sniper AIVAX.

Its subsidiaries are SysMate and DeepPoint. Equipment sales are the main business, and in the security market it competes with peers such as Ahnlab, where its IPS leadership is the main point of differentiation.

04

Earnings analysis

Annual revenue rose from KRW 101.4 billion in 2022 to KRW 106.9 billion in 2023, up 5.4% year on year, which marked the peak. It then declined to KRW 101.5 billion in 2024 (down 5.0%) and KRW 98.6 billion in 2025 (down 2.8%), with two consecutive years of decline from 2024 through 2025 following the 2023 peak.

Operating profit was KRW 22 billion in 2022, KRW 23.1 billion in 2023 (up 5.1%), KRW 20.4 billion in 2024 (down 11.8%) and KRW 21.2 billion in 2025 (up 3.9%). The operating margin was 21.7% in 2022, 21.6% in 2023, 20.1% in 2024 and 21.5% in 2025.

Net income attributable to controlling shareholders was KRW 19.3 billion in 2022, KRW 21.1 billion in 2023 (up 9.5%), KRW 19.8 billion in 2024 (down 6.3%) and KRW 18.4 billion in 2025 (down 6.9%).

Operating cash flow was KRW 21.4 billion in 2022 and KRW 29.5 billion in 2023, then KRW 19.5 billion in 2024 and KRW 16.1 billion in 2025.

The debt ratio fell from 16.5% in 2022 to 13.4% in 2025, with total equity of KRW 185.2 billion at year-end 2025, equity attributable to controlling shareholders of KRW 184.8 billion and total liabilities of KRW 24.8 billion. Quarterly results are strongly seasonal.

Revenue was KRW 18.6 billion in the third quarter of 2025 and KRW 40.2 billion in the fourth quarter, up 116.5% from the prior quarter, then KRW 17.5 billion in the first quarter of 2026, down 56.6%. Operating profit was KRW 3.43 billion, KRW 10.5 billion and KRW 1.73 billion in those quarters.

Preliminary first-quarter 2026 results, disclosed on April 30, 2026, showed consolidated operating profit down 31.4% year on year.

Revenue then rose to KRW 20.6 billion in the second quarter and KRW 22.7 billion in the third quarter of 2026, with operating profit of KRW 3.16 billion and KRW 4.31 billion, so both increased for two consecutive quarters.

Third-quarter net income attributable to controlling shareholders was KRW 3.5 billion, down 7.6% year on year, so operating profit and that net income moved in opposite directions.

Net income attributable to controlling shareholders for the last four quarters, from the fourth quarter of 2025 to the third quarter of 2026, totaled KRW 18.1 billion.

05

Industry analysis

WINS Technet's end market is the IT and security budgets of public institutions, telecom operators and financial institutions. These customers' budgets tend to be executed toward year-end.

The company has said that telecom operators and private companies, not only public institutions using leftover budgets, tend to step up project execution in the fourth quarter.

The pattern is visible in its results, and the company itself noted that its first-quarter figures are more meaningfully compared with the same quarter of the prior year because of the seasonal weight of fourth-quarter sales.

In terms of cycle, revenue has declined for two consecutive years since the 2023 peak, so whether demand for existing equipment recovers and whether new areas widen the revenue base are the key points to watch. Its leading share of the domestic IPS market is the clearest competitive strength.

In security software and services, it competes for customer budgets with specialist security firms such as Ahnlab and with companies in physical and monitoring security such as S-1. Naming zero-trust, cloud security and AI security as growth areas reads as an effort to offset the limits of equipment-led revenue.

The number of competitors in these areas is larger than in classic IPS equipment, so how the earnings structure changes will need to be confirmed through reported results.

Bull factors

Domestic IPS leadership and overseas reference

Sniper IPS holds the leading domestic market share and is the sole supplier of 100G-class security equipment to a Japanese mobile carrier. Existing references with telecom and public customers can act as an entry barrier for new products. Equipment sales alongside monitoring services broaden customer touchpoints.

Margin stability and financial capacity

The operating margin stayed in the low 20s, at 21.7% in 2022, 20.1% in 2024 and 21.5% in 2025, even as revenue declined. The debt ratio fell from 16.5% in 2022 to 13.4% in 2025, and total equity at year-end 2025 was KRW 185.2 billion. A light debt burden could create room for investment in new businesses.

Fourth-quarter leverage and rising treasury stock

In the fourth quarter of 2025, revenue reached KRW 40.2 billion and operating profit KRW 10.5 billion, showing that the fourth-quarter concentration can act as earnings leverage. Treasury stock rose from 12.26% as of June 2025 to 14.78% as of October 2, 2026.

Bear factors

Two years of revenue decline and past shortfall versus plan

Revenue fell for two consecutive years from 2024 through 2025 after peaking at KRW 106.9 billion in 2023. Standalone revenue in 2025 was KRW 93.3 billion, 15.18% below the plan set earlier that year. Reaching the 2026 target depends heavily on a strong fourth quarter.

Lower profit and cash flow

Net income attributable to controlling shareholders declined for two consecutive years from 2024 through 2025 after peaking at KRW 21.1 billion in 2023, reaching KRW 18.4 billion in 2025. Operating cash flow fell from KRW 29.5 billion in 2023 to KRW 19.5 billion in 2024 and KRW 16.1 billion in 2025. First-quarter 2026 operating profit was down 31.4% year on year.

Weak first half and quarterly volatility

First-half 2026 operating profit was reported as down 32.0% year on year. Quarterly revenue dropped from KRW 40.2 billion in the fourth quarter of 2025 to KRW 17.5 billion in the first quarter of 2026. The swing shows how sensitive quarterly figures are to the timing of budget execution.

10

Risk factors

Seasonality and earnings volatility

Because budgets are executed toward the fourth quarter, quarterly results swing sharply. The gap between KRW 40.2 billion in the fourth quarter of 2025 and KRW 17.5 billion in the first quarter of 2026 illustrates this. Judging trends from quarterly figures alone can be misleading, so annual figures need to be checked.

Customer concentration and procurement timing

Public institutions, telecom operators and financial institutions account for more than 80% of revenue. A change in budget allocation or procurement timing in any of these groups could shift when revenue is recognized. The degree of customer diversification needs to be checked in future disclosures.

Business transition and rising competition

The company is shifting weight from equipment-led revenue toward zero-trust, cloud and AI security. These new areas have more competitors than classic IPS, so profitability benchmarks may change. Until the revenue contribution of new products is visible, whether the operating margin holds is the key point to confirm.

Overall view

WINS Technet runs its security equipment business on a leading domestic IPS share and overseas telecom references, and its operating margin stayed in the low 20s from 2022 through 2025.

Revenue, however, fell for two consecutive years after peaking at KRW 106.9 billion in 2023, reaching KRW 98.6 billion in 2025, and net income attributable to controlling shareholders declined over the same period.

Earnings are heavily concentrated in the fourth quarter, so quarterly figures alone make the trend hard to judge. Operating profit fell 31.4% year on year in the first quarter of 2026, while revenue and operating profit rose for two consecutive quarters in the second and third quarters.

Whether the company reaches its 2026 standalone business plan depends on fourth-quarter concentration.

On valuation, market capitalization sits below equity attributable to controlling shareholders and the multiples are below industry averages, but this should be read together with the uneven earnings pattern and the limited free float.

Whether the shift toward zero-trust, cloud and AI security widens the revenue base is the medium-term issue. The next points to confirm are fourth-quarter 2026 results and the preliminary full-year figures in early 2027.
