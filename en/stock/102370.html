K Auction: Quarterly Breakeven Returns Amid Share Cancellations

02

Business structure

K Auction is an art auction and brokerage company founded in September 2005 and listed on KOSDAQ in January 2022. Its main revenue comes from art auctions, and it runs its own art auction management platform, K-Office.

According to an external data aggregator, the revenue mix in the second quarter of 2026 was 69% art auctions, 24% art sales, 4% other and 2% art brokerage. A 2024 media report put the 2023 mix at 76.7% auctions, 13.8% art sales, 4.1% brokerage and 5.4% other.

Its subsidiary handles art-investment contract securities, which a 2024 brokerage analysis described as a preparatory step toward entering the STO (security token) market. In 2024 the company decided to absorb three small subsidiaries, leaving the art-investment subsidiary as the main affiliate.

Six auction houses share the Korean market, and a 2024 report said K Auction ranked first by total hammer value in 2023.

In 2026 the company held a 117-lot September auction worth about KRW 11 billion and a separate online auction of goods seized by the National Tax Service, and a 118-lot auction worth about KRW 12.6 billion is scheduled for November 25.

A 2024 analysis noted that product sales carry lower profitability than commission-based auctions, so the revenue mix affects margins. The mix figures above come from an external aggregator and may differ in definition from segment disclosures in the DART annual report.

04

Earnings analysis

On a DART-filed basis, revenue fell 56.1% from KRW 27.7 billion in 2022 to KRW 12.2 billion in 2023, rose 60.1% to KRW 19.5 billion in 2024, and fell 36.9% to KRW 12.3 billion in 2025. 2022 was the largest revenue year and 2023 the smallest within 2022 to 2025.

A 2024 brokerage analysis attributed the 2023 decline to a weak overall market, which pushed the company toward low-value purchase transactions and a smaller business. The company attributed the 2024 revenue increase to higher product sales of its core art business and growth at its art-investment subsidiary.

Operating income went from KRW 5.82 billion in 2022 to an operating loss of KRW 4.2 billion in 2023, a loss of KRW 4.76 billion in 2024 and a loss of KRW 4.07 billion in 2025, which makes three consecutive years of losses from 2023 to 2025.

The operating margin moved from 21.0% in 2022 to -34.6% in 2023, -24.4% in 2024 and -33.1% in 2025. The company linked the 2024 losses to expansion of new businesses and fixed-cost burden.

For 2025, in its preliminary disclosure in February 2026, the company cited lower product sales and a one-off impairment of goodwill as drags.

Net income attributable to controlling shareholders went from KRW 4.03 billion in 2022 to net losses of KRW 5.06 billion in 2023, KRW 5.94 billion in 2024 and KRW 5.1 billion in 2025, again three consecutive years of losses from 2023 to 2025.

Net income including non-controlling interests, a different figure, was a net loss of KRW 6.37 billion in 2025. By quarter, revenue was KRW 2.44 billion with an operating loss of KRW 1.36 billion in the third quarter of 2025, and KRW 3.32 billion with a loss of KRW 934 million in the fourth quarter.

The first quarter of 2026 showed revenue of KRW 2.81 billion and an operating loss of KRW 317 million, and the second quarter reached revenue of KRW 4.03 billion with operating income of KRW 109 million.

In the third quarter of 2026 revenue was KRW 2.34 billion, down 4.2% year on year, with an operating loss of KRW 862 million that turned negative from the prior quarter. Net loss attributable to controlling shareholders that quarter was KRW 1.17 billion, wider than the KRW 811 million loss a year earlier.

The four quarters from the fourth quarter of 2025 through the third quarter of 2026 add up to a controlling-shareholder net loss of KRW 3.11 billion.

Industry analysis

The Korean art auction market is shared by six auction houses, and a 2024 media report said K Auction ranked first by total hammer value in 2023. The overall market was weak at that time, and a brokerage analysis linked the drop in purchase-based transactions to that weakness.

In January 2026 the company described 2025 as a recalibration period in which the domestic art market shed speculative excess, and said 2026 would be a year of stability.

A February 2026 auction was reported to show a wait-and-see stance from both consignors and bidders, with fewer business days around the Lunar New Year. The September 2026 auction offered 117 lots worth about KRW 11 billion, and the auction scheduled for November 25, 2026 offers 118 lots worth about KRW 12.6 billion.

For comparison, an October 2025 auction of Korean abstract works was reported at about KRW 10.6 billion in consignment value. Consignment values are not hammer prices, so the sell-through rate and total hammer value need to be checked separately.

Among listed peers in the distribution and consumer goods sector, the company ranked near the bottom by revenue on 2025 annual figures, while its debt ratio was below the sector average.

Because auction revenue depends on the economy and demand for high-value works, observers differ on whether the market is at an early recovery stage or still recalibrating.
