02

Business structure

KT Genie Music operates the genie music streaming platform and, through its consolidated e-book subsidiary, a book content business.

On a consolidated basis in Q1 2026, the music business generated about KRW 49.0 billion, or 65.2% of revenue, the book content business about KRW 22.7 billion (30.2%), and other businesses such as concerts and merchandise about KRW 3.4 billion (4.6%), according to an Investing.com report on the May 15, 2026 disclosure.

Profit contribution looked very different. The book content business produced KRW 3.5 billion, or 70.6% of operating profit, while music produced about KRW 1.3 billion (26.6%). The main customer channel for the music business is telecom-bundled products.

The company attributed part of the Q1 2026 revenue increase to music services bundled with KT and LG Uplus. Car music services began to be reflected in revenue from 2025.

In September 2025, the company launched AI DJ, a conversational AI music service that it described as the first of its kind among Korean music platforms.

Its music distribution unit has distributed CJ ENM music exclusively since 2018, and since December 2025 it has also held exclusive distribution rights for SBS drama soundtracks through a studio affiliate.

Competition is shifting away from streaming sales alone toward platforms that widen revenue through cars, telecom partnerships and content distribution. The segment data show that profit depends more on the e-book subsidiary and telecom B2B channels than on the core music platform.

04

Earnings analysis

In the annual figures, revenue was KRW 286.1 billion in 2022, KRW 307 billion in 2023 (up 7.3%), KRW 301.6 billion in 2024 (down 1.8%) and KRW 302.9 billion in 2025 (up 0.4%). 2023 was the largest year in the 2022–2025 period, and revenue has moved sideways around KRW 300 billion since then.

Operating profit rose from KRW 13.8 billion in 2022 to KRW 15.4 billion in 2023 (up 11.1%), fell to KRW 12.2 billion in 2024 (down 20.6%), and recovered to KRW 16.2 billion in 2025 (up 32.6%). 2025 was the largest year and 2024 the smallest for operating profit.

The company attributed the 2025 operating profit gain to growth in the e-book subscription business of its consolidated subsidiary. Operating margin was 4.8%, 5.0%, 4.0% and 5.3% from 2022 to 2025.

Controlling-shareholder net income fell from KRW 17.5 billion in 2022 to KRW 13.8 billion in 2023 (down 21.3%), then turned to a net loss of KRW 20.1 billion in 2024. The net loss widened to KRW 27.5 billion in 2025, making 2024 and 2025 two consecutive loss years.

Net income including non-controlling interests was a net loss of KRW 18.5 billion in 2025, a smaller loss than the controlling-shareholder figure; the gap suggests non-controlling interests were booked as a profit.

The company cited the recognition of an impairment on goodwill, an intangible asset, as the reason the 2025 net loss widened.

Quarterly results fell sharply in Q4 2025, with revenue of KRW 77 billion (down 7.3% from the prior quarter), operating profit of KRW 1.41 billion (down 75.2%), and a controlling-shareholder net loss of KRW 32.4 billion.

Revenue bottomed in Q1 2026 at KRW 75.2 billion (down 2.4%) and then rose to KRW 77.2 billion in Q2 and KRW 78.2 billion in Q3. Operating profit was KRW 4.96 billion in Q1 2026 (up 251.5%), KRW 1.89 billion in Q2 (down 61.8%) and KRW 3.99 billion in Q3 (up 110.9%).

Preliminary cumulative results released on November 12, 2026 showed nine-month revenue up 2.09% to about KRW 230.5 billion, while cumulative operating profit fell 26.5% to about KRW 10.8 billion.

A press report said the separate-basis operating result showed a loss in Q2 2026, which was attributed to bad-debt expense from a reassessment of recoverability of receivables tied to concert operations. Controlling-shareholder net losses over the last four quarters total KRW 30.9 billion.

05

Industry analysis

The industry view is that music platforms are being reshaped from relying on streaming subscriptions toward widening revenue through cars, telecom partnerships and content distribution. Subscription-based businesses and expansion of B2B services are seen as key variables for profitability.

KT Genie Music's B2B music services center on telecom-bundled product sales, and growth in bundled music customers contributed to revenue growth in Q1 2026. Car music services have been reflected in revenue more fully since 2025.

In music distribution, the company has added exclusive distribution of SBS drama soundtracks to its exclusive distribution of CJ ENM music, widening its content supply. On the book side, growth in B2B2C e-book subscribers at its e-book subsidiary lifted Q3 2025 results.

In September 2025 the company launched AI DJ, extending its listening experience with AI features. Its competitive position rests on combining telecom partnerships with subsidiary content, which distinguishes it from standalone music platforms. Market-wide subscriber trends and peer results are not compared on a like-for-like basis here.

06

Outlook

In its Q3 2025 results announcement, the company presented expansion of music distribution linked to virtual IP and game IP, stronger concert operations, and deeper cooperation with its e-book subsidiary as medium-term growth drivers.

In its Q1 2026 announcement, it said it would advance AI services on its music platform, expand car music services in earnest and strengthen music distribution partnerships. Whether AI DJ, launched in September 2025, converts into new subscriber inflows and a stronger listening experience remains to be seen.

Since the company's explanation centers on growing telecom-bundled music customers with KT and LG Uplus, whether that growth keeps feeding through to quarterly revenue is the next checkpoint. Car music services could shift the revenue mix of the music business depending on how fast they expand.

The e-book subsidiary's B2B2C subscription growth accounted for 70.6% of Q1 2026 operating profit, so any slowdown there would directly affect consolidated earnings.

Operating profit fell in Q2 2026 and recovered from the prior quarter in Q3, and revenue has risen for two consecutive quarters, which are cited as positive signs. However, Q3 2026 operating profit remains below the year-earlier level, so a full return to prior-year levels has not yet occurred.

In the 2026 annual report, whether goodwill impairment is recognized and the direction of full-year controlling-shareholder earnings will be key points to check.

08

Bull factors

Quarterly operating profit rebound and consecutive revenue gains

Q3 2026 operating profit of KRW 3.99 billion rose 110.9% from the prior quarter, and revenue rose for two consecutive quarters from Q2 through Q3 2026. Quarterly momentum improving since the Q1 2026 revenue low is cited as the bull case. However, the rebound remains below year-earlier levels.

Wider channels through telecom partnerships and car music

Growth in music customers bundled with KT and LG Uplus contributed to Q1 2026 revenue growth. Whether the car channel's revenue contribution continues is the next point to check. The September 2025 launch of AI DJ is read as adding room for subscription-based revenue expansion.

Lower leverage and subsidiary profit contribution

With the lower 2025 liabilities-to-equity ratio, the financial burden is lighter than before. Operating profit for 2025 rose 32.6% to KRW 16.2 billion. The e-book subsidiary produced 70.6% of Q1 2026 operating profit, showing its subscription business has become a central profit driver.

09

Bear factors

Controlling-shareholder losses persist

Controlling-shareholder net losses continued in 2024 and 2025, making two consecutive loss years. The 2025 loss of KRW 27.5 billion was larger than the KRW 20.1 billion loss in 2024.

Controlling-shareholder net losses over the last four quarters total KRW 30.9 billion, and goodwill impairment weighed on the 2025 net loss.

Year-on-year declines in Q3 2026

Q3 2026 revenue fell 5.9% from a year earlier and operating profit fell 29.9%. Controlling-shareholder net income of KRW 2.09 billion was also 8.2% lower than a year earlier. Preliminary cumulative operating profit released on November 12, 2026 fell 26.5% to about KRW 10.8 billion.

Weaker market assessment and bad-debt burden

Press reports say the company was moved from KOSDAQ's major-company segment to its mid-sized segment in May 2026. A separate-basis operating loss in Q2 2026 was reported as the first quarterly loss of that kind since Q2 2015. The bad-debt expense tied to concert-related receivables was cited as the cause.

10

Risk factors

Accounting and asset impairment

The 2025 net loss widened partly due to goodwill impairment. Additional impairment of goodwill could again swing net earnings sharply. Preliminary results may change during the external audit, so confirmed figures must be checked in the periodic report.

Dependence on partners

The revenue base relies on external channel arrangements, including telecom-bundled music services, car music and CJ ENM music distribution. Changes in partner terms or customer churn could disrupt B2B revenue.

With music contributing 26.6% of Q1 2026 operating profit, below the e-book subsidiary, the earnings power of the core music platform remains a challenge.

Receivables and business concentration

Bad-debt expense was recognized after a reassessment of the recoverability of concert-related receivables. Similar collection risk could recur as the company expands concerts and IP investment. Heavy reliance on the e-book subsidiary means a slowdown there could weigh on consolidated earnings.

Overall view

KT Genie Music centers on a music streaming platform but depends heavily on its e-book subsidiary and telecom-partnered B2B channels for profit. Revenue moved within a range of KRW 286.1 billion to KRW 307 billion over 2022–2025, and operating profit recovered to KRW 16.2 billion in 2025.

On a controlling-shareholder basis, however, the company posted net losses in both 2024 and 2025, with the 2025 loss at KRW 27.5 billion.

Operating profit in Q3 2026 recovered from the prior quarter but declined from a year earlier, and controlling-shareholder net losses over the last four quarters total KRW 30.9 billion.

On the bull side, the cited points are two consecutive quarters of revenue growth, B2B expansion through telecom partnerships and car music, and a lower liabilities-to-equity ratio.

On the bear side, the cited points are continued controlling-shareholder losses, the year-on-year decline in Q3 2026, and the 2026 move to KOSDAQ's mid-sized segment together with bad-debt pressure.

The next checkpoints are the preliminary full-year 2026 results around February 2027 and the 2026 annual report due by March 31, 2027, where a return to profit and whether goodwill impairment is recognized will become clear.
