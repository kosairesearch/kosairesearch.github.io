02

Business structure

Poongwon Precision, founded in 1996, is a specialist maker of metal masks for display manufacturing, using precision processing of thin metal sheets to produce products for OLED deposition. Its core products are fine metal masks (FMM), along with open metal masks (OMM), stick bar masks (SBM) and accessory sticks (ACC).

A company profile compiled by WiseReport says the firm develops and mass-produces products mainly for AMOLED deposition and has supplied more than 180 companies with over 14,000 developed products.

The company listed on KOSDAQ in February 2022 through the technology-special-listing route, and at the listing it projected FMM sales of KRW 27.1 billion for 2022 and KRW 94.0 billion for 2023, far above what it actually recorded.

Based on second-quarter 2026 sales, one data aggregator puts the segment mix at 89% metal masks and 11% metal thin-film encapsulation (contract processing).

Its customers are OLED panel makers, and it has worked with Samsung Display on technical cooperation and reportedly received a strategic investment from that company. In competition, Dai Nippon Printing (DNP) has been the de facto monopoly supplier of FMM, and press reports put DNP's operating margin in the 40% range.

Poongwon's push into FMM is an attempt to break that position, and it is regarded as a leading player in localization.

04

Earnings analysis

On disclosed figures, annual revenue moved from KRW 44.9 billion in 2022 to KRW 43.1 billion in 2023, a 4.1% decline, then rebounded to KRW 49.3 billion in 2024, up 14.4%, before falling to KRW 32.4 billion in 2025, down 34.3% and the smallest year of 2022 to 2025. The largest year in that span was 2024.

Profitability moved in the other direction: operating profit of KRW 751 million in 2022 turned into an operating loss of KRW 21.9 billion in 2023, narrowed to KRW 18.2 billion in 2024, and widened again to KRW 19.9 billion in 2025.

The operating margin went from 1.7% in 2022 to -50.9% in 2023, -37.0% in 2024 and -61.6% in 2025.

Net profit attributable to controlling shareholders was KRW 6.54 billion in 2022, followed by net losses of KRW 21.8 billion in 2023, KRW 30.1 billion in 2024 and KRW 23.6 billion in 2025, which makes three straight loss years.

Net income including non-controlling interests followed the same path, with figures of KRW 6.54 billion in 2022 and net losses of KRW 21.8 billion, KRW 30.1 billion and KRW 23.6 billion in 2023 to 2025.

The company attributed the 2025 sales decline to customers cutting costs and pointed to higher selling and administrative expenses from increased R&D spending. Industry commentary adds that OLED panel makers cut investment and ran lower utilization, which reduced metal mask demand while fixed costs stayed heavy.

Quarterly sales rose from KRW 7.72 billion in the third quarter of 2025 to KRW 10 billion in the fourth quarter, but the operating loss widened from KRW 4.46 billion to KRW 7.89 billion.

Sales were KRW 8.99 billion in the first quarter of 2026 with an operating loss of KRW 3.01 billion, and KRW 10.2 billion in the second quarter with an operating loss of KRW 4.54 billion, before falling to KRW 5.82 billion in the third quarter with an operating loss of KRW 3.16 billion.

A press report on the first half of 2026 put revenue at KRW 19.2 billion, up 31.5% year on year, while the operating loss narrowed only by KRW 30 million to KRW 7.55 billion, and R&D expense rose 36.0% to KRW 7.8 billion.

At end-2025, total equity was KRW 12.3 billion and total liabilities KRW 68 billion, and the pre-tax loss equaled 191.2% of equity.

05

Industry analysis

Demand for Poongwon's products depends heavily on investment cycles at OLED panel makers. A 2025 press report said that panel makers cut investment and ran lower utilization, which reduced demand for metal masks.

In OLED deposition, FMM is the metal mask that forms RGB sub-pixels, made by drilling fine holes in metal sheets only tens of micrometers thick, and it is regarded as a component that determines panel resolution and yield.

The market has been a de facto monopoly held by Dai Nippon Printing (DNP), and after Japan's 2019 export restrictions, Korean panel supply chains had a growing need to diversify. Samsung Display also relied almost exclusively on DNP for FMM, so the need to diversify suppliers has been discussed for some time.

In January 2026, a KB Securities analyst assessed that Poongwon is leading FMM localization and that real entry into the supply chain is becoming visible.

A 2025 press report, however, relayed an industry view that no major Korean supplier had yet succeeded in mass-producing FMM, and that Poongwon was struggling with yield improvement. On materials, a press report said Hyundai BNG Steel is reportedly working on localizing Invar, the nickel-iron alloy core to FMM.

In competitive terms, DNP is the reference point with proven technology and production, while Poongwon occupies a new-supplier position supported by the demand for domestic diversification.

Where the cycle goes depends on whether panel investment recovers and domestic FMM mass-production supply is confirmed at the same time.

06

Outlook

The direction the company has set out is building a mass-production system for 6th-generation half-cut FMM and pursuing supply to Samsung Display. A June 2026 press report said the company targets supply to BOE in China in 2027 and to CSOT in China in 2028.

A March 2026 report said the exclusive supply arrangement between Samsung Display and DNP has reportedly ended, and that talks on supplying some products have advanced because Poongwon's FMM reached mass-production-level quality and yield.

Initial volumes were reported to be for some full-HD panel models, with supply possibly starting between the first and second quarters. A 2025 press report, however, said Samsung Display viewed FMM as still in technical development and not yet confirmed for supply, so the accounts diverge.

On technology, KB Securities noted that the company was selected in July 2025 to lead a national project on 1000 ppi high-resolution FMM for G8.6H OLED, and completed an 8th-generation FMM photomask production facility in April 2025.

The company is also developing 1000 ppi ultra-high-resolution FMM for 8.6-generation IT OLED lines used in tablets and notebooks.

On financing, the KRW 3.0 billion rights offering in August 2026 and the conversion of the convertible bond in October 2026, which added 4,031,413 shares at KRW 3,820 per share, reinforced the capital base.

A press report put the pre-tax loss ratio at 88.5% at mid-2026, which suggests the distance from the administrative-issue threshold has changed. The next point to track is how quickly mass-production sales are reflected in quarterly profit and loss.

Bull factors

Expanding FMM customer base

The company has worked with Samsung Display on technology and reportedly received a strategic investment from it. Building a mass-production system for 6th-generation half-cut FMM and pursuing Samsung Display deliveries are the stated direction.

With supply targets set at BOE in 2027 and CSOT in 2028, a real expansion of customers could change the sales structure.

Sales rebound and narrower losses

Revenue for the first half of 2026 rose 31.5% year on year to KRW 19.2 billion. Second-quarter 2026 sales of KRW 10.2 billion were up 13.5% from the prior quarter. Net losses attributable to controlling shareholders narrowed from KRW 30.1 billion in 2024 to KRW 23.6 billion in 2025.

Strengthened capital base

A KRW 3.0 billion rights offering in August 2026 and the October 2026 conversion of a KRW 15.4 billion convertible bond strengthened equity. A press report says the pre-tax loss ratio fell to 88.5% at mid-2026. That has led to readings that the distance from the administrative-issue threshold has changed.

Bear factors

Shrinking sales and continuous losses

Third-quarter 2026 sales of KRW 5.82 billion fell 43.0% from the prior quarter and 24.6% year on year. The operating loss was KRW 3.16 billion, and the company has reported losses for five consecutive quarters on disclosed figures. Net losses attributable to controlling shareholders over the last four quarters total KRW 19.9 billion.

Limited financial headroom and funding strain

The debt-to-equity ratio stood at 551.5% at end-2025. A press report put it at about 749% at end-June 2026, with cash and equivalents of about KRW 660 million and current liabilities of KRW 59.6 billion.

The August 2026 rights offering drew only a 4.85% subscription, so Sangsangin Securities underwrote the remaining 95.15%, and the issuer bore the underwriting and failed-subscription fees.

Unverified mass production and missed past targets

FMM sales targets presented at the listing, KRW 27.1 billion for 2022 and KRW 94.0 billion for 2023, differed greatly from actual results. A 2025 press report said Samsung Display viewed FMM as still in technical development and not yet confirmed for supply.

A June 2026 report still described Samsung Display deliveries as being pursued, so the timing of mass-production revenue remains open.

Risk factors

Financial position, liquidity and listing status

Leverage is high and cash headroom is thin, so the company relies on external capital. The October 2026 bond conversion added 4,031,413 shares, equal to 17.11% of the 23,565,010 shares previously outstanding, which diluted existing holders.

The administrative-issue test looks at two of the last three fiscal years, so the 2026 annual report is decisive.

Customer concentration and panel cycle

Lower investment and utilization at OLED panel makers reduced metal mask demand, and the company also cited customer cost-cutting as a reason for lower sales. With heavy fixed costs, revenue swings flow directly into operating results. The 24.6% year-on-year decline in third-quarter 2026 sales illustrates this structure.

Yield, mass-production timing and share-price volatility

If yield and customer qualification are not proven, supply expansion could slip. A 2025 industry view relayed in the press said Poongwon was struggling with yield improvement. The exchange designated the stock for investment caution in June, August and October 2026, which points to large short-term swings in trading.

Overall view

Poongwon Precision is pursuing localization of FMM for OLED deposition, and its cooperation with Samsung Display and the supply targets for Chinese panel makers form the core of the business.

The disclosed results, however, show sales of KRW 32.4 billion in 2025, down 34.3%, and an operating loss of KRW 19.9 billion, extending a three-year run of losses. Third-quarter 2026 sales of KRW 5.82 billion and an operating loss of KRW 3.16 billion mean five straight loss quarters.

On the financial side, the debt-to-equity ratio was 551.5% at end-2025, the August 2026 rights offering drew a low subscription rate, and the October 2026 bond conversion diluted shareholders. The capital base was rebuilt in 2026, and a press report put the pre-tax loss ratio at 88.5% at mid-2026.

On mass-production supply, a 2026 report described talks on supply, while a 2025 report said Samsung Display viewed the product as still in development, so accounts diverge.

The next checkpoints are the annual profit and loss in the 2026 annual report, the administrative-issue determination, and how quickly mass-production sales appear in quarterly results. This report is provided for information only and contains no buy or sell opinion.
