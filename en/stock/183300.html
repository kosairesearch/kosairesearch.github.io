Report overview

Ceramic Parts Expand, Sales Grow While Profit Stalls

KoMiCo's parts business reached about half of revenue by 2025, and sales rose for two straight quarters in 2026, but operating profit has moved unevenly and the debt ratio has risen sharply since 2022.

  1. 1

    Sales reached KRW 604.1 billion in 2025, up 19.1% year on year and the largest year in 2022–2025, while operating profit fell 1.3% to KRW 111 billion.

  2. 2

    Q3 2026 sales of KRW 186.2 billion rose 17.1% year on year, but operating profit of KRW 24.7 billion fell 4.6%, so top-line growth has not yet carried through to profit.

  3. 3

    KoMiCo's stake in its subsidiary MiCo Ceramics was raised from 47.8% to 64.5%, according to June 2026 press reports.

  4. 4

  5. 5

02

Business structure

KoMiCo runs semiconductor equipment component cleaning and coating businesses together with a ceramic materials and parts business. The company says it serves global customers including Samsung Electronics, Intel and TSMC across eight countries.

The revenue mix has shifted sharply: parts rose from 7% of total sales in 2021 to 50% in 2025, while cleaning fell from 34% to 22% and coating from 59% to 28% over the same period.

The parts business centers on the subsidiary MiCo Ceramics, which designs and manufactures heaters and electrostatic chucks (ESC) for specific customers. MiCo Ceramics' utilization rose to 89.4% in 2025 from 67.1% in 2024, according to the annual report.

Samsung Electronics holds a 13.7% stake in MiCo Ceramics, per a 2025 report. Overseas cleaning and coating sites are in Huanggang, Huizhou and Qingdao in China and in Phoenix, USA. A September 2026 report put foundry customers at about 35% of cleaning and coating sales.

The company cites contamination removal, plasma-resistant special coatings and localization of ceramic parts as its strengths, and is pursuing vertical integration from cleaning and coating into ceramic materials.

04

Earnings analysis

Annual sales rose from KRW 288.4 billion in 2022 to KRW 604.1 billion in 2025, and 2025 was the largest year of 2022–2025. Sales were KRW 307.3 billion in 2023 (up 6.6%), KRW 507.1 billion in 2024 (up 65.0%) and KRW 604.1 billion in 2025 (up 19.1%), rising three years in a row from 2023 to 2025.

Operating profit was KRW 55.4 billion in 2022, KRW 33 billion in 2023 (down 40.4%), KRW 112.5 billion in 2024 (up 240.5%) and KRW 111 billion in 2025 (down 1.3%), with 2024 the largest year. The operating margin fell from 19.2% in 2022 to 10.7% in 2023, then recovered to 22.2% in 2024 and 18.4% in 2025.

In 2025 sales grew 19.1% while operating profit slipped 1.3%, so revenue gains did not fully convert into profit. Net profit attributable to controlling shareholders peaked at KRW 55.9 billion in 2024 and was KRW 49.9 billion in 2025 (down 10.7%).

Net profit including non-controlling interests was KRW 77.2 billion in 2025; the gap reflects the non-controlling share.

Quarterly, the company swung from a net loss of KRW 6.77 billion in Q3 2025 to net profit of KRW 14.5 billion in Q4 2025, and one securities firm's pre-results estimate attributed part of the improvement to a one-off gain from intercompany dealings with the subsidiary.

Q1 2026 sales were KRW 157.1 billion, down 4.3% from the prior quarter, and operating profit was KRW 20.5 billion, which one brokerage linked to fixed costs from recent large-scale investment.

Q2 2026 sales recovered to KRW 176 billion with operating profit of KRW 27.1 billion, but the company swung to a net loss of KRW 2.98 billion.

Q3 2026 sales of KRW 186.2 billion rose 17.1% year on year, while operating profit of KRW 24.7 billion fell 4.6%; net profit of KRW 6.62 billion turned positive versus the prior-year quarter.

At end-2025 total liabilities were KRW 660.4 billion and total equity KRW 369.8 billion, of which KRW 270.3 billion belonged to controlling shareholders. Operating cash flow fell from KRW 115.6 billion in 2024 to KRW 38.2 billion in 2025.

05

Industry analysis

KoMiCo's results track customer utilization and capital spending in semiconductor fabs. As chip processes shrink and stack higher, durability and yield management of equipment parts matter more.

Brokers have argued that rising customer utilization during the memory upcycle supports continued growth in cleaning and coating. Ceramic heaters and ESC are described as beneficiaries of China's equipment investment and localization drive.

New production lines at global chipmakers starting in 2027 are expected to add cleaning and coating demand. The company is described as the first in Korea to commercialize semiconductor cleaning and coating services.

With foundry at about 35% of cleaning and coating sales, one analysis sees reduced dependence on any single customer. Fixed-cost burdens remain at the Chinese units in Huanggang and Dalian, while utilization recovery in Korea and the US is seen as offsetting them.

06

Outlook

A September 2026 report expected KoMiCo's earnings improvement to continue through 2027, driven by recovering cleaning and coating utilization and ESC growth at MiCo Ceramics.

MiCo Ceramics plans to complete the G6 processing line expansion within 2026 and to add heater capacity for orders from Chinese and global equipment makers. A Mirae Asset Securities report from late May 2026 estimated that MiCo Ceramics' annual capacity would expand from KRW 350 billion to KRW 500 billion.

The same report saw new ESC revenue becoming significant from 2027. The Phoenix, USA site was completed at the end of 2025, and the company targeted completion of a second Tainan plant in Taiwan in the first half of 2026, which is expected to extend capability to 3-2 nanometer-class products.

The company targets a dividend payout ratio of at least 25% for 2025–2027, and the 2025 payout ratio was 28.3%. It plans to introduce quarterly dividends through a charter amendment. A June 2026 report said it was pursuing a stock split that lowers par value from KRW 500 to KRW 200 along with a 1-for-1 bonus issue. Governance changes involving MiCo Ceramics are also under way.

08

Bull factors

Ceramic parts as a growth engine

The parts share of sales rose from 7% in 2021 to 50% in 2025. MiCo Ceramics' utilization increased to 89.4% in 2025 from 67.1% in 2024. A September 2026 report said MiCo Ceramics' Q2 2026 sales of KRW 80.2 billion were its record quarter.

Customer diversification and leading-edge exposure

Foundry accounts for about 35% of cleaning and coating sales, and the company counts Samsung Electronics, TSMC and Intel among its customers. The second Tainan plant in Taiwan is planned to extend capability to 3-2 nanometer-class products. The Phoenix, USA site was completed at the end of 2025.

Shareholder returns and governance clean-up

The company targets a payout ratio of at least 25% for 2025–2027, and the 2025 ratio was 28.3%. It plans to introduce quarterly dividends via a charter amendment. Raising the stake in MiCo Ceramics to 64.5% is presented as a step to ease uncertainty from duplicate listing of a subsidiary.

09

Bear factors

Profit stagnation despite sales growth

Operating profit fell 1.3% in 2025 and 4.6% year on year in Q3 2026, even as sales grew. One brokerage attributed the Q1 2026 profit squeeze to fixed costs from large-scale investment. The operating margin declined from 22.2% in 2024 to 18.4% in 2025.

Higher leverage and slower cash generation

The debt ratio rose from 50.5% in 2022 to 178.6% in 2025, with total liabilities of KRW 660.4 billion well above total equity of KRW 369.8 billion. Operating cash flow fell from KRW 115.6 billion in 2024 to KRW 38.2 billion in 2025. Net profit attributable to controlling shareholders swung to a loss of KRW 2.98 billion in Q2 2026.

Uneven utilization across overseas units

Cleaning utilization at the Singapore unit was 54.2% in 2025, up from 47.9% in 2024 but still modest. The Chinese units in Huanggang and Dalian are reported to still carry fixed-cost burdens. If utilization recovery stays uneven across regions, the pace of margin improvement could be limited.

10

Risk factors

Customer capex cycle

Demand for cleaning, coating and ceramic parts depends heavily on customer utilization and capex. Brokers cite new lines from 2027 as a growth driver, but delayed investment would push demand timing back. With a high foundry share, order swings from industry shifts could show up quickly in results.

Intercompany dealings and group structure

One brokerage's analysis said the Q4 2025 result partly reflected a one-off gain from intercompany dealings with MiCo Ceramics. Such one-off items can distort the quality of quarterly profit.

Raising the MiCo Ceramics stake and discussions around integration could affect governance and how profit is split among shareholders.

Financial burden and heavy investment

Fixed costs from heavy investment have weighed on quarterly operating profit. With the debt ratio at 178.6%, changes in interest rates or borrowing terms could affect the earnings burden. If overseas expansion runs ahead of utilization recovery, cost pressure could persist for some time.

Overall view

KoMiCo continues to grow sales, reaching KRW 604.1 billion in 2025 as the parts business expands. Operating profit stalled at KRW 111 billion in 2025 after peaking at KRW 112.5 billion in 2024, and it declined year on year again in Q3 2026.

Higher utilization in ceramic parts and a broader foundry customer base are on the positive side. A lower operating margin, a debt ratio of 178.6% and weaker operating cash flow are on the negative side. The company posted a net loss attributable to controlling shareholders in Q2 2026 and returned to profit in Q3.

Over the last four quarters, that line totaled KRW 29.2 billion. The next checkpoints are the G6 expansion completion by end-2026, the full-year 2026 results in early 2027, and the 2026 annual report by end-March 2027.
