02

Business structure

Kumho HT is an automotive parts maker whose core businesses are automotive lighting and battery parts. Its main products are LED modules and incandescent bulbs for automakers, and it also runs a bio unit that develops antibody therapeutics and licenses technology.

Hyundai Motor and KIA are the principal end customers in its lighting business. A 2023 credit rating report estimated the company's share of the domestic automotive incandescent bulb market at above 95%, while noting limited growth potential for that segment.

Its battery-parts line makes PCB assemblies for battery management systems (BMS) and battery module assemblies (BMA). BMS-related sales rose 225.1% in 2024, and sales to India rose 102.8% from a year earlier, with production centered at its Chennai plant.

In May 2025 the company announced three interior mood-lamp orders as a new growth driver. The bio unit came in through a 2021 merger with an antibody developer, and the company acquired a pharmaceutical distributor in 2022.

Most of its bio pipeline remains in early clinical stages, so revenue is limited and profitability in this unit is low. Its autonomous-driving-linked micro LED headlamp is still in development.

04

Earnings analysis

On a reported basis, annual revenue rose from KRW 277.8 billion in 2022 to KRW 364.3 billion in 2023 (up 31.2%) and KRW 444.9 billion in 2024 (up 22.1%), then fell to KRW 385.4 billion in 2025 (down 13.4%). Within 2022 to 2025, 2024 was the largest year and 2022 the smallest.

The company described 2024 revenue as a record high. Operating profit went from KRW 9.29 billion in 2022 to KRW 10.8 billion in 2023 (up 15.9%) and KRW 11.9 billion in 2024 (up 10.3%), then dropped to KRW 6.25 billion in 2025 (down 47.4%).

The company attributed the 2025 decline to a slowdown in operating and production activity at its Indian subsidiary, along with higher non-operating costs including foreign exchange losses and translation losses. The operating margin fell each year, from 3.3% in 2022 to 3.0%, 2.7%, and finally 1.6% in 2025.

Attributable net losses were KRW 22.6 billion in 2022, KRW 20.2 billion in 2023, KRW 14.9 billion in 2024, and KRW 23.3 billion in 2025, which is four consecutive years of losses, with the loss widening in 2025. Net loss including non-controlling interests followed the same path.

Operating cash flow was KRW 13.3 billion in 2022, negative KRW 6.02 billion in 2023, KRW 40.8 billion in 2024, and KRW 13.9 billion in 2025.

Quarterly revenue was KRW 90.3 billion in Q3 2025, KRW 95.2 billion in Q4 2025 (up 5.5% quarter on quarter), KRW 85.3 billion in Q1 2026, KRW 94 billion in Q2 2026, and KRW 91.7 billion in Q3 2026 (up 1.6% year on year).

Operating profit was KRW 806 million in Q3 2025, KRW 2.06 billion in Q4 2025, KRW 1.63 billion in Q1 2026, KRW 2.54 billion in Q2 2026, and KRW 187 million in Q3 2026, down 76.8% year on year.

Attributable net income turned to a profit of KRW 3.78 billion in Q1 2026 and stayed positive at KRW 2.62 billion in Q2 2026, before swinging to an attributable net loss of KRW 7.57 billion in Q3 2026. The attributable net loss over the last four quarters totals KRW 22.2 billion.

05

Industry analysis

The core business is tied to automaker production volumes and parts adoption. A 2025 report cited growing demand for automotive lighting and battery parts (BMS/BMA) from a recovering global auto market and expanding eco-friendly vehicle policies as positives.

The same report flagged U.S. auto tariffs as a possible hit to export-heavy companies. India was described as the world's third-largest market, and the company is building local production capacity there.

Incandescent bulbs face structural pressure as vehicles move to LED and electrification, so LED modules and battery parts are replacing them as profit drivers. On the battery side, tightening EV safety rules and wider BMS adoption are the cited demand drivers.

Peers in the same sector tag, such as Hanwha Aerospace and Korea Aerospace Industries, are centered on defense and aircraft contracts, so their demand cycles differ from Kumho HT's. As a parts supplier, the company's volumes depend heavily on sales at a few automaker customers, Hyundai Motor and KIA. Market share figures beyond the company's own estimate were not available for this report.

Outlook

In May 2025 the company's stated plans were a full-scale revenue ramp at its Indian subsidiary and the addition of new BMA vehicle programs, alongside a broader push into interior mood lighting.

Overseas growth depends on the Chennai plant, and the 2025 operating profit decline was linked to a slowdown in that plant's operations, so whether the Indian operation returns to normal running is the next key point.

At the annual shareholder meeting on March 26, 2026, shareholders approved the 38th-term financial statements, a share consolidation, treasury stock cancellation, and a capital reduction. After a 2-for-1 free share consolidation effective June 9, 2026, common shares outstanding stood at 10,456,929.

The company said the capital reduction was intended to secure distributable reserves for dividends and to right-size its capital base. A holder of more than 10% increased its stake through open-market purchases in late October 2026, which bears on the ownership structure.

Operating profit fell to KRW 187 million in Q3 2026, so how full-year 2026 operating profit and attributable net income are tallied in the next annual report is a main checkpoint. New-program launch timing can only be confirmed through the company's own disclosures. Bio-unit contributions will depend on clinical results and whether any licensing deals close.

Overall view

Kumho HT is an automotive parts company centered on lighting and battery parts. Although the sector tag is aerospace and defense, its business is automotive parts. Attributable net losses ran for four consecutive years from 2022 through 2025, and the operating margin fell from 3.3% in 2022 to 1.6% in 2025.

Quarterly results showed a partial recovery in 2026, with attributable profits in Q1 and Q2, but Q3 returned to an operating profit of KRW 187 million and an attributable net loss of KRW 7.57 billion.

Battery parts are the growth leg, with BMS sales rising sharply in 2024, and the Indian subsidiary is the hub for overseas expansion.

Against that, the incandescent bulb business has limited growth potential, the bio unit has low profitability, and external factors such as U.S. tariffs and the Indian plant's operations remain.

Total equity of KRW 280.7 billion and a debt ratio of 42.5% indicate financial capacity, but earnings quality stays limited until the operating margin recovers. The next checkpoints are full-year 2026 results in the annual report, Q1 2027 results, and whether the Indian operation returns to normal.
