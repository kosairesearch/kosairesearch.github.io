02

Business structure

Seojeon Electric Machinery makes and sells switchgear, which a company profile service puts at 99.95% of product sales.

Based on the 2024 Q3 breakdown, high-voltage switchgear accounted for KRW 13.3 billion (44.03%), low-voltage switchgear for KRW 6.7 billion (22.12%), motor control centers and distribution boards with other items for KRW 7.4 billion (24.41%), and other products for KRW 2.8 billion (9.32%).

The company set up an in-house research institute in 2009 and has built up intellectual property and certifications while widening its portfolio into high-end power equipment.

It obtained nuclear-sector electrical certification (KEPIC-EN) in 2020, and reports say it supplies switchgear and motor control panels for nuclear plants. It also keeps approved-supplier status with KEPCO and five power generation companies.

A notable recent contract was a KRW 16.8 billion job for a 154kV switching and substation package at the Jeju Sumang solar project, disclosed in December 2024 and equal to 38.3% of the prior year's revenue. R&D spending was KRW 2.57 billion in 2024, or 4.06% of revenue.

Listed peers in the same industry include LS ELECTRIC, Hyosung Heavy Industries, HD Hyundai Electric, Cheryong Electric and Sanil Electric. Because switchgear makes up nearly all of its product mix, quarterly results can swing sharply with the timing of individual project awards.

04

Earnings analysis

Full-year figures from the regular reports show revenue and profit moving in different directions.

Revenue went from KRW 50.7 billion in 2022 to KRW 43.3 billion in 2023 (down 14.7% year on year), then rose to KRW 63.3 billion in 2024 (up 46.4%) and KRW 68.1 billion in 2025 (up 7.5%), making 2025 the largest year of 2022 to 2025.

Operating results went from an operating loss of KRW 2.91 billion in 2022 and KRW 3.46 billion in 2023, when losses widened, to an operating profit of KRW 612 million in 2024, a turn to profit. In 2025 the company posted an operating loss of KRW 8.8 billion, turning back to a loss, with an operating margin of -12.9%.

In its 2025 disclosure, the company cited higher non-operating income from the sale of its Seoul office property and rising raw material costs that lifted manufacturing costs.

Net income attributable to controlling shareholders rose to KRW 6.94 billion in 2025, up 540.5%, even though operations lost money, which points to non-operating items. Operating cash flow was negative KRW 3.97 billion in 2025, after positive KRW 494 million in 2024.

The debt-to-equity ratio fell from 101.5% in 2024 to 79.4% in 2025, with total equity of KRW 38.4 billion and total liabilities of KRW 30.5 billion at year-end 2025.

Quarterly, Q3 2025 revenue was KRW 20.3 billion with operating profit of KRW 289 million, and Q4 2025 revenue rose 59.3% to KRW 32.3 billion with an operating loss of KRW 372 million. Q1 2026 revenue fell 76.9% to KRW 7.44 billion with an operating loss of KRW 4.16 billion.

Q2 2026 revenue was KRW 12 billion with a narrower operating loss of KRW 948 million, and Q3 2026 revenue was KRW 13.2 billion with operating profit of KRW 844 million, though revenue was down 35.1% year on year.

Net income attributable to controlling shareholders in Q3 2025 was KRW 18.1 billion against operating profit of KRW 289 million, so non-operating items weighed heavily that quarter. Over the last four quarters, net loss attributable to controlling shareholders totaled KRW 6.61 billion.

05

Industry analysis

Switchgear and power conversion equipment demand moves with grid investment and construction orders. When orders cluster in certain quarters, revenue jumps and then drops during order gaps, so quarterly results swing widely.

After the Russia-Ukraine war, surging raw material prices and inflation raised cost burdens for power equipment makers, and Seojeon cited higher raw material costs as a factor behind its manufacturing cost increase in its 2025 annual disclosure.

On the demand side, interest in nuclear and SMR-related power equipment has risen. The company has said it plans to offer SMR-specific switchgear, extra-high-voltage equipment and SCADA and maintenance solutions as it pursues K-nuclear projects.

Larger peers in the same industry, such as LS ELECTRIC, HD Hyundai Electric and Hyosung Heavy Industries, cover a broader equipment range, while Seojeon is a smaller player centered on switchgear with gas-insulated switchgear (GIS) added.

The company has promoted its GIS for a compact design that reduces SF6 gas use and a seismic-resistant structure. R&D spending in 2024 was 4.06% of revenue, indicating continued investment in product differentiation.

06

Outlook

Three plans have been confirmed. First, a 2024 report said the company would supply switchgear and related equipment to the Gijang research reactor site in Busan through 2027. Second, it plans to bid for extra-high-voltage power equipment supply for Shinhanul Units 3 and 4.

Third, it is preparing power equipment exports for a solar power plant in Aomori, Japan, and plans to strengthen overseas sales in rail, drawing on its rail strengths as Southeast Asian urban rail networks expand. In a 2024 report, the company set a goal of making that year its first export year.

The company's 2024 history records completion and move-in of its Magok R&D center, so the research base is in place. At the regular shareholders' meeting on March 27, 2026, the 2025 financial statements were approved, and the planned dividend for that year was none.

Q4 2026 results have not yet been filed in a regular report, and whether the Q3 2026 return to operating profit continues is the next checkpoint. The timing at which contract awards reach reported results differs by project, so quarterly revenue swings should be read alongside the order flow.

Bull factors

Narrower losses and a Q3 2026 profit turn

After an operating loss of KRW 4.16 billion in Q1 2026, the loss narrowed to KRW 948 million in Q2 and turned into an operating profit of KRW 844 million in Q3 2026. Quarterly profit improved from the bottom, and room for operating margin improvement is the core of the bull case. The bull case still depends on whether this trend holds beyond a single quarter.

Expansion plans in nuclear and extra-high-voltage equipment

Reports describe a supply contract for switchgear at the Gijang research reactor through 2027 and plans to compete for extra-high-voltage equipment at Shinhanul Units 3 and 4. Holding nuclear electrical certification since 2020 is cited as a qualification for entering nuclear projects. The revenue contribution from these projects has not yet appeared in figures from the regular reports.

Improved balance sheet

The debt-to-equity ratio fell from 101.5% in 2024 to 79.4% in 2025, and total equity expanded to KRW 38.4 billion at year-end 2025. A thicker capital base gives room to absorb operating losses. The 2025 non-operating gain from the Seoul office disposal also added to financial flexibility.

Bear factors

Annual operating loss returned

The company posted an operating loss of KRW 8.8 billion in 2025, turning back to a loss from an operating profit of KRW 612 million in 2024, with an operating margin of -12.9%. On an annual basis, 2024 was the only profitable year from 2022 to 2025.

Including the KRW 3.46 billion operating loss in 2023, operating losses persisted for much of the period, which is the core bear point.

Quarterly revenue swings and cumulative net losses

Q1 2026 revenue fell 76.9% from the prior quarter to KRW 7.44 billion, and Q3 2026 revenue was down 35.1% year on year. Taken together, the last four quarters still show a net loss attributable to controlling shareholders, so how far quarterly profit narrows the cumulative loss is the next point to check.

If the Q3 profit remains a single-quarter event, it would be hard to treat it as a trend, which is the bear reading.

Reliance on non-operating income and negative operating cash flow

Net income attributable to controlling shareholders of KRW 6.94 billion in 2025 moved in the opposite direction from an operating loss of KRW 8.8 billion, and the company cited higher non-operating income from an asset sale as a main cause. Operating cash flow was also negative at KRW 3.97 billion in 2025. Whether core operations regain cash generation is the next item to check.

Risk factors

Market alerts and price volatility

In March 2026 the stock moved from the investment-caution watch segment to the mid-sized company segment, and in May 2026 a pre-designation notice for investment-caution status followed a sharp short-term rise. Under the market alert system, the caution and risk stages can lead to suspended trading. This is a risk that can translate into trading restrictions for a stock with large price swings.

Restrictions on public contract bidding

Reports said that from March 31 to June 30, 2026, participation in contracts with public corporations, quasi-government agencies and the national government would be restricted. In a structure with heavy public procurement, such restrictions can lead to gaps in new orders.

That period has already ended, but whether similar limits apply later is a matter to track through filings and reports.

Cost pressure and earnings structure

The 2025 annual disclosure cited higher raw material costs as a driver of manufacturing cost growth. On projects with fixed contract prices, raw material increases are hard to pass through immediately. The 2025 fiscal year dividend was planned at zero, so shareholder returns remain limited until profits recover.

Overall view

Seojeon Electric Machinery is a switchgear-centered power equipment maker that posted an operating loss in 2025 and a larger quarterly loss in Q1 2026 before returning to operating profit in Q3 2026.

The mix of a 2024 profit, a 2025 loss and uneven 2026 quarters shows that earnings stability has not yet been established. Net income in 2025 relied heavily on non-operating items such as the Seoul office disposal, so it should be read apart from core operating profit.

The balance sheet improved with a lower debt-to-equity ratio and a larger equity base, but operating cash flow in 2025 was negative. Plans in nuclear, extra-high-voltage and export markets are supportive factors, but their revenue contribution has not yet appeared in the regular reports.

On the market side, a pre-designation notice for investment-caution status and public bid restrictions add volatility. Valuation is hard to anchor on earnings given the lack of a calculable P/E and zero dividend, so book net assets and the operating margin trend form the frame.

The next checkpoints are the Q4 2026 and full-year preliminary results and the operating margin in the 2026 annual report.
