02

Business structure

SH Energy & Chemical is a synthetic resin and plastics manufacturer founded in 1958, with synthetic resins as its core business. Its main products are ABS resin and EPS (expanded polystyrene), and the company's earnings commentary also mentions the ANYBES product line.

Its production base is a plant in Gunsan, Jeollabuk-do, which halted production for scheduled maintenance from March 20, 2026, with restart disclosed for April 8, 2026. Overseas, the company has set up a local joint venture in China to produce ABS and EPS locally, according to its company profile.

Its first-half 2026 commentary, compiled by WiseReport, cited higher ANYBES sales tied to K-beauty demand, the launch of semi-noncombustible and high-insulation products, and export expansion as drivers of revenue growth. The same commentary said the company has maintained its share of the domestic EPS market.

On the demand side, commodity resins such as EPS are linked to construction activity, and the cumulative explanation for the first nine months of 2025 identified a construction slowdown as a reason for weaker demand for commodity products.

That explanation also pointed to currency and raw material price swings and a rise in low-priced imports as pressures on profitability.

In competition, large petrochemical companies such as LG Chem, Lotte Chemical and Kumho Petro Chemical have broader scale and portfolios, whereas this company is concentrated in commodity synthetic resins and is relatively more exposed to price competition and import pressure. As of June 2026 it had 149 employees.

04

Earnings analysis

In the annual figures from the regular filings, revenue went from KRW 167.4 billion in 2022 to KRW 134 billion in 2023 (down 19.9% year on year), KRW 126 billion in 2024 (down 6.0%) and KRW 98.6 billion in 2025 (down 21.8%), declining for three consecutive years from 2023 through 2025.

Among 2022 through 2025, 2022 was the largest year and 2025 the smallest. Operating profit of KRW 805 million in 2022 turned into an operating loss of KRW 4.12 billion in 2023, then KRW 9.69 billion in 2024 and KRW 12.2 billion in 2025, as losses widened.

The operating margin declined from 0.5% in 2022 to -3.1% in 2023, -7.7% in 2024 and -12.4% in 2025, and the company recorded operating losses for three consecutive years from 2023 through 2025.

Controlling-shareholder net income of KRW 1.84 billion in 2022 became a controlling-shareholder net loss of KRW 2.58 billion in 2023, then KRW 10.4 billion in 2024 and KRW 10.8 billion in 2025.

Operating cash flow was -KRW 2.34 billion in 2022, KRW 4.26 billion in 2023, -KRW 4.72 billion in 2024 and -KRW 3.91 billion in 2025, negative in three of the four years.

The debt ratio rose from 31.9% in 2022 to 46.1% in 2025, and at the end of 2025 total equity was KRW 69.7 billion, of which KRW 69.5 billion belonged to controlling shareholders, against total liabilities of KRW 32.1 billion.

Quarterly, revenue of KRW 21.7 billion and an operating loss of KRW 2.33 billion in the third quarter of 2025 became revenue of KRW 25.1 billion (up 15.7% quarter on quarter) and an operating loss of KRW 3.29 billion in the fourth quarter, with losses widening.

In the first quarter of 2026, revenue was KRW 30.8 billion (up 22.7% quarter on quarter) and the operating loss narrowed to KRW 1.23 billion. In the second quarter, revenue was KRW 28.3 billion (down 8.3% quarter on quarter) and operating profit was KRW 94.18 million, a swing to profit.

Third-quarter revenue of KRW 29.1 billion rose 2.8% quarter on quarter and 33.8% year on year, but the operating loss of KRW 3.56 billion was larger than the prior-year loss.

The scheduled maintenance at the Gunsan plant ran from March 20 to April 8, 2026, so the second-quarter results include a period of halted production.

Industry analysis

The commodity synthetic resin market is shaped at the same time by construction demand and import prices.

The cumulative explanation for the first nine months of 2025 said a construction slowdown, currency and raw material price swings, and a rise in low-priced imports cut demand for commodity products and pulled revenue lower.

Read against that explanation, the three-year revenue decline from 2023 looks like the combined result of weak end-market demand and price competition.

Whether the 33.8% year-on-year revenue increase in the third quarter of 2026 came from volume recovery or from higher selling prices cannot be separated using the filed figures alone.

According to the company, higher ANYBES demand tied to K-beauty and export expansion lifted revenue in the first half of 2026, and it has kept its share of the domestic EPS market.

In competition, LG Chem, Lotte Chemical, Kumho Petro Chemical and Hanwha Solutions operate large petrochemical portfolios, while this company sits closer to a small or mid-sized chemical maker concentrated in commodity product lines.

Within the same industry, it is distinguished by carrying a listing-maintenance issue tied to market capitalization. On the cycle, weak construction demand combined with rising imports persisted from 2023 through 2025, and in 2026 quarterly revenue has moved between recovery and slowdown.
