02

Business structure

DaehanPharmaceutical is a pharmaceutical manufacturer that has grown around IV fluids since its founding in 1945, selling mainly to hospitals and clinics in a B2B model.

The company produces IV fluids, sterile injectables such as ampoules and vials, and dialysis solutions at its injectable facility, and also makes OTC drugs and quasi-drugs. Its core products are basic IV fluids such as sterile physiological saline, Hartmann's solution and 10 percent glucose-containing saline.

Armix, a comprehensive amino acid nutrition IV solution, is presented as a product for compounding TPN (total parenteral nutrition). According to a newsfield report, IV fluid products accounted for KRW 74.1 billion, or 67.7%, of KRW 109.3 billion in first-half 2026 revenue.

Older aggregated data based on the third-quarter 2023 report put IV fluids at 76.60% of sales and ampoules and vials at 22.44% (Hitnews). A 2024 media report placed JW Pharmaceutical's share of the domestic IV fluid market at about 50%, with DaehanPharmaceutical second at about 30%.

A 2024 brokerage report noted that IV fluids are designated as drugs subject to supply-shortage management, so manufacturers receive a margin on production cost. The Ansan plant was completed in 2005 and expanded and rebuilt in 2013 to house its IV injectable lines. Because sales are concentrated in IV fluids, hospital admission demand and product mix drive results.

04

Earnings analysis

Annual revenue rose from KRW 184.3 billion in 2022 to KRW 195.9 billion in 2023 (up 6.3% year on year), KRW 204.2 billion in 2024 (up 4.2%) and KRW 208.6 billion in 2025 (up 2.2%), increasing for three straight years from 2023 through 2025 with the pace of growth narrowing each year.

Operating profit climbed from KRW 33 billion in 2022 to KRW 35.8 billion in 2023 (up 8.6%) and KRW 38.1 billion in 2024 (up 6.4%), making 2024 the largest year of the period. It then dropped to KRW 31.2 billion in 2025, down 18.1% year on year, as the operating margin fell alongside revenue growth.

The operating margin was 17.9% in 2022, 18.3% in 2023, 18.6% in 2024 and 14.9% in 2025, the lowest of the four years. Net income attributable to controlling shareholders peaked at KRW 33.8 billion in 2024 and was KRW 27.6 billion in 2025, down 18.1%.

Net income including non-controlling interests was disclosed at KRW 25 billion, KRW 28.4 billion, KRW 33.8 billion and KRW 27.6 billion for 2022 through 2025. At the end of 2025, total equity was KRW 300.2 billion and total liabilities were KRW 47.4 billion, and the debt ratio fell from 22.9% in 2022 to 15.8% in 2025.

Operating cash flow moved from KRW 38.9 billion in 2023 to KRW 35.6 billion in 2024 and KRW 25.6 billion in 2025, the lowest of the four years.

Quarterly, revenue of KRW 56.2 billion and operating profit of KRW 10.6 billion in the third quarter of 2025 were followed by a trough in the fourth quarter, with revenue of KRW 51.1 billion (down 9.1% quarter on quarter) and operating profit of KRW 3.57 billion (down 66.4%).

Revenue then rose for three consecutive quarters in 2026, to KRW 52.7 billion, KRW 56.6 billion and KRW 57 billion, and operating profit rose over the same stretch to KRW 7.86 billion, KRW 8.08 billion and KRW 8.19 billion.

However, third-quarter 2026 operating profit was down 23.0% year on year, and net income attributable to controlling shareholders peaked at KRW 6.98 billion in the first quarter of 2026 before falling to KRW 6.75 billion and KRW 5.65 billion over the next two quarters.

Industry analysis

A 2024 brokerage analysis argued that IV fluids, as basic medicines, see demand rise in line with an aging population and hospital admission days. The same analysis noted that admissions recovered after the endemic phase and rose again in 2023 with a flu season.

Because IV fluids are designated as drugs subject to supply-shortage management, manufacturers receive a margin on production cost and are relatively insulated from price cuts, according to the same report.

A 2024 media report put JW Pharmaceutical at about 50% of the domestic IV fluid market, with DaehanPharmaceutical second at about 30%.

High-value nutrition IV solutions received approval in September 2021, but a 2024 report said their launch had been delayed, so the pace of product-mix upgrades matters for narrowing the share gap.

Across the wider pharmaceutical sector, a 2026 report said the US president had mentioned raising tariffs on Korean-made drugs to 25% in January 2026, adding uncertainty. With a domestic hospital-supply focus, the direct effect of tariffs may be relatively limited.

Separately, after a low-PBR listing, traditional pharmaceutical companies have been discussed as candidates for expanded shareholder returns such as higher dividends and treasury share cancellation.

Structural support from demand and the stability of the regulated cost structure stand out as strengths, while the share gap with the market leader and the speed of new product launches remain weaknesses.

Regulatory and quality risk

In May 2026 a fine of KRW 134.4 million was imposed over manufacturing and quality-control issues for a sterile physiological saline product. In February 2026 some lots of an OTC cleansing product were recalled over a labeling error.

Policy and tariff risk

A report said the US president mentioned raising tariffs on Korean-made drugs to 25% in January 2026. Because sales are centered on domestic hospital supply, direct exposure may be relatively small.

However, if the policy status of IV fluids or the cost-recovery framework changes, margins could be affected, so any regulatory change is a checkpoint.

Demand and sales concentration risk

Because IV fluids account for most sales, a decline in hospital admission demand could affect revenue across the business. Admission days depend on factors such as an aging population and seasonal flu activity, so in years with weaker flu seasons or fewer admissions, IV orders could decline.

The high dependence on one product group leaves limited room to offset the drop with other products. Changes in hospital admission days and the IV share of sales are therefore the key variables shaping results.

Overall view

DaehanPharmaceutical is a domestic manufacturer for hospitals with a high share of IV fluids, and revenue and operating profit both rose from 2022 through 2024.

In 2025 revenue reached KRW 208.6 billion, a third straight annual increase, but the operating margin fell to 14.9% and operating profit dropped to KRW 31.2 billion.

In 2026 revenue rose for three consecutive quarters through the third quarter, and operating profit rose for three consecutive quarters from the fourth-quarter 2025 trough.

However, third-quarter 2026 operating profit was down 23.0% year on year and net income attributable to controlling shareholders declined for two consecutive quarters, so the recovery has not yet reached the 2025-and-earlier level.

Strengths include stable IV-driven demand, a cost-recovery structure tied to its designation as a drug subject to supply-shortage management, and shareholder-return discussion after the low-PBR listing.

Weaknesses include the margin decline, lower cash flow, the regulatory warning and the share gap with the market leader. Valuation is best read through the relationship between net assets, dividends and the pace of earnings recovery, and the reading depends on whether the operating margin recovers.

The next checkpoints are the operating margin in the 2026 annual report, the dividend decision at the March 2027 annual general meeting, and the first-quarter 2027 quarterly report.
