Report overview

Chip and LNG piping demand, with net profit slowing

Hy-Lok is a fittings and valves specialist whose third-quarter 2026 operating profit rose from a year earlier while profit attributable to controlling shareholders fell, so its earnings trend points in mixed directions.

02

Business structure

Hy-Lok is a maker of industrial pipe fittings and valves, and its main products are parts used in piping that carries gas and fluids. The company mainly produces instrumentation parts, which one analysis cited as giving it a higher share of plant-related sales than its peers.

A 2025 press report put petrochemical and onshore and offshore plant sales together at roughly half of revenue. Another analysis said the shipbuilding segment's share of revenue widened from 14.1% in 2022 to 26.3% in 2024, making it a central pillar of group results.

Segment share figures differ across sources because of different aggregation bases. High-value vessel valves and fittings are supplied as demand for LNG-fueled ships grows, and renewed FLNG orders are another demand source.

In semiconductors, demand for ultra-high-purity (UHP) valves and fittings tied to AI capital spending is the core, and the company is pursuing supply for large fab construction by SK hynix and Samsung Electronics in the Yongin semiconductor cluster.

According to Bosoop's summary of recent annual filings, exports account for 71.2% of sales and domestic sales for 28.8%.

In the United States, the company is gradually expanding supply through distributors, but it has not yet won a direct order for a US LNG terminal or gas project and is still in the stage of obtaining local supplier approval and winning new customers.

04

Earnings analysis

Annual results show revenue and profit rising together. Revenue went from KRW 182.9 billion in 2022 to KRW 188.8 billion in 2023 (up 3.3%) and KRW 190.5 billion in 2024 (up 0.9%), then reached KRW 214.6 billion in 2025 (up 12.6%), rising for three consecutive years from 2023 through 2025.

Operating profit jumped from KRW 40.7 billion in 2022 to KRW 51.9 billion in 2023 (up 27.5%), fell to KRW 50.1 billion in 2024 (down 3.4%), and recovered to KRW 58.9 billion in 2025 (up 17.5%).

The operating margin moved from 22.3% in 2022 to 27.5% in 2023, then 26.3% in 2024 and 27.5% in 2025, staying within a 25–28% band.

Net profit attributable to controlling shareholders rose from KRW 33.9 billion in 2022 to KRW 47.3 billion in 2023 (up 39.8%), grew only 0.4% in 2024 to KRW 47.5 billion alongside the operating profit decline, and reached KRW 50.3 billion in 2025 (up 5.9%).

Net profit including non-controlling interests, reported separately, was KRW 50.8 billion in 2025. At year-end 2025, total equity was KRW 459.1 billion, of which KRW 454.4 billion belonged to controlling shareholders, total liabilities were KRW 38.9 billion, and the debt-to-equity ratio was 8.5%.

Operating cash flow rose from KRW 19.7 billion in 2022 to KRW 47.3 billion in 2025.

By quarter, revenue was KRW 57.4 billion in the fourth quarter of 2025 (up 6.5% from the prior quarter), fell to KRW 54.1 billion in the first quarter of 2026 (down 5.6%), then recovered to KRW 59.3 billion in the second quarter and KRW 59.4 billion in the third.

Operating profit rose from KRW 14.2 billion in the third quarter of 2025 to KRW 16.8 billion in the third quarter of 2026 (up 18.3% year on year).

Net profit attributable to controlling shareholders peaked at KRW 15.2 billion in the first quarter of 2026, then fell to KRW 14.7 billion in the second quarter and KRW 9.72 billion in the third, down 27.7% year on year. Over the last four quarters, net profit attributable to controlling shareholders totaled KRW 53.8 billion.

05

Industry analysis

The end markets fall into three groups: semiconductor capital spending, LNG infrastructure, and offshore plants. In semiconductors, AI demand is lifting memory makers' capital spending, which is increasing orders for precision fittings and valves.

In a November 2026 press report, a company official said semiconductor-related orders are expected to keep rising into 2027. New orders in the first quarter of 2026 were KRW 56.5 billion, up 10.1% from a year earlier, according to Daishin Securities in May 2026.

In LNG, Daishin Securities expected construction to move quickly on US LNG export terminals using medium-scale modular trains and on FLNG projects that are under construction.

A 2025 analysis argued that the company supplies parts to a domestic shipbuilder with a high share of global FLNG construction, so it stands to benefit as orders resume. The high share of petrochemical and other cyclical sectors is a variable if capital spending slows.

Competitively, Hy-Lok specializes in instrumentation fittings and valves and serves both plant and semiconductor customers, so weakness in one end market can be partly offset by the other.

Outlook

The company's direction depends on whether semiconductor orders keep growing and how fast it enters the US LNG market. A company official said semiconductor orders are continuing to rise and are expected to grow into 2027 (November 2026 press report).

Daishin Securities, in a May 2026 report, estimated 2026 revenue at KRW 236.2 billion and operating profit at KRW 66.7 billion, and presented 2027 revenue at KRW 248.3 billion.

These are broker estimates and should be read apart from filed results; whether full-year 2026 figures land near these estimates is the first checkpoint once fourth-quarter 2026 results are released.

In the United States, the company is gradually expanding supply through distributors, and obtaining local supplier approval and new customers are the next steps. On the shipbuilding side, one analysis argues that structural demand for large offshore plants is rising with FLNG orders.

Entry into UHP parts for the Yongin semiconductor cluster is presented as a medium-term goal. On shareholder returns, the company retired KRW 15 billion of treasury shares in September 2025, and a March 2026 value-up plan set a target payout ratio of 25–35%, according to a securities report. How the third-quarter decline in net profit affects progress toward that target is the next point to watch.

08

Bull factors

Rising semiconductor orders

New orders in the first quarter of 2026 were KRW 56.5 billion, up 10.1% from a year earlier, and March orders were KRW 20.1 billion, according to Daishin Securities in May 2026. A company official said semiconductor orders are still rising and are expected to keep growing into 2027 (November 2026 press report).

Utilization at the Hy-Lok fitting plant reached 95% in the first half of 2026, up from 89% in the first half of 2025.

LNG and FLNG project demand

The company is gradually expanding supply for LNG export terminals and gas infrastructure through US distributors (November 2026 press report). Daishin Securities, in a May 2026 report, expected construction to move quickly on medium-scale modular LNG export terminals and FLNG projects.

A 2025 press report raised the possibility that the company would benefit from supplying a domestic shipbuilder with a large share of global FLNG construction.

Low financial burden and shareholder returns

Total liabilities at the end of 2025 were KRW 38.9 billion, small relative to total equity of KRW 459.1 billion, and the debt-to-equity ratio was 8.5% in 2025. The company retired treasury shares worth KRW 15 billion in September 2025, and its March 2026 value-up plan set a target payout ratio of 25–35%.

09

Bear factors

Third-quarter net profit decline

Net profit attributable to controlling shareholders was KRW 9.72 billion in the third quarter of 2026, down 27.7% from a year earlier and down 34.0% from the prior quarter. Operating profit in the same quarter rose 18.3% year on year, so the two measures moved in opposite directions.

Press reports pointed to lower pre-tax income as the cause. Why operating profit gains did not carry through to net profit is a point to check in the next periodic report.

High export share and an early-stage US push

According to Bosoop's summary, the export share is 71.2%, which makes results sensitive to overseas demand and currency moves. In the US LNG market, the company has not yet won a direct order and is still securing local supplier approval and new customers. It may take time before US expansion shows up in revenue.

Slower growth in some periods

Operating profit fell 3.4% in 2024 to KRW 50.1 billion, and profit attributable to controlling shareholders grew only 0.4% that year. First-quarter 2026 revenue fell 5.6% from the prior quarter.

A May 2026 broker estimate assumed higher full-year operating profit, while third-quarter 2026 net profit attributable to controlling shareholders declined from a year earlier.

10

Risk factors

Macro and capex cycle

End markets such as petrochemicals and shipbuilding are cyclical, so orders can fall when capital spending slows. Semiconductor orders rely heavily on AI demand, so a pullback in investment would reach UHP parts demand. LNG project orders can slip depending on the timing of final investment decisions.

Ownership concentration and float

Related parties of the largest shareholder hold 39.73%, and the free float ratio is 58.68% (WiseReport, data as of October 6, 2026). A high insider stake can limit the number of tradable shares and may amplify price swings. Foreign ownership stands at 29.84%.

Trade and currency factors

With a high export share, trade policy changes such as tariffs and currency swings can affect profitability. In the US, the distributor model means results depend heavily on how quickly new customers are won.

Because quarterly profit moves widely, policy changes concentrated in one quarter could make reported earnings more volatile.

12

Overall view

Hy-Lok Korea receives both semiconductor capital expenditure demand and LNG and plant demand through its fittings and valves. Based on reported results, revenue has risen for three consecutive years since 2022, and 2025 full-year operating profit of KRW 58.9 billion was the largest of the 2022–2025 period.

In the third quarter of 2026, revenue was KRW 59.4 billion and operating profit was KRW 16.8 billion, both up year over year, but net profit attributable to controlling shareholders fell 27.7% to KRW 9.72 billion, so the earnings picture was mixed.

Semiconductor orders rose to KRW 56.5 billion in the first quarter of 2026, and the company expects them to keep increasing in 2027, but the U.S. LNG market is at an early stage in which it has no direct order experience.

Bullish factors include a 95% utilization rate at its production facilities, a low financial burden with a debt-to-equity ratio of 8.5%, and its treasury share cancellation and dividend policies, while bearish factors include the decline in net profit, a high export share of 71.2%, and exposure to cyclical industries.

Brokerages assessed the valuation as discounted relative to the historical average PER, but because earnings move from quarter to quarter, the KRW 53.8 billion in net profit attributable to controlling shareholders over the latest four quarters serves as the baseline for judgment.

The next checkpoints are the preliminary fourth-quarter 2026 results (around February 2027), the 2026 annual report (due at the end of March 2027), and the first-quarter 2027 quarterly report. This report does not provide an investment opinion or a target price.
