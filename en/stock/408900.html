02

Business structure

Studio Mir is an animation production company whose core model is full production, covering planning, key animation and in-between production, editing and sound recording in-house. Revenue is split between full production, which handles all stages, and general production, which handles some stages.

In 2024, full-production revenue was KRW 16.4 billion, up 12.3% year on year, while general-production revenue was KRW 1.1 billion, down 86.7%. A Korean IR research body noted in a 2024 report that full-production work generally earns higher contract unit prices than general production.

Its customers are mainly global streaming platforms and overseas studios. It signed a long-term content supply deal with Netflix in 2019 and an additional animation production and supply contract in June 2023, with the contract value withheld until June 29, 2028.

It has also worked with DreamWorks and Paramount, and in June 2025 signed a production contract with Marvel Animation Studio. Representative titles released on Netflix include The Witcher: Nightmare of the Wolf, DOTA: Dragon's Blood and Lookism.

In December 2023 it spun off its 3D business division into a subsidiary, Studio Mir CGI. In September 2024 it signed a business agreement with Studio N and Toei Animation to animate webtoon IP, as it prepares its own IP business. Shares held by the largest shareholder and related parties are in the mid-60% range.

04

Earnings analysis

Annual results show revenue contraction and a swing into losses happening at the same time.

Revenue fell from KRW 25.8 billion in 2022 to KRW 23.2 billion in 2023 (down 10.2% year on year), KRW 17.6 billion in 2024 (down 24.1%) and KRW 16.4 billion in 2025 (down 6.7%), declining for three consecutive years from 2023 to 2025. 2022 was the largest year and 2025 the smallest within 2022 to 2025.

Operating profit fell from KRW 3.34 billion in 2022 to KRW 861 million in 2023 (down 74.2%). The company then posted an operating loss of KRW 1.9 billion in 2024, a swing into the red, and a wider operating loss of KRW 2.15 billion in 2025, marking two consecutive loss years.

The operating margin went from 13.0% in 2022 to 3.7% in 2023, -10.8% in 2024 and -13.1% in 2025. The company attributed the main swing in earnings to a temporary drop in sales while it built an IP development business structure.

Net profit attributable to controlling shareholders was KRW 1.51 billion in 2022 and KRW 2.5 billion in 2023 (up 65.7%), then fell to KRW 109 million in 2024 (down 95.7%) before turning into a net loss of KRW 1.43 billion in 2025.

Net income including non-controlling interests was a net loss of KRW 1.43 billion in 2025. Operating cash flow was KRW 3.02 billion in 2022, KRW 6.29 billion in 2023 and KRW 717 million in 2024, then turned negative at -KRW 1.39 billion in 2025.

The balance sheet remains light: at the end of 2025 total equity was KRW 38.6 billion, of which KRW 38 billion belonged to controlling shareholders, and total liabilities were KRW 4.1 billion, with the debt ratio falling from 21.0% in 2022 to 10.6% in 2025. Quarterly results are volatile.

After an operating loss of KRW 749 million in Q3 2025, Q4 2025 revenue rose to KRW 5.11 billion (up 31.5% quarter on quarter) and the company posted an operating profit of KRW 327 million.

Q1 2026 revenue was KRW 3.55 billion with an operating loss of KRW 902 million, Q2 2026 narrowed the operating loss to KRW 430 million, and Q3 2026 recorded an operating loss of KRW 651 million (narrower than a year earlier), the third consecutive loss quarter.

The net loss attributable to controlling shareholders was KRW 1.92 billion in Q3 2026, larger than the operating loss, and the total for the last four quarters was KRW 1.75 billion.

05

Industry analysis

Animation production depends heavily on how much global streaming platforms spend on content. A 2023 report cited Netflix's original animation spending at about USD 5 billion in 2022, more than 4.5 times the USD 1.1 billion of 2018. The model is contract-based, however, so revenue moves with individual projects.

A 2023 analysis pointed to the structural limit of revenue swinging with one-off large project wins. Revenue is also recognized on a percentage-of-completion basis tied to cost input, so gaps between projects feed directly into quarterly results.

A 2024 brokerage analysis expected general-production revenue to fall sharply because staffing capacity was limited. The company described the 2025 revenue decline as a temporary gap during its move toward an IP development business structure.

On competition, the company is seen as one of the few Korean animation studios to have earned recognition in overseas markets through work with major global partners such as Netflix and DreamWorks.

Even within the media and entertainment sector, it operates mainly on production contracts, unlike larger companies that own IP and handle distribution, which gives it lower earnings visibility.

The pricing advantage of full production and the sharp drop in general-production revenue are the two main variables for profitability. Its entry into IP was given concrete form by a September 2024 business agreement, and a Korean IR research body said later that year that IP expansion could support long-term growth.

06

Outlook

The contract pipeline is the clearest view of forward activity. After production contracts with company D (Work 14) in May 2025, company C (Work 15) in June 2025 and company D (Work 16) in September 2025, the company signed a contract with company A for Work 17 in April 2026.

The Marvel Animation production contract disclosed on October 27, 2026 covers Disney+ service countries as its sales territory, with the contract value withheld until January 31, 2029.

The value of the June 2023 Netflix contract is also withheld until June 29, 2028, and the company has said it will disclose it after the withholding period. The CEO has said the company plans to keep expanding partnerships with global partners and launch new projects.

On IP, the company has said it will secure IP through in-house development or acquisition. Research into CG technology and AI-based virtual idols at Studio Mir CGI has been presented as a path to combine with IP for games and 3D drama projects.

Even as contract disclosures continue, revenue is recognized by stage of completion, so contract signings and revenue growth may not fall in the same quarter.

The full-year 2026 results will be disclosed in the annual report due by late March 2027, which will first formally confirm Q4 2026 results and the full-year operating margin.

08

Bull factors

Ongoing work with global studios

After a long-term supply deal with Netflix in 2019, the company has continued working with DreamWorks, Paramount and Marvel Animation. A contract for Work 17 with company A in April 2026 was followed by a Marvel Animation production contract disclosed in October 2026. Some observers view it as one of the few Korean studios recognized in overseas markets.

Narrowing loss trend

The operating loss narrowed to KRW 430 million in Q2 2026 from KRW 902 million in Q1, and the Q3 2026 operating loss of KRW 651 million was smaller than the KRW 749 million of Q3 2025. Q4 2025 posted an operating profit of KRW 327 million, so quarterly results have moved sharply. Whether that profit was a one-off will be clearer in the next annual results.

Push into owned IP

The company is pursuing owned IP and in September 2024 signed a business agreement with Studio N and Toei Animation to animate webtoon IP. Its 3D division became Studio Mir CGI through a December 2023 spin-off, creating a base for CG expertise.

Entry into IP is cited as a source of long-term growth because it could diversify a structure built on production fees.

Bear factors

Revenue down three straight years

Revenue fell from KRW 25.8 billion in 2022 to KRW 16.4 billion in 2025, and Q3 2026 revenue of KRW 3.22 billion was down 17.1% year on year. The company cited a temporary sales decline tied to building its IP business structure as the main driver of 2025 earnings. On the disclosed record, Q3 2026 revenue is the lowest of the last five quarters.

Weaker operating cash flow

Operating cash flow moved from KRW 717 million in 2024 to -KRW 1.39 billion in 2025, so the losses also show up in cash generation. The operating margin of -13.1% in 2025 compares with 13.0% in 2022. When profit and cash flow weaken together, working capital and project payment timing become important to track.

Project dependence and fixed costs

Repeated criticism since 2023 has pointed to revenue swinging with individual project wins. Reports said executive compensation exceeded KRW 1.7 billion in 2024, and fixed costs are cited as a drag on earnings. Because revenue follows percentage of completion, any gap in contracts quickly shows up as a quarterly loss.

Risk factors

Market alerts and short-term volatility

The Korea Exchange designated the stock as an investment caution issue for one day on October 28, 2026, citing concentrated trading by specific accounts, and again on November 2, 2026, citing concentrated trading by a small number of accounts.

Both designations were triggered by large price moves over the prior three trading days. Reports noted that market alerts could escalate or trading could be suspended.

Governance and shareholder meeting

At the regular shareholder meeting in March 2026, a proposal to elect an auditor was left pending for lack of a quorum, the company disclosed. The largest shareholder and related parties hold shares in the mid-60% range, which limits the influence of outside shareholders. Executive pay and transactions with related parties in 2024 have been raised as points of concern.

Business and earnings volatility

General-production revenue fell 86.7% in 2024, and a 2024 analysis expected it to drop sharply due to limited staffing. Full-production revenue rose, but quarterly revenue swings widely depending on contract gaps. Because overseas OTT contract values are withheld, outside observers find it hard to gauge contract scale.

Overall view

Studio Mir has strengths in its full-production model and its work with global studios such as Netflix and Marvel Animation. On the other hand, disclosed revenue fell from KRW 25.8 billion in 2022 to KRW 16.4 billion in 2025, declining for three consecutive years, and operating losses have continued since 2024.

Losses persisted into 2026 across three consecutive quarters, and the net loss attributable to controlling shareholders over the last four quarters totaled KRW 1.75 billion. On the balance sheet, the debt ratio was a low 10.6% in 2025 and total equity was KRW 38.6 billion.

Price swings were large enough to bring two investment caution designations in October and November 2026. On valuation, an earnings-based multiple cannot be calculated, so the ratio to net assets is the main reference point.

Bullish factors include the contract pipeline and a narrower quarterly loss, while bearish factors include shrinking revenue, persistent losses and governance questions. The next checkpoint is how the full-year operating margin, which was -13.1% in 2025, changes in the 2026 annual report.
