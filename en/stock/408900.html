02

Business structure

Studio Mir is a Seoul-based animation production company founded in 2010, providing 2D and 3D animation planning and production services to global OTT platforms and major studios.

Its credits include Nickelodeon's The Legend of Korra, followed by Warner Bros./DC projects such as X-Men '97 and My Adventures with Superman, and Netflix titles including Devil May Cry, Voltron: Legendary Defender, and The Witcher: Nightmare of the Wolf.

More recently, the company has joined Nickelodeon and Paramount on a new theatrical animated feature set in the Avatar: The Last Airbender universe, while continuing additional Warner Bros. Animation projects such as Batman: Knightfall, Batman: Knightquest, and My Adventures with Green Lantern.

The company is also expanding ties with the Japanese animation market through a joint planning and development project called Gosu with Toei Animation and Studio N, and is providing CG modelling cooperation on Trigun Stargaze with Orange and Toho Animation.

Its subsidiary, Studio Mir CGI, handles CG and 3D work along with AI-based production tool research aimed at improving drawing consistency and productivity.

The company's revenue structure reflects an order-driven business where results depend on a limited number of large-scale projects and their delivery schedules, rather than proprietary IP licensing income.

Its client base is concentrated among a handful of global majors such as Netflix, Warner Bros., and Paramount, making the company sensitive to shifts in any single client's content investment policy.

04

Earnings analysis

Consolidated revenue declined for four straight years, from KRW 25.781 billion in 2022 to KRW 23.162 billion in 2023, KRW 17.571 billion in 2024, and KRW 16.391 billion in 2025.

Operating profit peaked at KRW 3.340 billion (13.0% margin) in 2022 before narrowing to KRW 861 million (3.7%) in 2023, then turning into an operating loss of KRW 1.902 billion (-10.8%) in 2024 and widening further to a loss of KRW 2.146 billion (-13.1%) in 2025.

Net income attributable to owners exceeded operating profit in 2022 (KRW 1.511 billion) and 2023 (KRW 2.504 billion), but collapsed to KRW 109 million in 2024 and swung to a net loss of KRW 1.434 billion in 2025.

Quarterly results show pronounced volatility: after posting revenue of KRW 3.593 billion with an operating loss of KRW 965 million and a net loss of KRW 1.159 billion in Q2 2025, revenue edged up to KRW 3.888 billion in Q3 with the operating loss narrowing to KRW 749 million.

Revenue then jumped to KRW 5.113 billion in Q4 2025, delivering a surprise swing to operating profit of KRW 327 million and net income of KRW 708 million, likely reflecting concentrated delivery and revenue recognition on specific projects.

However, Q1 2026 reverted to a loss, with revenue falling to KRW 3.548 billion alongside an operating loss of KRW 902 million and a net loss of KRW 219 million, and Q2 2026 continued the loss trend with revenue of KRW 3.869 billion, an operating loss of KRW 430 million, and a net loss of KRW 317 million.

Total equity attributable to owners rose sharply from KRW 20.940 billion in 2022 to KRW 36.852 billion in 2023, likely reflecting capital inflows around the company's listing, before growth slowed to KRW 39.209 billion in 2024 and edged down to KRW 38.002 billion in 2025.

Operating cash flow was solid at KRW 3.016 billion in 2022 and KRW 6.289 billion in 2023, but fell sharply to KRW 717 million in 2024 and turned to a net outflow of KRW 1.387 billion in 2025, mirroring the deterioration in profitability.

05

Industry analysis

The global animation outsourcing industry depends heavily on original content demand from major streaming platforms such as Netflix, Disney+, Max, and Crunchyroll.

Since the post-pandemic content spending boom ended, the industry has broadly noted that production order cycles among streamers have become more conservative than before.

Korean animation studios have secured global orders on the strength of relatively strong drawing quality and cost competitiveness, and Studio Mir is regarded as one of the few domestic firms to have secured all three major partners—Netflix, Warner Bros., and Paramount.

Generative AI-driven production innovation has been spreading, particularly in Hollywood, and the competitive landscape in Korea is also diversifying with the emergence of AI-focused content production companies such as Utopai Studios.

However, major studios still allocate only a portion of their production budgets to generative AI, suggesting that demand for traditional drawing-based production services is unlikely to shrink sharply in the near term.

Studio Mir is likewise developing AI-based tools in-house through its subsidiary to improve drawing consistency and efficiency, responding to this broader industry shift.

Relative to peers, the company's position benefits from a track record of repeat business with multiple global majors, but like its competitors it remains structurally exposed to revenue volatility during downturns in clients' content investment cycles given its order-based rather than IP-owning business model.

Bull factors

Diversified Global Order Pipeline

The company runs projects simultaneously with multiple global majors including Netflix, Warner Bros., Paramount, and Toei Animation, so results are not dependent on the success of a single title.

In 2026 alone, multiple new titles including Bass x Machina, Batman: Knightfall/Knightquest, and the Avatar: The Last Airbender theatrical film are set for release. Such a multi-client, multi-project pipeline can help cushion revenue gaps when any single contract ends.

Proven Track Record of Repeat Business

Since The Legend of Korra, the company has continuously delivered major global projects for over a decade, including X-Men '97, My Adventures with Superman, and Devil May Cry.

Extending its long-term production contract with Netflix in February 2026 and signing an additional new-title supply contract in September shows that repeat orders from existing partners continue. This suggests sustained confidence from global clients in the company's production quality and delivery reliability.

Investment in AI-Based Production Efficiency

Through its subsidiary Studio Mir CGI, the company has conducted in-house R&D on AI-based production tools aimed at improving drawing consistency.

If this can mitigate the chronic risk of inconsistent drawing quality in traditional 2D animation, it could enhance competitiveness in terms of production efficiency and delivery stability.

Amid industry-wide adoption of AI, the company's push to internalize such technology supports the possibility of medium- to long-term cost structure improvement.

Bear factors

Multi-Year Decline in Revenue and Profit

Consolidated revenue declined for four consecutive years, from KRW 25.781 billion in 2022 to KRW 16.391 billion in 2025, while operating profit swung from a surplus in 2022 to consecutive losses in 2024 and 2025.

This suggests not merely a temporary slowdown but a possible structural weakening of the order cycle itself. A clear and sustained reversal signal in revenue has not yet been consistently confirmed in quarterly results.

Unstable Quarterly Profitability

While the company posted a surprise operating profit of KRW 327 million in Q4 2025, it reverted to operating losses of KRW 902 million and KRW 430 million in Q1 and Q2 2026, respectively.

This reflects a structural limitation typical of order-based industries, where quarterly results swing sharply depending on project-specific revenue recognition timing. Whether the brief return to profit represents a sustained trend has not yet been confirmed.

Client Concentration and Lack of Owned IP

A substantial portion of revenue is concentrated among a handful of global majors such as Netflix, Warner Bros., and Paramount, meaning a change in any single client's content investment policy or contract reduction could directly hit results.

Because the company earns production service fees rather than owning IP and collecting royalty or licensing income, even a commercially successful title may not translate directly into additional revenue. This can amplify revenue volatility during downturns in clients' content investment cycles.

Risk factors

Earnings Volatility

Quarterly results fluctuate significantly depending on project-specific delivery and revenue recognition timing, as shown by the swing to losses in Q1 and Q2 2026 following a profit in Q4 2025. This structural characteristic makes it difficult to interpret a single quarter's profit as a sustained improvement.

Similar volatility could recur going forward depending on the timing of new title releases and revenue recognition.

Client Concentration Risk

High dependence on a small number of global majors such as Netflix, Warner Bros., and Paramount means that any reduction in content investment or change in contract terms by these platforms could create order gaps.

If the broader streaming industry's content investment stance becomes more conservative, securing new orders could be delayed. Whether contracts with specific clients are renewed could remain a key variable for future results.

Industry Structural Shift

As generative AI-driven production innovation spreads, particularly in Hollywood, there is discussion of potential long-term structural change in demand for traditional drawing-based outsourcing. The emergence of new AI-focused production companies is also diversifying the competitive landscape.

However, assessments suggest major studios' AI investment remains limited so far, meaning the pace of change remains uncertain.

Overall view

Studio Mir maintains its production pipeline by running multiple projects concurrently with global majors including Netflix, Warner Bros., Paramount, and Toei Animation, and the extension and addition of Netflix contracts in both February and September 2026 show that repeat business relationships continue.

However, consolidated revenue has declined for four consecutive years since 2022, operating losses have persisted for two straight years since 2024, and net income also swung to a loss in 2025.

Quarterly results have yet to show a clear sustained turnaround, swinging from a surprise profit in Q4 2025 back to losses in both Q1 and Q2 2026.

How a number of 2026 projects—including the November release of Bass x Machina, the Avatar theatrical film, and the Batman series—get reflected in future revenue recognition will be a key factor to watch.

Given a business model that relies on order-based production services rather than owned IP, clients' content investment cycles and individual project delivery schedules remain the core variables driving earnings volatility.

Readers should weigh the facts presented above together with upcoming disclosures and release schedules in forming their own judgment.
