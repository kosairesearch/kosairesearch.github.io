02

Business structure

Studio Mir is an animation production company founded in 2010 by CEO Yoo Jae-myung that has internalized the full production chain from pre-production through main production to post-production.

Its notable works include 'The Legend of Korra,' 'Voltron: Legendary Defender,' 'Dota: Dragon's Blood,' 'The Witcher: Nightmare of the Wolf,' 'X-Men '97,' and the Netflix original 'Devil May Cry,' and in 2026 it also participated in producing the Paramount+ release 'Avatar Aang: The Last Airbender.' Its key clients are global OTT platforms and studios including Netflix, Warner Bros.

Animation/DC, and Paramount/Nickelodeon, and in 2022 it also entered a cooperation agreement with Marvel Studios Animation and Lucasfilm. Its consolidated subsidiaries are Studio Mir US Inc. (established 2016 in North America), Studio Mir CGI, and Karon Investment, each wholly owned.

In March 2024 the company signed an agreement with Studio N and Toei Animation to jointly plan and develop global animation content, aiming to expand its production pipeline.

Rather than holding its own IP and earning royalties, the company generates revenue mainly on a work-for-hire basis tied to project delivery progress for clients, which tends to create significant quarter-to-quarter revenue volatility.

As of March 24, 2026, CEO Yoo Jae-myung and related parties held a combined 65.6% stake, resulting in concentrated control and a relatively limited free float.

Unlike domestic peers such as Samg Entertainment, Aurora World, and The Pinkfong Company that hold proprietary IP, Studio Mir is differentiated by its heavier reliance on production services commissioned by overseas clients.

04

Earnings analysis

Annual revenue fell for three straight years, from KRW 25.8 billion in 2022 to KRW 23.2 billion in 2023 (down 10.2% year-on-year), KRW 17.6 billion in 2024 (down 24.1%), and KRW 16.4 billion in 2025 (down 6.7%). Among 2022-2025, revenue was highest in 2022 and lowest in 2025.

Operating profit also plunged from KRW 3.34 billion in 2022 to KRW 861 million in 2023 (down 74.2%), before turning into an operating loss of KRW 1.9 billion in 2024 (a swing to loss) and an operating loss of KRW 2.15 billion in 2025 (loss continued and widened), marking two consecutive years of operating losses in 2024-2025.

The operating margin deteriorated steadily from 13.0% in 2022 to 3.7% in 2023, -10.8% in 2024, and -13.1% in 2025.

Net income attributable to controlling shareholders rose from KRW 1.51 billion in 2022 to KRW 2.5 billion in 2023 (up 65.7%), then fell sharply to KRW 109 million in 2024 (down 95.7%), before swinging to a net loss attributable to controlling shareholders of KRW 1.43 billion in 2025.

On a quarterly basis, the operating loss was KRW 965 million in Q2 2025, KRW 749 million in Q3 2025 (loss continued but narrowed), and the company turned to an operating profit of KRW 327 million in Q4 2025 (a swing to profit), only to post an operating loss of KRW 902 million in Q1 2026 (a swing back to loss) followed by an operating loss of KRW 430 million in Q2 2026 (loss continued but narrowed both sequentially and year-on-year), extending the loss streak to two consecutive quarters through Q1-Q2 2026.

The combined net loss attributable to controlling shareholders over the trailing four quarters (Q3 2025 through Q2 2026) totaled KRW 68.73 million. Operating cash flow turned negative at -KRW 1.39 billion in 2025, a reversal from the positive cash flow maintained from 2022 through 2024.

The debt-to-equity ratio declined from 21.0% in 2022 to 10.6% in 2025, keeping financial leverage at a relatively low level.

05

Industry analysis

Studio Mir's revenue is directly linked to the scale of original animation investment by global OTT platforms.

Major streamers such as Netflix, Paramount+, and Max have shifted toward prioritizing profitability in content spending following a slowdown in subscriber growth, a factor that can affect order volumes and schedules for production studios.

Even so, Netflix renewed its cooperation by signing a new content supply agreement in 2026, and Paramount brought Studio Mir onto the production of a new theatrical animated feature set in the Avatar universe.

Korea's animation production industry is split between companies like Samg Entertainment, Aurora World, and The Pinkfong Company that build proprietary IP for licensing and merchandising revenue, and work-for-hire models like Studio Mir's, where a stable client base can secure repeat orders but revenue remains exposed to clients' budget and schedule changes.

Competition comes not only from domestic studios but also from overseas studios in Southeast Asia and Japan with labor cost advantages, making cost management an important variable.

The joint planning and development agreement with Studio N and Toei Animation can be read as an attempt to expand beyond the work-for-hire model into co-developed IP.

Revenue declined for three consecutive years, from KRW 25.8 billion in 2022 to KRW 16.4 billion in 2025, and the operating margin also worsened from 13.0% to -13.1% over the same period.

The company recorded operating losses for two consecutive years from 2024 to 2025, and continued to post losses in both the first and second quarters of 2026. Whether this trend of shrinking top line and profitability together reverses will be key to watch in the next quarterly disclosure.

Volatility of Project-Based Revenue

The structure is such that quarterly revenue fluctuates significantly depending on the timing of finished product deliveries, with revenue dropping 30.6% from KRW 5.11 billion in Q4 2025 to KRW 3.55 billion in Q1 2026, showing large quarter-to-quarter swings.

If client budget cuts or schedule delays occur, they can directly affect performance. In 2025, operating cash flow also turned negative at -KRW 1.39 billion, indicating a burden in terms of cash flow as well.

Limited Float and Absence of Shareholder Returns

As of March 24, 2026, the largest shareholder and related parties held a 65.6% stake, limiting the free float. The dividend yield is 0%, with no shareholder returns being made.

Even amid continued losses, issues related to executive compensation and loans to management have been pointed out as concerns over governance transparency.
