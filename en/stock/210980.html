SK D&D: Losses Persist as Capital Structure Is Reshaped

02

Business structure

SK D&D is a property developer with a vertically integrated structure that keeps development, asset management and operations within one group.

The parent handles development of knowledge industry centers, offices and rental housing, while its wholly owned asset management subsidiary DDI manages REITs and project investment vehicles and earns fees.

Leasing and operations run through DDPS, in which SK D&D holds about 85.1%, covering residential, co-living, office and logistics assets. After the energy business was spun off in March 2024, revenue became concentrated in property development and operations.

Before the spin-off, in 2022, renewable energy and ESS sales totaled KRW 171.0 billion, or 30.4% of revenue. In 2025, property sales income was KRW 215.4 billion, or 48.31% of revenue, while service income of KRW 71 billion and construction income of KRW 61.9 billion lifted their combined share to 29.8%.

Dependence on related parties is high: in 2025, about KRW 16.1 billion of DDI's roughly KRW 17.1 billion in revenue came from related parties, while about KRW 1.9 billion of DDPS's roughly KRW 86.4 billion came from related parties.

According to the company, most development projects proceed as joint developments using REITs or project vehicles.

04

Earnings analysis

Annual revenue went from KRW 563.4 billion in 2022 to KRW 479.7 billion in 2023 (down 14.9% year on year), then rose to KRW 870.9 billion in 2024 (up 81.6%), the largest year in the 2022-2025 period, before falling to KRW 445.8 billion in 2025 (down 48.8%), the smallest year.

The 2024 jump reflected property sales income of about KRW 719.1 billion, which fell 70.05% in 2025 to KRW 215.4 billion.

Operating profit rose from KRW 64.3 billion in 2022 to a peak of KRW 190.4 billion in 2023 (up 195.9%), then fell to KRW 53.7 billion in 2024 (down 71.8%) and KRW 37.8 billion in 2025 (down 29.6%), declining for two consecutive years.

Operating margin moved from 11.4% in 2022 to 39.7% in 2023, 6.2% in 2024 and 8.5% in 2025. One brokerage analysis attributed part of the 2024 decline to losses from disposing of development assets, including the Yeongdeungpo and Ilsan logistics properties.

Net income attributable to controlling shareholders was KRW 76.2 billion in 2022, KRW 103.1 billion in 2023 (up 35.3%), KRW 44.2 billion in 2024 (down 57.1%) and KRW 7.1 billion in 2025 (down 83.9%).

Total net income in 2025 was KRW 5.4 billion, which differs from the controlling-shareholder figure because of non-controlling interests. Operating cash flow moved from -KRW 38.3 billion in 2022 and KRW 188.7 billion in 2023 and KRW 239.7 billion in 2024 to -KRW 265.2 billion in 2025.

Quarterly, operating profit of KRW 8.94 billion in the third quarter of 2025 turned into an operating loss of KRW 11.8 billion in the fourth quarter on revenue of KRW 115 billion (up 12.1% from the prior quarter).

In the first quarter of 2026, revenue was KRW 70.3 billion (down 38.9% from the prior quarter) with an operating loss of KRW 9.35 billion.

The second quarter showed revenue of KRW 84 billion (up 19.5%) and an operating loss of KRW 42.8 billion, and the third quarter showed revenue of KRW 139.7 billion (up 66.3% from the prior quarter and up 36.2% year on year) with an operating loss of KRW 20 billion.

A press report said about KRW 40.5 billion of revenue was cancelled in the first quarter of 2026 after some buyers went bankrupt. Net loss attributable to controlling shareholders widened to KRW 45.9 billion in the third quarter of 2026 from KRW 44.7 billion in the second quarter.

05

Industry analysis

A weak property market is cited as a background to the earnings decline. A financial data site summarized SK D&D's deterioration as a combination of a property downturn and expansion into new businesses.

Weaker property sales have been attributed to slow performance of its ji-san projects, and the market slowdown feeds directly into sales income. The Guro project completed in October 2025 recorded a sales rate above 80%, but move-ins have been delayed.

On the asset management side, the REIT and project-vehicle fee structure is seen as limiting the size of earnings, with fund income depending more on in-house development volume and project performance than on external growth.

On operations, DDPS is expanding its operating asset base through master leasing and the acquisition of Local Stitch, according to a company official. The renewable energy business left the group in the 2024 spin-off, narrowing the company's exposure to property.

Listed peers include SK REIT, Lotte REIT and Macquarie Korea Infrastructure Fund. Compared with rent-focused REITs, SK D&D has a larger share of sales profit, which makes its earnings more volatile.

06

Outlook

An August 2026 press report indicated that strengthening the balance sheet takes priority over a broad business overhaul in the near term. According to the same report, the KRW 136.7 billion rights offering is aimed at securing liquidity, including debt repayment, and the restructuring plan includes asset sales.

After minority shareholders objected to the rights offering, the controlling shareholder said it would not pursue a voluntary delisting for at least one year.

Disclosed backlog stood at KRW 244.9 billion at the end of March 2026, with the Guro project at KRW 130.7 billion and the Gunpo station complex at KRW 63.4 billion, together about 79%.

At the same date, KRW 169.9 billion of joint guarantees on interim loans and KRW 33.2 billion of guarantees on secured loans remained outstanding. Progress in collecting sale proceeds and settling cancelled contracts will affect results in coming quarters.

The gap between the 2026 targets of KRW 2 trillion in revenue and KRW 200 billion in net income set in an earlier mid-term plan and the 2025 disclosed results of KRW 445.8 billion in revenue and KRW 7.1 billion in controlling-shareholder net income is wide. The next checkpoint is the annual operating margin and debt ratio in the 2026 annual report.

08

Bull factors

Backlog and development pipeline

Disclosed backlog stood at KRW 244.9 billion at the end of March 2026. The Guro project accounts for KRW 130.7 billion and the Gunpo station complex for KRW 63.4 billion, together about 79%.

The Guro project was completed in October 2025 and recorded a sales rate above 80%, and the backlog forms the base for future revenue recognition.

Rising share of service and construction income

Service income reached KRW 71 billion in 2025, or 2.71 times the KRW 26.2 billion of the prior year, and construction income rose to KRW 61.9 billion from KRW 33.4 billion. Their share of total revenue increased from 6.8% in 2024 to 29.8% in 2025 and was 31.3% in the first quarter of 2026. A larger share of income less tied to property sales could reduce revenue volatility.

Narrower operating loss and sales recovery

The operating loss narrowed to KRW 20 billion in the third quarter of 2026 from KRW 42.8 billion in the second quarter. Revenue in the same quarter rose to KRW 139.7 billion, up 66.3% from the prior quarter and up 36.2% year on year.

However, the net loss attributable to controlling shareholders widened to KRW 45.9 billion from KRW 44.7 billion, so profit recovery is not yet visible.

09

Bear factors

Consecutive losses and accumulated deficit

The company recorded losses in each of the four quarters from the fourth quarter of 2025 through the third quarter of 2026. Net losses attributable to controlling shareholders totaled KRW 134.7 billion over that period.

The KRW 45.9 billion net loss in the third quarter of 2026 marked a swing from profit in the prior-year quarter. Operating cash flow also turned to -KRW 265.2 billion in 2025.

Lower property sales and contract cancellations

Property sales income fell 70.05% to KRW 215.4 billion in 2025 from KRW 719.1 billion a year earlier. Whether further revenue cancellations occur because of buyer bankruptcies is the next point to check.

Move-ins at the Guro project have been delayed despite a high sales rate, leaving the timing of revenue settlement uncertain.

High debt and capital restructuring burden

The debt-to-equity ratio was 174.2% at the end of 2025, with total liabilities of KRW 1.01 trillion. Total equity was KRW 578.7 billion, of which KRW 570 billion belonged to controlling shareholders.

According to a press report, the KRW 136.7 billion rights offering is aimed at liquidity, including debt repayment, and would dilute existing shareholders if executed.

Risk factors

Liquidity and borrowing

Total liabilities of KRW 1.01 trillion exceed total equity of KRW 578.7 billion. As of the end of March 2026, KRW 33.2 billion of secured-loan guarantees and KRW 169.9 billion of joint guarantees on interim loans remained, and their execution could affect cash flow. Operating cash flow was -KRW 265.2 billion in 2025.

Dependence on related-party transactions

About KRW 16.1 billion of DDI's roughly KRW 17.1 billion in 2025 revenue came from related parties. Fund income depends on in-house development volume and project results. The terms and share of related-party transactions can be traced in the related-party notes of the annual report.

Ownership change and capital raising

After the change in controlling shareholder, minority holders objected to the rights offering, and the new controlling shareholder said it would not pursue a voluntary delisting for at least one year. The burden on shareholders will depend on the terms of the KRW 136.7 billion rights offering. A press report said a major business overhaul would be difficult in the short term.

12

Overall view

SK D&D's operating profit has declined for two consecutive years, in 2024 and 2025, after peaking in 2023, and controlling-shareholder net income fell to KRW 7.1 billion in 2025.

Losses have persisted for four straight quarters from the fourth quarter of 2025 through the third quarter of 2026, with net losses attributable to controlling shareholders totaling KRW 134.7 billion over the last four quarters.

Still, third-quarter 2026 revenue of KRW 139.7 billion rose 36.2% year on year and the operating loss narrowed from the second quarter. Disclosed backlog of KRW 244.9 billion and a rising share of service and construction income are cited as strengths of the business base.

On the other side, the sharp fall in property sales, contract cancellations, a debt-to-equity ratio of 174.2% and operating cash flow of -KRW 265.2 billion remain financial burdens. The change in controlling shareholder and the reported rights offering could mark a turning point in the capital structure.

On valuation, PER cannot be calculated and the price-to-book ratio is below 1x. The next checkpoint is the annual results and debt structure in the 2026 annual report, due by the end of March 2027.
