02

Business structure

I-Robotics operates its core business in polyethylene (PE) resin distribution and industrial PE film manufacturing, producing LDPE, HDPE, LLDPE, and anti-corrosion VCI films supplied to partners including Hyundai Motor and LG Electronics.

The distribution segment sells domestic and imported PE resin in bulk and small lots, while the manufacturing segment produces roll-form films to order, cut and delivered per customer specification.

According to one data provider, as of Q2 2026 revenue the distribution segment accounted for roughly 68%, PE manufacturing about 28%, and the newer auto parts processing business about 4%.

Following a change in controlling shareholder in March 2025, the company added a robot reducer business to its charter and renamed itself from the former 'YOM' to 'I-Robotics' in June, declaring a business overhaul.

The new business adopts a dual-track structure separating a 'fabless' design role, where the company retains proprietary harmonic reducer design technology, from a 'foundry' manufacturing role carried out via domestic partner Haesung Aerorobotics and overseas partner Sling in China.

However, the majority of revenue still comes from PE-related operations, meaning the robotics segment's actual revenue contribution remains at an early stage.

Competitively, the legacy PE film and resin distribution business faces price competition from numerous small and mid-sized rivals, while the new robot reducer segment is a late entrant relative to established listed robotics names such as Rainbow Robotics and Doosan Robotics.

The company states it is strengthening its traditional business's market responsiveness through expanded automotive segment sales and facility efficiency amid economic uncertainty and tariff issues.

04

Earnings analysis

Consolidated revenue for 2025 reached KRW 36.43 billion, a modest increase from KRW 36.15 billion in 2024, while operating profit was only KRW 170 million, an operating margin of 0.5%. Net income attributable to owners swung to a profit of KRW 1.70 billion, reversing a loss of KRW 487 million in 2024.

Over the past four years revenue has been largely stagnant in a narrow band from KRW 37.4 billion (2022) to KRW 35.8 billion (2023) to KRW 36.1 billion (2024) to KRW 36.4 billion (2025), while operating margin fluctuated between 0.4% and 2.6% without breaking out of structurally thin levels.

Operating cash flow was negative at KRW -740 million in 2023 before recovering to KRW 178 million in 2024 and KRW 1.41 billion in 2025. On a quarterly basis, operating profit stayed modestly positive from Q2 through Q4 2025 at KRW 63 million, KRW 78 million, and KRW 257 million respectively.

However, Q1 2026 operating profit turned negative at KRW -80 million while net income remained positive at KRW 446 million.

In Q2 2026, revenue hit its highest level in the recent window at KRW 12.09 billion, yet the operating loss widened to KRW 747 million and net loss to KRW 1.05 billion, showing a clear divergence between top-line growth and profitability.

The debt ratio has declined steadily from 49.7% in 2022 to 29.2% in 2023, 20.3% in 2024, and 15.9% in 2025, reflecting continued balance sheet improvement.

05

Industry analysis

The legacy PE resin distribution and industrial film manufacturing business is sensitive to raw material prices and economic cycles given its commodity material characteristics, and sits within a broader KOSDAQ distribution sector that generally operates on thin margins, consistent with I-Robotics's stagnant profitability.

The newly declared robot reducer market carries strong growth expectations driven by rising humanoid and industrial robot demand, but many listed robotics companies both domestic and overseas are still absorbing operating losses during an investment expansion phase.

Doosan Robotics, for instance, posted Q2 2026 revenue of KRW 17.7 billion alongside an operating loss of KRW 14.4 billion, with revenue growing sharply year-over-year even as losses also widened. This illustrates that the robot components and finished-product market has not yet reached a stage of full monetization.

I-Robotics is attempting to reduce early-stage investment burden through a division-of-labor model focused on design (fabless) capability while outsourcing production to external foundries, but as a relative late entrant it still needs to prove order wins and mass-production stability.

In terms of competitive positioning, established robotics specialists have already secured a certain scale of revenue and customer base, whereas I-Robotics still derives the bulk of its revenue from non-robotics operations, placing it at an early stage of its business transition.

06

Outlook

The company has formalized a 'fabless-foundry' dual-track strategy separating harmonic reducer design from production, planning to use domestic partner Haesung Aerorobotics and China-based Sling as manufacturing bases.

In June 2026 it announced a design engineering contract win from Sling worth $150,000 for a small harmonic reducer model, stating this would be the starting point for expanding orders across 30 to 40 total model variants.

Based on this partnership, the company set a target of supplying one million robot reducer units annually to Tesla, though this is a company-stated aspiration and no confirmed supply contract has been verified.

On the funding side, a KRW 14 billion third-party private placement resolved in August 2025 was delayed by a shareholder-initiated injunction against the share issuance and was ultimately withdrawn in February 2026.

The Korea Exchange subsequently issued a preliminary designation of unfaithful disclosure status on April 24, 2026 due to the disclosure reversal, with the final designation decision due by May 21, 2026.

Separately, some media reports cited the Q1 business report to note that the company allocated KRW 4 billion to a fund aimed at acquiring equity in Haesung Aerorobotics rather than directly to robotics manufacturing equipment, raising questions about the actual pace of production capex.

Regarding the ownership dispute, a lawsuit seeking an injunction against and nullification of the share issuance was reported closed in March 2026 after the plaintiffs withdrew the case.

Bull factors

Early Order Win in New Robot Reducer Business

The company has detailed a fabless-foundry dual-track strategy built on lightweight, high-rigidity reducer technology using silicon nitride bearings.

In June 2026 it secured a $150,000 design engineering order from China's Sling for a small harmonic reducer model, marking the first tangible result of strategy execution. The company stated plans to expand orders across 30 to 40 total model variants building on this win.

Improving Balance Sheet Structure

The debt ratio has steadily declined from 49.7% in 2022 to 15.9% in 2025. Operating cash flow also turned positive in both 2024 and 2025 after a negative reading in 2023. This provides a degree of underlying financial stability supporting funding for new business investment.

2025 Annual Net Income Turned Positive

Net income attributable to owners swung from a loss of KRW 487 million in 2024 to a profit of KRW 1.70 billion in 2025. Net income remained positive in Q1 2026 at KRW 446 million as well. The company attributed this mainly to expanded overseas customer orders and improved cost ratios.

Bear factors

Sharp Profitability Deterioration in Q2 2026

Q2 2026 revenue reached its highest level in the recent window at KRW 12.09 billion, yet the operating loss widened to KRW 747 million and net loss to KRW 1.05 billion. The profit turnaround seen in Q1 2026 reversed within a single quarter. The gap between top-line growth and profitability is widening.

Minimal Actual Revenue Contribution from Robotics

As of Q2 2026 revenue, new business including auto parts processing accounted for only about 4% of the total, with most revenue still coming from PE resin distribution and film manufacturing. The pace of actual revenue transition appears slow relative to the company's rebranding and business overhaul declarations.

Governance Dispute and Disclosure Risk

The dispute between minority shareholders and management, ongoing since 2023, has persisted for more than two years.

A KRW 14 billion rights offering resolved in August 2025 was withdrawn in February 2026 after a court injunction, leading the Korea Exchange to issue a preliminary unfaithful disclosure designation in April 2026 for the disclosure reversal.

Rising litigation costs were cited as a cause of the sharp operating profit decline in 2025, suggesting that cost burdens from the ongoing dispute could recur.

10

Risk factors

Governance & Disclosure

The ownership dispute has not been fully resolved, and the outcome of the unfaithful disclosure designation process could lead to additional penalty points or a trading halt. The absence of a clearly articulated alternative funding plan following the withdrawn rights offering is also a source of uncertainty.

Some media have noted that new business investment has taken the form of equity fund contributions rather than direct manufacturing equipment spending.

New Business Execution Risk

The robot reducer business depends on external foundry partners (Sling, Haesung Aerorobotics) for production, requiring further verification of mass-production stability and quality control.

Orders secured to date are limited to a $150,000 design contract, and targets such as annual supply of one million units to Tesla remain company-stated aspirations with no confirmed contract verified.

As a late entrant, the company still needs to establish its position against established robotics specialists in technology and production competition.

Core Business Margin Pressure

PE resin distribution and film manufacturing have not broken out of structurally low operating margins ranging from 0.4% to 2.6% between 2022 and 2025. The business remains sensitive to external factors such as raw material price volatility, economic uncertainty, and tariff policy.

With new business investment costs rising, the legacy business's thin margins may continue to constrain overall profitability.

12

Overall view

I-Robotics has maintained revenue based on its traditional PE resin distribution and film manufacturing business, but operating margins have remained structurally low at around 1% for several years.

Annual net income turned from loss to profit in 2025, signaling improvement, but Q2 2026 saw both operating and net losses widen sharply again, re-highlighting earnings volatility.

The company has declared a pivot toward robot reducer business through silicon nitride bearing technology and a fabless-foundry strategy, achieving an early milestone with its first design order win in June 2026.

However, the robotics segment's actual revenue contribution remains minimal, and large-scale targets such as Tesla supply should be clearly understood as company-stated aspirations rather than confirmed contracts.

Compounding this are governance and disclosure uncertainties, including a shareholder dispute lasting more than two years, a withdrawn KRW 14 billion rights offering, and a preliminary unfaithful disclosure designation.

Alongside these, positive signals on financial stability exist, including a declining debt ratio and improving operating cash flow, so it is worth monitoring the legacy business's performance trajectory, the pace of new business execution, and the resolution of governance risks in a balanced manner.
