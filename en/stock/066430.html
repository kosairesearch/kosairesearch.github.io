Betting on Robot Reducers, Profitability Still Unproven

02

Business structure

Founded in 1999 and listed on KOSDAQ in 2002 under its former name YOM, I-robotics' traditional core business has been distribution of polyethylene (PE) raw materials and manufacturing of industrial PE films such as LDPE, HDPE, LLDPE and VCI anti-corrosion film, used in home appliance and automotive parts packaging.

The company supplies major partners including Hyundai Motor and LG Electronics on a stable basis. As of second-quarter 2026 revenue, the distribution segment accounts for roughly 68% of sales, PE manufacturing about 28%, and the newly added automotive parts processing business about 4%.

The company has recently expanded into the robot harmonic drive reducer business, declaring a strategic shift toward becoming a robotics specialist.

Its approach is a fabless model that separates design from mass production, concentrating on core tooth-profile design and engineering based on CEO Kim Hyung-mo's proprietary technology while outsourcing production to China's Sling overseas foundry network.

The company has also announced a three-way value chain alliance with domestic parts maker Khan STN and robot component specialist Haesung Aerobotics.

Still, the vast majority of revenue continues to come from the legacy PE business, leaving the robotics operation at an early stage not yet reflected as a distinct, confirmed revenue line.

04

Earnings analysis

Annual revenue fell 4.4% from KRW 37.4 billion in 2022 to KRW 35.8 billion in 2023, then rose for two consecutive years to KRW 36.1 billion in 2024 (up 1.0%) and KRW 36.4 billion in 2025 (up 0.8%) after bottoming in 2023.

Operating profit declined from KRW 219 million in 2022 to KRW 126 million in 2023 (down 42.7%), rebounded sharply to KRW 935 million in 2024 (up 644.0%), then contracted again to KRW 170 million in 2025 (down 81.8%).

The company attributed the 2025 operating profit decline mainly to higher fee expenses tied to litigation from the governance dispute.

Controlling-interest net income rose from KRW 576 million in 2022 to KRW 2.8 billion in 2023 (up 386.3%), swung to a net loss of KRW 487 million in 2024, then turned profitable again at KRW 1.7 billion in 2025, which the company linked to higher discontinued-operation income from the write-off of a provision following the statute-of-limitations expiry on a discontinued aviation business.

On a quarterly basis, revenue bottomed at KRW 8.85 billion in the fourth quarter of 2025 after falling from KRW 10.2 billion in the second quarter and KRW 8.88 billion in the third quarter (down 12.9%), then rose for two straight quarters to KRW 10.2 billion in the first quarter of 2026 (up 15.2%) and KRW 12.1 billion in the second quarter (up 18.5% both quarter on quarter and year on year).

Operating results, however, swung to losses for two consecutive quarters, from an operating loss of KRW 80.21 million in the first quarter of 2026 (a reversal from profit) to a widened operating loss of KRW 747 million in the second quarter (continued loss, reversal from a year-earlier profit), following an operating profit of KRW 257 million in the fourth quarter of 2025.

The first-quarter return to net profit was attributed to higher sales to Chinese partners and proactive cost efficiency measures, but the specific cause of the widened second-quarter operating loss was not separately disclosed, and controlling-interest net income also reversed from a profit of KRW 446 million in the first quarter to a net loss of KRW 1.05 billion in the second quarter, leaving the sum of controlling-interest net income over the last four quarters (third quarter 2025 through second quarter 2026) at just KRW 1.09 billion.

05

Industry analysis

I-robotics' legacy PE raw material distribution and film manufacturing business is a mature, commodity-based sector highly sensitive to raw material price swings and the economic cycle, structurally exposed to low-margin pressure.

By contrast, the newly entered robot reducer (harmonic drive) segment is attracting domestic and international investor attention amid expectations for growing humanoid and collaborative robot demand.

Precision tooth-profile machining and supply-chain yield management for reducers are notoriously difficult, with industry observers repeatedly noting that production volume does not easily translate into profit, which is part of the rationale behind I-robotics' design-production split strategy.

Globally, long-established leaders such as Japan's Harmonic Drive Systems dominate the field, while numerous domestic small and mid-sized firms are also pursuing localization of reducer technology.

I-robotics has chosen to combine its design capability with a Chinese partner's production system rather than building its own manufacturing base, lowering upfront investment but increasing reliance on external partners.

While growth expectations for the robotics industry remain high, the point at which actual mass-production supply contracts translate into meaningful revenue contribution has not yet been confirmed.

06

Outlook

I-robotics announced in June 2026 that it had won a design engineering order from China's Sling for a small, size-8 harmonic drive reducer model with a 100:1 reduction ratio, and stated plans to progressively expand design orders across its full lineup of 30 to 40 models starting from this win.

The company has also set a target of supplying one million robot reducers annually to major customers including Tesla, though this figure represents a company-stated goal rather than a confirmed supply contract.

On the financing side, a KRW 14 billion third-party equity issuance decided in August 2025 was withdrawn on February 25, 2026 after a court injunction tied to the governance dispute was granted, which led to an unfaithful disclosure designation warning on April 24, 2026, with the designation decision deadline disclosed as May 21.

In April 2026, the minority shareholder coalition withdrew its lawsuit seeking to block the share issuance, signaling some easing in relations with management, but no official confirmation has emerged on whether the equity issuance or the planned additional acquisition of Haesung Aerobotics stake will be relaunched.

The company has stated that it completed an investment of roughly KRW 4 billion to 5 billion in compact harmonic drive reducer production facilities using borrowed funds and available cash, suggesting an initial production line can operate without further external financing.

The timing and scale at which the robotics business actually translates into revenue and profit, however, remain points to confirm through future quarterly results and additional order disclosures.

Bull factors

Robot Reducer Value Chain Alliance

I-robotics secured its first fabless reference by winning a design engineering order from China's Sling for a small size-8 harmonic drive reducer. The company stated plans to expand design orders across a full lineup of 30 to 40 models building on this win.

Its three-way alliance with Khan STN and Haesung Aerobotics can be read as an attempt to build a domestic value chain.

Resumed Top-Line Growth

Revenue grew for two consecutive quarters in the first and second quarters of 2026 after bottoming in the fourth quarter of 2025, showing a recovery in top-line scale. Second-quarter revenue reached KRW 12.1 billion, up 18.5% year on year.

The gradual expansion of the automotive parts processing segment's revenue share can also be read as a signal of portfolio diversification.

Improving Balance Sheet Trend

The debt-to-equity ratio declined for four straight years, from 49.7% in 2022 to 15.9% in 2025, improving financial stability. Operating cash flow also turned positive at KRW 1.41 billion in 2025 after being negative at KRW 740 million in 2023.

Despite the funding burden of new business investment, reliance on external borrowing does not appear excessively high.

Bear factors

Two Straight Quarters of Operating Loss in 2026

Operating losses continued for two straight quarters in 2026, from KRW 80.21 million in the first quarter to a widened KRW 747 million in the second quarter. Revenue growth and profitability deterioration are occurring simultaneously, with top-line expansion failing to translate into earnings. Controlling-interest net income also reversed into a net loss of KRW 1.05 billion in the second quarter.

External Skepticism Over the Robotics Business

Media reports have raised doubts about the company emphasizing a robotics theme without sufficiently verified in-house production facilities or proprietary technology. Some commentary has drawn parallels to the company's 2018 pharmaceutical and bio business attempt, which ended without substantive results.

The fact that the large majority of revenue still comes from the legacy PE business underscores how early-stage the new business transition remains.

Governance Dispute and Funding Constraints

A governance dispute between the minority shareholder coalition and management lasting over two years led to the withdrawal of a KRW 14 billion equity issuance, disrupting funding for robotics business investment.

The disclosure reversal also resulted in an unfaithful disclosure designation warning in April 2026, weighing on market confidence. Higher litigation-related fee expenses have previously been cited as a direct cause of declining operating profit.

Risk factors

Governance and Litigation Risk

A governance dispute between the minority shareholder coalition and second-largest shareholder K-Humous, which began in 2023, has continued for over two years, accompanied by legal proceedings such as a lawsuit seeking to nullify the share issuance.

In April 2026, the minority coalition withdrew its injunction lawsuit, but the move was explained as reflecting the issuance's prior withdrawal rather than a genuine reconciliation, making a full resolution difficult to confirm.

Given the gap between shareholding and actual voting power under the company's supermajority voting structure, the possibility of renewed conflict cannot be ruled out.

Disclosure Reliability Risk

The equity issuance decided in August 2025 was withdrawn in February 2026, constituting a disclosure reversal that led to an unfaithful disclosure designation warning on April 24, 2026.

Under KOSDAQ disclosure rules, a penalty score of 8 points or more can result in a one-day trading halt, and a cumulative score of 15 points or more within one year can subject the company to a listing eligibility review. Repeated disclosure reversals or corrections could place a cumulative burden on market confidence.

Business Model Execution Risk

The strategy of separating design from production and relying on a Chinese partner's manufacturing system lowers upfront investment but may limit control over quality, yield, and delivery schedules.

The target of supplying one million units annually to major customers including Tesla is a goal stated by the company rather than a confirmed contract, leaving the actual scale and timing of orders uncertain.

The fact that robotics business revenue is not yet separately identifiable in disclosed financial statements also adds to the uncertainty surrounding investment judgment.

Overall view

I-robotics is attempting a business transformation by adding a new robot reducer growth pillar to its low-margin legacy PE distribution and film business.

Revenue has grown for two consecutive years since bottoming in 2023 and also rose for two consecutive quarters in early 2026, but operating results swung to losses over the same two quarters, highlighting a divergence between top-line growth and profitability.

The robotics business has shown concrete progress such as a design order win from China's Sling and a domestic three-way alliance, but targets such as supplying Tesla remain company-stated goals not yet reflected as confirmed revenue in the financial statements.

A governance dispute lasting more than two years led to the withdrawal of an equity issuance and an unfaithful disclosure designation warning, straining both funding capacity and disclosure credibility.

Financial fundamentals such as a declining debt ratio and positive operating cash flow have been improving, but these also directly affect the company's capacity to fund the new business.

Upcoming quarterly reports and further order- or litigation-related disclosures will be key to tracking the direction of growth, profitability, and governance stability together.
