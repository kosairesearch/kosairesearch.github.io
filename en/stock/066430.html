Robotics Pivot Hopes Amid Three Straight Operating Losses

02

Business structure

I-Robotics was founded in 1999 and listed on KOSDAQ in 2002. Its core operations are distribution of polyethylene (PE) raw materials and manufacturing of industrial PE film.

According to a third-party aggregator (Butler), its sales mix in Q2 2026 was 68% distribution, 28% PE manufacturing and 4% automotive parts processing, so distribution and PE manufacturing together account for 96% of sales.

The company says its combined manufacturing and distribution model captured a windfall when Middle East geopolitical risk disrupted PE film supply. Its PE film unit added overseas customer orders and new trading partners. The new growth driver is ultra-small robot reducers, for which the company says capex is complete.

Based on its high-precision reducer lineup, it is also pursuing robotics and defense businesses. However, disclosed figures do not yet show the revenue scale or profit contribution of the robotics unit. Automotive parts processing is small, so its earnings impact appears limited.

PE is a petrochemical product, so distribution margins and manufacturing costs move together with raw material prices and supply conditions. In competition, PE film depends on raw material sourcing and customer retention, while reducers may hinge on supply relationships with robot makers. At end-2025, total equity was KRW 44.1 billion and total liabilities were KRW 7 billion.

04

Earnings analysis

Annual revenue fell from KRW 37.4 billion in 2022 to KRW 35.8 billion in 2023 (down 4.4% year on year), then rose to KRW 36.1 billion in 2024 (up 1.0%) and KRW 36.4 billion in 2025 (up 0.8%), growing two years in a row from 2024 through 2025. 2022 was the largest year in the period and 2023 the smallest.

Operating profit was KRW 219 million in 2022, KRW 126 million in 2023 (down 42.7%), KRW 935 million in 2024 (up 644.0%) and KRW 170 million in 2025 (down 81.8%), with 2024 the largest year in the period.

The operating margin was 0.6% in 2022, 0.4% in 2023, 2.6% in 2024 and 0.5% in 2025, so apart from 2024 it stayed in the 0.4% to 0.6% range. The company attributed the 2025 operating profit decline to higher fees tied to management-rights litigation in its preliminary results disclosure.

Net income attributable to owners was KRW 576 million in 2022, KRW 2.8 billion in 2023 (up 386.3%), a net loss of KRW 487 million in 2024 (a swing into loss) and KRW 1.7 billion in 2025 (a swing to profit).

In 2025 net profit turned positive even as operating profit fell, and the company cited a rise in discontinued-operations income from writing off provisions for an aviation unit.

Operating cash flow was KRW 693 million in 2022, negative KRW 740 million in 2023, KRW 178 million in 2024 and KRW 1.41 billion in 2025, the largest of the four years.

Quarterly revenue went from KRW 8.88 billion in Q3 2025 and KRW 8.85 billion in Q4 2025 (down 0.3% quarter on quarter) to KRW 10.2 billion in Q1 2026 (up 15.2%) and KRW 12.1 billion in Q2 2026 (up 18.5%), before falling to KRW 8.13 billion in Q3 2026 (down 32.7% quarter on quarter and down 8.5% year on year).

Operating profit was KRW 257 million in Q4 2025, then an operating loss of KRW 80.21 million in Q1 2026, KRW 747 million in Q2 and KRW 750 million in Q3, making three straight loss-making quarters.

The company said operating income improvement and gains below the operating line both contributed to the Q1 2026 net profit. The company had expected faster growth in Q2, but the Q2 operating loss widened.

Net income attributable to owners was KRW 446 million in Q1 2026, then a net loss of KRW 1.05 billion in Q2 and KRW 1.58 billion in Q3, two straight loss quarters, and the net loss over the last four quarters totaled KRW 787 million.

05

Industry analysis

The PE film market is sensitive to raw material supply and geopolitical variables. The company's core argument is that a player with both manufacturing and distribution is relatively better placed on sourcing and pricing when supply is disrupted.

Cost-ratio improvements from process efficiency were also cited as supporting PE film profitability. The robotics sector is a theme where expectations of demand growth weigh heavily on share-price trading, and foreign flows shifting from semiconductors to robotics stocks were reported to have pushed prices up sharply.

On the day of the Q1 2026 results disclosure on May 18, 2026, the share price rose more than 15% in morning trading. Reducers are considered a core component controlling robot movement, and the company promotes its high-precision reducer lineup.

However, the company's reducer market share and revenue scale are not presented in disclosed figures, so its position against competitors is described mainly in terms of differentiation. Higher overseas customer orders and growing sales to Chinese partners were cited as drivers of Q1 2026 results.

This suggests a possible dependence on specific partners and overseas demand, so volatility from external conditions should also be considered. Following the end of the management-rights dispute, the company brought in experts in robotics engineering, finance and law, which can be read as a governance change.

Outlook

The company's second-half plan is to expand its robotics business on the back of secured order volume. A company official said the expansion of robotics operations in the second half and existing orders are expected to create synergies, and that growth was expected to accelerate further in Q2.

The company says capex for ultra-small robot reducers is complete, but the timing of mass production and of revenue conversion has not appeared in disclosures. The faster growth the company anticipated for Q2 showed up as a wider operating loss, leaving a gap between growth expectations and disclosed figures.

Whether sales to Chinese partners, cited as a driver of the Q1 2026 turnaround, continue into the second half is a point to check in future disclosures. The company frames the end of the management-rights dispute and the addition of experts as stabilization steps.

However, after withdrawing a third-party placement in February 2026, it received a pre-designation notice as an unfaithful disclosure filer in April 2026, and a designation decision was scheduled for May 21, 2026. How it follows disclosure obligations will show in later filings.

When the 2026 annual business report is filed, the Q4 results and full-year profit figures will be fixed, and whether the robotics unit's earnings contribution appears in numbers for the first time can be confirmed.

08

Bull factors

Q1 turnaround and overseas orders

Q1 2026 revenue rose 20.1% year on year to KRW 10.2 billion, and net income attributable to owners of KRW 446 million turned positive. The company attributes this to expanding overseas customer orders in its PE film unit and the addition of new trading partners.

An integrated manufacturing and distribution model also benefited from disrupted PE film supply tied to Middle East risk.

Financial capacity

Total equity at end-2025 was KRW 44.1 billion and total liabilities were KRW 7 billion. The debt ratio declined from 49.7% in 2022 to 15.9% in 2025, and operating cash flow was positive at KRW 1.41 billion in 2025. The low debt burden gives room to fund robotics capex and the business shift. Whether this capacity converts into profit recovery remains to be confirmed in later quarterly results.

Robotics preparation and stabilization

The company says it has completed capex for ultra-small robot reducers and plans to expand robotics in the second half, using secured orders. It is also pursuing robotics and defense businesses built on a high-precision reducer lineup, which is a new growth axis. The company says the management-rights dispute has effectively ended, a factor that supports stability.

Bear factors

Three straight quarters of operating losses

Operating losses have run for three quarters since Q1 2026, at KRW 747 million in Q2 and KRW 750 million in Q3. The net loss attributable to owners widened from KRW 1.05 billion in Q2 to KRW 1.58 billion in Q3. The widening loss after the Q1 profit is the central bearish factor. Whether the Q1 turnaround was temporary or sustained is not yet clear from the reported figures.

Q3 revenue decline

Q3 2026 revenue was KRW 8.13 billion, down 32.7% from Q2 and down 8.5% year on year, after Q2 peaked at KRW 12.1 billion. Whether the decline is one-off or structural is a question for the business descriptions in periodic reports.

Because distribution and PE manufacturing make up most sales, raw material supply and changes in trading partners could drive the revenue trend.

Disclosure history and governance strain

The company withdrew a third-party share placement announced in August 2025 in February 2026, and received a pre-designation notice as an unfaithful disclosure filer in April 2026. Litigation-related fees tied to the management-rights dispute appeared in disclosures as a factor lowering 2025 operating profit.

Because the 81.8% drop in 2025 operating profit overlaps with this disclosure history, governance risk is intertwined with earnings assessment.

10

Risk factors

Profitability and persistent losses

Three straight quarters of operating losses mean the sustainability of the turnaround is not yet established. The operating margin was 0.5% in 2025 and, apart from 2024, stayed in the 0.4% to 0.6% range. Robotics may take time to become profitable. Even with capex complete, utilization and order wins must follow before they show up in results.

Disclosure and governance

Withdrawing the August 2025 share placement and the pre-designation notice as an unfaithful disclosure filer weigh on disclosure credibility. The company says the management-rights dispute has effectively ended, but related costs were reflected in 2025 results. The effect of this burden on future financing and market trust will show in later disclosures.

Raw material supply and theme volatility

PE film is exposed to raw material supply instability tied to Middle East geopolitical risk, which can bring both windfalls and losses. The robotics theme has moved share prices sharply on shifts in foreign flows, which can create volatility unrelated to earnings. When these two factors overlap, short-term share price moves and earnings trends can diverge.

Overall view

I-Robotics is building its core around PE film distribution and manufacturing while pursuing robot reducers as a new growth axis.

On a disclosed-results basis, net income attributable to owners turned positive in 2025 at KRW 1.7 billion, but after a Q1 2026 profit, operating losses and net losses continued in Q2 and Q3.

Q3 2026 revenue fell to KRW 8.13 billion, and the net loss attributable to owners over the last four quarters totaled KRW 787 million. Financially, total equity of KRW 44.1 billion and a 15.9% debt ratio at end-2025 indicate a low debt burden.

Completion of robotics capex and a second-half expansion plan are confirmed, but the robotics earnings contribution has not yet appeared in disclosed figures.

On governance and disclosure, the company says the management-rights dispute has effectively ended, while a pre-designation notice as an unfaithful disclosure filer is also on record.

Bullish factors (the Q1 turnaround, financial capacity, completed robotics capex) and bearish factors (three straight quarters of operating losses, falling revenue, disclosure history) coexist, and the next checkpoints are the 2026 full-year preliminary results and the 2026 annual business report.
