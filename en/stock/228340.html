02

Business structure

Tongyang Pile manufactures PHC piles, the core foundation material used in construction work. According to the company profile, it operates three plants in Asan in Chungnam, Iksan in Jeonbuk and Haman in Gyeongnam, and supplies products nationwide from this network.

Its main customers are large construction companies, and apartment construction on soft ground and large-scale plant projects are cited as the main sources of demand. The same profile describes the company as one of the top seven players in the industry and part of the leading group.

Because the business is a large-scale capital-intensive operation with high upfront investment, the entry barrier is high. Its main raw materials are steel wire, cement and aggregate, which the profile says can be procured domestically on a stable basis.

Shipments are affected by housing starts, industrial facility investment and raw material prices. Earnings track construction cycles closely and are sensitive to government real estate policy, apartment sales conditions and social overhead capital investment.

Listed companies in the same construction and building materials sector include Hanil Cement (HANILCEMENTCO), Sampyo Cement (SAMPYO Cement) and IS Dongseo (IS Dongseo).

04

Earnings analysis

Annual revenue moved from KRW 72.6 billion in 2022 to KRW 71.1 billion in 2023 (down 2.1% year on year), KRW 56.6 billion in 2024 (down 20.4%) and KRW 55.9 billion in 2025 (down 1.1%), with 2022 as the largest year.

Operating profit fell from KRW 5.27 billion in 2022 to KRW 4.49 billion in 2023 (down 14.8%), then turned into an operating loss of KRW 4.42 billion in 2024, a swing from profit to loss. The 2025 operating loss of KRW 4.75 billion marked continued losses with a wider deficit.

The operating margin went from 7.3% in 2022 and 6.3% in 2023 to -7.8% in 2024 and -8.5% in 2025. Net profit attributable to shareholders of the parent reached KRW 5.17 billion in 2023 (up 5.3%), then posted a net loss of KRW 5.27 billion in 2024 and KRW 7.27 billion in 2025, two consecutive years of losses.

In its preliminary disclosure at the end of January 2026, the company attributed the deterioration to lower daily concrete demand from a weaker construction market, higher raw material prices and lower selling prices, which raised manufacturing costs.

Operating cash flow fell from KRW 4.63 billion in 2022, KRW 4.31 billion in 2023 and KRW 5.61 billion in 2024 to KRW 2 billion in 2025.

Quarterly, operating profit turned positive in the fourth quarter of 2025 at KRW 463 million after an operating loss of KRW 909 million in the third quarter, then reached KRW 1.01 billion in the first quarter of 2026 (up 117.6% quarter on quarter), KRW 2.34 billion in the second quarter (up 132.6%) and KRW 1.66 billion in the third quarter (down 29.0%).

In the fourth quarter of 2025 operating profit was positive yet the net loss attributable to the parent reached KRW 3.26 billion, which suggests non-operating items weighed heavily.

Net profit attributable to the parent was positive in each quarter of 2026 at KRW 1.39 billion, KRW 2.43 billion and KRW 1.82 billion, and the total for the last four quarters was KRW 2.38 billion.

05

Industry analysis

Demand for PHC piles lags construction orders, because shipments follow housing starts, industrial facility investment and raw material prices, according to a press report that noted a time lag between order growth and pile shipments.

The company said daily concrete demand fell in 2025 as the construction market weakened. A Valueline company profile notes that demand is sensitive to government real estate policy, apartment sales conditions and social overhead capital investment, and that it peaks in the spring and autumn seasons.

On the supply side, the high upfront investment required keeps new entry costly, and the profile describes a market shared by leading players.

On the cost side, steel wire, cement and aggregate prices drive manufacturing costs, and the 2025 deterioration was explained by higher raw material prices combined with lower selling prices. Margin recovery therefore depends on how far selling prices can pass through cost increases even if demand improves.

Large construction companies and cement-related listed firms share the same sector classification, but differences in product mix and customer base limit direct comparison.

The return to quarterly profit in 2026 appears to reflect both revenue recovery and pricing, and whether the industry trend holds will need to be confirmed in upcoming periodic reports.
