02

Business structure

LS THiRA-UTECH, founded in August 2006, is a smart-factory software company that listed on KOSDAQ in 2019. After being brought into the LS Electric group in 2024, it changed its name to LS THiRA-UTECH at a shareholders' meeting in March 2025.

Its core offerings are manufacturing execution system (MES), supply chain management (SCM) and equipment automation software for high-tech customers in batteries and semiconductors. As of June 2026, revenue was split into IT services at 69.02%, merchandise sales at 30.55% and services at 0.43%, according to WiseReport.

Merchandise sales include CAD solutions and equipment such as servos, PLCs and reducers. The robot business covers autonomous mobile robots (AMR) and orthogonal robots, and a robotics subsidiary developed the Tirabot autonomous robot in-house.

Its customers are mainly large domestic manufacturers, and the company is cited as a representative MES provider, a segment the industry counts among its three leading product areas. Company materials say it works with large SI providers such as Samsung SDS and LG CNS to widen its market.

The largest shareholder is LS Electric, with a 31.8% stake as of December 31, 2025. Revenue breakdowns differ across data providers because their classification standards differ.

04

Earnings analysis

Based on regulatory filings, revenue rose from KRW 35.9 billion in 2022 to KRW 54.4 billion in 2023 (up 51.2% year on year), KRW 57.5 billion in 2024 (up 5.7%) and KRW 58.9 billion in 2025 (up 2.6%).

Revenue increased for three consecutive years from 2023 through 2025, with 2025 the largest year and 2022 the smallest in the 2022–2025 span.

Operating losses were KRW 4.27 billion in 2022, KRW 3.85 billion in 2023, KRW 7.91 billion in 2024 and KRW 4.29 billion in 2025, so the company was loss-making at the operating line in all four years. The loss narrowed in 2023, widened in 2024 and narrowed again in 2025.

The operating margin was -11.9% in 2022, -7.1% in 2023, -13.8% in 2024 and -7.3% in 2025, so revenue growth did not steadily reduce the loss rate.

Net loss attributable to controlling shareholders was KRW 4.49 billion in 2022, KRW 5.62 billion in 2023, KRW 2.17 billion in 2024 and KRW 8.32 billion in 2025, the largest of the four years.

The total net loss including non-controlling interests was KRW 11.4 billion in 2025, larger than the controlling-shareholder figure. FnGuide's business summary cites delayed capital spending in downstream industries and a decline in project orders as factors limiting profitability while raising cost burdens.

At end-2025, total equity was KRW 70.2 billion, of which KRW 69.1 billion belonged to controlling shareholders, and total liabilities were KRW 41.3 billion. The debt-to-equity ratio was 100.7% in 2022, 322.4% in 2023, 117.6% in 2024 and 58.9% in 2025.

Operating cash flow was negative in each year, from -KRW 1.19 billion in 2022 to -KRW 2.32 billion, -KRW 4.32 billion and -KRW 6.87 billion in 2025, with the outflow growing every year.

Quarterly revenue went from KRW 13 billion in the third quarter of 2025 to KRW 18.4 billion in the fourth quarter (up 42.4% from the prior quarter), dipped to KRW 14 billion in the first quarter of 2026 (down 24.2%), then rose to KRW 16.8 billion and KRW 18.5 billion in the second and third quarters.

Operating profit moved from -KRW 1.63 billion in the third quarter of 2025 to KRW 113 million in the fourth quarter, then to -KRW 2.6 billion and -KRW 2.65 billion in the first and second quarters of 2026, before turning to KRW 332 million in the third quarter.

Controlling net income was KRW 1.14 billion in the third quarter of 2026, a swing to profit, but the losses across the latest four quarters (fourth quarter of 2025 to third quarter of 2026) still total -KRW 8.42 billion.

Company materials say it is widening its market by working alongside large SI providers such as Samsung SDS and LG CNS.

In competition, MES is a segment the industry counts among its three leading product areas, and the company is presented as a representative provider with large smart-factory references.

Revenue depends on capital spending and project orders from large domestic manufacturers. FnGuide's business summary points to delayed downstream capital spending and fewer project orders as limits on profitability.

In quarters such as the second quarter of 2026, when losses widened, these swings flowed straight into the bottom line.
