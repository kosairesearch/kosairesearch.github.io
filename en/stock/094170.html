Dongwoon Anatech: Auto chips expand as profit recovery remains open

Dongwoon Anatech is widening its business from smartphone OIS driver ICs into automotive chips, but operating profit has declined for two straight years and the company has posted four consecutive quarters of operating losses, so margin recovery is the main open question.

02

Business structure

Dongwoon Anatech is a system semiconductor company founded in 2006 and listed on KOSDAQ in 2015. It operates as a fabless designer that outsources production to foundries, and it has split its outsourced production and test partners to secure procurement stability.

Its main products are OIS ICs for smartphone cameras, HAPTIC ICs and BLDC drivers for automotive electronics, and VCSEL ICs for LiDAR. Revenue comes from domestic and overseas smartphone set makers, module makers and automotive electronics suppliers.

By segment, a 2025 report said OIS driver ICs accounted for more than half of total revenue, and the same report said AF driver IC sales rose 71.2% over the comparable period. In automotive, the CEO said in a January 2026 interview that haptic ICs are supplied across Hyundai Motor's full lineup.

The company has added BDC body control modules and power management ICs for ADAS camera modules. In automotive chips, the market is dominated by large overseas semiconductor makers, and the company has entered on a localization pitch.

The CEO said it is the only domestic company supplying both haptic ICs and BDC solutions. In new businesses it is preparing D-SaLife, a saliva-based diagnostic device, and it registered a US patent for a saliva glucose measurement system in February 2026.

04

Earnings analysis

On an annual basis, revenue rose from KRW 50.1 billion in 2022 to KRW 111.5 billion in 2023 (up 122.4% year on year), then reached KRW 138.3 billion in 2024 (up 24.0%), the largest year in the 2022-2025 period. Revenue was KRW 127.6 billion in 2025, down 7.7%.

Operating results moved from an operating loss of KRW 6.26 billion in 2022 to operating profit of KRW 25.1 billion in 2023, which marked a swing to profit.

Operating profit then fell to KRW 17.5 billion in 2024 (down 30.4%) and to KRW 2.42 billion in 2025 (down 86.2%), declining for two consecutive years from 2024 to 2025. Operating margin went from 22.5% in 2023 to 12.6% in 2024 and 1.9% in 2025.

Profit attributable to controlling shareholders swung from a net loss of KRW 8.41 billion in 2022 to net income of KRW 26.2 billion in 2023, then fell to KRW 24.7 billion in 2024 (down 5.7%) and KRW 1.02 billion in 2025 (down 95.9%).

For 2023 through 2025, net income including non-controlling interests was reported at the same values as profit attributable to controlling shareholders. The company attributed the 2025 decline to lower orders for OIS products and higher costs such as development expenses.

Operating cash flow went from KRW 23.6 billion in 2023 and KRW 34.2 billion in 2024 to -KRW 7.02 billion in 2025. Quarterly, revenue was KRW 30.7 billion in 3Q 2025 with operating profit of KRW 70.53 million, then KRW 36.7 billion in 4Q 2025 with an operating loss of KRW 59.34 million.

In 2026, revenue was KRW 30.8 billion in 1Q with an operating loss of KRW 1.85 billion, KRW 26.1 billion in 2Q with an operating loss of KRW 5.07 billion, and KRW 30 billion in 3Q with an operating loss of KRW 415 million, extending the operating-loss run to four consecutive quarters from 4Q 2025 to 3Q 2026.

The 3Q 2026 operating loss narrowed from the prior quarter but was a swing to loss from a year earlier.

Profit attributable to controlling shareholders was KRW 1.47 billion in 3Q 2025, KRW 3.15 billion in 4Q 2025, KRW 1.02 billion in 1Q 2026 and KRW 19.3 billion in 2Q 2026, then turned to a net loss of KRW 14.6 billion in 3Q 2026.

Because 2Q 2026 showed an operating loss of KRW 5.07 billion alongside controlling-shareholder profit of KRW 19.3 billion, the non-operating items behind it are a separate point to check. The sum of profit attributable to controlling shareholders over the last four quarters (4Q 2025 to 3Q 2026) is KRW 8.91 billion.

In a November 2024 report, the Electronics Times said cumulative revenue through the third quarter of that year was close to the prior full-year total and that the company was approaching its best results since founding.

05

Industry analysis

OIS and AF driver ICs for smartphone cameras are sensitive to smartphone shipment volumes and customers' inventory cycles. A 2025 report cited a supply delay for some products and a prolonged slump in the Chinese smartphone market as causes of weak 3Q 2025 results.

Because sales to major Chinese customers weighed directly on OIS revenue, exposure to customer mix is high. The company's rebound argument rests on wider adoption of eOIS driver ICs.

A 2025 report said eOIS supply had already started at a domestic smartphone maker's mid-range models, with a much larger supply expected in a flagship model slated for the following year.

In automotive electronics, haptic ICs are the main product, and a partnership with a US haptics leader was cited as helping the company enter the market quickly. In September 2025, Eugene Investment & Securities expected single supply for its body integrated control IC because it saw no competitors at home or abroad.

By contrast, the automotive chip market is led by large overseas makers, so competitive intensity is high. On technology, the company is credited with an edge in OIS, but dependence on the Chinese market and supply volatility are cited as limits. Automotive revenue was estimated at around KRW 20 billion in 2025.

Outlook

Through 3Q 2026, revenue was KRW 30 billion and the operating loss was KRW 415 million, which was smaller than the prior quarter's loss. The company announced in a March 26, 2026 disclosure under its shareholder value enhancement plan that it will cancel all treasury shares within 2026.

In the 1Q 2026 quarterly report, treasury shares stood at 695,491 shares, or 3.33% of the total. On automotive, the CEO said new products including BDC modules will be mass-produced and supplied to customers in 2026.

Over the medium term, the start of BDC body control module mass production and wider adoption of haptic IC models were presented as tasks for 2026.

The CEO set a goal of exceeding KRW 100 billion in automotive semiconductor revenue within three years by targeting overseas markets. eOIS adoption in flagship phones was presented as the key driver of revenue contribution after that.

For D-SaLife, a 2025 report said the company planned to pursue Korean regulatory approval in the first half of 2026. Overseas partnerships were described as starting in 2026, with the company aiming to pursue overseas approvals and distribution.

These goals and plans are as stated by the company or press reports, and actual progress will be checked in periodic filings and follow-up disclosures.

Bull factors

Wider automotive product range

Haptic ICs are supplied across Hyundai Motor's full lineup, and BDC and PMIC products have broadened the automotive range. The CEO set a target of KRW 100 billion in automotive chip revenue within three years through overseas markets.

A 2025 report expected single supply for the body integrated control IC, citing no competitors at home or abroad.

Wider eOIS adoption

eOIS driver ICs have started supply to mid-range models of a major domestic smartphone maker, and a 2025 report expected supply to grow significantly in a flagship model launching the following year. The same report described wider flagship adoption as a key inflection point. The report also noted that a technology edge in OIS is generally viewed as intact.

Balance sheet and treasury share cancellation

Total equity was KRW 69.2 billion and total liabilities were KRW 40.7 billion at the end of 2025, with a debt-to-equity ratio of 58.8%. Under a shareholder value enhancement plan disclosed on March 26, 2026, the company plans to cancel all treasury shares within 2026. Treasury shares were 695,491 shares, or 3.33%, as of the 1Q 2026 report.

Bear factors

Reliance on smartphone OIS and weak Chinese customers

OIS driver ICs saw sales fall 39.6% year on year in 3Q 2025 due to weak orders from a major Chinese customer. Supply delays on some products and a prolonged slump in the Chinese smartphone market were cited as causes of weak results in the same period. Customer concentration in the core segment amplifies earnings volatility.

Weaker profitability and consecutive operating losses

Operating margin declined from 22.5% in 2023 to 1.9% in 2025. The company recorded operating losses for four consecutive quarters from 4Q 2025 to 3Q 2026, and in 3Q 2026 it swung to a net loss attributable to controlling shareholders of KRW 14.6 billion.

Even though some quarters narrowed their losses, a return to sustained profitability has not yet appeared in filed results.

Overseas incumbents and small automotive scale

The automotive chip market is dominated by large overseas semiconductor makers, so customer wins will determine how fast the localization strategy scales. Automotive chip revenue was estimated at around KRW 20 billion in 2025, a large gap from the KRW 100 billion target. Mass production timing for new products was also presented as a medium-term task for 2026.

10

Risk factors

Customer and product concentration

High exposure to smartphone components such as OIS and AF driver ICs makes results sensitive to orders from major Chinese customers. The company cited lower OIS orders as a cause of the 2025 decline, which reflects the same pattern. Inventory adjustments or supply delays at customers can shift quarterly revenue.

Gap between quarterly operating and net results

In 2Q 2026 the company posted an operating loss of KRW 5.07 billion alongside profit attributable to controlling shareholders of KRW 19.3 billion, and in 3Q 2026 an operating loss of KRW 415 million came with a net loss of KRW 14.6 billion.

Swings in non-operating items can move net results sharply, so quarterly figures alone make the earnings trend hard to read. Annual confirmed figures and the composition of non-operating items need to be checked together.

Timing of new-business approvals

The timing of the D-SaLife business depends on Korean regulatory approval and clinical schedules. The approval target cited in press reports reflects a 2025 outlook and may differ from the latest progress.

Revenue from the new business would start only after approval, so any delay would push back the timing of earnings recovery.

12

Overall view

Dongwoon Anatech's operating profit has declined for two consecutive years since its 2023 swing to profit, and it has recorded four consecutive quarters of operating losses from 4Q 2025 to 3Q 2026.

Smartphone OIS driver ICs still account for a large share of revenue, and that segment has swung quarter to quarter with orders from major Chinese customers.

Automotive products such as haptic ICs and BDC modules are expanding through supply to Hyundai Motor's lineup and overseas customer development, and management has set a goal of KRW 100 billion in automotive chip revenue within three years.

On the balance sheet, total equity of KRW 69.2 billion and a debt-to-equity ratio of 58.8% at the end of 2025 indicate financial capacity, and the full treasury share cancellation plan remains a shareholder return item.

However, the net loss attributable to controlling shareholders of KRW 14.6 billion in 3Q 2026 and the 1.9% operating margin in 2025 mean that a recovery in profitability is not yet confirmed in filed results.

Strengths include wider eOIS adoption and a broader automotive range, while weaknesses include dependence on smartphones and Chinese customers and the reliability of earnings given large gaps between operating and net results.

The next confirmation points are the operating margin and non-operating items in the 2026 annual report, and the 1Q 2027 quarterly results.
