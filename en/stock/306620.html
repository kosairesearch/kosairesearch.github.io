02

Business structure

GIS makes semiconductor and display equipment and factory automation systems. Its merger effective January 1, 2026 absorbed the display machinery maker GIS and broadened the business. Disclosed business lines are semiconductor equipment, display equipment and automation, and drones.

Press reports citing electronic disclosures said the display equipment and automation segment has exceeded 50 to 60 percent of total sales each quarter. Main semiconductor products include the MLCC Cutter for ceramic capacitor cutting, the Dicing Saw for semiconductor and LED wafers, and the Saw & Sorter.

Dicing Saw sales were KRW 1.2 billion in 2023, KRW 1.6 billion in 2024 and KRW 6.4 billion in the first three quarters of 2025, according to press reports citing disclosures.

Samsung Electro-Mechanics, described as the largest domestic MLCC maker, is a key customer, and an in-line system for mid-to-large inductor production was reported in January 2026 as nearing supply. On the display side, LG Display is a major customer for OLED process automation and inspection equipment.

Kiwoom Securities said the MLCC Cutter offers higher-precision and faster cutting than competitors. The company says it has internalized integrated cutting, inspection and automation technology.

The drone business is expanding into defense, and in July 2026 GIS started development of localized parts and maintenance capabilities for Army and Navy logistics commands. The center of gravity is shifting toward semiconductor equipment.

04

Earnings analysis

Annual revenue rose from KRW 52.9 billion in 2022 to KRW 86.8 billion in 2023, up 64.0%, and reached KRW 104.5 billion in 2024, up 20.4%, the largest year in the 2022 to 2025 period. Revenue fell 27.4% to KRW 75.8 billion in 2025.

Operating profit moved from an operating loss of KRW 3.76 billion in 2022 to operating profit of KRW 2.17 billion in 2023, rose 67.4% to KRW 3.63 billion in 2024, and fell 55.0% to KRW 1.63 billion in 2025.

Operating margin was -7.1% in 2022, 2.5% in 2023, 3.5% in 2024 and 2.2% in 2025, staying in the low single digits.

Net loss attributable to controlling shareholders was KRW 8.02 billion in 2022, KRW 5.12 billion in 2023, KRW 1.63 billion in 2024 and KRW 4.03 billion in 2025, a loss in each of the four years from 2022 through 2025. Losses narrowed in 2023 and 2024 and widened again in 2025.

On a total net income basis including non-controlling interests, GIS earned KRW 261 million in 2024 and posted a net loss of KRW 3.65 billion in 2025. The company cited weaker performance at a subsidiary and a smaller foreign-exchange gain and translation benefit as reasons for the 2025 decline.

By quarter, revenue was KRW 23.1 billion in the third quarter of 2025 and KRW 21.1 billion in the fourth quarter, down 8.8% from the prior quarter. It fell to KRW 13.9 billion in the first quarter of 2026, down 34.2%, with an operating loss of KRW 1.41 billion.

Second-quarter 2026 revenue jumped 144.9% to KRW 34 billion, with operating profit of KRW 2.58 billion. Third-quarter revenue of KRW 24.3 billion was down 28.6% from the prior quarter and up 4.9% from a year earlier. Third-quarter operating profit of KRW 1.27 billion was down 50.9% sequentially and 3.7% year on year.

Net income attributable to controlling shareholders turned to a profit of KRW 2.78 billion in the third quarter, larger than operating profit, which suggests non-operating and tax items contributed.

Over the last four quarters from the fourth quarter of 2025 to the third quarter of 2026, the combined net loss attributable to controlling shareholders was KRW 322 million. At end-2025, total equity was KRW 43.1 billion and total liabilities were KRW 100.4 billion, a debt ratio of 233.2%, up from 139.6% in 2024.

Operating cash flow was negative in each year from 2022 to 2025, at KRW -1.42 billion, KRW -5.55 billion, KRW -498 million and KRW -4.67 billion.

05

Industry analysis

The key downstream demand driver is MLCC demand from AI servers. A March 2026 report said demand growth forecasts for AI servers at major global MLCC makers were raised from 18 percent to more than 30 percent.

Kiwoom Securities analysed that MLCC content per AI server is more than ten times that of conventional servers. Factory utilization and demand for cutting equipment therefore tend to rise. This demand, however, moves with customers' capital expenditure timing.

Orders fell in the first half of 2025 after a major customer cut investment, and as investment rebounded from the second half of 2025, orders carried into 2026 rose sharply. The cycle position reads as a recovery phase following the 2025 trough.

Samsung Electro-Mechanics is reported to be planning a new plant in the Philippines worth roughly KRW 1.2 trillion, with construction running from January 2026 to the second half of 2027 according to industry accounts.

Relative to competitors, the industry view holds that GIS has an integrated cutting, inspection and automation capability that gives it a cost and lead-time edge. On the semiconductor side, the company said rising chip-sector activity has increased supply of its Dicing Saw equipment.

The intensity of competition from global equipment makers and shifts in customer capex plans will determine its market position.

06

Outlook

The company said the improvement in the first half of 2026 became established from the second quarter onward. It said it will proceed without delay on expanding production capacity at its Gumi plant and establishing a Philippine branch, and will deliver secured orders steadily to sustain growth in the second half.

Third-quarter revenue, however, fell 28.6% from the prior quarter and rose only 4.9% year on year, so momentum has slowed after the second-quarter jump. The next checkpoint is how far the order backlog converts into quarterly revenue.

In a January 2026 report, Kiwoom Securities projected 2026 revenue of KRW 163 billion and operating profit of KRW 13.2 billion, and 2027 revenue of KRW 210 billion and operating profit of KRW 17.5 billion.

Compared with full-year 2025 revenue of KRW 75.8 billion and operating profit of KRW 1.63 billion, those projections imply a sharp step-up. Reports said first-half 2026 revenue rose 51.7% year on year, so the forecast depends on a meaningful revenue ramp after the first half.

On new products, an in-line system for mid-to-large inductor production for Samsung Electro-Mechanics was reported in January 2026 as nearing supply. The timing and contract size were not disclosed. On the display side, automation and inspection equipment for LG Display continues.

The drone business is pursuing orders in Korea and abroad, and in July 2026 the Army and Navy localization program entered full development. Revenue scale for defense MRO work has not yet appeared in disclosed figures.

Bull factors

Rising MLCC demand from AI servers

Analysts note that AI servers carry more than ten times the MLCC content of conventional servers. With the largest domestic MLCC maker as a key customer, customer capacity expansion can translate into orders.

Industry accounts that Samsung Electro-Mechanics' Philippine plant will be built from January 2026 to the second half of 2027 are also cited as an equipment order driver.

Profit turnaround during 2026

Operating profit turned positive in the second quarter of 2026 at KRW 2.58 billion and remained positive at KRW 1.27 billion in the third quarter. Net income attributable to controlling shareholders turned to a profit of KRW 2.78 billion in the third quarter.

The company announced that consolidated operating cash flow turned to net inflow in the first half of 2026, even though the debt ratio stood at 233.2% at end-2025.

Merger synergies and business expansion

The January 2026 merger brought semiconductor, display and automation businesses under one company. The company says it is raising synergies across those businesses after the merger.

In July 2026 the drone business reportedly began development of localized Army and Navy parts, and an inductor in-line system for Samsung Electro-Mechanics is nearing supply.

Bear factors

Loss history and financial strain

Net losses attributable to controlling shareholders ran for four consecutive years from 2022 through 2025. The debt ratio rose to 233.2% at end-2025 from 139.6% in 2024, with total liabilities of KRW 100.4 billion exceeding total equity of KRW 43.1 billion. Operating cash flow was KRW -4.67 billion in 2025.

Dependence on customer investment cycles

Orders fell sharply in the first half of 2025 after a major customer at the Gumi site cut investment, and full-year 2025 revenue dropped 27.4% to KRW 75.8 billion. Because MLCC equipment demand rests on AI-related investment, shifts in customer capex plans can produce large swings in orders. The 144.9% revenue jump in the second quarter of 2026 also reflects orders concentrated in a single quarter.

Thin operating margins and reliance on non-operating items

Operating margin stayed in the low single digits after 2023, at 2.5% in 2023, 3.5% in 2024 and 2.2% in 2025. Third-quarter revenue fell 28.6% sequentially and operating profit fell 50.9%.

Because third-quarter net income attributable to controlling shareholders exceeded operating profit, the size of non-operating contributions is the next point to confirm.

10

Risk factors

Customer capex variability

When the investment timing of major customers shifts, orders and revenue swing sharply from quarter to quarter. Revenue declines in the first half of 2025 and the first quarter of 2026 illustrate this.

The 144.9% rise in second-quarter 2026 revenue followed by a 28.6% drop in the third quarter also shows the timing gap in order flows.

Currency and raw material procurement

The company cited a smaller foreign-exchange gain and translation benefit as a factor in the 2025 decline. Raw material costs and valuation differences on foreign-currency assets and liabilities can move quarterly results.

The company said higher inventory in the first quarter of 2026 reflected raw material procurement and production preparation for incoming orders.

New business execution and financial capacity

Drone and defense MRO work and the inductor in-line system are at development or supply-preparation stages, and timing and contract size have not been disclosed. Expanding new businesses could raise cash needs and debt burden together. With the debt ratio at 233.2%, the capacity for further financing depends on whether profitability continues.

Overall view

GIS posted operating profit in the second and third quarters of 2026 on recovering demand for MLCC cutting equipment, and third-quarter net income attributable to controlling shareholders also turned to a profit.

However, net losses attributable to controlling shareholders ran for four consecutive years from 2022 through 2025, and the debt ratio stood at 233.2% at end-2025. Revenue is sensitive to customer investment cycles, with a decline in the first half of 2025 followed by a surge in the second quarter of 2026.

The combined net loss attributable to controlling shareholders over the last four quarters was KRW 322 million, a narrower loss, while third-quarter revenue fell 28.6% from the prior quarter. Strengths include AI-server MLCC demand, Samsung Electro-Mechanics' capacity plans and business expansion after the merger.

Weaknesses include financial strain, customer concentration and thin operating margins. Kiwoom Securities' 2026 forecast dates from a January 2026 report and is distant from later results.

The next checkpoints are whether operating margin and operating cash flow stay positive in the 2026 annual report and the first-quarter 2027 report.
