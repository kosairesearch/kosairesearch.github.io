02

Business structure

e SANG Networks runs exhibition operations alongside a B2B electronic-commerce payment platform.

According to the business breakdown in Jobkorea's company profile, exhibition hosting and operation accounts for 67% of revenue, followed by stainless steel imported goods at 11%, stainless steel plasma-processed products at 11%, e-commerce platform services at 7%, and other items at 3%.

The breakdown carries no reference date, so the current mix may differ. The exhibition business is handled by its subsidiary, and press reports say that subsidiary is pursuing overseas expansion, including operation of the Indian International Convention and Expo Centre (IICC) and exports of its own exhibitions.

The payment side is the B2B e-commerce platform e-MP, which press reports say is used by about 170,000 companies. Jobkorea's company profile says transaction volume passed KRW 10 trillion in 2023 and that the platform has 130,000 member companies.

On April 7, 2026, the Korea Federation of SMEs signed a business agreement with e SANG Networks on SME and small-business financial support, with plans to link mutual-aid system guidance and enrollment within e-MP. The Federation described e SANG Networks as one of the largest e-commerce platform operators in Korea.

Both businesses serve corporate customers, so business-to-business transaction activity and exhibition participation demand appear to drive results together.

04

Earnings analysis

Based on DART periodic reports, annual revenue rose from KRW 70 billion in 2022 to KRW 82.3 billion in 2023 (up 17.6% year on year), KRW 97.8 billion in 2024 (up 18.8%), and KRW 103.8 billion in 2025 (up 6.1%).

Revenue increased for three consecutive years from 2023 to 2025, and 2025 was the largest year of 2022 to 2025. Operating profit went from KRW 13.3 billion in 2022 to KRW 15.1 billion in 2023 (up 13.8%), KRW 20.7 billion in 2024 (up 36.9%), and KRW 22.9 billion in 2025 (up 10.5%).

For the 2024 increase, the company attributed the gain to lower accounting costs at its main subsidiary, which had booked KRW 3.4 billion of merger costs in the prior year, along with higher revenue, according to a report from early 2025.

The operating margin dipped from 19.0% in 2022 to 18.4% in 2023, then rose to 21.2% in 2024 and 22.1% in 2025.

Profit attributable to controlling shareholders fell from KRW 9.89 billion in 2022 to KRW 6.1 billion in 2023 (down 38.3%), then recovered to KRW 11.4 billion in 2024 (up 86.2%) and KRW 14.6 billion in 2025 (up 28.5%).

Net profit including non-controlling interests was KRW 11.9 billion in 2022, KRW 8.31 billion in 2023, KRW 16.6 billion in 2024, and KRW 20.6 billion in 2025.

At the end of 2025, total equity was KRW 171.8 billion, of which KRW 141 billion belonged to controlling shareholders, and total liabilities were KRW 30.1 billion. Operating cash flow grew from KRW 13.6 billion in 2022 to KRW 23.7 billion in 2025.

On a quarterly basis, revenue of KRW 29.5 billion and operating profit of KRW 7.3 billion in Q3 2025 were followed by KRW 27.4 billion and KRW 3.07 billion in Q4 2025, then KRW 24.7 billion and KRW 5.84 billion in Q1 2026, and KRW 28.8 billion and KRW 8.98 billion in Q2 2026.

Q2 2026 operating profit was the largest of the last five quarters. In Q3 2026, revenue of KRW 27.6 billion fell 4.0% from the prior quarter and operating profit of KRW 5.7 billion fell 36.6%. Profit attributable to controlling shareholders for the last four quarters totaled KRW 14.8 billion.

05

Industry analysis

Exhibition operations are the largest revenue block, so results depend heavily on exhibition demand and cooperation with hosting institutions.

In explaining Q1 2026 results, the company said that diversifying exhibitions through cooperation with government agencies and industry associations, together with stronger data-driven marketing, contributed to revenue growth.

It also said that expanding combined online and offline exhibition services improved profitability. Overseas, the company is pursuing IICC operations and exports of its own exhibitions, so overseas exhibition markets could become a new variable beyond domestic demand.

For the payment platform, the backdrop is the SME financing environment. Press reports say that Middle East-related geopolitical risk, high oil prices and exchange rates, and weak domestic demand have raised the bar for bank loans, while mutual-aid fund lending has been expanding and enrollment rising.

In a comparison of six companies in the internet and general retail sector compiled by AlphaSquare as of December 2025, the company ranked first on operating margin, fifth on revenue, and third on operating profit. Revenue scale is therefore in the lower tier while profitability ranks near the top.

The comparison covers only six companies on that site's methodology, so it is not a representative sector benchmark.

06

Outlook

The only dated event confirmed so far is the treasury share cancellation. On November 9, 2026, the board decided to cancel 362,490 treasury shares on November 27, 2026, and the company noted the date could change depending on consultations with the relevant authorities.

Once completed, shares outstanding will fall from 9,835,071 to 9,472,581, and share capital stays unchanged. The company has also said the remaining 240,000 treasury shares will be used for employee compensation.

On shareholder returns, the company announced in May 2025 a basic policy of returning at least 30% of the prior year's separate net profit as cash dividends for at least five years starting in 2025. At the annual general meeting on March 31, 2026, a plan for holding and disposing of treasury shares was approved.

On the business side, diversifying exhibitions and expanding combined online and offline exhibition services were cited as drivers in Q1 2026 results, and the IICC overseas business is in the pursuit stage.

For the payment platform, the company plans to strengthen links to mutual-aid system enrollment based on its April 2026 agreement with the Korea Federation of SMEs. Revenue and operating profit both fell year on year in Q3 2026, so whether exhibition growth can reverse that decline is the next checkpoint. Filings also show that major shareholder purchases on the market continued through mid-September 2026.

Bull factors

Rising margin and stronger cash generation

The operating margin rose from 19.0% in 2022 to 22.1% in 2025, while operating cash flow grew from KRW 13.6 billion to KRW 23.7 billion over the same period. Profit has been converting into cash rather than remaining only on paper.

Operating profit of KRW 5.84 billion in Q1 2026 and KRW 8.98 billion in Q2 2026 both rose well above the KRW 3.07 billion of Q4 2025.

Low debt burden and solid equity base

The debt ratio was 17.5% at the end of 2025, with total liabilities of KRW 30.1 billion. Of total equity of KRW 171.8 billion, KRW 141 billion belonged to controlling shareholders. With a small borrowing burden, the company has room for financial choices as it pursues exhibition investment and overseas expansion.

Treasury share cancellation and a dividend floor

On November 9, 2026, the board resolved to cancel 362,490 treasury shares. Once completed, shares outstanding will fall to 9,472,581. The company set a policy of returning at least 30% of the prior year's separate net profit as cash dividends for at least five years starting in 2025.

Risk factors

Disclosure and trading regulation

A notice in June 2026 said the company had been given a designation-warning for insincere disclosure, and related coverage mentioned the possibility of a trading halt. The original filing dated to November 25, 2024, concerning treasury share purchase orders, and the company changed that disclosure on May 26, 2026.

Coverage of the related suspension said a recurrence within six months would convert it into a formal designation. This disclosure history needs continued monitoring.

Business concentration and overseas expansion

Exhibition hosting and operation accounts for 67% of the business mix, so results are sensitive to event demand and cooperation with hosting institutions. The IICC operation and exhibition exports are reported as in the pursuit stage, and the scale of their effect on results needs to be confirmed in periodic reports. A larger overseas business could bring more local-operation and currency exposure.

Ownership structure and shareholder-return execution

The major shareholder holds 12.24%, and treasury share cancellation reduces shares outstanding. Because the dividend floor is set at 30% of the prior year's separate net profit, a fall in separate profit would also change the dividend amount.

Dividends and treasury share handling are fixed by resolutions at the annual general meeting, so each year's resolution needs to be checked.
