Report overview

A Fast-Charger Maker in Capital Restructuring

SK Signet has returned to positive equity but still posts operating losses, and its KONEX listing is set to end after a share exchange scheduled for January 5, 2027.

  1. 1

    Consolidated equity at end-2025 was KRW 26.9 billion, up from a deficit of KRW -102.8 billion at end-2024 that marked full capital impairment.

  2. 2

    Standalone operating loss for 2025 narrowed 80.8% to KRW 47.12 billion, but the company remained loss-making.

  3. 3

  4. 4

    SK has approved a share exchange and a stake sale to an SK Group affiliate, which would end the KONEX listing and change the controlling shareholder.

  5. 5

02

Business structure

SK Signet designs and sells fast-charging equipment for electric vehicles. The business was spun off in 2016 from the EV charger manufacturing division of Signet System and renamed in 2022. SK acquired a 55.5% stake for KRW 293 billion in 2021, after which the company became an SK Group affiliate.

Its core products are 100kW and 350kW ultra-fast chargers, supported by parallel modular distributed control technology and a 1-to-N multi-charger control system.

About 80% of revenue comes from the United States, a market reported at roughly KRW 7.2 trillion in 2025 and described as one of the world's three largest charger markets.

Reports describe the company as the leading U.S. ultra-fast charger maker by share, while a company profile lists it as the second-largest global player, so rankings differ by source.

In Korea, the company won a Ministry of Environment public fast-charger project for two consecutive years, with the 2025 project worth KRW 20 billion covering 305 units. Eight charger makers bid for that project, with technology weighted at 90% and price at 10%.

Export sales, however, fell from KRW 130.8 billion in 2022 to KRW 18.6 billion in 2024, and in 2023 customers returned chargers worth KRW 31.2 billion over quality shortfalls.

03

Recent trends

Based on disclosed figures, standalone revenue for 2025 was KRW 80.27 billion, up 7.9% from the prior year. Standalone operating loss for the same year narrowed 80.8% to KRW 47.12 billion, and the net loss narrowed 80.0% to KRW 49.40 billion.

On a consolidated basis as aggregated by a third-party data provider, 2025 revenue was KRW 100.73 billion (up 20.19%) with an operating loss of KRW 48.40 billion. The consolidated operating loss had widened to KRW 242.8 billion in 2024 from KRW 149.4 billion in 2023.

Equity was a deficit of KRW -102.8 billion at end-2024, but consolidated assets of KRW 178.9 billion, liabilities of KRW 152.0 billion and equity of KRW 26.9 billion at end-2025 indicate a return to positive equity.

The recovery rests on a KRW 150 billion third-party share issuance in March 2025, of which SK took up KRW 115 billion, and a further KRW 30 billion issuance later that year. Even so, the consolidated debt ratio at end-2025 was about 565%.

Shares closed at KRW 8,130 as of September 14, 2026, with one-year and six-month returns of -18.94% and -11.53%, respectively, and the close was KRW 8,100 on October 30, 2026.

On October 29, 2026, SK's board approved a comprehensive share exchange with SK Signet at KRW 8,200 per share, a 1.25% premium to the reference price of KRW 8,099 and equal to the tender offer price. After the tender offer, SK's stake rose from about 74.9% to 97.6%.

04

Outlook

The share exchange is scheduled for January 5, 2027, and after the exchange is completed, SK Signet is set to be delisted from KONEX through the required procedures. SK's board approved a share purchase agreement for the sale of SK Signet shares, with the sale planned for the first quarter of 2027.

Once the transaction closes, the largest shareholder will change to an SK Group affiliate. A merger in which that affiliate survives and SK Signet is absorbed is under review, but its terms and timing have not been set.

Before the sale, the company plans a KRW 25 billion third-party preferred share issuance to SK, and its terms and payment schedule remain undecided.

Proceeds are planned to fund operations over 2026 and 2027, including North American capacity expansion, a wider charger lineup, R&D for specification upgrades and raw material purchases. In a 2025 interview, the CEO stated that a return to profit was achievable in 2027. The direction of the U.S.

National Electric Vehicle Infrastructure (NEVI) program remains a key variable for results. A April 2025 report said the company had secured 189 of 985 NEVI-eligible sites, but whether those sites would receive subsidies was described as uncertain.

The next checkpoints are the progress of the share exchange, the terms of the preferred issuance, the outcome of the merger review and the execution of U.S. subsidies.

05

Bull factors

U.S. market base and ultra-fast technology

The United States generates about 80% of SK Signet's revenue and is its core market, which was reported at roughly KRW 7.2 trillion in 2025. According to a company profile, its 400kW model can charge a vehicle from 20% to 80% in 15 minutes. Its parallel modular control technology is cited as a differentiator in the U.S. market.

Track record in public charging projects

The company won the Ministry of Environment public fast-charger project for two consecutive years, and the 2025 project is worth KRW 20 billion. Eight manufacturers bid, and technical evaluation carried a 90% weight. Reports said that completing the 2024 project within the contract period was a positive factor in this award.

Parent support and equity recovery

Reports said SK had invested more than KRW 400 billion since its 2021 acquisition, including KRW 115 billion of a KRW 150 billion share issuance in March 2025. As a result, consolidated equity turned positive to KRW 26.9 billion at end-2025. Parent-company funding has been the practical basis for the equity recovery.

06

Bear factors

Persistent losses and slow revenue recovery

Standalone operating loss narrowed to KRW 47.12 billion in 2025, but the company remained unprofitable. Consolidated revenue fell from KRW 162.3 billion in 2022 to KRW 50.7 billion in 2023 and reached only KRW 83.8 billion in 2024. Reports said operating cash flow had been negative for three consecutive years as of 2024.

Quality issues and export decline

In 2023, customers returned chargers worth KRW 31.2 billion, raising provisions for returns and maintenance costs. Fast-charger export sales fell from KRW 130.8 billion in 2022 to KRW 18.6 billion in 2024. Reports said quality problems in the U.S. led to order cancellations and higher maintenance costs.

Financial burden and changing listing status

The consolidated debt ratio at end-2025 was about 565%, indicating a heavy debt burden relative to equity. The current ratio is reported to have fallen from 171.4% in 2023 to 56.7% in 2025.

Once the share exchange is completed, trading on KONEX ends and shareholders move to a cash-payment structure at the exchange price.

07

Risk factors

Macro

A prolonged slowdown in EV demand has continued to weigh on charger orders. About 80% of revenue comes from the U.S., so results are directly exposed to changes in the pace of U.S. EV adoption. A delayed demand recovery could also limit the pace at which losses narrow.

Policy

The execution path of the U.S. NEVI program remains uncertain. An April 2025 report said 189 eligible sites had been secured, but whether subsidies would be received at those sites was described as unclear. Public bid evaluation criteria also change from year to year, which could alter award conditions.

Financial and liquidity

The high debt ratio and low current ratio leave limited room for short-term payment capacity. The company plans further equity issuances to fund operations in 2026 and 2027. Because the terms have not been set, the scale of dilution and financial burden remains undetermined.

08

Overall view

SK Signet has returned to positive consolidated equity at end-2025, but it continues to post operating losses. Its business centers on the U.S. ultra-fast charger market, which generates about 80% of revenue.

Public charger awards and ultra-fast technology are cited as strengths, while quality-related returns and the sharp export decline have slowed the recovery. On the financial side, parent-led capital injections have rebuilt equity, but the debt ratio remains high.

On governance, the tender offer and comprehensive share exchange lead to delisting after the exchange scheduled for January 5, 2027. The next checkpoints on the business side are U.S. subsidy execution, progress on public installations and whether the company returns to profit in 2027. This summary is qualitative and based on disclosures and press reports.
