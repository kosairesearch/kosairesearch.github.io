Report overview

EV Charger Maker on a Delisting-and-Sale Path

After three straight years of operating losses, SK Signet sits in the middle of a tender offer, delisting and sale process led by its parent, SK Inc.

  1. 1

    SK Inc. decided on 2026-07-24 to launch a tender offer at KRW 8,200 per share, with the offer period running to 2026-08-24. The plan then calls for a share exchange, a voluntary delisting and a closing of the sale during the first quarter of 2027.

  2. 2

    Consolidated revenue fell to KRW 100.7 billion in 2025 from KRW 162.3 billion in 2022, and the company fell into full capital impairment at the end of 2024.

  3. 3

    The sale proposal under discussion is sized at up to KRW 200 billion, but the committed portion is the initial payment of KRW 75 billion. The remainder is tied to results in 2028 and 2029.

  4. 4

    Its products are EV chargers from 30kW to 600kW, spanning AC slow charging to DC ultra-fast charging, and they rest on compatibility verification with major automakers. The 400kW integrated ultra-fast model unveiled on 2026-03-23 is already in mass production in the United States.

  5. 5

02

Business structure

SK Signet is a Konex-listed company focused on manufacturing EV chargers and charging infrastructure solutions. Its lineup runs from AC slow chargers to DC fast and ultra-fast units, with output ranging from 30kW to 600kW.

It maintains supply chains in North America, Europe and Asia and markets products verified for compatibility with Nissan, Hyundai-Kia, BMW, Ford and Volkswagen, among others.

SK Inc. acquired the company in 2021 for KRW 293 billion and brought it into the group, and a production base in Texas was completed in June 2023. A June 2024 press report described it as the top player in the North American ultra-fast charger market.

The same report said it ranked first among charger suppliers in the US NEVI subsidy program, ahead of Tesla, with a share of about 14%. In Korea, it won both regions of a public fast-charger program run under the environment ministry.

The competitive field is led by Tesla, ABB, Siemens and ChargePoint, and the company positions its open charging network as a differentiator.

Although classified under electrical equipment, its products and customers differ from those of transformer and switchgear makers such as LS Electric, HD Hyundai Electric and Hyosung Heavy Industries.

03

Recent trends

Consolidated revenue declined from KRW 162.3 billion in 2022 to KRW 100.7 billion in 2025. After posting an operating profit of KRW 3.0 billion in 2022, the company recorded an operating loss of KRW 149.4 billion in 2023 and KRW 242.8 billion in 2024.

The 2025 operating loss narrowed to KRW 48.4 billion, but it was the third straight year of losses, and the company had not established independent cash generation. To fund operations, SK Inc. joined three third-party rights offerings across 2025 and 2026 and injected KRW 215 billion.

Including the acquisition price and capital increases, SK Inc.'s cumulative investment was reported at about KRW 508 billion. Operationally, the company unveiled a 400kW integrated ultra-fast charger on 2026-03-23, which is in production in the US, with a European launch planned for the end of 2026.

When the tender offer was decided on 2026-07-24, SK Inc. held 74.90%, and the offer targeted 25.07% of issued shares excluding treasury stock. After the first tender offer, SK Inc.'s stake rose to about 97%. A second offer at the same KRW 8,200 ran to 2026-10-26 and covered 1,112,823 shares, or 2.77% of issued shares.

The result of the second offer could not be confirmed. As of 17:02 KST on 2026-10-27, the share price is KRW 8,080 (+0.37%), market capitalization is KRW 184.1 billion and trading value is KRW 26,372,970, with the price about 1.5% below the tender price. No verified public figures are available for 2026 quarterly results or for revenue by segment.

04

Outlook

SK Inc. plans to make SK Signet a wholly owned subsidiary through a comprehensive share exchange between September and December 2026 and to complete a voluntary delisting. The target for the final closing of the sale is the first quarter of 2027.

The proposal under discussion pairs an initial payment of KRW 75 billion with up to KRW 125 billion more, depending on whether EBITDA targets are met in 2028 and 2029. Anchor Equity Partners is mentioned as the prospective buyer, and the memorandum of understanding is reported to be non-binding.

Anchor's side owns SK Electlink, which runs more than 5,000 fast chargers, and press commentary expected that this could allow vertical integration from manufacturing to operations.

Operationally, US production of the 400kW model and the planned European launch are the clues to a revenue recovery, but the actual improvement depends on EV demand and charging infrastructure policy. Shares not tendered are to be acquired through a share exchange. Exchange terms, trading suspension and delisting dates should be checked in follow-up filings.

05

Bull factors

Ultra-fast products and a North American base

The 400kW integrated ultra-fast charger is in production in the US, and a European launch is planned for the end of 2026. The company completed a Texas plant in June 2023 and was described as the top North American ultra-fast player in a June 2024 report. Local production gives it a base to serve US demand. A broad 30kW to 600kW lineup also leaves room for operating metrics to improve through tailored supply.

Parent funding and a simpler structure

SK Inc. injected KRW 215 billion through three rights offerings to support working capital. The operating loss narrowed from KRW 242.8 billion in 2024 to KRW 48.4 billion in 2025. Some analyses note that a wholly owned structure and a sale could simplify governance and speed up restructuring decisions.

Press reports also anticipated that combining a charger maker with a charging operator could lead to vertical integration.

A defined exit route for minority holders

The tender price of KRW 8,200 was more than 20% above the one-month volume-weighted average price before the decision. The second tender offer ran at the same price, giving holders another chance to sell.

Because untendered shares are to be settled through a share exchange, the process is defined, which removes part of the uncertainty. This describes the structure as fact and is not a view on the share price.

06

Bear factors

Three years of losses and capital impairment

Operating losses were KRW 149.4 billion in 2023, KRW 242.8 billion in 2024 and KRW 48.4 billion in 2025. Revenue shrank from KRW 162.3 billion in 2022 to KRW 100.7 billion in 2025, and full capital impairment occurred at the end of 2024.

No 2026 quarterly results could be confirmed, so there is little evidence on whether the narrowing of losses continues. Commentary has also pointed out that independent cash generation is hard without outside funding.

Sale terms low relative to capital invested

SK Inc.'s cumulative investment was reported at about KRW 508 billion. By contrast, the initial payment in the sale proposal is KRW 75 billion, and up to KRW 125 billion more depends on EBITDA targets in 2028 and 2029. The KRW 200 billion figure is a ceiling reached only if every condition is met. The structure can be read as reflecting the buyer's view that the business recovery is highly uncertain.

Liquidity constraints from delisting

SK Inc. aims to complete the delisting and the wholly owned subsidiary integration by the fourth quarter of 2026. Trading value on 2026-10-27 was KRW 26,372,970, which indicates thin trading.

The share price of KRW 8,080 is below the KRW 8,200 tender price, so market trading may be less attractive after the tender offer ended. After delisting, access to trading and the amount of disclosed information both decline.

07

Risk factors

Macro

Stalled EV demand has delayed a recovery in the charging infrastructure market and was a backdrop to SK Inc.'s decision to sell. A December 2025 report likewise said the prolonged slowdown had badly worsened the company's operating environment.

The direction of US charging subsidies and trade policy could change the flow of North American orders. Exchange rates and interest rates also affect capital spending and the ordering capacity of charging station operators.

Process and governance

The memorandum of understanding with the prospective buyer is reported to be non-binding. Whether a definitive agreement is signed and its final terms remain unconfirmed. The share exchange, delisting and closing span September 2026 to the first quarter of 2027, so the schedule could slip or change. The result of the second tender offer and the exchange terms should be checked in follow-up filings.

Industry and competition

The global charger market is led by Tesla, ABB, Siemens and ChargePoint, among others. Price and technology competition with larger rivals can make margin recovery harder. The pace of earnings improvement is uncertain through 2028 and 2029, when the earn-out conditions apply.

Revenue by product and region and customer concentration could not be confirmed, so the effect of swings in individual orders is hard to gauge.

08

Overall view

SK Signet is an EV charger maker with a North American production base and a 400kW ultra-fast product, yet it posted operating losses for three straight years amid stalled EV demand. The central issue is less business performance than the restructuring timetable of tender offer, delisting and sale driven by SK Inc.

The tender price was KRW 8,200 per share, and the share price at the time of writing, KRW 8,080, is below it. The initial payment in the sale proposal is KRW 75 billion, and the KRW 200 billion maximum requires meeting performance conditions in 2028 and 2029.

Strengths are the product lineup and the parent's record of funding support, while weaknesses are accumulated losses, capital impairment and liquidity limits from delisting. The non-binding nature of the memorandum and the pending filings remain open uncertainties. This report is a qualitative summary for information only, and any investment judgment is left to the reader.
