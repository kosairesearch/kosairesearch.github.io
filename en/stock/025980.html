Ananti: Shift to Operations as Sales Slump, Losses Remain the Test

Ananti has moved toward resort and hotel operations as condominium sales slumped, and although operating profit returned to the black in the third quarter of 2026, net losses attributable to controlling shareholders have continued for four straight quarters.

  2. 2

  3. 3

  4. 4

  5. 5

02

Business structure

Ananti operates a platform business running hotels, resorts and golf courses, alongside a platform development (sales) business tied to membership recruitment. Of first-quarter 2026 revenue of KRW 46.7 billion, platform operations contributed KRW 41.7 billion (89.3%) and development and sales KRW 5.0 billion (10.7%).

The six platforms in operation are Ananti At Gangnam (111 hotel rooms), Ananti Cove (216 resort rooms and 311 hotel rooms), Villaggio de Ananti (282 resort rooms and 110 hotel rooms), Ananti Cord (76 resort rooms), Ananti Namhae (18-hole golf course and 171 resort rooms), and Ananti Club Jeju (36-hole golf course and 52 resort rooms).

Resort and hotel room sales made up 78.0% of total revenue, sold mainly through the official website and app and overseas and domestic online travel agencies. Golf accounted for a small 5.9% share.

Sales were the segment that drove past growth, but according to press analysis they contracted sharply from 2024, shifting the mix toward operations.

In the first half of 2026, platform operations revenue was KRW 94.2 billion, slightly up from KRW 93.7 billion a year earlier, while development and sales revenue fell from KRW 25.4 billion to KRW 7.7 billion.

At its March 2026 general meeting, the company added wedding services to its business purpose and plans to use idle resort space for ceremonies.

According to Dealsite, platform assets such as hotels, resorts and golf courses totaled KRW 1.2273 trillion, about 78% of total assets of KRW 1.5718 trillion as of the first quarter of 2026.

Competitively, Ananti pairs rooms and golf with condominium sales, unlike casino- or duty-free-led listed peers, but its six-platform footprint is limited in scale.

04

Earnings analysis

Annual revenue rose from KRW 325.3 billion in 2022 to KRW 897.3 billion in 2023, a 175.8% increase, before falling 68.2% to KRW 285.2 billion in 2024 and 9.5% to KRW 258.1 billion in 2025. Among 2022 to 2025, 2023 was the largest year and 2025 the smallest.

Operating profit was KRW 115.2 billion in 2022 and KRW 267 billion in 2023 (up 131.8%), then collapsed to KRW 800 million in 2024 (down 99.7%). In 2025 operating profit recovered to KRW 8.32 billion, up 939.2%, but remained well below the 2022 and 2023 levels.

Operating margin fell from 35.4% in 2022 and 29.8% in 2023 to 0.3% in 2024 and 3.2% in 2025.

Money Today attributed the decline in earnings partly to lower condominium sales, while Dealsite said the slowdown in sales, which had driven past results, left the company unable to fund large development from its own cash generation alone.

Net profit attributable to controlling shareholders was KRW 34 billion in 2022 and KRW 210.4 billion in 2023 (up 519.1%), then turned to a net loss of KRW 30.5 billion in 2024 and widened to a net loss of KRW 39.5 billion in 2025.

Net loss including non-controlling interests was KRW 32.4 billion in 2024 and KRW 38 billion in 2025.

The gap between positive operating profit and net losses in 2025 appears tied to financing costs; according to Dealsite, annual finance costs reached KRW 35.3 billion, well above the KRW 15.2 billion operating profit of the platform operations segment.

On a quarterly basis, third-quarter 2025 revenue was KRW 73.4 billion with operating profit of KRW 9.19 billion and net profit attributable to controlling shareholders of KRW 6.83 billion, after which the fourth quarter of 2025 swung to a net loss of KRW 10.1 billion.

First-quarter 2026 revenue was KRW 46.7 billion (down 28.8% quarter on quarter) with an operating loss of KRW 9.51 billion, and second-quarter 2026 revenue was KRW 55.1 billion (up 18.0%) with an operating loss narrowed to KRW 5.36 billion.

Third-quarter 2026 revenue rose to KRW 74.7 billion (up 35.5% quarter on quarter and up 1.8% year on year), marking two consecutive quarters of growth from the second quarter of 2026, and operating profit turned to KRW 7.78 billion, though down 15.3% year on year.

The net loss attributable to controlling shareholders of KRW 10 billion compared with a net profit of KRW 6.83 billion a year earlier. The sum of net losses attributable to controlling shareholders over the last four quarters, from the fourth quarter of 2025 to the third quarter of 2026, was KRW 46.6 billion.

05

Industry analysis

In its first-quarter 2026 quarterly report, the company said the global tourism market has entered a full recovery phase and that a dualism in consumer values is deepening. Some securities analysts cited the recovery in domestic and overseas tourism and rising demand for premium resorts as positives for Ananti.

However, the operations business relies on domestic leisure demand, and press reports said operating revenue of KRW 1.567 trillion in the period was down 1% year on year, with occupancy and average spending not improving significantly.

Analysts have argued that sales are unlikely to rebound quickly in the short term, because the Jeju Myosanbong and Cheongpyeong projects must move into the operating stage before sales become meaningful.

Compared with listed peers that have casino or duty-free revenue streams, Ananti's results are more exposed to the condominium sales cycle. The company has pointed to growing demand for small, customized and destination weddings as a reason to expand its wedding business.

Whether the combination of room sales, weddings and golf can reduce the volatility of operating revenue will determine where the company stands within the sector. In short, a sector recovery could lift operating revenue, but company-specific factors such as sales and financing costs keep earnings volatility high.

06

Outlook

According to a September 2026 report, the company plans to use idle hotel and resort space for wedding services and to link its online shopping mall with offline stores, aiming to improve operating margin.

In the same report, the company disclosed that first-half 2026 consolidated revenue fell 14.5% to KRW 101.8 billion and the operating loss widened to KRW 14.9 billion. The first-half figures are consistent with the sum of the first and second quarters of 2026.

At the Jeju Myosanbong tourism complex, development of a 36-hole golf course and a 52-room resort is ongoing, and press reports at the time said KRW 32.4 billion had been invested as construction-in-progress. Signing sales contracts there could allow the company to recognize new sales revenue.

According to a July 1, 2026 report, the company pursued a private convertible bond issue of KRW 50 billion to KRW 65 billion and was reported to have set a 0% coupon. The new Cheongpyeong platform, tentatively named Lake de Ananti Cord, is listed in the first-quarter 2026 quarterly report.

No confirmed schedule has been announced for the start of sales, so for now development progress and operating revenue trends are the basis for the outlook. The development and sales segment's operating loss widened to about KRW 10.2 billion in the first half of 2026, which remains a drag until sales resume.

The key operating question is whether expanded wedding services and stronger room-sales channels can actually lift operating margin.

08

Bull factors

Cumulative growth in operating revenue

According to Dealsite, Ananti's operating revenue rose from KRW 120 billion in 2021 to the KRW 216 billion range in 2025. First-half 2026 platform operations revenue of KRW 94.2 billion was also slightly higher than a year earlier, so room and golf revenue has held up despite weak sales. The rooms and golf courses across six platforms are cited as the basis for the bullish case.

Jeju development and possible resumption of sales

Development of a 36-hole golf course and a 52-room resort is ongoing at the Jeju Myosanbong tourism complex, with KRW 32.4 billion reported as construction-in-progress. Expectations are that signing sales contracts could allow new sales revenue to be recognized.

An investment banking source was reported to expect earnings to improve significantly once sales of the Jeju resort begin.

Efforts to improve the cost structure

Third-quarter 2026 operating profit turned positive at KRW 7.78 billion, and revenue has grown for two consecutive quarters from the second quarter of 2026 through the third.

The company plans to reconfigure idle space across six resorts for weddings, and describes the approach as raising utilization of existing assets without building new facilities. Whether the margin improvement shows up in reported figures is the point to watch.

09

Bear factors

Sharp drop in sales and continued losses

Condominium sales have fallen sharply since 2024, and first-half 2026 development and sales revenue of KRW 7.7 billion compared with KRW 25.4 billion a year earlier.

Net losses attributable to controlling shareholders were KRW 30.5 billion in 2024 and KRW 39.5 billion in 2025, and the four-quarter total is KRW 46.6 billion. Third-quarter 2026 also swung to a KRW 10 billion net loss from a profit a year earlier.

Financing costs and debt maturities

According to Dealsite, annual finance costs reached KRW 35.3 billion in 2025. As of the first quarter of 2026, borrowings maturing were concentrated at KRW 38.4 billion in 2026, KRW 263.9 billion in 2027 and KRW 224 billion in 2028, against cash of about KRW 60 billion.

The company says most borrowings are secured loans with two- to three-year terms and that it refinances at maturity.

Reliance on capital raising and dilution concerns

Since 2018 the company has raised KRW 275 billion through convertible bonds, including a KRW 60 billion seventh tranche in 2026. Repeated issuance has been flagged as a source of dilution concern for existing shareholders. Continued reliance on fundraising despite operating profit is the core of the bearish case.

Risk factors

Executive legal risk

Press reports in July 2026 said that earnings gaps combined with legal risks involving executives contributed to a prolonged share price slump. The progress of the case and its effects on earnings and governance should be checked through regulatory filings and news reports. The risk could affect both financing terms and investor confidence.

Development timing and sales start

Analysts have said the Jeju Myosanbong and Cheongpyeong projects are still in development, so a sales rebound or a larger operating revenue base could take time.

Delays in construction or sales contracts could keep the development and sales segment's operating loss of about KRW 10.2 billion in the first half of 2026 in place. Development timing should be judged against dates the company has actually confirmed.

Borrowing terms and dilution

As of the first quarter of 2026, cash of about KRW 60 billion falls well short of KRW 263.9 billion maturing in 2027, so refinancing will be needed. Interest burden and dilution could vary with refinancing rates and the terms of new convertible bonds. A low coupon reduces interest expense, but conversion rights dilute existing shareholders.

12

Overall view

Ananti's revenue fell from KRW 897.3 billion in 2023 to KRW 258.1 billion in 2025, and the company is moving from a condominium-sales model toward a hotel and resort operations model.

Platform operations made up 89.3% of revenue in the first quarter of 2026, and first-half 2026 platform operations revenue of KRW 94.2 billion was slightly higher.

Net losses attributable to controlling shareholders, however, followed KRW 30.5 billion in 2024 and KRW 39.5 billion in 2025 with four straight quarters of losses through the third quarter of 2026, totaling KRW 46.6 billion over the last four quarters.

Operating profit turned to the black at KRW 7.78 billion in the third quarter of 2026, but financing costs and debt maturities remain a drag on net earnings.

Bullish factors include operating revenue from six platforms, the possible resumption of sales at the Jeju Myosanbong project, and the use of idle space for weddings. Bearish factors include the decline in sales, financing costs and dilution concerns from repeated convertible bond issuance.

This report is for information only and does not include a buy or sell opinion. The points to check next are the outcome of refinancing for 2026 maturities, whether Jeju sales contracts begin, and the full-year operating margin in the 2026 annual report.
