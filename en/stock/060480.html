02

Business structure

Kukil Metal rolls and extrudes copper alloys into products such as brass rods, hollow rods, copper balls, copper-alloy balls and bus bars. Founded in 1987, the company listed on KOSDAQ in December 2014.

According to a JobKorea business breakdown, brass and copper products account for about 81% of sales, and other brass, copper-ingot and rolled products for about 19%. Press reports put its share of the domestic brass rod market at about 8%.

Its largest customer is the affiliate Igu Industrial, with sales of about KRW 4.5 billion in 2025, or roughly 13% of total sales. Transactions with other affiliates, including Igu Trading, Igu Ecotech and Igu Engineering, also continue.

The company sells both domestically and overseas and emphasizes overseas customer development, quality and service differentiation, and a quality-focused sales policy.

Raw materials are copper-based metals, and processing margins can swing by quarter because of the timing of raw material price pass-through and inventory valuation. For 2025, the company attributed higher sales to greater shipment volume and higher selling prices tied to rising LME prices.

Competition centers on niche processing rather than large-scale smelting. JobKorea listed 49 employees as of June 30, 2026.

04

Earnings analysis

Annual sales fell from KRW 26.9 billion in 2022 to KRW 19.6 billion in 2023 (down 26.9% year on year), then rose to KRW 31.4 billion in 2024 (up 59.9%) and KRW 36.3 billion in 2025 (up 15.6%). After the 2023 low, sales rose for two consecutive years from 2024 through 2025, and 2025 was the largest year of 2022 to 2025.

The company attributed the 2025 increase to higher shipment volume and selling prices raised in line with the LME rally. Operating profit moved from KRW 385 million in 2022 to an operating loss of KRW 2.19 billion in 2023, an operating loss of KRW 1.7 billion in 2024 and KRW 210 million in 2025.

Operating losses were recorded in each year from 2023 through 2025. The company said the 2025 operating loss reflected continued cost increases for raw and subsidiary materials. The operating margin went from 1.4% in 2022 to -11.2% in 2023, -5.4% in 2024 and -0.6% in 2025.

Net income attributable to controlling shareholders went from KRW 1.01 billion in 2022 to a net loss of KRW 1.22 billion in 2023 and a net loss of KRW 3.35 billion in 2024, before turning to a profit of KRW 97.34 million in 2025. Net income including non-controlling interests for 2025 was the same KRW 97.34 million.

The debt ratio was 8.9% in 2022, 7.9% in 2023, 11.9% in 2024 and 10.2% in 2025. At the end of 2025, total equity was KRW 42.3 billion, all attributable to controlling shareholders, and total liabilities were KRW 4.32 billion.

Operating cash flow went from KRW 5.19 billion in 2022 to negative KRW 2.76 billion in 2023 and negative KRW 3.04 billion in 2024, then turned positive at KRW 4.62 billion in 2025.

Quarterly sales peaked at KRW 12.6 billion in the fourth quarter of 2025, up 70.6% from the prior quarter, and then recorded KRW 9.13 billion, KRW 10.2 billion and KRW 6.82 billion in the first through third quarters of 2026. Third-quarter 2026 sales fell 33.0% from the prior quarter and 7.4% from a year earlier.

Operating profit was KRW 33.18 million in the third quarter of 2026, a swing to profit from a year earlier but down 81.7% from the prior quarter.

Net income attributable to controlling shareholders was KRW 452 million in the fourth quarter of 2025, KRW 254 million in the first quarter of 2026 and KRW 407 million in the second quarter, followed by a net loss of KRW 303 million in the third quarter. Over the last four quarters, the total was KRW 810 million.

05

Industry analysis

The company's own performance commentary describes improving demand from downstream markets. Domestic construction is reviving, led by housing reconstruction, and growth in electronics, semiconductors, telecommunications, shipbuilding and automobiles is lifting demand for copper-based wrought products.

Because selling prices of these products track metal prices, changes in the industry affect the company through both volume and price. The company's share of the domestic brass rod market is about 8%.

Unlike large smelters, it competes in processing through quality and service differentiation and a quality-focused sales policy aimed at niche markets. Many companies in the same steel and metals sector are smelters or large rolling mills, so the difference in scale is wide.

The position of the industry in its cycle is hard to pin down with numbers. The 2025 operating loss, which the company tied to higher raw and subsidiary material costs, shows that cost swings can squeeze margins.

Market commentary has also pointed to a business model centered on affiliates, which leaves related-party trade as a variable in judging the company's standalone competitiveness.

Risk factors

Raw material prices and inventory valuation

Metal processing margins can swing by quarter because of the timing of raw material price pass-through and inventory valuation. The company tied its 2025 operating loss to higher raw and subsidiary material costs.

In a cost-up period, sales can rise while margins shrink, making this the largest variable for profit durability.

Market rules and trading volatility

The administrative-issue designation and the higher market capitalization threshold apply from January 1, 2027. The short-term alert designation follows a market warning system that is triggered when price changes and concentrated account buying are both high. In periods of heavy volatility, trading unrelated to business results can move the price.

Governance and largest shareholder stake

The largest shareholder group's stake rose to 54.24% in an October 20, 2026 disclosure, and the related party Igu Trading continued open-market purchases. As holdings concentrate, the free float available to trade may shrink.

Where affiliate trade is a large share of sales, the link between trade terms and minority shareholder returns needs to be tracked through disclosures.

Overall view

On an annual basis, Kukil Metal showed a return to net profit attributable to controlling shareholders in 2025 alongside a smaller operating loss.

However, it recorded operating losses for three consecutive years starting in 2023, operating profit fell for three consecutive quarters from the first quarter through the third quarter of 2026, and the third quarter swung to a net loss of KRW 303 million.

Positive factors include the narrower loss, total equity of KRW 42.3 billion, positive operating cash flow in 2025, and improving demand for copper-based wrought products.

Negative factors include reliance on affiliate trade, the share of sales from the largest customer, prior administrative-issue and short-term alert designations, and the KRW 30 billion market capitalization threshold from January 1, 2027.

Valuation multiples are hard to interpret given the small earnings base and large price swings. The next checkpoints are the full-year operating margin in the 2026 annual report, first-quarter 2027 operating profit, and whether the market capitalization threshold is met.

Both the supportive and the adverse factors are present, so whether profit holds up quarter by quarter is the central question.
