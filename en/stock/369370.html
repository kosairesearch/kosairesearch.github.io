02

Business structure

Blitzway Entertainment was founded in 2010 and began as a figure maker before shifting its center of gravity toward actor management and video content production, according to the company's investor materials.

The company describes its operations across three areas: artist management, figure production and drama production. A March 2025 shareholder resolution changed the name from Blitzway Studio to Blitzway Entertainment to clarify its content-focused identity.

The music business covers talent discovery, album planning and digital distribution, concerts and fan meetings, and official merchandise and IP licensing, run through the subsidiary Blitzway Music.

Drama production sits with the subsidiary Blitzway Production, whose credits include the tvN series Love Your Enemy (2024) and the KBS 2TV series Going to You at a Speed of 493㎞ (2022).

An external aggregator puts the second-quarter 2026 revenue mix at 65% artist management and 35% figure production; this is not a segment figure from the company's filings. The figure business is built on licensed Hollywood film and animation character IP, which the company cites as a source of global partnerships.

The management roster centers on actors such as Jeong Ji Hoon, Jung Ryeo Won and Chun Woo Hee, and corporate information describes the roster as widened by the 2024 absorption merger with H& Entertainment and the acquisition of KLAP Entertainment.

Economist reported that the company keeps its largest-shareholder position in KLAP and aims for a turnaround in earnings.

04

Earnings analysis

On a filed basis, revenue fell from KRW 19.1 billion in 2022 to KRW 8.94 billion in 2023, down 53.3% year on year, then rose to KRW 36.3 billion in 2024, up 305.7%, the largest year of 2022 to 2025. Revenue was KRW 30.2 billion in 2025, down 16.7% year on year.

The 2024 expansion coincides with the absorption merger with H& Entertainment, so the filings alone cannot separate merger effects from organic growth.

Operating losses were KRW 3.64 billion in 2022, KRW 8.09 billion in 2023 (losses widened), KRW 6.62 billion in 2024 (losses narrowed) and KRW 9.05 billion in 2025 (losses widened), a loss in each of the four years.

Operating margins were -19.0% in 2022, -90.5% in 2023, -18.3% in 2024 and -30.0% in 2025, so margins improved only modestly even when revenue jumped in 2024.

Net losses attributable to controlling shareholders were KRW 3.38 billion in 2022, KRW 9.56 billion in 2023, KRW 7.65 billion in 2024 and KRW 12.9 billion in 2025.

Net income including non-controlling interests, a different measure, was a loss of KRW 3.51 billion in 2022, KRW 10 billion in 2023, KRW 8.03 billion in 2024 and KRW 13.4 billion in 2025.

Quarterly revenue was KRW 7.53 billion in the third quarter of 2025, KRW 8.63 billion in the fourth quarter (up 14.7% quarter on quarter), KRW 7.12 billion in the first quarter of 2026 (down 17.5%), KRW 10.6 billion in the second quarter (up 49.2%) and KRW 12.1 billion in the third quarter (up 14.0% quarter on quarter and up 60.9% year on year).

Operating losses widened from KRW 2.18 billion in the third quarter of 2025 to KRW 2.97 billion in the fourth quarter, then narrowed to KRW 2.38 billion, KRW 1.06 billion and KRW 1 billion through the third quarter of 2026.

Net losses attributable to controlling shareholders over the last four quarters totaled KRW 8.64 billion. The debt ratio rose from 30.4% in 2022 to 55.2%, 69.9% and then 124.5% in 2025, when total equity was KRW 18.1 billion, of which KRW 17 billion belonged to controlling shareholders.

Operating cash flow was negative in all four years, at -KRW 3.37 billion, -KRW 6.4 billion, -KRW 953 million and -KRW 1.27 billion. Because segment profit figures are not in the filed table, the drivers of these changes must be read alongside the shift in business mix and the merger effect.

05

Industry analysis

Blitzway operates in a content sector that combines actor and music management, drama production and figures.

The company's own materials and a corporate profile aggregator both describe how the growth of digital platforms such as OTT, YouTube and social media is widening business opportunities for management agencies, while the global spread of K-content is encouraging overseas activity by Korean artists.

The same sources say the company plans to build global competitiveness through content planning with OTT platforms, drama and variety production, and recruiting new artists.

In competition, large agencies such as HYBE, SM Entertainment, JYP Ent. and YG Entertainment built idol IP from music and then extended into acting, whereas Blitzway's company materials describe building its IP around actor management. Classification affects peer comparisons.

In an external annual comparison against eight companies in the leisure products category, Blitzway ranked seventh in revenue and seventh in operating margin, and its price-to-book ratio sat above the category average.

Demand in the figure market depends on collector interest in premium products, and the company says it releases new products each year. With a smaller management roster than the large agencies, the performance of a single artist has a relatively large effect on revenue.

06

Outlook

The core of the strategy the company announced in January 2026 is a two-track structure that runs actor management and a music label together.

Chairman Bae Seong-ung said the key is to design actors and music in an integrated way from an IP perspective rather than separately, and called 2026 the year when the market would show how well this structure works. The addition of singer and actor Do Kyung-soo was presented as the first example.

Expansion plans include global growth through investment in Blitzway's Japan and US operations and the use of artist IP. Joint content planning with OTT platforms, drama and variety production, and recruiting new artists are also part of the plan.

On results, revenue rose for two consecutive quarters in the second and third quarters of 2026 while operating losses narrowed. Whether this leads to a full-year profit can only be confirmed from the 2026 annual report, which will show full-year operating results.

Economist reported that the company aims for an earnings turnaround while keeping its largest-shareholder position in KLAP. With the debt ratio at 124.5% and negative operating cash flow, how the expansion plans are balanced against financial burden is the next point to watch.

Bull factors

Quarterly revenue up two straight quarters

Second-quarter 2026 revenue was KRW 10.6 billion, up 49.2% from the prior quarter, and third-quarter revenue rose 14.0% further to KRW 12.1 billion, up 60.9% year on year. Over the same period operating losses fell to KRW 1.06 billion and KRW 1 billion, from KRW 2.97 billion in the fourth quarter of 2025. Revenue recovery and narrower losses over two quarters are cited as the bullish case.

Two-track actor and music strategy

In January 2026 the company announced the addition of singer and actor Do Kyung-soo and stressed running actor management and a music label together. Chairman Bae Seong-ung said integrating actors and music from an IP perspective is the key.

The company presents its actor-centered IP build-up as a point of difference from large agencies that extended idol IP into acting.

IP diversified across drama and figures

Drama credits include the tvN series Love Your Enemy (2024) and the KBS 2TV series Going to You at a Speed of 493㎞ (2022). The company's investor materials say the figure business supplies products under formal licenses for Hollywood film and animation characters. The ability to extend one IP across drama, music and figures is cited as a route to multiple revenue streams.

Bear factors

Four straight operating losses, widened in 2025

Operating losses ran for four straight years, from KRW 3.64 billion in 2022 to KRW 9.05 billion in 2025, and widened again in 2025 from 2024. The 2025 operating margin was -30.0% on revenue of KRW 30.2 billion. Quarterly losses narrowed in 2026, but no full-year profit has appeared in the filed results.

Rising debt and operating cash outflows

The debt ratio rose from 30.4% in 2022 to 124.5% in 2025, and total liabilities of KRW 22.5 billion at the end of 2025 exceeded total equity of KRW 18.1 billion. Operating cash flow was negative in every year from 2022 to 2025 and reached -KRW 6.4 billion in 2023. If this continues, the burden of raising additional funds could grow.

Accumulated net losses for controlling shareholders

Net losses attributable to controlling shareholders were KRW 12.9 billion in 2025, and over the last four quarters they totaled KRW 8.64 billion. In the third quarter of 2026 the loss was KRW 992 million, wider than the prior quarter.

Economist reported that weak results and a falling share price reduced the value of major shareholders' stakes well below earlier highs.

Risk factors

Concentration and reliance on talent

Artist management is about 65% of revenue on an external estimate for the second quarter of 2026, so results depend heavily on the work and activity of represented actors and artists. Contract expirations or gaps in activity could cause sharp swings in revenue. The roughly 35% figure business also relies on overseas IP licenses and collector demand.

Execution of expansion plans

The company's expansion plans include investment in Japan and US subsidiaries, joint content planning with OTT platforms, drama and variety production, and recruiting new artists.

These plans involve investment and personnel costs that arise before results, so if revenue recovery does not continue, the pace of narrowing operating losses could slow. The timing of contributions from the overseas subsidiaries and new artists to results has not yet been confirmed. If expansion costs and the timing of results diverge, the burden of executing the plans could rise.

Shareholder structure and share events

A 5-to-1 share consolidation took effect in July 2026, raising the par value from KRW 100 to KRW 500. Non-executive director Do Yong-jun's common shares were reported at 860,375 after the consolidation, with his stake unchanged at 8.63%. As of October 2025, the largest-shareholder group held 47.80% and the free float ratio was 47.72%.

Overall view

Blitzway Entertainment is shifting its center of gravity from figure making toward actor management, drama production and music. On a filed basis, operating losses ran for four straight years from 2022 to 2025, and in 2025 the operating loss was KRW 9.05 billion with an operating margin of -30.0%.

Signs of improvement include two straight quarters of higher revenue in 2026 and operating losses narrowing to KRW 1 billion in the third quarter. On the other side, the debt ratio rose to 124.5% in 2025 and operating cash flow was negative in all four years.

With artist management at about 65% of revenue, the performance of represented talent and financial capacity are the key variables. The next checkpoint is the full-year operating result and cash flow in the 2026 annual report, followed by the Q1 2027 quarterly report to test whether the quarterly trend holds.
