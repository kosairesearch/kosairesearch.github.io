02

Business structure

NanoSilikhan Advanced Materials has its headquarters and core production sites in Pyeongtaek, Gyeonggi Province, and its existing businesses span anti-counterfeiting security materials and labels, nucleic acid extraction bio materials, and functional display materials (The Bell, May 2026).

The main customers of the security business are Middle East governments, and an independent research house described in a November 2025 report that it had supplied more than 1 billion tax stamps since 2022 and was recording annual sales of roughly USD 3 million.

According to a November 2026 report, an African partner company obtained a manufacturing license in Tanzania and plans a label production facility with capacity of up to 3 billion units a year, with the parent supplying core security materials and the local partner handling printing and processing.

However, the security label plant ran at a 42% utilization rate as of end-September 2025, down from 74% in 2023, and bio materials production utilization was reported at 4% (Nate News, November 2025). The silicon anode, the new business, is pitched on a cost edge from skipping the grinding step.

The company said in May 2026 that it had nearly finished building 600-ton-a-year mass production equipment, and in September 2026 an independent research house said NDAs had been signed with two of the three major Korean cell makers.

A November 2026 report described the anode business as at the sample-supply stage, with related revenue of zero. The data center business is presented alongside it as another growth pillar expected to yield stable income over its operating period.

Because the anode cost advantage still rests on company claims, cell maker sample test results will be the first external verification.

04

Earnings analysis

On a DART-filed basis, annual revenue fell from KRW 7.14 billion in 2022 to KRW 6.46 billion in 2023 (down 9.5% year on year) and KRW 5.28 billion in 2024 (down 18.3%), before rebounding to KRW 5.98 billion in 2025 (up 13.2%). Among 2022 to 2025, 2022 was the largest revenue year and 2024 the smallest.

Operating losses went from KRW 3.59 billion in 2022 to KRW 2.48 billion in 2023 (losses continued, narrowing), then widened to KRW 3.44 billion in 2024 and KRW 6.58 billion in 2025, and the company has recorded four consecutive years of operating losses from 2022 through 2025.

Operating margin was -50.3% in 2022, -38.4% in 2023, -65.2% in 2024 and -110.1% in 2025. In 2025 revenue grew while the operating loss widened, meaning the sales recovery did not keep pace with rising costs.

The company attributed the main driver of earnings swings to higher R&D spending for its new secondary battery business (Nate News, February 2026).

Net income attributable to controlling shareholders moved from a net loss of KRW 2.4 billion in 2022 and a net loss of KRW 6.28 billion in 2023 (losses continued, widening) to a net profit of KRW 238 million in 2024 (a swing to profit), then back to a net loss of KRW 5.56 billion in 2025 (a swing back to loss).

Why 2024 showed a net profit despite an operating loss cannot be explained from the filed summary table alone, so the line items should be checked in the annual report.

Net income including non-controlling interests, which is a different figure, was a net loss of KRW 2.4 billion in 2022, a net loss of KRW 6.28 billion in 2023, a net profit of KRW 238 million in 2024, and a net loss of KRW 5.56 billion in 2025.

The debt ratio fell from 177.4% in 2022 to 71.8% in 2024 and 64.0% in 2025, with total equity at KRW 27.4 billion at end-2025, all attributable to controlling shareholders, and total liabilities of KRW 17.6 billion.

Operating cash flow was -KRW 1.1 billion in 2022, -KRW 35.63 million in 2023, -KRW 3.22 billion in 2024 and -KRW 11.6 billion in 2025, with the 2025 outflow the largest of the four years. On a quarterly basis, revenue fell from KRW 2.45 billion in Q3 2025 to KRW 1.36 billion in Q3 2026, a 44.4% year-on-year decline.

The operating loss was smallest in Q2 2026 at KRW 1.04 billion among the last five quarters and widened to KRW 1.93 billion in Q3 2026.

Over the last four quarters (Q4 2025 to Q3 2026), the net loss attributable to controlling shareholders totaled KRW 7.81 billion, and the company has posted losses in five consecutive quarters from Q3 2025 through Q3 2026.

05

Industry analysis

Silicon anodes are viewed as a material with high growth expectations as demand for high energy density batteries rises.

An independent research house said in a September 2026 report that the silicon anode market is expected to grow quickly on the spread of electric vehicles and rising demand for high energy density batteries.

The company said in December 2025 that securing battery cost competitiveness had become the industry's top priority amid a temporary slowdown in EV demand.

On technology, another independent research house said in a November 2025 report that the company's process showed better energy density, initial Coulombic efficiency and carbon content than existing products.

However, a securities industry source told a November 2025 news outlet that it was hard to believe the 600-ton capacity claim when compared with the investment scale of other anode makers.

The same report noted that R&D headcount was 16 as of end-September 2025, up from 10 at end-2024, a level that remains small for a commercial-scale ramp. On competition, the company cites its grinding-free process as a cost edge and says it is in NDA-stage talks with major domestic cell makers.

Compared with larger listed chemical companies in the sector, the company is small, so the timing of when anode sales enter reported earnings is a key variable. The same research house described the security business as having a stable structure of long-term supply to governments.

Outlook

The company said in December 2025 that it had completed commercial operation preparations and was in sample testing talks with three major Korean battery makers.

In a May 2026 report, the company said it aimed to sign supply contracts with major cell makers within 2026, and as of November 2026 reports showed no anode-related results, so the end of 2026 is the nearest checkpoint for that goal.

An independent research house said in its September 2026 report that it expects anode revenue to begin in 2027 once orders are secured. The same report expected the data center business to contribute to earnings in earnest from 2026.

Views on capacity expansion differ: in November 2025 the company said further capacity would be added in stages once financing conditions such as capital increases are met, while another research house said in November 2025 that the plant could expand to up to 1,800 tons a year using idle space.

On funding, a KRW 5.5 billion private convertible bond was issued in March 2026, with KRW 1.8 billion earmarked for operating funds and KRW 3.7 billion for debt repayment. The conversion period runs from March 31, 2027 to February 28, 2029, with a 100% conversion ratio. The report does not specify when the Tanzanian production facility will start operating.

Bull factors

Cost edge claim in anode

The company argues that skipping the grinding step lowers costs, and said in May 2026 that it had nearly completed 600-ton-a-year production equipment. In September 2026 an independent research house said NDAs had been signed with two of the three major Korean cell makers and that the path from MOUs to orders matters. The bull case is that converting sample tests into supply contracts could change the earnings profile.

Overseas expansion of security business

An independent research house said in November 2025 that tax stamps have been supplied to Middle East governments since 2022, totaling more than 1 billion units, with expansion to South Asia, Africa and North America under way.

A November 2026 report said a Tanzanian manufacturing license and a production site have been secured. If the targeted capacity of up to 3 billion units a year is reached, the security business could grow in scale.

Data center as a new growth pillar

An independent research house explained that the data center can generate stable income over its operating period and offers additional profit potential at final sale. The same report expected the business to contribute to results from 2026.

Alongside the existing security business and the anode project, the addition of a third business line is cited as a bull factor.

Bear factors

Falling sales and widening losses

Q3 2026 revenue fell 44.4% year on year to KRW 1.36 billion, and the operating loss widened to KRW 1.93 billion. The company has posted losses in five consecutive quarters since Q3 2025, and the net loss attributable to controlling shareholders over the last four quarters totaled KRW 7.81 billion.

The security plant's utilization fell to 42% as of end-September 2025, which is cited as a constraint on recovery of the core business.

Questions over anode progress

A November 2026 report by a Korean news outlet said the anode business is recorded at the sample stage, is absent from the production capacity disclosure, and carries zero related revenue. The same report put total machinery additions for the first half of 2026 at KRW 35 million.

The company maintains that its equipment build-out is complete, so the gap between equipment and revenue remains an open issue.

Related-party cash flows and funding burden

A Korean news outlet reported that in the second half of 2025 the company paid KRW 1.95 billion to a related unlisted company, labeled as research fees and purchase payments, and that the two companies share personnel ties.

A KRW 5.5 billion convertible bond was issued in March 2026, and operating cash flow in 2025 was -KRW 11.6 billion. Continued fundraising also brings dilution concerns as the share count rises.

Risk factors

Commercialization risk

If mass production and cell maker supply contracts for the anode are delayed, revenue from the new business could slip beyond 2027. The cost advantage has yet to be verified through cell maker sample testing. The overseas security business also needs time between license approval and production start-up.

Funding and dilution risk

The KRW 5.5 billion convertible bond issued in March 2026 can be converted from March 31, 2027, which adds to potential share count growth. Operating cash outflow in 2025 was KRW 11.6 billion. If further external funding is needed, dilution and financial burden could both increase.

Disclosure credibility and market assessment risk

Reports have continued to highlight gaps between business announcements and actual investment scale, making the market's trust in disclosures and investor relations content a live issue.

A November 2025 report cited 12 accumulated penalty points and noted that 15 or more points would trigger a substantive review for listing eligibility. Verifiability of company announcements is the starting point for managing this risk.

Overall view

NanoSilikhan Advanced Materials has posted losses for five consecutive quarters through Q3 2026, and its 2025 operating loss widened to KRW 6.58 billion. Revenue rebounded to KRW 5.98 billion in 2025 but fell to KRW 1.36 billion in Q3 2026.

The next checkpoints are whether a cell maker supply contract is signed by the end of 2026, the anode sales and equipment figures in the 2026 annual report, and the start of conversion rights from March 31, 2027.
