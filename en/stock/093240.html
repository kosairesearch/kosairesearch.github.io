02

Business structure

Hyungji Elite, a Fashion Group Hyungji affiliate, runs its school-uniform business centered on 'ELITE,' South Korea's first school-uniform brand launched in 1969, alongside a B2B workwear/uniform business and a sports merchandising business.

Hyungji Elite's school-uniform brand is considered the market leader among the four major uniform makers, and the company has broadened its lineup to include gym wear, casual wear, bags and belts.

Entered in 2020, the sports merchandising business designs, produces and sells goods and uniforms under licenses from professional baseball clubs such as Lotte Giants, Hanwha Eagles and SSG Landers, and football clubs including FC Seoul, FC Barcelona and Real Madrid.

Its workwear brand 'WillBe Lab' supplies corporate uniforms and has grown in recent years to a scale that now exceeds the uniform business.

According to one report, sports accounted for the largest share of total revenue (KRW 88.1 billion) at 39.1% in the second half of fiscal 2025, followed by B2B workwear at 32.16% and the ELITE uniform business at 28.71%.

Overseas, the company entered China's premium uniform market through Shanghai ELITE, a joint venture formed in 2016 with BONO, an affiliate of China's Bosideng Group, and became the first Korean uniform maker to supply uniforms to Japan's Kyoto International Senior High School and Osaka Kongo Gakuen.

Its competitive positioning differs from export-oriented OEM textile firms such as Youngone or Hansae, or diversified fashion houses such as LF or Handsome, as it operates in the niche of school uniforms, institutional apparel and sports licensing goods.

CEO and Vice Chairman Choi Jun-ho has led overall management since 2021, pursuing a '3S' strategy built around Students, Sports and Safety (workwear).

04

Earnings analysis

Annual revenue fell 31.2% from KRW 137.3 billion in 2022 to KRW 94.5 billion in 2023, then rose for two consecutive years to KRW 132.7 billion in 2024 (up 40.5%) and KRW 166.7 billion in 2025 (up 25.5%), moving past the 2023 trough. Among 2022-2025, revenue was highest in 2025 and lowest in 2023.

Operating profit fell from KRW 3.94 billion in 2022 to KRW 2.49 billion in 2023, then surged to KRW 7.01 billion in 2024 (up 182.0%) before declining 5.2% to KRW 6.64 billion in 2025.

Operating profit was highest in 2024 and lowest in 2023, while the operating margin rose from 2.9% in 2022 to 5.3% in 2024 before easing to 4.0% in 2025.

Controlling-shareholder net profit fell from KRW 8.09 billion in 2022 to KRW 2 billion in 2023 (down 75.3%), recovered to KRW 6.37 billion in 2024 (up 218.9%), then swung to a controlling-shareholder net loss of KRW 1.29 billion in 2025.

By quarter, first-quarter 2025 revenue of KRW 44.6 billion and operating profit of KRW 7.46 billion were solid, but an operating loss of KRW 178 million in the second quarter was followed by a much deeper slump in the third quarter, when revenue fell to KRW 21.1 billion, the operating loss widened to KRW 7.04 billion and the controlling-shareholder net loss reached KRW 8.12 billion, dragging down the full year.

The fourth quarter saw revenue jump to KRW 57.5 billion, up 172.6% quarter-on-quarter and 58.5% year-on-year, with operating profit turning positive at KRW 6.39 billion, though controlling-shareholder net profit of only KRW 545 million was down 88.0% from a year earlier.

The sum of controlling-shareholder net losses across the last four quarters (Q1-Q4 2025) matched the annual figure of KRW 1.29 billion, while operating cash flow deteriorated sharply from KRW 3.15 billion in 2024 to negative KRW 22.9 billion in 2025, highlighting a cash-generation strain distinct from the profitability swings.

05

Industry analysis

South Korea's school-uniform market faces both a structural headwind from a shrinking school-age population and intensifying government pressure on pricing.

Uniform prices are managed under a ceiling system set by the Ministry of Education and 17 regional education offices, and the most recent ceiling of KRW 344,530 was frozen after rising 2.6% the prior year.

President Lee Jae-myung has pointed to high uniform costs and ordered a review of distribution practices, raising the prospect that tighter price regulation could pressure uniform makers' profitability.

Hyungji Elite is considered the market leader among Korea's four major uniform brands, but judging growth potential in uniforms alone to be limited, it has been shifting weight toward non-uniform businesses such as sports merchandising and workwear.

The professional sports licensed-goods market is growing on the back of expanding club rosters and fandom-driven consumption, and Hyungji Elite has broadened its licensing portfolio from baseball and football into professional basketball with Busan KCC Egis.

Compared with other listed textile and fashion names, its business model differs from export-oriented OEM producers with overseas manufacturing bases such as Youngone or Hansae, or diversified fashion houses such as LF or Handsome, as it competes in the comparatively narrow niche of school uniforms, institutional apparel and licensed sports goods.

06

Outlook

In materials first disclosed as preliminary on August 24, 2026 and confirmed via an unqualified audit opinion on September 18, 2026, Hyungji Elite reported consolidated revenue of KRW 177.8 billion, operating profit of KRW 8.4 billion and net income of KRW 7.4 billion (turning positive) for the fiscal year from July 2025 to June 2026 — a period that falls after the disclosed results analyzed in this report (through 2025).

The company attributed the swing to profit to the resolution of a one-off accounting valuation loss that had weighed on the prior year.

On the business side, it said it revamped the uniform brand identity and widened its gym-wear, casual-wear and accessories lineup, while opening its first dedicated sports-merchandising retail store at Lotte World Mall in Seoul.

For logistics efficiency, it signed a memorandum of understanding with smart-tag firm Vionteq on October 15, 2026 to introduce an RFID system, to be applied from next season's products to cut inbound/outbound inspection and stocktaking time and accumulate demand-forecasting data.

On listing eligibility, shareholders approved a 5-for-1 share consolidation on August 25, 2026 that raises par value from KRW 500 to KRW 2,500; trading was halted from September 23 to October 19, 2026, with new shares set to list on October 20.

While the consolidation may resolve the penny-stock criterion, a separate market-capitalization shortfall that also triggered administrative-issue status remains, meaning both earnings improvement and higher market valuation would be needed to fully clear the designation.

The company has also said it aims for the sports segment to reach KRW 100 billion in annual revenue as it expands its licensing portfolio from baseball and football into basketball.

Bull factors

Diversified Business Portfolio

Hyungji Elite has shifted revenue weight from its single uniform business toward sports merchandising and workwear, helping drive two straight years of revenue growth after the 2023 trough. It has broadened its licensing beyond baseball and football into basketball, widening growth drivers outside the uniform business. This can be read as an attempt to partly offset the structural constraint of a shrinking school-age population.

Overseas Market Expansion

Following its entry into China's premium uniform market through joint venture Shanghai ELITE, the company became the first Korean uniform maker to sign supply contracts with Kyoto International Senior High School and Osaka Kongo Gakuen in Japan.

With the domestic market stagnant, overseas demand is being positioned as an additional growth path. The scale and profitability of overseas sales, however, still require further confirmation through disclosed figures.

Reported Turnaround for FY2026

According to materials disclosed as preliminary in August 2026 and confirmed by the September audit report, revenue and operating profit for the fiscal year from July 2025 to June 2026 grew from the prior year and net income turned positive.

The company explained the improvement in net income was mainly driven by the resolution of a prior-year one-off accounting valuation loss. This period, however, falls outside the disclosed results analyzed in this report and warrants separate confirmation.

Bear factors

Swing to a 2025 Annual Loss

Despite 25.5% revenue growth, the company swung to a controlling-shareholder net loss of KRW 1.29 billion in 2025. A KRW 7.04 billion operating loss and an KRW 8.12 billion controlling-shareholder net loss in the third quarter were the main drags on the full year.

Quarter-to-quarter volatility was substantial, showing that top-line growth did not translate directly into earnings growth.

Administrative-Issue Designation and Listing-Eligibility Concerns

Hyungji Elite was designated an administrative-issue stock in August 2026 for its share price falling below 20% of par value, with a market-cap shortfall added as a further reason in September.

A 5-for-1 share consolidation addresses the penny-stock criterion, but the market-cap shortfall remains a separate, unresolved issue. If the requirements are not restored within the prescribed window after designation, grounds for delisting could arise.

Balance-Sheet Strain and Weaker Cash Flow

The debt-to-equity ratio rose steadily from 61.2% in 2022 to 107.5% in 2025, while operating cash flow deteriorated sharply from KRW 3.15 billion in 2024 to negative KRW 22.9 billion in 2025. A KRW 21.3 billion rights offering in 2025 expanded the total share count by close to 60%, diluting existing shareholders.

Because Fashion Group Hyungji, Hyungji Global and Hyungji I&C hold cross-shareholdings in one another, capital changes at one affiliate can spill over into the others' financial statements through equity-method gains or losses.

Risk factors

Listing Eligibility

The stock carries overlapping administrative-issue designations from an August 2026 penny-stock violation and a September market-cap shortfall. Under the rule, failing to continuously restore the price and market-cap requirements within a set number of trading days after designation triggers delisting grounds.

A 5-for-1 share consolidation addresses the price requirement, but the market-cap requirement must still be met separately.

Financial and Liquidity

The debt-to-equity ratio widened from 61.2% in 2022 to 107.5% in 2025, and operating cash flow deteriorated to negative KRW 22.9 billion in 2025. The uniform business's need to pre-fund fabric purchases also adds working-capital strain to liquidity management. Further fundraising, if required, could again dilute existing shareholders.

Policy and Industry Conditions

Structural demand softness from a shrinking school-age population persists alongside the uniform price ceiling system and stepped-up government scrutiny of pricing. If the uniform price ceiling is frozen or pressured lower, it could directly affect profitability in the uniform segment.

IP licensing fees and sponsorship costs tied to the expanding sports-merchandising business are also a source of earnings volatility.

Overall view

Hyungji Elite has expanded from a leading position in the narrow school-uniform niche into sports merchandising, workwear and overseas uniform supply, recording two consecutive years of revenue growth after the 2023 trough.

In 2025, however, a steep third-quarter operating loss dragged down the full year, pushing the company to a controlling-shareholder net loss and a sharply negative operating cash flow.

Compounding this, the stock was placed under overlapping administrative-issue designations in 2026 for falling below penny-stock and market-cap thresholds, prompting a 5-for-1 share consolidation, with a separate market-cap requirement still to be met even after new shares list on October 20.

Materials released in August-September 2026 stated that revenue, operating profit and net income all improved and turned positive for the following fiscal year, but this falls outside the disclosed results analyzed in this report and requires reconfirmation through formal future filings.

Investors may wish to track the progress of business diversification, whether the administrative-issue designation is cleared, and the figures confirmed in the next quarterly report.
