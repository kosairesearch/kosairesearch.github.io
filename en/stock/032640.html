Report overview

AI Data Centers Reshape LG Uplus's Profit Mix

LG Uplus is restoring operating profit through AI data center (AIDC) growth and cost efficiency as wireless revenue growth slows, while it expands capital expenditure and shareholder returns at the same time.

  1. 1

    Operating profit for 2025 was KRW 892.1 billion, up 3.4% from the prior year and recovering from the 2024 low. The operating margin, however, has slipped from 7.8% in 2022 to 5.8% in 2025.

  2. 2

    In the third quarter of 2026, B2B infrastructure revenue rose 10.4% year on year, and AI data center revenue grew 27.7%.

  3. 3

    Mobile revenue in the third quarter of 2026 fell 0.9% year on year to KRW 1.69 trillion, so wireless growth has clearly slowed.

  4. 4

    The second-quarter 2026 operating profit of KRW 344.5 billion is what the company described as the largest quarterly figure on record.

  5. 5

    Over the most recent four quarters (4Q 2025 to 3Q 2026), shareholder net income attributable to controlling interests totaled KRW 669.9 billion, and the company enlarged its 2026 interim dividend and share buyback.

02

Business structure

LG Uplus earns revenue from wireless, smart home (broadband internet and IPTV), enterprise infrastructure (AIDC, solutions and enterprise lines), and device sales.

In the first quarter of 2026, wireless accounted for 57.4% of standalone service revenue, smart home 23.7%, enterprise infrastructure 15.8%, and telephony 3.1%. Mobile segment revenue for 2025 was KRW 6.67 trillion, up 3.7%, and smart home revenue was KRW 2.59 trillion.

Enterprise infrastructure revenue reached KRW 1.81 trillion, up 6%, and AIDC revenue within it was KRW 422.0 billion, up 18.4%. In the third quarter of 2026, mobile revenue was KRW 1.69 trillion (down 0.9%), smart home KRW 665.5 billion (up 2.9%), and enterprise infrastructure B2B KRW 472.2 billion (up 10.4%).

Mobile lines at the end of the second quarter of 2026 numbered about 31.5 million, and 5G penetration reached 84.9%. In wireless, the company competes for subscribers with SK Telecom and KT. In wireline, rising gigabit internet subscriptions are driving smart home growth.

In enterprise infrastructure, the company is widening its revenue base by winning design, build and operate (DBO) projects alongside colocation services. Device revenue was KRW 766.8 billion in the first quarter of 2026, forming one pillar of total operating revenue.

04

Earnings analysis

Annual revenue was KRW 13.9 trillion in 2022, KRW 14.4 trillion in 2023 (up 3.4% year on year), KRW 14.6 trillion in 2024 (up 1.8%), and KRW 15.5 trillion in 2025 (up 5.7%). Revenue rose for three consecutive years from 2023 through 2025, and 2025 was the largest year in the 2022 to 2025 span.

Operating profit was KRW 1.08 trillion in 2022, the largest year in the span, then fell to KRW 998 billion in 2023 (down 7.7%) and KRW 863.1 billion in 2024 (down 13.5%). It recovered to KRW 892.1 billion in 2025, up 3.4%.

Shareholder net income attributable to controlling interests was KRW 663.1 billion in 2022, KRW 622.8 billion in 2023 (down 6.1%), KRW 374.5 billion in 2024 (down 39.9%), and KRW 523.9 billion in 2025 (up 39.9%). 2024 was the smallest year, and the 2025 rebound moved in the same direction as the operating profit recovery.

Net income including non-controlling interests was KRW 509.2 billion in 2025, a different figure from controlling-interest net income. The operating margin declined from 7.8% in 2022 to 6.9% in 2023, 5.9% in 2024 and 5.8% in 2025, so revenue grew while margins narrowed.

Whether the mix shift is weighing on margins is a point to verify in the next regular report. On a quarterly basis, the third quarter of 2025 posted the lowest operating profit in the disclosed series at KRW 161.7 billion, a period in which the company said it booked structural restructuring costs.

Operating profit then rose to KRW 170.5 billion in the fourth quarter of 2025, KRW 272.3 billion in the first quarter of 2026 (up 59.7% quarter on quarter) and KRW 344.5 billion in the second quarter (up 26.5%).

In the third quarter of 2026 it was KRW 297.6 billion, down 13.6% from the prior quarter but up 84.1% from a year earlier. Controlling-interest net income was KRW 176.9 billion in the first quarter of 2026, KRW 219.7 billion in the second and KRW 184.2 billion in the third.

Operating cash flow fell from KRW 3.5 trillion in 2022 to KRW 2.96 trillion in 2025. The debt ratio declined from 134.0% in 2022 to 117.1% in 2025.

05

Industry analysis

The domestic mobile market is near saturation in 5G penetration, which limits subscriber growth, while operators continue to compete for customers. The company said 5G penetration reached 84.9% at the end of the second quarter of 2026.

Carrier contract-termination fee waivers by rivals were cited as a factor that pushed up its net wireless subscriber additions. Daishin Securities, in an April 2026 company analysis, pointed to a continued downtrend in wireless ARPU, which it attributed to growth in lower-revenue subscribers such as MVNO and IoT lines.

The company, by contrast, reported that first-quarter 2026 ARPU rose to KRW 35,646. The two readings point in opposite directions, so the trend should be tracked in upcoming quarterly disclosures.

In enterprise infrastructure, the company says accelerating AI adoption across Korean industry is sustaining data center demand. It plans to build new AIDC facilities, including in Paju, that it expects to be more profitable than existing centers.

Overseas AIDC projects are being pursued on a turnkey basis together with group affiliates. In wireline, rising gigabit internet subscriptions and ARPU gains support smart home growth. Relative to SK Telecom and KT, the company combines wireless scale and a wireline base with enterprise AI infrastructure. A direct comparison of AIDC scale against those peers is not presented here.

06

Outlook

For 2026, the company set a standalone service revenue target of KRW 11.61 trillion, implying growth of at least 2% from the prior year. First-half service revenue reached KRW 5.71 trillion, or 49% of that target.

Standalone service revenue in the first half rose 3.4% year on year, above the 2% guidance set at the start of the year. The company expects wireless revenue growth to ease in the second half but said it can fully achieve the annual guidance.

Capital expenditure for 2026 is being raised around AIDC investment and reinforcing security fundamentals. The company said it plans to keep expanding AI infrastructure investment in 2027 and grow its AI service business.

It will add AIDC capacity in step with customer demand and expand its AX business for enterprise and public clients. It also plans to win additional design, build and operate (DBO) data center projects to diversify AIDC revenue. Share buybacks will be run flexibly according to investment plans and financial conditions.

The 2026 interim dividend and buyback size were already increased from the prior year. The company said that although AI-driven changes may widen investment opportunities, it will keep its direction of raising corporate value and expanding shareholder returns.

The next checkpoints are whether the annual service revenue target is met in the 2026 fourth-quarter release and the size of 2027 capital expenditure.

08

Bull factors

AIDC emerging as a growth pillar

B2B infrastructure revenue rose 10.4% year on year in the third quarter of 2026, and AI data center revenue grew 27.7%. AIDC revenue in 2025 was KRW 422.0 billion, up 18.4%. The company says new AIDC sites such as Paju can improve profitability relative to existing centers. Enterprise infrastructure growth was also cited as a driver of the record second-quarter 2026 operating profit.

Operating profit recovery and cost efficiency

Operating profit for 2025 rose 3.4% to KRW 892.1 billion, and the company said the 3.5% growth in service revenue was its fastest in the last four years. Third-quarter 2026 operating profit was KRW 297.6 billion, up 84.1% from a year earlier. Controlling-interest net income for the quarter was KRW 184.2 billion. The company attributes part of the improvement to cost efficiency and the use of AI tools in operations.

Expanding shareholder returns

The 2026 interim dividend and share buyback were both enlarged from the prior year. The company said it will fully retire KRW 80 billion worth of treasury shares. Daishin Securities noted that the payout ratio has been above 40% since 2022. The company says it will keep its direction of raising corporate value and expanding shareholder returns.

09

Bear factors

Slowing wireless growth

Mobile revenue in the third quarter of 2026 fell 0.9% year on year to KRW 1.69 trillion. The company expects wireless revenue growth to ease in the second half. Wireless remains the largest business, so its slowdown constrains overall top-line growth. Whether the annual guidance can offset that slowdown is the key point to watch.

ARPU pressure and margin erosion

In an April 2026 company analysis, Daishin Securities flagged a continued decline in wireless ARPU. The annual operating margin fell from 7.8% in 2022 to 5.8% in 2025. Revenue rose to KRW 15.5 trillion in 2025, yet margin erosion persisted.

The divergence between that brokerage view and the company's reported first-quarter ARPU gain is another element of the bearish case.

Investment burden and cash flow

Capital expenditure for 2026 is set to rise, centered on AIDC and security. Operating cash flow fell from KRW 3.5 trillion in 2022 to KRW 2.96 trillion in 2025. Total liabilities at the end of 2025 were KRW 10.6 trillion, above total equity of KRW 9.07 trillion.

Whether cash generation can support higher investment and shareholder returns at the same time is the bearish question.

10

Risk factors

Security and operational risk

The company had planned to book costs for replacing USIM cards in the second quarter of 2026 after an IMSI security controversy. It said it would focus on improving earnings and cash flow on a larger subscriber base. Repeated security incidents could raise additional costs and subscriber churn risk. Reinforcing security fundamentals is part of its investment agenda for this reason.

Competition and marketing risk

Rival contract-termination fee waivers have been cited as a factor lifting net wireless subscriber additions, and the company expected the effect to continue. Intensifying competition for subscribers could raise subsidy and marketing costs, pressuring ARPU and margins. The brokerage observation of declining wireless ARPU is closely linked to this risk.

Capex and demand risk

Capital expenditure is rising with AI infrastructure investment, and the company says it will expand AIDC capacity in step with customer demand. If demand slows relative to plans, the payback period for those assets could lengthen.

With operating cash flow below its 2022 level, rising investment could narrow financial headroom. The timing of new AIDC operations and the size of contract wins will largely determine this risk.

Overall view

LG Uplus is growing enterprise infrastructure revenue, centered on AI data centers (AIDC), while keeping its wireless subscriber base. Operating profit for 2025 was KRW 892.1 billion, recovering from the 2024 low, though the operating margin has declined from 7.8% in 2022 to 5.8% in 2025.

Mobile revenue in the third quarter of 2026 fell 0.9%, and the company itself expects wireless growth to ease in the second half. On the other side, second-quarter 2026 operating profit was the largest quarterly figure on record, and third-quarter operating profit rose 84.1% from a year earlier.

The price-to-book ratio is below 1x, so the share price sits under book value, and dividends together with share buybacks and retirements form the core of shareholder returns. As capital expenditure rises, balancing operating cash flow against investment becomes the next question.

Bullish factors such as AIDC growth coexist with bearish ones such as wireless slowdown and ARPU pressure. The next checkpoints are the preliminary fourth-quarter 2026 results and the annual operating margin in the 2026 business report.
