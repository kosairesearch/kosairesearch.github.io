Report overview

Standardization Evidence Builds, Revenue Turn Still Pending

Curiox Biosystems is building the case for adoption through NIST standardization studies and a company-wide license with a global drugmaker. Reported 2025 results, however, show revenue of KRW 5.15 billion and an operating loss of KRW 12.3 billion, so a turn in scale and profitability has not yet shown up in the numbers.

  1. 1

    The company says it was the first in the world to commercialize C-FREE, a technology that skips the centrifugation step in cell washing. Its business consists of automated sample-preparation instruments, software and dedicated consumables for cell analysis.

  2. 2

    Annual revenue fell from KRW 7.24 billion in 2022 to KRW 4.59 billion in 2024, then rose 12.3% to KRW 5.15 billion in 2025. The company posted operating losses in each of the four years from 2022 to 2025.

  3. 3

    Revenue in 2Q 2026 was KRW 1.18 billion, up 18.1% year on year but down for a second straight quarter. The operating loss widened to KRW 4.46 billion, larger than both the prior quarter and the year-earlier quarter.

  4. 4

    Multi-unit orders came in one after another in 3Q 2026: about KRW 510 million from a global cell and gene therapy drugmaker, and a supply contract worth about KRW 700 million with a top-five global contract research organization (CRO).   5. 5

    The 2025 net loss attributable to controlling shareholders, KRW 27.2 billion, was far larger than the operating loss. The company named the main cause: the share-price surge sharply raised derivative valuation losses on the conversion rights embedded in its convertible bonds.

02

Business structure

Curiox Biosystems makes instruments that automate the washing and staining steps before cell analysis without a centrifuge. The company says its flow-control method replaces culture medium with wash buffer, lifts cell recovery from 70% to as much as 95%, and reduces operator involvement.

Its main revenue sources have been instruments such as Pluto LT and Pluto HT, plus consumables. Since 2026 it has been expanding Pluto Code, software installed on liquid-handling workstations that customers already own.

In April 2026 it signed a comprehensive Pluto Code software license with an unnamed major global pharmaceutical company. Segment revenue shares are not disclosed, so the most that can be said is that instruments dominate today and software is at an early stage.

The company sells directly through its US subsidiary and also works with global instrument makers. One example is the AUTO-CX1000 laminar wash system, co-developed with Revvity and launched for the Chinese market.

Its real competitor is less any one company than the manual centrifugation workflow that has been the standard for decades.

04

Earnings analysis

Annual revenue was KRW 7.24 billion in 2022, KRW 6.79 billion in 2023 (down 6.2%), KRW 4.59 billion in 2024 (down 32.4%) and KRW 5.15 billion in 2025 (up 12.3%). Between 2022 and 2025, revenue was highest in 2022 and lowest in 2024.

The company attributed the 2024 decline to weaker end markets amid global economic uncertainty, monetary tightening and geopolitical issues. Operating losses were KRW 11.4 billion in 2022, KRW 10.6 billion in 2023, KRW 13.4 billion in 2024 and KRW 12.3 billion in 2025, four straight years of losses.

The operating margin improved from -291.6% in 2024 to -239.0% in 2025, but revenue is still too small to cover selling and administrative costs.

Quarterly revenue rose from KRW 996 million in 2Q 2025 to KRW 1.85 billion in 4Q 2025, then fell for two straight quarters to KRW 1.32 billion in 1Q 2026 and KRW 1.18 billion in 2Q 2026.

Operating losses ran for five consecutive quarters, and the 2Q 2026 loss of KRW 4.46 billion was wider than the KRW 3.25 billion of the prior quarter.

The KRW 18.3 billion net loss attributable to controlling shareholders in 2Q 2025 was driven largely by non-cash derivative valuation losses on the convertible bonds; the company disclosed a first-half cumulative loss of about KRW 12.4 billion on this item.

Operating cash flow stayed negative, moving from -KRW 9.89 billion in 2022 to -KRW 11.1 billion in 2025, so real cash burn continues regardless of the accounting losses.

05

Industry analysis

Cell analysis, including flow cytometry, is a foundational process in drug discovery, clinical trials and quality control for cell and gene therapies, yet sample preparation still relies heavily on manual work.

The first study by a NIST-led consortium found that differences between operators during sample preparation, rather than the instruments, were a main driver of variation in results across institutions. The study was published in Frontiers in Immunology in September 2026.

According to the company, Curiox is the only participant providing a sample-preparation automation solution in the second study. Preliminary analysis presented at CYTO 2026 in June 2026 showed that variation of about 27% under manual processing fell to about 5.3% with automation.

On the demand side, CROs are a key link: when a CRO changes its process, the many drugmakers that outsource analysis to it receive data generated with the same workflow.

Instrument sales, however, tend to be volatile quarter to quarter and slow to close, because they depend on when customers release budgets and on long evaluation cycles. Unlike the large contract manufacturers and drug developers in the same sector, Curiox is a small innovator in research tools and instruments.

06

Outlook

The near-term question is whether the 3Q orders turn into installed instruments and revenue. For the top-five CRO contract, the company plans to finish delivery, installation and on-site user training within 2026.

The two sides agreed to validate performance at the US site first and then, from 2027, extend adoption step by step to the CRO's multinational trial sites. On the software side, Curiox is discussing similar agreements with several global drugmakers and is pursuing additional licenses within 2026.

The company says it expects the software business to overtake instruments in revenue share within three years. In an August 2026 interview, CEO Kim Nam-yong said Curiox was in active talks with more than 10 global drugmakers on equipment adoption and that more multi-unit contracts with advance payments were likely.

In the second NIST study, the first round of data collection was completed in March 2026, and final results will be released after NIST's analysis, but no release date has been confirmed. All of these are company targets; no numerical guidance on revenue size or timing has been disclosed.

08

Bull factors

Credible standardization evidence

The first NIST study put on record, in a peer-reviewed paper, that sample preparation rather than instruments is a main source of variation in results. The authors wrote that automating sample preparation is a necessary next step to cut assay uncertainty and improve reproducibility across instrument platforms.

In effect, third-party research has identified the very problem the company's technology targets. If this makes its way into standardization documents, demand could emerge from quality-control departments that must meet regulatory requirements.

Software model with low cost burden

Pluto Code is sold under a license, just like the instruments, but carries virtually no manufacturing cost. Under the comprehensive agreement, procurement is completed once, after which affiliated labs can adopt the software with a simple purchase order.

Because it also runs on third-party workstations, its potential customer base is wider than that of the company's own instruments. If the model takes hold, the room for operating margin improvement is larger than with instrument sales alone.

Diffusion through CROs

In July 2026 a global CRO installed Pluto automation lines at its European and US sites at the same time, and in August a global cell and gene therapy drugmaker deployed instruments at two major research sites.

The company says a growing number of customers with prior experience are placing follow-on orders without lengthy additional testing. A shift from single-unit purchases to multi-unit orders can be read as a sign that adoption is spreading inside customer organizations.

09

Bear factors

Revenue too small for the cost base

In 2Q 2026 revenue was KRW 1.18 billion while the operating loss was KRW 4.46 billion, so the quarterly loss was several times quarterly revenue. In some quarters the loss grew even as revenue rose.

Orders of the size booked in 3Q are therefore unlikely to change the profit structure on their own; a meaningful improvement would require recurring revenue on a much larger scale.

Wide gap versus listing-time projections

At listing, the 2025 outlook called for revenue of KRW 43.4 billion and an operating profit; actual revenue reached only a fraction of that, and losses continued. The company's timelines have also slipped more than once, and it has said the NIST study schedule was adjusted partly because of a US government shutdown. That track record is worth keeping in mind when weighing future plans the company announces.

Pace of big-pharma management approval

The company has said its biggest bottleneck is how quickly positive feedback from bench scientists turns into management approval. The CEO has also acknowledged that, because this is a new business model, decisions by big-pharma top management are taking longer than expected.

Even with a comprehensive license signed, the actual pace of purchase orders under it has not been confirmed by any disclosed figures.

Risk factors

Funding and dilution

Operating cash flow was negative every year from 2022 to 2025, at -KRW 11.1 billion in 2025. Total liabilities were KRW 25.2 billion at end-2025, and the debt ratio rose to 47.8% from 9.3% in 2023.

If cash outflows continue, additional financing may be needed, and depending on its form, existing shareholders could be diluted.

Accounting earnings volatility

Derivative features such as the conversion rights on the convertible bonds are measured at fair value, so a rising share price generates valuation losses that enlarge the net loss. That is why the 2025 net loss attributable to controlling shareholders was more than twice the operating loss.

The company disclosed a pre-maturity purchase of its first-series convertible bonds on June 29, 2026. Depending on the size of the remaining bonds and whether they are converted, quarterly net results may diverge from operating performance.

Disclosure and communication credibility

A derivative loss arose on August 14, 2025, but the company did not disclose it until November 14, 2025, and the delay brought a notice of possible designation as an unfaithful disclosure company.

Some 3Q orders were announced through investor Q&A posts on the company website rather than exchange filings, because the amount recognized as parent-company revenue fell below the disclosure threshold. As a result, investors rely more on company IR materials than on regular filings for verifiable information.

12

Overall view

Curiox Biosystems' technical case advanced in 2026. NIST research academically confirmed the variability in sample preparation, the company signed its first comprehensive software license with a global drugmaker, and multi-unit orders from CROs and drugmakers kept coming.

Reported results, however, do not yet reflect that progress: annual revenue has stayed between roughly KRW 4.6 billion and KRW 7.2 billion for four years, and the operating loss widened again in 2Q 2026.

The share price reflects a large premium to net assets, leaving a wide gap between expectations and disclosed figures. The things to check, in order, are whether orders show up in 3Q and 4Q revenue, whether additional licenses are signed within 2026, and whether the multinational rollout actually happens in 2027.
