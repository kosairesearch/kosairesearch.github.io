02

Business structure

Finebesteel was founded in September 2007 and operates as a hot-rolled and extruded product manufacturer, with a core focus on shipbuilding sections. It is a rolling specialist that uses semi-finished slabs as its raw material to produce sections.

Slabs are sourced domestically and overseas, with POSCO-made slabs accounting for the majority. Its core product is shipbuilding sections, and specialty items such as inverted angles face a market where imported products hold a high share, which gives domestic producers room to respond to localization demand.

Early-2010s reporting put the shipbuilding share of sales at 92%, which underscores how closely the business tracks the shipbuilding cycle. Diversification is underway.

At the March 31, 2026 shareholder meeting, the articles of incorporation added special steel and steel product manufacturing, processing, sales and distribution, which the company described as diversification. In June 2026 the company decided on a KRW 15.4 billion special steel production facility investment.

In July 2026 it signed a cooperation agreement with POSCO, under which Finebesteel handles production, development, sales and new market development, while POSCO supplies raw materials and co-develops new steel grades.

On the competitive side, earlier reporting noted shipbuilders increasing their use of cheaper Chinese angle sections, which keeps price pressure in place.

04

Earnings analysis

On a reported basis, annual revenue went from KRW 139.6 billion in 2022 to KRW 132.2 billion in 2023 (down 5.3% year on year) and KRW 119.2 billion in 2024 (down 9.9%), before rising to KRW 186.2 billion in 2025 (up 56.2%). Within 2022 to 2025, 2025 was the largest year and 2024 the smallest.

Operating income moved from KRW 719 million in 2022 to an operating loss of KRW 19.8 billion in 2023, then KRW 19.1 billion in 2024 and KRW 1.67 billion in 2025, so the loss narrowed. The company recorded operating losses for three consecutive years from 2023 through 2025.

The operating margin went from 0.5% in 2022 to -15.0% in 2023 and -16.1% in 2024, before improving to -0.9% in 2025. Reporting on 2023 noted that operating results turned to a loss for the first time in three years as sales fell 7.1%.

Cost control was the main margin lever: the cost of sales ratio was 111.1% in 2024, 106.0% in Q1 2025 and 94.7% in Q2 2025.

Net loss attributable to controlling shareholders was KRW 2.6 billion in 2022, KRW 17.2 billion in 2023, KRW 27.5 billion in 2024 and KRW 5.11 billion in 2025, a loss in each of the four years from 2022 through 2025.

Net income including non-controlling interests, which is a different figure, was a net loss of KRW 6.11 billion in 2025.

Quarterly revenue peaked at KRW 53.4 billion in Q4 2025 after KRW 48.5 billion in Q3 2025, then fell to KRW 50.6 billion in Q1 2026, KRW 49.1 billion in Q2 2026 and KRW 45.6 billion in Q3 2026, three straight quarterly declines.

Operating income was KRW 2.17 billion in Q4 2025, the largest of the last five quarters, and fell to KRW 762 million in Q3 2026. Net income attributable to controlling shareholders turned positive at KRW 211 million in Q1 2026, then KRW 74.48 million in Q2 2026, and a loss of KRW 555 million in Q3 2026.

The preliminary nine-month figures announced on November 17, 2026 show consolidated revenue of KRW 145.4 billion, up 9.5% year on year, and operating income of KRW 3.4 billion, against an operating loss of KRW 3.8 billion a year earlier.

The company attributed this to a recovery in shipbuilding and cost control, while financing costs from new facility investment remain a burden.

05

Industry analysis

Shipbuilding is the downstream market that drives Finebesteel's earnings. In its November 17, 2026 announcement, the company attributed the improvement to a recovery in the shipbuilding cycle and rising vessel build volumes.

A 2025 research note argued that tighter US-Korea shipbuilding cooperation, expanded vessel orders tied to China-related concerns, and rising demand for eco-friendly and alternative-fuel ships would support shipbuilding section sales.

The same report described the domestic market for shipbuilding angle sections as one where imports hold a high share, and cited the ability of domestic producers to respond to localization demand for specialty products as an advantage.

Earlier reporting noted that shipbuilders were increasing their use of cheaper Chinese angle sections to protect margins, and that the reporting predates the current period, so today's price picture may differ.

Because slab supply depends heavily on POSCO-made material, the spread between slab costs and product prices is a key driver of profitability. In special steel, the company said the investment responds to rising special steel demand from automotive and other downstream industries.

Finebesteel is a mid-sized steelmaker specializing in shipbuilding sections, and its positioning centers on niche products rather than the broad portfolios of large steelmakers. Whether shipbuilding orders and build volumes keep recovering will determine where demand sits in the cycle.

06

Outlook

On November 17, 2026, the company said it would develop new markets and broaden its order base, starting with sheet piles, whose production has resumed.

On April 27, 2026, the board approved a special steel production capacity expansion of about KRW 4.49 billion, with a period running from April 27, 2026 to April 27, 2027, roughly one year.

The company said the amount and schedule could change with business conditions and that payments would be made in stages by contract.

In June 2026 it decided on a KRW 15.4 billion special steel production facility investment, equal to 29.75% of equity capital, and described the aim as product portfolio diversification and a medium- to long-term growth base.

Under the POSCO agreement, POSCO will supply raw materials and co-develop new steel grades and R&D, so that structure will shape the cost and product development of the special steel business. Reporting listed a plan to raise KRW 30 billion through an asset-backed loan facility.

Financing costs are a factor that can weigh on non-operating results as facility investment grows. The business purpose added to the articles in March 2026 broadens the legal basis for special steel sales. The company's stated direction is to strengthen its shipbuilding section business while growing new businesses.

The next checkpoint is the 2026 annual business report, which will confirm full-year revenue and operating margin.

Bull factors

Shipbuilding recovery carried into a nine-month profit

The company said nine-month 2026 consolidated operating income turned positive at KRW 3.4 billion, driven by shipbuilding recovery and expanded sales to key customers. Q3 2026 three-month operating income of KRW 762 million also stayed in the black.

Expansion into special steel

In June 2026 the company decided on a KRW 15.4 billion special steel production facility. The POSCO agreement adds raw material supply and co-development of new grades, and the company said it will strengthen its shipbuilding section business while growing new businesses.

Cost ratio improvement

Reporting showed the cost of sales ratio fell from 111.1% in 2024 to 94.7% in Q2 2025. The operating margin improved from -15.0% in 2023 and -16.1% in 2024 to -0.9% in 2025, and the company said cost increases were held back in the nine months of 2026.

Bear factors

Accumulated losses and financial strain

Net losses attributable to controlling shareholders ran for four consecutive years from 2022 through 2025. Total liabilities of KRW 119.9 billion exceeded equity of KRW 51.8 billion at the end of 2025, a debt-to-equity ratio of 231.5%, after 353.6% in 2024. The company also cited financing costs tied to new facility investment.

Declining quarterly sales and profit

Quarterly revenue has fallen for three consecutive quarters since peaking in Q4 2025, and operating income has also declined for three quarters. Net loss attributable to controlling shareholders was KRW 555 million in Q3 2026, a swing back to a loss. Preliminary Q2 2026 results announced in July 2026 showed net income down 94.9%.

Shipbuilding dependence and import competition

Earlier reporting said shipbuilders were increasing their use of Chinese angle sections to protect margins. Shipbuilding made up a high share of sales in early-2010s data, which means the business is sensitive to swings in that cycle.

10

Risk factors

Slab raw material sourcing

The company sources slabs from home and abroad, most of them made by POSCO. The spread between slab costs and product prices determines profitability, so changes in procurement terms could weigh on gross margin.

Because POSCO will also supply raw materials for special steel, the cost structure could be tied more closely to the cooperation terms. Whether the cost ratio improvement lasts depends on whether this procurement structure holds.

Sheet pile order expansion

The company said it will develop new markets and broaden its order base, starting with sheet piles, whose production has resumed. Securing new sheet pile orders could supplement a revenue base centered on sections.

However, the timing and scale of customer qualification and trade partner wins in new markets have not been confirmed through disclosures. If order expansion is delayed, its role as a buffer against swings in shipbuilding orders may remain limited.

Execution of new businesses

Special steel is a new area separate from the existing section business, so customer acquisition and product approvals are required. The company said investment amounts and timing could change with conditions, and the POSCO partnership must translate into actual product sales.

Overall view

Finebesteel is a steelmaker centered on shipbuilding sections. After revenue growth and a narrower loss in 2025, nine-month 2026 operating income turned positive on a preliminary basis.

However, net losses attributable to controlling shareholders ran for four consecutive years from 2022 through 2025, the debt-to-equity ratio was 231.5% at the end of 2025, and quarterly revenue has fallen for three consecutive quarters through Q3 2026, so the durability of the trend is still to be confirmed.

Positive factors are the shipbuilding recovery, the lower cost ratio and expansion into special steel. Negative factors are dependence on the shipbuilding cycle, competition from Chinese sections and financing costs from facility investment.

The KRW 15.4 billion investment and the KRW 4.49 billion expansion aim at capacity and product diversification, but the company said timing and amounts could change.

The next checkpoints are full-year operating margin and net income direction in the 2026 annual business report, and the completion timing of the expansion. How valuation is read depends on whether earnings continue.
