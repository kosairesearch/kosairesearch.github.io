02

Business structure

DYD was founded in December 2000 and runs cosmetics manufacturing and distribution alongside a construction business. Cosmetics made up 99.11% of revenue in 2023 and construction 0.89%, down from 69.21% and 30.79% in 2022, so the construction share fell sharply in 2023.

The company owns no production facilities and outsources manufacturing as OEM production. According to a WiseReport company note, its main in-house brands are a color cosmetics brand, a skincare brand and a vegan lifestyle brand, and online, offline and overseas sales are expanding.

Domestic sales dominate, at 97.20% of 2023 revenue against 2.80% from exports. The construction business began after the company obtained a general construction license in April 2022 and started winning construction contracts. In September 2023 it signed a land acquisition agreement for a golf course project.

In 2025 the exit from construction removed related costs, but losses in the cosmetics segment weighed on overall profitability, according to FnGuide's summary. After the exit the business is close to a single cosmetics axis, and restoring profitability is the central task.

04

Earnings analysis

Annual revenue rose from KRW 20.8 billion in 2022 to KRW 28.5 billion in 2023 (up 36.6% year on year), KRW 37.4 billion in 2024 (up 31.3%) and KRW 44.9 billion in 2025 (up 20.1%), a rise of three consecutive years from 2023 to 2025. Of the 2022 to 2025 period, 2025 was the largest year and 2022 the smallest.

Operating profit of KRW 513 million in 2022 turned into an operating loss of KRW 3.32 billion in 2023, a swing from profit to loss; the loss narrowed to KRW 49.61 million in 2024 and widened again to KRW 490 million in 2025, leaving losses in each year from 2023 to 2025.

The operating margin fell from 2.5% in 2022 to -11.7% in 2023, then was -0.1% in 2024 and -1.1% in 2025.

Net income attributable to controlling shareholders went from KRW 902 million in 2022 to a net loss of KRW 19.1 billion in 2023, KRW 59.1 billion in 2024 (a wider loss) and KRW 13.7 billion in 2025 (a narrower loss). Net income including non-controlling interests was reported as the same loss figures for 2023 to 2025.

In 2023 and 2024 the net loss was far larger than the operating loss, so non-operating items are the key variable for interpreting those years.

On a quarterly basis, the third quarter of 2025 showed revenue of KRW 12.2 billion, operating profit of KRW 16.22 million and a net loss attributable to controlling shareholders of KRW 732 million.

The fourth quarter of 2025 brought revenue of KRW 13.1 billion (up 7.1% from the prior quarter), operating profit of KRW 447 million (up 2,654.3%) and net income attributable to controlling shareholders of KRW 1.31 billion, a swing to profit.

The first quarter of 2026 recorded revenue of KRW 15.6 billion (up 19.0%), operating profit of KRW 1.08 billion (up 142.6%) and net income attributable to controlling shareholders of KRW 277 million (down 78.9%).

The second quarter of 2026 recorded revenue of KRW 13.9 billion (down 10.7%), operating profit of KRW 215 million (down 80.1%) and a net loss attributable to controlling shareholders of KRW 170 million.

The third quarter of 2026 recorded revenue of KRW 17.5 billion (up 25.7% from the prior quarter and up 43.1% year on year), operating profit of KRW 1.87 billion (up 11,407.7% year on year) and net income attributable to controlling shareholders of KRW 935 million, a swing to profit.

Over the last four quarters, net income attributable to controlling shareholders totaled KRW 2.35 billion, a small base compared with the KRW 13.7 billion net loss for 2025.

05

Industry analysis

In an annual-basis tally of 47 cosmetics-sector stocks by AlphaSquare, the sector average operating margin for 2025 was -14.09%, below DYD's -1.1%. In the same tally, the sector average debt ratio was 65.63%, far below DYD's 195.91%.

A sector average in loss territory means profitability is weak across the industry, and DYD's loss margin sits at the smaller end of that range.

According to FnGuide's sector comparison, total annual revenue of the KOSDAQ distribution sector was KRW 456,705 billion, so DYD's 2025 revenue of KRW 44.9 billion is small relative to the sector.

Because domestic sales dominate, the company is exposed to changes in domestic cosmetics distribution, particularly the expansion of online and offline channels. Competition among its color cosmetics, skincare and vegan lifestyle brands drives revenue, and the company covers these segments with three brands.

After the construction exit the business scope is narrower, so the relevant peer comparison is mainly with cosmetics companies. The gap to the sector average on profitability shows room for margin improvement, but whether that improvement becomes an annual profit will be confirmed in the 2026 annual report.

On a quarterly basis, revenue fell in the second quarter of 2026 from the prior quarter and then rose to KRW 17.5 billion in the third quarter, while operating profit grew to KRW 1.87 billion.

Compared with the 2025 annual operating loss of KRW 490 million, the third quarter of 2026 operating profit of KRW 1.87 billion exceeds that loss in a single quarter. The 2026 fiscal year ends in late December 2026, and fourth-quarter results will first be compiled in the annual report.

08

Bull factors

Expanding quarterly operating profit

Operating profit in the third quarter of 2026 was KRW 1.87 billion, up 11,407.7% year on year, and quarterly operating profit has been positive in every quarter from the third quarter of 2025 onward.

Compared with the 2025 annual operating loss of KRW 490 million, one quarter of operating profit now exceeds that annual loss. If this trend continues, it provides a basis for a change in the direction of full-year operating results.

Simpler cost structure after the construction exit

FnGuide's summary says the 2025 exit from the construction business removed related costs. With cosmetics as the single core business, the scope of operations is also simpler to describe. Whether profitability in the cosmetics segment itself improves will determine whether this structure works.

Revenue and profit improved together in the first half of 2026

According to a WiseReport company note, revenue in the first half of 2026 rose 50.2% year on year on a standalone basis, and operating profit and net income both turned positive. The note cites expanded online, offline and overseas sales of in-house brands and diversified distribution channels as the drivers. Revenue growth and profit improvement appeared at the same time, which characterizes this trend.

09

Bear factors

Losses in three consecutive years

Operating results were losses from 2023 to 2025, with an operating loss of KRW 3.32 billion in 2023, KRW 49.61 million in 2024 and KRW 490 million in 2025. Net loss attributable to controlling shareholders was largest in 2024 at KRW 59.1 billion, and 2025 still recorded KRW 13.7 billion. Quarterly profits have appeared, but they are still small relative to the accumulated annual losses.

High debt ratio and thin equity

The debt ratio rose from 5.7% in 2022 to 95.6% in 2023, 200.2% in 2024 and 195.9% in 2025. At the end of 2025, total equity of KRW 10.1 billion stood against total liabilities of KRW 19.8 billion. The thin equity base that built up during the loss years remains a weakness in financial stability.

Persistent negative operating cash flow

Operating cash flow was -KRW 4.69 billion in 2022, -KRW 5.44 billion in 2023, -KRW 6.18 billion in 2024 and -KRW 1.64 billion in 2025. According to FnGuide's tally, it was about -KRW 2.6 billion in the first quarter of 2026. If positive quarterly operating profit does not translate into cash inflows, financial room remains limited.

10

Risk factors

Earnings volatility

Net income attributable to controlling shareholders has swung between loss and profit, with a loss in the third quarter of 2025, profit in the fourth quarter, profit in the first quarter of 2026, a loss in the second quarter and profit in the third quarter.

The KRW 2.35 billion total over the last four quarters is small enough that one or two quarters can move it substantially. Whether quarterly profits accumulate into annual profit is the largest variable.

Business concentration and reliance on outsourced production

Cosmetics accounted for 99.11% of 2023 revenue, and after the construction exit the company relies in effect on a single cosmetics axis. With no production facilities of its own, it depends on OEM outsourcing, so partner utilization and cost directly affect results. The room to respond to changes in brand demand or distribution channels may be limited.

Financial leverage and funding

With total liabilities of KRW 19.8 billion exceeding total equity of KRW 10.1 billion, interest burden and borrowing capacity are key items to monitor alongside results. With operating cash flow negative, operating funds may depend on external financing.

Because the 2024 net loss was far larger than the operating loss, the burden from non-operating items also needs attention.

Overall view

DYD exited the construction business and narrowed its focus to cosmetics. Quarterly operating profit has stayed positive since the third quarter of 2025, and the third quarter of 2026 recorded operating profit of KRW 1.87 billion and net income attributable to controlling shareholders of KRW 935 million.

A WiseReport note on first-half 2026 standalone revenue rising 50.2% is consistent with this trend.

On the other side, operating and net losses persisted for three consecutive years from 2023 to 2025, the debt ratio was 195.9% at the end of 2025, and operating cash flow remained negative through the first quarter of 2026.

Net income attributable to controlling shareholders over the last four quarters totals KRW 2.35 billion, which is small relative to past cumulative losses.

Bullish factors are the quarterly turn to profit and the simpler cost structure; bearish factors are accumulated annual losses, financial leverage and weak cash flow.
