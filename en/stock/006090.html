Report overview

Sales Growth, Shrinking Profit at a Seafood-Food Hybrid

Oyang's revenue rose three years in a row from 2023 through 2025, yet operating profit fell in 2025, and the company posted a net loss attributable to controlling shareholders of KRW 764 million in the third quarter of 2026.

02

Business structure

Oyang was founded in 1969 as a seafood processing and distant-water fishing company, joined the Sajo group in 2007, and expanded its food processing business through a 2015 merger with Sajo Nambu Ham.

A corporate information service puts the 2025 year-end revenue mix at roughly 80% food processing, 19% marine products including tuna and pollack catches and sales, and 1% rental income.

WiseReport splits product revenue differently, at 67.19% manufacturing, 17.68% catches, 14.73% merchandise and 0.40% other, so the shares vary by classification.

The food side centers on imitation crab and processed fish products, along with fish cakes, fish balls, ham and sausages, while the marine side centers on distant-water trawl and tuna purse-seine and longline catches.

As of September 2025, Oyang's market shares were 17.56% in distant-water trawl, 8.20% in imitation crab products, 2.75% in tuna purse-seine and 2.16% in tuna longline. In 2024, food products were sold entirely domestically, and merchandise such as pollack, cod and herring was 97.94% domestic.

The food manufacturing unit pursues brand synergy by linking with the distribution networks of affiliates. Largest shareholder Sajodaerim holds 57.97% and consolidates Oyang as a subsidiary.

The marine unit is pursuing better earnings through securing fishing quotas and widening fishing zones, and in 2025 it bought one vessel for trawl operations in the Falkland waters.

04

Earnings analysis

Annual revenue was KRW 3,691 eok (1 eok = KRW 100 million) in 2022, KRW 3,915 eok in 2023 (up 6.1% year over year), KRW 4,007 eok in 2024 (up 2.3% year over year), and KRW 4,174 eok in 2025 (up 4.2% year over year), so it rose for three consecutive years from 2023 to 2025, and 2025 was the largest year in the period.

Operating profit rose from KRW 102 eok in 2022 to KRW 163 eok in 2023 (up 59.6% year over year) and KRW 209 eok in 2024 (up 28.4% year over year), making 2024 the largest year in the period. Operating profit in 2025 fell to KRW 135 eok (down 35.3% year over year).

The operating margin rose from 2.8% in 2022 to 4.2% in 2023 and 5.2% in 2024, then fell to 3.2% in 2025. Taken together with media reports that the cost of sales ratio reached 89.6% in the same year, the 2025 pattern of rising revenue but a falling margin suggests that cost pressure may have been a major factor.

Net profit attributable to controlling shareholders peaked at KRW 203 eok in 2023 (up 138.1% year over year) from KRW 85.3 eok in 2022, then declined for two consecutive years, to KRW 126 eok in 2024 (down 37.7% year over year) and KRW 106 eok in 2025 (down 16.3% year over year).

In 2023, net profit attributable to controlling shareholders was markedly larger than operating profit, so factors outside operating profit, such as non-operating gains and losses, may have played a role.

Cash flow from operating activities rose from KRW -162 eok in 2022 to KRW 296 eok in 2023 and KRW 493 eok in 2024, then fell to KRW 117 eok in 2025.

At the end of 2025, total equity was KRW 2,472 eok, of which equity attributable to controlling shareholders was also KRW 2,472 eok, and total liabilities were KRW 1,891 eok.

On a quarterly basis, third-quarter 2025 revenue was KRW 1,096 eok and operating profit was KRW 36.5 eok, while in the fourth quarter of 2025 revenue fell sharply to KRW 1,040 eok (down 5.1% quarter over quarter) and operating profit to KRW 2.1 eok (down 94.2% quarter over quarter).

In the first quarter of 2026 the company recovered, with revenue of KRW 1,052 eok, operating profit of KRW 69.5 eok (up 3,159.6% quarter over quarter), and net profit attributable to controlling shareholders of KRW 47 eok, but in the second quarter it fell again, with revenue of KRW 921 eok (down 12.5% quarter over quarter) and operating profit of KRW 4.8 eok (down 93.2% quarter over quarter).

Operating profit in the third quarter of 2026 was KRW 24.8 eok (up 421.5% quarter over quarter and down 32.0% year over year), and, unlike the same period a year earlier when net profit attributable to controlling shareholders was KRW 14.7 eok, it swung to a net loss attributable to controlling shareholders of KRW 7.6 eok.

The sum of net profit attributable to controlling shareholders over the most recent four quarters (fourth quarter of 2025 to third quarter of 2026) is KRW 78.8 eok.

05

Industry analysis

Oyang's earnings split across two engines: food processing and marine products. The food side is shaped by demand for processed seafood items like imitation crab and by raw material prices, while the marine side depends on fishing quotas, fishing zones and resource conditions.

In marine operations, tighter resource conservation has become a policy variable, making quota access a key determinant of profit.

Sajo Industries, a group affiliate in the same seafood trade, recorded a third-quarter 2026 operating loss of KRW 1.4 billion on revenue of KRW 177.3 billion, turning negative from operating profit of KRW 11.9 billion a year earlier.

On cost of sales, Oyang's 2025 ratio of 89.6% was reported alongside Sajodaerim's 87.9% as among the highest in the food sector. In food, Oyang's 8.20% share of the imitation crab category as of September 2025 is a single-digit position.

In marine, its 17.56% share of distant-water trawl as of September 2025 is the company's clearest market foothold. On the cycle, resource regulation and raw material costs remain the main variables for earnings.

06

Outlook

Oyang's marine unit is pursuing better earnings through securing fishing quotas and widening its fishing zones. The 2025 purchase of one vessel for trawl operations in the Falkland waters is read as an investment to expand catch capacity.

Whether that vessel's contribution shows up in marine revenue will be visible in upcoming quarterly reports. The food manufacturing unit concentrated on new product development and raised operating profit in the first half of 2026.

Third-quarter 2026 revenue of KRW 115.4 billion, up 5.3% year on year, can be read as a sign of top-line recovery. The 32.0% year-on-year drop in operating profit in the same quarter, however, remains a margin challenge.

The next checkpoint is how the full-year operating margin, which will be fixed in the 2026 annual report, moves from 3.2% in 2025. On dividends, the company resolved a cash dividend for the 2025 fiscal year, and its articles set quarterly dividend record dates at March 31, June 30 and September 30.

If resource conservation keeps tightening, the scope of quota access will remain a variable that shapes the earnings direction.

Room to improve food margins through new products and costs

Operating cash flow fell to KRW 11.7 billion in 2025 from KRW 49.3 billion in 2024, but it has stayed positive since 2023 after the -KRW 16.2 billion recorded in 2022.

Pursuing more quotas and fishing zones in marine

The marine unit is working to improve its earnings structure by securing quotas and widening fishing zones. In 2025 it added one vessel for trawl operations in the Falkland waters. A 17.56% share of distant-water trawl as of September 2025 is read as the base for this segment.

Bear factors

Third-quarter loss and volatile earnings

Third-quarter 2026 operating profit of KRW 2.48 billion fell 32.0% year on year, and a net loss attributable to controlling shareholders of KRW 764 million compared with a KRW 1.47 billion profit a year earlier.

Quarterly operating profit has repeatedly dropped to low levels, such as KRW 213 million in the fourth quarter of 2025 and KRW 476 million in the second quarter of 2026, which weakens earnings stability. Profit attributable to controlling shareholders for the latest four quarters totaled KRW 7.88 billion.

Marine resource rules and rising competition

In the first half of 2026, the marine unit's sales and operating profit both fell on tighter resource conservation and stronger competition. Sajo Industries, a group affiliate, also swung to a third-quarter 2026 operating loss of KRW 1.4 billion.

Marine accounts for roughly 19% of revenue, yet its decline appears to have weighed on total first-half earnings.

High cost ratio and margin compression

Operating margin fell to 3.2% in 2025 from 5.2% in 2024. A press report put the 2025 cost-of-sales ratio at 89.6%, the highest on an absolute basis among the food companies it covered.

Operating profit dropped from KRW 20.9 billion in 2024 to KRW 13.5 billion in 2025 even as revenue grew, a pattern in which sales gains did not carry through to profit.

10

Risk factors

Regulatory and resource risk

Tighter resource conservation could limit fishing quotas and zones in the marine unit. Difficulty securing quotas would reduce distant-water trawl and tuna catch volumes, directly affecting marine revenue and operating profit. Changes in overseas fishing conditions, including in the Falkland waters, also remain a variable.

Cost and margin risk

The 2025 cost-of-sales ratio of 89.6% is high, and operating margin fell to 3.2%. If raw material and catch costs rise and cannot be passed on to prices, margins could narrow further.

Quarterly operating profit has moved from the KRW 200 million range to the KRW 69 million range, so cost swings feed into reported results quickly.

Ownership and affiliate transaction risk

Sajodaerim holds 57.97% of Oyang, a subsidiary structure in which affiliate transaction terms can affect reported results. Past press coverage has also pointed to the weight of intra-group dealings. Terms of affiliate transactions and their disclosure shape how earnings are interpreted.
