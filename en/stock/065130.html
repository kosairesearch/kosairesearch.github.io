02

Business structure

Top Engineering Company develops, manufactures and sells process equipment for semiconductors, LCD, OLED and secondary batteries, as well as camera modules, according to its corporate value enhancement plan disclosed in October 2026.

Its business is split into an equipment segment and a secondary battery and camera module segment, the latter including subsidiary Powerlogics.

In Q1 2026, consolidated revenue of KRW 263.9 billion comprised camera modules (CM) at KRW 189.1 billion (71.68%), secondary battery equipment and ES at KRW 51.6 billion (19.56%), other at KRW 13.7 billion (5.19%) and display at KRW 9.4 billion (3.57%).

The company name and product image point to display equipment, but its revenue share is small. Exports were 86.50% of revenue in the same quarter. With a 33.75% effective stake in Powerlogics, results of the secondary battery and camera module business weigh heavily on consolidated earnings.

In displays, the company is expanding automotive display bonding equipment that laminates vehicle display panels and touch glass, and an industry analysis source says it targets annual sales above KRW 25 billion from automotive equipment.

Its semiconductor glass-substrate cutting equipment is being co-developed with a laser technology partner, targeting delivery next year. In competition, it is compared with display process equipment makers in the same machinery and equipment sector, including Sunic System, People & Technology and Philoptics.

04

Earnings analysis

Based on reported results, annual revenue peaked at 959.4 billion won in 2023 (up 2.4% year on year) from 936.4 billion won in 2022, then fell to 815.4 billion won in 2024 (down 15.0% year on year) and 798.5 billion won in 2025 (down 2.1% year on year), declining for two consecutive years from 2024 to 2025. 2023 was the largest year in the period and 2025 the smallest.

Operating profit turned positive, moving from an operating loss of 28.5 billion won in 2022 to an operating profit of 21.9 billion won in 2023, but swung back to an operating loss of 7.47 billion won in 2024, and the loss continued and widened to 35.2 billion won in 2025.

The operating margin was -3.0% in 2022, 2.3% in 2023, -0.9% in 2024 and -4.4% in 2025. Net profit attributable to controlling shareholders was 22.1 billion won in 2023 and 3.19 billion won in 2024 (down 85.6% year on year), then turned to a net loss attributable to controlling shareholders of 36.6 billion won in 2025.

Net loss for the same year was 45.7 billion won, which includes the share attributable to non-controlling interests and therefore differs from the loss attributable to controlling shareholders.

As the causes of the wider loss in 2025, the company's March 2026 disclosure cited a higher development-related cost ratio and weak results at subsidiaries.

By quarter, third-quarter 2025 revenue was 209.1 billion won with operating profit of 1.99 billion won, which turned into fourth-quarter revenue of 172.7 billion won and an operating loss of 9.97 billion won.

In the first quarter of 2026, results recovered to revenue of 263.9 billion won, operating profit of 9.04 billion won and net profit attributable to controlling shareholders of 5.55 billion won, with the camera module business cited as the driver, as multi-camera adoption and demand for high-resolution premium models increased.

After that, second-quarter revenue was 238.4 billion won (down 9.6% quarter on quarter) and third-quarter revenue was 227.9 billion won (down 4.4% quarter on quarter), falling for two consecutive quarters. The combined net loss attributable to controlling shareholders over the latest four quarters is 10.7 billion won.

Because operating profit stayed positive while profit attributable to controlling shareholders turned negative, the direction of non-operating items and subsidiary earnings should be watched together. Operating cash flow also fell from 37.8 billion won in 2022 to 19.4 billion won in 2025.

05

Industry analysis

On the demand side, smartphone camera modules were the main driver of revenue. In Q1 2026, growth was attributed to multi-camera adoption and rising demand for premium high-resolution models. In secondary batteries, growth in medium and large battery pack business tracked the expansion of EV and ESS markets.

In display equipment, wider adoption of OLED panels by Chinese-speaking regions was cited as a new demand factor. One securities research summary expected new secondary battery facility investment as domestic battery makers' order backlogs grow.

A press headline also framed renewed investment by global panel makers as favorable for equipment orders. In automotive electronics, the company aims to expand with bonding equipment that integrates instrument clusters and center information displays.

A high export share makes results sensitive to overseas customers' capital spending. Compared with listed display process equipment peers, its mix is weighted toward camera modules and secondary battery equipment rather than displays.

06

Outlook

The company has disclosed three directions. First, its October 16, 2026 corporate value enhancement plan puts operational competitiveness and cost-structure improvement of existing businesses at the top of its targets.

Second, it plans to diversify its portfolio by applying existing equipment technology more widely and to grow higher-value business by entering the semiconductor test equipment market.

Third, it lists expanded shareholder return policies, linking employee compensation to share price, and broader investor relations channels. In automotive electronics equipment, the company is pursuing product diversification and market expansion with a target of annual sales above KRW 25 billion.

Its semiconductor glass-substrate cutting equipment is targeted for delivery next year with a co-development partner, so whether deliveries and revenue recognition follow is the checkpoint.

New order reports alone do not pin down when results will be reflected; production, delivery, inspection and revenue recognition must be confirmed through disclosures.

Wider OLED adoption in Chinese-speaking markets and expected secondary battery facility investment are cited as positive demand factors, but no confirmed quantitative forecast is available. Since camera-module-led sales fell in both Q2 and Q3, the pace of recovery needs to show in the next reported quarter.

Bull factors

Q1 turnaround and camera-module demand

Operating profit returned to KRW 9.04 billion in Q1 2026 after a loss in Q4 2025, and net profit attributable to controlling shareholders was KRW 5.55 billion. Multi-camera adoption and demand for premium high-resolution smartphone models were cited as the drivers. Q3 2026 operating profit rose 193.7% year on year.

Secondary battery and OLED demand

The secondary battery equipment and ES segment was 19.56% of Q1 2026 revenue, and medium and large battery pack business supported results as EV and ESS markets expand. Wider OLED panel adoption in Chinese-speaking markets was cited as a demand factor for display equipment.

Expectations for new battery facility investment are also being raised as domestic battery makers' backlogs grow.

Value enhancement plan and new business targets

The corporate value enhancement plan disclosed on October 16, 2026 sets targets for cost-structure improvement, wider application of existing equipment technology and entry into semiconductor test equipment. It also states expanded shareholder return policies and more active investor relations. Automotive electronics equipment has a target of annual sales above KRW 25 billion.

Bear factors

Wider 2025 losses and cost pressure

The 2025 operating loss was KRW 35.2 billion and the net loss was KRW 45.7 billion, both larger than the prior year. A March 2026 disclosure cited higher cost ratios on development projects and weak subsidiary results. The company recorded operating losses for two consecutive years from 2024 through 2025.

Consecutive revenue declines and a return to shareholder-level loss

Revenue peaked at KRW 263.9 billion in Q1 2026, then declined to KRW 238.4 billion in Q2 and KRW 227.9 billion in Q3, two straight quarterly declines. Q3 2026 saw a net loss attributable to controlling shareholders of KRW 3.14 billion.

Small display share in revenue

Display segment revenue was KRW 9.4 billion in Q1 2026, only 3.57% of the total. Although the company is often associated with display equipment, camera modules made up 71.68% of revenue. Exports at 86.50% also leave results exposed to overseas demand and exchange rates.

Risk factors

Subsidiary earnings risk

Powerlogics, in which the company holds a 33.75% effective stake, carries a large share of consolidated results from the secondary battery and camera module business. Weak subsidiary performance was cited for 2025, so whether costs rise faster than subsidiary sales must be watched. The gap between attributable and total results also needs tracking.

Customer and export concentration risk

In Q1 2026 the camera module segment made up 71.68% of revenue, so dependence on specific products and demand is high. With exports at 86.50%, sales can move with overseas customers' capital spending and exchange rates. Customer diversification and product-level adoption must be checked in disclosures and results.

New business execution risk

The semiconductor glass-substrate cutting equipment is targeted for delivery next year, and entry into semiconductor test equipment appears only as a target in the value enhancement plan. New order reports alone do not establish when results will be reflected. Production, delivery, inspection and revenue recognition must be confirmed through disclosures.

Overall view

Top Engineering Company's disclosed results moved from a profit in 2023 back to losses in 2024 and 2025, so whether the Q1 2026 operating profit turnaround continues is the central question.

In Q1 2026, 71.68% of consolidated revenue came from camera modules, while the display segment accounted for only 3.57%, a gap between the company's image and its revenue mix.

Bullish factors include camera module and medium and large battery pack demand, wider OLED adoption in Chinese-speaking markets, cost-structure improvement and new business targets.

Bearish factors include wider 2025 losses, two straight quarterly revenue declines in Q2 and Q3 2026, a Q3 2026 net loss attributable to controlling shareholders, and a combined KRW 10.7 billion attributable net loss over the last four quarters.

The share price trades at a low level relative to book value, and a PBR improvement plan has been disclosed, but earnings-based multiples cannot be calculated because of losses.

The next checkpoints are Q4 and full-year 2026 results in the 2026 annual report, deliveries of glass-substrate cutting equipment, and whether operating profit and attributable results improve together in Q1 2027.
