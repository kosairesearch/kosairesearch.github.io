Report overview

Earnings Rebound Meets Expanded Shareholder Returns

KTis's controlling-interest net profit jumped 206.6% year-on-year to KRW 36.4 billion in 2025, largely driven by a one-off gain from divesting its digital advertising business, while quarterly operating profit has fluctuated through 2026 and a newly adopted shareholder-return policy has become a fresh variable.

02

Business structure

KTis was established in 2001 when KT spun off its 114 directory-assistance business, and the company listed on the KOSPI in 2010. Its largest shareholder, KT, holds a 29.30% stake.

Core operations consist of outsourced contact-center services, a distribution business selling KT's telecom products, and advertising and merchandise-sales operations.

In the 2025 revenue mix, KT customer-center operations and general contact-center operations accounted for 44.6% and 32.4% respectively, together reaching 77% of the total, while the distribution business contributed 18.7% and the 114 directory-assistance business 3.1%.

As the directory-assistance segment has shrunk to a single-digit share while the customer-center and contact-center segments have grown, Korea Financial News noted that some market participants have classified KTis as a call-center operator with limited growth prospects.

In 2025, the company acquired a 100% stake in HNC Network, a contact-center operator specializing in the financial sector, and completed the merger by year-end, adding financial-industry outsourcing capacity.

Headcount stood at 9,088 at the end of 2025, with a workforce skewed toward women given the nature of counseling operations.

On the competitive front, affiliate KTcs (058850) runs a similar call-center business within the KT group, and the AICC platform businesses pursued by the three telecom carriers are also cited as a broader competitive factor.

04

Earnings analysis

Revenue rose from KRW 535.8 billion in 2022 to KRW 593 billion in 2023 (up 10.7% year-on-year) and KRW 603.9 billion in 2024 (up 1.8%), before slipping to KRW 588.8 billion in 2025 (down 2.5%). Among 2022-2025, revenue was largest in 2024 and smallest in 2022.

Operating profit grew from KRW 19.5 billion in 2022 to KRW 20.5 billion in 2023 (up 5.0%), KRW 21 billion in 2024 (up 2.3%), and KRW 24.7 billion in 2025 (up 17.9%), with the operating margin rising from 3.5-3.6% in 2022-2024 to 4.2% in 2025.

Operating profit was largest in 2025 and smallest in 2022 over the same period. Controlling-interest net profit fell from KRW 15.9 billion in 2022 to KRW 13.9 billion in 2023 (down 12.5%) and KRW 11.9 billion in 2024 (down 14.8%), before rebounding sharply to KRW 36.4 billion in 2025 (up 206.6%).

It was smallest in 2024 and largest in 2025 among the four years. The company cited a non-operating gain from spinning off and selling its digital advertising business as the main driver of the net-profit surge.

On a quarterly basis, second-quarter 2026 revenue reached KRW 155.4 billion, up 0.3% from the prior quarter's KRW 154.9 billion and up 22.3% from KRW 127.1 billion a year earlier.

Operating profit was KRW 7.13 billion, up 36.6% quarter-on-quarter from KRW 5.22 billion but down 6.5% from KRW 7.62 billion a year earlier, while controlling-interest net profit came to KRW 5.79 billion, up 21.5% from KRW 4.77 billion the prior quarter and up 5.6% from KRW 5.48 billion a year earlier.

Controlling-interest net profit over the last four quarters (Q3 2025-Q2 2026) totaled KRW 36.9 billion.

On the balance sheet, the debt ratio rose to 119.3% in 2023 and 125.8% in 2024 before dropping sharply to 88.6% in 2025, with total equity of KRW 238.5 billion at year-end 2025 entirely attributable to controlling shareholders.

Operating cash flow grew from KRW 50.6 billion in 2023 to KRW 61.3 billion in 2024 before falling to KRW 36.2 billion in 2025, meaning cash generation did not rise in proportion to the reported profit increase.

05

Industry analysis

South Korea's contact-center industry appears to be reshaped by two parallel trends: the spread of AICC and growing demand related to security-incident response. The AICC market itself has grown at an average annual rate of 23.7% since 2020 and is projected to keep expanding through 2030.

Within the KT group, KTis and affiliate KTcs are both seen as having leaned on AICC adoption and business diversification as growth drivers.

However, in the first half of 2026 KTcs posted a 1.9% year-on-year decline in consolidated revenue, and its second-quarter operating profit fell 24.7% year-on-year, underscoring sizable quarter-to-quarter swings even within the group.

The three telecom carriers—SK Telecom, KT, and LG Uplus—are each upgrading their AICC services using proprietary voice and language technology to expand market share. KT has broadened its AICC brand, A'Sen, to include subscription-based offerings as it upgrades its group-wide customer-center business.

As AI handles a growing share of contact volume, traditional labor-intensive call-center operators are facing pressure to adapt their revenue structures.

06

Outlook

In the second half of 2025, KTis acquired and merged with HNC Network, a contact-center operator specializing in the financial sector, reinforcing its financial-industry outsourcing capacity.

AICC has already been deployed in the 114 directory-assistance service, reportedly handling a substantial share of call volume year-round. In a September 2026 board meeting, the company formalized its first mid-term shareholder-return policy for 2026-2028, setting 40% of adjusted net income as the payout base.

Under the policy, cash dividends are paid first, with remaining funds used to buy back and cancel treasury shares, and starting in 2027 the company plans to move to semi-annual dividends by adding a quarterly payout alongside the year-end dividend.

At the same meeting, the board decided to cancel 3,397,048 of the 4,229,048 treasury shares it holds—equal to 9.76% of total shares outstanding—on October 30, 2026, while keeping the remainder as compensation reserves for employees.

This treasury-share cleanup is seen as part of a broader capital-policy shift playing out simultaneously across listed KT-group affiliates such as KTcs and KT NASMedia.

The company plans to recalculate and execute its shareholder-return scale every February from 2027 through 2029, meaning actual execution records will serve as the key gauge of the policy's durability going forward.

08

Bull factors

Strengthened Shareholder-Return Policy

In September 2026 the board set 40% of adjusted net income as the payout base for the first time and decided to cancel treasury shares equal to 9.76% of shares outstanding on October 30. Starting in 2027, a quarterly dividend will be added to the year-end payout, moving to a semi-annual system.

Building on a record of 21 consecutive fiscal years of year-end dividends, the formalized payout ratio and minimum dividend floor have made shareholder returns more predictable.

Improved Balance Sheet and Margin Recovery

The debt ratio fell sharply to 88.6% in 2025 from 125.8% in 2024, and year-end 2025 total equity of KRW 238.5 billion was entirely attributable to controlling shareholders. The operating margin also improved to 4.2% in 2025 from the mid-3% range in 2022-2024. Disposal of non-core businesses appears to have contributed to the expanded financial headroom.

Diversification via AICC and Financial-Sector Outsourcing

AICC has already been deployed at the 114 directory-assistance center, reportedly handling a substantial share of call processing, and in 2025 the company acquired and merged with HNC Network, a financial-sector-focused contact-center operator, to add financial-industry capacity.

Amid industry-wide reshaping of call centers around AICC, the company is pursuing growth drivers alongside similar KT-group affiliates.

09

Bear factors

Structural Slowdown Risk in Core Revenue Lines

KT customer-center operations and contact-center services, which together account for about 77% of revenue, could face structural pressure from declining simple call volumes as AICC spreads. The directory-assistance segment has already shrunk to just over 3% of total revenue.

Given the organization's labor-intensive structure, it remains uncertain whether AI transition will directly translate into revenue growth.

Thin Operating Margin and High SG&A Structure

Even after improving in 2025, the operating margin remained at just 4.2%, limiting profit generation relative to revenue. The labor-intensive, counseling-centered business structure keeps SG&A costs structurally high given the large wage component. This makes it difficult for revenue growth to translate directly into profit growth.

One-Off Nature of the 2025 Net-Profit Surge

While 2025 controlling-interest net profit rose 206.6% year-on-year, the company itself cited a non-operating gain from the digital-advertising divestiture as the main driver. Second-quarter 2026 operating profit, at KRW 7.13 billion, actually fell 6.5% year-on-year, pointing to a mixed trend in core profitability.

The direction of recurring earnings power, excluding one-off gains, will require several more quarters of observation to assess.

10

Risk factors

Dependence on the KT Group Structure

Majority shareholder KT holds a 29.30% stake, and KT customer-center operations, the core revenue driver, are effectively based on an outsourcing contract with the parent. Changes in contract terms or the parent's outsourcing strategy could directly affect KTis's revenue structure. Aligning with group-wide capital policy may also constrain the individual company's financial flexibility.

Workforce Structure and Cost Risk

Out of 9,088 employees at the end of 2025, a substantial portion are engaged in counseling work, making the organization labor-intensive. Minimum-wage increases or changes to labor regulations can directly affect SG&A costs.

Workforce reallocation driven by AICC adoption could generate costs or friction related to employment-structure adjustments.

Execution Risk of the Shareholder-Return Policy

The company stated in its disclosure that the shareholder-return policy and its detailed implementation could change depending on business conditions and market circumstances.

The scheduled treasury-share cancellation date and the 2027 quarterly-dividend plan still require further board resolutions and shareholder-meeting approvals before being finalized. The fact that adjusted net income itself can swing significantly due to one-off items also reduces the predictability of the payout scale.

12

Overall view

KTis's 2025 controlling-interest net profit rose 206.6% year-on-year to KRW 36.4 billion, but a substantial portion stemmed from a one-off gain tied to the digital-advertising divestiture, while operating profit itself grew a more moderate 17.9% to KRW 24.7 billion.

Heading into 2026, quarterly revenue has grown at double-digit rates year-on-year, yet second-quarter operating profit actually declined from the prior year, leaving core profitability without a clear direction so far.

On the balance sheet, the debt ratio improved to 88.6% in 2025 from 125.8% in 2024, and in September 2026 the company unveiled a mid-term shareholder-return policy paying out 40% of adjusted net income alongside a large treasury-share cancellation plan.

On the other hand, KT customer-center and contact-center operations, which make up about 77% of revenue, are exposed to structural changes from shrinking call volumes as AICC spreads, and the operating margin remains in the low-4% range.

Since the company itself has stated that the shareholder-return policy could change depending on business conditions, the next checkpoints are whether the October 30 treasury-share cancellation and the subsequent quarterly-dividend rollout proceed as planned.

The one-off nature of the 2025 profit surge should be weighed together with the trajectory of recurring profitability from 2026 onward.
