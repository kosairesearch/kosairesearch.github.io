Report overview

KTis Expands Shareholder Returns Amid AI Shift

KTis saw net income jump in 2025 on a one-off gain from the divestiture of its digital advertising business, and it has since formalized its capital allocation framework through a new mid-term shareholder return policy and a large treasury share cancellation.

  1. 1

    2025 consolidated net income attributable to owners rose sharply to KRW 36.37 billion from KRW 11.86 billion a year earlier, mainly driven by a non-operating gain from the spin-off and sale of the digital advertising business.

  2. 2

    On September 23, the board adopted its first mid-term shareholder return policy, setting 40% of adjusted net income as the payout base for 2026-2028, and plans to cancel 3,397,048 treasury shares (9.76% of shares outstanding, over 80% of its treasury holdings) on October 30.

  3. 3

    Operating margin has gradually improved from 3.6% in 2022 to 4.2% in 2025.

  4. 4

    Revenue is anchored by the KT customer center business (44.6%) and third-party contact center outsourcing (32.4%), with AI contact center (AICC) transformation positioned as the medium-term growth driver.

  5. 5

02

Business structure

KTis was established in 2001 when KT spun off its 114 directory assistance business, and it listed on the KOSPI in 2010 as KT group's customer service subsidiary.

Its operations consist of running the KT customer center for the parent's product and service inquiries, operating outsourced contact centers (BPO) for external clients across finance, healthcare, public sector and retail, plus a retail distribution business and the 114 directory assistance service.

Based on the most recent disclosed breakdown, the KT customer center accounts for 44.6% of revenue, general (third-party) contact center operations 32.4%, the retail business 18.7%, while directory assistance has fallen to just 3.1%.

KTis operates contact centers for more than 120 institutions and is combining its operational experience with KT group's AI technology to pursue a transition toward becoming an 'AX Contact Platform Company.' Notably, it has embedded KT's cloud-based AICC solution, the A'Cen Cloud consulting application, into its own contact center operations, commercializing AI call summarization and chatbot/voicebot features.

Its former digital advertising business (including elevator advertising) was spun off and sold during 2025 and is no longer part of current operations.

On the competitive front, Ubase Group, the country's largest BPO operator, along with fellow KT group affiliate KTcs and Hyosung ITX, compete in the domestic contact center and AICC market.

The KT customer center business provides a stable revenue base rooted in a captive contract structure with the parent, but this also comes with a structurally high degree of dependence on KT.

04

Earnings analysis

KTis's consolidated revenue rose for three straight years, from KRW 535.78 billion in 2022 to KRW 592.96 billion in 2023 and KRW 603.90 billion in 2024, before slipping slightly to KRW 588.76 billion in 2025.

Operating profit, however, climbed steadily from KRW 19.54 billion in 2022 to KRW 24.74 billion in 2025, with operating margin improving from 3.6% to 4.2% over the same period.

Net income attributable to owners declined from KRW 15.92 billion in 2022 to KRW 11.86 billion in 2024 before surging to KRW 36.37 billion in 2025, a jump attributable to a non-operating gain from spinning off and selling the digital advertising business during the year.

This one-off effect pulled the officially disclosed 2025 dividend payout ratio down to 11.8%, though the normalized payout ratio excluding that gain is estimated at 33.9%. The debt ratio improved markedly as well, falling from 125.8% in 2024 to 88.6% in 2025.

Operating cash flow, however, declined to KRW 36.21 billion in 2025 from KRW 61.27 billion in 2024, suggesting much of the net income increase came from a disposal gain with limited cash impact.

On a quarterly basis, revenue was KRW 127.07 billion with operating profit of KRW 7.62 billion and owner net income of KRW 5.48 billion in Q2 2025; by Q1 2026, revenue had grown to KRW 154.90 billion but operating profit eased to KRW 5.22 billion and owner net income to KRW 4.77 billion.

Q2 2026 then showed a recovery, with revenue of KRW 155.36 billion, operating profit of KRW 7.13 billion and owner net income of KRW 5.79 billion, both profit lines rebounding from the prior quarter.

05

Industry analysis

Korea's contact center and BPO industry is being rapidly reshaped by generative AI adoption.

According to an industry column, the domestic contact center workforce has fallen 6.05% over two years to roughly 106,725 people, as AI absorbs simple repetitive inquiries while specialized counseling skills and AI utilization capability become the new competitive benchmark.

Field experts, however, argue that AI is moving toward collaboration with human agents rather than wholesale replacement.

In practice, even when AI handles simple inquiries upfront, completion rates remain only 20-30%, with the remaining cases still routed to human agents, and the total number of domestic call center workers, around 200,000, has not meaningfully declined.

Ubase Group, the country's largest BPO operator, has pursued a "hybrid AICC" strategy that clearly divides roles between AI and human agents while building its own small language model (sLM) to internalize the technology.

Against this backdrop, BPO companies are shifting from an "OEM-style" model centered on staffing supply toward an "ODM-style" model that co-designs AI systems alongside operations.

As a KT group affiliate, KTis is pursuing its AICC transition on the back of stable parent-company volume, positioning it somewhat differently from purely private-sector BPO operators such as Ubase Group.

06

Outlook

KTis's board approved its first mid-term shareholder return policy for the 2026-2028 fiscal years at its September 23 meeting. The policy sets 40% of adjusted net income as the return funding base, ten percentage points above the roughly 30% level the company had customarily applied in the past.

The return method prioritizes cash dividends first, with any remaining funds used to buy back and cancel treasury shares, and starting in 2027 the company plans to move to a semi-annual dividend system paying out twice a year alongside the regular year-end dividend.

At the same board meeting, the company decided to cancel 3,397,048 of its 4,229,048 treasury shares (9.76% of total shares outstanding, 80.4% of treasury holdings) on October 30, a move that reduces total shares outstanding without reducing paid-in capital.

The remaining 832,000 treasury shares are earmarked for employee compensation purposes. The company plans to calculate and execute specific shareholder return amounts every February from 2027 through 2029, with details to be finalized through future board resolutions and shareholder meeting approvals.

On the operating side, KTis continues to pursue efficiency gains by upgrading its AICC solutions to shorten post-call processing time, and management has framed AI as an "opportunity, not a crisis" for the contact center business, outlining a roadmap evolving from AICC to an AI-transformation (AX) contact center and, longer term, toward autonomous ("agentic") AICC.

08

Bull factors

New Shareholder Return Policy and Large Treasury Share Cancellation

KTis has announced its first mid-term shareholder return policy for 2026-2028, fixing 40% of adjusted net income as the payout base. It plans to cancel more than 80% of its treasury shares on October 30 and will shift to a semi-annual dividend system from 2027, improving dividend predictability.

This move aligns with a broader trend of KT group listed affiliates, including KT itself, KTcs and KT NAS Media, stepping up treasury share cancellation and shareholder returns.

Improving Operating Margin Trend

Operating margin has steadily improved from 3.6% in 2022 to 4.2% in 2025. Operational efficiency gains, including shorter post-call processing times enabled by upgraded AICC solutions, underpin this margin improvement. The fact that profitability metrics improved even as revenue stagnated can be read as a positive signal.

Stable Captive Revenue Base

KT customer center operations, which account for 44.6% of total revenue, provide a stable captive revenue base. This is complemented by external contact center operations serving more than 120 institutions across finance, healthcare, public sector and retail, diversifying the client base.

The debt ratio also improved markedly, falling from 125.8% in 2024 to 88.6% in 2025, strengthening balance sheet stability.

09

Bear factors

Stalling Revenue Growth

Consolidated revenue declined year-on-year to KRW 588.76 billion in 2025 from KRW 603.90 billion in 2024, marking the first revenue contraction after three consecutive years of growth. Securing additional growth drivers for the core contact center business remains a key challenge.

One-Off Nature of the 2025 Net Income Surge

While net income attributable to owners rose sharply in 2025, this was primarily driven by a non-operating gain from the spin-off and sale of the digital advertising business.

The normalized payout ratio excluding this effect is estimated at 33.9%, making it difficult to assume the earnings level is sustainable based on the headline figures alone.

Operating cash flow also declined to KRW 36.21 billion in 2025 from KRW 61.27 billion in 2024, suggesting much of the net income increase stemmed from a disposal gain with limited cash impact.

Structural Burden from AI Transition

The contact center industry is undergoing structural change as AI adoption reduces demand for simple consultations, with the domestic workforce already down 6.05% over two years.

As AICC investment and labor cost pressures occur simultaneously, there may be a time lag between investment and monetization that warrants attention.

10

Risk factors

Parent Company Dependence and Captive Structure Risk

With 44.6% of revenue derived from operating the parent KT's customer centers, results can be affected by changes in KT's business strategy or outsourcing policy. The market has at times viewed this captive-market dependence as a valuation discount factor. Changes in KT group-level capital policy could also become linked to KTis's own return policy.

Labor-Intensive Business Structure Risk

The contact center business is labor-intensive, requiring a large consulting workforce, and cost burdens can rise depending on minimum wage increases or labor supply conditions. With the domestic workforce having shrunk over the past two years, competition to secure specialized consulting talent has also intensified.

Uncertainty Over AI Investment Monetization

While investment in AICC solutions and system upgrades continues, it has been noted that the actual revenue contribution from the AICC business is difficult for outside observers to verify clearly. If AI technology competition intensifies, the timing of monetization relative to investment could also be delayed.

12

Overall view

KTis saw net income rise sharply in 2025 due to a one-off gain from the sale of its digital advertising business, even as core revenue declined slightly year-on-year and operating margin showed gradual improvement.

The recent establishment of a mid-term shareholder return policy and the decision to cancel a large block of treasury shares can be viewed as positive developments that enhance the predictability of capital allocation, though their actual execution and continuity need to be verified over coming quarters.

On the business side, stable captive revenue centered on the KT customer center and efforts to improve profitability through AICC transformation are proceeding in parallel, while the broader call center industry is in a transitional phase shifting toward AI-human collaboration.

Dependence on the parent company, a labor-intensive cost structure, and a potential lag between AI investment and monetization are variables that warrant continued attention.

On valuation, the shares have continued to trade at a discount to net asset value, and interpretations of this discount vary among market participants and media.

Investors will want to continue monitoring scheduled events, including the October treasury share cancellation, the November Q3 earnings disclosure, and the finalization of shareholder return amounts in early 2027, to track the pace of earnings normalization and the execution of capital policy.
