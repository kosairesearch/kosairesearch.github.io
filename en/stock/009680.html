Motonic: Profit Dip Amid Hyundai Group Reliance

Motonic's operating profit fell 22.1% in 2025 within a business heavily dependent on Hyundai Motor Group, and its operating profit also declined 15.9% year on year in the third quarter of 2026, while shareholder returns continued through dividends and a treasury share cancellation.

  1. 1

    Annual sales in 2025 were KRW 279.7 billion, down 3.8% from the prior year, operating profit was KRW 18.5 billion, down 22.1%, and profit attributable to controlling shareholders was KRW 27 billion, down 16.6%.

  2. 2

    Third-quarter 2026 sales were KRW 64.9 billion, down 4.2% year on year, and operating profit was KRW 3.74 billion, down 15.9%. Nine-month cumulative operating profit rose 7.9% to KRW 13.9 billion, according to a news report dated November 16, 2026, on the third-quarter filing.

  3. 3

    Hyundai Motor Group affiliates accounted for 73.9% of sales in the third quarter of 2024, down from 80% in 2022 but still above 70%.

  4. 4

    The 2025 dividend totaled KRW 17.1 billion, up 31.7% from 2024, with a payout ratio of 63.3%. In March 2026 the company disclosed the cancellation of 6,316,906 treasury common shares, with a planned amount of about KRW 13.8 billion.

  5. 5

    On October 22, 2026 Motonic signed an MOU with DGIST and plans joint research on robot actuators and cooling systems for AI data centers; the new business remains at the planning stage.

02

Business structure

Motonic was founded in 1974 and operates as a single business segment in which automotive parts account for more than 90% of both sales and total assets. Its products fall into fuel system parts, electronic parts, and powertrain parts, with sales shares of 52.45%, 28.48%, and 18.67% respectively as of December 2024.

The fuel system line includes liquid-fuel LPI systems and throttle bodies, and the range has widened to OPU hybrid transmission oil pump controllers and fuel cell electric vehicle (FCEV) parts.

Hyundai Motor Group affiliates are the core customers, and the group's share of sales fell from 80% in 2022 to 74.2% in 2023 and 73.9% in the third quarter of 2024, though it remains above 70%.

On a 2025 consolidated basis, domestic sales were 68.9% and exports 31.1%; fuel system parts were 96.1% domestic, while electronic and powertrain parts each exported more than 60%. R&D spending in 2025 was KRW 7.055 billion, or 2.52% of sales.

Among 140 listed auto parts companies on a 2025 annual basis, Motonic ranked 18th by operating margin (Alphasquare). A 2024 report noted limited expansion beyond auto parts. In October 2026 the company signed an MOU with DGIST and plans joint research in robotics and AI data center applications.

04

Earnings analysis

Annual sales were KRW 223.9 billion in 2022, KRW 244.3 billion in 2023, KRW 290.6 billion in 2024, and KRW 279.7 billion in 2025, with 2024 the largest year and 2022 the smallest. Sales rose 19.0% in 2024 and fell 3.8% in 2025.

Operating profit went from KRW 13.8 billion in 2022 to KRW 15.2 billion in 2023 (up 9.9%) and KRW 23.8 billion in 2024 (up 57.0%), before falling 22.1% to KRW 18.5 billion in 2025. The operating margin was 6.2% in 2022, 6.2% in 2023, 8.2% in 2024, and 6.6% in 2025.

The 2024 gain reflected higher fuel system part prices, which the third-quarter 2024 report showed rising 47.7% compared with 2023. Profit attributable to controlling shareholders was KRW 21.5 billion in 2022, KRW 27.5 billion in 2023, KRW 32.4 billion in 2024, and KRW 27 billion in 2025.

The 2023 figure included a one-off gain on disposal of an investment in a subsidiary tied to the liquidation of the China unit. The cost of sales ratio rose from 83.39% in 2024 to 86.07% in 2025 (FnGuide), squeezing margins.

Quarterly sales were KRW 67.8 billion in the third quarter of 2025, KRW 69.3 billion in the fourth quarter (up 2.3% quarter on quarter), KRW 68.3 billion in the first quarter of 2026, KRW 73.7 billion in the second quarter (up 7.8%), and KRW 64.9 billion in the third quarter (down 11.8% quarter on quarter and down 4.2% year on year).

Third-quarter 2026 operating profit was KRW 3.74 billion, down 15.9% year on year. Profit attributable to controlling shareholders in that quarter was KRW 5.72 billion, down 13.9%, and the last four quarters total KRW 25.8 billion.

According to a news report on the third-quarter 2026 filing, nine-month cumulative operating profit rose 7.9% to KRW 13.9 billion, while nine-month sales fell 1.6% to KRW 206.9 billion.

05

Industry analysis

Motonic's results move with vehicle production, so its earnings track the production and sales flow of Hyundai Motor Group. The group's share of its sales fell from 80% in 2022 to 73.9% in the third quarter of 2024 but remains above 70%, so customer diversification is still at an early stage.

Fuel system parts were 96.1% domestic in 2025, which leaves them directly exposed to domestic vehicle output and model mix. LPI systems and similar liquid-fuel products serve internal combustion vehicles, so how demand for this group evolves as vehicles electrify is a key point to monitor.

Electronic and powertrain parts each exported more than 60%, making them more sensitive to overseas vehicle production and exchange rates.

On a 2025 annual basis (Alphasquare), Motonic ranked 18th by operating margin among 140 auto parts companies but 78th by sales, so profitability is in the upper group while scale is in the lower half.

In the same dataset, the industry average debt ratio was 185.35%, compared with Motonic's 9.88%, which highlights a wide gap in balance sheet headroom. Competition from overseas parts makers persists in electronic and powertrain areas, leaving broader customer diversification as an open task.

06

Outlook

The company has laid out three directions in its disclosures. First, in its corporate value-up plan disclosed on April 24, 2026, it set a goal of expanding growth potential by identifying new growth engines, pursuing strategic investment, and keeping a stable dividend policy within distributable profit.

The plan notes that the company qualifies as a high-dividend company under the Restriction of Special Taxation Act.

Second, the 2025 dividend totaled KRW 17.1 billion, up 31.7% from KRW 13 billion in 2024, and the company said its dividend level and shareholder return policy will be reviewed in light of business results and shareholder value.

Third, on October 22, 2026 the company signed an MOU with DGIST on promoting AI transformation in the semiconductor industry and plans joint research on robot actuators and cooling systems for AI data centers.

The new business is at the research-planning stage, and no commercialization timing or scale has been disclosed. At the March 2026 annual general meeting, an amendment to the articles of incorporation passed, and a treasury share cancellation was disclosed the same month.

The next checkpoint is the annual operating margin in the 2026 annual report, which is due by the end of March 2027.

08

Bull factors

Expanded shareholder returns

The 2025 dividend totaled KRW 17.1 billion, up 31.7% from KRW 13 billion in 2024, with a payout ratio of 63.3%. In its April 2026 corporate value-up plan the company said it would keep a stable dividend policy within distributable profit and recorded itself as a high-dividend company.

In March 2026 it disclosed the cancellation of 6,316,906 treasury common shares, with a planned amount of about KRW 13.8 billion.

Balance sheet headroom

Total equity at the end of 2025 was KRW 482.8 billion, all attributable to controlling shareholders. Total liabilities were KRW 47.7 billion, and the debt ratio stood at 9.9% in 2025.

On a 2025 annual basis, the industry average debt ratio cited by Alphasquare was 185.35%, so borrowing burden is low relative to the sector. This headroom provides the financial basis for reviewing the strategic investment and dividend policy the company has described.

New business exploration begins

On October 22, 2026 the company signed an MOU with DGIST to promote AI transformation in the semiconductor industry. Under the agreement, joint research is planned on robot actuators and cooling systems for AI data centers.

The company said it would build on its automotive parts development and mass-production experience and precision control technology. Its April 2026 value-up plan also cited the search for new growth engines as a goal.

Bear factors

Dependence on Hyundai Motor Group

As of the third quarter of 2024, Hyundai Motor Group affiliates accounted for 73.9% of sales, still concentrated in one customer group. Parts orders follow vehicle output, so the group's production and sales flow feeds directly into results. The share has trended down from 80% in 2022 but remains above 70%.

Slowing quarterly profit in 2026

Third-quarter 2026 sales were KRW 64.9 billion, down 11.8% quarter on quarter and down 4.2% year on year. Operating profit was KRW 3.74 billion, down 15.9% year on year, and profit attributable to controlling shareholders was KRW 5.72 billion, down 13.9%.

Cumulative nine-month operating profit rose, but the third quarter alone showed declines in both sales and profit.

Cost ratio and product mix

After the price increases that lifted 2024, the cost of sales ratio rose to 86.07% in 2025 from 83.39% in 2024. Fuel system parts were 96.1% domestic in 2025, leaving them directly exposed to domestic vehicle production and model mix.

LPI and related liquid-fuel parts serve internal combustion vehicles, so the effect of vehicle electrification on demand could become a weakness.

10

Risk factors

Customer concentration

With Hyundai Motor Group affiliates still above 70% of sales, production adjustments or model changes at the group would pass directly into results. Customer diversification is under way, but the disclosed decline in the group's share has so far been gradual, from 80% in 2022 to 73.9% in the third quarter of 2024.

Changes in the operating environment of the automakers therefore translate quickly into Motonic's sales and profit.

Cost and pricing terms

The 2024 price increases lifted profit, but in 2025 the cost of sales ratio rose to 86.07% and the operating margin fell to 6.6%. If cost inputs move out of line with contract pricing, margins could come under renewed pressure.

Whether the 2024 price effect was temporary or lasting can be judged from quarterly cost ratios in the periods ahead.

New business and shareholder-return variability

The new business with DGIST is at the joint research-planning stage, with no set timing or scale for commercialization. The value-up plan says dividends will be reviewed within distributable profit in light of business results and shareholder value, so dividend levels could change if profit declines. The 63.3% payout ratio in 2025 also depends on earnings levels.

Overall view

Motonic is an auto parts maker with high dependence on Hyundai Motor Group, and its 2025 annual sales, operating profit, and profit attributable to controlling shareholders all declined. Cumulative nine-month operating profit rose in 2026, but the third quarter alone showed declines in both sales and operating profit.

The operating margin fell from 8.2% in 2024 to 6.6% in 2025, with a higher cost of sales ratio of 86.07% appearing to have compressed it. The balance sheet shows a 9.9% debt ratio and KRW 482.8 billion in total equity, all attributable to controlling shareholders.

Shareholder returns continued through the 2025 dividend of KRW 17.1 billion, a 63.3% payout ratio, and the 2026 treasury share cancellation. The new growth initiatives remain at the joint research stage with DGIST. This report includes no investment rating, target price, or buy or sell opinion.
