Motonic, an LPG Parts Leader: Earnings Recovery Meets a Shareholder-Return Test

Motonic, an LPG fuel-system specialist closely tied to Hyundai Motor and Kia, saw 2025 earnings soften before recent quarterly margins showed renewed improvement, while its thick cash balance and potential changes to treasury-share policy are also drawing attention.

02

Business structure

Motonic was founded in 1974 as Changwon Carburetor Industry, listed on the Korea Exchange in 1994, and adopted its current name in 2002. The company operates its headquarters in Seoul along with production plants in Daegu and Seongju, and holds overseas subsidiaries Motonic India and Beijing Motonic.

Its core product is the LPI System, a liquid-phase LPG fuel-injection system, and the company was the first in the world to apply this technology to mass production. Revenue is led by fuel-system parts, followed by powertrain and electronic components.

Customers are almost entirely Hyundai Motor and Kia, supplied on an OEM basis, meaning the company's position as an in-group supplier to the automaker matters more than competition among independent parts makers.

Beyond the LPI System, the product lineup has expanded to future-mobility parts such as the oil-pump controller (OPU) for hybrid automatic transmissions and the Regulator, a high-pressure storage system component for fuel-cell electric vehicles.

The company holds 224 patents and focuses on securing cost reductions and new technologies from the earliest stages of new-vehicle design.

Given this structure, revenue is highly dependent on the two automakers, making the business model one where changes in their production and sales strategies are directly reflected in results.

04

Earnings analysis

Consolidated revenue in 2025 came to KRW 279.70 billion, down from KRW 290.60 billion in 2024, while operating profit also fell to KRW 18.54 billion from KRW 23.78 billion. As a result, the operating margin declined from 8.2% in 2024 to 6.6% in 2025.

Net profit attributable to owners was KRW 27.00 billion, down from KRW 32.36 billion in 2024, but still above the KRW 21.50 billion recorded in 2022 and KRW 27.48 billion in 2023.

However, 2025 operating cash flow surged to KRW 56.34 billion from KRW 24.34 billion in 2024, showing improved cash generation separate from the earnings trend.

On a quarterly basis, revenue and operating profit rose from KRW 71.20 billion and KRW 3.87 billion (a 5.4% margin) in 2Q25 to KRW 67.78 billion and KRW 4.45 billion (6.6%) in 3Q25, and further to KRW 69.33 billion and KRW 5.65 billion (8.2%) in 4Q25.

The pace moderated slightly in 1Q26 to KRW 68.32 billion in revenue and KRW 4.35 billion in operating profit (6.4%), before improving again in 2Q26 to KRW 73.66 billion in revenue and KRW 5.81 billion in operating profit (7.9%).

Taken together, margins fluctuated but showed a gradual recovery over the most recent four quarters (3Q25 through 2Q26), during which combined net profit attributable to owners totaled approximately KRW 26.72 billion.

This quarter-to-quarter volatility reflects the pattern typical of a parts supplier whose results move sensitively with automaker production schedules and cost factors.

05

Industry analysis

According to the Korea Automobile Manufacturers Association (KAMA), of the 1,368,337 domestic vehicles sold last year, LPG vehicles totaled 138,540 units, exceeding EV sales of 128,124 units and ranking third behind gasoline and hybrid models.

Hyundai offers LPG options on the Grandeur, Sonata, Avante, and Staria, while Kia offers LPG variants on the Sportage, K8, and K5, and this lineup directly links to demand for Motonic's core product, the LPI System.

The LPG industry association has proposed LPG hybrid taxis as a next-generation option and is developing them with a target of mass production in 2027, which could become a new source of demand.

At the same time, the government's mid-term LPG utilization and supply policy projects that the share of transport-use LPG demand will decline as EV and hydrogen vehicle adoption grows and LPG vehicle numbers fall, while industrial and petrochemical use expands, raising concerns about a structural plateau in LPG vehicle parts demand.

In terms of competitive positioning, Motonic is known as the leading domestic supplier of LPI systems to Hyundai and Kia, so shifts in the automakers' powertrain strategy matter more than direct competition among independent parts suppliers.

Changes in Hyundai and Kia's domestic production volumes are cited as a factor with a direct impact on Motonic's revenue. Overall, the industry appears to be in a phase where a short-term revival of interest in LPG vehicles is unfolding alongside a longer-term structural shift toward eco-friendly vehicles.

06

Outlook

Building on its 224 patents, the company continues to focus on cost reduction and new-technology adoption from the earliest stages of new-vehicle design, and continues developing future-mobility parts such as the Regulator for fuel-cell electric vehicles and the OPU for hybrid vehicles.

A key point to watch is whether the LPG industry association's 2027 mass-production target for LPG hybrid taxis materializes into a new source of demand.

The government has continued LPG-industry support measures, including cutting the tariff quota rate on crude oil used for LPG production to zero in the second half of 2026, maintaining a cost-favorable policy environment.

Financially, observers have noted that the low debt ratio (9.9% in 2025) and thick cash holdings give the company room to expand investment or shareholder-return policies.

However, whether this capacity actually translates into expanded dividends or further treasury-share retirement is something that needs to be confirmed through future board resolutions and disclosures.

The two automakers' production plans and LPG model sales trends remain the largest variable for revenue, and pressure to adjust the mid- to long-term business portfolio is likely to continue depending on the pace of the shift toward eco-friendly vehicles.

Future results may continue to fluctuate depending on quarterly automaker production schedules and the outcome of cost-reduction efforts.

08

Bull factors

Solid Financial Structure and Cash Capacity

The 2025 debt ratio stood at 9.9%, remaining low within the 6.7-9.9% range seen over the past four years, and Hana Securities noted in a past report that Motonic's cash and cash equivalents exceeded its market capitalization.

Such thick cash holdings have been cited as a factor that could support both investment capacity and future expansion of shareholder-return policy. 2025 operating cash flow also rose sharply to KRW 56.3 billion from the prior year, indicating improved cash-generation capability as well.

Renewed Interest in LPG Vehicles and Lineup Expansion

Last year, domestic LPG vehicle sales exceeded EV sales and ranked third in the market, drawing renewed attention, while Hyundai and Kia's flagship models continue to offer LPG options.

The LPG industry association is developing LPG hybrid taxis with a 2027 mass-production target, which could become a new source of demand. This is a factor directly tied to demand for the LPI System, Motonic's core revenue source.

Potential Shift in Shareholder-Return Policy

Even after retiring a large portion of its treasury shares in early 2025, Motonic is still assessed to hold a relatively high proportion of treasury stock, and the regulatory environment is also changing as the mandatory treasury-stock retirement rule under revised commercial law took effect for the first time from 2026.

Against this backdrop, further retirement or changes to dividend policy have been cited as factors that could affect per-share metrics. However, the specifics of implementation and timing need to be confirmed through future board resolutions and disclosures.

09

Bear factors

Dependence on Two Automakers

Because revenue depends almost entirely on OEM supply to Hyundai Motor and Kia, changes in the two automakers' production and sales plans have a direct effect on results. This also means the company has a relatively limited independent demand base. Fluctuations in automaker production schedules have been a primary driver of quarter-to-quarter revenue swings.

Concerns Over Mid-Term Stagnation in LPG Transport Demand

The government's mid-term LPG utilization and supply policy projects that the share of transport-use LPG demand will decline as EV and hydrogen vehicle adoption grows and the number of LPG vehicles falls.

Industry observers have also noted that a weakening butane (LPG vehicle fuel) demand base is possible if new model launches remain limited. This is a structural variable directly tied to the mid- to long-term demand base for the LPI System, Motonic's core business.

Quarterly Margin Volatility

Operating margins fluctuated between 6.4% and 8.2% over the most recent four quarters, and the full-year 2025 operating margin also fell to 6.6% from 8.2% in 2024. This shows that margins can move sensitively depending on external factors such as costs, exchange rates, and automaker production schedules.

Whether the earnings recovery continues stably is something that needs to be verified through future quarterly results.

10

Risk factors

Business Concentration Risk

With revenue concentrated on just two customers, Hyundai Motor and Kia, results could be significantly affected if their production strategies or parts-sourcing policies change. This is a structural risk typical of parts suppliers heavily dependent on a single automotive group. It is also worth noting that diversification toward new customers has progressed only to a limited extent.

Industry Transition Risk

If the shift toward eco-friendly vehicles such as EVs and hydrogen vehicles accelerates, mid- to long-term demand for internal-combustion and LPG fuel systems could structurally decline.

While the company is expanding its portfolio into FCEV and HEV parts, it remains to be seen how much these new businesses can offset existing LPI System revenue. Pressure to restructure the business portfolio could intensify depending on the pace of the industry transition.

Policy and Regulatory Variables

Ongoing changes to shareholder-return-related regulations, including the mandatory treasury-stock retirement rule effective from 2026 and the total-dividend-amount criterion under the high-dividend tax incentive, could affect the company's dividend and retirement policy decisions.

Changes to government LPG-industry support measures, such as the crude-oil tariff quota, could also affect the cost structure. These policy variables warrant attention as external factors outside the company's direct control.

Overall view

Motonic is an LPG fuel-system specialist closely tied to Hyundai Motor and Kia; while revenue and operating profit declined in 2025 compared with 2024, operating margins showed a gradual recovery with fluctuations over the most recent four quarters.

The low debt ratio and thick cash holdings are factors viewed positively for financial stability, supported further by the rise in 2025 operating cash flow.

On the industry side, LPG vehicle sales have drawn renewed attention by surpassing EV sales, while concerns persist over a mid- to long-term plateau in transport-use LPG demand amid the shift toward eco-friendly vehicles.

On shareholder returns, a high proportion of treasury shares remains even after a large retirement in early 2025, and the mandatory treasury-stock retirement rule effective from 2026 could become a variable in future policy direction.

The structure in which revenue depends almost entirely on two automakers creates both stability and vulnerability for the business, making it worth watching future quarterly results and shareholder-return disclosures together.
