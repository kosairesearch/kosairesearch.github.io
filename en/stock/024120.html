02

Business structure

KB Autosys makes brake pads and linings, the friction materials that slow and stop vehicles. It supplies automakers as an original equipment (OE) supplier and also serves the replacement parts market (AM).

According to a Daejeon Ilbo report, its major customers have broadened from Hyundai and Kia to GM, Stellantis and Ford. WiseReport data shows that in December 2025, domestic sales accounted for 54.45% of pad and lining product sales and exports for 45.55%.

The same Daejeon Ilbo report put the export share of total sales above 65%, so the two measures use different bases and should be read separately.

Production is based in Asan, Korea, with overseas plants in Georgia in the United States (completed in 2022 with a USD 38 million investment), Zhangjiagang in China and Chennai in India.

The company describes itself as the first in Korea to develop asbestos-free friction material and now mass-produces copper-free products for domestic and North American markets. R&D spending in 2025 was KRW 9,341 million, or 4.34% of sales. Among listed auto parts companies, its defining feature is that it focuses on friction materials.

04

Earnings analysis

On a reported basis, annual revenue rose from KRW 178 billion in 2022 to KRW 203.7 billion in 2023 (up 14.4% year on year), KRW 210.2 billion in 2024 (up 3.2%) and KRW 215 billion in 2025 (up 2.3%).

Revenue increased for three consecutive years from 2023 to 2025, and 2025 was the largest year of the 2022 to 2025 period. Growth slowed to low single digits in 2024 and 2025, so profit gains came more from margin improvement than from sales growth.

Operating profit went from KRW 2.44 billion in 2022 to KRW 4.04 billion in 2023 (up 65.9%), KRW 4.63 billion in 2024 (up 14.6%) and KRW 8.53 billion in 2025 (up 84.1%). The operating margin rose from 1.4% in 2022 to 2.0%, 2.2% and 4.0% in 2025.

According to a Digital Today report, the company attributed the 2025 change mainly to margin gains from a favorable exchange rate environment for exports and improved profit at overseas subsidiaries.

Net profit attributable to controlling shareholders fell from KRW 1.1 billion in 2022 to KRW 1.06 billion in 2023 (down 3.5%), then rebounded to KRW 2.94 billion in 2024 (up 177.0%) and KRW 3.9 billion in 2025 (up 32.4%).

Quarterly, the fourth quarter of 2025 stood out: operating profit of KRW 3.66 billion (up 402.4% from the prior quarter) and controlling-shareholder net profit of KRW 2.67 billion made up a large share of the full-year total.

Operating profit then fell to KRW 1.54 billion in the first quarter of 2026 (down 58.0% from the prior quarter) and recovered to KRW 1.87 billion in the second quarter (up 21.4%). In the third quarter of 2026 the company posted an operating loss of KRW 268 million.

Third-quarter revenue was KRW 53.8 billion, down 8.9% from the prior quarter but up 2.2% year on year. The net loss attributable to controlling shareholders was KRW 5.73 billion, far larger than the operating loss, which suggests non-operating items may have widened the loss.

Net profit attributable to controlling shareholders over the latest four quarters totaled KRW 1.27 billion. Operating cash flow went from negative KRW 5.75 billion in 2022 to KRW 5.19 billion in 2023, KRW 8.1 billion in 2024 and KRW 5.4 billion in 2025, staying positive after 2023.

The debt-to-equity ratio rose from 102.4% in 2024 to 115.4% in 2025, with total liabilities of KRW 137.5 billion and total equity of KRW 119.1 billion at year-end 2025.

05

Industry analysis

Brake pad demand depends on automaker production volumes and whether a model adopts the company's parts (OE), while replacement demand (AM) tracks the vehicle parc and service cycles. Because OE awards are central to this company's revenue, new model adoption shapes its medium-term volumes.

On materials, the company points to its copper-free products, now mass-produced in Korea and North America, as a response to environmental regulation. A Korea Economic Daily report said the company is using eco-friendly friction materials to expand its share of the electric vehicle market.

EV growth could change braking patterns and pad wear, which makes it a variable for friction material demand. On trade, the company's US production is its main tool for managing tariff and logistics risk.

The exchange rate is also a key variable: the company cited a favorable rate environment for its 2025 margin gains, so a reversal could weigh on export margins. Listed peers in the same sector include Hyundai Mobis, HL Mando, Hankook Tire & Technology and Hyundai WIA, which are larger diversified parts makers.

With KRW 215 billion in annual revenue, KB Autosys is small by comparison, and it differentiates by concentrating on a single product category. Third-quarter 2026 revenue rose only 2.2% year on year, so automaker production trends remain a key variable for the next quarters.

Operating profit went from KRW 1.54 billion in the first quarter and KRW 1.87 billion in the second to an operating loss of KRW 268 million in the third, which interrupted the recovery in profitability.

On dividends, the payout for fiscal 2026 is expected to be set at the annual shareholders' meeting in March 2027. Whether the stable dividend policy holds despite the third-quarter loss is the checkpoint.

Bull factors

Earnings recovery

Overseas production base

Together with Zhangjiagang in China and Chennai in India, production is spread across several countries. Exports accounted for 45.55% of pad and lining product sales in December 2025.

Shareholder return policy

The corporate value-up plan disclosed on March 30, 2026 put a stable dividend policy at its center. The 2025 dividend of KRW 2.875 billion rose 25% from KRW 2.3 billion in 2024, and the payout ratio was reported at 73.8%. The company says it will pursue higher shareholder returns alongside profit-focused management.

Bear factors

Q3 2026 swing to loss

The third quarter of 2026 recorded an operating loss of KRW 268 million and a net loss attributable to controlling shareholders of KRW 5.73 billion. Operating profit of KRW 1.54 billion in the first quarter of 2026 was 58.0% below the fourth quarter of 2025, so the slowdown was visible in the quarterly trend. The reasons behind the swing to loss need to be confirmed in upcoming periodic reports.

Higher financial leverage

The debt-to-equity ratio rose from 102.3% in 2024 to 115.4% in 2025, and total liabilities were KRW 137.5 billion at year-end 2025. With liabilities up relative to KRW 119.1 billion of equity, swings in quarterly profit could weigh more heavily on the balance sheet. The capacity to absorb such pressure with earnings may be narrowing.

Dependence on exchange rates

The company explained its 2025 margin gains partly through a favorable exchange rate environment for exports. If the rate turns, export margins could narrow, so the durability of profit needs further checking. Operating cash flow also fell from KRW 8.1 billion in 2024 to KRW 5.4 billion in 2025.

Risk factors

Exchange rates and trade

High export exposure makes the company sensitive to exchange rates and tariffs. Exports were 45.55% of pad and lining product sales in December 2025, while another report put the export share of total sales above 65%.

US local production is a tariff countermeasure, but overseas subsidiary profit can also move with exchange rates.

Customer and demand concentration

Major customers are automakers including Hyundai, Kia, GM, Stellantis and Ford. OE volumes depend on automaker production and whether models adopt the company's parts, tying demand to vehicle production cycles. Third-quarter 2026 revenue fell 8.9% from the prior quarter to KRW 53.8 billion.

Balance sheet and earnings volatility

The debt-to-equity ratio reached 115.4% at year-end 2025, above 2024, with total liabilities of KRW 137.5 billion. The third-quarter 2026 loss may reduce earnings' ability to absorb debt costs.

Net profit attributable to controlling shareholders over the latest four quarters totaled KRW 1.27 billion, with large swings between quarters.

Overall view

KB Autosys posted three consecutive years of revenue growth from 2023 to 2025, and operating profit and net profit attributable to controlling shareholders recovered sharply in 2025.

The operating margin rose from 1.4% in 2022 to 4.0% in 2025, which the company attributed to a favorable exchange rate environment and better overseas subsidiary profit.

However, the third quarter of 2026 brought revenue of KRW 53.8 billion, an operating loss of KRW 268 million and a net loss attributable to controlling shareholders of KRW 5.73 billion.

Net profit attributable to controlling shareholders over the latest four quarters totaled KRW 1.27 billion, less than the KRW 3.9 billion for full-year 2025.

On the balance sheet, the debt-to-equity ratio climbed to 115.4%, while the company has put a stable dividend policy forward and the 2025 payout ratio was reported at 73.8%.

Bullish factors are earnings recovery, the overseas production base and the shareholder return policy; bearish factors are the third-quarter loss, sensitivity to exchange rates and tariffs, and rising debt.

The next checkpoint is whether the 2026 annual report, due by late March 2027, shows fourth-quarter profit and operating margin holding at 2025 levels. Based on current data, bullish and bearish factors coexist, and the direction of quarterly results will define the balance.
