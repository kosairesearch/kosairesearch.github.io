02

Business structure

Heung-A Shipping, founded in 1961, is centered on chemical tanker shipping that carries liquid petrochemical products across Asia. After spinning off its container ship division in 2019, the company has concentrated on operating tanker vessels that carry liquid cargo.

Shipping accounts for roughly 80 to 90 percent of revenue according to press reports. Based on FnGuide's consolidated breakdown, the chemical tanker business accounts for 86.56% of revenue and other segments, including real estate leasing, account for 13.44%.

For 2022, the shipping segment generated KRW 146 billion in revenue and other segments generated KRW 31.9 billion, according to a securities research report. Other segments consist of real estate leasing and results of consolidated subsidiaries, playing a supporting role in the revenue mix.

The company reportedly has 11 affiliates in Korea and abroad, and its parent company's business performance is directly reflected in its financial statements, though the timing and scale of revenue recognition require separate review.

The company is focusing on strengthening its liquid petrochemical shipping capability in Asia to secure cost competitiveness. Relative to large shipping lines, its higher share of small and mid-sized tankers makes its share price respond quickly to freight rate changes.

Customer-level contract structures and fleet size can be traced in the business description section of the annual report.

Earnings analysis

Annual revenue fell from KRW 177.9 billion in 2022 to KRW 164.8 billion in 2023, a 7.3% decline, then rose to KRW 188 billion in 2024, a 14.1% increase, making 2024 the largest year of 2022 to 2025 by revenue. Revenue for 2025 was KRW 180.8 billion, down 3.8% from the prior year.

Operating profit was largest in 2022 at KRW 29.7 billion within the 2022 to 2025 period, and smallest in 2025 at KRW 11.3 billion, down 59.1% from the prior year. The operating margin moved between 14.6% and 16.7% from 2022 to 2024 before dropping to 6.2% in 2025.

Net income attributable to controlling shareholders rose from KRW 22.7 billion in 2022 to KRW 34.1 billion in 2023, a 50.6% increase, and KRW 39.6 billion in 2024, a 16.0% increase, before falling to KRW 30.6 billion in 2025, down 22.7%.

Net income including non-controlling interests was KRW 30.3 billion in 2025, a different measure from net income attributable to controlling shareholders. Press reports cited freight rate cuts as the main cause of the 2025 profit decline, and said the issue eased in 2026.

For the first nine months of 2025, the company cited crude oil price swings and oversupply of vessel capacity as pressures on gross profit and operating profit, together with weaker cargo volumes from slower global growth.

Quarterly operating profit went from KRW 1.22 billion in Q3 2025 to an operating loss of KRW 462 million in Q4 2025, which marked a swing into deficit.

It then returned to KRW 1.1 billion in Q1 2026, a swing back to profit, rose to KRW 3.14 billion in Q2, a 184.4% increase from the prior quarter, and eased to KRW 1.77 billion in Q3, a 43.7% decline from the prior quarter.

Revenue bottomed at KRW 40 billion in Q1 2026 and then rose to KRW 50 billion in Q2 and KRW 51.1 billion in Q3, two consecutive quarters of growth. Operating cash flow was positive in every year from 2022 to 2025, at KRW 32.7 billion, KRW 25.4 billion, KRW 47.3 billion and KRW 38 billion.

Q4 2025 shows net income attributable to controlling shareholders of KRW 20.9 billion despite an operating loss, so non-operating items should be reviewed in the annual report notes.

A securities research summary for the first half of 2026 also described chemical tanker results as highly volatile with oil prices, charter costs and international conditions, and noted that weaker global growth and a shipping slowdown reduced revenue and operating profit.

In cycle terms, the company moved past its 2022 to 2024 peak, when operating margins were around 14% to 17%, through freight declines in 2025 that pulled the margin down to 6.2%, and is now attempting a quarterly recovery in 2026.

Compared with peers, HMM operates a container fleet and Pan Ocean is centered on bulk, so Heung-A's exposure is concentrated in liquid chemical tankers. A higher share of small and mid-sized tankers magnifies earnings during freight upswings but adds fixed-cost pressure during downturns.
