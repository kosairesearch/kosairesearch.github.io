02

Business structure

Philenergy was spun off in April 2020 from Philoptics' energy business unit as a secondary battery equipment specialist and listed on KOSDAQ in 2023.

Its main products include Laser Notching and Stacking equipment and 46XX battery winding machines, and according to a company profile article it is also strengthening process technology for all-solid-state batteries.

The recently disclosed orders are all supply contracts for battery assembly equipment produced in-house, so revenue is recognized on a equipment delivery contract basis.

Counterparties and supply regions are kept confidential in each filing on trade secret grounds, so the customer mix is difficult to confirm from outside.

For the October 2026 contract, payment is structured as a 20% down payment after receipt of the purchase order, 70% interim payment after factory acceptance test (FAT) approval, and a 10% balance after site acceptance test (SAT) approval.

Under this structure, revenue and cash inflows move with equipment acceptance schedules, so the signing date and revenue recognition timing can differ from quarter to quarter.

In competition, Philenergy supplies production equipment, unlike listed cell and materials makers such as LG Energy Solution, Samsung SDI, POSCO Future M, Ecopro BM and L&F. Within the same value chain, equipment demand depends on cell makers' expansion plans, so customers' investment schedules drive order volumes.

The precise segment mix can be checked in the business divisions disclosure of the annual report, which is the next checkpoint.

Earnings analysis

Annual revenue rose from KRW 189.7 billion in 2022 to KRW 196.7 billion in 2023 (up 3.7%), then reached KRW 285.4 billion in 2024 (up 45.1%), the largest year in the period. 2025 revenue fell to KRW 33.2 billion, down 88.4%, making it the smallest year.

In a disclosure dated February 9, 2026, the company cited delayed capital investment in the secondary battery industry as the main cause of the revenue decline.

Operating income went from KRW 16.8 billion in 2022 to KRW 15.3 billion in 2023 (down 8.9%) and KRW 14.2 billion in 2024 (down 7.3%), and turned into an operating loss of KRW 22.2 billion in 2025. Operating margin moved from 8.9% in 2022 to 7.8% in 2023 and 5.0% in 2024, then to -66.8% in 2025.

Controlling-shareholder net income swung from KRW 3.72 billion in 2022 to a net loss of KRW 6.42 billion in 2023, a return to profit of KRW 14.1 billion in 2024, and a net loss of KRW 15.8 billion in 2025.

In 2024, controlling-shareholder net income of KRW 14.1 billion and net income including non-controlling interests of KRW 13.4 billion differ, so the two measures should be read separately.

The debt-to-equity ratio fell from 471.0% in 2022 to 48.3% in 2024 before rising to 67.1% in 2025, when total equity was KRW 126.7 billion (KRW 126.5 billion attributable to controlling shareholders) and total liabilities were KRW 85 billion.

Operating cash flow was -KRW 21 billion in 2022, KRW 90.5 billion in 2023, -KRW 16.6 billion in 2024 and KRW 5.75 billion in 2025, varying widely from year to year.

Quarterly revenue went from KRW 1.36 billion in the third quarter of 2025 to KRW 7.95 billion in the fourth quarter (up 484.2% quarter on quarter), then KRW 1.74 billion in the first quarter of 2026 (down 78.1%) and KRW 2.39 billion in the second quarter (up 37.5%).

Third-quarter 2026 revenue was KRW 52 billion, up 2,076.6% from the prior quarter and 3,721.5% from a year earlier, and operating income of KRW 1.3 billion reversed a KRW 7.14 billion operating loss in the second quarter.

Quarterly operating losses were KRW 5.38 billion in the third quarter of 2025, KRW 6.92 billion in the fourth quarter, KRW 3.88 billion in the first quarter of 2026 and KRW 7.14 billion in the second quarter. Controlling-shareholder net losses over the last four quarters total KRW 13 billion.

The third-quarter surge appears to coincide with the timing of large contracts signed in May and July 2026, but the exact recognition basis needs confirmation in the annual report.

Industry analysis

Philenergy's earnings are structured to move with the capital investment cycle of secondary battery cell makers. The company's explanation of the 2025 revenue drop as delayed investment by its customers illustrates this linkage.

In 2026, contracts of about KRW 14.77 billion in May, KRW 71.19 billion in July and KRW 86.08 billion in October followed one another, so the order flow did not break.

LG Energy Solution, a listed peer in the same industry, announced preliminary third-quarter 2026 results with revenue of KRW 9.64 trillion, up 59.0% from a year earlier.

This can be read as a sign of cell makers expanding production, but it is not evidence that LG Energy Solution's results translate directly into Philenergy orders.

Technology shifts toward all-solid-state batteries and the move to 46XX cylindrical cells could create equipment replacement demand different from existing lines.

In competition, listed cell and materials peers have revenue tied to battery sales or shipments, whereas Philenergy's revenue swings sharply with the number and size of equipment deliveries.

A single large contract now exceeds the 2025 revenue of KRW 33.2 billion, so delays in acceptance testing on one or two contracts could move quarterly results.

Where the cycle stands can be judged by whether order intake continues after the 2025 trough, which can be checked in the order and revenue flow of the 2026 annual report.

Overall view

Third-quarter 2026 revenue of KRW 52 billion and operating income of KRW 1.3 billion marked a quarterly return to profit.
