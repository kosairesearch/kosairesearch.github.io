Sales Rebound Amid Losses; Humanoid Reducer Orders Are the Test

02

Business structure

SBB Tech makes bearings, precision reducers and actuators for drive systems. Its core lines are composite bearings for harsh environments and ceramic bearings, and in the first quarter of 2026 bearing products accounted for 33.31% of sales through exports and 46.89% through domestic sales.

Adding bearing goods bought and resold (13.22%), bearing-related sales made up more than 93% of the total. Domestic reducer sales were 6.56% over the same quarter. The reducer range covers harmonic and planetary gear types, and a 2023 research report cited annual reducer capacity of about 52,000 units.

The company is widening its scope from standalone reducers to integrated actuators and drive modules that combine a reducer, motor and controller, and at the 2026 Robo World show it unveiled a very small reducer for humanoid fingers and a lighter-weight model.

It supplies steering and height-adjustment actuators for MobED, the compact mobility platform of the Hyundai Motor Group Robotics Lab, and also delivers to domestic humanoid developers and robot makers.

Securities analysts have noted that the company has reference supply of harmonic reducers for the K9 self-propelled howitzer. In the reducer market, HDS (Harmonic Drive Systems) holds strong technology and brand recognition, while Nidec-Shimpo and Chinese makers share the rest of the market.

A 2023 article reported that SBB Tech sells harmonic reducers at 60% to 80% of HDS prices. As of March 31, 2026, the largest shareholder, KPF, held a 39.71% stake.

04

Earnings analysis

Annual sales fell from KRW 7.47 billion in 2022 to KRW 5.13 billion in 2023 (down 31.3% year on year), then recovered to KRW 5.47 billion in 2024 (up 6.5%) and KRW 7.2 billion in 2025 (up 31.7%). Among the 2022 to 2025 years, 2022 was the largest and 2023 was the smallest.

Operating losses were KRW 1.84 billion in 2022, KRW 5.57 billion in 2023, KRW 6.89 billion in 2024 and KRW 6.86 billion in 2025, a four-year run of losses. Operating margins were -24.6%, -108.5%, -126.1% and -95.3% across the same years, so the 2025 loss rate improved from 2024.

A 2023 research report attributed the failure to narrow the 2022 operating loss to higher labor costs from R&D hiring and higher selling and administrative expenses tied to internal controls and the listing. The company said the 2025 operating loss stayed at a level similar to the prior year.

Net losses attributable to controlling shareholders were KRW 5.27 billion in 2022, KRW 11 billion in 2023, KRW 897 million in 2024 and KRW 9.45 billion in 2025. The swing in 2024 and back in 2025 happened while operating losses stayed at similar levels, which points to changes in non-operating items and taxes.

Operating cash flow was -KRW 2.16 billion in 2022, -KRW 3.15 billion in 2023, -KRW 4.08 billion in 2024 and -KRW 7.07 billion in 2025, with the outflow widening for four straight years. The debt-to-equity ratio rose from 39.4% in 2022 to 369.7% in 2023, then eased to 138.0% in 2024 and 157.8% in 2025.

Quarterly sales were KRW 2.01 billion in the third quarter of 2025, KRW 2.17 billion in the fourth quarter (up 8.0% from the prior quarter), KRW 1.77 billion in the first quarter of 2026 (down 18.4%), KRW 2.7 billion in the second quarter (up 52.6%) and KRW 4.88 billion in the third quarter (up 80.6%).

Quarterly operating losses went from KRW 2.14 billion in the third quarter of 2025 to KRW 901 million in the fourth quarter, then widened to KRW 1.85 billion in the first quarter of 2026 and KRW 2.95 billion in the second quarter, before narrowing to KRW 1.74 billion in the third quarter.

The first-quarter 2026 net loss attributable to controlling shareholders of KRW 8.81 billion was far larger than that quarter's operating loss of KRW 1.85 billion, which indicates non-operating costs were recognized in the same period.

05

Industry analysis

The humanoid robot market is still at an early stage. An IBK Securities report, citing data from Omdia, projects humanoid shipments rising from about 3,000 units in 2026 to 60,000 in 2028 and 3 million by 2035.

At the 2026 Robo World show, however, the company's CEO said humanoid drive components lack settled standards, so customer-specific development is required. He also noted that a robot hand may use about 13 joints per hand, or about 26 for both hands, which could widen demand for components.

A 2023 research report projected the global precision reducer market growing from USD 700 million in 2020 to USD 1.2 billion in 2024.

Among competitors, HDS and Nidec-Shimpo hold established technology positions abroad, while in Korea Robotis makes reducers and actuators, and Rainbow Robotics and Neuromeka have planned reducer businesses, according to a 2023 report.

The CEO identified the main entry barrier for domestic parts as component reliability and the risk of changing suppliers, since a faulty part can affect an entire production line even when performance and price are competitive.

Robot makers seeking cost-effective reducers to control costs are also cited as a demand driver. On the end-market side, a recovery in the semiconductor cycle lifted bearing sales, and the company said bearing revenue rose about 56% in 2025.

The company is shifting from a development-focused organization to one that can handle mass production, bringing in staff with automotive quality-control experience.

06

Outlook

The CEO told the 2026 Robo World show that, on the back of growth in the robotics segment, 2026 sales are expected to more than double from the prior year. He also expected sales to roughly double again in 2027, supported by a wider defense order book.

For the 2026 forecast to hold, sales would need to exceed KRW 14.4 billion, and the first three quarters of 2026 total about KRW 9.35 billion.

Disclosed contracts include a KRW 670 million steering-eccentric unit for MobED signed in December 2025 with a domestic counterparty, a KRW 1.21 billion humanoid reducer contract in May 2026 (16.7% of sales, running through April 29, 2028), and a KRW 1.92 billion humanoid reducer contract in August 2026 (26.7% of sales, running through April 29, 2028).

Because each contract equals roughly 12% to 27% of annual sales, the timing of awards can move quarterly revenue materially. A Daol Investment and Securities analyst said that revenue from remote weapon station (RCWS) defense projects will start to be recognized in 2026, with one project in 2026 and three in 2027.

The same report expected MobED output of about 1,000 units in 2026, with deliveries for a follow-on mobility platform also planned. The company is building a dedicated actuator plant in Ipjang-myeon, Cheonan, Chungcheongnam-do, and has said 2026 is the first year of its growth push.

Development of a very small reducer for humanoid fingers and integrated actuators is also under way, and the company plans to firm up its integrated actuator business with motor and sensor partners.

08

Bull factors

Sales recovery and contract disclosures

Sales rose 31.7% in 2025, and third-quarter 2026 sales were up 142.9% from a year earlier. Humanoid reducer contracts worth KRW 1.21 billion in May 2026 and KRW 1.92 billion in August 2026 were disclosed, and the August contract equals 26.7% of sales. The CEO expects 2026 sales to more than double.

Early results of the business shift

The company is shifting from a bearing-centered model toward reducers, actuators and integrated drive modules. The MobED steering actuator is moving into mass production, and a dedicated actuator plant in Cheonan is expanding production capacity. Very small reducers for humanoid fingers and lighter models are also in development.

Scarce domestic supply and defense references

Securities analysts say few domestic companies can produce precision reducers, which could lead to more orders from existing experience. The company is also reported to hold a reference for harmonic reducers used in the K9 self-propelled howitzer. Defense projects built on long-term supply contracts are expected to help smooth earnings volatility.

09

Bear factors

Accumulated losses and cash outflows

Operating losses have run for four straight years through 2025, and the operating margin was -95.3% that year. Operating cash flow worsened to KRW -7.07 billion in 2025 from KRW -2.16 billion in 2022. The bear case centers on the view that sales growth alone may not quickly narrow these losses.

Volatility in quarterly net losses

The first-quarter 2026 net loss attributable to controlling shareholders was KRW 8.81 billion, far above that quarter's KRW 1.85 billion operating loss. Until profits turn positive, quarterly results may remain volatile.

Bearing dependence and limited contract size

Reducers were just 6.56% of sales in the first quarter of 2026, with bearings making up most of the business. Humanoid reducer contracts of KRW 1.21 billion and KRW 1.92 billion each equal roughly 12% to 27% of annual sales, so single contracts carry outsized weight.

Because drive components lack standards, customer-specific development may also limit how fast volumes can scale.

10

Risk factors

Customer and order concentration

MobED volumes are tied to a Hyundai Motor Group platform, and humanoid reducer sales depend on supply contracts with a handful of domestic robot makers. Delays in defense or humanoid project schedules could make the 2026 doubling target harder to reach. Because individual contracts are small, a customer order delay flows directly into quarterly results.

Technology standards and adoption barriers

Humanoid drive components lack standard specifications, which keeps dependence on customer designs high. The CEO noted that reliability concerns around replacing existing parts make new adoption difficult. If competitors in Japan and China keep pressing on price and performance, margin recovery could slow.

Financial structure and funding needs

At the end of 2025, total liabilities were KRW 19.7 billion against total equity of KRW 12.5 billion, a debt-to-equity ratio of 157.8%. The ratio had reached 369.7% in 2023. If operating losses and cash outflows persist, the need for additional funding could increase.

Overall view

SBB Tech entered a sales recovery in 2025, with revenue up 31.7% to KRW 7.2 billion, yet the operating loss of KRW 6.86 billion means the four-year pattern of losses has not yet reversed.

Third-quarter 2026 sales of KRW 4.88 billion rose for two consecutive quarters and the operating loss narrowed to KRW 1.74 billion, but net losses attributable to controlling shareholders over the last four quarters totaled KRW 17.1 billion.

Bearings still drive most sales, and reducers were just 6.56% of first-quarter 2026 sales. The shift rests on humanoid reducers and actuators, MobED mass production and defense projects, and the disclosed contract sizes equal roughly 12% to 27% of annual sales.

The company expects 2026 sales to more than double, but the full-year preliminary results that would test that outlook come only in early 2027. Bull-side factors are sales recovery and contract disclosures, while bear-side factors are accumulated losses, cash outflows and a 157.8% debt-to-equity ratio. IBK Securities presented a KRW 75,000 target price on July 1, 2026, based on a sales-multiple approach.
