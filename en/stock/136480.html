Harim: Core Margins Soft, Net Income Recovers

Operating profit fell 71.0% from a year earlier in the third quarter of 2026, while net income attributable to controlling shareholders rose 49.0%, a split between weaker core margins and better financial income.

02

Business structure

Harim is a vertically integrated chicken company spanning feed, breeding, slaughtering and processing, and meat processing.

In the first-quarter 2026 quarterly report, the revenue mix was chicken (fresh, marinated, cut and portioned meat) at 70.70%, processed meat at 13.37%, and other at 15.93%, with domestic sales at about 98.96%.

The report states that Harim holds about 20.0% of the domestic chicken market by slaughter volume, ranking first. Its main brands are Harim chicken for fresh meat and Harim nuggets for processed products.

The feed business runs plants in Gimje and Jeongeup in Jeonbuk with annual capacity of 520,000 tons of compound feed, supporting its contract-farm model.

On the input side, raw chicken accounted for 72.29% and grain for 27.71% of purchases in the first quarter of 2026, so the company is exposed to both chicken prices and grain costs. Harim leads the chicken segment but is regarded as a late entrant in processed and convenience foods.

When chicken prices are low, the profit contribution from the chicken segment, which carries the largest revenue share, shrinks. The core structural point is that most revenue is tied to chicken prices and supply volume.

04

Earnings analysis

Annual revenue moved from KRW 1.34 trillion in 2022 to KRW 1.41 trillion in 2023 (up 5.1%), KRW 1.29 trillion in 2024 (down 8.9%), and KRW 1.44 trillion in 2025 (up 12.0%). Among 2022 to 2025, 2025 was the largest year and 2024 the smallest.

Operating profit went from KRW 46.8 billion in 2022 to KRW 40.4 billion in 2023 (down 13.6%) and KRW 28.5 billion in 2024 (down 29.5%), before reaching KRW 47.7 billion in 2025 (up 67.2%), with operating margins of 3.5%, 2.9%, 2.2% and 3.3%.

Net income attributable to controlling shareholders swung to a net loss of KRW 12.3 billion in 2024 and back to a profit of KRW 38.5 billion in 2025. Net income including non-controlling interests was KRW 38.7 billion in 2025, a different figure from the controlling-shareholder number.

By quarter, the fourth quarter of 2025 saw revenue of KRW 315.9 billion (down 23.8% from the prior quarter), operating profit of KRW 2.48 billion (down 86.6%) and a net loss attributable to controlling shareholders of KRW 2.57 billion.

Operating profit recovered to KRW 11.7 billion in the first quarter of 2026 and then KRW 17.3 billion in the second quarter, while the second-quarter revenue was KRW 425.8 billion.

In the third quarter of 2026, revenue was KRW 405.8 billion (down 4.7% from the prior quarter) and operating profit was KRW 5.36 billion (down 69.0%, or 71.0% year on year).

The company said avian influenza led to culls of breeder birds, cutting chicken supply and sales in the June to August peak season, and that chicken prices fell in July to September. It attributed the third-quarter net income increase to financial income from the lower exchange rate. Cumulative operating profit for January to September 2026 was KRW 34.3 billion (preliminary), down 24.1%.

05

Industry analysis

The main supply-side factor in the third quarter of 2026 was highly pathogenic avian influenza, which led to culls of breeder birds and reduced edible chicken supply. Combined with lower chicken prices, this pressured both volumes and selling prices.

Fresh chicken prices rose from KRW 3,763 per kilogram in 2024 to KRW 4,082 in 2025 and KRW 4,215 in the first quarter of 2026, so price levels are high but quarterly results still swing with disease control and supply.

A 2024 press analysis also pointed to annual swings in fresh chicken prices as a direct driver of earnings. On the cost side, cost of sales was 85.9% of revenue in the first half of 2024, and grain prices and the exchange rate affect both costs and financial income.

Harim ranks first in the domestic chicken market with a slaughter-based share of about 20.0%, giving it scale advantages.

Its processed-meat and convenience-food businesses are late-entrant positions, so its earnings are more directly linked to raw-material prices than those of processed-food peers such as CJ Cheiljedang or Nongshim.

06

Outlook

The verified facts point to supply-side causes for weaker third-quarter 2026 sales, namely breeder-bird culls and lower chicken prices. The timing of supply recovery is the key variable for sales volumes after the fourth quarter.

On production, chicken output was 317,792 tons in 2025 versus 316,852 tons in 2024, and 73,497 tons in the first quarter of 2026 versus 73,397 tons a year earlier. Compound feed output in the first quarter of 2026 was 634,092 tons, nearly flat from 634,680 tons a year earlier.

Disease control and prices therefore appear to drive earnings swings more than capacity does. The exchange rate is another variable, since the company attributed the third-quarter net income gain to financial income from a lower rate, so a reversal in currency direction could change the net income trend.

Revenue fell 23.8% from the third to the fourth quarter of 2025, so year-end quarters should be read alongside prices and disease conditions. The next checkpoints are the fourth-quarter 2026 preliminary results and the 2026 annual report.

08

Bull factors

Room for chicken-price recovery

Fresh chicken prices rose from KRW 3,763 per kilogram in 2024 to KRW 4,082 in 2025 and KRW 4,215 in the first quarter of 2026, per the quarterly reports. Full-year 2025 operating profit rose 67.2% to KRW 47.7 billion, showing the price recovery reaching earnings. If prices keep rising, the operating margin has room to improve beyond the 3.3% of 2025.

Net income rising after the low

Net income attributable to controlling shareholders rose from KRW 3.48 billion in the first quarter of 2026, the low point, to KRW 8.92 billion in the second quarter and KRW 12.7 billion in the third.

Operating profit recovered from KRW 11.7 billion to KRW 17.3 billion before falling to KRW 5.36 billion in the third quarter, so quarterly swings are large. Financial income also contributed to the third-quarter net income gain.

Improving balance-sheet trend

The debt-to-equity ratio fell from 202.2% in 2022 to 162.8% in 2025. Operating cash flow rose from KRW 61.7 billion in 2022 to KRW 129.1 billion in 2025. Total equity stood at KRW 325.4 billion at the end of 2025.

09

Bear factors

Thin operating margin

The preliminary operating margin for the third quarter of 2026 was 1.3%, down from 4.5% a year earlier. The annual margin fell from 3.5% in 2022 to 2.2% in 2024 and recovered only to 3.3% in 2025. The thin margin means raw-material and price swings pass through to earnings quickly.

Double pressure from disease and prices

Highly pathogenic avian influenza led to breeder-bird culls, reducing chicken supply and cutting sales in the June to August peak season. Chicken prices fell further in July to September, according to the company. Disease control determines when supply recovers, which keeps earnings variables high.

Net income reliant on financial income

The company attributed the third-quarter net income gain mainly to financial income from the lower exchange rate. Net income rose while operating profit fell, which means non-core factors played a large role. Cumulative net income for January to September 2026 was KRW 25.1 billion, down 39.2%.

Risk factors

Disease risk

Highly pathogenic avian influenza reduces chicken supply through breeder-bird culls and disrupts sales timing. It was cited as the direct cause of weaker peak-season sales in the third quarter of 2026. A prolonged outbreak would weigh on both chicken output and processing utilization. Because supply runs through contract farming, the impact depends on how far disease spreads.

Input costs and FX

Grain accounted for 27.71% of raw-material purchases in the first quarter of 2026. Higher grain and feed costs would squeeze margins unless chicken prices move in the same direction. The exchange rate affects net income through financial income as well as costs.

If the currency reverses, the pattern in which net income outperformed operations in the third quarter could also reverse.

Financial leverage

Total liabilities at the end of 2025 were KRW 529.9 billion, above total equity of KRW 325.4 billion. The debt-to-equity ratio of 162.8% is lower than 202.2% in 2022 but still shows liabilities exceeding equity. In quarters when profit falls, debt burden can magnify earnings swings. Changes in borrowing terms would also weigh more heavily on net income.

Overall view

Harim moved from a net loss to a profit in 2025, and net income attributable to controlling shareholders rose in the second and third quarters of 2026 after a first-quarter low.

However, third-quarter 2026 operating profit was KRW 5.36 billion (preliminary), down 71.0% year on year, and the operating margin fell to 1.3%. The third-quarter net income gain came mainly from financial income tied to the lower exchange rate, so core profitability and net income should be read separately.

Chicken prices and avian influenza control drive supply and sales, which makes earnings swings large. The debt-to-equity ratio fell from 202.2% in 2022 to 162.8% in 2025, but liabilities still exceed equity.

The next checkpoints are the fourth-quarter 2026 preliminary results, the 2026 annual report due by March 31, 2027, and the first-quarter 2027 quarterly report.
