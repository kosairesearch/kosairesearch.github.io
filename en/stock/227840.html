02

Business structure

Hyundai Corporation Holdings was founded on October 1, 2015 and listed on the KOSPI market on October 23 of the same year. It was spun off through a corporate split in October 2015 from the trading company Hyundai Corporation, from which it originated.

Its businesses fall into two main areas, brand business and foodstuff business, and its new-business segment includes livestock wholesale, packaging production and sales, and mushroom production and sales.

The brand business extends the Hyundai brand from automobiles, machinery and shipbuilding into home appliances and hardware, covering items such as small generators, power tools, lighting and batteries, which are planned and sourced and then produced and sold together with overseas partner manufacturers.

Financial information sites classify this segment as brand licensing and OEM business and view its revenue structure as relatively stable.

The foodstuff business centers on distribution of beef, pork and chicken, and is expanding into meat processing, the import and distribution of agricultural and marine products, and cold storage operations.

According to the 2025 annual business report, the consolidated group includes 13 companies, including overseas subsidiaries, and seven additional companies beyond the affiliate Hyundai Corporation are accounted for under the equity method.

As a result, the holding company's earnings reflect both operating results of consolidated subsidiaries and equity-method income.

The core assets of the brand business are the scope of use of the Hyundai name and the production network of overseas partners, while cold storage and import sourcing capability are viewed as differentiators in the foodstuff business.

Some of its business areas overlap with distribution and trading operators within general trading company groups, but the company relies more on licensing income and subsidiary results than on the volume of intermediary trade.

Revenue by segment should be checked year by year in the segment information of the business report for accuracy.

04

Earnings analysis

Annual revenue rose from KRW 164.3 billion in 2022 to KRW 188.3 billion in 2023 (up 14.6% year on year), KRW 215.6 billion in 2024 (up 14.5%) and KRW 250.2 billion in 2025 (up 16.1%), and increased for three consecutive years from 2023 through 2025.

Operating profit over the same period grew from KRW 14.3 billion in 2022 to KRW 18.7 billion in 2023 (up 31.2%), KRW 19.5 billion in 2024 (up 4.1%) and KRW 21.4 billion in 2025 (up 9.6%).

Operating margin, however, peaked at 9.9% in 2023 and then slipped to 9.0% in 2024 and 8.5% in 2025, so profit growth did not keep pace with revenue growth.

Profit attributable to controlling shareholders moved in alternating directions, from KRW 33.6 billion in 2022 to KRW 29.2 billion in 2023 (down 13.2%), KRW 42.7 billion in 2024 (up 46.3%) and KRW 36.7 billion in 2025 (down 14.1%).

In 2024, profit attributable to controlling shareholders exceeded total net income of KRW 42.3 billion, which means the non-controlling interest share was a loss.

The wide gap between operating profit and attributable profit suggests a meaningful weight of non-operating and equity-method items, and the line-item detail should be checked in the consolidated income statement of the annual business report.

Operating cash flow was KRW 6.84 billion in 2022, KRW 21.2 billion in 2023, KRW 18.9 billion in 2024 and KRW 23.3 billion in 2025, a steadier path than attributable profit.

Quarterly operating profit rose from KRW 4.78 billion in Q3 2025 to KRW 6.21 billion in Q4 2025 (up 29.7% quarter on quarter), then KRW 6.22 billion in Q1 2026 (up 0.3%), KRW 7.53 billion in Q2 2026 (up 21.1%) and KRW 7.68 billion in Q3 2026 (up 1.9%), a run of four consecutive quarterly increases.

Q3 2026 revenue of KRW 82.1 billion rose 26.2% year on year, and Q3 2026 operating profit of KRW 7.68 billion rose 60.5% year on year. In the same quarter, profit attributable to controlling shareholders of KRW 9.38 billion rose only 1.0% year on year, a much smaller gain than operating profit.

Attributable profit of KRW 24.4 billion in Q2 2026 far outpaced the 21.1% rise in operating profit, which points to possible non-operating or one-off items that should be checked in the semi-annual report.

Profit attributable to controlling shareholders totaled KRW 53.1 billion over the last four quarters, from Q4 2025 through Q3 2026.

05

Industry analysis

For a holding company, earnings are essentially the results of its owned subsidiaries and equity-method affiliates, so subsidiary-level performance and the weight of non-operating items often matter more than the broader industry cycle.

Listed holding companies in the same category range from finance and investment-focused groups to manufacturing and distribution-focused ones, which makes a single industry average multiple difficult to use for comparison. Hyundai Corporation Holdings faces two end markets.

One is demand for finished home appliances and hardware, along with the production costs of partner manufacturers, and the other is livestock prices and import conditions for beef, pork and chicken.

In appliances and hardware, the scope of brand use and the production capacity of partners are the key variables for profitability. In foodstuffs, livestock price swings and demand for cold storage widen the range of margin fluctuation.

Compared with trading companies that operate at a larger intermediary transaction scale, this company depends more on licensing income and subsidiary results.

Quarterly operating profit has risen for four consecutive quarters from Q4 2025 through Q3 2026, but whether that reflects a cyclical recovery or changes in individual businesses should be distinguished through the segment explanations in the business report.

Operating margin fell from 9.9% in 2023 to 8.5% in 2025, so it is not yet clear whether revenue expansion has translated into margin improvement.

Bull factors

Consecutive gains in core operating profit

Q3 2026 operating profit of KRW 7.68 billion rose 60.5% year on year, while revenue of KRW 82.1 billion rose 26.2%. Revenue and core operating profit growing together is the main bullish factor.

The debt-to-equity ratio declined from 32.1% in 2022 to 24.4% at end-2025. Total equity stood at KRW 327.9 billion and total liabilities at KRW 79.9 billion at the end of 2025. A thicker equity base and lower debt burden are strengths in financial stability.

Share price low relative to net assets

Bear factors

Downward trend in operating margin

Revenue grew 16.1% in 2025, but operating profit rose only 9.6%. Revenue expansion not translating into margin gains is a bearish factor.

High volatility in attributable profit

Profit attributable to controlling shareholders fell 14.1% from KRW 42.7 billion in 2024 to KRW 36.7 billion in 2025. After KRW 24.4 billion in Q2 2026, it dropped 61.5% to KRW 9.38 billion in Q3 2026. Large swings across years and quarters make the durability of earnings hard to gauge, which is a bearish factor.

Gap between revenue growth and net profit

In Q3 2026, revenue rose 26.2% and operating profit rose 60.5%, yet profit attributable to controlling shareholders rose only 1.0%. Changes in non-operating or equity-method items may have offset the gains. Whether operating improvement carries through to final profit remains a bearish question.

10

Risk factors

Concentration of earnings structure

The attributable profit of a holding company depends on subsidiary results, equity-method income and non-operating items. In some years the gap between operating profit and attributable profit has been wide, so one-off items can move quarterly results considerably. Amounts by income statement line should be confirmed in the quarterly and semi-annual reports.

Price and sourcing risks in foodstuffs

The foodstuff business is exposed to beef, pork and chicken prices and to import conditions. Margins in meat processing and cold storage operations can shift with raw material costs and storage demand. Sharp livestock price moves can become a source of quarter-to-quarter operating profit volatility.

Overseas subsidiaries and currency

The 13 consolidated companies include overseas subsidiaries. Overseas transactions and subsidiary results are affected by exchange rate movements and local trading conditions. If overseas subsidiaries grow in weight within consolidated results, currency factors could add to earnings volatility.
