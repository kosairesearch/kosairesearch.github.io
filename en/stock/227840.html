Report overview

Brand Licensing and Equity-Method Gains Drive Earnings

Hyundai Corporation Holdings has posted steady revenue and operating profit growth from 2022 to 2025, driven by high-margin growth in its brand licensing/OEM business and expanding equity-method gains from affiliate Hyundai Corporation.

02

Business structure

Hyundai Corporation Holdings was established in 2015 through a spin-off of the brand and new-business divisions from what is now Hyundai Corporation.

The company's largest revenue segment is livestock and meat wholesale, importing meat directly from overseas for supply to domestic large-format retailers and department stores; the segment faces intense price competition from numerous suppliers and holds a relatively weaker competitive position versus vertically integrated distributors.

In contrast, the brand licensing and OEM business leases the 'HYUNDAI' trademark from HD Hyundai Heavy Industries and sells the right to use the logo on appliances, tools, and small generators to overseas customers, and has expanded from simple licensing into OEM, commissioning manufacturers in China and Southeast Asia to produce goods sold under the Hyundai brand.

This brand segment carries an operating margin in the 40-50% range, offsetting the earnings volatility of the livestock wholesale business. The revenue gap between the two segments has narrowed from about 15 percentage points in 2022.

The company also runs smaller overseas new businesses, including corrugated packaging production in Cambodia and Asian mushroom cultivation in the UK and Australia.

Structurally, the most important pillar is its status as the largest shareholder holding roughly a 24% stake in core affiliate Hyundai Corporation (011760, a general trading company in steel, petrochemicals, passenger-vehicle parts, and energy-related equipment), which is accounted for under the equity method and significantly affects non-operating results.

The company is also pursuing diversification through auto-parts manufacturer acquisitions and ambitions in eco-friendly waste recycling. A governance feature is the gradual accumulation of shares by third-generation family members, who are also involved in operational roles.

04

Earnings analysis

Annual results show a clear growth trajectory. Revenue rose every year over four years, from KRW 164.3bn in 2022 to KRW 188.3bn in 2023, KRW 215.6bn in 2024, and KRW 250.2bn in 2025.

Operating profit also climbed steadily, from KRW 14.3bn in 2022 to KRW 18.7bn in 2023, KRW 19.5bn in 2024, and KRW 21.4bn in 2025, though the operating margin edged down slightly from 9.9% in 2023 to 9.0% in 2024 and 8.5% in 2025.

Owners' net income, by contrast, was more volatile than operating profit, falling from KRW 33.6bn in 2022 to KRW 29.2bn in 2023, jumping to KRW 42.7bn in 2024, then easing back to KRW 36.7bn in 2025 — a pattern that appears heavily influenced by equity-method income from its affiliate and foreign-exchange effects rather than core operations.

On a quarterly basis, operating profit was KRW 5.43bn in Q2 2025, KRW 4.78bn in Q3 2025, KRW 6.21bn in Q4 2025, KRW 6.22bn in Q1 2026, and KRW 7.53bn in Q2 2026, showing a clear upward trend in more recent quarters.

Owners' net income moved in a comparatively narrower range from KRW 7.38bn in Q2 2025 to KRW 9.28bn, KRW 10.12bn, and KRW 9.25bn through Q1 2026, before surging to KRW 24.38bn in Q2 2026.

This jump far exceeded the concurrent improvement in operating profit (from KRW 6.22bn to KRW 7.53bn), suggesting that a large portion of the increase reflects expanded equity-method gains tied to strong results at affiliate Hyundai Corporation that same quarter.

Cumulative owners' net income over the trailing four quarters (Q3 2025 through Q2 2026) reached roughly KRW 53.0bn, already exceeding the full-year 2025 figure of KRW 36.7bn.

05

Industry analysis

The livestock/meat wholesale industry is structurally competitive, with numerous suppliers and limited ability to differentiate on quality, resulting in persistent price competition; the company is viewed as having a comparatively weaker scale and profitability position versus vertically integrated distributors.

The brand licensing/OEM market, by contrast, benefits from entry barriers rooted in brand recognition and residual contractual relationships, supporting a relatively stable earnings structure.

The core affiliate accounted for under the equity method, Hyundai Corporation, operates a general trading business spanning steel, petrochemicals, passenger-vehicle parts, machinery/marine, energy-related equipment, and resource development, and its results are sensitive to the cyclicality inherent in trading companies, including US tariff policy, commodity prices, and currency swings.

Reports indicate the affiliate's preliminary consolidated operating profit rose 24.8% year over year in Q1 2026, and in Q2 2026 it posted revenue of KRW 2.82tn and operating profit of KRW 62.9bn, up 46.8% and 81.6% year over year respectively, marking a record quarterly result.

Such a recovery at the affiliate flows through to the holding company's results via equity-method income.

On the new-business front, the company is attempting to leverage its existing trading network to expand into auto-parts manufacturing, eco-friendly waste recycling, and overseas food-ingredient distribution, though these are still viewed as early-stage initiatives with limited revenue contribution so far.

06

Outlook

In 2025, the company issued its first public corporate bond, raising KRW 37bn, with a significant portion used to repay existing bank borrowings as it sought to establish capital-market access.

Built on a virtually debt-free balance sheet (a 2025 debt ratio of 24.4%), the company has continued to pursue M&A such as auto-parts manufacturer acquisitions to enter manufacturing, part of a strategy to broaden its portfolio beyond trading-centric operations.

In the brand segment, the company has indicated it is expanding its product lineup into household, kitchen, and beauty appliances while extending sales into emerging markets such as the Middle East, Latin America, and Southeast Asia.

Its equity-method affiliate, Hyundai Corporation, has also shown a recovery in 2026 across key segments including steel, petrochemicals, and energy-related equipment; if this trend continues, it could positively affect the holding company's equity-method income.

That said, sharp swings in owners' net income such as the one seen in Q2 2026 could recur depending on the affiliate's earnings cycle or currency movements, meaning quarter-to-quarter predictability remains limited.

New-business investment is expected to be funded primarily through internally generated operating cash flow, suggesting the company retains financial capacity to continue investing without relying heavily on external financing.

08

Bull factors

Structural growth in the high-margin brand business

The brand licensing/OEM business carries an operating margin in the 40-50% range and continues to grow by expanding its product lineup and sales regions. This segment has gradually narrowed the revenue gap with the livestock wholesale segment, which could support overall earnings-mix improvement. Its asset-light licensing model also means additional capex requirements are relatively limited.

Expanding equity-method income from affiliate recovery

Affiliate Hyundai Corporation, in which the company holds a 24% stake, has reportedly posted a recovery in steel, petrochemical, and energy-related equipment segments in 2026, reaching a record quarterly result.

If this trend continues, the holding company's equity-method income could improve in tandem, with the Q2 2026 surge in owners' net income cited as a supporting instance.

Strong, virtually debt-free financial structure

The 2025 debt ratio remained low at 24.4%, and the company has historically maintained a virtually debt-free structure. This provides financial capacity to pursue new-business investments such as auto-parts M&A without heavy reliance on external financing.

09

Bear factors

Weaker competitive position in the livestock wholesale segment

The livestock wholesale segment, the largest revenue contributor, faces intense price competition due to a fragmented supplier base and is viewed as competitively weaker than vertically integrated distributors.

Stagnant profitability in this segment may be one factor behind the decline in overall operating margin from 9.9% in 2023 to 8.5% in 2025.

Net income volatility from reliance on equity-method income

Owners' net income has fluctuated more sharply than operating profit—from KRW 33.6bn in 2022 to KRW 29.2bn in 2023, KRW 42.7bn in 2024, and KRW 36.7bn in 2025—and surged sharply quarter over quarter in Q2 2026.

This suggests a significant influence from non-operating factors such as equity-method income, implying net income could soften again if the affiliate's earnings cycle reverses.

Early-stage new businesses and execution risk

Diversification efforts—entry into auto-parts manufacturing, eco-friendly recycling, and overseas food-ingredient distribution—are ongoing but are still viewed as early-stage with limited revenue contribution.

It may take time for new businesses to develop stable earnings power, and integration risk from M&A execution cannot be ruled out.

10

Risk factors

Business risk

The livestock wholesale segment is exposed to meat import costs and currency fluctuations, and intensifying competition could continue to pressure margins. The brand segment's business model, which relies on a specific trademark licensing arrangement, is also exposed to changes in contract terms.

Governance risk

The combined stake of Chairman Chung Mong-hyuk and related parties is around 29%, and third-generation family members have continued purchasing shares in what appears to be succession preparation.

Any governance restructuring or changes in intra-group transaction structures during succession could warrant monitoring for their impact on minority shareholders.

Earnings volatility risk

A substantial portion of the holding company's net income derives from equity-method gains tied to affiliate Hyundai Corporation, whose general trading business is inherently cyclical, subject to tariffs, trade conditions, and commodity prices. A slowdown at the affiliate could similarly affect the holding company's non-operating results.

Overall view

Hyundai Corporation Holdings operates a distinctive holding-company structure built on two pillars—a high-margin brand licensing/OEM business and a highly competitive livestock wholesale business—while equity-method income from its 24% stake in Hyundai Corporation materially influences overall results.

From 2022 through 2025, revenue and operating profit grew steadily each year, but owners' net income showed greater volatility due to equity-method effects, culminating in a sharp increase in Q2 2026 that appears linked to strong results at the affiliate.

The company's financial structure remains stable, with a low debt ratio and a near debt-free position that underpins its ongoing new-business M&A efforts.

That said, the structural competitive weakness in livestock wholesale, the early-stage nature of new businesses, and the volatility in non-operating results tied to the affiliate's earnings cycle are factors worth monitoring together.

Ongoing share purchases by third-generation family members related to succession are also a point to watch over the medium to long term.

Going forward, the affiliate's earnings releases, progress on the holding company's own new businesses, and the continuity of its dividend policy are likely to be the key observation points for assessing earnings trends and shareholder value.
