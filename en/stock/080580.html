Memory test parts turnaround: the next checkpoints

02

Business structure

OKins Electronics was founded in 1998 to make semiconductor test sockets and listed on KOSDAQ in 2014. It set up a semiconductor division in 2006 and also runs back-end test services.

Its core business is the BiTS division, which generates sales from burn-in and test sockets, probe pins and pogo pins, and magnetic collets.

Burn-in sockets are a key product used in the back-end stage that screens chips for defects, and the press has described the company as holding distinctive technology and share in this field.

In explaining its first-half 2026 results, the company said its three main product lines of burn-in sockets, connectors and probe pins grew evenly.

Higher-spec memory products and generational shifts lifted demand for high-spec burn-in sockets such as those for low-power DDR, and connector and probe pin supply rose with customers' production.

Samsung Electronics and CXMT are reported as major customers, and the company has said its China-related sales share has risen. In December 2025 the company said it had begun supplying HBM collets to a global memory maker, bringing deliveries to all three major memory makers.

Outside semiconductors, the company obtained Hyundai Kia connector SQ certification and is developing 5G communication connectors. Its competitive set overlaps with listed test-socket and probe makers such as LEENO Industrial, TSE and ISC.

04

Earnings analysis

On the basis of DART-filed results, annual revenue fell from KRW 64.2 billion in 2022 to KRW 56.9 billion in 2023, a decline of 11.4%, then rose 17.3% to KRW 66.7 billion in 2024 and 41.5% to KRW 94.4 billion in 2025. Revenue grew in 2024 and 2025, and 2025 was the largest year of 2022 to 2025.

Operating profit fell from KRW 2.61 billion in 2022 to KRW 374 million in 2023, the smallest year, an 85.7% drop, then rose to KRW 1.9 billion in 2024 and KRW 11.1 billion in 2025, two straight years of growth. Operating margin moved from 4.1% in 2022 and 0.7% in 2023 to 2.9% in 2024 and 11.7% in 2025.

Net profit attributable to controlling shareholders went from KRW 1.07 billion in 2023 to a net loss of KRW 5.42 billion in 2024, then returned to a profit of KRW 7.74 billion in 2025.

The company attributed the 2025 improvement to a larger share of high value-added products, a portfolio reshaped around profitability, and structural efficiency across sales, development and business units. The balance sheet also improved, with the debt ratio falling from 215.7% in 2024 to 88.8% in 2025.

At the end of 2025, total liabilities were KRW 45 billion and total equity was KRW 50.7 billion. Operating cash flow rose from KRW 7.62 billion in 2022 to KRW 16.9 billion in 2025.

On a quarterly basis, revenue was KRW 23.3 billion in the third quarter of 2025, KRW 25.1 billion in the fourth quarter (up 7.6% quarter on quarter), KRW 25.6 billion in the first quarter of 2026 (up 2.1%), KRW 35.1 billion in the second quarter (up 37.0%) and KRW 37.9 billion in the third quarter (up 8.1%), four straight quarters of growth.

Operating profit, however, fell 30.9% to KRW 2.55 billion in the fourth quarter of 2025, recovered to KRW 3.61 billion in the first quarter of 2026 and KRW 8.39 billion in the second quarter, then dropped 14.7% to KRW 7.16 billion in the third quarter.

Net profit attributable to controlling shareholders was KRW 8.69 billion in the second quarter and KRW 5.1 billion in the third quarter, and the four-quarter total from the fourth quarter of 2025 to the third quarter of 2026 was KRW 21.3 billion.

Because revenue growth has not always carried through to profit in the same quarter, quarterly margin trends need to be tracked alongside sales.

05

Industry analysis

The company's main end market is the memory semiconductor test process. According to a Korea IR Council report from February 2026, the industry's center of gravity is shifting toward HBM and next-generation memory modules, which is pushing memory test processes toward greater sophistication and equipment conversion.

The same report said higher-spec memory raises test difficulty, which tends to lift unit prices and replacement demand for sockets and probe parts. Customers' move to CLT equipment was identified as the main driver of memory socket sales.

Growth in pogo pin sales tied to global probe card customers and expanding HBM-related magnetic collet supply were also cited as profit drivers. Press reports point to rising test-part demand from power semiconductors, including silicon carbide, and from high-performance chips.

Within competition, OKins Electronics centers on burn-in sockets and also supplies probe pins, connectors and magnetic collets.

It shares the test socket and probe segment with listed companies such as LEENO Industrial, TSE and ISC, so product generation changes and the speed of customer qualification are the variables that determine share.

The company said it is developing new materials and heat-resistance research to address next-generation hybrid bonding. Memory investment appears to be expanding, but the pace of equipment spending depends on memory makers' own demand decisions.

Outlook

Beyond expanding burn-in socket and connector supply, it expects the revenue share of probe pins, next-generation semiconductor parts and next-generation memory interface products to rise gradually. Earlier in 2026, it set a goal of upgrading its business portfolio and realigning its company-wide strategy.

In a February 2026 report, securities research cited forecasts of KRW 140.5 billion in 2026 revenue and KRW 20.7 billion in operating profit, and the press reported similar forecasts from Korea IR Council and Yuanta Securities.

Compared with the disclosed revenue of KRW 25.6 billion, KRW 35.1 billion and KRW 37.9 billion for the first three quarters of 2026, reaching the forecast would require a substantial increase in fourth-quarter revenue.

How much fourth-quarter operating profit recovers from the third quarter is therefore the key variable for full-year profit.

The company's outlook rests on customers' production increases and the shift of new products into mass production, so the timing of customer orders and mass-production transitions will determine whether the outlook is realized.

The next concrete data points will be the full-year 2026 provisional results and the 2026 annual report, which should show annual revenue and profit mix in numbers.

Bull factors

Revenue growth continues after the 2025 return to profit

Net profit attributable to controlling shareholders moved from a net loss of KRW 5.42 billion in 2024 to a profit of KRW 7.74 billion in 2025. Revenue has risen for four straight quarters through the third quarter of 2026, and third-quarter revenue of KRW 37.9 billion was up 62.7% year on year.

Operating margin moved from a range of 0.7% to 4.1% in 2022 to 2024 to 11.7% in 2025. Growth in sales and improved profitability together form the core of the bull case.

Higher-value memory testing demand

A February 2026 Korea IR Council report said memory sockets and pogo pin sales have entered a growth phase on HBM and SOCAMM demand. Higher-spec memory raises test difficulty, which the report linked to higher unit prices and replacement demand for socket and probe parts.

In December 2025 the company said HBM collet supply brought deliveries to all three major memory makers. How much of this structure shows up in full-year results is the next thing to confirm.

Product expansion and new mass production

In its first-half 2026 results, the company said newly developed products moved into mass production after customer qualification and began contributing new sales. It expects the share of probe pins, next-generation semiconductor parts and next-generation memory interface products to rise gradually.

It is also preparing mass production of memory test solutions for DDR5 server DRAM and test products for next-generation memory modules. Outside semiconductors, it has obtained Hyundai Kia connector SQ certification.

Bear factors

Concentration in customers and region

Samsung Electronics and CXMT are reported as major customers, so results are closely tied to these customers' capital spending. Because China-related sales share has risen, changes in US-China regulation or export controls could have an effect.

With its customer base concentrated among memory makers, an order adjustment by a single customer could move quarterly revenue. The pace of customer diversification is the main checkpoint for the bear case.

Quarterly profit volatility

Operating profit fell 30.9% to KRW 2.55 billion in the fourth quarter of 2025, and the third-quarter 2026 figure of KRW 7.16 billion was down 14.7% from the prior quarter. In some quarters, profit did not move in step with rising revenue.

Operating profit fell to KRW 374 million in 2023, so the record of sustained profit recovery is still short. Net profit attributable to controlling shareholders was KRW 5.1 billion in the third quarter, below KRW 8.69 billion in the second quarter.

Share price volatility and multiple burden

After the first-half 2026 results disclosure in August 2026, the share price moved sharply, rising more than 16% in one day. The price-to-book ratio is above year-end levels of 2023 and 2024, and market capitalization is well above total equity. The dividend yield is below the industry average. Because the turnaround is recent, the multiple may react sharply to whether profit is sustained.

Risk factors

Capex cycle and equipment conversion timing

Burn-in socket orders depend on customers' equipment conversion schedules. If major customers' move to CLT equipment is delayed, memory socket sales could shift in timing. Even while memory investment expands, spending pace differs across customers. Whether quarterly revenue growth stalls at any point is worth monitoring.

Next-generation technology response

The outcome of new-material development and heat-resistance research is key to responding to next-generation hybrid bonding. As test difficulty rises, spec requirements for sockets and probe parts rise as well.

If development does not move into mass production on time, the company could fall behind peers in product generation changes. Customer qualification periods for new products determine when sales are recognized.

Balance sheet and cash flow variability

The debt ratio fell from 215.7% in 2024 to 88.8% in 2025, but total liabilities were still KRW 45 billion at the end of 2025. Operating cash flow rose to KRW 16.9 billion in 2025, but it can swing with working capital and equipment needs.

If the company directs funds to new equipment and research, cash flow and financial flexibility will be affected. The annual trends in debt and cash flow should be checked together in the 2026 annual report.
