02

Business structure

Woniltni makes equipment that stores, vaporizes and supplies natural gas and hydrogen, along with high-purity hydrogen production units.

Its main product is the high-pressure combustion vaporizer (SCV), which the company localized in 2013 and which a gas industry publication described as accounting for more than half of total revenue.

According to a third-party aggregator, 2025 revenue was made up of hydrogen storage alloys at 35.0%, gas heaters and related products at 36.0%, and SCVs at 24.5%.

The company also produces gas filters and gas recondensers for natural gas supply and manufactures SCV components and hydrogen cylinder inspection at its Gimpo plant.

In hydrogen, it signed a technology transfer agreement for high-purity hydrogen production in 2020 and invested KRW 5.0 billion to secure localized engineering design capability.

A 2025 report said it supplies hydrogen storage alloys exclusively to a Korean shipbuilder, and it is preparing to extend hydrogen storage technology to overseas defense markets alongside domestic shipbuilders' submarine bids.

Customers center on LNG terminal contractors, district-energy operators and power-plant engineering firms, and in 2026 it signed contracts with an SK group engineering contractor, a Hanwha power unit, a Yeosu power operator and a steel-group contractor.

Drawing on SCV deliveries to a Chinese LNG terminal, it has outlined plans to expand sales of vaporizers to India, Southeast Asia and Europe.

Its competition overlaps with plant equipment makers such as BHI, which the company cited as a peer at its listing, and its localized gas-equipment capability is cited as the core strength.

04

Earnings analysis

Annual results turned from profit to loss between 2024 and 2025. Revenue fell 29.8% year on year to KRW 29.6 billion from KRW 42.2 billion, and operating profit of KRW 6.38 billion became an operating loss of KRW 1.75 billion.

Net income attributable to controlling shareholders of KRW 7.87 billion turned into a net loss of KRW 2.06 billion, while net income including non-controlling interests moved from KRW 7.87 billion to a net loss of KRW 2.06 billion. The operating margin fell from 15.1% in 2024 to -5.9% in 2025.

The company attributed the decline to one-time costs tied to its listing and IPO-related expenses, a delay in revenue recognition after detailed contract negotiations, and higher fixed-cost burden as sales fell.

The debt ratio declined from 102.1% to 34.3%, and total equity at the end of 2025 was KRW 48.2 billion, all attributable to controlling shareholders. Operating cash flow stayed negative at -KRW 3.84 billion in 2024 and -KRW 4.05 billion in 2025.

Quarterly, revenue went from KRW 4.27 billion with an operating loss of KRW 1.01 billion in Q3 2025 to KRW 11.6 billion with operating profit of KRW 948 million in Q4 2025.

Q1 2026 revenue dropped to KRW 3.99 billion with an operating loss of KRW 2.01 billion, then Q2 2026 revenue rose to KRW 9.95 billion with operating profit of KRW 543 million.

Q3 2026 revenue of KRW 8.55 billion was up 100.5% from a year earlier, and operating profit of KRW 374 million moved from a loss a year earlier to a profit, but the net loss attributable to controlling shareholders widened to KRW 1.43 billion.

The net loss attributable to controlling shareholders over the last four quarters totaled KRW 31.55 million. Because quarterly revenue swings with contract recognition timing, the gap between operating profit and net income attributable to controlling shareholders needs to be read alongside revenue.

05

Industry analysis

Demand from downstream markets comes from three directions. The first is LNG infrastructure, where Woniltni's SCVs are used as vaporization equipment at LNG terminals, with a Shanghai-linked contract for a Shenzhen LNG terminal expansion signed in late December 2025 as a representative case.

The second is district energy and power generation, where the 2026 gas pressure-regulating equipment order for a Yongin district-energy project fits. The third is hydrogen and defense, where hydrogen storage alloy technology is discussed alongside Korean shipbuilders' overseas submarine bids.

According to a gas industry publication, Korean shipbuilders are taking part in submarine tenders in Canada and Poland, and projects with Saudi Arabia and the Philippines are under discussion.

The timing of these defense projects has not been made public, so when they would translate into company revenue remains uncertain. On competition, BHI, which the company cited as a peer at its listing, was reported to have grown revenue and operating profit sharply in 2025.

The same report pointed out that Woniltni's 2025 decline stood out against its peer's growth. The cycle position depends on the pace of LNG facility investment and hydrogen policy, and at present the evidence is a rising count of contract awards.

Outlook

The nearest checkpoints among the disclosed firm contracts are their end dates. The Shenzhen LNG terminal SCV contract signed in late December 2025 runs through December 30, 2026, and the two Yongin district-energy contracts signed in February 2026 run through January 15, 2027.

A Yeosu power operator order disclosed in August 2026 (KRW 6.79 billion) runs through April 21, 2029, and a steel-group contractor's Gwangyang No. 8 CGL order (KRW 7.2 billion) runs through October 31, 2028.

A Hanwha power unit's Yeosu heater performance upgrade order disclosed in November 2026 (KRW 4.39 billion) runs from November 16, 2026 to March 10, 2028. The company says these schedules and scope may be revised by agreement as work progresses.

Payment on the Hanwha order is staged, starting with a 10% advance, followed by payments on raw-material deposit, equipment delivery and commissioning, so revenue recognition and cash collection may fall in different periods.

In a July 2025 report, the company said it planned to use listing proceeds for capacity expansion, and as of September 30, 2025, none of the KRW 13.24 billion IPO proceeds had been spent. Its medium-term plan includes expanding vaporizer sales to India, Southeast Asia and Europe.

The next items to check are how IPO proceeds are used and how quickly the contract backlog converts into revenue, both of which can be followed in the 2026 annual report.

Quarterly revenue recovery

Q2 2026 revenue of KRW 9.95 billion rose 149.5% from the prior quarter, and Q3 2026 revenue of KRW 8.55 billion was up 100.5% from a year earlier. Q3 2026 operating profit of KRW 374 million moved from a loss a year earlier to a profit.

Together with the KRW 948 million operating profit in Q4 2025, the quarterly revenue scale shows signs of recovery.

Localized technology and defense linkage

The high-pressure combustion vaporizer was localized in Korea in 2013, and hydrogen storage alloys were reported in 2025 as supplied exclusively to a Korean shipbuilder.

A gas industry publication reported that the company is preparing to apply its hydrogen storage technology to overseas defense markets alongside domestic shipbuilders' submarine bids. Experience supplying localized equipment could extend into defense and hydrogen markets.

Bear factors

Thin earnings base

The company posted an operating loss of KRW 1.75 billion and a net loss attributable to controlling shareholders of KRW 2.06 billion in 2025. An operating loss of KRW 2.01 billion also appeared in Q1 2026.

In Q3 2026, operating profit of KRW 374 million coexisted with a net loss attributable to controlling shareholders of KRW 1.43 billion, so revenue recovery has not consistently reached the bottom line.

Weak cash generation

Operating cash flow stayed negative, at -KRW 3.84 billion in 2024 and -KRW 4.05 billion in 2025. Even as orders grow, staged payment terms that tie collections to material deposits, delivery and commissioning mean cash does not arrive at once. Whether the order backlog becomes operating cash inflow has yet to be shown.

Delayed investment and peer gap

As of September 30, 2025, none of the KRW 13.24 billion in IPO proceeds earmarked for facilities and operations had been spent. BHI, cited as a peer at listing, was reported to have grown revenue and operating profit sharply in 2025, making Woniltni's decline stand out. Delays in both fund deployment and earnings turnaround are a bearish factor.

Risk factors

Revenue recognition timing

The company attributed the 2025 shortfall partly to schedule adjustments during contract negotiations, which delayed revenue recognition. Quarterly revenue swings sharply with contract timing, as seen between KRW 3.99 billion in Q1 2026 and KRW 9.95 billion in Q2 2026. The company also notes that contract terms can change by agreement as progress varies.

Dependence on defense and overseas projects

Growth linked to hydrogen storage alloys and defense depends on whether overseas submarine projects are awarded. A gas industry publication described Canada, Poland, Saudi Arabia and the Philippines as under discussion, and no firm timing has been made public. If overseas projects are delayed, the expected revenue expansion could also slip.

Fund deployment and investment plan

Planned uses of IPO proceeds, including capacity expansion, a melting furnace replacement and R&D staffing, were reported as not yet executed as of September 30, 2025. Monthly average ordinary R&D expense in the third quarter of 2025 was 17% below the 2024 level. Delayed deployment could constrain capacity and the pace of new product development.

Overall view

Woniltni swung to losses in 2025, with revenue falling and both operating and net results turning negative, and operating cash flow was negative in both 2024 and 2025.

Several contracts in district energy, LNG terminals and hydrogen and gas equipment were disclosed in 2026, and revenue recovered on a quarterly basis in the second and third quarters of 2026.

Operating profit was positive in Q3 2026, but the net loss attributable to controlling shareholders widened to KRW 1.43 billion, so operating-level improvement has not yet carried through to net income. The debt ratio fell from 102.1% to 34.3%, which eases the financial structure.

Because the last four quarters show net losses, valuation cannot be described with an earnings multiple, and the closest benchmark is that the share price sits close to book net asset value.

Bullish factors are the wider contract flow and localized technology, while bearish factors are continued losses in earnings and cash flow and delayed use of IPO proceeds.

The next checkpoints are whether operating margin turns into a sustained positive trend in the 2026 annual report and how quickly the ongoing contracts convert into revenue and cash.
