02

Business structure

Chunil Express is an express bus operator founded in Busan in 1949 and listed on the KOSPI exchange in June 1977.

Nationwide passenger transport accounts for the bulk of revenue, with a historical segment breakdown showing roughly 96% from passenger transport and about 2% each from rental income and commission/other income. The company had 278 employees as of the end of June 2026.

Beyond its core operations, its key asset is a 16.67% stake in the Seoul Express Bus Terminal (SBT) in Banpo-dong, Seocho-gu, Seoul, where top shareholder Shinsegae Central City holds 70.49% and Dongyang Express holds 0.17%.

In the competitive landscape, the company competes with other listed express bus operators such as Dongyang Express, while high-speed rail services like KTX and SRT and low-cost air travel more broadly substitute for long-distance passenger demand.

In terms of ownership, the controlling shareholder group holds the large majority of outstanding shares, leaving a limited free float. Paid-in capital stands at roughly KRW 7.1 billion, against a balance sheet with a very high debt load relative to equity.

As a result, market valuation of the company appears to be driven more by expectations around the SBT real estate revaluation than by the transport business's operating performance.

04

Earnings analysis

Annual revenue rose for three straight years, from KRW 37.7 billion in 2022 to KRW 44 billion in 2023, KRW 44.7 billion in 2024, and KRW 45.6 billion in 2025, yet the operating loss, after narrowing from KRW 7.41 billion in 2022 to KRW 5.15 billion in 2023, widened again to KRW 5.27 billion in 2024 and KRW 5.94 billion in 2025, extending the operating loss streak to four consecutive years.

The net loss attributable to controlling shareholders also narrowed from KRW 6.01 billion in 2022 to KRW 4.81 billion in 2023 and KRW 4.2 billion in 2024 before widening again to KRW 5.62 billion in 2025.

The company cited the recognition of a value-in-use impairment on tangible assets as a key driver of the 2025 earnings swing. The operating margin improved from -19.7% in 2022 to -11.7% in 2023 before deteriorating again to -11.8% in 2024 and -13.0% in 2025, showing no clear turnaround.

On a quarterly basis, the company posted operating losses for five consecutive quarters from the second quarter of 2025 through the second quarter of 2026, though the second-quarter 2026 operating loss of KRW 1.08 billion narrowed both sequentially and year on year.

Net income attributable to controlling shareholders swung to a KRW 998 million profit in the second quarter of 2025 before turning to losses for four straight quarters from the third quarter of 2025 through the second quarter of 2026, with the second-quarter 2026 net loss of KRW 1.25 billion representing a swing from profit in the same quarter a year earlier.

Cumulative net losses attributable to controlling shareholders over the trailing four quarters (third quarter 2025 through second quarter 2026) reached KRW 7.63 billion.

Operating cash flow moved from -KRW 1.53 billion in 2022 and -KRW 1.39 billion in 2023 to -KRW 107 million in 2024 and then turned positive at KRW 312 million in 2025, suggesting a gradual improvement in cash generation.

05

Industry analysis

The express bus industry has faced a structural headwind as passenger demand has not fully recovered since the COVID-19 pandemic, with high-speed rail and low-cost air travel increasingly diverting long-distance travelers.

According to one media report, Chunil Express's operating result swung from a KRW 560 million profit in 2019 to a KRW 9.9 billion loss in 2020 when the pandemic began, followed by a KRW 9.8 billion loss in 2021.

From 2022 onward, the disclosed operating loss narrowed to the KRW 7 billion range but never turned positive. Industry commentary has noted that the core express bus business has been unable to escape operating losses in recent years across the sector.

Competitor Dongyang Express has faced similarly weak profitability, suggesting the issue reflects a broader structural challenge rather than one specific to a single company.

Against this backdrop, the share prices of both Chunil Express and Dongyang Express have shown an unusual pattern of moving in line with expectations for the Seoul Express Bus Terminal redevelopment rather than with core operating results.

Because the terminal site sits on prime real estate in Gangnam, Seoul, the possibility of a real estate value re-rating if the complex redevelopment proceeds continues to be discussed.

06

Outlook

As of October 2026, Seocho-gu's new municipal administration has reportedly been accelerating major private complex redevelopment projects, including the Seoul Express Bus Terminal site, continuing to relay operator requests and the district's position to relevant authorities through working-level discussions.

However, the complex redevelopment remains a long-horizon project requiring a pre-negotiation process with the Seoul city government, and no concrete permitting schedule or completion timeline has been formalized.

On the core business side, CEO Park Do-hyun indicated at the company's New Year ceremony in early 2026 that the external business environment would remain difficult, suggesting management itself is emphasizing operational discipline rather than projecting a near-term earnings turnaround.

The company disclosed a decision in April 2026 to increase short-term borrowings by KRW 2 billion to fund working capital, underscoring that operating cash generation alone has not been sufficient to cover funding needs.

On the revenue side, second-quarter 2026 revenue growth year on year points to some recovery, but whether this can be sustained quarter after quarter toward an operating profit turnaround requires confirmation in upcoming results.

Both the terminal redevelopment theme and core business performance are likely to remain the two separate variables shaping investor attention in the near term.

08

Bull factors

Seoul Express Bus Terminal Stake Revaluation Expectations

As the second-largest shareholder of the Seoul Express Bus Terminal with a 16.67% stake, Chunil Express benefits from Seocho-gu's accelerated push on the complex redevelopment project, which underpins expectations for an asset revaluation.

Because the terminal site sits on prime real estate in Gangnam, Seoul, a more concrete redevelopment could affect how the company's holding is valued. However, the project remains at the pre-negotiation stage, and no concrete permitting or completion schedule has been formalized.

Narrowing Quarterly Losses

Second-quarter 2026 revenue rose 8.6% year on year to KRW 12.4 billion, and the operating loss narrowed both sequentially and year on year to KRW 1.08 billion. The fourth quarter of 2025 also saw a sequential narrowing of the operating loss, suggesting some periods of improvement even as quarterly losses remain uneven. Whether this pattern can translate into a full-year operating profit turnaround has not yet been confirmed.

Operating Cash Flow Turned Positive

Operating cash flow remained negative from 2022 through 2024 before turning positive at KRW 312 million in 2025. This suggests a gradual improvement in cash flow terms even as the income statement continued to show losses. However, the absolute size remains small, limiting its impact on overall balance sheet improvement.

09

Bear factors

Four Straight Years of Operating Losses and Rising Leverage

The operating loss has persisted for four consecutive years, from KRW 7.41 billion in 2022 to KRW 5.94 billion in 2025, while the net loss attributable to controlling shareholders also widened to KRW 5.62 billion in 2025. The debt-to-equity ratio rose sharply each year, from 106.1% in 2022 to 458.6% in 2025. Repeated increases in short-term borrowings to fund working capital point to accumulating financial strain.

Terminal Redevelopment Still at an Early Stage

The Seoul Express Bus Terminal complex redevelopment remains at the pre-negotiation stage, and observers have noted that a considerable period is needed before the discussions show concrete progress.

While the market has speculated the terminal's overall value could exceed KRW 1 trillion, no officially disclosed figure has been confirmed. Given the project's long-term nature, delays remain possible at each stage, including permitting and design changes.

High Volatility from Low Free Float

With the controlling shareholder group holding roughly 85% of shares, actual free-floating shares are limited. As a result, relatively small buy or sell volumes have repeatedly led to large price swings. This limited-liquidity structure carries the risk of distorted price discovery.

Risk factors

Financial Soundness Risk

The debt-to-equity ratio reached 458.6% at the end of 2025, with total equity of only KRW 9.58 billion, reflecting a very high debt burden relative to equity capital. In April 2026, the company disclosed a decision to increase short-term borrowings by KRW 2 billion to fund working capital.

If operating losses persist alongside greater reliance on external borrowing, interest expense burdens could increase further.

Business Structure Risk

The express bus industry faces structural dispersion of long-distance passenger demand due to the expansion of alternative transport modes such as high-speed rail and low-cost air travel. Gross margins have remained very low, reflecting slow profitability improvement in the core transport business.

Fixed-cost items such as fuel and labor expenses accrue regardless of revenue fluctuations, pressuring the overall profit structure.

Governance and Liquidity Risk

With the controlling shareholder group holding roughly 85% of shares, free float is limited, a structural feature that amplifies share price volatility.

Repeated short-term supply-and-demand shifts tied to the Seoul Express Bus Terminal redevelopment theme can widen information asymmetry, complicating investment assessment. Disclosure around the valuation methodology for the key SBT stake also remains relatively limited.

12

Overall view

Chunil Express has struggled to restore profitability in its core express bus business, recording operating losses for four consecutive years from 2022 through 2025, while its debt-to-equity ratio has steadily deteriorated to 458.6%.

Still, the second quarter of 2026 showed both revenue growth and a narrower operating loss year on year, and operating cash flow turned positive for the first time in 2025, offering some positive signals alongside the structural challenges.

Market attention and share price moves have been driven less by transport business performance and more by expectations for a revaluation of the company's 16.67% stake in the Seoul Express Bus Terminal, a theme that has gained volatility alongside news of Seocho-gu accelerating the complex redevelopment project.

However, the terminal redevelopment remains at the pre-negotiation stage with no formalized permitting or completion schedule, and with the controlling shareholder group holding roughly 85% of shares, limited free float means even small trading volumes can move the share price sharply.

The next quarterly report and any official announcements on the terminal redevelopment will likely serve as the next key junctures for assessing both core business performance and asset value expectations.

Readers should weigh both the disclosed financial results and the progress of the redevelopment project before forming any investment view.
