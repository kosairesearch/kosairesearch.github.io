02

Business structure

Chunil Express is a passenger transport company listed on KOSPI since 1977, centered on intercity bus operations. Press reports describe the company as operating 125 bus routes. Before the COVID-19 pandemic, revenue was around KRW 60 billion, but bus transport sales fell sharply when passenger numbers dropped.

Revenue in 2025 was KRW 45.6 billion according to the filing. Detailed revenue breakdown by segment is not included here because no verified figures were available. Press reports put the gross margin at roughly 2%, which reflects a low-profitability core transport business.

Competition centers on route-level rivalry with other intercity bus operators, with Dongyang Express as the most common comparison.

On the asset side, the key item is the 16.67% stake in Seoul Express Bus Terminal, where Shinsegae Central City holds 70.49% as the largest shareholder and Chunil Express is the second-largest. The largest shareholder and related parties hold 85.74% of the shares, which limits the tradable float.

04

Earnings analysis

Based on the filed results, revenue rose from KRW 37.7 billion in 2022 to KRW 44 billion in 2023, a 16.7% increase, then to KRW 44.7 billion in 2024, up 1.6%, and to KRW 45.6 billion in 2025, up 1.9%. Revenue has grown for three consecutive years from 2023 to 2025, but the pace of growth slowed sharply after 2023.

Operating losses were KRW 7.41 billion in 2022, KRW 5.15 billion in 2023, KRW 5.27 billion in 2024 and KRW 5.94 billion in 2025, which is four consecutive years of operating losses from 2022 to 2025. The operating margin improved from -19.7% in 2022 to -11.7% in 2023, then weakened to -11.8% in 2024 and -13.0% in 2025.

Net losses attributable to controlling shareholders were KRW 6.01 billion, KRW 4.81 billion, KRW 4.2 billion and KRW 5.62 billion for 2022 to 2025. Operating cash flow moved from negative KRW 1.53 billion in 2022 to negative KRW 107 million in 2024 and turned to positive KRW 312 million in 2025.

Press reports quoted the company as attributing the 2025 change to the recognition of impairment on the value in use of tangible assets.

On a quarterly basis, revenue was KRW 10.9 billion in the third quarter of 2025, KRW 12.5 billion in the fourth quarter, KRW 11.2 billion in the first quarter of 2026, KRW 12.4 billion in the second quarter and KRW 12.1 billion in the third quarter of 2026, which was up 10.7% year on year.

Operating losses ran for five consecutive quarters from the third quarter of 2025 to the third quarter of 2026. Net losses attributable to controlling shareholders widened to KRW 2.86 billion in the fourth quarter of 2025, and the combined loss for the last four quarters was KRW 7.65 billion.

05

Industry analysis

Intercity bus operations are directly exposed to passenger demand and the transport cycle. Financial press reports have described Dongyang Express and Chunil Express as posting long stretches of losses as the transport environment weakened.

Industry conditions recovered after COVID-19, but Chunil Express did not return to profit, according to the same coverage. Dongyang Express reported a return to profit in 2025, according to a February 2026 report by Sisaweek.

This shows that results can diverge within the same market depending on cost structure and route efficiency. Revenue growth slowed to 1.6% in 2024 and 1.9% in 2025, so sales expansion has not translated into better operating results. On the demand side, the pace of passenger recovery is the key variable.

Redevelopment of large urban terminals could reshape routes and facilities over the long term, although these are still at the pre-negotiation stage. Securities reports have repeatedly described the company as a small or mid-sized intercity bus operator that trails profitable peers in both scale and operating margin.

06

Outlook

At the January 2026 New Year ceremony, CEO Park Do-hyun said the external environment would remain difficult and called for consolidating the business and specializing in its strengths.

Third-quarter 2026 revenue rose 10.7% year on year and the operating loss narrowed from a year earlier, which is consistent with that direction. The key variable is the redevelopment of Seoul Express Bus Terminal.

In November 2025, Seoul City announced that it would begin full-scale pre-negotiations with Shinsegae Central City and Seoul Express Bus Terminal on a mixed-use redevelopment of the Seocho-gu site.

A private proposal describes consolidating the aging intercity terminals underground and modernizing them, with a maximum height above 60 floors. Pre-negotiations are still at an early stage, and zoning changes, building permits and resident consultation remain ahead.

Media estimates repeatedly put the start of construction five to six years away. In May 2026, after Shinsegae reported strong results, redevelopment expectations returned and the stock hit the daily upper limit in early trading. Whether the core business recovers can be judged by whether quarterly operating losses keep narrowing.

Bull factors

Value of the Seoul Express Bus Terminal stake

The company holds a 16.67% stake in Seoul Express Bus Terminal as the second-largest shareholder. Seoul City announced the start of pre-negotiations for a mixed-use redevelopment on November 26, 2025, and a private proposal includes underground consolidation and a height above 60 floors. Bulls argue that actual progress could provide grounds for revaluing this stake.

Narrowing quarterly losses

Third-quarter 2026 revenue was KRW 12.1 billion, up 10.7% year on year, and the operating loss of KRW 1.61 billion was smaller than a year earlier. The KRW 1.08 billion operating loss in the second quarter of 2026 was the smallest among the last five quarters. If this pattern continues, it would support a narrowing of core business losses.

Turn to positive operating cash flow

Operating cash flow improved from negative KRW 1.53 billion in 2022 to negative KRW 107 million in 2024 and turned positive at KRW 312 million in 2025. Revenue has grown for three consecutive years from 2023 to 2025. Bulls point to the cash generation shown even while operating losses continue.

Bear factors

Continued core operating losses

Operating losses have continued for four years from 2022 to 2025, and the 2025 loss of KRW 5.94 billion widened from KRW 5.27 billion in 2024. The operating margin was -13.0% in 2025. Bears point to five consecutive quarters of operating losses from the third quarter of 2025 to the third quarter of 2026.

Rising financial burden

The debt ratio climbed from 106.1% in 2022 to 458.6% in 2025. Total liabilities were KRW 44 billion at the end of 2025 against total equity of KRW 9.58 billion. Press coverage pointed to short-term borrowings that are large relative to cash. Bears argue that the structure leaves little room for earnings to absorb debt.

Gap between share price and earnings

Securities reports have repeatedly pointed to a wide gap between redevelopment expectations and reported earnings. The largest shareholder and related parties hold 85.74%, leaving a thin float, and the stock has already been subject to investment warnings and trading halts. Bears point to the history of sharp rallies driven by events rather than earnings.

Risk factors

Redevelopment timing risk

The mixed-use redevelopment of Seoul Express Bus Terminal is still in pre-negotiations and must clear zoning changes, building permits and resident consultation. Media estimates put the first groundbreaking five to six years away. Delays or changes in scale could reset expectations for the value of the stake.

Liquidity and financial risk

With a debt ratio of 458.6%, changes in borrowing conditions could raise funding pressure. Operating cash flow turned positive in 2025, but the amount was only KRW 312 million. If core losses persist, reliance on borrowing could rise.

Trading flow and volatility risk

The largest shareholder and related parties hold 85.74%, so the tradable float is small. As a result, modest trading volumes have moved the share price sharply, and the exchange has previously designated the stock for investment warnings and halted trading. Sharp declines driven by trading flows remain possible.
