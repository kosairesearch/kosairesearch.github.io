02

Business structure

TYM runs three strategic business units: agricultural machinery, filters, and other products. It supplies tractors, combines, and riding rice transplanters, with main agricultural machinery sites in Korea, the United States, Canada, and Europe.

Agricultural machinery accounts for the bulk of sales, at 92.4% of sales in a disclosure summary. The filter segment supplies cut tobacco and cigarette filters, and its sales fell 2.6% year on year in the latest summary.

North America is the growth engine: expansion of large-model lineups and the dealer network were cited as drivers of first-half 2026 improvement in the farm machinery segment. Large tractor orders tied to the Philippines' agricultural mechanization policy are also cited as overseas support.

In May 2025, a KRW 35 billion tractor supply contract with Indonesia was reported. On technology, the company's autonomous farm machinery passed Korea's national type inspection, and in November 2025 it began full-scale development of an autonomous lawn mower with Samsung C&T's resort division.

Among domestic farm machinery makers, TYM is described as one of the two largest. In North America, dealer networks and service coverage look central to competitiveness.

04

Earnings analysis

Annual sales went from KRW 1.17 trillion in 2022 to KRW 836.5 billion in 2023, down 28.3% year on year, and KRW 788.8 billion in 2024, down 5.7%, before rising to KRW 929.4 billion in 2025, up 17.8%. 2022 was the largest year in the 2022–2025 span and 2024 the smallest.

Operating profit fell from KRW 122 billion in 2022 to KRW 76.5 billion in 2023, down 37.3%, and KRW 16.1 billion in 2024, down 79.0%, then recovered to KRW 64.1 billion in 2025, up 298.5%.

Operating margin was 10.5% in 2022, 9.1% in 2023, 2.0% in 2024, and 6.9% in 2025, so profitability recovered after sales and margins contracted together.

Net income attributable to controlling shareholders was KRW 98.2 billion in 2022, KRW 60.4 billion in 2023, KRW 18.2 billion in 2024, and KRW 40.1 billion in 2025, with 2024 the smallest year. Over these years, total net income including non-controlling interests was reported at the same amounts.

The debt ratio fell from 133.8% in 2022 to 102.3% in 2025. At year-end 2025, total liabilities were KRW 431 billion and total equity was KRW 421.4 billion. Operating cash flow was KRW 20.7 billion in 2022, KRW 15.1 billion in 2023, KRW 89.8 billion in 2024, and KRW 131.3 billion in 2025.

Quarterly operating profit went from KRW 8.78 billion in Q4 2025, down 44.9% from the prior quarter, to KRW 35 billion in Q1 2026, up 299.2%. Sales peaked at KRW 289.7 billion in Q1 2026, up 33.6% from the prior quarter, then fell to KRW 262.5 billion in Q2 and KRW 260.1 billion in Q3, two straight quarterly declines.

Operating profit fell to KRW 27.6 billion in Q2, down 21.2%, then rose to KRW 35.8 billion in Q3, up 29.7%, diverging from sales.

Net income attributable to controlling shareholders peaked at KRW 30.3 billion in Q1 2026, then fell to KRW 26.5 billion in Q2 and KRW 20.1 billion in Q3, and the last four quarters total KRW 82.8 billion. In the first half of 2026, consolidated sales rose 13.1% and operating profit rose 59.1% year on year.

05

Industry analysis

Demand for agricultural machinery, unlike construction equipment, moves with the farm population, farm incomes, and government mechanization policy. Farm income, grain prices, and interest rates act as the variables that shape the demand cycle.

In the domestic farm machinery industry, new businesses such as agricultural robots and autonomous machinery have drawn investor attention. Autonomous farm machinery connects to this trend, and TYM highlights that its autonomous models passed Korea's national type inspection.

In North America, large tractor demand and the dealer channel are the key factors. First-half 2026 expansion of large models and the dealer network was reflected in results. In Southeast Asia, mechanization policy appears to determine orders.

Policy cooperation in the Philippines led to large tractor orders, and the KRW 35 billion Indonesian contract follows the same pattern. In October 2025, TYM said it would upgrade its Southeast Asian service and parts system, with an integrated center planned by 2026 and technical staff training.

Currency and tariffs are also variables. Reporting on first-half 2025 results noted a response to a 15% US reciprocal tariff through a high-margin portfolio. Whether the profit recovery reflects industry conditions or company-specific factors should be separated through the quarterly trend.

Outlook

The business directions confirmed so far are three: expanding North American lineups, entering Europe and Southeast Asia, and commercializing autonomous farm machinery.

The next checkpoint is whether the North American large tractor expansion and dealer network growth cited for first-half 2026 improvement continue in later quarters.

On shareholder returns, TYM decided on November 11, 2026 to buy back shares worth about KRW 10 billion, covering 1,538,461 shares, roughly 3.7% of issued shares, from November 12, 2026 to February 11, 2027. The interim dividend was raised to 2.4 times the prior year.

After a large treasury share cancellation decided last year, the company is pairing a higher interim dividend with additional buybacks this year. The autonomous lawn mower work with Samsung C&T's resort division signals expansion beyond farm machinery.

In October 2025, the company said it planned an integrated Southeast Asian service center by 2026. The next formal checkpoint is the 2026 annual report, which will confirm full-year 2026 sales and operating profit. Whether the decline in sales after Q1 2026 reflects seasonality or a trend can be judged from Q1 2027 results.

Bull factors

Operating profit recovery

Q3 2026 operating profit was KRW 35.8 billion, up 124.7% year on year. First-half 2026 consolidated sales rose 13.1% and operating profit rose 59.1%. Q1 and Q3 2026 are the top two operating profit quarters among the last five on the disclosed record. Whether the margin recovery from 2.0% in 2024 to 6.9% in 2025 continues is the point to watch.

Expanded shareholder returns

The interim dividend was raised to 2.4 times the prior year alongside buybacks. The company decided on buybacks of about KRW 10 billion, covering roughly 3.7% of issued shares. The buyback period runs through February 11, 2027.

Following a large treasury share cancellation last year, the continuation of this return policy is cited as a supportive factor.

North American and Southeast Asian expansion

Reports cite North American large-model expansion, dealer network growth, and Philippine mechanization policy cooperation, which led to large tractor orders, as drivers of farm machinery segment improvement.

The point to check next is when the Indonesian tractor supply contract reported in May 2025 is reflected in actual sales. With sites in North America and Europe, regional expansion can broaden the profit base.

Bear factors

Consecutive quarterly sales declines

Sales peaked at KRW 289.7 billion in Q1 2026, then fell to KRW 262.5 billion in Q2 and KRW 260.1 billion in Q3. Q3 sales rose 16.0% year on year but fell 0.9% from the prior quarter. Operating profit rose in Q3, but if sales keep declining, the durability of the profit recovery becomes an open question.

Long-run profitability decline

Operating margin fell from 10.5% in 2022 to 2.0% in 2024 and recovered to 6.9% in 2025, still below the 2022 level. Net income attributable to controlling shareholders fell from KRW 98.2 billion in 2022 to KRW 18.2 billion in 2024 before reaching KRW 40.1 billion in 2025. That the recovery is smaller than the earlier peak is a bearish factor.

Concentration in farm machinery

A disclosure summary put agricultural machinery at 92.4% of sales, so the business is concentrated in farm machinery. Filter sales fell 2.6% year on year. A slowdown in farm machinery demand or in North America could move overall results together, which is a bearish factor.

Risk factors

Tariffs and currency

Reporting on first-half 2025 results noted a response to a 15% US reciprocal tariff through a high-margin portfolio. The larger the North American share of sales, the more tariffs and currency moves can affect margins. Whether price pass-through holds will show in quarterly margin trends.

Financial costs and debt

At year-end 2025, total liabilities were KRW 431 billion and total equity was KRW 421.4 billion, close in size. Financial costs of KRW 6.5 billion in Q1 2025 were reported as a burden on profitability. The debt ratio fell from 133.8% in 2022 to 102.3% in 2025, but interest rate changes can still affect earnings.

Dependence on large orders and policy timing

Large orders such as the Philippine policy-linked tractor deals and the Indonesian KRW 35 billion contract can depend on the policy and budget timing of specific countries. Quarterly sales swings in 2026 show how timing can move results. Earnings visibility depends on how much order backlog and delivery schedules are disclosed.

Overall view

TYM restored its operating margin from 2.0% in 2024 to 6.9% in 2025 and continued the profit recovery, with Q3 2026 operating profit up 124.7% to KRW 35.8 billion.

Over the same period, sales peaked at KRW 289.7 billion in Q1 2026 and then declined for two straight quarters, so profit recovery and the sales trend are diverging.

Supportive factors include dealer network expansion in North America, large tractor orders in the Philippines and Indonesia, a higher interim dividend, and a KRW 10 billion buyback.

Negative factors include concentration in farm machinery, a smaller profit base than in 2022, and exposure to tariffs, currency, and financing costs. On valuation, a share price well below book value per share coexists with a lower earnings multiple as profits recover.

The next formal checkpoints are the 2026 annual report and the Q1 2027 quarterly report, with the progress of the buyback also worth tracking. Whether Q4 2026 profit holds near the Q3 level is the first indicator of how durable the recovery is.
