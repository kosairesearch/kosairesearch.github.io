Foodwell: Margins Rise as Capacity and Debt Weigh

02

Business structure

Foodwell is a KOSDAQ-listed food manufacturer headquartered in Daegu that started with jams, syrups and beverages and expanded through mergers and acquisitions into frozen foods, health functional foods and home meal replacements (HMR).

Based on the third-quarter 2026 report, sales for the first nine months by product were KRW 57.7 billion for jams and syrups, KRW 52.4 billion for health and functional foods, and KRW 49.5 billion for dumplings, and these three categories together exceed half of total sales.

HMR sales rose 45.1% year on year to KRW 15.5 billion over the same period, while frozen products and purees fell 25.6% to KRW 28.0 billion, showing a wide growth gap across product lines.

The company operates mainly on an original equipment manufacturing (OEM) and business-to-business (B2B) basis, so customer order volumes and raw material prices directly affect profitability.

As of March 2026, the disclosed market shares were 30% for strawberry syrups and 28% for exported peeled chestnuts, which are shipped to Japan and other markets. Production is spread across a Daegu plant, a Qingdao plant in China, and three subsidiary plants in Cheonan.

Qingdao Foodwell Food Co. recorded sales of KRW 66.3 billion and net income of KRW 7.6 billion in the first nine months of 2026 and was identified as a major profit contributor. The average utilization of a health-food subsidiary plant was 177.09% in the first half of 2026, above its rated capacity.

Because the customer base is concentrated in B2B relationships, dependence on individual buyers also remains a factor in earnings swings.

04

Earnings analysis

Annual revenue rose from KRW 183.9 billion in 2022 to KRW 228.5 billion in 2023 (up 24.2% year over year), KRW 273.0 billion in 2024 (up 19.5% year over year), and KRW 320.3 billion in 2025 (up 17.3% year over year), increasing for three consecutive years from 2023 to 2025, and 2025 was the highest year in the 2022–2025 period.

Operating profit rose from KRW 6.06 billion in 2022 to KRW 8.29 billion in 2023 (up 36.7% year over year), KRW 13.9 billion in 2024 (up 68.1% year over year), and KRW 18.4 billion in 2025 (up 31.8% year over year), increasing for three consecutive years from 2023 to 2025.

The operating margin improved to 3.3% in 2022, 3.6% in 2023, 5.1% in 2024, and 5.7% in 2025.

Net profit attributable to controlling shareholders grew from KRW 1.45 billion in 2022 to KRW 6.33 billion in 2023 (up 337.2% year over year), KRW 7.3 billion in 2024 (up 15.3% year over year), and KRW 10.0 billion in 2025 (up 37.3% year over year).

Net profit including non-controlling interests was KRW 12.0 billion in 2025, which differs from net profit attributable to controlling shareholders, and the difference is the non-controlling interests' share.

Operating cash flow rose from KRW 6.8 billion in 2022 to KRW 21.7 billion in 2024, then fell to KRW 16.9 billion in 2025.

On a quarterly basis, from revenue of KRW 81.7 billion, operating profit of KRW 5.66 billion, and net profit attributable to controlling shareholders of KRW 3.0 billion in the third quarter of 2025, the fourth quarter of 2025 declined to revenue of KRW 79.0 billion (down 3.3% quarter over quarter), operating profit of KRW 1.72 billion (down 69.6% quarter over quarter), and net profit attributable to controlling shareholders of KRW 1.12 billion (down 62.7% quarter over quarter).

After recovering to operating profit of KRW 5.85 billion in the first quarter of 2026 (up 239.6% quarter over quarter) and KRW 8.88 billion in the second quarter (up 52.0% quarter over quarter), the company posted third-quarter revenue of KRW 93.4 billion (up 14.4% year over year) and operating profit of KRW 7.37 billion (up 30.3% year over year).

The sum of net profit attributable to controlling shareholders over the latest four quarters is KRW 15.1 billion. According to the third-quarter 2026 report, the cumulative January–September operating margin was 8.1%, higher than 6.9% in the same period of 2025.

In the third quarter, the growth rate of cost of sales (13.4%) was lower than the growth rate of revenue (14.4%), so gross profit increased 19.5%, and the growth rate of selling and administrative expenses was only 11.2%.

05

Industry analysis

Demand from downstream markets is strongest in health functional foods and frozen foods. In the third quarter of 2025, three of the five subsidiary plants averaged utilization above 100%, and the health-food plant recorded 177.09% utilization in the first half of 2026.

When the 2025 reports were published, the company said it had no plan to add factories and would extend operating hours to round-the-clock, but capital spending increased sharply in 2026.

In food manufacturing, profitability depends largely on how much raw material cost can be passed through to supply prices, a point that matters more for Foodwell given its OEM and B2B model.

Lotte Wellfood, a listed peer in the same sector, reported third-quarter 2026 sales of KRW 1,173.9 billion (up 1.5% year on year) and operating profit of KRW 89.9 billion (up 29.7%), with a cumulative operating margin of 5.7% for the first three quarters.

The scale gap is large, so comparing absolute figures has limited meaning, but the direction of operating profit growth at both companies points to a shared sector trend.

Foodwell's cumulative operating margin of 8.1% for the first nine months of 2026 was higher than Lotte Wellfood's 5.7% over the same period, although the two business models differ and a direct comparison has limits.

Within Foodwell, jams and syrups, health foods and dumplings drove growth while frozen products and purees declined, so product-level cycles diverge.

06

Outlook

The most concrete items to watch are the execution and payoff of capital spending. Property, plant and equipment purchases reached KRW 24.0 billion in the first nine months of 2026, more than triple the KRW 7.6 billion of a year earlier, with KRW 19.5 billion concentrated in the second quarter.

The company said the increase reflected the relocation of a health-food subsidiary plant, that the acquisitions are complete, and that the relocation will be finished in 2027 after remodeling and construction. The next checkpoint is therefore how far utilization falls once the relocation is done.

In the third quarter, operating cash flow of KRW 16.6 billion and capital spending of KRW 3.6 billion lifted cash and equivalents from KRW 14.1 billion at the end of June to KRW 23.7 billion at the end of September.

Working capital changes accounted for 41% of third-quarter operating cash generation, so the quality of cash inflows depends on inventory and advance receipts.

Borrowings stood at KRW 78.6 billion at the end of September 2026, KRW 4.6 billion more than at the end of 2025, and KRW 47.6 billion of them carried variable rates.

On shareholder returns, the company resolved in September 2026 to cancel 200,000 treasury shares and dispose of 331,743 shares; of the disposal, 250,000 shares were sold to an investment firm through an off-market block trade, and the remaining 81,743 shares will be granted as employee performance rewards.

On product mix, HMR sales rose 45.1% in the first nine months of 2026 while frozen products and purees fell 25.6%, so how the growing categories shift the sales mix in coming quarters is the next point to track.

Bull factors

Sustained margin improvement

The operating margin rose from 3.3% in 2022 to 5.7% in 2025, and the cumulative margin for the first nine months of 2026 reached 8.1%, above 6.9% a year earlier. In the third quarter, gross profit growth of 19.5% outpaced the 11.2% rise in selling and administrative expenses. Sales growth has continued to translate into profit.

Growth from newer product lines

Home meal replacement sales reached KRW 15.5 billion in the first nine months of 2026, up 45.1% year on year. Health and functional foods reached KRW 52.4 billion and dumplings KRW 49.5 billion, and these three lines together exceed half of sales. A mix that does not depend on a single product line is a supporting factor.

Demand running ahead of capacity

A health-food subsidiary plant ran at 177.09% utilization in the first half of 2026, which points to demand above rated capacity. The company has raised property, plant and equipment purchases to KRW 24.0 billion in the first nine months of 2026 in response. If the new capacity comes online, it could translate into higher volumes.

Bear factors

Volatility in quarterly earnings

Operating profit fell to KRW 1.72 billion in the fourth quarter of 2025, down 69.6% from the prior quarter, and controlling-shareholder net profit dropped 62.7% to KRW 1.12 billion. Earnings recovered in 2026, but large swings have recurred quarter to quarter. The coexistence of an annual uptrend with sharp quarterly swings is a weak point.

Debt and capital spending burden

Borrowings were KRW 78.6 billion at the end of September 2026, of which KRW 57.8 billion falls due within one year, and KRW 47.6 billion carries variable rates. In the second quarter, capital spending of KRW 19.5 billion far exceeded operating cash flow of KRW 6.9 billion. The debt-to-equity ratio rose to 104.9% at the end of 2025 from 97.4% in 2024.

Product-level gaps and cost pass-through

Sales of frozen products and purees fell 25.6% year on year to KRW 28.0 billion in the first nine months of 2026. Because the company operates mainly on OEM and B2B terms, failing to pass higher raw material costs into supply prices would weigh on profitability. Declines in some product lines and the limits of cost pass-through are weak points.

10

Risk factors

Raw materials and supply pricing

Under an OEM and B2B model, profitability depends on how quickly raw material cost increases are passed into supply prices. Fluctuations in customer order volumes can move utilization and sales at the same time. With operating margins in the 5 to 8% range, delayed pass-through could compress margins quickly.

Capacity execution and plant relocation

Relocation of a health-food subsidiary plant is planned for completion in 2027. Production disruption during relocation and remodeling could delay any utilization gains. Because capital spending has grown, the pace at which the investment pays back will matter for future earnings.

Interest rates and debt maturities

Of KRW 78.6 billion in borrowings at the end of September 2026, KRW 57.8 billion falls due within one year. Variable-rate borrowings of KRW 47.6 billion could raise financing costs if rates rise.

Cash and equivalents rose to KRW 23.7 billion, but financing conditions remain important given the near-term repayment burden.

Overall view

Foodwell's revenue and operating profit both rose from 2022 through 2025, and the operating margin improved from 3.3% to 5.7%. The cumulative operating margin for the first nine months of 2026 reached 8.1%, above the same period a year earlier. Growth lines such as HMR and health foods are broadening the sales mix.

On the other hand, quarterly earnings swung sharply, as in the fourth quarter of 2025, and property, plant and equipment purchases rose to KRW 24.0 billion in the first nine months of 2026. Of KRW 78.6 billion in borrowings, KRW 57.8 billion falls due within one year, so financial burden also needs to be tracked.

A health-food plant running at 177% utilization is evidence of demand above capacity, but it also requires that capacity execution and cost pass-through hold up. On shareholder returns, the company decided in September 2026 to cancel and dispose of treasury shares.
