Foodwell: Nine-Month Sales Up 13% as Profit Recovers

Foodwell's nine-month sales through the third quarter of 2026 rose 13.0% year on year, and operating profit has grown for three straight years through 2025. Quarterly earnings volatility and the decline in frozen and puree sales are the points to track next.

02

Business structure

Foodwell was founded in 1968 as a farm produce storage business and listed on KOSDAQ in 1993, later widening its scope through several acquisitions. Its structure, once centered on jams and syrups, is broadening into frozen foods, home meal replacement (HMR) and health functional foods.

Nine-month sales by product through the third-quarter 2026 report were KRW 57.7 billion for jams and syrups, KRW 52.4 billion for health and health functional foods, and KRW 49.5 billion for dumplings. HMR sales in the same period reached KRW 15.5 billion, up 45.1% year on year.

Frozen and puree sales, by contrast, fell 25.6% to KRW 28.0 billion, so growth rates diverge across product lines. Press coverage of the third quarter cited jams and syrups, health foods and dumplings as the main growth drivers. Acquisitions have been identified as a major factor behind recent top-line growth.

The company is known as a food maker based in Daegu. Competition varies by category: jams, syrups and dumplings compete with large packaged-food listed companies for shelf space, while frozen foods and HMR are compared with larger players such as Otoki and CJ Cheiljedang. Health functional foods depend on raw material access and distribution channels.

04

Earnings analysis

On an annual basis, sales rose from KRW 183.9 billion in 2022 to KRW 228.5 billion in 2023 (up 24.2% year on year), KRW 273 billion in 2024 (up 19.5%) and KRW 320.3 billion in 2025 (up 17.3%).

Operating profit grew from KRW 6.06 billion in 2022 to KRW 8.29 billion in 2023, KRW 13.9 billion in 2024 and KRW 18.4 billion in 2025, and the operating margin widened from 3.3% to 5.7%. Operating profit rose for three consecutive years from 2023 through 2025, and 2025 was the largest year of the 2022 to 2025 span.

Attributable net income moved from KRW 1.45 billion in 2022 to KRW 6.33 billion in 2023 (up 337.2%), KRW 7.3 billion in 2024 and KRW 10 billion in 2025.

Net income including non-controlling interests, which is a different measure, was KRW 2.02 billion in 2022 and KRW 12 billion in 2025, and the gap reflects the share belonging to non-controlling interests.

At the end of 2025, total equity was KRW 116.2 billion, of which KRW 102.7 billion belonged to controlling shareholders, against total liabilities of KRW 121.9 billion. The debt-to-equity ratio accordingly rose from 97.4% in 2024 to 104.9% in 2025.

Operating cash flow climbed from KRW 6.8 billion in 2022 to KRW 21.7 billion in 2024 before falling to KRW 16.9 billion in 2025.

On a quarterly basis, sales went from KRW 81.7 billion in the third quarter of 2025 to KRW 79 billion in the fourth quarter (down 3.3% from the prior quarter), then reached KRW 94.2 billion in the second quarter of 2026, and the third quarter came in at KRW 93.4 billion (down 0.8% from the prior quarter, up 14.4% year on year).

Operating profit dropped to KRW 1.72 billion in the fourth quarter of 2025, a 69.6% decline, and then recovered to KRW 5.85 billion in the first quarter of 2026 and KRW 8.88 billion in the second quarter, before KRW 7.37 billion in the third quarter (down 17.0% from the prior quarter, up 30.3% year on year).

Attributable net income hit a low of KRW 1.12 billion in the fourth quarter of 2025 and has risen for three consecutive quarters since, reaching KRW 5.88 billion in the third quarter of 2026.

Press coverage put the third-quarter 2026 operating margin at 7.9%, about one percentage point above the roughly 6.9% of a year earlier. Quarterly swings are large, so it is too early to judge the trend from one or two quarters.

05

Industry analysis

Foodwell's core products depend on household consumption and demand for processed foods. Jams, syrups and dumplings are sensitive to price levels and consumption patterns. Frozen foods and HMR connect to changes in meal habits, such as the spread of dual-income and single-person households.

Health functional foods are affected together by raw material prices, regulation and shifts in online distribution. Across the food and beverage sector, the ability to pass raw material cost changes on to selling prices is widely cited as a key driver of profit margins.

Listed Korean food companies differ in business mix across processed foods, beverages and dairy, and Foodwell is small in sales scale compared with the largest players such as CJ Cheiljedang and Lotte Wellfood.

Within the same sector, Foodwell combines long-established core lines such as jams and syrups with acquired frozen and HMR operations. The decline in frozen and puree sales can be read as a challenge arising from portfolio restructuring. Where the sector sits in its cycle is difficult to determine from the available data alone.

Bull factors

Measured against the KRW 1.12 billion low in the fourth quarter of 2025, the recovery is pronounced. Nine-month sales also rose 13.0% year on year, so top-line and profit are moving together.

Growth in newer businesses such as HMR

HMR sales reached KRW 15.5 billion in the first nine months of 2026, up 45.1% year on year, according to the third-quarter 2026 report. Press coverage links the expansion into frozen foods and health functional foods to acquisitions. Compared with the past, when revenue leaned heavily on jams and syrups, the business mix is being diversified.

Shareholder return measures and dividend trend

In September 2026 the company decided to cancel 200,000 treasury shares and to dispose of 331,743 shares. Dividends have increased over the last three year-end readings. These steps could be read as an effort to expand cash returns to shareholders regardless of the share price level.

Bear factors

Large swings in quarterly profit

Operating profit fell 69.6% from the prior quarter to KRW 1.72 billion in the fourth quarter of 2025, and the third quarter of 2026 was 17.0% lower than the second quarter. Swings of this size make it hard to read the trend from one or two quarters.

Third-quarter operating profit was higher than a year earlier but below the KRW 8.88 billion of the second quarter.

Uneven growth across product lines

Frozen and puree sales fell 25.6% year on year to KRW 28.0 billion over the first nine months of 2026. Even as core lines grow, the decline in this category could limit overall top-line growth.

Whether this is a transitional effect of restructuring will need to be checked against product-level trends in the 2026 annual report.

Balance sheet load and slower cash generation

The debt-to-equity ratio rose to 104.9% at end-2025 from 97.4% a year earlier, and total liabilities stood at KRW 121.9 billion. Operating cash flow fell from KRW 21.7 billion in 2024 to KRW 16.9 billion in 2025. If acquisitions and treasury share disposals continue, managing financial capacity will matter more.

Risk factors

Acquisition integration and portfolio restructuring

Integrating the acquired frozen food and HMR operations with the existing organization remains a task for raising profitability. Frozen and puree sales are declining, so the pace of restructuring directly affects results.

Whether acquired assets are impaired can be checked in the notes to the financial statements in periodic reports.

Quarterly earnings volatility and cost pressure

Quarterly operating profit ranged widely, from KRW 1.72 billion in the fourth quarter of 2025 to KRW 8.88 billion in the second quarter of 2026. Because raw material prices and product mix affect quarterly margins, rising cost pressure could disrupt the profit recovery. The risk may be larger in categories where price pass-through is difficult.

Treasury share disposals and supply effects

A block of 250,000 treasury shares was sold through an off-market block trade, which could increase the volume available in the market. Because cancellation and disposal are proceeding together, changes in shareholder composition could affect supply and demand. The remaining disposal plan will need to be confirmed through disclosures.

Overall view

Foodwell is a company whose revenue and operating profit growth since 2022 can be confirmed through its filings. Operating profit and attributable net income both rose for three consecutive years from 2023 through 2025, and nine-month sales through the third quarter of 2026 were up 13.0% year on year.

On the business side, the shift from a jam and syrup focus toward HMR and health functional foods is underway. On the other hand, the decline in frozen and puree sales, large swings in quarterly operating profit, a higher debt-to-equity ratio and lower operating cash flow remain weak points.

On valuation, the current multiples sit below their year-end levels of the past three years, and the price-to-book ratio is below 1x, which are factual comparisons. Whether the low multiples reflect a judgment on earnings durability or capital structure and supply factors cannot be settled from filings alone.

The points to track are product-level sales and the operating margin in the 2026 annual report, the attributable net income trend in the first quarter of 2027, and the progress of remaining treasury share disposals.
