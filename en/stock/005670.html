02

Business structure

Foodwell is a food processing company founded in 1968 in Daegu, producing fruit syrups and jams, beverages, canned goods, frozen products and purees, frozen dumplings and health foods.

Its annual report says it has produced and sold health functional foods in stick, tablet and pill forms since an acquisition in August 2022. In the 2024 annual report, the revenue mix was led by dumplings at 22.9 percent, health and functional foods at 19.8 percent, and jams and syrups at 16.9 percent.

For the first half of 2026 on a consolidated basis, jams and syrups accounted for 22.4 percent and health foods for 20.2 percent. Health food sales grew through 2023 and 2024, while the 2022 base needs care because it reflects only part of a year after the acquisition.

Exports center on peeled chestnuts and strawberry syrup. As of March 2026, the company's share of its main export product, peeled chestnuts, was reported at 28 percent, and its strawberry syrup share at 30 percent.

The domestic business is heavily OEM and contract processing, so customer order swings matter, and the export mix links results to the Japanese economy.

Subsidiaries include a frozen dumpling maker, a food additives maker, a health food maker in which Foodwell holds 70 percent, and a China-based processor in which it holds 100 percent. Its annual report also notes competitive factors in some items such as chestnut products and fruit-pulp syrups.

04

Earnings analysis

Annual revenue rose from KRW 183.9 billion in 2022 to KRW 228.5 billion in 2023, KRW 273 billion in 2024, and KRW 320.3 billion in 2025. Revenue grew for three consecutive years from 2023 through 2025, and 2025 was the largest year in the 2022 to 2025 window at 17.3 percent growth.

Operating profit moved from KRW 6.06 billion in 2022 to KRW 18.4 billion in 2025, and the operating margin climbed from 3.3 percent to 5.7 percent. Profitability improved while revenue expanded, so margin gains explain a large part of the profit growth.

Net income attributable to controlling shareholders rose from KRW 1.45 billion in 2022 to KRW 10 billion in 2025. The 337.2 percent jump in 2023 was largely a low-base effect from 2022. Net income including non-controlling interests, which is a different figure, reached KRW 12 billion in 2025.

Reports attribute part of the revenue growth to demand for health functional foods and frozen dumplings, linked to rising single-person households.

Quarterly revenue fell from KRW 81.7 billion in the third quarter of 2025 to KRW 79 billion in the fourth quarter, and operating profit dropped to KRW 1.72 billion, the low point in the period reviewed.

It recovered to KRW 85.1 billion of revenue and KRW 5.85 billion of operating profit in the first quarter of 2026, and to KRW 94.2 billion and KRW 8.88 billion in the second quarter.

Third-quarter 2026 revenue was KRW 93.4 billion, up 14.4 percent year on year, and operating profit was KRW 7.37 billion, up 30.3 percent year on year but down 17.0 percent from the prior quarter. Net income attributable to controlling shareholders was KRW 5.88 billion, up 95.8 percent.

The last four quarters total KRW 16.3 billion, and the figure has risen for three quarters since the fourth quarter of 2025. On the balance sheet, the debt-to-equity ratio rose to 104.9 percent in 2025 from 97.4 percent in 2024, with total liabilities of KRW 121.9 billion against total equity of KRW 116.2 billion.

Operating cash flow fell from KRW 21.7 billion in 2024 to KRW 16.9 billion in 2025, and the cause cannot be pinned down from the disclosed data alone. No one-off items of material size were identified in the sources reviewed.

05

Industry analysis

Reports point to rising demand for health functional foods and frozen foods as single-person households grow.

In the third quarter of 2025, average utilization was reported at 128.4 percent for frozen products at the China unit, 126.75 percent for frozen dumplings at a subsidiary, and 115.55 percent for health functional foods.

In the first half of 2026, average utilization at the health food plant was 177.09 percent and at the China syrup plant 161.28 percent, according to the semiannual report. Utilization above 100 percent signals that volume response is constrained without added capacity.

Reports also said three of five subsidiary plants ran above 100 percent average utilization in the third quarter of 2025. Based on these reports, demand appears to be running ahead of production capacity.

Peers in packaged foods and beverages include Otoki, Samyang Foods, Lotte Wellfood and Pulmuone Corporate, but a like-for-like share comparison by product cannot be built from disclosures alone.

The 28 percent share in peeled chestnut exports and the 30 percent share in strawberry syrup are as of March 2026 and show positions in specific niches. Raw material prices and currency moves are cited as operating variables.

Bull factors

Demand running ahead of capacity

Average utilization was reported at 177.09 percent for the health food plant and 161.28 percent for the China syrup plant in the first half of 2026. Reports frame demand exceeding production capacity as the reason for expanding equipment investment. First-half 2026 consolidated revenue rose 12.3 percent year on year.

Continuity of profit recovery

Third-quarter 2026 operating profit rose 30.3 percent year on year. Net income attributable to controlling shareholders has increased for three quarters running since the fourth quarter of 2025, and the last four quarters total KRW 16.3 billion.

Balance sheet cushion and shareholder returns

Cash and in-kind dividends were decided and disclosed in February 2026. The reserve ratio stood at about 2,082 percent at the end of 2025 according to a financial data provider. Operating cash flow stayed positive at KRW 16.9 billion in 2025.

Bear factors

Limited profitability cushion

The operating margin rose to 5.7 percent in 2025 but remains in single digits. Operating profit fell 69.6 percent from the prior quarter to KRW 1.72 billion in the fourth quarter of 2025, showing large quarterly swings. Whether the recovery lasts needs confirmation from further quarterly results.

Rising financial leverage

The debt-to-equity ratio climbed from 97.4 percent in 2024 to 104.9 percent in 2025. Total liabilities at the end of 2025 exceeded total equity. Variable-rate borrowings are cited as exposure to interest rate changes.

Concentration in exports and customers

Operating cash flow fell from KRW 21.7 billion in 2024 to KRW 16.9 billion in 2025. Exposure to domestic OEM demand and to Japanese exports is also noted. Both channels depend on outside orders and economic conditions, which is the core of the bear case.

10

Risk factors

Raw materials, FX and rates

Raw material prices, currency moves and interest costs on variable-rate borrowings are cited as operating variables. With margins in the mid single digits, cost swings can matter relatively more for profit. Cost and currency sensitivity should be read in the quarterly results.

Customer and Japan export dependence

A large share of domestic business is OEM and contract processing, which makes results sensitive to customer orders. Export exposure to Japan is cited as a factor linked to the Japanese economy. A drop in demand from key customers or export markets would affect utilization and revenue together.

Capex and utilization management

Plants running above full capacity raise the risk of execution strain and capital spending pressure. Reports said three of five subsidiary plants exceeded 100 percent average utilization in the third quarter of 2025. If new capacity comes online late, volume response could lag demand.

Overall view

Foodwell's disclosed results show revenue, operating profit and net income attributable to controlling shareholders each rising for three consecutive years from 2022 through 2025. The operating margin reached 5.7 percent in 2025 but remains in single digits.

In 2026, net income attributable to controlling shareholders has risen for three quarters running, and third-quarter operating profit grew 30.3 percent year on year.
