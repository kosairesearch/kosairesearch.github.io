Revenue Up, Profit Under Pressure at Hansae YES24 Holdings

Hansae YES24 Holdings recovered operating profit in the first half of 2026 but swung to an operating loss in the third quarter, leaving leverage and subsidiary profitability as the key items to track.

02

Business structure

Hansae YES24 Holdings is a holding company whose portfolio spans apparel, culture, education and mobility affiliates. Its largest business is the apparel ODM subsidiary Hansae, whose first-half 2026 revenue reached KRW 1.03 trillion, up 9.2% year on year, with operating profit of KRW 46.5 billion.

Hansae plans to start its Guatemala production project in the fourth quarter of 2026 to build an integrated chain from yarn to sewing. The culture and education segment is run by the online bookstore YES24 and an education publishing subsidiary.

YES24 posted annual revenue of KRW 671.1 billion in 2024 and KRW 626.5 billion in 2025, and projects KRW 620 billion for 2026. Books and music sales account for 93.6% of YES24 revenue, a concentration that press coverage has flagged.

The education publisher's revenue for January to September 2026 reached KRW 76.2 billion, up 14.0%, driven by textbook and reference book sales. The mobility unit posted first-half 2026 revenue of KRW 254.4 billion, up 13.4%, and an operating profit of KRW 10.8 billion, a turn to profit.

YES24 competes with large e-commerce platforms, while Hansae emphasizes delivery speed from its Central American production base. The company said goods made in Central America reach the US East Coast in about 8 to 10 days, compared with 35 to 40 days from Asia. Holding-company standalone earnings depend on dividend income from subsidiaries.

04

Earnings analysis

Annual results show revenue and profit moving in opposite directions.

Revenue fell from KRW 3.32 trillion in 2022 to KRW 2.78 trillion in 2023 (down 16.3%), then rose to KRW 2.83 trillion in 2024 (up 1.8%) and KRW 3.41 trillion in 2025 (up 20.5%), growing for two consecutive years from 2024 through 2025. 2025 was the largest year of the 2022-2025 period.

Operating profit went from KRW 192.9 billion in 2022 to KRW 189.7 billion in 2023 (down 1.6%), KRW 152.3 billion in 2024 (down 19.8%) and KRW 65.5 billion in 2025 (down 57.0%), declining for three consecutive years from 2023 through 2025.

The operating margin rose from 5.8% in 2022 to 6.8% in 2023, then fell to 5.4% in 2024 and 1.9% in 2025, the lowest level of the period in a year when revenue grew.

Net income attributable to controlling shareholders rose to KRW 56.4 billion in 2023 (up 164.8%), dropped to KRW 4.55 billion in 2024 (down 91.9%), and turned into a net loss of KRW 33.1 billion in 2025. Net loss including non-controlling interests was KRW 23.2 billion in 2025.

The debt ratio climbed from 145.0% in 2022 to 222.5% in 2025, and operating cash flow shrank from KRW 221.1 billion to KRW 32.9 billion.

Quarterly, operating profit was KRW 23.9 billion in the third quarter of 2025, KRW 20.3 billion in the fourth quarter, KRW 21.2 billion in the first quarter of 2026 and KRW 40.4 billion in the second quarter, a rebound of 90.7% on the quarter.

In the third quarter of 2026, revenue of KRW 909.3 billion rose 3.4% year on year, yet the company posted an operating loss of KRW 4.11 billion.

Press reports attribute the swing mainly to bad-debt expense of KRW 17.4 billion, up from KRW 0.9 billion a year earlier, while interest expense for January to September totaled KRW 53.4 billion.

Net loss attributable to controlling shareholders was KRW 21.8 billion in the third quarter, and the last four quarters total a net loss of KRW 20.3 billion attributable to controlling shareholders.

05

Industry analysis

Apparel ODM demand moves with trade conditions in major import markets such as the United States and with consumer spending. The company said its 2026 revenue outlook reflects domestic and overseas economic forecasts, client sales conditions and ongoing orders.

Central American production is presented as a strength because of short delivery times to the US East Coast. Hansae said that starting its Guatemala project will complete vertical integration across apparel production and that output will expand in earnest from 2027.

Unlike SK Square, which is largely valued on the value of its stakes, this group's manufacturing, distribution and education subsidiaries feed operating profit directly into holding-company results.

The publishing market faces structural pressure from a domestic market slowdown and competition with large e-commerce platforms. YES24 spent 0.79% of its first-half 2026 revenue on research and development, a level critics say is small for platform technology.

In education publishing, Dong-A Publishing expects its education content revenue to exceed KRW 120 billion on stronger textbook and reference sales.

The mobility unit is expanding overseas plants and localization based on auto parts mass-production capability, and it has said it is also considering entry into physical AI robotics.

Because apparel and publishing demand are moving in different directions, group results are likely to depend on how quickly each business recovers.

06

Outlook

The company's medium-term target is KRW 6 trillion in consolidated revenue by 2032.

Vice Chairman Kim Seok-hwan said the goal will be reached through growth of existing businesses without additional M&A, and projected average annual growth of around 8% from expanded Central American production at Hansae and overseas plant additions at the mobility unit.

However, applying the 4% average annual revenue growth of 2020-2025 through 2032 implies about KRW 4.49 trillion, a gap of roughly KRW 1.51 trillion from the target. The 2026 consolidated revenue outlook of KRW 3.55 trillion represents growth of about 4.1% over 2025 results.

The company did not give operating profit or net income forecasts. YES24 expects 2026 annual revenue of KRW 620 billion and plans to open a smart logistics center in the second half of 2026. Press reports, however, flag possible completion delays.

Once the center is completed, depreciation would be recognized at once, and borrowing costs previously capitalized into construction-in-progress would flow directly into financial expenses. Hansae said it will start the Guatemala project in the fourth quarter of 2026 and expand production in earnest from 2027.

The education publisher plans to launch a digital learning platform with an AI tutor linked to its reference books by year-end. On shareholder returns, the company plans to pay out at least 40% of dividend income from affiliates for 2026 through 2028 and keep a minimum dividend level.

08

Bull factors

Profit rebound in the second quarter

Consolidated operating profit for the first half of 2026 was KRW 61.6 billion, up KRW 40.3 billion year on year. Second-quarter operating profit of KRW 40.4 billion rose 90.7% from the first quarter, the sharpest rebound in the filed quarterly results. First-half revenue of KRW 1.77 trillion grew 6.9%, so sales and profit both improved.

Growth at subsidiaries

Hansae's first-half 2026 revenue rose 9.2% and its operating profit reached KRW 46.5 billion. The Guatemala production base and shorter delivery times from Central America are presented as the foundation for expanding higher-value products.

The mobility unit returned to profit in the first half of 2026, showing that the new business has started to contribute earnings.

Expanded shareholder return policy

The company plans to pay out at least 40% of dividend income from affiliates for 2026 through 2028 and keep a minimum dividend level. It has paid dividends for 17 consecutive years since the holding company was formed, a sign of continuity.

Because the policy is tied to dividends from subsidiaries, a recovery at subsidiaries would flow through to shareholder returns.

09

Bear factors

Third-quarter swing to loss

The third-quarter 2026 operating loss of KRW 4.11 billion compares with operating profit of KRW 23.9 billion a year earlier. Bad-debt expense of KRW 17.4 billion, cited as the main cause, was far above KRW 0.9 billion in the year-earlier quarter.

Net loss attributable to controlling shareholders widened to KRW 21.8 billion, and the last four quarters total KRW 20.3 billion.

Rising financial burden

The consolidated debt ratio rose to 258.8% at end-September 2026 from 222.5% at end-2025. Borrowings due within one year total KRW 1.05 trillion, or 70.3% of total borrowings and more than three times cash and short-term financial assets of KRW 340.2 billion.

Standalone cash at the holding company was only about KRW 64 million, which press reports say requires careful short-term liquidity management.

YES24 shrinkage and subsidiary weakness

YES24 revenue fell from KRW 671.1 billion in 2024 to KRW 626.5 billion in 2025, and press coverage says the 2026 projection of KRW 620 billion extends the decline. YES24 swung to an operating loss of KRW 2.5 billion in the first half of 2026.

Some subsidiaries are reported to be in full capital impairment, and redemption of convertible preferred shares at a related company, Bukpal, has not been carried out.

10

Risk factors

Financial and liquidity

Short-term borrowings exceed KRW 1 trillion while the third quarter posted an operating loss, reducing earnings available to cover interest. Interest expense for January to September 2026 totaled KRW 53.4 billion and weighs on profit.

Once the logistics center opens, depreciation and borrowing costs that were previously capitalized could hit the income statement at once.

Subsidiary earnings and credit losses

Bad-debt expense rose sharply from the prior year, exposing collection risk at clients. YES24 posted an operating loss in the first half, and press reports say loans to related parties of KRW 21.1 billion carry allowances of KRW 23.1 billion.

Because holding-company earnings rely on subsidiary dividends, weakness at subsidiaries feeds directly into lower holding-company income.

External conditions and target delivery

The company cited uncertainty in external conditions and stressed profitability-focused operations. An analysis applying the 4% average growth of 2020-2025 suggests about KRW 4.49 trillion by 2032, well short of the KRW 6 trillion target. That gap is cited as an execution risk.

12

Overall view

Hansae YES24 Holdings' filed results show growing revenue alongside weak profit. Revenue has grown for two consecutive years from 2024 through 2025 to KRW 3.41 trillion, while operating profit declined for three consecutive years from 2023 to KRW 65.5 billion in 2025.

The first half of 2026 showed a recovery to KRW 61.6 billion in operating profit. The third quarter, however, reversed that trend with an operating loss of KRW 4.11 billion as bad-debt expense surged.

On the financial side, the 258.8% debt ratio at end-September 2026 and KRW 1.05 trillion in short-term borrowings remain the largest burdens.

At subsidiaries, growth and a return to profit at Hansae and the mobility unit are positives, while YES24's revenue decline and reports of full capital impairment at subsidiaries are negatives.

The shareholder return policy and the KRW 6 trillion revenue target for 2032 are directions the company has set, and their attainment depends on subsidiary results.

Since positive and negative factors coexist with similar weight, the next checkpoints are the operating margin and debt ratio in the 2026 annual report and the profit trend in the first-quarter 2027 report.
