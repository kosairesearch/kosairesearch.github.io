KEM Tech narrowed its operating loss to KRW 265 million in Q3 2026, but revenue fell to KRW 13.02 million, extending a four-quarter decline, and trading remains suspended after a listing-eligibility review was triggered on November 16, 2026.

02

Business structure

KEM Tech is a KOSDAQ-listed electronic components maker headquartered in Osan, Gyeonggi, organized into electronic components, F&B and secondary battery units.

The electronic components unit produces IR filters, a core part of smartphone camera modules, and relies on an outsourced production setup built on its IR filter technology and manufacturing know-how.

The Valueline company profile says the main customer, Innowave, supplies camera module parts into Samsung Electronics smartphones, and Innowave is disclosed as a de facto controlling-shareholder related party, so electronic components revenue may depend heavily on orders from this related customer.

The F&B unit supplies food, beverages and household goods through the distribution networks of large domestic companies. The battery unit set up a secondary battery parts division in July 2023 and an Osan plant in September 2023, and has since pursued Can and Cap Assembly components.

Wisereport's company overview lists product sales mix as 66.45% camera module parts, secondary battery materials and manufacturing distribution, 30.36% trading of secondary battery materials and MRO-related goods, and 3.19% other, without a stated year.

The same service puts the export share of manufactured products at 99.97% for December 2025. The US subsidiary KEM Tech America, a manufacturing entity established in January 2024, is included in consolidation.

Listed peers in the sector include Samsung Electro-Mechanics and LG Innotek, while KEM Tech's 2025 revenue of KRW 7.57 billion is far smaller in scale.

04

Earnings analysis

Annual results show revenue shrinking while losses widened. Revenue went from KRW 28.9 billion in 2022 to KRW 4.99 billion in 2023, down 82.7% year on year, rebounded to KRW 17.6 billion in 2024, up 252.7%, and then fell to KRW 7.57 billion in 2025, down 57.0%.

In its 2025 filing, the company attributed the sales decline to lower IR filter business revenue and the loss deterioration to higher new business development and investment costs.

Operating income went from KRW 688 million in 2022 to an operating loss of KRW 3.68 billion in 2023, then KRW 4.35 billion in 2024 and KRW 9.23 billion in 2025. The operating margin moved from 2.4% in 2022 to -73.7% in 2023, -24.7% in 2024 and -121.8% in 2025.

The margin improved in 2024 relative to 2023, but in 2025 the loss grew even as revenue shrank, pushing the margin to -121.8%.

Net loss attributable to controlling shareholders was KRW 11.3 billion in 2023, KRW 4.14 billion in 2024 and KRW 12.7 billion in 2025, while net loss including non-controlling interests was KRW 12.6 billion in 2025, so the two figures differ.

The debt ratio rose from 19.3% in 2022 to 89.3% in 2023, then 61.6% in 2024 and 74.2% in 2025. Operating cash flow was negative in every year, at KRW -3.53 billion in 2022, KRW -6.29 billion in 2023, KRW -5.09 billion in 2024 and KRW -2.7 billion in 2025.

Quarterly, the operating loss was KRW 2.12 billion in Q3 2025 and KRW 3.7 billion in Q4 2025, then KRW 3.16 billion, KRW 2.62 billion and KRW 265 million in Q1 to Q3 2026.

Over the same stretch, the net loss attributable to controlling shareholders widened from KRW 5.61 billion in Q4 2025 to KRW 3.52 billion in Q3 2026 after narrowing in between, and the last four quarters total KRW 15.6 billion.

In Q3 2026 the operating loss was KRW 265 million while the net loss attributable to controlling shareholders was KRW 3.52 billion, and the gap between the two should be checked against the non-operating items in the filing. Wisereport's R&D tally rose from KRW 326 million in 2024 to KRW 844 million in 2025.

05

Industry analysis

KEM Tech's electronic components business centers on IR filters used in smartphone camera modules, so demand tracks smartphone shipments and changes in camera specifications.

The company attributed its 2025 sales decline to lower IR filter business revenue, and its electronic components sales appear to depend heavily on orders from a related customer.

Listed peers in the same sector include Samsung Electro-Mechanics, LG Innotek, Isupetasys and Korea Circuit, but KEM Tech's KRW 7.57 billion revenue in 2025 makes direct comparison difficult.

On the battery side, a company profile from Komachine says automakers are increasingly favoring prismatic cells, which is presented as a demand driver. That profile is a company self-description rather than independent market data, so it is weak evidence for market size.

The same profile points to a supply shortage of prismatic-cell-specific parts as US-China restrictions tighten, which it frames as an opportunity. Exchange listing rules set a quarterly revenue benchmark of KRW 300 million, and the Q3 2026 revenue of KRW 13.02 million falls far below it, according to a Bloter report.

Competitively, KEM Tech is a small components maker combining outsourced production with dependence on a related-party customer, and the filings do not show the diversified customer base of larger component makers. Valuation comparisons with listed sector peers are limited given the scale gap.

06

Outlook

The company's stated direction centers on expanding its secondary battery parts business. According to the Valueline company profile, KEM Tech signed an MOU with a global battery maker in 2024 and won an ESS project in June 2025, with full-scale mass production planned from the fourth quarter.

A company profile updated on August 31, 2026 says mass-production supply from the US subsidiary's production line is expected to start ahead of a main ESS project contract with a global battery maker.

The same profile says a strategic investment from Daejin Advanced Materials is supporting expanded capacity for prismatic EV battery parts and supply chain stability.

A rights offering disclosed on January 19, 2026 covers 8.64 million common shares, with proceeds targeted at KRW 17.84 billion in operating funds and KRW 7 billion in debt repayment, and payment was scheduled for May 15, 2026.

In April 2026 the company acquired KRW 4.5 billion of its 21st convertible bond tranche in an over-the-counter purchase, citing early debt settlement and risk management after an event of default. The purchase was funded from own cash, and the company said the treatment of the bonds will be decided later by the board.

In April 2026 the CEO structure changed to a single representative director, Yoo Seong-jun. Whether the battery expansion reaches revenue can be checked in the segment sales disclosures of the 2026 annual report.

The resolution of the audit opinion disclaimer and the going-concern uncertainty are the first items to check in that report.

Bull factors

Operating losses narrowing

The operating loss fell from KRW 3.7 billion in Q4 2025 to KRW 3.16 billion in Q1 2026, KRW 2.62 billion in Q2 and KRW 265 million in Q3. The revenue base has weakened, so the drivers of the narrowing need to be checked in the detailed line items. Any margin recovery depends on whether this trend holds.

Battery component mass-production push

Valueline's company overview notes an ESS project win in June 2025 with mass production planned from Q4. A company profile updated August 31, 2026 says supply from the US production line for an ESS project is expected to start.

The annual report describes the battery parts business as being in mass-production readiness, which could become a second revenue pillar.

Capital-raising steps underway

A rights offering disclosed in January 2026 covers 8.64 million common shares, targeting KRW 17.84 billion in operating funds and KRW 7 billion for debt repayment. A strategic investment from Daejin Advanced Materials is described as being pursued.

A stronger capital base could widen room for responding to the listing review and for production capacity investment.

Bear factors

Weakening revenue base

Revenue was KRW 7.57 billion in 2025, down 57.0%, and has fallen for four consecutive quarters from Q4 2025 through Q3 2026. Q3 2026 revenue of KRW 13.02 million was down 99.1% from a year earlier. That is far below the KRW 300 million quarterly revenue benchmark in exchange listing rules.

Losses and financial uncertainty

The 2025 operating loss was KRW 9.23 billion, with an operating margin of -121.8%. Net loss attributable to controlling shareholders over the last four quarters totals KRW 15.6 billion.

The auditor issued a disclaimer of opinion citing uncertainty over going-concern viability, and current liabilities exceeded current assets by KRW 14.95 billion at the end of 2025 according to the annual report.

Governance and disclosure credibility

A major shareholder's share pledge arose on April 25, 2025 but was disclosed only on February 5, 2026, leading to a designation as an unfaithful disclosure company. The de facto controlling shareholder Innowave disclosed on March 9, 2026 that it sold a total of 234,033 shares on the market on February 27 and March 9. Disclosure credibility is tied to how the exchange weighs business continuity in the listing review.

Risk factors

Listing status

The exchange disclosed on November 16, 2026 that a listing-eligibility review trigger had arisen and plans to decide whether to review by December 7, 2026. The delisting ground tied to the audit opinion has a cure period through April 10, 2027, and the stock was designated as an administrative issue on April 20, 2026. Trading stays suspended until both grounds are resolved.

Financial and liquidity

According to a Bloter report, cash and cash equivalents on a consolidated basis were about KRW 29.91 million at the end of Q3 2026, down 88.1% from a year earlier. The January 2026 rights offering plan and the KRW 4.5 billion of own cash used for the April 2026 bond purchase point to heavy funding needs. The annual report shows current liabilities exceeded current assets by KRW 14.95 billion at the end of 2025.

Business and customer concentration

The main customer is described as a de facto controlling-shareholder related company, so sales flow may depend heavily on one customer's orders. Unless battery parts revenue scales in mass production, it will be hard to offset the decline in electronic components sales. The move to a single CEO in April 2026 also makes management continuity a variable to watch.

Overall view

KEM Tech's revenue fell from KRW 28.9 billion in 2022 to KRW 7.57 billion in 2025, and Q3 2026 revenue dropped to KRW 13.02 million. The operating loss narrowed to KRW 265 million in Q3 2026, yet the 2025 full-year operating loss of KRW 9.23 billion was larger than the KRW 4.35 billion recorded in 2024.

On the financial side, total equity of KRW 30 billion and a 74.2% debt ratio at the end of 2025 remain, but operating cash flow was negative in every year from 2022 through 2025.

On the listing side, trading has been suspended since March 12, 2026, and a further listing-eligibility review was triggered on November 16, 2026, with a decision on whether it applies expected by December 7, 2026.

On the business side, the ESS project and the US production plan are presented as positives, but battery parts revenue is not separately disclosed in the reported results, which limits its use as evidence of a broad revenue recovery.

Bullish factors include the narrowing operating loss, the battery mass-production plan and capital-raising steps, while bearish factors include falling sales, the audit disclaimer and going-concern uncertainty, the listing review and governance disclosure problems.

The next checkpoints are the December 7, 2026 review decision, the 2026 annual report due by March 31, 2027, and the end of the cure period on April 10, 2027. This report is for information only and does not include a buy or sell opinion or a target price.
