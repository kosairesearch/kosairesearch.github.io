Capital raised amid losses; core profitability recovery still to be confirmed

02

Business structure

Edge Foundry built its business on automotive sensors and is expanding its infrared thermal imaging sensor and camera business to diversify. Following its March 2025 merger with Hanwha Intelligence, the company positioned uncooled infrared (IR) thermal sensor development and mass production as its core business.

Its flagship Thermo K is a QVGA-class uncooled thermal camera module, and the company emphasizes cost advantages from CMOS process compatibility and wafer-level packaging. Target applications include automotive ADAS, military, security, industrial safety and medical uses.

In automotive, SLS accounted for 25% of revenue in the second quarter of 2026, followed by NAPS at 21%, IAPS at 13%, Controller (PWM/HI Console) at 11% and Solenoid at 11%; these shares are based on a third-party aggregator (Butler), not on company filings.

In March 2022 the company signed a KRW 13.5 billion thermal camera supply contract (Quantum Redmini) with Hanwha Systems, but it terminated the remaining contract at the end of April 2026 after pandemic-era demand disappeared.

Cumulative deliveries were KRW 2.7 billion, the execution rate was 20%, and supply against the unfulfilled order balance of KRW 10.8 billion was halted.

In drones and defense, the company showed a Thermo K-equipped observation drone at DSK 2026 in February 2026 and has partnerships with a Korean AI chip fabless company, DeepX, and an industrial drone platform company, AMP.

As of September 9, 2026, shareholders were Aicore Biz with 16.68%, Hanwha Systems with 8.76%, and treasury shares with 8.48%.

04

Earnings analysis

Annual revenue rose from KRW 36 billion in 2022 to KRW 37 billion in 2023 (up 2.9% year on year) and KRW 37.3 billion in 2024 (up 0.7% year on year), then fell to KRW 36.4 billion in 2025 (down 2.4% year on year). The largest year in 2022-2025 was 2024, and the smallest was 2022.

The operating loss went from KRW 2.05 billion in 2022 to KRW 4.84 billion in 2023, KRW 5.07 billion in 2024 and KRW 15.5 billion in 2025. The operating margin moved from -5.7% in 2022 to -13.1% in 2023, -13.6% in 2024 and -42.7% in 2025.

The company attributed the wider 2025 operating loss to higher depreciation on assets taken over in the March 2025 merger and to higher personnel costs.

Net loss attributable to controlling shareholders was KRW 5.42 billion in 2022, KRW 21.3 billion in 2023 and KRW 18 billion in 2024, before turning to a net profit of KRW 15.7 billion in 2025. Net profit including non-controlling interests was also KRW 15.7 billion in 2025.

Press reports, however, attribute much of the 2025 turnaround to non-operating factors such as disposal gains on equity stakes in other companies rather than to operating activity.

Operating cash flow was KRW 3.64 billion in 2022, then negative at KRW -3.26 billion in 2023, KRW -3.11 billion in 2024 and KRW -12.5 billion in 2025. The debt-to-equity ratio fell from 67.7% in 2022 and 85.8% in 2023 to 71.2% in 2024 and 37.9% in 2025, and total equity at the end of 2025 was KRW 128.4 billion.

Quarterly revenue went from KRW 8.95 billion in the third quarter of 2025 to KRW 8.61 billion in the fourth quarter, peaked at KRW 8.88 billion in the first quarter of 2026, then declined for two consecutive quarters to KRW 8.83 billion in the second quarter and KRW 8.08 billion in the third quarter of 2026; third-quarter revenue was down 9.7% year on year.

Quarterly operating losses widened from KRW 4.56 billion in the third quarter of 2025 to KRW 6.06 billion in the second quarter of 2026 before narrowing to KRW 2.95 billion in the third quarter of 2026.

Quarterly net results were volatile: a net profit attributable to controlling shareholders of KRW 2.69 billion in the fourth quarter of 2025 gave way to KRW 918 million in the first quarter of 2026 and then a net loss of KRW 17.2 billion in the second quarter, followed by a KRW 3.98 billion net loss in the third quarter of 2026.

Net losses attributable to controlling shareholders over the latest four quarters total KRW 17.6 billion. The second-quarter net loss was far larger than the operating loss, which suggests non-operating items weighed heavily in that quarter.

05

Industry analysis

WISEreport classifies Edge Foundry under Electrical & Electronics on KOSDAQ, and the company also carries automotive exposure.

On the automotive sensor side, slower growth in electric vehicles reduced supply of non-contact automotive sensors, while an increase in Hall type APS orders was expected to raise total supply volume, according to WISEreport.

On the thermal side, component localization and AI integration emerged as key themes in the drone and defense market at DSK 2026. Whether demand for domestically made thermal modules will rise in drone surveillance and public missions has not yet been confirmed by disclosed order announcements.

The company's competitive position in thermal sensors depends on how uncooled, lower-cost products substitute for cooled high-performance products.

The company says Thermo K has a cost advantage over cooled products, but how far uncooled products can displace cooled ones in high-performance applications has not been proven in reported results.

The termination of the Quantum Redmini contract reflects a sharp drop in pandemic-era demand for thermal cameras, and the company is shifting its focus to industrial and safety applications. Fire monitoring for switchgear, cited with the partner Mtake, is presented as a new value-added application.

Automotive volumes depend on the pace of electric vehicle adoption and vehicle production plans. Drone and defense components require qualification and mass-production systems, so there may be a lag between orders and revenue.

06

Outlook

The company has not published numerical guidance that can be verified, so progress against its plans will be judged through quarterly figures in periodic filings.

Of the KRW 50.76 billion rights offering, KRW 20 billion is earmarked for facilities, KRW 15.765 billion for operations and KRW 15 billion for debt repayment, and subsequent disclosures will need to show what capacity the facility funds add.

Trading in the shares resumed on August 12, 2026, when the par value consolidation and the listing change were completed. After the rights offering decision, the company also decided to issue an 18th series convertible bond of KRW 10 billion, with the full proceeds to be used to repay existing bonds and borrowings.

In March 2026 the company resold KRW 7.5 billion of its own 16th series convertible bonds to Hyulim Robot, and those bonds mature on December 17, 2027.

In defense and drones, the company attended CES 2026 and ISC WEST 2026 to showcase its thermal sensor technology and stated its aim to enter the market for drone components used in public missions such as military reconnaissance, disaster monitoring and environmental observation.

It also said it had completed a composite drone demonstration test with AMP. Whether that aim has produced revenue has not yet appeared in reported results.

In the next periodic report, the 2026 annual report, the full-year 2026 operating margin and operating cash flow will be the main indicators of whether the financial structure has changed after the capital raise.

08

Bull factors

Return to profit in 2025 with revenue held near KRW 36 billion

Net profit attributable to controlling shareholders turned to KRW 15.7 billion in 2025 from losses in prior years. Revenue of KRW 36.4 billion in 2025 stayed close to the KRW 36 billion recorded in 2022. Automotive sensor revenue is spread across several product lines, led by SLS at 25% in the second quarter of 2026.

The quality of the swing to profit needs separate review, but the direction of earnings recovery is confirmed in reported results.

Product diversification into thermal, drone and defense applications

Thermo K is a QVGA-class uncooled thermal module, and the company highlights cost competitiveness through CMOS process compatibility and wafer-level packaging. It unveiled a Thermo K-equipped observation drone at DSK 2026 in February 2026 and also attended CES 2026 and ISC WEST 2026.

It has partnerships with an AI chip fabless company and an industrial drone platform company. Whether the shift from automotive sensors toward thermal applications leads to actual orders remains the key point to verify.

Capital raise as an attempt to improve the balance sheet

The debt-to-equity ratio fell from 85.8% in 2023 to 37.9% in 2025, with total equity of KRW 128.4 billion at the end of 2025. Of the KRW 50.76 billion rights offering, KRW 15 billion is allocated to debt repayment and KRW 20 billion to facilities.

If the fundraising reduces financial burden and supports capital investment, it could provide a base for a change in the business structure.

Bear factors

Widening operating losses and continued negative cash flow

The operating loss widened from KRW 2.05 billion in 2022 to KRW 15.5 billion in 2025, with an operating margin of -42.7%. Operating cash flow deteriorated to KRW -12.5 billion in 2025. The third-quarter 2026 operating loss narrowed to KRW 2.95 billion, but losses have persisted for five consecutive quarters.

Quality of profit and volatility in quarterly results

Reports indicate the 2025 turnaround owed much to non-operating gains such as disposal gains on equity stakes in other companies. In the second quarter of 2026, the net loss attributable to controlling shareholders was KRW 17.2 billion, well above the KRW 6.06 billion operating loss. Quarterly earnings have swung in direction, so the durability of any recovery has not yet been established.

Funding burden, dilution and a terminated supply contract

The KRW 50.76 billion rights offering issues 11 million new shares at a 30% discount to the reference price. An 18th series convertible bond of KRW 10 billion was also decided.

The Quantum Redmini supply contract was terminated effective April 30, 2026, halting supply against an unfulfilled order balance of KRW 10.8 billion. The termination also raises the possibility of an unfaithful-disclosure designation tied to a reversal of earlier disclosure.

10

Risk factors

Drone and defense commercialization risk

The company unveiled a Thermo K-equipped observation drone in February 2026 and completed a composite drone demonstration test with AMP. Its goal of entering the market for military reconnaissance and disaster monitoring components has not yet appeared in revenue.

Drone and defense components require certification and mass-production systems first, so a lag may arise between orders and revenue. If this process is delayed, the effect of product diversification on earnings may also be reflected late.

Capacity expansion execution risk

Of the rights offering proceeds, KRW 20 billion is allocated to facilities for expanding production capacity. However, what capacity increase this investment will produce has not yet been confirmed in disclosures. If equipment utilization fails to keep pace with demand, the investment burden may remain in earnings. If the investment effect appears late, the timing of any reduction in the operating loss may also be delayed.

Automotive demand risk

Automotive sensor revenue is affected by slower growth in electric vehicles. WISEreport noted that supply of non-contact automotive sensors declined and losses widened.

Higher Hall type APS orders were expected to lift total supply volume, but whether this leads to better profitability must be confirmed in quarterly results. Given the weight of automotive revenue, changes in vehicle production plans can feed directly into earnings.

12

Overall view

Edge Foundry is widening its business from automotive sensors into thermal sensors, drones and defense components. Net profit attributable to controlling shareholders turned positive at KRW 15.7 billion in 2025, but the operating loss widened to KRW 15.5 billion, with an operating margin of -42.7%.

The third-quarter 2026 operating loss narrowed to KRW 2.95 billion, yet losses have persisted for five consecutive quarters.

The rights offering and convertible bond issuance have lowered the debt-to-equity ratio, but negative operating cash flow and the termination of the Quantum Redmini contract remain factors that hold back a change in core profitability.

Reports also indicate that much of the 2025 turnaround came from non-operating items, so the quality of earnings must be assessed separately. Strengths include balance sheet improvement and product diversification, while weaknesses include persistent operating losses and funding burden. The next checkpoint is the operating margin and operating cash flow in the 2026 annual report.
