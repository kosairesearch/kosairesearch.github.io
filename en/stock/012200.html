Report overview

Losses Persist as Auto-Motor Dependence Meets Robotics Pivot

Keyang Electric Machinery recorded an operating loss of KRW 27.1 billion in 2025, extending its losses to four consecutive years, and has posted operating losses for five straight quarters through Q3 2026. Dependence on the Hyundai Motor Group and the planned shift toward robotics are the key variables.

02

Business structure

Keyang Electric Machinery operates in two segments, electrical components (Jeonjang) and industrial products. According to the Q3 2025 quarterly report, electrical components accounted for 73.7% of sales and industrial products for 26.3%.

Other data aggregators show a different split, with electrical components at 63.4% and industrial products at 36.6%. Electrical components centers on DC motors for automobiles and seat motors supplied to automakers including Hyundai Motor and Kia.

In Q1 2026, sales ultimately supplied to Hyundai Motor and Kia accounted for 86.1% of electrical component sales and 63% of total revenue. Industrial products cover power tools such as grinders, drills and cutters, along with small engines and industrial hand tools.

The company holds high shares in domestic grinder, drill and cutter categories and is expanding its cordless tool lineup. In power tools, competition from low-priced Chinese products has weighed on profitability.

In 2023, the industrial products segment posted an operating loss of KRW 3.4 billion and electrical components posted an operating loss of KRW 0.3 billion. A subsidiary in the Suzhou area of China handles power tool manufacturing and parts sales.

Since 2024 the company has pursued robotics by applying its motor design and precision machining capabilities, and plans to raise robotics' share of sales from 1.5% in 2026 to 15.4% in 2030.

04

Earnings analysis

Annual revenue rose from KRW 365.1 billion in 2022 to KRW 381.6 billion in 2023, a 4.5% increase, then fell to KRW 369.1 billion in 2024, a 3.3% decline, before reaching KRW 389.8 billion in 2025, a 5.6% increase and the largest year in the 2022 to 2025 period.

Operating losses over the same period were KRW 10.6 billion in 2022, KRW 3.89 billion in 2023, KRW 15.3 billion in 2024 and KRW 27.1 billion in 2025. Operating margins were -2.9%, -1.0%, -4.1% and -7.0%, with 2025 the weakest year.

Net loss attributable to controlling shareholders was KRW 24.5 billion in 2022, KRW 1.26 billion in 2023, KRW 61.2 billion in 2024 and KRW 36.9 billion in 2025, marking four consecutive years of losses from 2022 to 2025. The 2025 net loss including non-controlling interests was KRW 36.9 billion.

Operating cash flow was -KRW 6.29 billion in 2022, KRW 10.2 billion in 2023, -KRW 26.5 billion in 2024 and -KRW 20.6 billion in 2025. The debt ratio climbed from 132.6% in 2022 and 247.2% in 2024 to 959.5% in 2025, when total equity was KRW 20.8 billion and total liabilities KRW 199.6 billion.

The company attributed the 2025 change in profitability mainly to higher operating costs from transferring power tool operations overseas and reallocating production across plants.

Quarterly revenue went from KRW 102.3 billion in Q3 2025 to KRW 91.4 billion in Q4, a 10.6% quarter-on-quarter decline, then peaked at KRW 98.9 billion in Q1 2026 before falling to KRW 97.4 billion in Q2 and KRW 90.9 billion in Q3, which was down 11.2% from a year earlier.

Operating losses moved from KRW 1.02 billion in Q3 2025 to KRW 14.8 billion in Q4, narrowed to KRW 1.94 billion in Q1 2026, then widened to KRW 5.11 billion in Q2 and KRW 7.51 billion in Q3. The Q4 2025 figure was calculated from the annual disclosure in one press analysis.

Net income attributable to controlling shareholders swung from a KRW 2.58 billion profit in Q3 2025 to a KRW 31.1 billion loss in Q4, a KRW 826 million profit in Q1 2026, then losses of KRW 6.11 billion in Q2 and KRW 2.64 billion in Q3. The four quarters from Q4 2025 through Q3 2026 total a controlling-shareholder net loss of KRW 39 billion.

05

Industry analysis

Demand for automotive motors is rising with the shift to electric vehicles and the spread of comfort features in cars. A March 2024 report described growing supply of power seat motors, electronic parking brake motors and electronic steering wheel motors.

The automotive industry is an oligopoly dominated by a few makers, so competition is intense among a limited pool of automakers. The inability to pass cost increases on to supply prices has also weighed on the profitability of the automotive motor business.

Power tools are shifting from corded to cordless models, and demand for lithium-ion battery cordless tools with lighter weight, higher voltage and greener features is growing quickly. Keyang Electric Machinery is launching new products with its own battery technology and electronic drive functions to meet that demand.

Motor technology at the core of robot actuation is drawing attention, but robotics is planned to account for about 1.5% of total sales in 2026. Growth in automotive motor demand and price competition in power tools exist side by side, and the key question is how much each weighs on profitability.

By revenue, the company ranked 65th of 137 companies in the auto parts sector in a mid-2026 tally, which places it in the middle tier.

06

Outlook

The company's robotics revenue target rises in steps from an expected KRW 6.2 billion in 2026 to KRW 6.5 billion in 2027, KRW 37 billion in 2028, KRW 66 billion in 2029 and KRW 99 billion in 2030.

If the plan holds, robotics would account for 7.2% of sales in 2028 and 15.4% in 2030, while electrical components would fall from 75.8% in 2026 to 61.2% in 2030. The relationship with the Hyundai Motor Group, where the robotics business began, is described as a challenge still to be addressed.

A seat motor supply contract with Hyundai Motor and Kia, disclosed in 2024, totaled KRW 288.4 billion, equal to about 75% of the prior year's consolidated revenue. In September 2026 a rights offering issued 8.2 million common shares and completed payment of KRW 24.35 billion.

How this capital is used for operations and balance sheet repair is the next point to watch. The company expects demand for electrical components to rise with electric vehicles and autonomous driving, and aims to raise profitability by diversifying its customers.

The Q4 2026 results have not yet been filed in a periodic report, and whether the full-year 2026 operating loss narrows relative to the first three quarters is the key checkpoint. Whether robotics sales track toward the KRW 6.5 billion planned for 2027 is also worth watching.

08

Bull factors

Temporary narrowing of quarterly losses

The operating loss narrowed from KRW 14.8 billion in Q4 2025 to KRW 1.94 billion in Q1 2026, and net income attributable to controlling shareholders turned to a KRW 826 million profit in the same quarter. Q1 2026 sales of KRW 98.9 billion were the peak of the last five quarters.

Losses widened again to KRW 5.11 billion in Q2 and KRW 7.51 billion in Q3, so whether the improvement persists is the key question.

Robotics expansion plan

The company has set a robotics revenue target of KRW 99 billion for 2030, a large increase from an expected KRW 6.2 billion in 2026. The strategy applies motor design and precision machining capabilities built in existing businesses to robots.

If robotics reaches 15.4% of sales by 2030, the company says its dependence on automotive motor sales would ease.

Automaker supply base and new capital

The seat motor supply contract with Hyundai Motor and Kia, worth KRW 288.4 billion, shows the scale of the electrical components business. The September 2026 rights offering brought in KRW 24.35 billion and may ease some of the financial burden. Rising motor demand from electric vehicles and comfort features is also cited as a positive factor.

09

Bear factors

Operating losses widening again

Operating losses widened in Q2 2026 to KRW 5.11 billion and in Q3 to KRW 7.51 billion. Q3 2026 sales of KRW 90.9 billion fell 11.2% from a year earlier. Operating losses have now run for five straight quarters since Q3 2025.

Dependence on the Hyundai Motor Group and cost pressure

Sales ultimately supplied to Hyundai Motor and Kia were 63% of total revenue and 86.1% of electrical component sales in Q1 2026. Difficulty passing cost increases on to supply prices has been cited as a factor behind past profitability declines. Competition is also intense in an oligopolistic market served by a few automakers.

Balance sheet and cash flow strain

Total liabilities were KRW 199.6 billion against total equity of KRW 20.8 billion at year-end 2025, a debt ratio of 959.5%. Operating cash flow was negative in both 2024, at -KRW 26.5 billion, and 2025, at -KRW 20.6 billion.

10

Risk factors

Pricing and cost competition

The power tool business faces ongoing price competition from low-cost Chinese products. The automotive motor business has also been described as having difficulty passing on cost increases. If both pressures persist together, a recovery in operating margins could be delayed.

Customer concentration

Sales ultimately supplied to Hyundai Motor and Kia account for 63% of total revenue. Any change in that relationship would affect both sales volume and the broader business plan. The robotics business also starts from this relationship, so customer concentration extends into the new business.

Financial burden and execution of the pivot

A debt ratio of 959.5% leaves limited room for additional borrowing. The September 2026 rights offering increased the share count, which complicates per-share comparisons. The robotics plan to reach KRW 99 billion by 2030 starts from an expected KRW 6.2 billion in 2026, so execution speed is a central variable.

12

Overall view

Keyang Electric Machinery has posted operating and controlling-shareholder net losses continuously from 2022 through 2025, even as 2025 revenue of KRW 389.8 billion was the largest in the period.

The operating loss narrowed to KRW 1.94 billion in Q1 2026 and net income turned positive, but losses widened again in Q2 and Q3 2026. Concentration on Hyundai Motor and Kia, which accounted for 63% of revenue in Q1 2026, adds to earnings volatility.

The robotics plan targets KRW 99 billion by 2030 against an expected KRW 6.2 billion in 2026, so execution is central. A debt ratio of 959.5% and equity of KRW 20.8 billion remain a burden, which the KRW 24.35 billion rights offering in September 2026 may partly ease.

Positive factors include rising electric-vehicle motor demand, the shift toward cordless tools and the robotics plan, while negative factors include continuing operating losses, price competition, customer concentration and financing strain.

The next checkpoints are the preliminary full-year 2026 results and the operating margin and cash flow in the 2026 annual report.
