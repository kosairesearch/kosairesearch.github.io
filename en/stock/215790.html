02

Business structure

Inno Instrument, founded in 2007, makes optical communication equipment. Its main product is the optical fusion splicer, and it also produces and exports cleavers and optical fiber measuring instruments.

By segment, based on second-quarter 2026 revenue, optical fusion splicers account for 82%, other products 12%, cleavers 4%, and optical fiber measuring instruments 2%. With fusion splicers above 80% of the mix, demand and pricing for this product drive most of the results.

The company says it is widening its optical communication lineup with products such as OTDR and PA equipment. Production runs at two sites, Weihai in China and Malaysia, and supplies customers across China and overseas.

One report states that 98% of revenue comes from overseas, and the company has local subsidiaries in China, Germany, the United States and India. Products are supplied to more than 90 countries, and the FnGuide company summary states that it has secured the number one share in the Chinese market.

The company is ranked among the top global suppliers of fusion splicers, but it is a B2B company that is little known to general investors. Demand moves with the scale of optical cable infrastructure investment.

04

Earnings analysis

Annual revenue fell from KRW 49.9 billion in 2022 to KRW 43.9 billion in 2023, down 12.0% year on year, and to KRW 41.2 billion in 2024, down 6.1%. It then recovered to KRW 43.7 billion in 2025, up 6.1% year on year. The largest year in the 2022 to 2025 range was 2022, and the smallest was 2024.

Profitability shifted much more sharply. Operating profit of KRW 2.92 billion in 2022 turned into an operating loss of KRW 18.9 billion in 2023, and the loss widened to KRW 27.5 billion in 2024 before narrowing to KRW 9.19 billion in 2025.

The operating margin was 5.9% in 2022, -43.0% in 2023, -66.7% in 2024 and -21.0% in 2025. The company has posted losses for three consecutive years from 2023 through 2025.

Net income attributable to controlling shareholders moved from KRW 5.62 billion in 2022 to a net loss of KRW 18.1 billion in 2023 and KRW 39.4 billion in 2024, and was a net loss of KRW 11.1 billion in 2025.

Net income including non-controlling interests followed the same path, with a net loss of KRW 11.1 billion in 2025. Operating cash flow was -KRW 7.44 billion in 2023 and -KRW 6.96 billion in 2024, and turned positive at KRW 200 million in 2025.

The debt-to-equity ratio rose from 31.4% in 2022 to 90.6% in 2025, with total equity of KRW 34.2 billion and total liabilities of KRW 31 billion at the end of 2025.

Quarterly, revenue was KRW 10.1 billion with an operating loss of KRW 411 million in the third quarter of 2025, and the fourth quarter of 2025 showed revenue of KRW 9.46 billion with an operating loss of KRW 2.34 billion.

The first quarter of 2026 recorded revenue of KRW 7.93 billion and an operating loss of KRW 312 million. The second quarter of 2026 recorded revenue of KRW 9.88 billion and an operating profit of KRW 3.28 billion, turning to profit from the prior quarter.

Third-quarter 2026 revenue was KRW 5.96 billion, down 39.7% from the prior quarter and down 41.1% year on year, with an operating loss of KRW 472 million that widened year on year. The sum of net losses attributable to controlling shareholders over the last four quarters was KRW 1.02 billion.

The company cited cost cuts and structural improvements from its first-half 2026 preliminary accounts and said improvements would take hold from the third quarter, but the third-quarter 2026 filing showed a wider operating loss.

Whether the second-quarter 2026 profit is sustained will be confirmed through the quarterly profit structure in the next regular report.

Industry analysis

Demand for optical communication equipment is being linked by reports to 5G investment and the expansion of optical cable networks as a global trend.

At GTC 2026 in the United States in March 2026, Jensen Huang, CEO of Nvidia, named optical communication technology as a core part of next-generation AI infrastructure, and that remark spread the view that AI data center expansion would lift optical demand.

The FnGuide company summary notes that expanding optical subscriber networks in the United States and Europe is raising drop cable demand, and that a FTTH project in Indonesia has started. The company's optical fusion splicers are regarded as ranking among the top global suppliers by market share.

Peers in optical communications often cited within the sector include TAIHAN Fiberoptics and Seojin System. The company says its production in China and Malaysia gives it price competitiveness.

Because production and sales are concentrated around the Chinese market, Chinese equipment investment and the trade environment have a direct effect.

Share prices in the first half of 2026 moved sharply on optical-theme trading flows, which is why whether orders and revenue actually expand is seen as the key variable apart from the share price.

After theme trading faded, the stock was designated as an administrative issue in August 2026, showing a gap between sector expectations and the financial results.

Narrowing losses and a quarterly profit turn

The operating loss narrowed from KRW 27.5 billion in 2024 to KRW 9.19 billion in 2025, and operating cash flow turned positive at KRW 200 million in 2025. The second quarter of 2026 recorded an operating profit of KRW 3.28 billion, turning to profit from the prior quarter.

Overseas production bases and a large supply contract

The possibility of using the Malaysian site to bypass tariffs is an interpretation at the level of press reports, so its actual effect needs confirmation in regular filings.

The company has posted losses for three consecutive years from 2023 through 2025, and it was designated an administrative issue in August 2026. A correction filing on March 31, 2026 changed the auditor's opinion on the 2025 accounts from adverse to unqualified, which is also an item to confirm.
