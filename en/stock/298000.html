Losses Narrowed, but PP Spreads Remain the Key Variable

02

Business structure

Hyosung Chemical is a chemical affiliate of Hyosung Group with a vertically integrated structure that links PDH units, which make propylene from propane, to polypropylene (PP) units that use that propylene as feedstock.

PP capacity is 620 thousand tons in Korea (Ulsan) and 600 thousand tons in Vietnam, or 1.22 million tons in total, and the Vietnam subsidiary runs PDH and PP together at the Cai Mep industrial zone in Ho Chi Minh City.

Its product lineup centers on PP and also includes PET and nylon film, TAC film and polyketone, an eco-friendly engineering plastic. In 2025 the company sold its specialty gas business and its Onsan tank terminal, halted its TPA business and began selling its TAC film business, which increased its dependence on PP.

In 3Q 2026 the PP/DH segment's profitability weakened as higher logistics costs raised production costs and product selling prices fell. In the same quarter polyketone widened its profit on higher sales for automotive and service robot parts, while the optical segment stayed profitable on lower manufacturing costs.

The high-value product share of the Vietnam subsidiary's sales rose to 49% in 1H 2026 from 43% in 2025. Key customers are downstream PP users in automotive interiors, high-clarity packaging and pipes, and competition comes from domestic and overseas petrochemical companies that produce PP from PDH.

An August 2025 report said Taekwang Industrial had fully halted its PDH operations, and commentary has pointed out that PDH, which produces only propylene, is vulnerable to market swings.

04

Earnings analysis

Based on disclosed figures, annual sales fell from KRW 2.88 trillion in 2022 to KRW 2.79 trillion in 2023 (down 3.0% year on year), rose to KRW 2.84 trillion in 2024 (up 1.7%), and then dropped to KRW 2.34 trillion in 2025 (down 17.5%). 2022 was the largest year in the 2022 to 2025 period and 2025 the smallest.

The operating loss was KRW 336.7 billion in 2022, KRW 188.8 billion in 2023, KRW 170.5 billion in 2024 and KRW 160.5 billion in 2025, which means four consecutive years of operating losses with the loss shrinking each year.

The operating margin, however, improved from -11.7% in 2022 to -6.8% in 2023 and -6.0% in 2024 before worsening to -6.9% in 2025. The 2025 loss reduction came as sales fell 17.5%, so it cannot be read as a margin improvement.

The net loss attributable to controlling shareholders narrowed from KRW 408.9 billion in 2022 to KRW 346.9 billion in 2023 and KRW 325.7 billion in 2024, and turned to a KRW 393.1 billion profit in 2025.

Total net income in 2025 was KRW 326 billion, below the controlling-shareholder figure, which suggests that the non-controlling interest share was a loss.

Quarterly, the operating loss was KRW 26.1 billion in 3Q 2025 on sales of KRW 580.3 billion, and widened to KRW 73.3 billion in 4Q 2025 on sales of KRW 570.5 billion. In 1Q 2026, sales were KRW 587 billion and operating profit was KRW 277 million, a swing to profit.

In 2Q 2026, sales reached KRW 871.3 billion (up 48.4% from the prior quarter) and operating profit reached KRW 170.8 billion. Reports attributed the 1H 2026 profit to a recovery in Vietnam plant utilization and higher PP prices.

In 3Q 2026, sales were KRW 733.6 billion (up 26.4% year on year), but the company posted an operating loss of KRW 19.5 billion, and the net loss attributable to controlling shareholders was KRW 58.1 billion.

Reports attributed the 3Q loss to higher production costs from rising logistics expenses and lower selling prices, which narrowed the PP/DH spread.

05

Industry analysis

In a December 2025 report, Korea Investors Service (KIS) analyzed that the PP spread over propane has stayed at a level well below USD 200 per ton since 2022.

The same report said PP capacity grew by about 5.5 million tons a year net through 2024, and that propylene-based supply would keep rising by more than 8 million tons a year in 2026 and 2027, so supply-demand conditions would improve only after 2028.

Reports put China's propylene capacity at about 50 million tons last year, of which about 16 million tons came from PDH units. Analysts noted that PDH-focused producers without a naphtha cracker are under pressure as oversupply builds, and Taekwang Industrial halted its PDH operations entirely.

Hyosung Chemical has a vertically integrated chain from PDH to PP, which helps on feedstock sourcing, but its heavy reliance on PP makes it hard to respond to market shifts. An October 2026 report noted that rising propane costs pushed PP prices up, while Vietnamese plastic processors were holding off on new purchases.

Because it has become difficult to pass cost increases through to selling prices, there is concern that the 4Q 2026 earnings recovery could slow. Raising the share of high-value products is cited as the main tool for defending spreads.

06

Outlook

The company has said it aims to raise the high-value product share to 54.5% in 2026 and 75% by 2030. Reports on 3Q 2026 said the company plans to raise the share of high-value production and sales, centered on specialty PP, to improve profitability.

The Vietnam subsidiary's sales rose year on year after its plant returned to normal operation, and polyketone sales are growing on demand for automotive and service robot parts. Still, rising propane prices and softer PP purchasing demand in Southeast Asia could pressure 4Q 2026 spreads.

On the financial side, reports put the debt ratio at 193.1% at the end of June 2026, but 1H 2026 operating cash flow was negative at KRW 3.6 billion. Interest paid over the same half was KRW 41.2 billion, and the guarantee burden on the Vietnam subsidiary's debt remains.

On the business side, the pending sale of the TAC film business is a variable that could change PP dependence. The next checkpoints are whether the 3Q 2026 loss continues in the 4Q 2026 preliminary results and how the high-value share target progresses.

08

Bull factors

Quarterly Profit and Narrowing Losses

The operating loss narrowed to KRW 160.5 billion in 2025 from KRW 336.7 billion in 2022. In 2Q 2026 sales reached KRW 871.3 billion and operating profit reached KRW 170.8 billion, a quarterly profit. Net income attributable to controlling shareholders turned to a KRW 393.1 billion profit in 2025. The bull case rests on the possibility that the annual loss structure changes if earnings power recovers.

Balance Sheet Recovery and Lower Debt Burden

Total equity, negative at end-2024, recovered to KRW 633.1 billion at end-2025. The debt ratio fell from 309.8% in 2025 to 193.1% at the end of June 2026, according to reports. Hyosung said the improvement should be seen as a structural recovery in financial strength rather than simple external funding.

High-Value PP Mix and Vietnam Normalization

The high-value product share at the Vietnam subsidiary rose to 49% in 1H 2026 from 43% in 2025, and the company has set targets of 54.5% for 2026 and 75% by 2030. In 3Q 2026 the Vietnam plant ran normally and its sales rose year on year. Polyketone widened its profit on higher sales for automotive and service robot parts.

09

Bear factors

Prolonged Weakness in PP Spreads

KIS analyzed that the PP spread has stayed far below USD 200 per ton since 2022. It expects propylene-based supply of more than 8 million tons a year in 2026 and 2027, with conditions improving only after 2028. The 3Q 2026 operating loss of KRW 19.5 billion is linked to the narrower PP/DH spread.

3Q Loss and Concerns for 4Q

The company returned to an operating loss of KRW 19.5 billion in 3Q 2026 after a 2Q profit. A late-October 2026 report raised concern that rising propane prices and weak Southeast Asian PP purchasing could slow the 4Q earnings recovery. The difficulty in passing cost increases through to selling prices was cited as a main cause.

Cash Generation and Interest Burden

Operating cash flow was -KRW 85 billion in 2025, and 1H 2026 operating cash flow was negative at KRW 3.6 billion. Interest paid over the same half reached KRW 41.2 billion. Reports noted that the guarantee burden on Vietnam subsidiary debt remains and that stable cash generation is needed.

10

Risk factors

Feedstock and Logistics Costs

Propane is the key feedstock for the Vietnam PDH unit. If propane prices rise and PP selling prices cannot follow, the spread narrows. Production costs rose in 3Q 2026 because of higher logistics expenses.

Because the gap between feedstock cost and selling price determines profitability, margins could fall if both move against the company at once.

Financial and Credit Risk

KIS rates the company's senior unsecured bonds BBB with a stable outlook, citing growing reliance on PP earnings and a heavy financial burden as rating factors. The possibility of support from Hyosung Group in an emergency is reflected in the rating. If group support shrinks or asset sales are delayed, financial strain could resurface.

Plant Operations and Portfolio Restructuring

Vietnam PDH operations were repeatedly halted during stabilization in 2022, and a rating agency said recurrence could lead to downward pressure on ratings. In 2025 the company sold its specialty gas business and Onsan tank terminal, halted TPA and is still selling its TAC film business. Until the restructuring is complete, changes in plant utilization feed directly into earnings.

12

Overall view

Hyosung Chemical posted operating losses from 2022 through 2025, and the size of the loss shrank each year, but the operating margin worsened again in 2025. In 2025 the company turned controlling-shareholder net income to a profit, total equity returned to positive territory and the debt ratio fell.

The 2Q 2026 operating profit of KRW 170.8 billion was attributed in reports to a recovery in Vietnam plant utilization and higher PP prices, but 3Q 2026 returned to an operating loss of KRW 19.5 billion. The 3Q loss is explained by higher logistics costs and lower selling prices that narrowed the PP/DH spread.

KIS expects PP spreads to remain low and propylene supply to continue through 2027, with conditions improving only after 2028.

Strengths include a rising high-value PP mix, normal operation at the Vietnam plant and a stronger balance sheet, while weaknesses include weak PP spreads, feedstock and logistics costs, a negative 1H 2026 operating cash flow and KRW 41.2 billion in interest paid over the same half.

The next checkpoints are the 4Q 2026 preliminary results, the confirmed annual operating margin and cash flow in the 2026 annual report, and progress of the TAC film sale.
