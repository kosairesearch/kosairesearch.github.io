02

Business structure

Aromatica is a clean-beauty brand built around aromatherapy-based scalp and skin care, with a fully vegan product lineup and a vertically integrated structure spanning ingredient sourcing, manufacturing, and R&D through its own factory and research center.

The company continuously allocates about 2.4% of revenue to R&D, enabling in-house production of formulations that are difficult to outsource to conventional ODM manufacturers. It listed on KOSDAQ on November 27, 2025, with the IPO price fixed at KRW 8,000, the top end of the indicative range.

The domestic business runs through offline retail such as Olive Young, Costco, and military PX stores alongside online platforms including Naver, Kakao, Coupang, and Kurly, with first-half 2026 domestic revenue up 21.8% year-on-year to KRW 20.5 billion.

The overseas business is centered on Amazon US across B2B and B2C channels together with global distribution partners such as Silicon 2 and YesAsia, and in 2026 the company established a US subsidiary, Aromatica USA Inc., to expand its global capabilities.

Overseas offline distribution points grew from 763 at the end of 2024 to 1,589 in the first half of 2026, with a plan to expand to roughly 3,000 locations in the second half, including Target in the US and Superdrug in the UK.

Competitively, the company faces large cosmetics players such as Amorepacific and LG H&H as well as numerous indie brands including Able C&C, Manyo Factory, VT, and d'Alba Global in both domestic and overseas markets.

The company has stated that IPO proceeds will be used entirely for global marketing, distribution network expansion, and logistics infrastructure investment rather than debt repayment.

04

Earnings analysis

Full-year 2025 revenue came to KRW 51.2 billion, down 2.7% from KRW 52.6 billion in 2024, while operating profit fell 9.1% to KRW 5.14 billion from KRW 5.65 billion, pulling the operating margin down slightly from 10.7% to 10.0%.

Net income attributable to owners, however, rose 3.4% to KRW 4.58 billion from KRW 4.43 billion—identical to the consolidated net income figure including minority interests—reflecting the benefit of a leaner balance sheet and lower non-operating costs.

The company attributed the revenue and operating profit decline to short-term cost burdens from expanding overseas distribution channels and marketing and personnel investment.

By quarter, revenue was KRW 13.7 billion in Q4 2025, then rose for two consecutive quarters to KRW 14 billion in Q1 2026 (up 1.8% quarter-on-quarter) and KRW 17.7 billion in Q2 2026 (up 26.7%).

Operating profit improved markedly over the same period, moving from KRW 995 million to KRW 1.34 billion (up 34.2%) and KRW 2.25 billion (up 68.2%), while net income attributable to owners also rose from KRW 1.24 billion to KRW 1.27 billion (up 3.0%) and KRW 1.75 billion (up 37.7%).

The sum of net income attributable to owners over the last four quarters (Q1–Q4 2025) was KRW 4.58 billion, matching the full-year 2025 figure.

At the end of 2025, total equity stood at KRW 42.5 billion (with equity attributable to owners at the same level) against total liabilities of KRW 9.09 billion, cutting the debt-to-equity ratio sharply from 76.5% in 2024 to 21.4% in 2025 as IPO-related capital inflows took effect.

Operating cash flow, however, declined from KRW 7.91 billion in 2024 to KRW 5.1 billion in 2025, suggesting that revenue mix shifts and working-capital changes weighed on cash generation.

05

Industry analysis

The global aromatherapy market, estimated at roughly USD 10.5 billion in 2025, is projected to grow at a compound annual rate of 8.32% through 2034, with North America cited as the largest region on the back of mature wellness retail infrastructure.

Growth in the sub-segment Aromatica operates in is being supported by the spread of the 'skinification' trend, which treats scalp care with the same rigor as facial skin care, boosting demand for natural and vegan scalp-care products.

Across K-beauty broadly, exports to non-China markets such as the Americas and Japan have continued to drive growth, and Aromatica itself has posted strong growth in its priority markets of the Americas and Japan.

The domestic cosmetics market features a mixed competitive landscape in which comprehensive players such as Amorepacific and LG H&H compete alongside small and mid-sized indie brands like Able C&C and Manyo Factory for the same retail channels such as Olive Young.

Aromatica has itself disclosed in its securities filing that competition could intensify due to rising new-entrant activity and pharmaceutical companies entering the cosmeceutical market.

Its vertically integrated structure, with in-house manufacturing and R&D, is cited as a point of differentiation from indie brands that rely on outsourced production, particularly in quality control and lead-time responsiveness.

China's cosmetics import market has contracted for several consecutive years but showed a narrower decline in 2025, meaning Aromatica's limited exposure to China could be a relatively risk-limiting factor in its portfolio mix.

Outlook

The company has outlined plans to strengthen its growth base in the second half of 2026 through expanded global offline distribution and market-specific strategies, with CEO Kim Young-gyun stating that global expansion centered on the United States and stronger domestic retail channels were the key drivers of growth.

Specifically, the company plans to expand its overseas offline footprint to roughly 3,000 stores, including Target in the US and Superdrug in the UK, while adding TikTok Shop as a new online channel in the second half.

Hana Securities analyst Park Jong-dae projected in a September 2026 report that Aromatica's third-quarter revenue and operating profit would rise 38% and 41% year-on-year to KRW 18.0 billion and KRW 2.7 billion, respectively, and forecast full-year revenue of about KRW 70 billion with operating profit of KRW 9.1 billion, implying a roughly 13% operating margin; the report did not provide a separate investment rating or target price.

Shinhan Investment Corp also published a report in the first half of 2026 focused on North American offline channel expansion, underscoring that sell-side attention is focused on the pace of overseas distribution growth.

IPO proceeds raised at listing are slated to be deployed entirely for global marketing, logistics infrastructure, and store expansion rather than debt repayment, with execution planned in stages through 2026–2027.

How this channel expansion and investment pace translates into actual quarterly results will likely be the key point to watch in upcoming disclosures.

Bull factors

Global Channel Expansion and Amazon Momentum

First-half 2026 Amazon US sales surged 109% year-on-year, helping lift the global revenue share to 35.5%. Overseas offline store count grew from 763 at the end of 2024 to 1,589 in the first half of 2026, with plans to expand to about 3,000 including Target in the US and Superdrug in the UK in the second half. This channel diversification is acting as a key driver of the recovery from the 2025 slowdown.

Operating Leverage from Vertical Integration

With its own factory and R&D center, the company continuously invests 2.4% of revenue in R&D, giving it direct control over quality and lead times. Operating profit rose 34.2% and 68.2% quarter-on-quarter in Q1 and Q2 2026, respectively, outpacing revenue growth. This points to fixed-cost dilution benefits as sales expand.

Improved Balance Sheet Post-Listing

IPO proceeds from the November 2025 KOSDAQ listing helped cut the debt-to-equity ratio sharply from 76.5% in 2024 to 21.4% in 2025. Total equity at the end of 2025 stood at KRW 42.5 billion, well above total liabilities of KRW 9.09 billion.

This relatively light balance sheet gives the company room to fund overseas marketing and logistics investment from its own resources.

Bear factors

2025 Earnings Stagnation and Recurrence Risk

Full-year 2025 revenue fell 2.7% year-on-year to KRW 51.2 billion, and operating profit declined 9.1% to KRW 5.14 billion. The company attributed this to short-term cost burdens from expanding overseas distribution and marketing and personnel investment. Similar profitability pauses cannot be ruled out if new market-entry costs recur in the future.

Intensifying Domestic Cosmetics Competition

The company has stated in its securities filing that intensifying competition from new market entrants and pharmaceutical companies moving into the cosmeceutical space could lead to lower market share or slower revenue growth.

Large comprehensive cosmetics companies and numerous indie brands compete for the same domestic retail channels. If domestic growth slows, reliance on overseas channels could increase further.

Supply-Demand Volatility of a Recent Listing

Having listed in November 2025, the stock is less than a year into trading, with lock-up shares scheduled to be released in stages over time. Changes in the free float can influence trading volume and price volatility. As the company pays no dividend, there is currently limited cushioning from shareholder returns.

Overall view

Aromatica saw revenue and operating profit both decline in 2025 amid cost burdens from restructuring its overseas channels, but moved into 2026 with revenue, operating profit, and net income attributable to owners all rising for two consecutive quarters, driven by Amazon US momentum and expanding global offline distribution.

The debt-to-equity ratio fell sharply after listing, improving the balance sheet, and the company's vertically integrated structure spanning in-house manufacturing and R&D is cited as a strength from an operating-leverage perspective.

Some brokerages, including Hana Securities, have issued improved full-year 2026 forecasts, but these remain projections rather than confirmed disclosed results.
