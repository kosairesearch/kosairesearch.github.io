02

Business structure

YM Tech was founded in 2004 and listed on KOSDAQ in 2021 as a maker of DC power control relays. According to Wisereport's company monitor, EV relays accounted for 54.12% of 2025 product sales, DC relays for 4.01%, and other products for the rest.

JobKorea's business breakdown instead shows EV relays at 54%, semiconductor parts processing equipment and others at 39%, and DC relays at 6%, so the main mix looks different depending on the classification.

Most sales come from EV charger and ESS-related makers, and because the customer base is concentrated in a small number of large companies, order timing drives the sales pattern. Orders usually follow production plans set early in the year, which concentrates sales in the second half.

Wisereport notes that seasonality is gradually easing as renewable energy demand grows and customers diversify overseas. Exports made up 72.21% of 2025 EV relay sales, against 27.79% domestic. R&D spending in 2025 was about KRW 1.9 billion, or 6.64% of sales.

With customers concentrated among a few large buyers and a high export share, sales are sensitive to a small number of orders and to overseas demand.

04

Earnings analysis

Annual sales went from KRW 49.3 billion in 2022 to KRW 34.9 billion in 2023 (down 29.2% year on year), KRW 30.9 billion in 2024 (down 11.4%) and KRW 29.1 billion in 2025 (down 5.8%). Sales have declined for three consecutive years from 2023 through 2025, with 2022 the largest year and 2025 the smallest among 2022-2025.

Operating profit fell from KRW 14.9 billion in 2022 to KRW 5.96 billion in 2023, KRW 4.32 billion in 2024 and KRW 1.74 billion in 2025, when it was down 59.6% year on year. Operating margin slid from 30.2% in 2022 to 17.1% in 2023, 14.0% in 2024 and 6.0% in 2025.

Net profit attributable to controlling shareholders fell from KRW 12.7 billion in 2022 to KRW 3.17 billion in 2025, and net profit including non-controlling interests was disclosed at the same amounts. The company attributed the 2025 decline mainly to reduced orders from major customers and weaker demand in China.

Operating cash flow dropped from KRW 10.7 billion in 2022 to KRW 3.69 billion in 2025.

On a quarterly basis, the third quarter of 2025 showed sales of KRW 6.39 billion and operating profit of KRW 712 million, then the fourth quarter brought sales of KRW 5.67 billion (down 11.2% from the prior quarter) and an operating loss of KRW 1.36 billion, a swing into loss.

The first quarter of 2026 lifted sales to KRW 14 billion (up 146.4% quarter on quarter) and returned operating profit of KRW 473 million.

The second quarter posted sales of KRW 17.7 billion, operating profit of KRW 1.84 billion (up 290.2%) and net profit attributable to controlling shareholders of KRW 1.34 billion (up 400.3%).

The third quarter reached sales of KRW 17.5 billion (down 1.1% quarter on quarter, up 173.4% year on year), but operating profit eased to KRW 1.46 billion (down 20.6%) and the controlling-shareholder result swung to a net loss of KRW 140 million, a swing into loss year on year.

Over the last four quarters, net profit attributable to controlling shareholders totaled KRW 1.09 billion. Sales have recovered sharply, but whether margins rebuild to earlier levels remains unclear.

05

Industry analysis

End demand centers on EV chargers and ESS installations. According to the company, demand from renewable energy is growing quickly, and its customers are broadening from domestic to overseas buyers.

Relay orders move with the installation schedules of charger and ESS makers, so demand shifts pass through to quarterly sales quickly. Because orders tend to follow production plans set early in the year, sales have historically clustered in the second half.

However, second-quarter 2026 sales of KRW 17.7 billion were close to the KRW 17.5 billion of the third quarter, which suggests the pattern may be weakening. The company pointed to weaker demand in China as a reason for the 2025 decline.

Renewable and ESS demand is a medium-term growth factor, but the 2025 results show that this trend does not lift sales automatically. On competition, the key variables are how steadily the company wins orders from large customers and how far it can grow its overseas share. Judging the cycle position will require tracking EV charger and ESS installation trends.

Outlook

The 2026 annual results will be fixed only once the annual business report is filed. Sales ran at KRW 14 billion, KRW 17.7 billion and KRW 17.5 billion across the first three quarters of 2026, with operating profit of KRW 473 million, KRW 1.84 billion and KRW 1.46 billion.

Quarterly sales are now approaching the 2025 annual level of KRW 29.1 billion, so whether this pace carries into the fourth quarter and the full year is the central checkpoint.

If orders keep clustering in the second half as in past patterns, the fourth quarter of 2026 should be watched against the third quarter's level. Year-end closing costs can move fourth-quarter results, as the operating loss of the fourth quarter of 2025 showed.

The third-quarter net loss attributable to controlling shareholders also means non-operating items and tax effects will matter for full-year net profit. With a high export share, exchange rates and overseas customer order timing are also variables for quarterly results.

The company's numerical annual targets are not part of the data underlying this report, so the full-year trend is best judged against the annual business report figures. The next quarterly report will show whether higher sales translate into a better operating margin.

Bull factors

Quarterly sales scale-up

First-quarter 2026 sales reached KRW 14 billion, up 146.4% from the prior quarter, followed by KRW 17.7 billion in the second quarter and KRW 17.5 billion in the third. Third-quarter 2026 sales rose 173.4% year on year.

Compared with KRW 5.67 billion in the fourth quarter of 2025, the quarterly sales base expanded clearly in 2026. The size of the sales recovery is the main bullish point.

Operating profit recovery after returning to profit

Operating results moved from an operating loss of KRW 1.36 billion in the fourth quarter of 2025 to operating profit of KRW 473 million in the first quarter of 2026.

Second-quarter 2026 operating profit of KRW 1.84 billion rose 290.2% from the prior quarter, and net profit attributable to controlling shareholders of KRW 1.34 billion rose 400.3%. The recent quarterly direction of profit recovery is a bullish factor.

Low leverage and high export share

At the end of 2025, the debt ratio stood at 5.2%, with total liabilities of KRW 2.96 billion against total equity of KRW 57.1 billion. The light debt load limits pressure on the balance sheet even in quarters with losses.

Exports accounted for 72.21% of 2025 EV relay sales, and the company pointed to growing renewable energy demand and customer diversification overseas.

Lower operating cash and investment outflow

According to JobKorea's financial data, net cash used in investing activities in 2025 was about KRW 9.9 billion. Investment outflows far exceeded operating cash generation, which reduced cash headroom and is a bearish factor.

Risk factors

Customer mix and contract terms

Sales depend on a small number of large customers in EV chargers and ESS. A change in contract terms or in how supply volumes are allocated among them could quickly move quarterly sales.

Because orders follow production plans set early in the year, a shift in a large customer's supply plan would widen the gap between quarters. The more concentrated the customer base becomes, the more directly this risk reaches earnings.

FX and overseas demand

Exports made up 72.21% of 2025 EV relay sales, so results are exposed to exchange rates and to overseas customers' investment schedules. Because overseas expansion is central to the diversification strategy, a slowdown in overseas demand or a sharp currency move could slow the sales recovery. If export prices and costs move in different currencies, margins would also be affected.

Policy and installation volume

Installation volumes for EV chargers and ESS depend on subsidies and installation policy, so changes in these rules could shift relay order volumes. The semiconductor parts processing equipment portion, which JobKorea's classification places at 39% of business, moves with downstream investment schedules. Both businesses rely on outside policy and customers' investment decisions.

Overall view

YM Tech is a specialist maker whose main product is EV relays, and its annual sales and profits have declined since 2022. Sales were KRW 29.1 billion in 2025 and operating profit was KRW 1.74 billion, with the operating margin falling to 6.0%.

Quarterly sales in 2026 rose sharply, to KRW 14 billion in the first quarter, KRW 17.7 billion in the second and KRW 17.5 billion in the third, and operating profit reached KRW 1.84 billion in the second quarter. The third quarter returned to a net loss attributable to controlling shareholders of KRW 140 million.

On the financial side, the 5.2% debt ratio and KRW 57.1 billion in total equity at the end of 2025 are strengths. On the other side, operating cash flow declined and investing outflows in 2025 were large, weakening cash generation.

Strengths and weaknesses carry comparable weight, and whether the sales recovery has become a profit recovery is still unclear. The next checkpoints are the operating margin in the 2026 annual business report and the quarterly trend in the first quarter of 2027.
