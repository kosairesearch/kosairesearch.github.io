Report overview

Plywood Exit Tests Eagon's Turnaround

Eagon Industrial is restructuring around flooring and renewable energy ahead of halting domestic plywood production on November 27, 2026, while its disclosed results show operating losses, net losses and falling sales since 2025.

  1. 1

    The board resolved on November 10, 2026 to end domestic plywood production, effective November 27, 2026. The segment accounted for 9.79% of 2025 consolidated sales.

  2. 2

    On a DART basis, 2025 operating loss was KRW 3.83 billion and net loss attributable to controlling shareholders was KRW 14.2 billion, with losses in each year since 2023. The 2025 operating margin was -1.3%.

  3. 3

    Operating profit turned positive in the first and second quarters of 2026 (KRW 1.15 billion and KRW 1.41 billion), then the third quarter posted an operating loss of KRW 3.37 billion. Quarterly sales declined for four consecutive quarters from the fourth quarter of 2025 through the third quarter of 2026.

  4. 4

    At the end of 2025, the debt ratio was 111.9%, with total equity of KRW 188.8 billion and total liabilities of KRW 211.3 billion. The ratio has eased from 120.8% in 2022, but continued losses keep the financial burden in view.

  5. 5

    A sale of an ancillary land parcel in Dohwa-dong and a share buyback with planned cancellation have been announced, but the completion timing and sale price have not been fixed.

02

Business structure

Eagon Industrial is a wood-based building materials company founded in 1972, and after its 1988 listing it led the domestic construction plywood business as an oligopoly, according to WISEreport's company profile.

Its consolidated subsidiaries include a Chile subsidiary and a Solomon Islands subsidiary, and the group makes and sells plywood and flooring, trades timber, runs forestry operations and a district energy business. Segment mix varies by data provider.

Butler's company data puts wood at 83%, energy at 14% and forestry at 3% based on second-quarter 2026 sales, while JobKorea's March 2026 breakdown shows plywood and flooring at 80%, steam at 13% and raw timber at 6%.

In 2025 the plywood segment that is now being halted generated KRW 29.12 billion, or 9.79% of consolidated sales and 18.34% of standalone sales, according to the exchange disclosure.

WISEreport says the Chile subsidiary maintains solid profitability by using local log sourcing, low labor costs and a free trade agreement. The energy segment is described as having stable revenue from long-term supply contracts and low-cost wood chip fuel.

Flooring is described as a business-to-business supply model centered on construction companies. The company said it will focus domestically on flooring and renewable energy and pursue plywood opportunities in the Americas and Europe through the Chile subsidiary (Incheon Ilbo).

Low-priced imported plywood is the largest competitive pressure, and after Sunchang Industry, now SunL, stopped plywood production in 2023, Eagon Industrial is also exiting domestic plywood.

04

Earnings analysis

On a DART basis, annual sales moved from KRW 329.3 billion in 2022 to KRW 311.6 billion in 2023 (down 5.4% year on year), KRW 327 billion in 2024 (up 4.9%), and KRW 297.3 billion in 2025 (down 9.1%). 2022 was the largest year in the period and 2025 the smallest.

Operating profit fell from KRW 19.3 billion in 2022 to KRW 9.12 billion in 2023 (down 52.8%), then recovered to KRW 13.8 billion in 2024 (up 51.3%), before 2025 turned into an operating loss of KRW 3.83 billion. The operating margin was 5.9% in 2022, 2.9% in 2023, 4.2% in 2024 and -1.3% in 2025.

Net income attributable to controlling shareholders was KRW 5.36 billion in 2022, turned into a loss of KRW 5.59 billion in 2023, narrowed to a loss of KRW 1.93 billion in 2024, and widened to a loss of KRW 14.2 billion in 2025.

Operating cash flow was KRW 10.4 billion in 2022, KRW 392 million in 2023, KRW 17 billion in 2024 and KRW 11.4 billion in 2025, staying positive even in loss years. The quarterly pattern is more pronounced.

Sales went from KRW 77 billion in the third quarter of 2025 to KRW 67.2 billion in the fourth quarter (down 12.7% quarter on quarter), KRW 65.1 billion in the first quarter of 2026, KRW 64.6 billion in the second quarter and KRW 52.4 billion in the third quarter (down 31.9% year on year), declining for four consecutive quarters.

Operating profit moved from an operating loss of KRW 6.19 billion in the fourth quarter of 2025 to operating profits of KRW 1.15 billion in the first quarter of 2026 and KRW 1.41 billion in the second quarter, then back to an operating loss of KRW 3.37 billion in the third quarter.

Net loss attributable to controlling shareholders widened to KRW 4.39 billion in the third quarter of 2026, and the last four quarters total KRW 14.8 billion in losses. Separately, WISEreport's data show first-half 2026 consolidated sales down 15.3% while operating profit rose 9.8%.

05

Industry analysis

Domestic construction plywood was an oligopoly, but low-priced imported plywood has intensified price competition. Construction slowdowns have reduced demand, and WISEreport describes falling housing starts as a factor that weighed on profitability in the wood segment.

The same source notes that SunL's production halt could open an opportunity to expand market share. Domestic plywood capacity has narrowed further, following SunL's exit and now Eagon Industrial's halt.

The company says it sustained the business through production optimization and cost reduction, but cumulative losses followed as price competition persisted.

Against peers, the Chile subsidiary benefits from local log sourcing, lower labor costs and a free trade agreement, which is described as giving it a relatively better cost position. The flooring business supplies construction projects and is therefore tied to domestic construction activity.

Whether the construction cycle is recovering can be tracked through indicators such as housing starts. Whether the remaining business can restore profitability after the domestic plywood exit has not yet been reflected in disclosed results.

06

Outlook

The company disclosed that it expects sales to decline after halting domestic plywood production, but it anticipates profitability gains from fixed-cost reductions and from ending loss-making operations. The production halt is scheduled for November 27, 2026, and no resumption date has been listed.

Domestically the company plans to improve profitability centered on flooring and to build renewable energy as a new business. Proceeds from selling an ancillary land parcel in Dohwa-dong, Michuhol-gu, Incheon, are mentioned as a funding source.

The parcel covers 15,747.6 square meters and had a book value of KRW 27.4 billion at the end of 2025. The sale price, completion timing and scale of fund deployment have not been fixed.

On shareholder returns, a share buyback trust contract was signed for the period from August 24, 2026 to February 23, 2027, with KRW 1.5 billion allocated and 600,000 common shares planned to be fully cancelled.

On products, the company's history records the launch of a premium high-density stone flooring line in January 2026. The next checkpoints are the size of the fourth-quarter sales decline after the plywood halt, whether the land sale is executed, and whether the share cancellation is completed.

08

Bull factors

Fixed-Cost Savings From the Plywood Exit

The company disclosed that although future sales may decline due to the suspension of plywood production, it expects profitability to improve from lower fixed costs and the discontinuation of loss-making operations.

In 2025, this segment accounted for 9.79% of sales on a consolidated basis and 18.34% on a separate basis. Given that the 2025 operating margin was -1.3%, the next thing to check is how the effect of shedding the loss-making business is reflected in the margin.

Stability From the Chile Unit and Energy

WiseReport company information explains that the Chilean subsidiary maintains solid profitability by using local log sourcing, low labor costs, and free trade agreements. The energy segment's sales stability stems from long-term supply contracts and low-cost wood chip fuel.

Energy accounted for 14% of sales in the second quarter of 2026, so segments other than timber may serve as one pillar of business stability.

Profitable Quarters in the First Half of 2026

Based on reported results, operating profit was KRW 1.15 billion in the first quarter of 2026 and KRW 1.41 billion in the second quarter, marking two consecutive quarters of profit. According to WiseReport's tally, operating profit in the first half of 2026 rose 9.8% from a year earlier. A positive factor is that there were quarters of positive operating profit even as sales declined.

Bear factors

Sales Down for Four Straight Quarters

Based on reported results, sales fell from KRW 77.0 billion in the third quarter of 2025 to KRW 52.4 billion in the third quarter of 2026, with the third quarter of 2026 down 31.9% year over year. Sales declined for four consecutive quarters from the fourth quarter of 2025 through the third quarter of 2026.

This is explained as the combined result of fewer housing starts and low-priced imported plywood. The company also stated in its disclosure that suspending plywood production could lower sales further.

Return to Losses in the Third Quarter of 2026

In the third quarter of 2026, the operating loss was KRW 3.37 billion and the net loss attributable to owners of the parent was KRW 4.39 billion, ending the profitable stretch of the second quarter.

The net loss attributable to owners of the parent has been in the red for five consecutive quarters since the third quarter of 2025.

The combined net loss attributable to owners of the parent over the last four quarters, KRW 14.8 billion, exceeds the full-year 2025 net loss attributable to owners of the parent of KRW 14.2 billion. The key question is whether the pattern of losses accumulating quarter after quarter continues.

Financial Burden and Reliance on Asset Sales

At the end of 2025, total liabilities were KRW 211.3 billion, exceeding total equity of KRW 188.8 billion, and the debt ratio was 111.9%. Since net losses have been recorded for three consecutive years, the timing of an earnings recovery is a precondition for easing the financial burden.

The company is pursuing the sale of land used for ancillary facilities to fund new businesses, but the sale price and completion date have not been finalized.

10

Risk factors

Industry and Demand Risk

If the construction downturn is prolonged, demand for both plywood and flooring could decline together. The decline in housing starts is cited as a factor weighing on the profitability of the timber segment.

The situation in which low-priced imported plywood continues to put pressure on domestic prices also remains a risk.

Execution Risk of the Restructuring

After the plywood exit, the decline in sales could outweigh the effect of fixed-cost savings. The results of the new businesses in flooring and renewable energy have not yet been confirmed in reported earnings.

The price and timing of the land sale, and whether the cancellation of treasury shares will be completed, also remain unconfirmed.

Ownership and Float Structure Risk

According to WiseReport company information, the ownership stake of the largest shareholder and related parties is 35.24% (as of October 28, 2026), and the free-float ratio is 63.16%. In March 2026, the CEO changed from Baek Oh-hyeon to Moon Bo-young (Saramin company information).

The change in management and the ownership structure are tied to the pace at which the business restructuring is carried out.

12

Overall view

Eagon Industrial is about to halt domestic plywood production as part of a business restructuring. Its disclosed results show a 2025 operating loss after prior profits, losses in each year since 2023, and sales declining for four consecutive quarters through the third quarter of 2026.

Operating profit in the first and second quarters of 2026 is read as evidence of improvement. However, the return to an operating loss in the third quarter of 2026 and the KRW 14.8 billion in losses attributable to controlling shareholders over the last four quarters show that the recovery is not yet sustained.

The company expects fixed-cost savings and the end of loss-making operations to help, but disclosures have not yet shown whether the sales decline will offset those gains.

The Chile subsidiary and the energy segment are described as stable sources of earnings, while the results of the flooring and renewable energy businesses remain unconfirmed. The low price-to-book multiple and continuing losses should be read together.

The next checkpoints are sales after November 27, 2026, whether the land sale is executed, and full-year results in the 2026 annual report.
