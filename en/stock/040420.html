JLS: Profit Resilience and Dividend Durability Amid a Shrinking Student Base

JLS's revenue and profit have trended lower since the 2023 peak. Operating profit rebounded in the first quarter of 2026 but then fell in both the second and third quarters, making the durability of the earnings recovery and the dividend policy the key items to track.

02

Business structure

JLS is an English-language education company serving primary and secondary school students, and its education business accounts for nearly all of its segment mix. It runs both directly operated academies and franchise branches; a press report noted 56 direct branches and 78 franchise branches at the end of 2021.

Its core programs are CHESS for elementary students and ACE for middle school students, and both are supplemented by online classes. Online education accounted for 15.8% of revenue as of the third quarter of 2024, so offline academies still generate most sales.

Its math academy brand, which adds math classes to English branches, opened three branches in October 2025. In 2026, franchise branches opened in January, March and June, and one branch was converted from direct operation to franchise in March.

The publishing and distribution unit supplies required textbooks for its school-age programs and expands sales channels through English titles such as CARAMEL TREE.

Competitively, the company positions itself as a premium English academy, and its tuition policy and shuttle bus operations are seen as key variables for price competitiveness.

In 2025 it contributed KRW 11.5 billion through a private equity fund to join an acquisition of an education content company, and it has named securing education platforms as a priority.

04

Earnings analysis

Annual results have been on a downward path since the 2023 peak. Revenue rose from KRW 110.8 billion in 2022 to KRW 113.4 billion in 2023, an increase of 2.3%, but then fell to KRW 105.8 billion in 2024 (down 6.6%) and KRW 103.1 billion in 2025 (down 2.6%), a two-year decline.

The company attributed the 2024 decline to lower revenue from the business environment and the resulting drop in profit. Operating profit went from KRW 16.7 billion in 2022 to KRW 16.4 billion in 2023 (down 2.1%), KRW 10.1 billion in 2024 (down 38.2%) and KRW 9.77 billion in 2025 (down 3.5%), a three-year decline.

The operating margin moved from 15.1% in 2022 to 14.4% in 2023, 9.6% in 2024 and 9.5% in 2025, stepping down in 2024 and holding there.

Profit attributable to controlling shareholders fell from KRW 13.3 billion in 2023, which was flat year on year, to KRW 7.61 billion in 2024 (down 42.9%) and KRW 6.64 billion in 2025 (down 12.7%); net profit including non-controlling interests is the same amount.

Operating cash flow fell from KRW 22.5 billion in 2022 to KRW 12.6 billion in 2024 before recovering modestly to KRW 14.6 billion in 2025. The debt ratio declined from 28.2% to 23.7% over the period, so financial burden eased while earnings power weakened.

By quarter, operating profit was KRW 2.56 billion in the third quarter of 2025 and KRW 1.89 billion in the fourth quarter (down 26.4% from the prior quarter), then rebounded to KRW 3.03 billion in the first quarter of 2026 (up 60.6%).

It then fell to KRW 2.63 billion in the second quarter (down 13.2%) and KRW 2.32 billion in the third quarter (down 11.6% quarter on quarter and down 9.4% year on year), a two-quarter decline.

Profit attributable to controlling shareholders dropped to KRW 90.06 million in the fourth quarter of 2025, rose to KRW 2.37 billion in the first quarter of 2026 and KRW 2.84 billion in the second quarter, then fell to KRW 1.81 billion in the third quarter. The last four quarters total KRW 7.12 billion.

05

Industry analysis

The key variable for JLS's private education market is the shrinking school-age population. A 2025 securities analysis projected that the elementary segment would decline at an annual rate of 7.0% from 2024 through 2030, which narrows the student base.

The company's student count grew at an annual rate of 4.2% from 2017 to 2023 but fell 3.6% in 2024, according to the same analysis.

Under these conditions, brokerage analysts focused on revenue per student, citing elementary tuition increases and partial charging for shuttle buses as drivers of higher revenue per student. Students in the math academy brand rose 7.9% in 2024, and analysts pointed to it as a growth lever.

In early 2026, brokers expected operating profit to rise as cost pressure from shuttle bus operations eased. However, third-quarter 2026 operating profit fell 9.4% year on year, so the gap between that forecast and actual results remains.

Competitively, the company is keeping its premium English academy positioning while strengthening exam preparation in its middle school program. A 2024 press report described its digital transition as slow given the small share of online education.

Its private equity investment in an education content company can be read as one response to these industry shifts.

06

Outlook

The corporate value-enhancement plan the company disclosed in March 2026 centers on building educational competitiveness through major student-achievement gains and on profit-focused management through selective concentration.

In the same plan, the company said it would keep paying dividends above the prior year's level every year. The plan does not include numerical annual operating profit targets, so earnings trends need to be tracked through quarterly disclosures.

In early 2026, brokers expected operating profit to rise year on year on lower shuttle bus costs.

Through the third quarter of 2026, operating profit rebounded in the first quarter but fell in the second and third quarters, while third-quarter revenue of KRW 25.5 billion edged up from KRW 24.9 billion in the second quarter.

Whether full-year 2026 operating profit ends higher than in 2025 therefore depends largely on fourth-quarter 2026 results. On expansion, franchise branches opened through 2026, and the open question is whether they broaden the revenue base.

Expanding math classes in English branches and strengthening middle school exam preparation are also ongoing priorities. Raising tuition and charging for shuttle buses are the pricing checkpoints, and whether these moves offset any drop in enrolled students is the key question.

The earnings contribution from the private equity investment in an education content company has not yet appeared in quarterly disclosures.

08

Bull factors

First-quarter profit rebound and easing cost pressure

Operating profit in the first quarter of 2026 rose 60.6% from the prior quarter to KRW 3.03 billion. Profit attributable to controlling shareholders in the second quarter rose 20.0% from the prior quarter to KRW 2.84 billion. Brokers in early 2026 expected operating profit to increase as cost pressure from shuttle buses eased.

A formalized shareholder return framework

In its March 2026 corporate value-enhancement plan, the company said it would keep paying dividends above the prior year's level every year. For fiscal 2025 it applied differentiated dividends between general shareholders and the largest shareholder. The 2025 payout ratio was 108.38%.

Low leverage and an asset base

Total equity at the end of 2025 was KRW 83.4 billion, all attributable to controlling shareholders. Total liabilities were KRW 19.8 billion, and the debt ratio declined from 28.2% in 2022 to 23.7% in 2025. Operating cash flow in 2025 was KRW 14.6 billion.

Bear factors

Shrinking school-age population and limits of tuition policy

A 2025 securities analysis projected that the elementary segment would decline at an annual rate of 7.0% from 2024 through 2030. The company's student count fell 3.6% in 2024. Third-quarter 2026 revenue fell 2.1% year on year.

Continued operating profit declines

Operating profit declined for three consecutive years from 2023 through 2025. In 2026, it fell in the second and third quarters after peaking in the first quarter. Third-quarter operating profit of KRW 2.32 billion was down 9.4% from a year earlier.

Dividend burden above earnings

Profit attributable to controlling shareholders in 2025 was KRW 6.64 billion, down 12.7% from 2024. The 2025 payout ratio of 108.38% exceeded earnings. If profit keeps declining, the burden of maintaining the dividend policy could grow.

Risk factors

Business structure and digital transition

As of the third quarter of 2024, online education accounted for only 15.8% of revenue. Research and development spending in the same quarter fell 32.7% year on year to KRW 879 million. Analysts warned that lower R&D could weigh on the competitiveness of the CHESS and ACE programs.

Cash use and liquidity

A 2024 press report noted that cash and equivalents fell 52.2% from KRW 11.9 billion at the end of 2023 to KRW 5.7 billion at the end of the third quarter of 2024. The current ratio was 99.9% in the same period. In 2025 the company committed KRW 11.5 billion to a private equity fund for an education content acquisition.

Pricing policy and enrolled students

A securities analysis said the company's conservative tuition increases left operating profit growth limited even when student numbers rose in earlier years. Raising tuition further carries the risk of student attrition.

Partial charging for shuttle buses could also produce different results depending on how enrolled students respond.

Overall view

JLS is an education company whose earnings power has declined from KRW 110.8 billion in revenue and KRW 16.7 billion in operating profit in 2022 to KRW 103.1 billion and KRW 9.77 billion in 2025.

The operating margin stepped down to 9.6% in 2024 and held at 9.5% in 2025, and the debt ratio fell to 23.7% over the same period, so the financial structure is relatively stable.

Operating profit rebounded to KRW 3.03 billion in the first quarter of 2026, but it fell to KRW 2.63 billion and KRW 2.32 billion in the second and third quarters, so the durability of the recovery needs more quarterly confirmation.

Bullish factors include financial stability, the March 2026 plan to keep raising dividends each year, and brokers' expectations of easing cost pressure.

Bearish factors include the shrinking school-age population, centered on the elementary segment, three consecutive years of operating profit decline, and the dividend burden relative to earnings shown by the 108.38% payout ratio for 2025.

Dividend durability can be judged by whether earnings and cash flow move together, so full-year 2026 results and the 2026 business report are the next checkpoints.

Valuation sits toward the upper part of the company's past PER band while the share price trades below book value, and the reading will depend on whether earnings recover. This report does not present an investment rating or target price and summarizes facts and checkpoints.
