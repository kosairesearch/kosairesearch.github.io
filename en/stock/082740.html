Report overview

Orders are piling up; earnings await the capacity expansion

Hanwha Engine booked KRW 2.80 trillion in cumulative new orders through the third quarter of 2026 and holds a backlog of KRW 5.65 trillion, yet profit attributable to controlling shareholders fell to KRW 11.6 billion in the third quarter as the capacity expansion is still ahead.

02

Business structure

Hanwha Engine is a marine equipment maker whose core business is ship engines, and ship engine orders accounted for KRW 2.6 trillion of the KRW 2.80 trillion in cumulative new orders through the third quarter of 2026.

Its main product is the low-speed (two-stroke) engine, and production of medium-speed (four-stroke) engines resumed in 2026. Dual-fuel (DF) engines made up 78% of the ship engine backlog as of the third quarter of 2026, reflecting a shift toward eco-friendly vessels that can run on LNG and other alternative fuels.

Its key customers are the affiliated shipbuilder Hanwha Ocean and Samsung Heavy Industries, and Chinese shipyards have emerged as a major customer group. iM Securities said in a November 2026 report that the share of sales to China rose from 22.3% in the second quarter of 2026 to 23.8% in the third quarter.

According to an SK Securities report, Hanwha Engine delivered 31 engines in the third quarter of 2026, comprising 17 for LNG carriers, 10 for tankers and 4 for container ships.

SK Securities also expected growth in the aftermarket (AM) business from a stronger overseas sales network, as stated in its January 2026 outlook. In competition, HD-Hyundai Marine Engine reportedly passed KRW 1.1 trillion in new engine orders in 2026, so rivalry among domestic ship engine makers is intense. The move by Chinese shipyards to expand engine capacity is cited as a long-term competitive variable.

04

Earnings analysis

On a disclosed basis, annual sales rose from KRW 764.2 billion in 2022 to KRW 854.4 billion in 2023 (up 11.8% year on year), KRW 1.2 trillion in 2024 (up 40.7%) and KRW 1.37 trillion in 2025 (up 14.0%).

Sales have grown for three consecutive years from 2023 through 2025, and 2025 was the largest year in the 2022 to 2025 period.

Operating profit moved from an operating loss of KRW 29.5 billion in 2022 to a profit of KRW 8.73 billion in 2023, a turn to profitability, and then rose to KRW 71.5 billion in 2024 (up 719.5%) and KRW 130.1 billion in 2025 (up 81.8%), growing for two consecutive years.

The operating margin was -3.9% in 2022, 1.0% in 2023, 6.0% in 2024 and 9.5% in 2025.

Profit attributable to controlling shareholders was a net loss of KRW 40.3 billion in 2022 and a net loss of KRW 426 million in 2023, when losses narrowed but persisted; it turned to a profit of KRW 79.2 billion in 2024 and rose 119.5% to KRW 173.8 billion in 2025.

Net profit including non-controlling interests was disclosed at the same amounts in each of these years. Operating cash flow in 2025 was KRW 330.3 billion, far above operating profit of KRW 130.1 billion, although IB Tomato noted that slower sales growth can reduce the cash inflow from working capital.

The debt-to-equity ratio fell from 407.2% in 2023 to 259.3% in 2024 and 204.9% in 2025, and at the end of 2025 total equity of KRW 556.1 billion was entirely attributable to controlling shareholders, against total liabilities of KRW 1.14 trillion.

On a quarterly basis, sales rose from KRW 297.3 billion with operating profit of KRW 26.6 billion in the third quarter of 2025 to KRW 345.2 billion with operating profit of KRW 51.4 billion in the first quarter of 2026.

Second-quarter 2026 sales reached KRW 362.2 billion, up 4.9% from the prior quarter, while operating profit fell 26.2% to KRW 37.9 billion.

Third-quarter 2026 sales rose 14.2% to KRW 413.6 billion and operating profit rose 7.9% to KRW 40.9 billion, but profit attributable to controlling shareholders fell 65.0% to KRW 11.6 billion.

SK Securities had projected third-quarter operating profit of KRW 47.5 billion, citing about KRW 5 billion in extra costs per quarter, and the disclosed figure came in at KRW 40.9 billion.

Why sales grew while profit to controlling shareholders declined cannot be fully explained by the quarterly disclosed figures alone.

05

Industry analysis

Demand for marine engines is rising as the shipping fleet shifts toward eco-friendly vessels and container ship replacement demand picks up.

According to an Asia Today report, Korean marine engine makers are continuing to win orders worth trillions of won, as shipowners rush to adopt dual-fuel vessels that can run on LNG or methanol instead of conventional bunker fuel.

Chinese shipyards, which compete with Korea in building vessels, are increasingly requesting Korean-made engines as a core component, which strengthens the bargaining position of Korean engine makers. Industry participants generally say a marine engine takes about 18 months from order to shipment.

LNG carrier orders have been recovering since late 2025 as global LNG development projects continue, according to a report from April 2026.

Analyst Lee Dong-heon at Shinhan Investment Corp. cited the rising share of DF engines and engine shortages at Chinese shipyards as drivers of order growth, while noting that the Iran situation could bring short-term volatility.

HD-Hyundai Marine Engine reportedly passed KRW 1.1 trillion in new orders in 2026, and 15 of the 21 new engine supply contracts it disclosed were related to China.

By contrast, SK Securities said in an October 2026 report that concerns about Chinese engine capacity expansion exist, but in the short term that expansion does not outpace the yard expansion of Chinese shipyards and does not threaten Korean engine makers.

Backlog covering several years of sales is cited as a relative strength for Hanwha Engine in this phase of the cycle.

06

Outlook

The company has said it plans to complete the expansion of its low-speed engine production facilities by the fourth quarter of 2026, and IB Tomato reported that the deadline for the expansion investment was extended to the end of 2026.

Once completed, annual capacity is expected to rise from 3.3 million to 5.33 million horsepower, an increase of about 61.5% (Asia Today, November 2026).

IB Tomato said sales growth would accelerate once the mass-production system stabilizes next year. iM Securities projected fourth-quarter 2026 sales of KRW 442.5 billion, operating profit of KRW 65.4 billion and an operating margin of 14.8%.

SK Securities estimated 2027 sales at KRW 2.41 trillion and operating profit at KRW 375.2 billion, with sales up 56.4% from 2026 (October 2026 report).

On data centers, SK Securities said Hanwha Engine could supply 300 to 400 megawatts of power generation engines to North American AI data centers through Hanwha Group affiliates from 2028.

In an April 2026 NDR follow-up, Korea Investment & Securities pointed to annual medium-speed engine capacity of 900 MW and the group's entry into data-center power generation. iM Securities said the engines currently on order mainly have deliveries in 2028.

Points to verify include whether the expansion is completed and mass production stabilizes after commissioning, and whether a four-stroke data-center license production contract is signed. SK Securities said in a September 2026 report that such a license production contract is highly likely to be concluded smoothly.

08

Bull factors

Backlog covers 3.6 years of sales

iM Securities said in a November 2026 report that the backlog of KRW 5.65 trillion equals 3.6 years of sales. Engines on order mainly carry 2028 delivery dates, which gives visibility on volumes in 2027 and 2028.

Korea Investment & Securities cited 167 medium-speed engines on order in a June 2026 report and factored additional second-half orders into its estimates. The long delivery schedule is cited as support for future sales visibility.

Eco-friendly DF engines dominate orders

DF engines accounted for 78% of the ship engine backlog of KRW 5.6 trillion as of the third quarter of 2026. A report said about 85% of the total backlog of KRW 5.2386 trillion was DF engines in the first quarter of 2026, which shows the order mix shifting toward eco-friendly engines. Industry observers say shipowners are ordering LNG-capable engines in advance to cut carbon emissions.

Capacity expansion plan lifts output 61.5%

Once the expansion is completed in the fourth quarter of 2026, annual capacity is expected to rise from 3.3 million to 5.33 million horsepower, about 61.5% higher. Because existing facilities run at high utilization, delivery volumes have been constrained, so the expansion is cited as a precondition for higher sales.

SK Securities estimated 2027 sales at KRW 2.41 trillion. Meritz Securities described the expansion in an April 2026 report as a strategy to raise average selling prices by increasing horsepower per engine.

09

Bear factors

Third-quarter profit to controlling shareholders plunged

Profit attributable to controlling shareholders in the third quarter of 2026 was KRW 11.6 billion, down 65.0% from the prior quarter and down 61.7% from a year earlier. Over the same period operating profit rose to KRW 40.9 billion, so the two measures moved in opposite directions.

SK Securities forecast in October 2026 that about KRW 5 billion in extra costs per quarter would keep operating profit below market expectations, and the disclosed third-quarter operating profit came in below its KRW 47.5 billion estimate. Whether these extra costs persist is a point to watch in later quarters.

Expansion delays and capacity bottlenecks

The deadline for the expansion investment was extended to the end of 2026, and IB Tomato reported in October 2026 that existing facilities running at high utilization constrain delivery volumes. The same report said slower sales growth could reduce the cash inflow from working capital.

Cash tied up in inventory was also flagged as a near-term burden. If mass-production stabilization is delayed, the pace of sales growth could slow as well.

Chinese engine capacity and customer concentration

SK Securities noted in an October 2026 report that concerns exist over Chinese engine capacity expansion. Supply contracts for marine engines with the affiliate Hanwha Ocean were disclosed at KRW 139.3 billion in June 2026 and KRW 128.9 billion in August 2026, so dependence on a single customer remains a variable.

There is also a geopolitical factor, as Shinhan Investment Corp. said in an April 2026 report that the Iran situation could bring short-term volatility.

Risk factors

Cost structure and outsourcing

JobPost noted that the mix of eco-friendly dual-fuel engine orders and raw material and outsourcing costs determine profitability. SK Securities analyzed that about KRW 5 billion in extra costs arises each quarter. If the cost structure does not improve as expected, the pace of margin gains could slow.

Chinese competition and capacity

Demand from Chinese shipyards is growing, but expanding Chinese engine capacity is seen as a potential long-term source of competitive pressure. SK Securities judged that in the short term the expansion does not outpace Chinese shipyards' yard expansion and is not a threat. Domestic rival HD-Hyundai Marine Engine is also growing its orders, which intensifies competition for share.

Currency and geopolitics

In an April 2026 report, Meritz Securities said Hanwha Engine may have lowered the hedge ratio on existing orders and that the exchange rate could be a source of earnings improvement. Shinhan Investment Corp. said in the same period that the Iran situation is expected to cause short-term volatility.

If the direction of the exchange rate or geopolitical conditions shifts, quarterly earnings could swing more widely.

12

Overall view

Hanwha Engine's 2026 shows order growth and earnings volatility appearing together. On a disclosed basis, annual sales reached KRW 1.37 trillion in 2025 after three consecutive years of growth, and operating profit grew for two consecutive years to KRW 130.1 billion in 2025 after turning profitable in 2023.

Cumulative new orders through the third quarter of 2026 reached KRW 2.80 trillion, above the full-year 2025 total, and the backlog of KRW 5.65 trillion equals 3.6 years of sales.

By contrast, profit attributable to controlling shareholders fell to KRW 11.6 billion in the third quarter of 2026, down 65.0% from the prior quarter, and operating profit and controlling-shareholder profit moved in different directions.

Completing the expansion to 5.33 million horsepower annually and stabilizing mass production are cited as preconditions for faster sales growth, and the timing falls between the end of 2026 and the first half of 2027. The wide range of target prices issued by brokers reflects differing views on 2027 earnings estimates.

Strengths include the backlog, the DF mix and the expansion plan, while weaknesses include the third-quarter profit decline, possible expansion delays, Chinese competition and customer concentration.

The next checkpoints are completion of the expansion at year-end 2026, the 2026 annual business report, and first-quarter 2027 results.
