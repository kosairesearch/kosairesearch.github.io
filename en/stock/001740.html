Report overview

An AI-pivot holding company where core earnings strength is the key question

SK Networks is shifting its center of gravity toward AI investment after selling its car rental unit. Its disclosed revenue has fallen for three straight years, and operating profit has declined for two consecutive years since its 2023 peak, so whether core earnings recover is the next point to watch.

  3. 3

02

Business structure

SK Networks describes itself as an AI-centered business holding company and is reshaping the portfolio it inherited from its trading-house past. In 2024 it sold 100% of its car rental subsidiary for KRW 820 billion, removing that business from the portfolio.

Its information and communications unit handles handset distribution centered on SK Telecom, which gives the group a relatively stable base. Handset profit swings from quarter to quarter depending on when marketing spending is timed around new device launches.

Its home-appliance rental and subscription subsidiary holds a top-two position in Korea's rental market, but domestic rental accounts have declined since 2024. The hotel business accounts for roughly 5% of the portfolio. The trading business shrank from 15% of revenue in 2023 to 9% in the first nine months of 2025.

A digital marketing and commerce company became a subsidiary in January 2026. The company has invested KRW 142 billion cumulatively in the AI firm Upstage since 2024, and financial investment houses value that stake at about KRW 230 billion.

Restructuring has reduced businesses that were closely linked to the group's core affiliates, which some analysts view as weakening the portfolio. In rental, the company competes with players such as Coway and LOTTE rental.

04

Earnings analysis

Annual revenue declined from KRW 9.67 trillion in 2022 to KRW 9.13 trillion in 2023, KRW 7.66 trillion in 2024, and KRW 6.75 trillion in 2025.

The car rental sale and the shrinking trading business are cited as the main drivers, and one credit rating report judged that overall earnings power weakened after the 2024 SK Rent-a-Car sale.

Operating profit rose 53.9% to KRW 154.2 billion in 2022 to KRW 237.3 billion in 2023, the peak, then fell to KRW 113.9 billion in 2024 and KRW 86.3 billion in 2025. The operating margin dropped from 2.6% in 2023 to 1.5% in 2024 and 1.3% in 2025.

Attributable net income swung from a loss of KRW 711 million in 2023 back to profit in 2024 at KRW 47.8 billion and then KRW 49.8 billion in 2025, a recovery in direction.

Quarterly operating profit fell to KRW 4.44 billion in the fourth quarter of 2025 before rebounding to KRW 33.4 billion in the first quarter of 2026, helped by growth in subscription accounts at the appliance rental subsidiary and timing of marketing spend at the telecom unit.

It then dropped to KRW 24.8 billion in the second quarter and KRW 20.1 billion in the third, marking two straight quarterly declines. The third quarter also absorbed one-off costs tied to a career transition program.

Per the preliminary results announced on November 6, 2026, the telecom unit's operating profit rose by KRW 21.0 billion year on year to KRW 35.9 billion, and the hotel unit's rose 57% to KRW 9.1 billion, suggesting other units offset those gains.

Over the last four quarters, attributable net income totaled KRW 96.7 billion, exceeding operating profit over the same period, which points to a meaningful contribution from non-operating gains such as AI-related investment valuations.

05

Industry analysis

The company's earnings have long relied on group-affiliated demand and domestic consumer markets. In handsets, the timing of new device launches and marketing spend drives quarterly results.

In rental, Hana Securities analysts noted that SK Intellix's earnings have entered a declining trend because of intensifying domestic competition, and that cumulative accounts are shrinking. In hospitality, rising domestic and overseas tourist traffic lifted room and food-and-beverage sales at the Walkerhill hotel.

In AI infrastructure, corporate demand for AI adoption increased server and storage sales at its IT-services subsidiary. Divestitures and exits have shrunk the top line, from KRW 11.0 trillion in 2021 to KRW 5.1 trillion in cumulative consolidated revenue through September 2025.

A person familiar with the industry said that moving the business from rental toward AI will require substantial resources to hit near-term revenue targets.

Against peers, the rental business competes with Coway and LOTTE rental, while the telecom unit is regarded as having a stable market position through its SK Telecom channel. As group-affiliated transactions have declined, the business has become less reliant on affiliate volume than before.

06

Outlook

The company says it will keep integrating AI into its operations and building a portfolio aligned with future directions. The telecom unit plans to manage marketing strategy by watching handset market trends and strengthen logistics capability.

The marketing subsidiary is focusing on developing new AI marketing solutions on top of steady growth in advertising and commerce. The data-solutions subsidiary is pursuing new solutions to support corporate AI transformation and was designated an OpenAI partner in the third quarter.

Hana Securities expects the telecom and hotel units to remain solid in the fourth quarter, while judging that a recovery at SK Intellix will be difficult for now.

A Shinhan Investment analyst expects investment results from Upstage to take shape through its follow-on fundraising and the selection process for the government's independent AI foundation model project.

Skepticism remains, however, because large conglomerates entered AI late and many see meaningful results as hard to achieve. With subsidiary consolidation and stake sales under way, how the portfolio changes flow through to earnings matters.

The key checkpoint is whether the operating margin in the fourth quarter of 2026 and the 2026 annual report move away from the 1.3% recorded in 2025.

Earnings moved from an attributable net loss in 2023 to profit in both 2024 and 2025, and attributable net income over the last four quarters totaled KRW 96.7 billion. 08

Bull factors

Balance sheet repair

Reports say borrowing eased after the 2024 car rental stake sale, and the debt-to-equity ratio fell from 322.6% in 2023 to 148.9% at the end of 2025. Attributable equity stood at KRW 2.02 trillion and total liabilities at KRW 3.02 trillion at the end of 2025. Operating cash flow was KRW 222.3 billion in 2025, still well above the KRW 127.5 billion recorded in 2023.

Telecom, hotel and marketing units as earnings support

In the third quarter of 2026, the telecom unit's operating profit rose by KRW 21.0 billion year on year to KRW 35.9 billion, per preliminary results. The hotel unit's operating profit rose 57% to KRW 9.1 billion, and the marketing unit's rose about 50% on new game advertising demand. The bull case rests on these three units cushioning a quarter in which group operating profit declined.

Value of AI investment holdings

Financial investment houses estimate the value of the Upstage stake at about KRW 230 billion. The company has invested KRW 142 billion cumulatively since 2024, and valuation gains from AI-related funds were reflected in first-quarter 2026 results.

Upstage's placement among the top firms in the government's independent AI foundation model project is cited as a sign of technical credibility.

09

Bear factors

Thinner core operating margin

The operating margin on disclosed results was 1.3% in 2025, about half the 2.6% recorded in 2023. Operating profit declined in both the second and third quarters of 2026, and one-off costs weighed on the third quarter.

Domestic rental accounts at SK Intellix have fallen since 2024, and brokerages cite intensifying competition behind the decline.

Shrinking revenue base and portfolio

A credit rating analysis notes consolidated revenue fell from KRW 11.0 trillion in 2021 to KRW 5.1 trillion cumulatively through September 2025. The analysis expects earnings power to stay below the past level for some time, since the higher-profit car rental business has exited. Shrinking trading operations and fewer group-affiliated transactions also reduce the revenue base.

Uncertain returns from AI investment

Coverage in 2024 noted that many observers doubted the company could achieve meaningful results in AI. Its starting point is later than large conglomerates that entered the field late too.

If valuation gains drive quarterly net income, earnings swing independently of the core business, and the third quarter of 2026 turned to an attributable net loss of KRW 3.59 billion.

10

Risk factors

Execution risk in restructuring

With the car rental unit sold and trading shrinking, the business base is narrower, and the AI pivot may not translate into earnings as quickly as hoped. Overlapping subsidiary consolidation and stake sales mean reorganization costs may recur in quarterly results. Industry observers expect heavy resource commitments to meet near-term revenue targets.

Volatility from AI valuation gains and losses

If the valuation of stakes such as Upstage shifts with outside fundraising or market conditions, pre-tax and net income could swing sharply. Attributable net income exceeding operating profit over the last four quarters also signals heavy reliance on non-operating items. If valuation gains fade, quarterly results could turn to losses.

Competition and demand shifts in core units

SK Intellix faces intensifying domestic competition, and its cumulative accounts are declining, while new initiatives need time to show up in results. The telecom unit's quarterly profit swings with handset sales and marketing spend. If weakness in both units coincides, gains elsewhere may not be enough to offset it.

What to watch next

  1. Early February 2027 (based on the 2025 timing)

    Preliminary fourth-quarter 2026 results. The low base of KRW 4.44 billion in fourth-quarter 2025 operating profit makes it important to see whether the third-quarter one-off costs recur.

  2. Late March 2027 (annual report filing deadline)

    Filing of the 2026 annual report. The key items are how far the operating margin moves from 1.3% in 2025, whether the 148.9% debt-to-equity ratio holds, and how much of attributable net income came from valuation gains.

  3. Around May 2027 (first-quarter results timing)

    First-quarter 2027 results. Timing of handset marketing spend at the telecom unit and the trend in SK Intellix subscription accounts are the variables that will shape quarterly profit direction.

  4. Timing not fixed (tied to project stages)

    Final selection in the government's independent AI foundation model project and Upstage's follow-on fundraising. Selection outcomes could change the valuation of the stake and are a major driver of valuation gains and losses.

12

Overall view

SK Networks has reshaped its balance sheet through the car rental sale and lower borrowing, and the debt-to-equity ratio fell from 322.6% in 2023 to 148.9% at the end of 2025. However, disclosed operating profit has declined for two years since the 2023 peak, and the operating margin was 1.3% in 2025.

Operating profit recovered to KRW 33.4 billion in the first quarter of 2026 but fell in the second and third quarters, and the third quarter also absorbed one-off AX transition costs that produced an attributable net loss.

The telecom, hotel and marketing units are holding up results, while the decline in SK Intellix's domestic accounts remains a drag. AI investments add earnings volatility through valuation gains on stakes such as Upstage.

The price-to-book ratio below 1x reads as a discount to net assets, but how that discount is interpreted depends on whether earnings recovery is sustained.

The next checkpoints are the preliminary fourth-quarter 2026 results and the 2026 annual report, with the repeatability of operating margin and attributable net income as the central questions.
