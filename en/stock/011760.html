Report overview

Revenue passes KRW 7 trillion, margins stay near 2%

Hyundai's revenue rose for a third straight year to KRW 7.55 trillion in 2025, but its operating margin held at 1.9%, so whether sales growth lifts margins is the key point to track.

02

Business structure

Hyundai is a general trading company founded in 1976, organized into six business segments: steel, passenger vehicle parts, petrochemicals, commercial energy, machinery and shipbuilding, and other businesses such as resource development.

Based on the 2022 business report, petrochemicals accounted for 36.5% of revenue, steel 34.4%, passenger vehicle parts 18.5%, machinery and shipbuilding 5.2%, commercial energy 2.7% and other businesses 2.7%.

In the first quarter of 2025, trading businesses in petrochemicals, steel and passenger vehicles generated more than 80% of total revenue, according to press reports.

Its main customers are fixed-contract buyers within the Hyundai group, including Hyundai Motor and KIA, from which it sources products for export overseas. It also maintains fixed trading relationships with other large steel and energy groups.

The company describes a network of about 40 overseas branches and subsidiaries supporting trade and distribution across vehicles, smart mobility, energy, machinery and shipbuilding, petrochemicals and steel.

In the second quarter of 2026, growth came from higher passenger vehicle shipments to CIS and other key regions and from broader mobility lines including commercial, military and rail vehicles. Transformer exports to North America, expanded petrochemical activity and EDCF-linked project business also contributed.

The company says it is diversifying products and regions to reduce reliance on any single item or area. New businesses include solar panel recycling and a planned stake acquisition in an auto parts maker. The segment shares above come from the 2022 business report.

04

Earnings analysis

On a reported basis, revenue rose from KRW 6.13 trillion in 2022 to KRW 6.58 trillion in 2023 (up 7.4%), KRW 7 trillion in 2024 (up 6.3%) and KRW 7.55 trillion in 2025 (up 8.0%), growing for three straight years from 2023 to 2025.

Operating profit rose from KRW 66.8 billion in 2022 to KRW 99.3 billion in 2023 (up 48.6%), KRW 133.5 billion in 2024 (up 34.4%) and KRW 140.1 billion in 2025 (up 4.9%), also rising three years in a row, but the 2025 increase slowed sharply.

The operating margin rose from 1.1% in 2022 to 1.5% in 2023 and 1.9% in 2024, then held at 1.9% in 2025. In 2025 revenue grew 8.0% while operating profit grew only 4.9%, leaving little room for margin gains.

Net profit attributable to controlling shareholders went from KRW 78.7 billion in 2022 to KRW 83.7 billion in 2023 and KRW 121.1 billion in 2024, then fell 28.3% to KRW 86.8 billion in 2025. Net profit including non-controlling interests was KRW 86.8 billion in 2025.

At end-2025, total equity was KRW 694.8 billion and total liabilities KRW 1.63 trillion, and the debt-to-equity ratio rose to 234.6% from 214.7% in 2024. Quarterly revenue was KRW 2.07 trillion in Q1 2026 (up 9.7% from the prior quarter), KRW 2.82 trillion in Q2 (up 35.9%) and KRW 2.7 trillion in Q3 (down 4.2%).

Operating profit was KRW 46.1 billion in Q1 2026 (up 38.7%), KRW 62.9 billion in Q2 (up 36.6%) and KRW 50.4 billion in Q3 (down 20.0%).

Yonhap News reported that Q2 operating profit was the highest quarterly figure on record, and the company cited stronger transformer exports to North America and higher petrochemical earnings on rising oil prices as drivers.

Net profit attributable to controlling shareholders was KRW 11.1 billion in Q1 2026 (down 58.6%), KRW 40.2 billion in Q2 (up 263.4%) and KRW 15.9 billion in Q3 (down 60.4%), showing wide quarterly swings. Over the last four quarters, from Q4 2025 to Q3 2026, that profit totaled KRW 94 billion.

Operating cash flow went from KRW 386.5 billion in 2022 to KRW -69.9 billion in 2023, KRW 40.5 billion in 2024 and KRW -294.7 billion in 2025.

05

Industry analysis

Petrochemicals are a core pillar of the business. Wisereport's company monitor describes reconstruction demand after the Ukraine and Russia conflict, rising demand in emerging markets and the adoption of eco-friendly fuels as opportunities for the segment.

In the second quarter of 2026, the company itself cited higher oil prices as a factor that lifted petrochemical earnings. Steel moved in the opposite direction, with the company saying the segment was somewhat weaker in the quarter because of global trade restrictions.

Hana Securities said in an early-2026 report that steel volumes and margins fell under tariff effects, but that export diversification was expected to support earnings. Passenger vehicles are being driven by higher shipment volumes to CIS and other key regions, a contrast with steel.

Transformers sit within the commercial energy segment, where North American export demand helped second-quarter results. Domestic general trading peers that are usually compared include POSCO International, Samsung C&T and LX International.

The fixed reliance on Hyundai group buyers ties performance to the volume flows of those affiliates. On a comparison basis, the operating margin of about 2% makes the pace of margin improvement the central benchmark against peers.

Bull factors

Quarterly revenue above KRW 2 trillion through 2026

Quarterly revenue stayed above KRW 2 trillion in each of the first three quarters of 2026, and second-quarter revenue rose 35.9% from the prior quarter. Cumulative revenue for January to September reached KRW 7.59 trillion, up 33.94% year on year, based on the third-quarter preliminary release.

Cumulative operating profit rose 49.04% to KRW 159.4 billion over the same period, so sales growth carried into profit gains.

Diversification drives broad-based growth

Second-quarter 2026 results were attributed to steady growth across mobility, transformers, petrochemicals and project businesses. The company said the outcome reflects reduced dependence on specific products or regions.

Yonhap News reported that second-quarter operating profit of KRW 62.9 billion was the highest quarterly figure on record.

Profit-focused restructuring and new businesses

The company says it will keep improving its structure around profitability. Solar panel recycling expansion and a planned stake acquisition in an auto parts maker were reported as new businesses.

Bear factors

Operating margin still near 2%

The operating margin rose from 1.1% in 2022 to 1.9% in 2025 but was flat at 1.9% in both 2024 and 2025. Operating profit in Q3 2026 was KRW 50.4 billion, down 20.0% from the prior quarter. Whether profit grows in step with revenue remains the open question.

Volatility in profit attributable to controlling shareholders

Net profit attributable to controlling shareholders fell 28.3% to KRW 86.8 billion in 2025 from KRW 121.1 billion in 2024. Quarterly results swung widely in 2026, with KRW 11.1 billion in Q1 and KRW 15.9 billion in Q3. Q3 2026 profit attributable to controlling shareholders was down 23.4% year on year.

Weaker cash flow and steel trade exposure

Operating cash flow turned to KRW -294.7 billion in 2025 from KRW 40.5 billion in 2024. The company said its steel segment was somewhat weak in Q2 2026 because of global trade restrictions. The debt-to-equity ratio was 234.6% at end-2025, up from 214.7% a year earlier.

Risk factors

Trade restrictions and tariffs

The steel segment is exposed to global trade restrictions and tariffs. Hana Securities noted in an early-2026 report that tariffs reduced steel volumes and margins. Export diversification is cited as a defense, but broader regulation across regions could increase earnings swings.

Oil prices and petrochemical demand

Petrochemical earnings expanded in the second quarter of 2026 with oil prices cited as a driver. A decline in oil prices could reduce that segment's contribution. Reconstruction and emerging-market demand are cited as opportunities, but demand cycles remain a variable.

Major shareholder stake and free float

Hyundai Corporation Holdings, a major shareholder, held 23.95% as of a June 1, 2026 disclosure. A high insider stake can limit the volume of shares traded in the market. Reliance on fixed-contract buyers within the Hyundai group links results to those affiliates' volume flows.

Overall view

Hyundai's 2025 revenue of KRW 7.55 trillion rose for a third straight year, but its operating margin of 1.9% is thin for a revenue base of that size.

Quarterly revenue topped KRW 2 trillion in each quarter of 2026, and Yonhap News reported that Q2 operating profit of KRW 62.9 billion was the highest quarterly figure on record.

Net profit attributable to controlling shareholders fell 28.3% to KRW 86.8 billion in 2025, and the last four quarters totaled KRW 94 billion. Strengths include steady growth across mobility, transformers, petrochemicals and projects, along with diversification by product and region.

Weaknesses include steel trade restrictions, dependence on oil prices and operating cash flow turning negative in 2025. The valuation multiples sit below the industry average, which may reflect both the thin margin and earnings volatility.

The high stake held by a major shareholder also limits the free float and is worth watching. The next checkpoints are whether the operating margin rises above 1.9% in the 2026 annual business report and in the Q1 2027 preliminary results, and whether quarterly profit swings persist.
