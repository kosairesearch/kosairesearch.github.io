Overseas Reach Expands as Upfront IP Spending Weighs

SAMG Entertainment is widening overseas retail channels for its owned IP and kept quarterly operating profit positive through the third quarter of 2026, while cumulative operating profit has fallen on new-IP investment.

  3. 3

02

Business structure

SAMG Entertainment builds its business on owned intellectual property, including Catch! Teenieping, The Teenieping, Mini Special Squad, Metal Card Bot and Wish Cat, spanning characters, licensing, products, digital content and retail spaces.

The company points to its production capability, which it describes as one of the largest 3D animation operations in Korea, as the starting point of the business. Its value chain extends from production into broadcast rights, toys, merchandise, distribution, licensing, platforms and global distribution.

According to a securities firm's report, product sales made up 74% of revenue in the third quarter of 2025, while licensing rose to 19.7% from 11.8% in 2023. In the third quarter of 2026, the company said product sales grew 137% and licensing sales grew 81% year on year, reflecting the theatrical film.

Overseas, Teenieping goods are being rolled out in about 5,600 stores of MINISO and SANFU in China, and the company entered about 2,000 stores of the U.S. retailer Target. Cumulative China sales reached about KRW 41.3 billion through the third quarter of 2026 and led overseas growth.

The customer base is also widening: as of the first quarter of 2026, buyers in their 30s and 40s accounted for 68.3% of Metal Card Bot product purchases on the company's own mall and 82.6% on Naver Smart Store.

The company holds exclusive domestic distribution rights for Mattel's KPop Demon Hunters toys and also handles distribution of a Transformers toy line.

Among kids and family IP peers, The Pinkfong Company and Aurora World are the closest comparisons, and the company stresses that it ties production, distribution and licensing into one structure.

04

Earnings analysis

Annual revenue rose from KRW 68.3 billion in 2022 to KRW 95.1 billion in 2023 (up 39.2% year on year), KRW 116.4 billion in 2024 (up 22.4%) and KRW 141.3 billion in 2025 (up 21.3%), marking three consecutive years of growth from 2023 through 2025. 2025 was the largest year in the 2022 to 2025 period, and the company announced it as its record annual revenue since founding.

Operating loss of KRW 364 million in 2022 widened to KRW 9.42 billion in 2023, then narrowed to KRW 6.11 billion in 2024. Operating profit of KRW 22.6 billion in 2025 marked a turn to profit, with operating margin moving from -0.5% in 2022, -9.9% in 2023 and -5.2% in 2024 to 16.0% in 2025.

The company attributed the KRW 28.7 billion year-on-year improvement in 2025 operating profit to in-house distribution and more efficient production management, and said the cost ratio on product and licensing sales fell by more than 10 percentage points.

Net loss attributable to controlling shareholders of KRW 23 billion in 2022, KRW 17.2 billion in 2023 and KRW 19.4 billion in 2024 gave way to a net profit of KRW 30.1 billion in 2025.

Net income including non-controlling interests was KRW 30.1 billion in 2025, matching the controlling-shareholder figure, while in 2023 it was a net loss of KRW 17.1 billion, slightly different from the controlling-shareholder loss.

Operating cash flow moved from -KRW 1.93 billion in 2022 and -KRW 11.9 billion in 2023 to KRW 32.8 billion in 2024 and KRW 23.8 billion in 2025. Quarterly, the fourth quarter of 2025 was the peak, with revenue of KRW 48.4 billion and operating profit of KRW 11.7 billion.

Operating profit then fell to KRW 3.88 billion in the first quarter of 2026, KRW 2.07 billion in the second and KRW 1.15 billion in the third, while third-quarter revenue rose to KRW 46 billion.

Controlling-shareholder net profit of KRW 4.55 billion in the first quarter of 2026 exceeded that quarter's operating profit of KRW 3.88 billion, and the composition of non-operating items is a point to verify in the periodic report notes.

The company attributed the year-to-date decline in operating profit in 2026 to investment in new IP production, character development and business category expansion.

05

Industry analysis

In its July 2026 corporate identity announcement, the company said it reorganized its business to match a shift in kids and family IP from video content toward products, retail and spatial experiences.

Within that shift, SAMG Entertainment said it would move away from expanding its business only after content succeeds and instead build consumer touchpoints first through products, retail and fan experiences.

Licensing is viewed as a high-margin revenue source, and a securities firm's 2025 report said a rising share of licensing and overseas revenue would accelerate a structure for better profitability.

The target age range is also shifting, from kids aged 4 to 7 toward teens and Gen MZ fans, and the company said about 65% of new online mall sign-ups for The Teenieping's adult-oriented brand in the first quarter of 2026 were in their 20s and 30s.

China remains the central overseas market for its IP, and the company said cumulative Metal Card Bot unit sales in China exceeded 11.8 million.

Among competitors, The Pinkfong Company and Aurora World are the direct comparisons in kids IP, while entertainment companies that build collaborative structures with music-based IP owners such as SM Entertainment are also relevant reference points.

In July 2026 SAMG Entertainment agreed with SM Entertainment on a joint production of a global family animation with a total production budget of KRW 10 billion. Given the nature of this business, the performance of individual IP hits tends to affect company results more than broader industry movements.

06

Outlook

In the fourth quarter, Catch! Teenieping Season 7 is scheduled for release on November 18, 2026, and a launch of Mini Special Squad Season 8 is being pursued. According to a report by The Bell, the company expects its new Teenieping season and the year-end peak season to push annual revenue to a new record.

In China, the company is negotiating with local distributors to target a first-quarter 2027 release of the second Teenieping feature film. In Japan, the first Teenieping feature film is set for release in March 2027 through a distribution deal with a Japanese partner.

Metal Card Bot is expanding in China and Russia, and the company presents this as grounds for branding it as a K-mechanic IP. Two new IPs are being prepared for launches in or after 2026 and 2027.

The Superhero Idol Project, co-produced with SM Entertainment, is set to reveal its main details in 2027 before a global OTT release. The CEO said the second half of 2026 should bring visible results both domestically and overseas as the theatrical release, season launches and country-by-country expansion coincide.

Analysis reported in the press, however, identifies the box office performance of the film as a key variable for second-half results. In the quarter when the first film opened, third-quarter sales rose 54.7% year on year, so how the second film's release is reflected after the fourth quarter is the central question.

The next points to confirm are the reception of Season 7, whether the first-quarter 2027 China release is finalized, the March 2027 Japan release, and the operating margin in the 2026 annual report.

08

Bull factors

Wider overseas retail channels

Teenieping goods are being rolled out in about 5,600 MINISO and SANFU stores in China, and the company has entered about 2,000 Target stores in the U.S. The company said cumulative overseas sales passed KRW 50 billion in the first three quarters of 2026, up 93.5% year on year.

China sales of about KRW 41.3 billion led that growth, and the company describes this retail base as the foundation for expansion into North America.

Normalized balance sheet

The debt-to-equity ratio fell from 216.3% at the end of 2024 to 62.6% at the end of 2025, and the company announced it reached 43.8% at the end of the third quarter of 2026. The company fully retired its convertible bonds in the second quarter, and borrowed debt stands at about 5.8% of equity. The company frames this as the financial capacity to fund new IP and global expansion.

Product and licensing growth with lower cost ratio

In the third quarter of 2026, the company said product sales rose 137% and licensing sales rose 81% year on year. For 2025, it said the cost ratio on product and licensing sales fell by more than 10 percentage points.

It also said fourth-quarter 2025 operating margin was about 24%, which it presented as evidence of the profitability typical of IP businesses.

09

Bear factors

Three straight quarters of lower operating profit

Operating profit fell across three consecutive quarters, to KRW 3.88 billion in the first quarter of 2026, KRW 2.07 billion in the second and KRW 1.15 billion in the third.

Cumulative operating profit through the third quarter of 2026 declined from the prior-year period, which the company attributes to investment in new IP production, character development and category expansion. The fact that profit has declined even as revenue grew is a bearish point.

Hit-driven results and quarterly volatility

Third-quarter results reflect the effect of the theatrical release. After the fourth-quarter 2025 operating profit peak, controlling-shareholder net profit swung from a KRW 370 million loss in the second quarter of 2026 to KRW 204 million profit in the third.

Controlling-shareholder net profit over the last four quarters totals only KRW 4.28 billion, so quarterly earnings can move sharply if an IP release underperforms.

China dependence and upfront investment

Of cumulative overseas sales through the third quarter of 2026, China accounted for the largest share at about KRW 41.3 billion. A first-quarter 2027 release of the second China feature film is still under negotiation with distributors.

The KRW 10 billion animation co-produced with SM Entertainment involves in-kind investment covering 90% of production costs, so a delay in overseas results could extend the investment burden.

10

Risk factors

Dependence on hits

Revenue depends heavily on the box office of theatrical films and new season launches. In the quarter when the first film opened, sales rose 54.7% year on year, so weak reception could produce a similar reverse effect.

Season 7 and Mini Special Squad Season 8 launches are being pursued at the same time, which concentrates production and marketing costs.

Concentration in overseas channels

Overseas sales rely heavily on China and on retail partners such as MINISO and SANFU. If the distribution environment or partner policies change in a specific country, the pace of overseas growth could be affected.

The Target listing in North America is at an early stage, so its contribution to sales relative to store count will need to be confirmed in later results.

Investment burden and capital allocation

After normalizing its capital structure, the company has put the financial capacity it secured back into new IP and category expansion. That process has been accompanied by a stretch of declining operating profit.

If the launches of two new IPs or the co-production project slip or underperform, the recovery of investment could be delayed.

12

Overall view

SAMG Entertainment turned to a full-year operating profit of KRW 22.6 billion in 2025, and through the third quarter of 2026 its product and licensing sales and overseas distribution have grown together.

However, operating profit has declined for three consecutive quarters since the first quarter of 2026, and cumulative operating profit has fallen from the prior year on new-IP investment.

The balance sheet improved, with the debt-to-equity ratio falling from 62.6% at the end of 2025 to 43.8% at the end of the third quarter of 2026. Bullish factors include wider retail distribution in China and the U.S., easing financial burden and a lower cost ratio.

Bearish factors include hit-driven volatility, the trend of declining operating profit, dependence on China and the burden of upfront investment. Controlling-shareholder net profit over the last four quarters totals KRW 4.28 billion, so earnings-based metrics are sensitive to quarterly swings.

The next checkpoints are performance after Season 7 launches on November 18, 2026, the Japanese theatrical release in March 2027, and the operating margin and controlling-shareholder net profit in the 2026 annual report. This report does not present an investment opinion, rating or target price.
