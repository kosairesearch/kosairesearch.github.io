02

Business structure

NKGen Biotech Korea was established in 2002 to manufacture research reagents and conduct R&D, and it listed on KOSDAQ on October 23, 2015. The business spans immunodiagnostic kits, NK cell immunotherapy development, and health functional food sales.

Based on the revenue mix shown on a securities information page, the NK Vue KIT accounts for the largest share at 47.60%, followed by research reagents at 33.82%, NK365 and related products at 11.60%, research services at 6.55%, and other services at 0.41%.

The NK Vue KIT is reported to be used at more than 2,400 medical institutions, and its sales declined after the product moved to non-covered treatment in 2024. Overseas, Thailand's MEDEZE Group, a listed cell therapy company, holds a network of more than 260 hospital partners.

MEDEZE previously imported the company's test kits for its own use and now plans to extend testing to partner hospitals and clinics. The health functional food Slim by 365 sold about 200,000 initial capsules within roughly six months of launch, and a second batch has entered production.

On the therapy side, the company develops immune cell therapies on its SuperNK platform, targeting cancer and Alzheimer's disease; SNK01 has FDA Fast Track designation, and SNK02 has IND clearance for solid tumors including gastric cancer.

A December 2025 report said immune cell therapies still need considerable time before commercialization, so near-term results depend mainly on diagnostics, reagents, and health food sales.

The largest shareholder, NKGen Biotech, Inc., holds 64.14%, so decisions at the US parent also bear on the direction of the Korean company.

04

Earnings analysis

Annual revenue fell from KRW 11.3 billion in 2022 to KRW 9.95 billion in 2023 (down 11.7% year on year), KRW 8.8 billion in 2024 (down 11.5%), and KRW 7.86 billion in 2025 (down 10.7%).

Revenue has declined for three consecutive years from 2023 to 2025, with 2022 the largest year and 2025 the smallest in the 2022–2025 period.

The operating loss widened from KRW 49.7 billion in 2022 to KRW 67.3 billion in 2023, then narrowed to KRW 4.06 billion in 2024 and KRW 2.53 billion in 2025, marking four consecutive years of losses. The operating margin moved from -440.9% in 2022 to -676.8% in 2023, then improved to -46.2% in 2024 and -32.2% in 2025.

The net loss attributable to controlling shareholders was KRW 53.5 billion in 2022, KRW 153.4 billion in 2023, KRW 36.9 billion in 2024, and KRW 7.34 billion in 2025. The net loss including non-controlling interests was KRW 55.6 billion in 2022 and matched the controlling-shareholder figure from 2023 onward.

At the end of 2025, total equity was KRW 7.1 billion, equity attributable to controlling shareholders was KRW 7.1 billion, and total liabilities were KRW 7.2 billion, giving a debt ratio of 101.5%. The negative debt ratios shown for 2023 and 2024 reflect negative total equity in those years.

Operating cash flow was -KRW 38.1 billion in 2022, -KRW 35.1 billion in 2023, -KRW 1.26 billion in 2024, and -KRW 3.2 billion in 2025. Quarterly revenue bottomed at KRW 1.57 billion in Q1 2026, down 28.8% from the prior quarter, and then rose to KRW 2.14 billion in Q2 and KRW 2.34 billion in Q3, a two-quarter increase.

Operating profit of KRW 43.79 million in Q3 2025 turned into an operating loss of KRW 1.35 billion in Q4 2025, then a KRW 186 million operating profit in Q2 2026, and KRW 48.66 million in Q3 2026, down 73.8% from the prior quarter.

The net loss attributable to controlling shareholders was KRW 316 million in Q3 2026, and the last four quarters total a net loss attributable to controlling shareholders of KRW 1.85 billion.

Quarters with positive operating profit still showed controlling-shareholder net losses, which suggests non-operating items are driving quarterly results; the breakdown should be checked in the periodic report notes.

05

Industry analysis

The company handles both NK cell-based immunotherapy and NK cell activity diagnostic kits, so the therapy market and the diagnostics market should be considered separately.

Industry classifications differ by source: one lists the company under KOSDAQ pharmaceuticals, another under life science tools and services, and Alphasquare compares it within the medical devices and supplies group of 97 companies, so industry comparisons depend on the benchmark chosen.

In that Alphasquare comparison, the operating margin is higher than the industry average but ranks 68th among the 97 companies. On the diagnostic side, the NK immune test measures NK cell activity from a blood sample, and the company plans to use it to track immune status before and after cell therapy.

On overseas demand, company officials said cell therapy is growing actively in Thailand and that NK activity testing could become an important monitoring tool there. The partner MEDEZE Group holds a Thai cell bank standard certification and is the only private company in Thailand's ATMP sandbox.

Immunotherapy commercialization still requires clinical and regulatory steps, so the pace of competing trials shapes the company's position.

The diagnostic kits serve two demand pools, domestic hospital checkup centers and overseas hospitals, and which of these demand pools grows will matter more for results than the broader cycle.

06

Outlook

The company's most visible growth lever is expanding overseas test supply. MEDEZE Group said it plans to expand test adoption to partner hospitals and clinics in Thailand that use its NK cell therapy program.

However, the scale of the expansion will depend on whether individual medical institutions adopt the test, so the size is hard to gauge yet. For health functional foods, the company is moving beyond online-mall sales toward more distribution channels after its first batch sold out.

On the therapy side, the FDA Fast Track designation for SNK01 may speed development, but designation alone is not approval, so the commercialization timing cannot be pinned down until follow-up clinical data are available.

On the financing side, the largest shareholder signed a share-pledged loan agreement on April 22, 2026, covering about 46.4 million shares with a loan amount of roughly KRW 39 billion, and this structure links the parent's funding position to governance at the Korean company.

The valuation date for the parent's forward purchase agreement falls on December 31, 2026. Official figures for the 2026 full year will come with the 2026 annual report, and the next checkpoints are whether quarterly profit holds and whether revenue growth extends beyond the two-quarter rise into a sustained recovery.

Two straight quarters of revenue growth after a trough

Revenue bottomed at KRW 1.57 billion in Q1 2026 and rose to KRW 2.14 billion in Q2 and KRW 2.34 billion in Q3. Q3 revenue was up 6.0% year on year. If the trend holds in Q4, it could signal an end to the annual revenue decline.

Pipeline and overseas distribution expansion

SNK01 has FDA Fast Track designation and SNK02 has IND clearance. The MEDEZE network of more than 260 hospitals in Thailand could widen test supply. The sell-out of the first health food batch shows a consumer channel beyond the diagnostics business.

Thin equity and renewed quarterly losses

Parent-level financial strain and a low free float

Risk factors

Controlling shareholder and governance

The 64.14% stake and the pledged loan structure mean the parent's funding position could affect decisions and share liquidity at the Korean company. A low free float can also make trading more sensitive to price moves. Parent-level contract dates should be tracked alongside company filings.

Timing of therapy commercialization

Assessments say immune cell therapies still need considerable time before commercialization. Fast Track designation and IND clearance mark stages of clinical development, not approval. Results below expectations could significantly change how the pipeline is valued.

Earnings volatility and audit history

Quarterly operating results have alternated between profit and loss, and profitable quarters still showed controlling-shareholder net losses. The 2023 audit report carried a disclaimer of opinion from the external auditor, which led to a delisting decision and an injunction request. Follow-up outcomes of that history should be checked through company filings.
