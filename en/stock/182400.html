02

Business structure

NKGen Biotech Korea was founded in 2002 to manufacture research reagents, became NKMAX after a 2019 merger with the former Atgen, and changed its name again to its current form in 2025.

The business spans four areas: immune cell therapeutics built on the SuperNK natural killer cell platform with the SNK01, SNK02 and SNK03 pipeline candidates; immune diagnostics through the NK Vue Kit, which measures NK cell activity; research reagents; and health supplements under the NK365 brand.

The NK Vue Kit is used at more than 2,400 medical institutions, though revenue declined after a 2024 shift to non-reimbursed status, and the company offset part of the impact through cost reduction.

In the supplement business, the 'Slim by 365 Probiotics and Diet' product launched around April 2026 sold out its initial production run of about 200,000 capsules within roughly six months and moved into a second production batch. The controlling shareholder is NKGen Biotech, Inc.

(OTC: NKGN), headquartered in Santa Ana, California, which took management control through a 2025 share acquisition.

NKGen Biotech, Inc. is a clinical-stage biotech developing both autologous and allogeneic NK cell therapeutics, while the Korean entity functions largely as the subsidiary carrying commercialized revenue sources such as diagnostics, reagents and supplements.

Product sales of recombinant proteins and monoclonal antibodies are understood to account for a major share of revenue.

The NK cell therapy field globally has no commercialized product yet, and while NK cell approaches are drawing attention as a potential alternative to CAR-T in solid tumors, competition remains intense.

Compared with large domestic listed biopharmaceutical companies, the company's revenue scale remains that of a small-cap biotech.

04

Earnings analysis

Annual revenue fell from KRW 11.3 billion in 2022 to KRW 9.95 billion in 2023 (down 11.7% year on year), KRW 8.8 billion in 2024 (down 11.5%), and KRW 7.86 billion in 2025 (down 10.7%), marking three consecutive years of decline from 2023 through 2025.

Over the same period, the operating loss widened from KRW 49.7 billion in 2022 to KRW 67.3 billion in 2023, then narrowed for two straight years to KRW 4.06 billion in 2024 and KRW 2.53 billion in 2025.

The controlling-interest net loss likewise widened from KRW 53.5 billion in 2022 to KRW 153.4 billion in 2023 before narrowing to KRW 36.9 billion in 2024 and KRW 7.34 billion in 2025, remaining in the red for four consecutive years from 2022 through 2025.

The operating margin improved from negative 440.9% in 2022 and negative 676.8% in 2023 to negative 46.2% in 2024 and negative 32.2% in 2025.

The debt-to-equity ratio fell from 103.9% in 2022 to negative 683.1% in 2023 and negative 191.5% in 2024 amid capital impairment, before returning to 101.5% in 2025 following a capital reduction and rights offering.

On a quarterly basis, the operating loss of KRW 700 million in the second quarter of 2025 swung to an operating profit of KRW 43.79 million in the third quarter, then reverted to a KRW 1.35 billion operating loss in the fourth quarter, before narrowing to a KRW 1.21 billion loss in the first quarter of 2026 and swinging to a KRW 186 million operating profit in the second quarter, up both quarter on quarter and year on year.

Controlling-interest net profit followed a similarly volatile path, turning to a KRW 216 million profit in the third quarter of 2025, reverting to a KRW 783 million loss in the fourth quarter, widening to a KRW 926 million loss in the first quarter of 2026, and then swinging to a KRW 171 million profit in the second quarter.

The cumulative controlling-interest net loss over the last four quarters (third quarter 2025 through second quarter 2026) stood at KRW 1.32 billion, indicating that the multi-year narrowing of annual losses has not yet translated into a consistently profitable quarterly run rate.

05

Industry analysis

NK cell therapy is drawing attention as a potential complement to CAR-T therapy, which faces criticism over high cost and limited efficacy outside blood cancers, but no NK cell product has achieved commercialization anywhere in the world.

Hundreds of related clinical trials are reportedly underway globally, making the field highly competitive.

NKGen Biotech Korea's immune diagnostics business, centered on the NK Vue Kit that measures NK cell activity, has secured more than 2,400 medical institution customers, though its revenue base narrowed after a 2024 shift to non-reimbursed status.

The health supplement business is in an early stage of establishing itself as a new revenue source under the common theme of immune management, with its overall contribution to results still appearing limited.

The parent, NKGen Biotech, Inc., is developing autologous and allogeneic NK cell therapies for cancer and neurodegenerative disorders such as Alzheimer's and Parkinson's disease, respectively, and in September 2026 the World Health Organization approved 'troculeucel' as the international nonproprietary name for SNK01.

Unlike large domestic listed biopharmaceutical companies with revenue bases built around biosimilars or novel drug candidates, this company has a structure in which commercialized revenue sources—diagnostics, reagents and supplements—are separate from the cell-therapy clinical growth driver.

In terms of industry cycle, cell therapy remains at an early commercialization stage, so revenue contribution continues to depend on existing businesses such as diagnostics and supplements.

Bull factors

Signs of a Quarterly Earnings Turnaround

Both operating profit and controlling-interest net profit turned positive in the third quarter of 2025 and the second quarter of 2026. On an annual basis, the operating loss narrowed from KRW 49.7 billion in 2022 to KRW 2.53 billion in 2025, continuing a multi-year trend of shrinking losses. Quarter-to-quarter volatility remains high, but the overall direction has moved toward loss reduction.

Expansion of the New Health Supplement Business

Slim by 365 under the NK365 brand sold out its initial production run of about 200,000 capsules within roughly six months and moved into a second production batch. The company has stated plans to diversify distribution channels beyond its online-centered sales model.

This can be seen as an attempt to secure a new revenue source as legacy diagnostics and reagent revenue has declined.

Progress in the Parent's Clinical Pipeline

The parent, NKGen Biotech, Inc., is developing SNK01 for neurodegenerative disorders such as Alzheimer's and Parkinson's disease as well as oncology indications. In September 2026, the World Health Organization approved 'troculeucel' as the international nonproprietary name for SNK01, adding a development milestone.

Whether such clinical progress translates directly into revenue for the Korean entity requires further confirmation.

Three Consecutive Years of Annual Revenue Decline

Revenue fell for three consecutive years from KRW 11.3 billion in 2022 to KRW 7.86 billion in 2025. The 2024 shift of the NK Vue Kit to non-reimbursed status has been cited as a key factor shrinking the core revenue base. How much the new health supplement business can offset this remains to be validated.

Highly Volatile Balance Sheet Structure

The debt-to-equity ratio fell from 103.9% in 2022 to negative 683.1% in 2023 and negative 191.5% in 2024 amid capital impairment, before returning to 101.5% in 2025 following a capital reduction and rights offering.

Repeated capital reductions and rights offerings during this process diluted existing shareholders' equity value. Operating cash flow also remained negative at KRW 3.2 billion in 2025.

Prolonged Trading Halt and Listing Eligibility Uncertainty

Trading was halted after a 2023 audit-opinion-related delisting trigger, and even after a 2025 capital reduction and the conclusion of court rehabilitation in August-September, reports as of spring 2026 still confirmed the halted status. Whether and when trading will resume remains uncertain. This is a direct liquidity constraint for investors.

Risk factors

Listing Eligibility Risk

A past delisting trigger related to the audit opinion led to a prolonged trading halt. The formal audit-opinion-related reason was disclosed as resolved in August 2025, but the trading halt is understood to have continued afterward. The timing of any resumption of trading may depend on the outcome of further exchange review.

Governance and Parent-Company Linkage Risk

The controlling shareholder, NKGen Biotech, Inc., disclosed in April 2026 that it had entered into a stock pledge loan agreement covering part of its holdings. Changes in the parent's financial condition or management could affect the stability of the Korean subsidiary's governance structure. Continued monitoring of shareholding changes is warranted.

Clinical and Commercialization Uncertainty

The NK cell therapy field globally has no product that has achieved commercialization yet. Clinical development is led mainly by the parent company, making the timing of any direct revenue contribution to the Korean entity uncertain.

Profitability defense in existing commercialized businesses such as diagnostics and supplements is likely to remain the central variable for results in the near term.
