02

Business structure

Green Plus runs two businesses: the design and construction of smart-farm greenhouses, and the production, processing and sale of aluminum products. In 2023, revenue mix was 44% smart farm, 52% aluminum and 3% other, according to a 2024 securities research note.

The company said its smart-farm segment grew 41% and subsidiary revenue rose 20.5% in 2025. The smart-farm unit markets a one-stop model covering design, materials and construction.

The same 2024 note called this setup unique in Korea and cited a domestic smart-farm share above 20%, along with a construction ranking first for 13 consecutive years. The aluminum unit depends on demand from automotive lightweighting, renewable energy, defense and power-plant projects.

In 2024 it supplied roughly 500 tons of aluminum materials for water and storage tanks in Saudi Arabia. EV component sales were partly halted from September to December 2025 because of tariff issues.

Overseas, the Australian subsidiary established in January 2023 serves government, corporate and university clients, and Middle East expansion is under way. Because the competitive ranking cited above dates from 2024, it may not reflect the current competitive landscape.

04

Earnings analysis

Annual revenue on a reported basis fell from KRW 83 billion in 2022 to KRW 58.9 billion in 2023, then rose to KRW 87.7 billion in 2024 and KRW 105.7 billion in 2025, with growth in both 2024 and 2025 after the 2023 low. Revenue in 2025 was up 20.6% year on year, and the company described it as a record high.

The same year brought an operating loss of KRW 952 million, down from an operating profit of KRW 2.49 billion in 2024, and a temporary loss at the aluminum unit was cited as a factor.

The company said wider greenhouse orders at home and abroad, higher aluminum selling prices and new products lifted sales, while higher raw material costs cut operating profit. Fair-value losses on convertible bonds and a loss from the disposal of biological assets at an associate pushed the net loss deeper.

The company also booked an impairment of about KRW 16 billion at a consolidated subsidiary as part of a balance sheet clean-up. As a result, the net loss attributable to controlling shareholders was KRW 11.4 billion in 2025 and the total net loss was KRW 13 billion, with the gap reflecting non-controlling interests.

A 2024 securities research note had guided to KRW 115 billion in revenue and a 9% operating margin for that year, while actual 2024 revenue came in at KRW 87.7 billion.

On a quarterly basis, the fourth quarter of 2025 posted an operating loss of KRW 1.37 billion and a controlling-shareholder net loss of KRW 9.27 billion, followed by operating profit of KRW 429 million in the first quarter of 2026 and KRW 949 million in the second.

Third-quarter 2026 revenue was KRW 35.4 billion, up 27.2% year on year, but the quarter returned to an operating loss of KRW 85.62 million.

The controlling-shareholder net loss over the last four quarters totaled KRW 8.9 billion, the debt ratio rose from 74.5% in 2022 to 158.6% in 2025, and operating cash flow was -KRW 2.05 billion in 2025.

05

Industry analysis

Demand for smart farms is shifting toward data-driven environmental control, driven by a shrinking farm population, aging, and greater production volatility from climate change. Growing inflows of young farmers and upgrades to facility horticulture are pulling smart-farm investment higher.

The government is pushing a smart-agriculture expansion policy and the build-out of smart-farm innovation valleys, which the company cites as a driver of its greenhouse construction orders. Globally, demand for advanced greenhouses is widening on food-security and productivity grounds.

A 2024 research note put the company's construction record at more than 290,000 pyeong in Korea and more than 650,000 pyeong in Japan. Because smart-farm orders depend heavily on public and local-government projects, the flow of policy budgets is a variable for order intake.

The aluminum unit, according to the company, entered a normalization phase from the first quarter of 2026, supported by defense and power-plant demand and North American autonomous-vehicle programs.

Compared with large construction groups in the sector, its portfolio combines greenhouse construction with aluminum materials, so its business mix differs from most peers.

06

Outlook

Orders in 2026 opened in March with a youth smart farm in Boryeong with a contract value of KRW 5.5 billion. In May the company won two youth smart-farm projects in Sangju and Nonsan, worth KRW 3.1 billion and KRW 2.6 billion, with construction running from November 2026 to the end of May 2027.

In June it announced a KRW 6.2 billion strawberry smart farm covering 2.5 hectares in Yesan, Chungnam. In July it was selected as the lead member of a joint venture building smart-farm greenhouses for a rural settlement project in Hapcheon, Gyeongnam.

The company said it expects further orders from public smart-farm projects in the second half.

In the aluminum unit, the mass production of a flagship model at a global automaker is scheduled for the second half, and the company expects related revenue to rise from about KRW 5.7 billion in 2025 to more than KRW 9 billion in 2026.

Overseas, it is expanding into Australia and the Middle East, where a 2024 supply project in Saudi Arabia is cited as a reference. On governance, a change-of-control share purchase agreement was signed on October 26, 2026, with the transfer scheduled for December 8, 2026.

Combining the share purchase price with the participation in a third-party allotment rights offering gives a total of KRW 14.96 billion. The filing notes that the schedule and terms may change depending on negotiations and procedures.

In September 2026 the company acquired KRW 1 billion of its fourth convertible bond at the bondholders' early redemption request, using internal funds.

08

Bull factors

A Run of 2026 Contract Announcements

Following a March 2026 youth smart-farm award in Boryeong worth KRW 5.5 billion, the company announced two May projects worth KRW 5.7 billion, a KRW 6.2 billion June strawberry farm and a July greenhouse job in Hapcheon. One compiled dataset puts first-quarter 2026 revenue up 43.0% year on year. Greenhouse construction orders and higher aluminum sales are cited as the drivers.

Signs of Normalization in Aluminum

The company said the aluminum unit entered normalization from the first quarter of 2026 and is pursuing North American autonomous-vehicle clients and European customers. It expects related revenue to exceed KRW 9 billion in 2026 as a domestic flagship model enters mass production in the second half. That figure is the company's own forecast.

Quarterly Profit Turn and Wider Operating Profit

Operating profit of KRW 429 million in the first quarter of 2026 turned a prior-quarter loss into a profit. In the second quarter, operating profit reached KRW 949 million and controlling-shareholder net profit reached KRW 697 million, with operating profit up 121.4% from the prior quarter.

Revenue of KRW 38 billion in that quarter was the highest among the last five quarters, showing sales and profit moving together.

Bear factors

Thin Earnings Base

The 2025 operating loss of KRW 952 million and total net loss of KRW 13 billion turned the year into a loss, with an operating margin of -0.9%. The third quarter of 2026 returned to an operating loss of KRW 85.62 million, so profit did not hold for consecutive quarters. In 2023 the operating margin had fallen to -13.9%, showing how sharply results have swung before.

Rising Financial Leverage

The debt ratio climbed from 74.5% in 2022 to 158.6% in 2025, with total liabilities of KRW 59.4 billion against total equity of KRW 37.4 billion at year-end 2025. Operating cash flow was -KRW 2.05 billion in 2025.

Negative non-controlling interests leave total equity below the controlling-shareholder portion, which highlights the financial burden.

Cost Pressure and One-Off Charges

Aluminum raw material prices rose about 31% in 2025, and tariff issues halted part of the EV component business. An impairment of about KRW 16 billion at a consolidated subsidiary weighed heavily on annual results.

Actual 2024 revenue of KRW 87.7 billion fell short of the KRW 115 billion guidance cited by a securities note, which keeps execution in question.

10

Risk factors

Dependence on Policy and Public Orders

Smart-farm orders depend heavily on government policy and public or local-government projects. The company itself said it expects further orders from the public smart-farm market in the second half. If policy budgets shrink or tenders slip, the timing of construction revenue could be pushed back.

Raw Materials and Tariffs

Aluminum price increases and EV tariff issues weighed on 2025 results at the same time. If automotive component sales do not grow as the company expects in 2026, the benefit from normalizing the aluminum unit would shrink. How much cost can be passed on to customers is the central variable for margins.

Governance and Capital Raising

The change of the largest shareholder is scheduled for December 8, 2026, and the filing notes that terms may change with negotiations and procedures. New share subscriptions and convertible bond purchases are reshaping the capital structure. With debt burden higher at the end of 2025, access to funding will shape financial stability.

Overall view

Green Plus grew revenue to KRW 105.7 billion in 2025 but recorded an operating loss and a net loss, while 2026 brought a run of contract announcements and higher sales.

Operating results turned to a loss again in the third quarter of 2026, and the controlling-shareholder net loss over the last four quarters totals KRW 8.9 billion. The debt ratio rose to 158.6% in 2025, adding financial strain.

On the bull side, the normalization of the aluminum unit and smart-farm orders could lift the earnings base, while on the bear side, raw material costs, tariffs and one-off impairments could recur.

Changes to the capital structure, including the change of control, convertible bonds and rights offerings, should be tracked around December 8, 2026. The next scheduled checkpoint is whether the full-year 2026 operating margin turns positive in the annual report.
