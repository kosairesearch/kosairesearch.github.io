02

Business structure

Cheryong Industrial was established in 2011 through a spin-off and manufactures electrical equipment for power transmission, distribution and underground cables, along with telecommunications equipment and railway materials.

The business is organized into metal products and synthetic resin products, and both segments are produced in small batches of many varieties to customer order.

Major customers are Korea Electric Power Corporation and other domestic public institutions, along with private-sector buyers, so sales are shaped by the timing of public-sector orders. In its 2025 filing, the company attributed higher sales to growth in HVDC insulators and wire fittings.

Explanations of 2026 results also centered on growth in the metal products segment. Metal product orders for HVDC 500kV fittings were described as stable, and quality and delivery responsiveness were reported to have improved customer satisfaction.

Because its products are fittings and materials rather than large equipment, its overlap with large electrical equipment makers such as LS ELECTRIC, Hyosung Heavy Industries and HD HYUNDAI ELECTRIC appears limited.

Given the metal focus, swings in steel and other metal input prices feed directly into costs, and 2026 commentary noted that sharp raw material price increases tied to the Middle East situation coincided with strong sales growth. The synthetic resin segment receives comparatively little attention in results commentary.

04

Earnings analysis

On the DART periodic-report basis, annual sales went from KRW 59.3 billion in 2022 to KRW 40.9 billion in 2023 (down 31.0% year on year) and KRW 29.3 billion in 2024 (down 28.3%), before rising to KRW 37.9 billion in 2025 (up 29.3%). Among 2022 to 2025, the largest sales year was 2022 and the smallest was 2024.

Operating profit fell from KRW 9.61 billion in 2022 to KRW 5.56 billion in 2023 (down 42.1%) and KRW 4.09 billion in 2024 (down 26.5%), then rose to KRW 10.6 billion in 2025 (up 158.6%). 2025 was the largest year for operating profit in the period.

The operating margin moved from 16.2% in 2022 and 13.6% in 2023 to 13.9% in 2024 and 27.9% in 2025. In 2025 operating profit grew far faster than sales, so margin improvement drove most of the gain.

Net profit attributable to controlling shareholders was KRW 9.13 billion in 2022, KRW 7.29 billion in 2023 (down 20.1%), KRW 6.24 billion in 2024 (down 14.4%) and KRW 11.5 billion in 2025 (up 84.1%). Net profit including non-controlling interests was reported at the same amounts in each year.

Operating cash flow was KRW 8.37 billion in 2022, KRW 5.58 billion in 2023, KRW 7.63 billion in 2024 and KRW 13 billion in 2025, the highest of the four years.

Quarterly sales went from KRW 8.43 billion in Q3 2025 to KRW 12.1 billion in Q4 2025, KRW 13.9 billion in Q1 2026 and KRW 16.3 billion in Q2 2026, then fell to KRW 10.2 billion in Q3 2026.

Operating profit followed a similar path, from KRW 1.48 billion to KRW 3.4 billion, KRW 4.84 billion, KRW 5.84 billion and then KRW 2.66 billion. Q3 2026 sales were down 37.4% quarter on quarter but up 21.0% year on year, and operating profit was down 54.4% quarter on quarter but up 79.5% year on year.

Net profit attributable to controlling shareholders peaked at KRW 9.44 billion in Q2 2026, the highest of the last five quarters, and then came to KRW 1.28 billion in Q3 2026 (down 86.5% quarter on quarter and down 34.6% year on year).

Because the drop in that net profit line was larger than the drop in operating profit, the quarter's non-operating items and tax effects need to be read alongside operating results. Over the last four quarters, net profit attributable to controlling shareholders totaled KRW 19.7 billion.

External compilations put first-half 2026 standalone sales up 73.8%, operating profit up 87.5% and net profit up 152.6% year on year.

Industry analysis

Demand from the sector is driven mainly by grid replacement and expansion. In the United States, roughly 70% of large transformers had passed a 25-year service life as of 2020, and in November 2022 the federal government announced a grid investment plan totaling USD 10.5 billion.

In Korea, next-generation grid investment is expanding under energy transition policy, and public-institution-centered power markets are cited as a stable order base. Rising HVDC (high-voltage direct current) orders appear linked to wider investment in long-distance transmission networks.

The spread of AI data centers is also cited in the industry as adding demand for power equipment. In cycle terms, the path from the 2024 low through the 2025 profit recovery and the improvement in the first half of 2026 looks like a recovery phase.

Among sector peers, LS ELECTRIC, Hyosung Heavy Industries and HD HYUNDAI ELECTRIC focus on large equipment, while Cheryong Industrial focuses on fittings and materials, so direct comparisons are limited by product scope.

Metal input prices are a common cost variable for the industry, and 2026 results commentary cited sharp increases tied to the Middle East situation. The company's market share within its end markets is not reported in the sources used here.

Outlook

The direction reflected in company commentary and external compilations is an expansion of HVDC orders, improved profitability from cost reduction and productivity gains, and stable metal product orders.

First-half 2026 standalone sales rose 73.8% year on year, operating profit rose 87.5% and net profit attributable to controlling shareholders rose 152.6%. However, third-quarter 2026 sales fell 37.4% quarter on quarter, so the first-half trend did not carry through at the quarterly level.

On the order side, demand for HVDC 500kV fittings appears to be the key variable. AI data centers and the replacement of aging power equipment are cited as supporting the market outlook.

The items to check are whether the third-quarter sales decline reflects temporary delivery timing and whether the order backlog converts into sales in the following quarters.

On an annual basis, the next checkpoint is how the operating margin and net profit attributable to controlling shareholders in the 2026 annual report compare with 2025 (operating margin of 27.9% and net profit attributable to controlling shareholders of KRW 11.5 billion).

Fourth-quarter 2026 results are confirmed only with the annual filing. Metal input prices and the timing of public-sector orders remain variables for results.

Operating profit declined from KRW 9.61 billion in 2022 to KRW 4.09 billion in 2024 before reaching KRW 10.6 billion in 2025. Given the size of these past swings, one or two years of recovery are not enough to establish stability.

Dependence on raw materials and public orders

Because the customer base centers on domestic public institutions, sales move with the timing of orders.

10

Risk factors

Raw material prices

Because metal input costs drive the cost base, a sharp rise in raw material prices can pressure margins. Results commentary for 2026 noted that sales grew strongly even as raw material prices surged after the Middle East situation. How much of the cost increase was passed through to selling prices needs to be checked quarter by quarter.

Concentration of public-sector orders

Major customers are domestic public institutions such as Korea Electric Power Corporation, so results depend on order timing and budget execution. When public orders cluster in particular periods, quarterly sales swing more widely.

Quarterly earnings volatility

Net profit attributable to controlling shareholders moved from KRW 9.44 billion in Q2 2026 to KRW 1.28 billion in Q3 2026, an extreme swing between adjacent quarters. Operating profit and net profit fell by different percentages, so quarterly cost structure and non-operating items need to be read together.

Whether the KRW 19.7 billion total over the last four quarters represents a stable earnings level needs confirmation from further quarterly results.
