02

Business structure

Nsys is a specialist in secondary-battery vision inspection equipment, listed on KOSDAQ in April 2021. Its inspection lineup covers battery manufacturing stages from electrode and assembly to formation. Its key customers include LG Energy Solution and Samsung SDI, among major domestic battery makers.

In pre-listing broker materials, secondary-battery products accounted for 78.3% of projected 2020 revenue. Overseas sales were 41.5% of revenue in the first half of 2022, while raw materials are settled in Korean won domestically.

Since 2024 the company has pursued a shift from inspection tools toward a broader integrated secondary-battery equipment solution business.

An August 2024 Hana Securities report pointed to pouch degassing equipment, copper foil inspection, 46-cell cylindrical inspection, dry-process equipment and all-solid-state battery inspection as new portfolio items.

Its subsidiary, formerly Ra Kyung Engineering, was acquired to expand into battery-process equipment, and Nsys's stake in it stood at 97.23% per an October 2026 media report.

In 2026 Nsys disclosed a vertical mixer equipment contract with Hanwha Aerospace worth KRW 7.51 billion (announced January 16, 2026), a turnkey formation-process line for an Indian prismatic cell maker worth KRW 8.55 billion (announced April 27, 2026), and a Vision inspection equipment supply contract for North American ESS worth KRW 4.87 billion.

A 2024 media report also cited broker views that the customer base is broadening beyond the domestic big three toward overseas customers.

04

Earnings analysis

Annual revenue rose from KRW 42 billion in 2022 to KRW 51.2 billion in 2023 (up 22.1% year on year) and KRW 62.9 billion in 2024 (up 22.8%), then fell to KRW 48.8 billion in 2025 (down 22.4%). Among 2022 to 2025, 2024 was the largest year and 2022 the smallest.

Operating profit dropped from KRW 4.06 billion in 2022 to KRW 1.85 billion in 2023 (down 54.3%) and recovered to KRW 10.5 billion in 2024 (up 463.6%). In 2025 the company posted an operating loss of KRW 6.11 billion, a swing into deficit.

The operating margin was 9.7% in 2022, 3.6% in 2023, 16.6% in 2024 and -12.5% in 2025.

Net income attributable to controlling shareholders was KRW 3.53 billion in 2022, KRW 2.25 billion in 2023 (down 36.4%) and KRW 10.2 billion in 2024 (up 351.6%), before a net loss of KRW 6.76 billion attributable to controlling shareholders in 2025.

Net income including non-controlling interests was KRW 9.98 billion in 2024 and a net loss of KRW 6.84 billion in 2025, a figure distinct from the controlling-shareholder line.

For 2025, the company attributed the revenue decline to battery customers cancelling or delaying capacity expansion amid weaker EV demand, and it cited bad-debt treatment of receivables after one customer applied for court-supervised rehabilitation, plus weak subsidiary results, in a February 2026 disclosure report.

Operating cash flow was KRW 8.94 billion in 2022, negative KRW 395 million in 2023, KRW 12.2 billion in 2024 and KRW 3.71 billion in 2025.

Quarterly, operating losses of KRW 858 million in Q3 2025, KRW 1.83 billion in Q4 2025 and KRW 1.65 billion in Q1 2026 were followed by Q2 2026, when revenue reached KRW 25.5 billion (up 123.4% from the prior quarter) and operating profit reached KRW 2.91 billion.

Q3 2026 revenue was KRW 19.6 billion (up 59.4% year on year) and operating profit was KRW 1.38 billion, down 52.7% from the prior quarter. Net losses attributable to controlling shareholders over the last four quarters total KRW 3.52 billion.

A October 2026 media report also said first-half 2026 consolidated operating cash flow turned to a net outflow of about KRW 7.84 billion.

05

Industry analysis

Nsys's end demand comes from capital spending by domestic and overseas secondary-battery cell and module makers. In 2025 the company said battery customers cancelled or delayed capacity expansions as EV demand weakened.

Inspection-equipment orders tend to cluster or thin out with customers' investment timing, so whether capacity expansion resumes is central to the industry read.

A 2024 broker view called future orders important for equipment makers and described delayed investment by domestic and overseas companies as a somewhat negative factor. In the same discussion, the broker noted that orders were rising relatively as the overseas customer base widened.

A 2026 media report described US and Hungarian battery investment as widening sales channels for domestic equipment makers. Hungary-related investment mentioned there includes prismatic cells for automakers and 46-millimeter cylindrical cells.

Nsys is described as covering inspection from electrode to module and pack processes, giving it a broad process coverage. Market share figures for competitors are not sufficiently confirmed here to draw a firm conclusion. Within the machinery and equipment sector, Nsys's earnings track battery investment cycles fairly directly.

Outlook

Among 2026 disclosed orders, the Hanwha Aerospace vertical mixer equipment contract is worth KRW 7.51 billion, running from January 16, 2026 to December 31, 2027. Under its terms, a 40% advance payment is billed after a performance bond is in place, and goods payments are made in cash within 10 days of month-end.

The Indian formation-process turnkey contract worth KRW 8.55 billion was disclosed on April 27, 2026, with payment milestones of 30% deposit, 20% and 25% interim payments tied to completion and factory acceptance tests, and 25% balance tied to site acceptance.

That contract's disclosed period ends August 30, 2026, so the timing of acceptance and final payment is the next point to confirm. The North American ESS Vision inspection contract worth KRW 4.87 billion was disclosed as a US-based deal.

At a September 2024 media event, the company said it would aim to pass KRW 300 billion in 2026 revenue, while 2025 revenue on a disclosed basis was KRW 48.8 billion. The same report cited dry-coater inspection, copper foil equipment and 46-millimeter cylindrical assembly equipment as new growth items.

Hana Securities expected a full-scale earnings contribution from new 2025 equipment in its August 2024 report. The subsidiary's capital turned positive in the first half of 2026, but the parent still depends on capital reinjections.

The next checkpoints are how fast the order backlog converts into quarterly revenue and where the operating margin goes after Q4 2026.

Bull factors

Quarterly operating profit recovers

Compared with the KRW 1.65 billion operating loss in Q1 2026, the quarterly profit structure has shifted. Q2 2026 revenue of KRW 25.5 billion rose 123.4% from the prior quarter. Whether this continues in subsequent quarters is the checkpoint for the bullish case.

More 2026 order disclosures

In 2026 Nsys disclosed a Hanwha Aerospace vertical mixer contract worth KRW 7.51 billion (announced January 16, 2026), an Indian turnkey formation-process contract worth KRW 8.55 billion (announced April 27, 2026) and a North American ESS inspection contract worth KRW 4.87 billion.

Counterparties extending beyond the domestic big-three battery makers can be read as supporting the bullish case. Whether these orders translate into revenue depends on delivery and acceptance progress.

Past profitability recovery

In 2024 Nsys recorded revenue of KRW 62.9 billion and a 16.6% operating margin, the highest margin among 2022 to 2025. The bullish argument is that a return of customer investment could reopen a path back to margins seen after the 2025 loss. A 2024 broker view said orders were rising as the overseas customer base grew. This reading relies on past results and does not guarantee future profit levels.

Bear factors

Annual loss and continued net losses

The 2025 operating loss of KRW 6.11 billion and net loss of KRW 6.76 billion attributable to controlling shareholders marked an annual swing into deficit. Controlling-shareholder net losses over the last four quarters total KRW 3.52 billion, and Q3 2026 still showed a KRW 1.18 billion net loss.

Q3 2026 was an operating profit quarter, yet the bottom line remained negative, leaving a gap between operating and net results.

Weaker cash flow

Operating cash flow was negative KRW 395 million in 2023 and positive KRW 3.71 billion in 2025. A October 2026 media report said first-half 2026 consolidated operating cash flow turned to a net outflow of about KRW 7.84 billion. The bearish case rests on cash outflows appearing even while operating profit is positive.

Subsidiary financial burden

The subsidiary had negative total equity of KRW 178.71 million at year-end 2025, a state of capital impairment. Nsys reinjected KRW 970 million in 2026, and cumulative impairment losses at the subsidiary were reported at KRW 3.8 billion.

Equity turned positive at KRW 194.37 million in the first half of 2026, but the parent still depends on reinjections.

10

Risk factors

Customer concentration

Key customers are concentrated among a few battery makers, including LG Energy Solution and Samsung SDI, so the investment timing of any single customer heavily influences results. In 2025 receivables from one customer were written off after it applied for court-supervised rehabilitation.

Customer diversification is underway, but the share of overseas orders and collection flows need continued confirmation through disclosures.

Industry and investment cycle

If EV demand weakness leads battery makers to cancel or delay capacity expansion, inspection equipment orders could decline. A 2024 broker view also flagged delayed investment by domestic and overseas companies as a negative.

Conversely, battery investment in the US and Hungary could widen sales channels, so the direction remains mixed.

Subsidiary and parent liquidity

Media reports said continued reinjections into the subsidiary in 2026 reduced the parent's cash capacity. The reports cited a 41% decline in parent cash and a doubling of short-term borrowings in the first half of 2026. If subsidiary results recover slowly, the burden of further support could grow.

Overall view

In 2026, quarterly profitability has been visible, with operating profit of KRW 2.91 billion in Q2 and KRW 1.38 billion in Q3.
