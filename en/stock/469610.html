02

Business structure

Innotech, established in June 2013, designs and manufactures composite reliability environmental test equipment, chambers that expose electronic components to harsh conditions such as temperature, humidity, vibration and vacuum to detect defects and performance degradation.

Its business is divided into display reliability test equipment, semiconductor reliability inspection equipment, and other areas including secondary batteries and MLCC.

The display segment centers on flexible, foldable display inspection equipment, and KB Securities said the company holds a market share of roughly 90% in flexible display reliability test equipment at a major customer.

The semiconductor segment has widened to test chambers for MLCC, SSD and DRAM, and its share of revenue rose from about 0.5% in 2024 to 11.6% in 2025. Customer concentration is high, with about 90% of standalone revenue generated by one domestic display maker referred to as S.

Against competitors that depend on outsourced production, KB Securities noted that Innotech internalizes its production capacity, which it sees as a driver of faster market penetration. Consolidated subsidiaries include two unlisted companies, among them a water-treatment business acquired in June 2022.

The headquarters is in Asan, Chungcheongnam-do, and the company listed on KOSDAQ in November 2025. Revenue tends to move with customers' new line investments, product transitions, tighter quality standards and maintenance demand after delivery.

04

Earnings analysis

On a disclosed basis, 2025 revenue was KRW 75.7 billion, up 23.7% from KRW 61.2 billion in 2024. Operating profit rose from KRW 5.7 billion to KRW 8.64 billion, up 51.4%, and the operating margin improved from 9.3% to 11.4%.

Net profit attributable to controlling shareholders grew from KRW 5.03 billion to KRW 8.19 billion, up 62.8%. Total net profit, which includes non-controlling interests, was KRW 5.44 billion in 2024 and KRW 8.52 billion in 2025, and it should be kept separate from the controlling-shareholder figure.

The balance sheet also strengthened. The debt-to-equity ratio fell from 85.6% to 46.1%, with total equity of KRW 64.4 billion at end-2025, of which KRW 61.8 billion belonged to controlling shareholders, and total liabilities of KRW 29.7 billion. Operating cash flow rose from KRW 6.88 billion to KRW 10.4 billion.

Quarterly results started from an operating loss of KRW 402 million in 4Q 2025 on revenue of KRW 17.6 billion.

In 1Q 2026, revenue reached KRW 19.9 billion, up 13.1% from the prior quarter, and operating profit of KRW 3.05 billion marked a swing back to profit, while net profit attributable to controlling shareholders reached KRW 3.95 billion, up 7,510.9% from the prior quarter.

In 2Q 2026, revenue was KRW 19.6 billion, down 1.5%, and operating profit fell 70.7% to KRW 894 million.

In 3Q 2026, revenue was KRW 19.8 billion, up 1.2%, yet operating profit fell 47.4% to KRW 470 million, and the company posted a net loss attributable to controlling shareholders of KRW 2.02 billion, a swing into loss.

KB Securities attributed the year-on-year margin decline in 1Q 2026 to higher R&D spending and selling and administrative costs from hiring.

Operating profit peaked in 1Q 2026 and then fell for two consecutive quarters, while revenue stayed in a narrow range of KRW 19.6 billion to KRW 19.9 billion, so margin compression is the central issue in the recent results.

05

Industry analysis

Demand for display inspection equipment is tied to the spread of flexible displays. In a June 2026 report, KB Securities said North American company A is expected to unveil its first foldable phone in the second half of the year.

The same report said customer S is understood to have signed a three-year exclusive supply deal with company A for OLED panels for foldable phones. Semiconductor demand is linked to the recovery in the MLCC market.

KB Securities said MLCC conditions at major domestic customers are recovering and semiconductor equipment sales growth is continuing.

Investment commentary frames the period as one where hopes for a recovery in semiconductor and electronic component end markets coincide with the possible expansion of the foldable smartphone market.

On competition, the company's point of difference is that it internalizes production of test chambers for MLCC, SSD and DRAM, rather than relying on outside manufacturing.

Foldable volumes at key customers, however, depend heavily on how well specific new products sell, so the position of the cycle cannot be pinned down with certainty. The timing of customers' equipment spending and line expansions determines how quickly orders reach Innotech.

06

Outlook

The most concrete plan the company has confirmed is the construction of a new plant in Pyeongtaek. Through a June 2, 2026 disclosure, the company said it will build a factory on a new site in the Brain City area of Pyeongtaek, Gyeonggi-do, with about KRW 26.0 billion invested in land, buildings and equipment.

Production capacity, currently at a level of KRW 30.0 billion to KRW 40.0 billion in revenue terms, is short of space because of MLCC test chamber production.

According to KB Securities, most of the proceeds from the IPO are going into this expansion, with KRW 12.0 billion for land and KRW 12.4 billion for factory construction, a total of KRW 24.4 billion. Reports differ on timing.

One report said completion is targeted for March 2027 and that capacity would rise about fourfold, while another said completion in the first half of 2027 would lift total capacity to about KRW 100 billion, roughly threefold. Customer-side factors also matter.

Whether company A's foldable phone launch in the second half of 2026 sells well will shape whether customer S expands its lines, which would open the way for additional equipment orders. In April 2026, a domestic credit rating agency assigned a BBB- rating, upgraded from BB+ the previous year.

Among the company's publicly stated targets, the production capacity plan is the clearest, and how fast it is executed will determine the scale of revenue in the next phase.

08

Bull factors

Revenue mix shifting toward semiconductor equipment

KB Securities said the semiconductor equipment share of revenue rose from 0.5% in 2024 to 11.6% in 2025. The same firm forecast the share would exceed 30% in 2026. A lineup of test chambers for MLCC, SSD and DRAM, backed by in-house production capacity, was cited as the basis for the expansion. The shift reduces reliance on display equipment.

Foldable volumes and a high share at a key customer

KB Securities reported that the company's share of flexible display reliability test equipment at its key customer is about 90%. Customer S is understood to have a three-year exclusive supply deal with company A for foldable-phone OLED panels. Analysts see additional equipment orders possible if foldable sales grow and lines are expanded.

Pyeongtaek plant and capacity expansion

A June 2026 disclosure confirmed a new factory on a site in Pyeongtaek's Brain City. About KRW 26.0 billion will go into land, buildings and equipment, and most of the IPO proceeds are being used. Reports differ, with capacity expected to rise threefold or fourfold on completion. The expansion is intended to relieve space constraints in MLCC test chamber production.

09

Bear factors

Dependence on a single customer

KB Securities said about 90% of standalone revenue comes from one domestic display maker, S. Semiconductor equipment sales are growing, but the single-customer structure behind total revenue has not changed.

If the customer's equipment investment timing slips, deliveries and revenue recognition could be delayed as well.

Quarterly profit slowdown and a swing into loss

Operating profit peaked at KRW 3.05 billion in 1Q 2026 and then fell to KRW 894 million in 2Q and KRW 470 million in 3Q, marking two consecutive quarterly declines. In 3Q 2026 the company posted a net loss attributable to controlling shareholders of KRW 2.02 billion.

Revenue stayed between KRW 19.6 billion and KRW 19.9 billion over the same period, so margin compression remains the key issue.

Expansion spending and uncertainty over timing

Most of the roughly KRW 26.0 billion in IPO proceeds is going into the Pyeongtaek plant, with KRW 12.0 billion for land and KRW 12.4 billion for construction, a total of KRW 24.4 billion, according to reports. The investment burden could show up in results before the plant is operating.

Reports also differ on completion, between March 2027 and the first half of 2027, which leaves the start-up schedule uncertain.

Risk factors

Customer concentration risk

A large share of revenue depends on one customer, so changes in its investment decisions or product strategy flow directly into results. If sales of new foldable products fall short, customer line expansions could be delayed. Progress on diversification should be checked against changes in revenue mix in periodic reports.

Execution risk on the expansion

Most of the IPO proceeds are being used for the Pyeongtaek plant, which will substantially raise capacity once complete. Delays or low utilization after completion could slow the recovery of the investment.

Because reports differ on capacity increase and completion timing, the company's own filings should be the reference.

Margin volatility risk

KB Securities attributed margin pressure to higher R&D spending and selling and administrative costs tied to hiring. When revenue does not move much, rising costs can reduce operating profit quickly. With a controlling-shareholder net loss recorded in 3Q 2026, whether the cost structure stabilizes is a central issue.

12

Overall view

Based on disclosed 2025 results, Innotech posted revenue of KRW 75.7 billion and operating profit of KRW 8.64 billion, with both growing, and its debt-to-equity ratio fell to 46.1%.

The business is broadening from display inspection equipment into semiconductor test chambers, and quarterly revenue held between KRW 19.6 billion and KRW 19.9 billion through 3Q 2026.

However, operating profit over the same period fell from KRW 3.05 billion in 1Q 2026 to KRW 470 million in 3Q, and 3Q produced a net loss attributable to controlling shareholders of KRW 2.02 billion.

The sum of controlling-shareholder net profit over the last four quarters is KRW 4.99 billion, below the KRW 8.19 billion for full-year 2025. Bullish factors are the rising semiconductor equipment share, foldable customer volumes and the capacity expansion at Pyeongtaek.

Bearish factors are single-customer concentration, quarterly margin compression and the burden of expansion spending, and the two sides deserve equal weight. KB Securities presented an 'Not Rated' opinion in its June 2026 report. The next checkpoints are the 2026 annual report and the completion timing of the Pyeongtaek plant.
