02

Business structure

Hana Aero Dynamics was founded on January 12, 2007, and is headquartered in Sacheon, Gyeongsangnam-do, operating in aircraft jigs and tools, component manufacturing and airframe structure assembly.

Its business runs through three stages: jig and tool design for aircraft production, precision component manufacturing, and airframe structure assembly.

The 2022 acquisition of Daeshin Aerospace Industry expanded the structure assembly business, and the company emphasizes high-value processes staffed heavily by engineering personnel. By customer, Korea Aerospace Industries (KAI) accounted for 59.1% of first-half 2026 sales and Bell for 32.1%, a combined 91.2%.

Bell has been a direct export customer since 2023, and since 2024 the company has supplied four fuselage structures for the Bell 412 helicopter, including the tail boom, with Bell 412 related sales at 26.4% of first-half 2026 revenue.

The company says it is widening its customer base to include Israel Aerospace Industries (IAI) and Japan's Subaru, although the scale of those relationships is not disclosed in detail. Of trade receivables of KRW 3.5 billion at the end of the first half of 2026, 75.9% were owed by KAI and Bell. For the offering valuation, Hanwha Aerospace and Shinhwa Precision were selected as comparable companies.

03

Recent trends

The offering process began when the company filed for preliminary listing review with the Korea Exchange KOSDAQ market division on May 29, 2026, and the exchange approved the listing on August 28, 2026. The securities registration statement was submitted to the Financial Supervisory Service on September 21, 2026.

Institutional demand forecasting ran from November 2 to 6, 2026, drawing 2,152 domestic and foreign institutions with a competition ratio of 1,051.38 to 1, and 99% of demand was priced at or above the top of the band.

The offer price was fixed at KRW 5,700, the top of the KRW 4,700 to 5,700 indicative range, on 4.4 million offered shares. Retail subscription ran from November 10 to 11, 2026, with a combined first-day competition ratio of 2.8 to 1.

According to the registration statement, the valuation applied an average EV/EBITDA of 17.89 times from Hanwha Aerospace and one other listed peer, yielding KRW 6,950 per share, and the indicative band implies a 17.99% to 32.37% discount to that value.

Financials show 2025 sales of KRW 33.29 billion, operating profit of KRW 4.99 billion and net income of KRW 2.42 billion, a 15.0% operating margin on a standalone basis.

First-half 2026 sales were KRW 20.59 billion, up 4.9% year on year, with operating profit of KRW 4.83 billion, but a net loss of KRW 118 million due to non-operating expenses. Third-quarter 2026 sales were about KRW 10.1 billion with operating profit of about KRW 500 million.

Net proceeds of KRW 24.81 billion at the top price are to be invested over two years in large sheet-metal forming equipment, heat treatment and post-processing equipment, and factory construction.

04

Outlook

The company plans to direct offering proceeds into production infrastructure through 2027, while pursuing composite material facilities and new business in space and unmanned aerial vehicles.

The KRW 55.15 billion backlog is scheduled for revenue recognition of KRW 9.31 billion in 2026, KRW 23.97 billion in 2027 and KRW 21.87 billion in 2028, so recognized revenue steps up sharply in 2027. That revenue path depends on how closely equipment investment and capacity expansion keep pace.

The registration statement notes that major customers tend to adjust fourth-quarter orders for year-end inventory management, which could reduce second-half profit.

President Son Chang-hwan said 2026 sales had already reached 90% of the internal target and cited new Bell contracts as the basis for expecting higher sales in 2027. On margins, the key question is whether the lower outsourced processing ratio and stabilized mass production can be sustained.

Items to track after listing include the timing of the 2027 equipment start-up, progress in diversifying customers such as IAI and Subaru, and how much new jig and tool customers contribute to revenue.

05

Bull factors

A shift in profitability structure

Operating margin rose from 5.4% in 2023 to 15.0% in 2025 and 23.5% in the first half of 2026. The company attributes this to better profitability in its jig and tool business, stabilized mass production in assembly, and reduced reliance on outsourcing. Outsourced processing costs fell from 31.1% of sales in 2024 to 21.1% in the first half of 2026.

KRW 55 billion in confirmed backlog

Confirmed backlog at the time of the registration statement stood at KRW 55.15 billion, about 1.7 times 2025 sales. The backlog is scheduled for revenue recognition of KRW 9.31 billion in 2026, KRW 23.97 billion in 2027 and KRW 21.87 billion in 2028. The company says the figure includes only volumes with confirmed production plans.

Concentrated institutional demand

The institutional demand forecast drew 2,152 domestic and foreign institutions with a 1,051.38 to 1 competition ratio, and 99% of demand was priced at or above the top of the band.

The KRW 25.1 billion offering is set to fund large sheet-metal forming equipment, heat treatment and post-processing equipment, and factory construction. The equipment plan was disclosed in the same process that drew this demand.

06

Bear factors

Heavy reliance on two customers

KAI accounted for 59.1% of first-half 2026 sales and Bell for 32.1%, bringing the combined share to 91.2%. Production delays on specific models or inventory and order adjustments by these customers could weigh heavily on results.

Bell 412 related sales alone were 26.4% of the total, so changes on a single platform flow directly into revenue.

Uneven quarterly earnings

In 2025, most annual operating profit was earned in the first half, and the fourth quarter posted an operating loss in the KRW 200 million range. Third-quarter 2026 sales of about KRW 10.1 billion were close to the first-half quarterly average, but operating profit fell to about KRW 500 million. The company notes that key customers tend to adjust fourth-quarter orders for year-end inventory management.

Slowing growth and financing-related costs

Sales jumped to KRW 32.31 billion in 2024 before rising only 3.0% to KRW 33.29 billion in 2025. The first-half 2026 net loss of KRW 118 million reflects higher non-operating expenses, including a KRW 3.27 billion loss from convertible bond derivative valuation.

The debt-to-equity ratio fell from 305.2% at year-end 2025 to 102.6% at the end of June 2026, but swings in financing costs can still move net income.

07

Risk factors

Macro

The offer price was derived from the average EV/EBITDA multiple of Hanwha Aerospace and Shinhwa Precision. If the comparable companies' multiples move, the basis for the valuation would shift as well. The registration statement says EBITDA was chosen to reflect the depreciation burden typical of the industry.

Industry

Recognition of the KRW 55.15 billion confirmed backlog is concentrated in 2027, at KRW 23.97 billion. That timing depends on whether the large sheet-metal forming equipment and new factory come online as planned. Delays in capacity expansion would push back the conversion of backlog into revenue.

Company and supply

Valuation of convertible bond derivatives can swing reported profit from quarter to quarter depending on market conditions and contract terms. The short lock-up period for institutional holders has been flagged as a challenge.

Supply pressure could emerge once listed shares begin trading more freely, which will need to be tracked after listing.

08

Overall view

Hana Aero Dynamics combines a clearly improved operating margin of 23.5% in the first half of 2026 with a confirmed backlog of KRW 55.15 billion. Institutional demand of 1,051.38 to 1, with 99% of bids at or above the top of the band, shows strong institutional interest during the offering.

On the other side, 91.2% of first-half 2026 sales came from KAI and Bell, so changes in orders from a single customer carry a large effect on results. Quarterly earnings variability and the first-half 2026 net loss tied to convertible bond derivative valuation are additional factors for assessing earnings stability.

Whether the equipment investment funded by roughly KRW 24.8 billion in net proceeds lines up with the 2027 revenue recognition period is the central variable for post-listing performance. Share price behavior after listing on November 20, 2026 can only be assessed with trading data accumulated from that date.
