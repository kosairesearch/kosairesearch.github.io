Report overview

Vehicle-care sales grow as losses narrow unevenly

Obigo's sales reached KRW 33.5 billion in 2025, up 132.5% year on year, and rose for three straight quarters through Q3 2026, but operating losses have continued for five consecutive quarters since Q3 2025.

02

Business structure

Obigo is a mobility AX (AI transformation) platform company that started with smart-car software.

According to company figures reported in May 2026, the total vehicle-management platform segment generated KRW 7.1 billion in Q1 2026, or 67.85% of revenue, while the smart-car software platform contributed KRW 2.8 billion and the smart-car content service platform KRW 500 million.

Vehicle-management growth comes from a subsidiary acquired in April 2025, which runs a nationwide vehicle-maintenance network and serves B2B customers including car-rental and car-sharing operators. The subsidiary posted a record quarterly revenue of KRW 9 billion in Q3 2025, the company said.

Its AI content series PickSeries comprises PickNow, PickJoy and P!ckle, and the company said in November 2026 that P!ckle has expanded to Hyundai Motor Group, Kia PV5, Renault, Volvo and Polestar models. Royalty revenue is tied to Hyundai Motor Group's overseas vehicle volumes.

The third pillar is the physical AI browser BOLT, which completed a proof-of-concept with a global energy company and is being developed for substation design and monitoring.

On the competitive side, Obigo co-exhibited with ThunderSoft, which is described as holding the top share of China's cockpit software market, at Auto China 2026. Dependence on automaker groups and corporate clients defines the revenue structure.

04

Earnings analysis

On a DART-filed annual basis, Obigo's sales were KRW 12.1 billion in 2022, KRW 15.1 billion in 2023 (up 24.7%), KRW 14.4 billion in 2024 (down 4.3%) and KRW 33.5 billion in 2025 (up 132.5%). 2025 was the largest year and 2022 the smallest in the 2022–2025 period.

Growth in 2025 was led by the vehicle-management platform, while royalty sales from Hyundai Motor Group's North American and European operations fell short of expectations and the commercial launch of the PickJoy in-car game service lifted subscription revenue.

Operating losses were KRW 2.97 billion in 2022, KRW 537 million in 2023, KRW 2.69 billion in 2024 and KRW 3.72 billion in 2025, a loss record for four consecutive years from 2022 to 2025. Operating margins were -24.5%, -3.6%, -18.7% and -11.1% in the same years.

The company attributed the wider 2025 loss to one-off costs from the subsidiary acquisition and organizational normalization.

Controlling-shareholder net results were a KRW 2.61 billion loss in 2022, KRW 399 million profit in 2023, a KRW 1.67 billion loss in 2024 and a KRW 1.94 billion loss in 2025, while net income including non-controlling interests was a KRW 2.99 billion loss in 2025.

Quarterly sales rose from KRW 13 billion in Q3 2025 to KRW 8.63 billion in Q4 2025, a 33.4% drop, then grew to KRW 10.5 billion, KRW 11 billion and KRW 11.9 billion through Q3 2026.

Operating losses widened from KRW 643 million in Q3 2025 and KRW 1.29 billion in Q4 2025 to KRW 1.48 billion in Q1 2026, then narrowed to KRW 1.15 billion in Q2 and KRW 862 million in Q3.

Controlling-shareholder net income was KRW 17.87 million in Q3 2025, a KRW 715 million loss in Q4, a KRW 901 million loss in Q1 2026, KRW 730 million profit in Q2 and a KRW 3.82 billion loss in Q3. The four quarters from Q4 2025 to Q3 2026 total a controlling-shareholder net loss of KRW 4.71 billion.

The company said the Q3 2026 net loss reflected a derivative valuation loss tied to the share price rise, a non-cash accounting item. The debt ratio climbed from 11.9% in 2022, 11.4% in 2023 and 13.2% in 2024 to 41.5% in 2025, when total equity was KRW 35.6 billion.

Operating cash flow was -KRW 1.16 billion in 2022, KRW 1.78 billion in 2023, KRW 595 million in 2024 and -KRW 1.24 billion in 2025.

05

Industry analysis

Obigo's core market is the shift toward software-defined vehicles (SDV). Trade coverage of CES 2026 described SDV as a central theme in the industry.

Automakers require in-vehicle software to run reliably on limited computing resources over long product life cycles, and the company says it has covered browser engines and embedded software platforms from design through operation.

In its H1 2026 announcement, the company said its core new businesses had entered a practical commercialization stage in global markets. New demand is coming from AI content subscriptions and vehicle-management services, widening revenue sources beyond the royalty-driven legacy solutions.

On the competitive side, the partnership with ThunderSoft, which is described as holding the top share of China's cockpit software market, serves as a channel into overseas automakers.

Domestically, the relationship with Hyundai Motor Group anchors royalty revenue, so the group's overseas sales volumes feed directly into results. Compared with other listed IT and software companies that handle vehicle software, Obigo's combination of software and vehicle-care services is its differentiator.

Physical AI is extending into power, defense, shipbuilding, rail and robotics but remains largely at the verification stage. The KRW 15 billion raised is planned for physical AI M&A, so the pace of industry expansion will depend on deal execution and verification results.

06

Outlook

The company has said 2026 will be the first year of building sales expansion and profitability. It announced on August 18, 2026 that H1 2026 cumulative sales reached KRW 21.5 billion, up 80% year on year, which matches the sum of the disclosed Q1 and Q2 figures. Three business plans were presented.

First, the AI vehicle-management service linked to the vehicle-care subsidiary was targeted for commercial launch within 2026, and in November 2026 the company said it had launched an official maintenance program with a domestic automaker, with plans to widen cooperation with automakers and insurers.

Second, PickSeries is in supply discussions with several global automaker groups, and the company said it had signed a supply contract for PickNow with a global company.

Third, BOLT completed a verification project with a global power-infrastructure company and moved into follow-on procedures, and in October 2026 the company said it joined the Korea Internet & Security Agency (KISA) supply-chain security program as the automotive lead.

The KRW 15 billion raised through the convertible bond is planned for physical AI M&A. The company said it will focus in the second half of 2026 on growing the AI care business and commercializing its core AX platform.

Last year's annual results were announced in mid-February 2026, so 2026 annual results are likely around the same time. In the 2026 annual report, whether the operating loss shrinks from KRW 3.72 billion in 2025 is the earnings checkpoint.

08

Bull factors

Three straight quarters of sales growth

Sales bottomed at KRW 8.63 billion in Q4 2025 and then rose to KRW 10.5 billion in Q1 2026, KRW 11 billion in Q2 and KRW 11.9 billion in Q3. The company attributed the growth to vehicle-management platform sales and AI content revenue now being fully reflected. It also cited sales growth outpacing operating cost growth as a factor behind the narrower Q3 loss.

Narrowing losses and a Q2 profit

Operating losses peaked at KRW 1.48 billion in Q1 2026 and then narrowed to KRW 1.15 billion in Q2 and KRW 862 million in Q3. Controlling-shareholder net income turned positive at KRW 730 million in Q2.

The company described H1 2026 cumulative sales of KRW 21.5 billion and the Q2 profit as signs of improving profitability.

Expansion into AI care and physical AI

AI Care, which uses the nationwide maintenance network of the vehicle-care subsidiary, launched an official service linked to a domestic automaker in November 2026. The BOLT physical AI browser completed a verification project with a global energy company and moved into follow-on procedures. The company said PickSeries is in supply discussions with several global automaker groups.

09

Bear factors

Four straight years and five straight quarters of operating losses

Operating losses ran for four consecutive years from 2022 to 2025, and the 2025 operating loss of KRW 3.72 billion was wider than in 2024. Losses have continued for five straight quarters from Q3 2025 to Q3 2026. The company said research spending and upfront investment for new businesses keep losses in place.

Higher leverage and negative operating cash flow

The debt ratio rose from 13.2% in 2024 to 41.5% in 2025. Total liabilities were KRW 14.8 billion at the end of 2025, below total equity of KRW 35.6 billion, but the pace of increase accelerated. Operating cash flow turned from KRW 595 million inflow in 2024 to -KRW 1.24 billion in 2025.

Royalty volatility and revenue concentration

The company said royalty revenue from Hyundai Motor Group's North American and European operations was weaker than expected in 2025. The vehicle-management platform accounted for 67.85% of Q1 2026 revenue, showing high dependence on one segment. The Q3 2026 controlling-shareholder net loss of KRW 3.82 billion reversed the Q2 profit.

10

Risk factors

Convertible bond dilution

The KRW 15 billion convertible bond issued in March 2026 has a conversion price of KRW 4,289. Full conversion would issue 3,497,318 shares, equal to 27.67% of total shares outstanding.

The conversion window runs from April 9, 2027 to March 9, 2029, and the conversion price can be adjusted in a rights offering priced below market, with a floor of KRW 3,432.

Derivative valuation volatility

The company said the Q3 2026 net loss reflected a derivative valuation loss tied to the share price rise. It described this as an accounting loss without actual cash outflow. Because net results can diverge sharply from operating results when the share price moves, the two measures need to be read separately.

Commercialization and M&A execution

The KRW 15 billion raised is planned for physical AI M&A. The company describes overseas automaker supply and BOLT's industrial deployment as still in supply discussion or verification stages.

When new-business revenue exceeds research and upfront investment costs should be confirmed through the operating loss trend in upcoming regular reports.

Overall view

Obigo expanded its top line, with 2025 sales of KRW 33.5 billion, up 132.5%, and sales rising for three straight quarters through Q3 2026.

However, operating losses ran for four consecutive years from 2022 to 2025, the 2025 operating loss widened to KRW 3.72 billion, and losses have continued for five straight quarters since Q3 2025.

The operating margin moved from -3.6% in 2023 to -18.7% in 2024 and -11.1% in 2025, and the Q3 2026 operating loss narrowed from the prior quarter. Controlling-shareholder net income turned positive in Q2 2026 but returned to a KRW 3.82 billion loss in Q3.

On the financial side, the debt ratio rose to 41.5% in 2025 and operating cash flow turned negative, while the KRW 15 billion convertible bond leaves dilution potential.

Vehicle-care, AI content and physical AI now form three expansion lines, but overseas automaker supply and BOLT's industrial deployment remain at the supply-discussion or verification stage.

Strengths and weaknesses coexist, and the next checkpoints are the 2026 preliminary full-year results and the operating loss trend in the 2026 annual report. This content is provided for information only and contains no buy or sell opinion.
