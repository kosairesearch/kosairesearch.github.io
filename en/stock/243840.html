02

Business structure

Shin Heung Energy & Electronics was founded in 1979 and makes secondary-battery parts, including round and prismatic cap assemblies, current interrupt devices (CID), and cans. It runs three domestic sites in Osan, Yangsan and Gijang, plus five overseas subsidiaries in Malaysia, China, Hungary and the United States.

Based on 2025 sales, medium-to-large prismatic cap assemblies made up 75%, followed by small round N-CID products for power tools at 17%. In 1H 2026, prismatic cap assemblies for medium and large cells generated KRW 176.8 billion, or 69.3% of KRW 255.2 billion in consolidated sales.

Its main customer is Samsung SDI, which by one estimate sources 60–70% of its medium and large prismatic cap assemblies and 80–90% of round CIDs; another analysis put its share within that customer above 80%. The company is also pursuing Hyundai Motor's P6 prismatic cells in Europe.

Its Indiana plant investment of about KRW 150 billion is complete, while its Ulsan plant ships all of its ESS cap assemblies to the United States. Measured by share at key customers, it ranks first in small round cells and in medium and large prismatic cells. Main rivals include SANGSIN ENERGY DISPLAY PRECISION CO and several other domestic suppliers.

04

Earnings analysis

Annual revenue rose from KRW 477.8 billion in 2022 to KRW 539.9 billion in 2023, a 13.0% increase that marked the peak year. Revenue then fell 19.8% to KRW 432.8 billion in 2024 and 5.7% to KRW 408.2 billion in 2025, a two-year decline from 2024 through 2025.

Operating profit was largest in 2023 at KRW 43.7 billion, up 40.7%, and smallest in 2025 at KRW 5.93 billion, down 55.0%. The operating margin fell from 8.1% in 2023 to 3.0% in 2024 and 1.5% in 2025.

Net profit attributable to controlling shareholders swung from KRW 22 billion in 2024 to a net loss of KRW 5.81 billion in 2025.

Press analysis attributed the 2025 profit drop to the sale of high-cost inventory combined with delayed recovery in prismatic battery volumes, and the company cut headcount by about 25% over the prior two years. In the same year, the Hungary base's revenue declined as a key customer shipped fewer EVs in Europe.

On a quarterly basis, operating profit has been positive in every quarter since 3Q 2025, at KRW 2.7 billion, KRW 5.38 billion, KRW 1.59 billion, KRW 5.2 billion and KRW 646 million. Revenue peaked at KRW 131.6 billion in 2Q 2026 before falling 15.5% quarter on quarter to KRW 111.1 billion in 3Q 2026.

Net profit attributable to controlling shareholders turned to a net loss of KRW 195 million in 2Q 2026 after a KRW 8.49 billion profit in 1Q, and the loss widened to KRW 44.9 billion in 3Q.

The reasons for the gap between KRW 646 million of operating profit and that net loss, including non-operating items and taxes, are for the next regular report to show. The net loss attributable to controlling shareholders over the last four quarters totals KRW 28.7 billion.

05

Industry analysis

Demand from downstream markets falls into three groups. First, energy storage systems (ESS): Samsung SDI is expanding output centered on ESS batteries, and analysts expect supplier earnings to improve as a result.

Second, data-center backup batteries (BBU): demand growth lifted round CID revenue by 44% year on year in 3Q 2025. Third, electric vehicles: the EV line at the SPE1 plant ran at less than 20% utilization in 2025, and in August 2024 the company flagged that an EV slowdown could weigh on earnings.

Main competitors include SANGSIN ENERGY DISPLAY PRECISION CO and several other domestic suppliers. A rival entered the cap assembly supply for the SPE Indiana plant 1, but analysts expect the company to supply about 80% of total volume there, based on its production history and quality record.

Revenue timing is tied to the customer's U.S. ESS line conversion schedule. The pace of EV recovery and the pace of ESS line conversion are therefore the two key industry variables.

06

Outlook

The confirmed schedule is as follows. Samsung SDI plans to bring online its 24 GWh lithium iron phosphate (LFP) ESS battery lines in sequence starting in 4Q 2026. Analysts expect the company to begin recording LFP-based BESS cap assembly sales from the same period.

The U.S. subsidiary is expected to convert 75% of its capacity to the customer's ESS products by early 2027. A forecast also puts North American subsidiary revenue above KRW 100 billion in 2027.

The Ulsan plant has space for four additional automated lines beyond its existing four and could expand to eight lines in line with orders. Two additional Hungary EV models, one premium European and one domestic-brand, are scheduled to be added in the second half.

Analysts expect the main earnings growth to be concentrated in 4Q 2026. In November 2025, Eugene Investment & Securities projected 2026 operating profit of KRW 24.4 billion, while 1Q to 3Q 2026 operating profit was reported at KRW 1.59 billion, KRW 5.2 billion and KRW 646 million by quarter. Closing that gap is the next point to watch.

08

Bull factors

U.S. ESS line conversion

North American subsidiary revenue rose from KRW 25.4 billion in 2025 to KRW 32.0 billion in 1H 2026. Analysts expect LFP-based ESS cap assembly sales to start from 4Q 2026. Press coverage projected North American revenue above KRW 100 billion in 2027.

Customer share and BBU demand

Samsung SDI is cited as taking 60–70% of medium and large prismatic cap assemblies and 80–90% of round CIDs. Data-center BBU demand lifted round CID revenue 44% year on year in 3Q 2025. Analysts expect round CID growth at the Malaysia subsidiary to follow BBU demand.

Cost restructuring and sustained profitability

The company says it cut headcount by about 25% over two years and raised line yields. Operating profit in 1H 2026 turned to KRW 6.8 billion from a KRW 2.2 billion loss a year earlier. Quarterly operating profit has stayed positive since 3Q 2025.

09

Bear factors

Wider net loss in 3Q 2026

3Q 2026 revenue was KRW 111.1 billion, down 15.5% quarter on quarter, and the net loss attributable to controlling shareholders was KRW 44.9 billion, a swing from a profit a year earlier. Losses have run for two consecutive quarters since 2Q 2026. The four-quarter total is KRW 28.7 billion. The cause of the gap between a KRW 646 million operating profit and this loss is for the regular report.

Two years of decline and thin margins

Revenue fell in both 2024 and 2025, and the operating margin dropped from 8.1% in 2023 to 1.5% in 2025. Total liabilities at end-2025 of KRW 522 billion exceed total equity of KRW 353.9 billion. A balance sheet with more debt than equity can limit room for interest costs and capital spending.

Weak EV line utilization

A drop in European EV shipments by a key customer also reduced revenue at the Hungary base. If EV recovery is slow, the gap before ESS conversion could extend.

10

Risk factors

Customer concentration

Dependence on Samsung SDI is high. Estimates of its share of supply range from 60–70% to above 80%, depending on the source. A change in the customer's volume allocation or ESS order timing could quickly shift the revenue outlook.

Capex and fixed costs

About KRW 150 billion went into the Indiana plant and KRW 80 billion into the Ulsan plant. Low utilization at new plants raises fixed-cost burden, and analysts argue that higher utilization is needed to offset it. Delays in utilization ramp-up would weigh directly on profit.

Raw materials and technology transition

Cap assemblies are made by pressing aluminum sheet. Fluctuations in sheet prices could flow straight into costs. The 4680 round CID was described as in sample testing in August 2024. A delay in mass production of next-generation products would also delay the benefit of this technology shift.

12

Overall view

Shin Heung Energy & Electronics posted 2025 operating profit of KRW 5.93 billion, down 55.0%, the smallest year of 2022–2025. Operating profit was positive in 1Q and 2Q 2026, but the net loss attributable to controlling shareholders widened to KRW 44.9 billion in 3Q, and the four-quarter total is KRW 28.7 billion.

On the business side, the conversion of U.S. ESS cap assembly lines and the rise in North American revenue are cited as the main recovery drivers. Customer concentration, a 147.5% debt ratio at end-2025, and weak EV line utilization remain risk factors.

On valuation, the price-to-book ratio has stayed below 1 since 2025, but the price-to-earnings ratio is not calculated while losses continue. The next points to check are LFP line operation in 4Q 2026, the 2026 preliminary full-year results in early 2027, and the 2026 annual report.
