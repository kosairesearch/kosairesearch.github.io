02

Business structure

Founded in 1970 and listed on KOSDAQ in 1994, Bosung Powertec is a power equipment specialist with production facilities in Chungju, North Chungcheong Province, and Naju, South Jeolla Province.

The business is split into two divisions: the steel structure division, which produces transmission towers, tubular support structures, and steel structures for power plants and substations, and the heavy electrical equipment division, which makes gas switchgear, pole-mounted transformers, and related switching and protection devices.

Transmission towers, the core product, have typically made up about 40-50% of annual revenue, but that share expanded to roughly 70% in 2025 due to the Donghaean-Singapyeong HVDC project.

Major customers include KEPCO, its generation subsidiaries, and domestic and overseas EPC contractors, with the KEPCO supply relationship forming the backbone of the company's growth history.

In exchange sector classification, the company sits in the KOSDAQ metals category (machinery under the WI26 standard), competing domestically with similarly sized power equipment names such as Jeryong Electric and Jeryong Industry.

The company has also reportedly pursued expansion into new energy areas such as solar and into overseas markets including the Philippines and Myanmar.

Annual revenue, which was in the low billions of won in its early years, grew past the 100 billion won mark in the 2010s before settling into a roughly 50-80 billion won range over the past three to four years, then jumped sharply again in 2025.

The largest shareholder group is led by Lim Jae-hwang and two related parties, with an ownership change recorded in December 2025.

04

Earnings analysis

Consolidated revenue in 2025 reached 147.49 billion won, up roughly 91% from 77.06 billion won a year earlier, while operating profit surged 456% to 26.81 billion won from 4.82 billion won.

Owner-attributable net profit also rose sharply to 20.31 billion won from 5.95 billion won, and the operating margin jumped from 6.3% to 18.2%.

This follows a steady profitability recovery from an operating loss in 2022 (-4.49 billion won, margin -8.3%) through 2023 (operating profit of 3.35 billion won, margin 4.3%) and 2024 (4.82 billion won, 6.3%), before a single large contract pushed results to another level in 2025.

On a quarterly basis, revenue and operating profit peaked in the second quarter of 2025 at 55.83 billion won and 13.66 billion won (owner net profit of 11.01 billion won), then gradually moderated to 36.81 billion won and 6.17 billion won in the third quarter and 37.39 billion won and 5.42 billion won in the fourth quarter.

The first quarter of 2026 saw a modest rebound to 40.86 billion won in revenue and 5.95 billion won in operating profit, but the second quarter of 2026 saw revenue drop sharply to 24.39 billion won with operating profit falling to 4.73 billion won.

Notably, the operating margin in that second quarter still held around the high-teens percentage, suggesting profitability itself was relatively well defended even as revenue scale contracted.

On cash flow, operating cash flow in 2025 came in at 43.70 billion won, a marked improvement from negative 10.00 billion won in 2024, while the debt ratio at the end of 2025 stood at 20.9%, somewhat higher than the 5.8-13.9% range seen from 2022 to 2024 but still at a low level overall.

05

Industry analysis

The power equipment industry's key driver is the surge in electricity demand from AI data centers and semiconductor clusters, alongside expanding investment in high-voltage direct current (HVDC) transmission needed to move power generated in one region to the capital area and industrial complexes.

Bosung Powertec has already built up large-scale supply experience on domestic 500kV HVDC overhead transmission lines and has used that track record to participate in follow-on tenders that include load-testing requirements for 500kV HVDC six-bundle tubular towers.

The domestic tower and tubular tower market is heavily dependent on KEPCO's order schedule, a structure in which individual companies' results can swing significantly depending on the timing and scale of contract awards.

The competitive landscape includes similarly sized power equipment small-caps such as Jeryong Electric, Jeryong Industry, and GNC Energy, many of which are known to maintain relatively stable double-digit operating margins.

Demand for replacing aging grid infrastructure and expanding transmission networks to connect renewable energy are also cited as medium-to-long-term growth drivers for the sector as a whole.

However, because large order announcements are a primary catalyst for both share price and earnings momentum in this market, a gap in new orders at any given point can widen the disconnect between industry optimism and actual revenue.

Compared with large global EPC players, Bosung Powertec operates at a different scale and scope, positioning itself more as a component and structure supplier than a direct competitor.

06

Outlook

The company's 2025 business report stated that last year's operating results were heavily dependent on the single Donghaean-Singapyeong HVDC transmission line contract, which ended on February 28, 2026.

Following that, the company attempted to diversify its order book by signing a 17.3 billion won supply contract with Sean ENC in October 2025 for transmission tower materials for the Eumseong natural gas power plant project.

In 2026, KEPCO issued an additional tender for 500kV HVDC tubular towers on the Donghaean-Dongseoul route, an estimated 9.28 billion won bid that was re-advertised as of last May and includes load-testing requirements for 500kV HVDC six-bundle tubular towers, conditions viewed as favorable for a company with existing reference experience.

The next regular disclosure, the third-quarter 2026 report, must be filed by November 16, 2026 under the Capital Markets Act, at which point it should become clear whether the second-quarter revenue gap persists into the third quarter or whether new orders have been reflected.

The company is also known to be continuing efforts to expand cooperation networks with overseas EPC contractors and diversify into new energy business areas beyond KEPCO and its generation subsidiaries.

Still, the fact that no follow-on mega order on the scale of the Donghaean-Singapyeong contract had been disclosed as of the business report filing remains a key variable for future earnings direction.

08

Bull factors

Leading Domestic Transmission Tower Track Record

Bosung Powertec has already secured large-scale supply experience on 500kV HVDC overhead transmission lines, and this real manufacturing and testing track record can serve as a competitive edge in follow-on ultra-high-voltage tenders that include load-testing requirements.

KEPCO continues to issue additional HVDC tubular tower tenders, including on the Donghaean-Dongseoul route, placing a supplier with existing reference experience in a favorable position.

If the trend of expanding transmission investment to meet surging power demand continues, this technical standing could translate into ongoing order opportunities.

Low Leverage and Improved Cash Generation

The debt ratio stood at a low 20.9% at the end of 2025, and operating cash flow that year came in at 43.70 billion won, a clear improvement from the negative figure the previous year.

The company's financial structure has remained comparatively healthy, leaving relatively ample capacity to fund future capital expenditure or execute new orders. The debt ratio staying in the low double digits or below from 2022 through 2024 also supports the case for financial stability.

Positioned for Data Center and Grid Expansion Demand

The surge in power demand from AI data centers and semiconductor clusters requires expanded ultra-high-voltage transmission investment to move electricity from generation regions to the capital area and industrial complexes, a structural driver for demand for transmission towers and tubular towers.

Demand for replacing aging grid infrastructure and expanding transmission networks for renewable energy integration are also cited as medium-to-long-term growth factors for the market as a whole.

The heavy electrical equipment division's gas switchgear and pole-mounted transformers could likewise benefit if new substation demand increases.

Bear factors

Single-Contract Dependence and Order Gap Concerns

The 2025 earnings surge effectively came from a single Donghaean-Singapyeong HVDC contract, which ended on February 28, 2026. The fact that no similarly sized follow-on mega order had been disclosed as of the business report filing adds uncertainty around the direction of 2026 earnings.

The sharp drop in second-quarter 2026 revenue compared with the first quarter can be seen as this concern showing up in the numbers.

Highly Volatile, Project-Based Revenue Structure

Quarterly revenue ranged widely from roughly 55.8 billion won in the second quarter of 2025 to about 24.4 billion won in the second quarter of 2026, reflecting a structure in which results swing significantly depending on construction progress and contract revenue recognition timing.

When a large contract's revenue is concentrated in a particular quarter, a base-effect gap can appear in the following quarter. This volatility makes it difficult for investors to judge a clear trend from quarterly results alone.

Lack of Formal Analyst Coverage and Thematic Flow Dependence

This stock is understood to lack clearly established formal sell-side research coverage, and as a result its share price has tended to move more on thematic expectations tied to nuclear power, data centers, and HVDC than on fundamentals alone.

In the absence of a formal consensus, individual investors may also have a more limited basis for forming judgments.

10

Risk factors

Customer and Order Concentration Risk

A significant portion of revenue depends on KEPCO's order schedule, and a revenue gap can emerge if new order disclosures are delayed after a major contract ends.

The sharp revenue decline in the second quarter of 2026 can be viewed as this risk materializing, leaving securing follow-on orders as a key variable for future earnings.

Raw Material Price Volatility Risk

Fluctuations in the prices of key raw materials such as heavy steel plate used in steel structure manufacturing can directly affect the cost structure. If the timing of a large project award coincides with a period of rising raw material prices, margins could end up lower than expected depending on contract terms.

Policy and Budget Execution Delay Risk

If the timing of KEPCO's investment plans or government policy and budget execution related to grid expansion is delayed, expected follow-on order schedules could be pushed back as well.

As illustrated by the Donghaean-Dongseoul HVDC tubular tower tender having gone through a re-advertisement, public procurement project schedules can shift, which is worth keeping in mind.

Overall view

Bosung Powertec has shown a clear turnaround trajectory from an operating loss in 2022 through a gradual recovery in 2023-2024 to a sharp profit improvement in 2025, but a structural feature is that most of that performance came from a single KEPCO contract for the Donghaean-Singapyeong HVDC transmission line.

After that contract ended in February 2026, second-quarter revenue fell sharply from the first quarter, and no similarly sized follow-on mega order had been disclosed as of the business report filing, leaving both concerns about an earnings gap and hopes for new orders present at the same time.

On the financial side, the low debt ratio and the sharply improved operating cash flow in 2025 stand out as positive factors, while KEPCO's continued expansion of HVDC tubular tower tenders, including on the Donghaean-Dongseoul route, provides a favorable medium-to-long-term industry backdrop.

That said, the relative absence of formal sell-side research coverage alongside a comparatively strong influence of thematic trading flows, and the very high quarter-to-quarter revenue volatility, are factors that also warrant consideration.

The next quarterly report (statutory deadline of November 16, 2026) and the outcome of the Donghaean-Dongseoul route tender are cited as the key events that will shape the earnings direction going forward.

Overall, this stock represents a case where a structural growth theme tied to grid expansion coexists with earnings volatility stemming from dependence on a single customer and a single contract.
