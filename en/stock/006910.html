02

Business structure

Bosung Power Technology makes transmission towers, steel tubular poles, switchgear, transformers and other equipment used in power infrastructure. The company was listed on KOSDAQ in September 1994 and runs a steel structure business and a heavy electrical equipment business.

It produces gas switchgear, pole-mounted transformers, transmission towers and substation steel structures at its Chungju and Naju plants. According to the 2025 business report, domestic tower sales were KRW 106.3 billion, or 74.2% of total sales, while the other product lines together made up roughly a quarter.

In the first quarter of 2026, transmission towers were again the largest revenue source at 77.01% of sales. The effective customer is Korea Electric Power Corporation (KEPCO), so tower demand depends heavily on its orders.

Large projects have repeatedly lifted sales sharply, and sales returned to normal levels once those projects ended. Competition runs through competitive bidding, which makes it hard to pass higher input costs on to customers.

Steel is the main raw material, so margins are exposed to iron ore, scrap, coal prices and exchange rates. The company holds Korea's first KEPIC seismic Grade 1 certification for steel structures and has supplied steel structures to Shin-Gori units 3 and 4. In April 2026 it received a BBB+ credit rating from a domestic rating agency.

04

Earnings analysis

Annual results began with 2022 sales of KRW 54.2 billion and an operating loss of KRW 4.49 billion. In 2023 sales rose 44.8% to KRW 78.5 billion and operating profit of KRW 3.35 billion marked a turn to profit. Sales fell 1.8% to KRW 77.1 billion in 2024, yet operating profit rose 44.1% to KRW 4.82 billion.

Sales jumped 91.4% to KRW 147.5 billion in 2025, the largest year in the 2022 to 2025 period, and operating profit rose 455.7% to KRW 26.8 billion. Operating profit has increased for two consecutive years from the 2023 low, through 2024 and 2025.

Net profit attributable to controlling shareholders moved from a loss of KRW 11.1 billion in 2022 to KRW 4.35 billion in 2023, KRW 5.95 billion in 2024 and KRW 20.3 billion in 2025. The operating margin went from -8.3% in 2022 to 18.2% in 2025.

The company attributed the 2025 increase to higher sales of HVDC steel tower products. However, the 234 billion won KEPCO contract signed on September 30, 2025 was the core of that project, and reports differ on its end date, with February 28, 2026 cited by one source and April 15 by another.

Quarterly sales peaked at KRW 40.9 billion in Q1 2026 with operating profit of KRW 5.95 billion, then fell 40.3% to KRW 24.4 billion in Q2 with operating profit of KRW 4.73 billion.

In Q3 2026 sales were KRW 17.8 billion, operating profit KRW 1.06 billion and profit attributable to controlling shareholders KRW 657 million, down 84.9% from a year earlier. Profit attributable to controlling shareholders over the last four quarters totals KRW 13.6 billion. Operating cash flow was KRW 43.7 billion in 2025, after a negative KRW 10 billion in 2024.

05

Industry analysis

Demand for power equipment is planned around the government's electricity supply plan and KEPCO's demand forecasts. The company sets its production plans on that basis and cites capital investment and rising power demand from economic growth as growth drivers.

Demand for transmission and distribution materials is tied more to national policy and total energy demand than to short-term economic swings. Over the longer term, the expansion of renewable energy and the replacement of aging equipment are cited as growth drivers.

Nuclear-related expectations also matter: reports said Korea's nuclear export prospects were being priced across related companies, and in February 2026 expectations of further nuclear orders in the Czech Republic and Temelin pushed the share price sharply higher.

The gap between those expectations and the company's disclosed orders is notable, since no follow-on large order had been disclosed by the time the 2025 business report was filed. Its position in the cycle is a return to normal sales after the large HVDC project ended.

On competition, one report credits the company with unique steel tower and substation structure capability, though this is the article's view and should be weighed against competitive bidding and the dependence on a single utility customer.

06

Outlook

The company said it expects results to return to normal levels once HVDC-related sales end, and reports indicate it has not yet set out a specific growth strategy. A correction disclosure on a single-sale supply contract was filed on May 7, 2026, and the market read it as a partial change to the existing business plan.

Reports also described investors adopting a more cautious stance on earnings visibility and reducing positions. A follow-on large order that would set the direction of 2026 results had not been disclosed by the time the 2025 business report was filed.

On governance, a change in the largest shareholder took place on March 27, 2026 through an inheritance-related transfer of shares, and reports note no clear move toward a new growth engine. No dated capacity expansion or new product schedule has been announced.

The outlook therefore depends on the size and duration of newly disclosed supply contracts and on whether products beyond towers begin to grow. The 2026 business report, the next periodic filing, will show the full-year sales mix and tower share, and the Q4 2026 results will show whether the post-HVDC base holds.

Bull factors

Recovery in earnings power

Operating profit reached KRW 26.8 billion in 2025 with an 18.2% operating margin, a sharp improvement from the 2022 loss. Profit attributable to controlling shareholders rose to KRW 5.95 billion in 2024 and KRW 20.3 billion in 2025 after turning positive in 2023.

Financial cushion

Total liabilities were KRW 23 billion and total equity KRW 110.2 billion at the end of 2025, a debt ratio of 20.9%. Operating cash flow turned positive at KRW 43.7 billion in 2025 from negative KRW 10 billion in 2024. A BBB+ credit rating was assigned in April 2026.

Power infrastructure demand and nuclear expectations

Production planning follows the government's electricity supply plan and KEPCO's demand forecasts, so power equipment demand underpins the business. The company holds KEPIC seismic Grade 1 certification for steel structures and has delivered steel structures to Shin-Gori units 3 and 4. Expectations for Korean nuclear exports have also lifted related stocks as a group.

09

Bear factors

Dependence on a single project

Sales reached KRW 147.5 billion in 2025, but transmission towers made up 74.2% of that total. Analysts said the year's gains rested almost entirely on one Donghaean to SinGapyeong transmission project.

When large projects end, sales return to normal levels, which means the dependence translates directly into earnings volatility.

Consecutive quarterly declines

Sales peaked at KRW 40.9 billion in Q1 2026, then fell to KRW 24.4 billion in Q2 and KRW 17.8 billion in Q3, two consecutive quarterly declines. Q3 2026 operating profit was KRW 1.06 billion, down 82.9% from a year earlier, and profit attributable to controlling shareholders fell to KRW 657 million.

Growth strategy and succession

The inheritance of the company's leadership was completed, and the change in the largest shareholder is seen as a simple transfer of shares, which is a burden for growth prospects. No clear move toward new growth drivers or a changed business structure has emerged, which weighs on the outlook.

10

Risk factors

Raw materials and FX

Steel is the main input, so margins are exposed to iron ore, scrap and coal prices as well as exchange-rate moves. Under competitive bidding, cost increases are hard to pass on to customers, so margins could come under pressure first in periods of rising input costs.

Customer concentration and order gaps

Sales are concentrated in deliveries to KEPCO, so results move with its investment cycle. Reports pointed out that the contract that accounted for most of the business ended on February 28, 2026, and if follow-on large contracts do not follow, quarterly sales could stay at lower levels.

Disclosure corrections and contract execution

A correction disclosure filed on May 7, 2026 was read as a partial change to the existing business plan. In order-driven businesses, changes to contract terms can affect the timing of revenue recognition and profitability. Confirming the execution of disclosed contracts is cited as a prerequisite for stabilizing the outlook.

Overall view

Bosung Power Technology recorded operating profit of KRW 26.8 billion and an 18.2% operating margin in 2025, a sharp recovery from the 2022 operating loss. That result, however, centered on the HVDC steel tower project, which made up 74.2% of sales.

Sales fell to KRW 24.4 billion in Q2 2026 and KRW 17.8 billion in Q3 2026, and Q3 2026 operating profit was KRW 1.06 billion. The financial base is solid, with a debt ratio of 20.9% and total equity of KRW 110.2 billion, and operating cash flow turned positive in 2025.

Positive factors include the earnings recovery, power infrastructure demand and nuclear-related expectations. Negative factors include dependence on a single project, quarterly sales declines and the absence of a clear growth strategy after the leadership transition.

The next checkpoints are new supply-contract disclosures with utilities, the tower share and Q4 trend in the 2026 business report, and Q1 2027 results.
