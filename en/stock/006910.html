Report overview

After HVDC Peak, Order Gap Test

Bosung Power Technology posted its best-ever annual results in 2025 on concentrated revenue from the Donghae–Singapyeong HVDC steel-pipe transmission tower contracts, but with those contracts expiring in stages through the first half of 2026, securing follow-on large orders has become the key variable for the second half and beyond.

02

Business structure

Bosung Power Technology was founded in 1970 and listed on KOSDAQ in September 1994, producing transmission and distribution materials and steel structures for power plants and substations at its Chungju plant in North Chungcheong Province and its Naju plant in South Jeolla Province.

Its business is broadly split into a steel-structure segment (transmission towers, tubular support structures, steel structures) and a heavy-electrical-equipment segment (gas switchgear, pole-mounted transformers, fuses, among others).

In the first quarter of 2026, transmission towers accounted for 77.01% of total revenue, the largest single item, followed by tubular support structures at 4.42%, switchgear at 4.01%, overhead line materials at 1.82%, steel structures at 1.77%, and merchandise sales at 7.09%.

In the second quarter of that year, the tower share eased to about 64% but remained the company's largest business line. Transmission towers have historically made up roughly 40% to 50% of annual revenue, a share that has recently run well above that range.

As of the first quarter of 2026, key product prices stood at KRW 2,140 per kilogram for towers, KRW 3,400 per kilogram for tubular support structures, and KRW 1,830 per kilogram for steel structures.

The bulk of revenue depends on order volume from Korea Electric Power Corporation (KEPCO), a structure that allows relatively quick cash collection after delivery but also produces large swings in results tied to the order cycle.

The company has also sold into overseas markets such as the Philippines and Myanmar, though domestic KEPCO orders remain overwhelmingly dominant, and in April 2026 it received a BBB+ credit rating from eCredible.

04

Earnings analysis

Annual revenue rose from KRW 54.2 billion in 2022 to KRW 78.5 billion in 2023 (up 44.8%), slipped to KRW 77.1 billion in 2024 (down 1.8%), then jumped to KRW 147.5 billion in 2025 (up 91.4%).

Operating results swung from an operating loss of KRW 4.49 billion in 2022 to operating profit of KRW 3.35 billion in 2023, then rose for two straight years to KRW 4.82 billion in 2024 (up 44.1%) and KRW 26.8 billion in 2025 (up 455.7%).

The operating margin climbed from -8.3% in 2022 to 4.3% in 2023, 6.3% in 2024, and 18.2% in 2025.

Net profit attributable to controlling shareholders turned from a loss of KRW 11.1 billion in 2022 to profit of KRW 4.35 billion in 2023, then rose for two straight years to KRW 5.95 billion in 2024 (up 36.8%) and KRW 20.3 billion in 2025 (up 241.3%).

Operating cash flow swung to KRW 43.7 billion in 2025 from -KRW 10 billion in 2024, lifting total equity to KRW 110.2 billion at year-end 2025 while the debt-to-equity ratio stayed at 20.9%.

On a quarterly basis, revenue of KRW 55.8 billion and operating profit of KRW 13.7 billion in the second quarter of 2025 marked a peak, followed by a slowdown through the third quarter (revenue KRW 36.8 billion, operating profit KRW 6.17 billion) and fourth quarter (revenue KRW 37.4 billion, operating profit KRW 5.42 billion), before a modest rebound in the first quarter of 2026 to revenue of KRW 40.9 billion and operating profit of KRW 5.95 billion.

In the second quarter of 2026, however, revenue fell to KRW 24.4 billion (down 40.3% quarter on quarter and 56.3% year on year) and operating profit fell to KRW 4.73 billion (down 20.6% quarter on quarter and 65.4% year on year).

The sum of net profit attributable to controlling shareholders over the last four quarters (third quarter 2025 through second quarter 2026) was KRW 17.3 billion, below the full-year 2025 figure of KRW 20.3 billion, and the company's own annual report attributes the 2025 earnings improvement directly to the revenue increase from the Donghae–Singapyeong HVDC steel-pipe transmission tower contract, indicating that a large share of the profit gain originated from a single project.

05

Industry analysis

South Korea's power equipment industry is heavily order-driven, moving in step with KEPCO's transmission and substation investment and ordering schedules.

Growth in power demand tied to the expansion of AI data centers and semiconductor clusters has recently made grid expansion—moving power generated in outlying regions to the metropolitan area and industrial complexes—a central industry theme.

The government implemented a special act on expanding the national power grid in September 2025, and an amended version passed the National Assembly plenary session on August 20, 2026, opening the way for qualified private operators to participate in national grid construction via a Build-Transfer (BT) scheme, moving beyond the existing KEPCO-centered procurement structure.

In a related example, an approximately KRW 300 billion underground cable procurement for the Donghae–Dongseoul HVDC project was split and awarded to leading cable makers LS Cable & System, Daehan Cable, and Iljin Electric by section in the first half of 2026.

Along the same route, structural orders such as steel-pipe towers have continued, with KEPCO re-announcing in May 2026 a tender for additional 500kV HVDC steel-pipe towers on the Donghae–Dongseoul section at an estimated price of about KRW 9.28 billion.

Bosung Power Technology holds supply experience in 500kV HVDC overhead-line steel-pipe towers as well as fabrication references for 154kV, 345kV, and 765kV towers, giving it facility and testing-infrastructure competitiveness during this phase of ultra-high-voltage grid expansion.

Still, the order concentration and quarter-to-quarter revenue volatility typical of order-driven industries remain a common feature across the sector.

06

Outlook

The company has indicated that once the Donghae–Singapyeong HVDC steel-pipe tower supply contracts wind down in stages through the first half of 2026, revenue could revert to normal levels.

Indeed, contracts worth about KRW 32.1 billion and about KRW 23.4 billion ended on April 15, 2026, contributing to a 56.3% year-on-year decline in second-quarter revenue. Follow-on orders have not disappeared entirely, however.

In October 2025 the company signed a KRW 17.3 billion contract with Sean E&C to supply transmission-line tower materials for the Eumseong LNG power plant, with a contract term running to June 30, 2028, signaling some diversification of order sources.

KEPCO is also pursuing additional 500kV HVDC steel-pipe tower orders on the Donghae–Dongseoul section, re-announcing in May 2026 a tender estimated at about KRW 9.28 billion, with the award outcome worth watching as a potential bridge over the post-HVDC order gap.

In April 2026 the company obtained a BBB+ credit rating from eCredible, providing an external assessment of its financial stability.

Possibilities around nuclear decommissioning or entry into nuclear export supply chains have been discussed within and around the industry, but as of October 2026 no official order or contract tied to Bosung Power Technology has been confirmed in this area.

Whether expanded private-sector participation under the amended grid special act actually translates into new orders from the second half of 2026 onward stands as the key swing factor for medium-term results.

Bull factors

Potential Beneficiary of Expanding Grid Investment

The government has allowed private operators to participate in national grid construction under the special act on grid expansion, and an amended version passed the National Assembly plenary session on August 20, 2026, broadening the institutional basis further.

Rising power demand from expanding data centers and semiconductor clusters is seen as underpinning overall demand for grid expansion.

KEPCO continues to place ultra-high-voltage transmission infrastructure orders, including additional 500kV HVDC steel-pipe towers on the Donghae–Dongseoul section, so demand for Bosung Power Technology's core product lines has not disappeared entirely.

Improved Financial Strength and Cash Generation

Operating cash flow swung to KRW 43.7 billion in 2025 from -KRW 10 billion in 2024, while total equity rose to KRW 110.2 billion at year-end with the debt-to-equity ratio staying at just 20.9%.

In April 2026 the company also received a BBB+ credit rating from eCredible, gaining external recognition of its creditworthiness. The larger cash and equity cushion suggests it has some financial buffer for future capital spending or executing new orders.

Early Signs of Order Diversification

A KRW 17.3 billion contract signed with Sean E&C in October 2025 to supply transmission tower materials for the Eumseong LNG power plant, with a term running to June 30, 2028, can be read as a signal of moving away from reliance on a single HVDC project.

The heavy-electrical-equipment segment (gas switchgear, pole-mounted transformers) tends to be less volatile than the steel-structure business and is tied to maintenance and replacement demand for power equipment.

The company also has a track record of selling into overseas markets such as the Philippines and Myanmar, a potential avenue to reduce reliance on the domestic order cycle.

Bear factors

Revenue Gap After the End of the HVDC Boom

The Donghae–Singapyeong steel-pipe tower supply contracts wound down in stages through the first half of 2026, and the company itself has said revenue could revert to normal levels afterward.

Second-quarter 2026 revenue indeed fell to KRW 24.4 billion, down 40.3% from the prior quarter and 56.3% year on year, with operating profit down 65.4% year on year to KRW 4.73 billion.

The absence of a disclosed major follow-on contract by the time the annual report was filed supports near-term concern about an earnings gap.

Dependence on a Single Customer

Most revenue depends on order volume from KEPCO, producing significant quarter-to-quarter volatility tied to the order cycle.

The 2025 annual report itself attributes the earnings improvement directly to higher steel-pipe tower revenue from a specific HVDC project, confirming that the quality of earnings did not come from a diversified customer base.

The competitive-bidding structure also makes it difficult to pass on rising costs to selling prices, a burden on the stability of profitability.

Governance Change and a Gap in Growth Strategy

The controlling shareholder changed following the completion of an inheritance process in March 2026, which observers characterize as a simple transfer of ownership rather than a strategic shift. No concrete strategy for business diversification or new growth drivers has yet been clearly laid out externally.

With interpretation leaning toward maintaining the existing business footprint, market confidence in a mid-to-long-term growth path remains in an early stage of forming.

Risk factors

Customer and Order Concentration Risk

An overwhelming share of revenue comes from KEPCO order volume, so changes in the timing and scale of specific projects directly affect results.

As reliance on a single large project—such as the Donghae–Singapyeong HVDC steel-pipe tower contracts that drove the 2025 earnings surge—grows, so does the risk of a revenue gap once that project ends. This risk can persist until the timing and scale of a major follow-on order are officially confirmed.

Raw Material and Foreign Exchange Risk

Steel, the main raw material, is exposed to swings in iron ore, scrap, and coking coal markets as well as foreign exchange movements. Because orders are won through competitive bidding, cost increases cannot be passed through to selling prices quickly, so a sharp rise in input costs could erode profitability. Global supply-chain instability or tariff issues could also become variables affecting steel procurement costs.

Governance and Succession Risk

The controlling shareholder changed through an inheritance process completed in March 2026, with the related-party stake held at 14.56%.

With an ongoing pattern of internal family succession, the pace and direction of decisions on business diversification or new investment may not be fully explained to outside shareholders.

Given the limited liquidity typical of small-cap stocks, changes in shareholding structure or issues related to the controlling shareholder could amplify share price volatility.

Overall view

Bosung Power Technology turned from operating and net losses in 2022 to profit in 2023, and in 2024–2025 delivered earnings growth that lifted the operating margin to 18.2%, driven by concentrated revenue from the Donghae–Singapyeong HVDC steel-pipe tower contracts.

However, because the core driver of that growth was a single project, the staged expiration of those contracts in 2026 fed directly into quarterly earnings deceleration.

Second-quarter 2026 revenue and operating profit fell 56.3% and 65.4% year on year respectively, confirming in numbers the concern over a post-HVDC-boom earnings gap.

At the same time, signals that follow-on demand has not vanished entirely also exist, including the KRW 17.3 billion contract with Sean E&C, KEPCO's additional steel-pipe tower order, and expanded private-sector participation under the amended grid special act.

Financial strength appears relatively solid, with a debt-to-equity ratio of 20.9% and operating cash flow of KRW 43.7 billion at the end of 2025, but the future direction of results hinges on the scale and timing of officially confirmed follow-on large orders.

The upcoming quarterly disclosures, including the regular third-quarter 2026 report, are likely to serve as important reference points for gauging where post-HVDC 'normal level' results ultimately settle.
