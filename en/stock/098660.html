02

Business structure

STO is a men's apparel company. Since 2014 it has moved away from shirt-and-tie specialty stores and now plans and sells men's total fashion items under a 'FORMAL SPA' model, according to its company profile, with its store network shifting from small outlets to large street-front stores.

As of June 2026, its main brands are the STCO offline integrated retail brand, DIEMS, and the online brands ZERO LOUNGE and CODI GALLEY. By business segment, STCO accounted for 72.6% of sales and non-STCO segments for 27.4% in 2019.

The company has been expanding into women's wear and street brands, and it has said internal investment in these new businesses weighed on its 2025 results. It also plans to launch new multi-brand select shop formats through its nationwide store network and carry third-party brands.

The select-shop approach can be read as a way to diversify product lines and raise average spend per customer on the existing network.

Listed peers in the same apparel and lifestyle sector include Handsome, F&F, LF, Shinsegae International and Youngone, while STO's men's formal focus sets it apart from these firms' broader positioning, so direct comparisons have limits.

04

Earnings analysis

Annual sales were KRW 73.2 billion in 2022, KRW 74 billion in 2023 (up 1.2% year on year), KRW 70.2 billion in 2024 (down 5.2%) and KRW 71.5 billion in 2025 (up 1.8%). Among 2022 to 2025, 2023 was the largest year and 2024 the smallest.

Operating profit was KRW 3.05 billion in 2022 and KRW 3.55 billion in 2023 (up 16.5%), then fell to KRW 512 million in 2024 (down 85.6%), and in 2025 the company swung to an operating loss of KRW 1.41 billion. The operating margin was 4.2% in 2022, 4.8% in 2023, 0.7% in 2024 and -2.0% in 2025.

Net income attributable to controlling shareholders was KRW 1.6 billion in 2022 and KRW 2.02 billion in 2023 (up 26.2%). It turned to a net loss of KRW 996 million in 2024, and in 2025 the loss widened to KRW 4.8 billion.

Net income including non-controlling interests shows the same net losses of KRW 996 million in 2024 and KRW 4.8 billion in 2025. The company has reported losses for two consecutive years, 2024 and 2025.

The company attributed the 2025 operating loss to higher costs from launching new brands, reorganizing existing brands and restructuring its distribution. Operating cash flow was KRW 4.12 billion in 2022, KRW 4.95 billion in 2023, KRW 1.57 billion in 2024 and KRW 5.6 billion in 2025.

At the end of 2025, total equity was KRW 22.4 billion and total liabilities were KRW 49.6 billion, with the debt-to-equity ratio at 221.4%.

Quarterly results moved from a KRW 2.24 billion operating loss on KRW 13.3 billion of sales in the third quarter of 2025 to KRW 22.3 billion of sales and KRW 1.06 billion of operating profit in the fourth quarter, which was a swing to profit quarter on quarter.

Sales were KRW 15.9 billion in the first quarter of 2026, down 28.6% quarter on quarter, with a KRW 721 million operating loss, which was a swing to loss. Second-quarter 2026 sales were KRW 20 billion, up 25.3% quarter on quarter, with KRW 310 million of operating profit.

Third-quarter 2026 sales were KRW 13.3 billion, down 33.5% quarter on quarter and down 0.4% year on year, with a KRW 1.95 billion operating loss.

Net losses attributable to controlling shareholders have run for five consecutive quarters from the third quarter of 2025 to the third quarter of 2026, totaling KRW 5.09 billion over the latest four quarters. Some quarters returned to operating profit, but losses have recurred.

05

Industry analysis

STO operates in the apparel, fashion and lifestyle sector, which is sensitive to consumer spending. The company's 2025 first-quarter results summary cited a domestic consumption slump as a drag on performance. A 2020 securities research report also pointed to sales declines tied to a weak economy.

A men's formal-focused model is exposed to demand for suits and business wear, and the move into casual and women's wear can be read as an attempt to reduce that exposure.

Listed peers in the same sector include Youngone, F&F, Hyosung TNC, LF, Handsome, Shinsegae International and Hansae, but their business mix and price positioning differ, which limits direct comparison.

The company has attributed its earnings swings more to company-specific factors, such as new brand costs and distribution restructuring, than to the industry cycle.

Quarterly sales moved from KRW 22.3 billion in the fourth quarter of 2025 to KRW 13.3 billion in the third quarter of 2026, which may reflect seasonal shipment timing combined with the distribution changes.

The select-shop strategy has been presented as a way to widen product lines and spending per customer in existing channels. Whether an industry rebound turns into earnings recovery depends on new brands contributing sales while the cost structure improves.

06

Outlook

The nearest checkpoint is the 2026 business-year annual report. A news report said the final size and form of the dividend will be determined by 2026 results and a resolution at the annual shareholder meeting.

According to a September 2026 report, the company plans to distribute treasury shares in kind to general shareholders instead of canceling them, with a stated target of a 15% market-price dividend yield. The report said the target is possible because the dividend source is not cash.

Treasury shares were 603,728 shares, or 4.83% of issued shares, as of the June 2026 semi-annual report. After a trust contract was terminated at the end of July 2026, the company acquired 371,900 more shares, bringing holdings to 975,628 shares (7.80%).

On August 5, 2026 it entered into a new trust contract covering 587,544 shares, running until August 4, 2027.

The largest shareholder holds 67.73% of issued shares, which the report said leaves about 3.06 million shares eligible for dividends excluding the largest holder and treasury stock, and it calculated that the treasury shares needed for a 15% payout are less than half of current holdings.

The company said it skipped the 2025 dividend after weighing internal investment in women's wear and street brands and its loss-making position. Whether the new business investment shows up in 2026 operating results is therefore the next key question. Fourth-quarter 2026 results will be confirmed through the 2026 annual report as formal filed figures.

Bull factors

Treasury shares widen shareholder-return options

After terminating a trust contract at the end of July 2026, the company bought 371,900 more shares, raising holdings to 975,628 shares (7.80%). On August 5, 2026 it signed a new trust contract for 587,544 shares, running until August 4, 2027.

Holding more treasury shares gives the company more ways to use them, including cancellation or in-kind distribution, which is cited as a positive factor.

In-kind dividend plan with a 15% yield target

A September 2026 news report said the company plans to distribute its treasury shares in kind to general shareholders rather than cancel them. The report said a 15% market-price dividend yield target is possible because the dividend source is not cash.

The plan follows the decision to skip the 2025 dividend, and it is read as a change in the direction of shareholder returns.

Quarters with operating profit

Fourth-quarter 2025 sales rose 67.4% quarter on quarter to KRW 22.3 billion, and operating profit turned positive at KRW 1.06 billion. Second-quarter 2026 sales of KRW 20 billion, up 25.3% quarter on quarter, also produced KRW 310 million of operating profit.

Operating profit turned positive in some quarters despite the annual loss pattern, which is cited as a supporting factor.

Flat sales and new-business costs

Risk factors

Financial stability

A 221.4% debt-to-equity ratio and KRW 49.6 billion of total liabilities mean the debt burden is large relative to equity. If equity keeps shrinking, financing capacity and credit conditions could come under pressure. Operating cash flow was only KRW 1.57 billion in 2024 and rose to KRW 5.6 billion in 2025.

Domestic demand and new-business results

The company's first-quarter 2025 results summary cited a domestic consumption slump as a backdrop for weak performance. If new brands such as women's wear and street lines do not add meaningful sales, investment costs could keep weighing on operating results.

Quarterly sales swung from KRW 15.9 billion in the first quarter of 2026 to KRW 20 billion in the second and KRW 13.3 billion in the third, which points to demand volatility.

Dividend and treasury-share execution

The in-kind dividend will be finalized only by a shareholder meeting resolution, so it could remain at the plan stage. A 15% market-price dividend yield is a target whose actual level depends on the number of eligible shares and the share price.

The size of the shareholder-return effect depends on whether treasury-share purchases under the trust contract are carried out as planned.
