Report overview

Profit Swings Amid Expansion into AI, Robotics and Payments

Finger returned to annual operating profit in 2025, but quarterly results remain volatile as it extends its financial IT platform into physical AI, robotics and payments.

  1. 1

    Sales rose 27.9% to KRW 91.6 billion in 2025 and operating profit turned positive at KRW 1.47 billion, but net profit attributable to controlling shareholders fell for a second straight year to KRW 296 million.

  2. 2

    Sales reached KRW 27.8 billion in the third quarter of 2026, up for two consecutive quarters, yet the company posted an operating loss of KRW 93.36 million and a net loss attributable to controlling shareholders of KRW 9.95 billion.

  3. 3

    After a change in its largest shareholder in the first half of 2026, Finger moved beyond financial IT into physical AI through the acquisition of an industrial robot maker, and expansion into payments is also being discussed.

  4. 4

    On November 12, 2026, the board approved a KRW 17 billion loan to a subsidiary at 4.6% interest, with the term ending November 12, 2027.

02

Business structure

Finger is a first-generation Korean fintech company founded in 2000, and its core business is building and maintaining dedicated platforms for non-face-to-face banking channels at financial institutions.

Its platform segment accounted for 68% of revenue in the first quarter of 2026, the highest share among its business lines. The company also runs blockchain-related operations including remittance and payments, ERP, and security token offerings (STO).

A separate breakdown by Butler, based on second-quarter 2026 revenue, shows system construction and maintenance at 99% and software sales at 1%, so the weight of each segment depends on the classification used.

Consolidated subsidiaries include a software development and supply company, an e-commerce retail company and another software company, and headcount stood at 303 as of the end of June 2026.

In the first half of 2026, a group of investors including an electronics group and a payment-related company put in about KRW 110 billion, and one of them became the largest shareholder. The company then moved beyond group IT integration into physical AI through the acquisition of an industrial robot maker.

In blockchain, it is developing an all-in-one STO solution using blockchain and MPC technology in line with government policy to activate the STO market, and it is building references in stablecoins.

Its customers are mainly financial institutions, and revenue comes mostly from platform construction and the maintenance contracts that follow. Because its customers are largely financial firms, their IT budget timing directly shapes its revenue schedule, which is the defining feature of its business structure.

04

Earnings analysis

Annual sales moved from KRW 90.1 billion in 2022 to KRW 83.1 billion in 2023 (down 7.7% year on year) and KRW 71.6 billion in 2024 (down 13.9%), before recovering to KRW 91.6 billion in 2025 (up 27.9%). Among 2022 to 2025, 2025 was the largest year and 2024 the smallest.

Operating profit fell from KRW 5.4 billion in 2022 to KRW 3.67 billion in 2023 (down 32.0%), and the company recorded an operating loss of KRW 470 million in 2024, a swing into deficit.

In 2025 operating profit returned to KRW 1.47 billion, and the operating margin improved from -0.7% in 2024 to 1.6% in 2025, though it remains below the 6.0% of 2022 and 4.4% of 2023.

Net profit attributable to controlling shareholders peaked at KRW 4.45 billion in 2023 (up 155.5%), then fell to KRW 803 million in 2024 (down 81.9%) and KRW 296 million in 2025 (down 63.1%), a decline in both 2024 and 2025.

Net profit including non-controlling interests was KRW 333 million in 2025, a different measure from the controlling-shareholder figure.

At the end of 2025, total equity was KRW 47.7 billion, of which KRW 45.9 billion belonged to controlling shareholders, and total liabilities were KRW 19.9 billion; the debt ratio rose from 35.1% in 2022 to 41.7% in 2025.

Operating cash flow was KRW 10.7 billion in 2022, KRW 7.45 billion in 2023, KRW 1.09 billion in 2024 and KRW 7 billion in 2025.

Quarterly sales were KRW 25.3 billion in the third quarter of 2025, KRW 23.9 billion in the fourth quarter (down 5.5% from the prior quarter), and KRW 20.5 billion in the first quarter of 2026 (down 14.3%), the low point before recovering to KRW 22.6 billion in the second quarter (up 10.2%) and KRW 27.8 billion in the third quarter (up 23.3% quarter on quarter and up 10.0% year on year).

Operating results went from an operating profit of KRW 1.09 billion in the third quarter of 2025 to an operating loss of KRW 350 million in the fourth quarter and KRW 326 million in the first quarter of 2026, then an operating profit of KRW 370 million in the second quarter, and an operating loss of KRW 93.36 million in the third quarter, a swing into deficit year on year.

Net profit attributable to controlling shareholders reached KRW 6.72 billion in the second quarter of 2026, up 13,523.5% from the prior quarter, even though operating profit in that quarter was only KRW 370 million, so non-operating items and one-off factors need to be read alongside it.

The combined net loss attributable to controlling shareholders over the last four quarters was KRW 5.39 billion. A company-monitoring commentary attributed quarterly sales swings and one-off costs to heavier investment concentrated in the financial platform construction business.

05

Industry analysis

Finger's earnings recovery in 2025 is explained by demand from digital transformation at financial institutions. FnGuide's company overview reported that cumulative sales through the third quarter of 2025 rose 31.8%, citing digital transformation demand as the backdrop.

The same overview said platform adoption among second-tier financial institutions is growing and the company is entering non-financial sectors, widening its scope.

Financial IT system construction depends on the investment schedules of banking channels, so the investment cycle of financial firms is the key variable for revenue flow. On policy, the government's push to activate the security token market is cited as a factor that could lift demand for STO-related services.

Stablecoins are another area where the company is building references in payment infrastructure.

Within the competitive landscape, Finger is a financial-specialist platform company, smaller and with a different customer mix than large system integrators such as Samsung SDS and LG CNS, which focus on group IT and large projects.

Kiwoom Securities said the key question is how fast a financial data company can extend into physical AI built on manufacturing-floor data.

Robotics and automation demand follows a different cycle from financial IT, so how quickly new businesses show up in reported results becomes the benchmark for assessing the industry position.

06

Outlook

Two new business lines are being presented. The first is physical AI through the industrial robot maker acquired recently. A Kiwoom Securities analyst expects the company to learn from manufacturing-floor data and supply automation solutions to manufacturers across various industries.

The second is payments, where a recent strategic investment in a payment-related company is seen as a route to widen the business into payment services. The same analyst also said Finger's role is likely to grow as an electronics group expands its business aggressively.

On the financial side, the board approved a KRW 17 billion loan to a subsidiary on November 12, 2026, at 4.6% interest with a term ending November 12, 2027. According to a DigitalToday report based on the disclosure, the total outstanding balance of loans to subsidiaries stands at KRW 60.2 billion.

On the top line, the key question is whether the sales recovery seen across two consecutive quarters through the third quarter of 2026 can continue. The revenue contribution from new businesses is not yet separately visible in reported results.

The first checkpoint is whether the annual operating margin in the 2026 annual report exceeds the 1.6% recorded in 2025.

08

Bull factors

A new growth axis in physical AI

Kiwoom Securities, in an August 2026 report, assessed that Finger secured a new growth axis by entering the physical AI market through the acquisition of an industrial robot maker. The direction presented is to supply automation solutions across industries based on manufacturing-floor data. Whether a company that handled financial data can extend into manufacturing data is the crux of this argument.

Sales recovery

Sales rose 27.9% to KRW 91.6 billion in 2025. Third-quarter 2026 sales of KRW 27.8 billion were up 23.3% from the prior quarter and 10.0% from a year earlier. Sales have grown for two consecutive quarters since the second quarter of 2026.

Group synergy and payment expansion

After group affiliates invested in the first half of 2026 and a new largest shareholder emerged, a Kiwoom Securities analyst judged that Finger's role would grow as the group expands aggressively. A recent strategic investment in a payment-related company is also seen as a route into payment services. Neither effect is yet separated out in reported figures, so it has to be confirmed through future results.

09

Bear factors

Weakening earnings base

Net profit attributable to controlling shareholders peaked at KRW 4.45 billion in 2023, then fell to KRW 803 million in 2024 and KRW 296 million in 2025, a decline in both 2024 and 2025. The operating margin remains low, at 1.6% in 2025 versus 6.0% in 2022. The bear case points out that the 2025 return to profit came on a small earnings base.

Recurring quarterly losses

The fourth quarter of 2025 recorded a net loss attributable to controlling shareholders of KRW 2.21 billion, and the third quarter of 2026 a net loss of KRW 9.95 billion. The third-quarter 2026 operating loss of KRW 93.36 million marked a swing into deficit from a year earlier.

The combined four-quarter net loss of KRW 5.39 billion suggests the annual profit has not yet carried through to quarterly results.

Fund flows and debt burden

Total liabilities were KRW 19.9 billion at the end of 2025, and the debt ratio rose from 35.1% in 2022 to 41.7% in 2025. The board decided on November 12, 2026 to lend KRW 17 billion to a subsidiary at 4.6% interest, with the term ending November 12, 2027.

As intra-group lending grows, how to read the financial burden on a consolidated basis becomes the central question.

10

Risk factors

Customer concentration and sales volatility

The platform segment made up 68% of first-quarter 2026 revenue, which means high dependence on financial institutions' non-face-to-face channel investment. Shrinking IT budgets at financial firms or intensifying competition with large system integrators could widen revenue swings. The first-quarter 2026 low of KRW 20.5 billion also shows how sensitive quarterly sales are to project timing.

Execution risk in new businesses

Physical AI and robotics only show up as earnings once manufacturing-data learning and delivery track records accumulate after the acquisition. STO and stablecoin market size may depend on policy and regulatory timing.

New-business investment costs could hit the quarterly results of the financial platform business before revenue arrives.

Ownership change and earnings volatility

Since the largest shareholder changed in the first half of 2026, intra-group transactions and lending have increased, so consolidated financial flows need to be read together.

A company-monitoring commentary attributed quarterly sales swings and one-off costs to concentrated investment in financial platform construction. Because net profit attributable to controlling shareholders moves sharply each quarter, non-operating items and one-off factors need to be checked.

12

Overall view

Finger's annual results entered a recovery phase in 2025, with sales of KRW 91.6 billion (up 27.9%) and operating profit of KRW 1.47 billion after a swing back to profit.

However, net profit attributable to controlling shareholders peaked at KRW 4.45 billion in 2023 and fell to KRW 296 million by 2025, a decline in both 2024 and 2025, and the 2025 operating margin was only 1.6%.

Sales rose for two consecutive quarters in 2026, but the third quarter swung back into deficit with an operating loss of KRW 93.36 million and a net loss attributable to controlling shareholders of KRW 9.95 billion.

The bull case rests on a structure in which physical AI, blockchain and payments are added to a non-face-to-face financial platform base.

The bear case rests on dependence on financial clients, profit volatility from concentrated quarterly investment, and fund flows within the group, including the KRW 17 billion subsidiary loan.

On valuation, with no earnings-based multiple available, the price relative to net asset value becomes the main point of comparison. The next checkpoints are whether the annual profit carries into quarterly results and how much of the new-business contribution appears in reported figures.
