02

Business structure

Daehan Flour Mill is a flour milling company founded in 1952 whose core businesses are wheat flour and animal feed, supplemented by warehousing and handling, pet products and food and beverage.

The main revenue in the wheat flour food segment comes from Gompyo-branded flour and premix products such as batter and fried-food flours.

Korea Ratings said in its June 2026 report that the feed business accounts for about 41% of consolidated sales and noted that the company maintains transactions with large food makers including Nongshim and Otoki.

Segment shares compiled by Asia Economy for the third quarter of 2025 were 33.8% wheat flour, 41.3% feed, 6.8% pet business, 8.7% forage and handling, and 7.2% food service and others. Milling and feed together exceed 75% of sales, so these two lines still account for most revenue.

A different dataset for the second quarter of 2026 classifies the mix as 40% feed, 31% wheat flour, 10% handling, 7% food and beverage and 6% other, so the segment definitions vary across sources.

Its main raw materials, wheat, corn and soybean meal, are largely imported, which exposes margins to global grain prices and the exchange rate. Exports are below 1% of sales, so the business is effectively domestic.

The company began pet and food and beverage businesses as new ventures in 2020, and the Gompyo brand underpins its wheat flour business in the domestic market.

04

Earnings analysis

On a disclosed annual basis, sales rose from KRW 1.37 trillion in 2022 to KRW 1.44 trillion in 2023 (up 5.4% year on year), the largest year in the period, then came in at KRW 1.37 trillion in 2024 (down 4.6%) and KRW 1.38 trillion in 2025 (unchanged year on year).

Operating profit rose from KRW 43.3 billion in 2022 to KRW 54.9 billion in 2023 (up 26.8%) and KRW 72.3 billion in 2024 (up 31.8%), then fell to KRW 62.3 billion in 2025 (down 13.9%). The operating margin climbed from 3.2% in 2022 to 5.3% in 2024 before easing to 4.5% in 2025.

Korea Ratings attributed the margin improvement after 2023 to a downward-stabilizing international wheat price and the effect of higher selling prices. Even as margins improved, sales stayed in the mid-KRW 1 trillion range, so margins rather than volume drove earnings.

Attributable net income was KRW 41.2 billion in 2022, KRW 81.2 billion in 2023 (up 97.3%) and KRW 48.7 billion in 2024 (down 40.0%), and in 2025 the company swung to an attributable net loss of KRW 25.3 billion.

The 2025 net loss of KRW 25.1 billion followed the recognition of KRW 94.4 billion in other provisions tied to the flour price collusion case, according to the filing.

Quarterly operating profit slipped from KRW 16.6 billion in the third quarter of 2025 to KRW 15 billion in the fourth quarter (down 9.6% quarter on quarter), then to KRW 7.02 billion in the first quarter of 2026 (down 53.2%).

Operating profit was flat at KRW 7.03 billion in the second quarter of 2026, then fell to KRW 4.65 billion in the third quarter (down 33.8% quarter on quarter and 72.0% year on year). Sales rose for two consecutive quarters after the first-quarter 2026 low, but profit did not recover over the same stretch.

The attributable net loss was KRW 82.8 billion in the fourth quarter of 2025 and KRW 5.53 billion in the third quarter of 2026, and operating cash flow was KRW 140.1 billion in 2025, up from KRW 76 billion in 2024.

05

Industry analysis

The domestic flour market is split among a few millers, and Korea Ratings views Daehan Flour Mill as part of an oligopoly alongside CJ Cheiljedang. Its main raw materials, wheat, corn and soybean meal, are largely imported, so global grain prices and the exchange rate feed directly into costs.

International wheat prices stabilized downward after 2023, which supported the margin recovery.

Conditions changed in 2026, when the collusion sanction brought pressure to cut selling prices, a high-exchange-rate environment and rising raw material costs combined, and the rating agency expected limited near-term profit recovery.

The Fair Trade Commission found that seven millers colluded on flour supply prices and volumes over six years and imposed KRW 671.0 billion in total penalties, of which Daehan Flour Mill's share is KRW 179.3 billion. The sanction raises regulatory pressure on pricing across the milling sector.

Purchasing policies and cost burdens at large food customers such as Nongshim and Otoki can also affect volumes and selling prices. CJ Cheiljedang said in its third-quarter 2026 results that raw material and logistics cost burdens continued while it defended profitability. Cost pressure across the food sector is acting as a common drag on margins.
