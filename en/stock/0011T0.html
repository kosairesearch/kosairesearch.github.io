Report overview

Growing Sales, Persistent Losses

Chaevi leads Korea's private fast-charging market, but it still runs at a loss on a reported basis, so the key question is whether rising utilization can absorb its fixed costs.

02

Business structure

Chaevi is an EV charging infrastructure company founded in 2016. It covers the whole chain from charger development and manufacturing to installation, operation and after-sales care. Its business rests on two pillars: charger manufacturing and sales, and direct operation of charging stations.

Full-year 2025 revenue was KRW 101.7 billion, with an operating loss of KRW 29.6 billion. Revenue from charging-station operation rose from KRW 13.0 billion in 2022 to KRW 52.4 billion in 2025.

It owns and operates about 6,000 fast-charging bays, the most among private operators in Korea, and manages about 10,000 bays when government-supplied units are included.

However, its share of newly added capacity fell to 4.8% in 2025 from 22.2% a year earlier, and its share of all fast-charging bays slipped from 13.0% to 11.3%.

The large-scale exit of conglomerate affiliates from charging, including LG, SK, Hanwha and Lotte units, has curbed new supply and changed the competitive landscape. On the manufacturing side, the company builds 12 charger models ranging from 50 kW to 350 kW.

It also operates hybrid charging sites that combine solar, energy storage and charging in cooperation with Korea Electric Power.

03

Recent trends

Chaevi listed on KOSDAQ on April 29, 2026, raising KRW 110.7 billion by offering 9 million shares at KRW 12,300. On the first day it closed 83.33% above the offer price, after an intraday high of KRW 30,750. The shares fell below the offer price on May 27 and had not recovered to that level by June 22.

As of October 27, 2026, the share price was KRW 4,105, down 0.73% from the previous session, with a market capitalization of KRW 192.5 billion and trading value of KRW 0.3 billion. On a filed basis (DART), first-half 2026 cumulative revenue was KRW 29 billion, up 36.9% from the same period a year earlier.

The operating loss for the period was KRW 10 billion and the net loss was KRW 11.4 billion, with both losses larger than a year earlier. Profitability indicators show some improvement. The EBITDA margin of the charging service business was -3.2%, an improvement of 14.1 percentage points from a year earlier.

Average first-half utilization was estimated at about 5.5%, above the 4.9% full-year figure the company projected at listing. Half-year figures quoted in some press reports rest on a different scope from the filed figures, and this report follows the filed figures.

04

Outlook

The CEO has set targets of EBITDA breakeven in the fourth quarter of 2026 and an operating profit in 2027. One news outlet noted that whether an operating profit is achieved in 2027 will be the main yardstick for judging the strategy.

On the policy side, Chaevi takes part in the government charging-facility subsidy program through 2027, having been selected in all four operator and manufacturer categories. It was also named the first operator of the public fast-charging Plug & Charge pilot.

Under the 2025 highway rest-area program it is installing 138 fast chargers at 27 sites in phases. Overseas, IPO proceeds are earmarked for a U.S. plant, added domestic capacity, an India joint venture and energy-storage R&D.

The company says high-margin export volumes are concentrated in the fourth quarter, so results should be read on a full-year basis. Across the industry, analysts say the competitive focus is shifting from build-out speed to equipment utilization and profitability. Separately, the filing showed tradable shares rising to 60.60% of the total at the six-month mark.

05

Bull factors

Public-land site advantage

Chaevi says it holds about 71% of its sites on public land, which lowers its rent burden. New fast-charging installations fell 95% year on year in the first quarter of 2026. With fresh supply curtailed, utilization at existing sites could rise. This reflects the company's and some press accounts, and whether it persists remains to be confirmed.

Improving charging-service margins

The charging-service EBITDA margin was -3.2% in the first half of 2026, 14.1 percentage points better than a year earlier. Average utilization was estimated at about 5.5%, above the target set at listing. The company has said that an average of 2.8 charging sessions per bay per day would be enough to reach breakeven.

Operating metrics are moving toward management's targets, which points to room for improvement in business indicators.

Vertical integration and policy access

Chaevi has been selected as a subsidy-program operator for nine straight years and was also chosen in the newly created manufacturer category in 2026. Combining manufacturing and operations makes it easier to feed field experience back into product design.

Its launch of a home-charger subscription service with Hyundai Motor is an example of broadening its business scope. Links to both public and private demand may widen its room to respond to demand swings.

06

Bear factors

Widening loss trend

The annual operating loss grew from KRW 13.9 billion in 2022 to KRW 26.3 billion in 2023, KRW 27.6 billion in 2024 and KRW 29.6 billion in 2025. The first-half 2026 filing also shows larger operating and net losses than a year earlier. Sales growth has not yet translated into profit. Whether the targeted turnaround is met will have to be confirmed in coming quarterly results.

Fixed-cost and depreciation burden

Charging infrastructure carries recurring costs such as depreciation, power demand charges and maintenance. Even if EBITDA turns positive, operating losses can persist if the burden of capital spending is not offset. In the first quarter of 2026, depreciation in the charging segment equaled 24% of charging revenue. The manufacturing segment's losses must also narrow before overall earnings improve.

Supply-demand and balance-sheet pressure

The 2025 debt ratio was 200.34%, above the industry average, and total borrowings were about KRW 88.9 billion. At the six-month mark, tradable shares rise to 28,353,940, or 60.60% of the total.

A pre-listing analysis estimated that shares financial investors could sell within six months were 6.1 to 7.3 times the retail allocation. With the shares below the offer price, the market impact of this supply is a variable.

07

Risk factors

Macro

The 2026 EV sales outlook reflected both higher global oil prices and government policy favoring eco-friendly vehicles. A fall in oil prices or a policy change could alter the pace of EV adoption and charging demand. Because charging has a large fixed-cost component, demand swings weigh heavily on earnings. Changes in interest rates and market sentiment could also affect the debt burden and funding conditions.

Industry & Competition

The exit of conglomerate affiliates, which curbed new supply, is cited as a backdrop to better utilization. If supply conditions change and competitors return, this setup could shift. Chaevi's share of fast-charging bays declined in 2025. Overseas markets have different competitive structures, so domestic experience may not transfer directly.

Company-specific

Chaevi's offer-price valuation was derived from estimated 2028 EBITDA. With 2025 EBITDA at KRW -13.6 billion, that implies a sharp improvement within a short period. If targets slip, additional funding could be required. Overseas investment and new ventures also carry execution risk.

08

Overall view

Chaevi combines a fast-charging site network with in-house manufacturing, and its first-half revenue grew sharply on a filed basis. Both the operating loss and the net loss widened, however, so top-line growth has not yet carried through to earnings.

The bullish factors are its public-land site base, improving charging-service margin indicators and its role in government programs. The bearish factors are the widening-loss trend, fixed costs such as depreciation and power demand charges, a high debt ratio and the lock-up release.

The shares have traded below the KRW 12,300 offer price since listing, and a lock-up release is scheduled for October 29, 2026. Items to watch are whether EBITDA turns positive in the fourth quarter of 2026 and whether the 2027 operating-profit target is met. This report is for information only, and any judgment is left to the reader.
