A Mid-Sized Broker Whose Earnings Track Trading Volumes

LS Securities swung back to profit in the first half of 2026, but third-quarter earnings fell sharply as trading volumes shrank, and its annual operating margin narrowed from 3.1% in 2022 to 1.0% in 2025.

02

Business structure

LS Securities changed its name from the former eBEST Investment & Securities in June 2024 and belongs to the LS Group. Its largest shareholder, an LS Group affiliate, holds 60.98% of common shares.

The company is a small-to-mid-sized broker centered on brokerage and investment operations, and a local credit rating agency put its market share at roughly 1% based on equity and operating net revenue.

Earnings come from three sources: commission on brokerage, interest on credit lending, and gains and losses on financial instruments. In the third quarter of 2026, fee income reached KRW 48.7 billion, up 39.2% from a year earlier, and net interest income rose 65.9% from the prior quarter to KRW 13.0 billion.

Trading gains and losses move with market volatility, making this the most variable of the three sources. The investment banking unit is shifting from real estate project finance toward structured finance, corporate finance and alternative investments.

Underwriting, arranging and advisory fees remain small, so its IB presence is still limited. Equity of KRW 878.7 billion at the end of 2025 constrains large deals that draw on balance sheet capacity.

Participation in financing for LS Group affiliates is cited as the starting point for IB revenue, and one analysis points to possible links with the group's power and energy infrastructure network. On the retail side, the company is expanding digital channels, including simulated trading and a strategy platform.

04

Earnings analysis

Annual revenue fell from KRW 1.35 trillion in 2022 to KRW 1.32 trillion in 2023, down 2.0% year on year, then rose to KRW 1.65 trillion in 2024, up 25.1%, and to KRW 2.3 trillion in 2025, up 38.8%. Revenue has grown for two consecutive years since the 2023 low, and 2025 was the largest year in the 2022–2025 period.

Operating profit fell from KRW 41.8 billion in 2022 to KRW 33.2 billion in 2023, down 20.5%, and to KRW 21.8 billion in 2024, down 34.3%, which was the smallest year. It edged up to KRW 22.2 billion in 2025, up 1.9%.

Net profit attributable to controlling shareholders went from KRW 29.7 billion in 2022 and KRW 28.7 billion in 2023 to KRW 16.6 billion in 2024, then rose to KRW 23 billion in 2025, up 38.1%. 2022 was the largest year for that line.

Revenue grew over two years while operating profit fell or barely rose, so the operating margin declined each year from 3.1% in 2022 to 2.5%, 1.3% and 1.0% in 2025. Revenue gains did not fully carry through to profit.

Operating cash flow was negative KRW 1.54 trillion in 2023 and negative KRW 743.7 billion in 2024, then turned to a KRW 45.6 billion inflow in 2025. The debt ratio rose from 571.0% in 2022 to 1,082.3% in 2025, when total liabilities were KRW 9.51 trillion and total equity was KRW 878.7 billion.

Quarterly results show an operating loss of KRW 679 million in Q3 2025 and KRW 20.3 billion in Q4 2025, followed by operating profit of KRW 39.2 billion in Q1 2026. Q2 2026 operating profit was KRW 58.1 billion, up 48.1% from the prior quarter, and attributable net profit was KRW 43.6 billion, up 43.7%.

In Q3 2026, revenue was KRW 1.48 trillion, down 35.6% from the prior quarter, operating profit was KRW 8.78 billion, down 84.9%, and attributable net profit was KRW 6.35 billion, down 85.4%. Against a year earlier, operating profit turned positive and attributable net profit rose 118.0%.

Press reports said third-quarter fee income rose but net fee income fell 35.5% from the prior quarter to KRW 26.2 billion, and the company attributed this to shrinking trading value and an unfavorable market. Reports also cite net losses on financial instrument valuation and disposal as a drag in the quarter. Attributable net profit over the last four quarters totals KRW 66.1 billion.

05

Industry analysis

Domestic brokerage earnings are most sensitive to stock market trading value and margin loan balances. In the first half of 2026, industry commentary said higher trading value and a strong market worked in favor of brokerage and trading results.

In the third quarter, a market pullback and shrinking trading value hit fee income and trading gains together. A local credit rating agency tallied LS Securities' Q1 2026 operating net revenue at more than double the year-earlier level.

Large brokers, by contrast, are using their equity to expand into corporate finance, acquisition finance, project finance and alternative investments, which leaves LS Securities' IB competitiveness still in a growth phase.

On asset quality, the ratio of equity to impaired-grade assets fell from 20.6% at end-2023 to 7.0% at end-2025 and was 8.0% at end-March 2026, below the 11.1% average the same agency calculated for small and mid-sized brokers.

Ahead of the resumption of short selling, financial authorities asked institutional investors to adopt preventive measures, and brokers that route orders must verify compliance in advance and conduct regular checks.

Rising investment in power grids and energy infrastructure could lift financing demand from related companies, and one analysis sees room for a differentiated model if the group's industrial network is combined with IB capability.

06

Outlook

The company said its third-quarter 2026 results were affected by an unfavorable market environment and lower trading value, and that it is working to strengthen competitiveness in each business division and to manage risk.

In IB, the company has added structured finance, corporate finance and alternative investment work since its 2024 reorganization, centered on a comprehensive finance division.

In a June 2026 assessment, a local credit rating agency said profitability improved in Q1 2026 and that additional credit losses on previously extended project finance loans were expected to ease compared with earlier periods.

The same assessment noted that intensifying competition and a widening competitiveness gap with large brokers mean trading performance and the pace of IB profitability recovery will remain variables.

In a bond issuance in September 2026, the credit rating was presented at around A0, while the higher adjusted leverage was flagged as a burden. Whether deals with group affiliates translate into higher IB fee income is the next thing to watch.

In the near term, a recovery in trading value and the swing in financial instrument gains and losses will drive quarterly results. Once short selling resumes, compliance checks on brokers add to their operational load and make internal controls a review item apart from earnings.

08

Bull factors

Profit Restored on a Quarterly Basis

Net profit attributable to controlling shareholders turned positive at KRW 30.3 billion in Q1 2026 and rose 43.7% to KRW 43.6 billion in Q2. Q3 stayed profitable at KRW 6.35 billion attributable net profit and KRW 8.78 billion operating profit, up 118.0% in net profit from a year earlier. Compared with 2025, when losses recurred, earnings capacity has recovered.

Expanding Fee Base

Q3 2026 fee income reached KRW 48.7 billion, up 39.2% from a year earlier, and press reports highlighted strong growth in brokerage commissions. Fee income rising while trading value shrank is read as a sign that the brokerage base has grown. Net interest income rose 65.9% from the prior quarter, giving the business a partial cushion.

Rebound in Asset Quality Indicator

The ratio of equity to impaired-grade assets fell from 20.6% at end-2023 to 7.0% at end-2025 and rebounded to 8.0% at end-March 2026. It remains below the 11.1% average for small and mid-sized brokers, but an assessment expects credit losses to ease. A rating around A0 supports continued access to the corporate bond market.

09

Bear factors

Sharp Swings in Quarterly Profit

Q3 2026 operating profit of KRW 8.78 billion fell 84.9% from the prior quarter, and attributable net profit of KRW 6.35 billion fell 85.4%. Press reports cited large losses on financial instrument valuation and disposal.

Q4 2025 posted an operating loss of KRW 20.3 billion and an attributable net loss of KRW 14.1 billion. Quarterly profit remains highly sensitive to trading value.

Falling Margin and High Leverage

The operating margin fell from 3.1% in 2022 to 1.0% in 2025, while the debt ratio rose from 571.0% to 1,082.3%. A local credit rating agency flagged higher adjusted leverage, driven by a larger trading asset base and rising receivables, as a burden. If profit growth lags revenue growth, the debt burden becomes relatively heavier.

Gap in IB Scale and Capital

Total equity was KRW 878.7 billion at end-2025, a gap relative to the acquisition finance and project finance businesses that large brokers expand using their balance sheets. Underwriting, arranging and advisory fees remain small. Until deals with group affiliates show up in IB results, one assessment sees limited growth drivers.

10

Risk factors

Market Trading Value and Volatility

When trading value shrinks or the market corrects, brokerage commissions and credit lending interest weaken together. Net fee income fell 35.5% from the prior quarter in Q3 2026. If market conditions shift, quarterly earnings swings could widen again.

Trading and Credit Risk

Gains and losses on financial instruments can turn negative with market prices, and PF-related credit losses remain a swing factor for IB profitability. With adjusted leverage high, the buffer for absorbing losses may thin.

Because the asset quality ratio fell to 7.0% at end-2025 before rebounding to 8.0% in March 2026, whether further losses emerge is a key management issue.

Regulation and Internal Controls

Ahead of the resumption of short selling, financial authorities required institutional investors to adopt preventive measures and made brokers responsible for verifying compliance and conducting regular checks.

As brokers take on a larger role in routing short-sale orders, internal control burdens and sanction risk may also rise. The detailed rules and their timing are difficult to pin down from current information.

Overall view

LS Securities' revenue has grown since 2022, while operating profit and operating margin have declined. In 2025 attributable net profit rose 38.1% to KRW 23 billion, a recovery, yet the 1.0% operating margin remains below the 3.1% of 2022.

Profit held in Q1 and Q2 2026, but Q3 operating profit fell to KRW 8.78 billion amid shrinking trading value and losses on financial instruments. Attributable net profit of KRW 66.1 billion over the last four quarters reflects this volatility.

The debt ratio rose to 1,082.3%, and operating cash flow turned to a KRW 45.6 billion inflow in 2025. Strengths cited include fee growth, a rebound in the asset quality ratio and a credit rating around A0. Weaknesses include sharp quarterly swings, a thin margin, and the gap in IB scale and capital.

The next checkpoints are preliminary Q4 2026 results, the operating margin and debt ratio in the 2026 annual report, and whether trading value recovers in Q1 2027.
