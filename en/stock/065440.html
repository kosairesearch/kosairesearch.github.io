02

Business structure

Eluon is a telecom solutions specialist that supplies mobile network platforms and value-added services. Its business has two pillars.

The first covers core mobile network equipment such as HLR, roaming gateways, PDSN and authentication centers, along with value-added services including ringback tones and caller ID display.

The second is an other-systems segment made up of storage and backup systems, card issuing and dispatch machines with card issuing software, and rental income. Based on 1Q 2024 data, the telecom solutions and services segment accounted for about 46% of revenue and other systems for about 54%.

That is a 2024 snapshot, so it is difficult to carry these weights directly into the current structure. Overseas, the company signed a storage virtualization distribution agreement with the US firm ACTIFIO and expanded its IC card inspection machine and dispatch business through a contract with a German partner.

These businesses were cited as factors that weighed on results in the first half of 2025. Its main customers can be viewed as mobile carriers and card-related businesses.

Compared with large SI companies such as Samsung SDS and LG CNS, its smaller scale means results can move significantly with individual contract wins. The head office is in Seongnam, Gyeonggi Province.

Earnings analysis

Annual revenue went from KRW 56.6 billion in 2022 to KRW 56 billion in 2023, a 0.9% decline, and then rose to KRW 68.5 billion in 2024, up 22.3%, making 2024 the largest year of 2022 to 2025. Revenue fell 10.2% to KRW 61.6 billion in 2025.

Operating profit grew from KRW 941 million in 2022 to KRW 3.03 billion in 2023, up 222.4%, and KRW 3.1 billion in 2024, up 2.0%, before dropping 50.4% to KRW 1.53 billion in 2025. The operating margin moved from 1.7% in 2022 to 5.4% in 2023 and 4.5% in 2024, then fell to 2.5% in 2025.

Net income attributable to controlling shareholders was largest in 2024 at KRW 5.62 billion and smallest in 2025 at KRW 1.61 billion, down 71.4%. Net income including non-controlling interests, at KRW 1.6 billion in 2025, is a separate figure and should not be read as the same measure.

The company attributed the 2025 decline in operating profit to lower revenue and weaker profitability in some business units. It cited the absence of a one-time income tax benefit recognized in 2024 as a base effect that added to the drop in net income.

Operating cash flow went from KRW 89.28 million in 2022 and KRW 5.61 billion in 2023 to KRW 7.79 billion in 2024, then turned to -KRW 979 million in 2025.

Quarterly results show an operating loss of KRW 1.14 billion and a net loss attributable to controlling shareholders of KRW 985 million in 3Q 2025, followed by a 4Q 2025 swing to KRW 26.7 billion in revenue and KRW 2 billion in operating profit. 1Q 2026 revenue was KRW 11.1 billion with an operating loss of KRW 1.19 billion, returning to a loss, and 2Q 2026 revenue was KRW 9.61 billion with an operating loss of KRW 751 million, a loss that narrowed. 3Q 2026 revenue was KRW 17.2 billion, up 78.6% from the prior quarter and 78.6% year on year, with operating profit of KRW 223 million, a swing back to profit.

Net income attributable to controlling shareholders was KRW 150 million in 3Q 2026, and the four quarters from 4Q 2025 to 3Q 2026 sum to KRW 142 million, so quarterly swings in revenue and profit are the central feature of the earnings picture.

05

Industry analysis

Demand for core mobile network equipment and software tends to rise and fall with the capital expenditure cycles of mobile carriers. Eluon's annual revenue rose from KRW 56.6 billion in 2022 to KRW 68.5 billion in 2024 and then fell to KRW 61.6 billion in 2025, which reflects that cyclicality.

Whether demand for core network replacement emerges during the transition to next-generation mobile networks will depend on carriers' investment plans and the pace at which government digital infrastructure budgets are spent. The competitive landscape has two groups.

Large IT services companies such as Samsung SDS and LG CNS lead in SI scale and bidding capacity, while telecom specialists compete in core network equipment and solutions.

Eluon combines telecom solutions with storage and card-related systems, so its profit structure differs from others even within the IT and software sector.

Comparisons with platform-centered companies in the same sector such as NAVER and Kakao are not meaningful given the large differences in revenue structure and margin levels, so peer comparisons should center on telecom equipment and SI companies.

New businesses such as storage virtualization and IC card inspection machines depend heavily on overseas partner contracts, which makes the timing of revenue hard to predict. The company's position in the sector cycle can be gauged as carriers announce investment plans.

Its strength lies in its experience with core mobile network solutions, and its weaknesses are its scale and customer concentration.
