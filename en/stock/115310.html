02

Business structure

Founded in 2000 to provide value-added mobile and fixed-line telecom services and listed on KOSDAQ in 2010, INFOvine is an IT security company whose core business is the UBIKey mobile certificate storage service.

The service is operated in partnership with Korea's three mobile carriers, SK Telecom, KT, and LG Uplus, providing certificate storage and digital signature functions to banks, securities firms, card companies, insurers, e-commerce platforms, and public institutions.

Users can transfer a joint certificate (formerly the accredited certificate) stored on a PC to a smartphone app or USIM, with the service billed as a small monthly telecom add-on fee of KRW 990 (including VAT) on the carrier bill.

Core competencies cited include wired/wireless network integration technology, data encryption technology, and mobile application development capability. The business is described as a low-value telecom add-on service with income-inelastic characteristics, making it relatively less sensitive to economic cycles.

The company is classified as a small and medium-sized venture enterprise with two subsidiaries.

On the competitive front, private simple-authentication services that do not require a stored certificate—Kakaopay Certification, Naver Certificate, Toss Certificate, and the carrier-run PASS platform—have been expanding their footprint, coexisting alongside INFOvine's traditional role of storing and transmitting joint certificates.

Separately from its core business, the company has repeatedly been cited as the KOSPI/KOSDAQ-listed firm with the highest treasury share ratio, a feature that draws market attention independent of its operations.

04

Earnings analysis

Consolidated annual revenue rose for four consecutive years, from KRW 20.9bn in 2022 to KRW 22.8bn in 2023, KRW 25.2bn in 2024, and KRW 28.7bn in 2025.

Operating profit, however, climbed from KRW 6.9bn in 2022 to KRW 7.8bn in 2023 and KRW 7.9bn in 2024 before falling to KRW 6.5bn in 2025, with the operating margin declining sharply from 34.0% in 2023 and 31.3% in 2024 to 22.5% in 2025. The key driver of this margin decline is visible in the quarterly data.

In Q2 2025, the company posted revenue of KRW 7.3bn alongside an operating loss of KRW 0.32bn, temporarily swinging into the red, with net income collapsing to roughly KRW 30mn.

Profitability then recovered over four consecutive quarters: Q3 2025 (revenue KRW 7.2bn, operating profit KRW 2.39bn), Q4 2025 (KRW 7.1bn, KRW 2.10bn), Q1 2026 (KRW 6.9bn, KRW 1.92bn), and Q2 2026 (KRW 6.7bn, KRW 2.16bn), each generating operating profit in the roughly KRW 2.0bn range.

Quarterly revenue itself, however, has trended mildly lower over five consecutive quarters from KRW 7.3bn to KRW 6.7bn, meaning annual revenue growth and a recent quarterly slowdown are both visible in the data.

Net income attributable to owners rose from KRW 6.0bn in 2022 to KRW 7.2bn in 2023 and KRW 9.0bn in 2024, before declining to KRW 7.0bn in 2025, reflecting the impact of the weak Q2 2025 quarter on the full-year result.

On the balance sheet, the debt ratio remained low at roughly 8-10% throughout 2022-2025, while operating cash flow gradually declined from KRW 7.2bn in 2022 to KRW 4.7bn in 2025.

05

Industry analysis

Korea's digital signature and identity verification market has been restructured since the 2020 abolition of the accredited certificate system and its renaming to the joint certificate, with private simple-authentication services expanding rapidly since then.

PASS, operated jointly by the three mobile carriers, has reportedly been running a joint campaign to promote QR-based ID verification, expanding from identity confirmation toward broader digital identity infrastructure.

Kakaopay Certification, Naver Certificate, and Toss Certificate have also reportedly expanded their partnerships with public and financial institutions, adding to the range of alternatives that allow identity verification and digital signing without storing a joint certificate at all.

Despite this trend, INFOvine's UBIKey service is understood to retain demand as a storage and transmission tool in channels such as banking, securities, and insurance where joint-certificate-based transaction practices persist.

The steady annual revenue growth from 2022 through 2025 indicates that demand for certificate storage has not disappeared abruptly in the short term.

Still, the mild sequential decline in quarterly revenue over the past five quarters suggests that erosion pressure from expanding simple-authentication services may be gradually showing through.

The small add-on fee structure billed via telecom carriers acts both as an entry barrier and a friction point that makes cancellation less convenient, which is understood to result in relatively slow subscriber churn.

Within the industry, the company is positioned as a smaller niche player specializing in conservative channels that still require legacy certificate infrastructure, rather than competing head-on with large platform operators.

Outlook

No specific numerical guidance for future performance from the company has been identified.

However, based on confirmed disclosure history, the company announced treasury share acquisition plans for the purpose of 'share retirement to enhance shareholder value' three times in January, February, and April of 2025, with the actual purchases completed in February, April, and July respectively.

After a period during which the retirement timing was not clearly disclosed, market attention continued, and it is confirmed that a disclosure deciding on an actual share retirement was made in June 2026.

This can be interpreted as the company having acted, at least in part, on its previously flagged retirement intentions, though no further information on the retirement scale or the handling of remaining treasury shares has been confirmed.

On the policy front, legislative discussion of a Commercial Act amendment requiring treasury shares to be retired within one year of acquisition in principle has reportedly been under review in the National Assembly, with its final passage and implementation timing remaining undecided.

On the business side, continued competition in digital identity infrastructure—illustrated by the carriers' PASS QR-verification campaign—leaves as a medium-term watch point how well INFOvine can sustain its position in the legacy joint-certificate storage segment.

Bull factors

Earnings Recovery Trend

After a one-off operating loss in Q2 2025, operating profit recovered to roughly KRW 2.0bn per quarter over four consecutive quarters from Q3 2025 through Q2 2026. Net income also stayed above KRW 2.0bn each quarter during this period.

Steady annual revenue growth from 2022 through 2025 further underscores the continuity of the underlying business.

Low Leverage and Solid Balance Sheet

The debt ratio has remained low at roughly 8-10% throughout 2022-2025, giving the company substantial financial flexibility. Consistent annual operating cash inflows suggest limited need for external funding.

The small add-on fee-based revenue structure also carries the characteristic of being relatively less sensitive to economic cycles.

Treasury Share Retirement in Progress

After three treasury share acquisitions disclosed in 2025 for the stated purpose of retirement, an actual share retirement decision was confirmed to have been disclosed in June 2026.

Given that the company has repeatedly been cited as holding the highest treasury share ratio among KOSPI and KOSDAQ listed firms, the progress and scale of the retirement warrant further confirmation through subsequent disclosures.

Ongoing National Assembly discussion of a Commercial Act amendment mandating treasury share retirement has also drawn market attention in connection with this company.

Bear factors

Erosion Risk from Simple-Authentication Expansion

Simple-authentication services that do not require a stored joint certificate—Kakaopay Certification, Naver Certificate, Toss Certificate, and the carriers' PASS platform—have reportedly expanded their partnerships with public and financial institutions.

The three mobile carriers have recently run a PASS ID QR-verification campaign, extending beyond identity confirmation into broader digital identity infrastructure. If this trend continues, demand for INFOvine's traditional role of storing and transmitting joint certificates could decline over the long term.

Mild Downtrend in Quarterly Revenue

Quarterly revenue has declined slightly for five consecutive quarters, from KRW 7.3bn in Q2 2025 to KRW 6.7bn in Q2 2026. Separately from the annual revenue growth, the recent quarterly trend alone could be read as a signal of deceleration. If this pattern continues, it could eventually weigh on annual revenue growth as well.

Uncertainty Around Treasury Share Retirement

It has been noted that a considerable amount of time passed before the treasury shares acquired in 2025 for stated retirement purposes were actually retired. This was cited as a case of unusually slow execution relative to the market norm of prompt retirement following a purchase made for that purpose.

Although a retirement decision was disclosed in June 2026, the plan and timeline for handling the remaining treasury shares require further confirmation.

Risk factors

Regulatory and Technological Substitution Risk

INFOvine's core role as a joint-certificate storage service could structurally shrink if the government and financial institutions continue steering users toward simple authentication. The continued expansion of authentication services by large platforms—the three carriers, Kakaopay, Naver, and Toss—supports this risk. The pace and direction of related legal and regulatory change remain a variable requiring ongoing monitoring.

Telecom Carrier Dependency Risk

Because the revenue collection structure depends on the billing-based add-on service model of the three mobile carriers, changes in carrier add-on service policy or fee terms could affect the business.

The concentration of distribution channels among a small number of carriers can itself be a constraint on negotiating leverage. This structural dependency is an external variable that the company cannot control unilaterally.

Governance and Treasury Share Handling Risk

It has been pointed out that the industry-leading treasury share ratio could act as a potential overhang if the shares are not retired and are instead disposed of at some future point.

The fact that shares acquired for stated retirement purposes were held longer than expected in past instances also illustrates uncertainty around the handling plan. The final outcome of the related Commercial Act amendment discussion could change both the method and timing of how the shares are handled.

Overall view

INFOvine is a certificate storage service company that has maintained a stable revenue base as a small telecom add-on service through its partnership with Korea's three mobile carriers, with annual revenue growing steadily from 2022 through 2025 alongside a low debt ratio and solid cash generation.

At the same time, a recovery in profitability over four consecutive quarters following the one-off operating loss in Q2 2025 has coincided with a mild sequential decline in quarterly revenue itself over five consecutive quarters—two signals pointing in different directions.

On the business side, the expansion of simple-authentication services that do not require a stored certificate—Kakaopay Certification, Naver Certificate, Toss Certificate, and PASS—stands as a long-term competitive variable.

Beyond the financials, the industry-leading treasury share ratio and the actual retirement decision disclosed in June 2026 draw market attention, alongside the related National Assembly discussion on Commercial Act amendments governing treasury share handling.

On valuation, the stock appears to trade at a discount to net assets on a self-calculated basis, though this is a factual observation rather than a directional judgment.

Overall, the company presents a combination of business stability and financial soundness on one hand, and competitive landscape shifts and treasury share handling uncertainty on the other.
