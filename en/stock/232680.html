02

Business structure

Raon Robotics, founded in 2000, develops robots and automation systems and was renamed in January 2026.

Its core product is the vacuum transfer robot that moves semiconductor wafers inside high-vacuum, high-temperature process equipment, supplied on a turnkey basis together with atmospheric robots, aligners and control software.

The company says it secures more than 90% of its core robot technology and components in-house and aims for full internalization over the long term.

Its technical differentiation rests on an individual-control design that drives the two arms independently rather than through a linked structure, along with direct-drive (DD) motors.

Its main customers are domestic memory chipmakers, Samsung Electronics and SK hynix, and it also supplies U.S., Chinese and Japanese chip companies through equipment makers. Exports account for roughly 10% of sales and are mostly to China, and its domestic share is reported at 30 to 40%.

Vacuum transfer robots were long dominated by U.S. and Japanese suppliers, and the company is regarded as having localized the technology. Beyond semiconductors, it is expanding into transfer modules for flat-panel display lines, pharmaceutical and bio automation, and AI inspection equipment.

04

Earnings analysis

On the disclosed annual figures, revenue went from KRW 59.4 billion in 2022 to KRW 34.5 billion in 2023 (down 41.9% year on year), then rose to KRW 49.2 billion in 2024 and KRW 53.8 billion in 2025, increasing two years in a row from 2024 through 2025.

Among 2022 to 2025, 2022 was the largest year and 2023 the smallest. Operating profit fell from KRW 8.94 billion in 2022 to KRW 1.98 billion in 2023 (down 77.8%), then rose to KRW 2.49 billion in 2024 and KRW 3.95 billion in 2025, also two years in a row.

The operating margin was 15.0% in 2022, 5.7% in 2023, 5.0% in 2024 and 7.3% in 2025, a modest recovery from a low base. In 2023 the drop in operating profit (77.8%) far exceeded the drop in revenue (41.9%), which points to a sharp deterioration in profitability.

Profit attributable to controlling shareholders went from KRW 7.48 billion in 2022 to KRW 2.32 billion in 2023, KRW 5.23 billion in 2024 and KRW 3.69 billion in 2025 (down 29.4%), so in 2025 operating profit rose while attributable net profit fell.

The reasons for that divergence sit in non-operating items and income tax, which should be checked in the body of the periodic report. The debt ratio eased from 89.9% in 2024 to 87.2% in 2025, with total liabilities of KRW 31.2 billion and total equity of KRW 35.7 billion at year-end 2025.

Quarterly revenue rose from KRW 13 billion in the third quarter of 2025 to KRW 15 billion in the fourth quarter, dipped to KRW 14.2 billion in the first quarter of 2026, then reached KRW 18.2 billion and KRW 25.9 billion, rising for two consecutive quarters.

Press coverage linked the third-quarter jump to memory and foundry line-expansion orders being recognized as sales. Operating profit fell to KRW 450 million in the second quarter of 2026 (down 48.0% quarter on quarter) before rising to KRW 3.71 billion in the third quarter (up 724.0%).

Attributable net profit for the last four quarters totals KRW 5.65 billion. No one-off items were identified in the disclosures.

05

Industry analysis

In semiconductor front-end equipment, wafer transfer is a core process that governs throughput and precision, and vacuum robots in this segment were long led by U.S. and Japanese companies. Raon Robotics is reported to have localized the technology and to be embedded in the supply chains of domestic memory customers.

Demand in the segment depends on line expansions by domestic memory makers and on the spread of high-vacuum, high-precision processes, and the press linked the third-quarter results to expansion orders being recognized as sales.

The order backlog rose from KRW 29.32 billion in the first quarter of 2026 to KRW 52.35 billion in the second, which reads as a sign that the expansion cycle is showing up at the order stage.

Reports also noted that quarterly results can swing sharply depending on when orders are recognized and when deliveries take place.

In China, the company is targeting the local semiconductor robot market directly rather than only selling through domestic equipment makers, and reports say it passed a qualification test with a Chinese customer.

Chinese expansion could raise the export share but exposes the company to local competition and geopolitical factors. Robot-focused listed peers in the sector, including Doosan Robotics, Rainbow Robotics and Robotis, overlap only partly with vacuum transfer robots, so they are limited as direct benchmarks.

Within the domestic equipment ecosystem, the company positions itself on in-house component ratios and individual-control technology.

06

Outlook

The company has set a medium-term target of KRW 200 billion in revenue and a 60% export share by 2030.

The new plant covers a site of 4,499 square meters and a gross floor area of 23,861 square meters across one basement level and seven above ground, about three times the size of the existing Suwon site, with an investment of KRW 60 billion.

The plant is scheduled for completion in late 2027, with the headquarters and production facilities to move in early 2028. Once the new building is complete, development, testing and mass-production capacity for vacuum robots and transfer systems is expected to expand.

A company official said next-generation wafer transfer vacuum robots are in early prototype testing with customers, at the stage of verifying product performance and fit with customer equipment.

Efforts to tap China's semiconductor robot market are also reported to be moving forward, with individual-control vacuum robots said to meet local equipment makers' requirements. The company is also pursuing diversification into pharmaceutical and bio automation and AI inspection equipment.

On shareholder returns, KRW 4 billion of capital reserves were transferred to retained earnings in December 2025, followed by a capital-reserve dividend in March 2026 of KRW 200 per share, KRW 2.4 billion in total.

In October 2026 the board resolved to convene an extraordinary shareholders' meeting on November 12, 2026 to reduce capital reserves.

Because the new plant spending and capital-reserve dividends run in parallel and draw down internal reserves, the financing method and the completion schedule will shape the company's balance sheet going forward.

Bull factors

Quarterly results and backlog rising together

Third-quarter 2026 revenue was KRW 25.9 billion and operating profit KRW 3.71 billion, up 99.7% and 261.0% year on year. Press reports said the backlog rose from KRW 29.32 billion to KRW 52.35 billion between the first and second quarters and linked this to third-quarter sales. Annual operating profit also rose two years in a row from 2024 through 2025.

Technical differentiation and in-house components

Its vacuum robots use an individual-control design and direct-drive motors, and the company is regarded as having localized a market once dominated by foreign suppliers. It says it secures more than 90% of its core technology and components in-house. Its domestic share is reported at 30 to 40%.

Customer base widening via China and new products

Reports say the company passed a qualification test with a Chinese customer, and next-generation vacuum robots are in early prototype testing with customers. It has set a 2030 target of KRW 200 billion in revenue and a 60% export share. Exports are currently around 10% of sales, which leaves room to expand that segment.

Bear factors

Annual earnings base is still thin

The operating margin fell from 15.0% in 2022 to 7.3% in 2025, and attributable net profit dropped 29.4% to KRW 3.69 billion in 2025. Attributable net profit over the last four quarters totals KRW 5.65 billion, which is small relative to the swings seen in individual quarters. In 2025 operating profit rose while attributable net profit fell, so the quality of earnings needs scrutiny.

Volatility in quarterly profit

Second-quarter 2026 revenue rose to KRW 18.2 billion, yet operating profit fell 48.0% quarter on quarter to KRW 450 million. Third-quarter operating profit rebounded to KRW 3.71 billion, but reports noted that quarterly results can swing with order recognition and delivery timing. A single quarter makes it hard to read the trend.

Investment burden and shareholder-return funding

The KRW 60 billion plant investment is about 1.7 times equity of KRW 35.7 billion at the end of 2025. The debt ratio was 89.9% in 2024 and 87.2% in 2025, both high.

Capital-reserve dividends have run two years in a row, reducing internal reserves, and related parties including the CEO hold a stake in the mid-30% range, so dividend cash flows to the largest shareholder's family.

10

Risk factors

Customer concentration and capex cycles

Key customers are concentrated among domestic memory makers such as Samsung Electronics and SK hynix, so their capex scale and timing drive sales. Even with a larger backlog, a change in the capex cycle could quickly adjust quarterly revenue. A change in orders from a single customer can show up strongly in results.

China exposure and export dependence

Because most exports come from China, shifts in Chinese demand and regulation would flow directly into overseas sales. The company is pushing harder into the Chinese market, where local equipment and robot makers may compete intensely. Changes in U.S.-China technology rules or supply-chain policy could become variables for the export plan.

Plant execution and financing

The KRW 60 billion plant is scheduled for completion in late 2027 and a move in early 2028. The investment exceeds annual revenue and is about 1.7 times equity, so construction delays or cost overruns could weigh on finances. If next-generation robots move to mass production late, plant utilization would also be affected.

Overall view

Raon Robotics localized vacuum robots that move semiconductor wafers and supplies domestic memory customers such as Samsung Electronics and SK hynix.

Annual results since 2022 dipped in 2023, then revenue and operating profit rose two years in a row in 2024 and 2025, although the operating margin remains low at 7.3% in 2025 versus 15.0% in 2022.

Third-quarter 2026 revenue of KRW 25.9 billion and operating profit of KRW 3.71 billion rose sharply, and press reports tied this to a growing order backlog.

Whether this quarterly improvement carries into full-year 2026 results has to be checked against the fourth quarter onward, and attributable net profit over the last four quarters totals KRW 5.65 billion.

The KRW 60 billion new plant, scheduled for completion in late 2027, is large relative to equity, so financing and execution pace will shape the financial burden. Bullish factors are order growth and technical differentiation, while bearish factors are a thin earnings base, quarterly volatility and investment burden.

The next checkpoints are the preliminary 2026 full-year results, the 2026 annual report and progress on the new plant.
