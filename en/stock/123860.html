02

Business structure

Anapass is a fabless semiconductor company founded in 2002 that designs and supplies system chips for driving display panels.

Its core products are the timing controller (T-Con) that generates the video signals for the display and the TED (TCON Embedded Driver IC), which combines the T-Con and the display driver IC (DDI) into a single chip.

T-Cons are mainly used in OLED panels for IT devices such as tablets and notebooks, while TEDs are mainly used in OLED panels for smartphones and game consoles.

By share of cumulative sales through the third quarter of 2023, OLED T-Con and TED accounted for 77.5%, LCD T-Con for 0.7%, and other products for 21.8%, according to a January 2024 securities research report. Its main customer is Samsung Display, and production is outsourced to foundries such as UMC.

The company says it holds more than 150 patents. In competition, Anapass holds the top share in T-Cons for Samsung Display's IT OLED products, but was reported in early 2024 to rank third in smartphone OLED TEDs, behind Samsung Electronics' System LSI and Magnachip.

The company's profile lists automotive displays and AR/VR devices as new application areas. Because revenue follows customers' panel shipment volumes and the customer base is concentrated, production changes at one customer weigh heavily on results.

04

Earnings analysis

Annual results moved from an operating loss in 2022 to a peak in 2024, followed by lower sales in 2025. Sales rose from KRW 43.3 billion in 2022 to KRW 71.5 billion in 2023 (up 65.1% year on year), then to KRW 182.2 billion in 2024 (up 155.0%), the largest year in 2022 to 2025.

Sales in 2025 were KRW 108.2 billion, down 40.6% year on year. Operating results moved from an operating loss of KRW 7.02 billion in 2022 to operating profit of KRW 4.47 billion in 2023, which marked a turn to profit.

Operating profit then reached KRW 20 billion in 2024 (up 348.4%) and KRW 20.2 billion in 2025 (up 0.5%), rising two years in a row from 2024 to 2025. The operating margin went from -16.2% in 2022 to 6.3% in 2023, 11.0% in 2024 and 18.6% in 2025.

In 2025 operating profit held up while sales declined, which lifted the margin. Net income attributable to controlling shareholders, however, fell from KRW 18.9 billion in 2024 to KRW 2.71 billion in 2025, down 85.6%, so the gap between operating profit and net income widened.

In a December 2025 interview, the CEO said the company expected record operating profit for the year, but the confirmed 2025 operating profit of KRW 20.2 billion was close to the KRW 20 billion of 2024.

Quarterly, operating profit peaked at KRW 9.53 billion in Q3 2025 on sales of KRW 32.5 billion, then fell in Q4 2025 to KRW 3.06 billion on sales of KRW 27.7 billion (down 14.6% quarter on quarter).

In Q1 2026 sales fell to KRW 15.2 billion (down 45.1% quarter on quarter) and operating profit to KRW 480 million (down 84.3%). Sales recovered to KRW 18.1 billion in Q2 2026 (up 19.4%), but the company posted an operating loss of KRW 4.19 billion, a return to losses.

In Q3 2026 sales were KRW 26.6 billion (up 46.6% quarter on quarter, down 18.1% year on year) and operating profit was KRW 2.99 billion, a return to profit.

Net income attributable to controlling shareholders was a net loss of KRW 2.87 billion in Q2 2026 and KRW 634 million in Q3 2026, two consecutive loss-making quarters, after a KRW 3.9 billion profit in Q1 2026.

The sum of net losses attributable to controlling shareholders over the last four quarters was KRW 7.64 billion.

05

Industry analysis

The key driver for the end market is wider adoption of OLED panels. According to a securities report citing market researcher Omida, OLED penetration in the IT panel market was only 1.6% in 2023, and domestic display customers held about 86% share.

The same report estimated Anapass shipped about 5 million IT OLED T-Cons in 2023. As notebooks adopt more OLED, T-Con volumes rise, and brokerages see AI PC growth as a structural expansion of the IT OLED market.

On mobile, a report noted that domestic customers supply 300 million to 400 million mobile OLED panels a year. Anapass has supplied TEDs to some models, mainly in China and Taiwan, since 2018, but according to the report, chip shortages held back volume expansion.

A 2024 press report said mass-production volume of OLED TEDs for the Galaxy S24 FE had been secured, which raised hopes for a earnings recovery. In competition, Anapass is reported to hold the top share in IT OLED T-Cons at Samsung Display and third place in smartphone OLED TEDs.

In the cycle, foundry capacity shortages affected 2021 and 2022, and weak smartphone demand limited mobile OLED T-Con volumes in 2023. On the AI PC side, compatibility certifications with Intel, AMD, Qualcomm and NVIDIA have been accumulating.

Public data on the latest industry indicators is limited, so quarterly results are the practical way to read the cycle indirectly.

06

Outlook

The mid-term target the company has presented is sales of KRW 500 billion or more in 2027 and an operating margin of 15% to 20% (December 2025 press report, CEO interview). The 18.6% operating margin in 2025 sits within that range, but 2025 sales of KRW 108.2 billion are far below the target scale.

Securities analysts and company commentary point to wider smartphone TED share and higher IT OLED T-Con volumes driven by AI PCs as growth pillars.

On certifications, the company announced ISO 26262 functional safety certification for automotive display chips in March 2025, compatibility certification for NVIDIA GeForce RTX 50 series based on Blackwell in April 2025, and certification for NVIDIA Advanced Optimus dynamic display switch functionality in July 2025.

In May 2024 it announced official Intel AI PC partner certification. Automotive displays and AR/VR are presented as new application areas, but there is no quantitative data on their revenue share.

On shareholder returns, a securities report summary said the company decided after its March 2026 annual general meeting to buy back and cancel shares worth KRW 6.9 billion.

The next checkpoints are how the 2026 annual report presents the business plan behind the 2027 target, and whether the operating margin enters the 15% to 20% range in 2027 quarterly results.

Bull factors

Accumulating AI PC certifications for IT OLED T-Cons

Certification of compatibility with AI PC chipmakers such as Intel, Qualcomm and NVIDIA has broadened the supply base for notebook T-Cons. A market study cited for 2023 put OLED penetration in IT panels at only 1.6%, which bulls use to argue there is room for expansion. The company's top share in T-Cons for Samsung Display's IT OLED products is also cited as a base of business.

Room to expand smartphone TED share

In smartphone OLED TEDs the company ranked third, and share gains were cited as the key to earnings in an early 2024 report. A 2024 press report said mass-production volume for OLED TEDs in the Galaxy S24 FE had been secured, which raised hopes of recovering annual sales of KRW 100 billion. The 18.6% operating margin in 2025 can be read as an example of profitability improving when volumes rise.

Earnings recovery and a stronger balance sheet

Operating results moved from a KRW 7.02 billion operating loss in 2022 to KRW 20.2 billion in operating profit in 2025. The debt ratio fell from 172.8% in 2022 to 63.7% in 2025, and operating cash flow in 2025 was KRW 28.9 billion.

A securities report summary said a share buyback and cancellation worth KRW 6.9 billion was decided after the March 2026 annual general meeting.

Bear factors

Volatile quarterly earnings

Operating profit was KRW 480 million in Q1 2026 and there was an operating loss of KRW 4.19 billion in Q2 2026, far below the KRW 9.53 billion peak in Q3 2025. Net losses attributable to controlling shareholders have continued for two straight quarters since Q2 2026, and the four-quarter total is KRW 7.64 billion. The return to operating profit in Q3 2026 is a single quarter, so it is hard to confirm as a trend.

Concentration on one customer and outsourced production

The main customer is Samsung Display alone, and OLED T-Con and TED accounted for 77.5% of sales through the third quarter of 2023. Production is outsourced to foundries such as UMC, so supply timing and costs are exposed to outside factors. Foundry capacity shortages affected the company in 2021 and 2022.

Lower sales and net income, plus competition

Sales in 2025 were KRW 108.2 billion, down 40.6% year on year, and net income attributable to controlling shareholders fell 85.6%. In smartphone OLED TEDs the company ranks third behind Samsung Electronics' System LSI and Magnachip, according to an early 2024 report. Operating profit rose only 0.5% in 2025, so the rise in profit did little to offset the drop in sales.

10

Risk factors

Customer concentration risk

Dependence on Samsung Display volumes is high, so any change in the customer's panel production plans directly affects sales. The swings in quarterly results in 2026 can be read in connection with volume changes at this customer. The pace of customer diversification is a key variable for earnings stability.

Track record of affiliate investment losses

A 2024 report said the company had already recognized about KRW 100 billion of equity-method losses tied to equity investments and loans. Whether the losses tied to affiliate GCT Semiconductor still weigh on the balance sheet needs to be checked in the asset and equity-method items of periodic filings. The effect of affiliate results on consolidated profit and loss also needs to be reviewed.

Product generation transition risk

TEDs are chips that drive smartphone OLEDs, so product demand can shift if customers change panel architecture or chip integration. Because volume tends to concentrate on specific product generations, supply gaps during generation changes can amplify earnings swings. The pace at which competitors gain share is another variable.

Overall view

Anapass is a fabless company that designs timing controllers and TED chipsets and supplies them to Samsung Display.

Its earnings capacity recovered from an operating loss in 2022 to KRW 20.2 billion in operating profit and an 18.6% operating margin in 2025, and the lower debt ratio of 63.7% is confirmed in its disclosed results.

On the other side, 2025 sales fell 40.6%, net income attributable to controlling shareholders fell 85.6%, and quarterly operating profit swung to KRW 480 million in Q1 2026 and an operating loss of KRW 4.19 billion in Q2 2026.

Operating profit returned to the black in Q3 2026 at KRW 2.99 billion on sales of KRW 26.6 billion, but the net loss attributable to controlling shareholders of KRW 634 million continued.

Strengths are accumulated certifications for IT OLED T-Cons in AI PCs, room to expand smartphone TED share, and a stronger balance sheet. Weaknesses are dependence on Samsung Display, quarterly earnings volatility, and a history of affiliate investment losses.

The company's 2027 targets of sales above KRW 500 billion and an operating margin of 15% to 20% are far from current results, so the 2026 annual report and 2027 quarterly results are the next checkpoints.
