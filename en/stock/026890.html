02

Business structure

STIC Investments was founded in 1982 and listed on the KOSPI market in 1997 as a private equity fund (PEF) and venture capital manager. Its main investment areas include new industries, promising companies in Southeast Asia, and infrastructure and real estate assets in Korea and abroad.

At the end of 2025, assets under management stood at about KRW 10.31 trillion, of which PEF commitments were the largest at KRW 7.60 trillion, followed by venture investment funds at KRW 772.6 billion and general private equity trusts at KRW 1.94 trillion.

Revenue falls into four lines: management fees, performance fees, investment income, and management advisory fees. Management fees rose 6% to KRW 63.6 billion in 2025, while performance fees, reported at KRW 15.4 billion, increased 75%.

Performance fees are earned when a fund's internal rate of return exceeds the hurdle rate and are recognized mainly when investments are realized, so their size varies widely from year to year. In 2021, fund liquidations produced performance fees of about KRW 84.5 billion.

The fund liquidated on September 30, 2026, a KRW 121 billion vehicle, disclosed a performance fee of KRW 906 million for the company. After a revision of the Capital Markets Act eased PEF restrictions, the company moved into credit-related business and has continued forming funds in that area.

Earlier blind funds drew on National Pension Service and teachers' mutual aid associations as major limited partners. Among competitors, the company is noted for being the first PEF manager listed on the KOSPI.

04

Earnings analysis

On disclosed annual results, revenue was KRW 60.4 billion in 2022, KRW 93.8 billion in 2023, KRW 79.5 billion in 2024, and KRW 91.2 billion in 2025. 2023 was the largest year in the period and 2022 the smallest.

Operating profit rose from KRW 10.3 billion in 2022 to KRW 33.1 billion in 2023, an increase of 220.0%, then fell to KRW 13.4 billion in 2024, a decline of 59.5%, before recovering to KRW 17.2 billion in 2025, up 28.1%.

In 2023 operating profit grew far faster than revenue, which rose 55.1%, indicating a marked improvement in profitability. In 2024 revenue fell 15.2% while operating profit fell 59.5%, showing how sensitive earnings are to the revenue base.

Operating margins were 17.1% in 2022, 35.3% in 2023, 16.9% in 2024, and 18.8% in 2025. Net profit attributable to controlling shareholders was KRW 15.8 billion in 2022, KRW 27.6 billion in 2023, KRW 9.32 billion in 2024, and KRW 15 billion in 2025.

Net profit including non-controlling interests, a separate measure, was KRW 14.9 billion in 2025. The debt-to-equity ratio moved from 9.3% in 2022 and 12.7% in 2023 to 6.4% in 2024, then rose to 20.3% in 2025.

At the end of 2025, total equity was KRW 254.3 billion, of which KRW 254.2 billion belonged to controlling shareholders, and total liabilities were KRW 51.5 billion.

Operating cash flow was negative KRW 25.9 billion in 2022, positive KRW 32.1 billion in 2023, negative KRW 21 billion in 2024, and negative KRW 5.74 billion in 2025, so every year except 2023 was negative.

Quarterly revenue was KRW 17.9 billion in 3Q 2025 and KRW 29.9 billion in 4Q 2025, up 66.7% from the prior quarter, then KRW 16.8 billion in 1Q 2026, KRW 20.3 billion in 2Q 2026, and KRW 14.5 billion in 3Q 2026, down 19.3% year on year.

Operating profit peaked at KRW 14.1 billion in 4Q 2025 before falling to KRW 4.58 billion in 1Q 2026 and KRW 3.06 billion in 2Q 2026, and the company posted an operating loss of KRW 516 million in 3Q 2026.

Net profit attributable to controlling shareholders over the last four quarters, from 4Q 2025 to 3Q 2026, totaled KRW 17.8 billion. Press reports said performance fees were zero in 1Q 2025, when equity-method losses widened to KRW 12 billion and the company swung into a loss.

05

Industry analysis

The private equity and venture capital market in which STIC Investments operates has been widening its scope since the Capital Markets Act was revised. The company says that after PEF restrictions were eased, it moved into credit-related funds to meet rising corporate funding demand.

In cycle terms, the company said that in 1Q 2025 it concentrated on new investments while realizations fell, which limited the recognition of performance fees. That pattern suggests performance fees can swing sharply from quarter to quarter when investment and harvesting phases overlap.

The contrast between KRW 84.5 billion of performance fees from large fund liquidations in 2021 and KRW 15.4 billion in 2025 illustrates this cycle difference.

Compared with brokerage-led financial holding companies such as Mirae Asset Securities and NH Investment & Securities, the company's earnings depend more on its asset base and fee structure than on balance sheet size. On competitive positioning, the company is noted as the first PEF manager to list on the KOSPI.

Corporate funding demand and participation by pension funds and mutual aid associations are cited as variables that shape the pace of fund formation.

06

Outlook

On January 19, 2026, the company disclosed its 2026 corporate value-up plan. The plan targets assets under management of KRW 15 trillion and fee-paying assets under management (FPAUM) of KRW 11 trillion.

For profitability, it set an FRE margin of 35% or higher, and for medium-term performance it set a target of 20% annual total shareholder return. Given assets of about KRW 10.31 trillion at the end of 2025, the asset targets imply growth from the current base.

Non-financial goals include raising compliance with corporate governance guidelines to 60% or higher. The compensation framework will shift toward share-based incentives to align employees with shareholders. A March 27, 2026 filing showed three new outside directors, lifting the outside director ratio to 62.5%.

On November 12, 2026, the company announced a KEMI ESG Awards 2026 excellence prize for its sustainability report. The next checkpoint is whether assets under management and performance fees in the 2026 annual report track these targets. On quarterly results, the question is in which quarter performance fees return after the 3Q 2026 loss.

08

Bull factors

Asset-growth target

The corporate value-up plan disclosed in January 2026 targets KRW 15 trillion in assets under management and KRW 11 trillion in fee-paying assets. With about KRW 10.31 trillion at the end of 2025, growth in assets would widen the base for management fees. Management fees rose 6% to KRW 63.6 billion in 2025, supporting steadier revenue.

Upside from realization-phase fees

Performance fees arise when a fund clears its hurdle rate and investments are realized, and KRW 84.5 billion was recognized in a large 2021 liquidation. Press reports said performance fees reached KRW 15.4 billion in 2025, up 75%.

Realizations continued with the September 2026 fund liquidation, where the company's share was disclosed at KRW 906 million.

Earnings recovery and margins

Operating profit rose 28.1% to KRW 17.2 billion in 2025 from KRW 13.4 billion in 2024, and the operating margin improved from 16.9% to 18.8%. Net profit attributable to controlling shareholders rose 61.4% to KRW 15 billion in 2025, a recovery after the 2024 decline.

According to an aggregator's tally, operating profit and net profit both swung to a profit in 1Q 2026 from a year earlier.

Bear factors

Loss in 3Q 2026

The company posted an operating loss of KRW 516 million in 3Q 2026, and its net result attributable to controlling shareholders turned to a loss of KRW 231 million. Revenue fell 19.3% year on year to KRW 14.5 billion.

Net profit attributable to controlling shareholders dropped from KRW 3.65 billion in 1Q 2026 to KRW 1.73 billion in 2Q before turning negative in 3Q.

Timing dependence of performance fees

Performance fees were KRW 15.4 billion in 2025 but zero in 1Q 2025, and they concentrate in periods of heavy realizations. The company's share of the September 2026 fund liquidation was KRW 906 million, a small part of the fund's KRW 18.9 billion in profit. Because performance fees follow realization timing, quarterly earnings are hard to forecast.

Direction of financial indicators

The debt-to-equity ratio rose from 6.4% in 2024 to 20.3% in 2025, and total liabilities were KRW 51.5 billion at year-end 2025. Operating cash flow was negative in both 2024 (KRW 21 billion outflow) and 2025 (KRW 5.74 billion outflow).

Equity-method results also swing with portfolio valuations, as seen when equity-method losses widened to KRW 12 billion in 1Q 2025.

10

Risk factors

Performance fees and exit timing

Performance fees cluster around fund liquidations and asset realizations, so revenue can fall sharply in quarters where exits are delayed. Zero performance fees in 1Q 2025 illustrate this. Until a large fund's liquidation is disclosed, the size of quarterly performance fees is hard to gauge.

Shareholder structure and governance

Press reports point to a change in the largest shareholder, and the company's ownership structure is in transition. A related report describes a small shareholder group calling for treasury share cancellation and a bonus issue.

The higher outside director ratio and the governance compliance target are the company's stated responses to these issues.

Regulation and valuation gains or losses

The revised Capital Markets Act widened PEF business scope, but results in newer areas such as credit may vary with operating experience and risk controls. The company said that in 1Q 2025 accounting valuation losses on some portfolio holdings were reflected in results.

Changes in the valuation of investment assets move quarterly results through non-operating and equity-method income.

Overall view

STIC Investments' earnings combine a stable management-fee base with volatile performance fees. On disclosed results, operating profit rose to KRW 17.2 billion in 2025, and the operating margin was 18.8%. However, the company posted an operating loss of KRW 516 million in 3Q 2026, and revenue declined year on year.

The January 2026 value-up plan sets targets of KRW 15 trillion in assets and an FRE margin of 35% or higher, but these are plan targets rather than results. The debt-to-equity ratio rose to 20.3% at the end of 2025 and operating cash flow was negative in 2025, both of which warrant attention.

The next checkpoints are the operating margin and cash flow in the 2026 annual report, and the quarter in which performance fees are recognized.
