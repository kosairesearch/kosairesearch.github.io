Vina Tech: Supercapacitor Demand and Quarterly Earnings Swings

02

Business structure

Vina Tech is a KOSDAQ-listed components maker whose main products are supercapacitors and membrane electrode assemblies (MEA) for hydrogen fuel cells.

A supercapacitor is an energy storage device that delivers large bursts of power; according to the company, its energy capacity is about one-hundredth that of a secondary battery, while its output is roughly 100 times higher.

That profile makes it a complement to secondary batteries, and the company says it began mass-producing 3.0V supercapacitors in 2010, a world first. In fuel cells, Vina Tech highlights vertical integration from support and catalyst materials through to MEA, and offers both PEMFC and DMFC MEA products.

Target applications include AI data centers, smart grids, industrial machinery and fast-charging infrastructure, as well as building-use and aviation fuel-cell systems. A May 2025 contract will supply supercapacitors for AI data centers to Bloom Energy through 2027.

In June 2025 the company won an order from MTU Aero Engines worth USD 1.3 million for aviation MEA, slated for MTU's 600kW Flying Fuel Cell project. It also signed a supply contract with Doosan Mobility Innovation for building-use fuel-cell MEA. The company says about 90% of its revenue comes from overseas. Its plants are located in the Jeonju and Wanju Techno Valley areas of North Jeolla Province.

04

Earnings analysis

Annual results began in 2022 with revenue of KRW 70.7 billion, operating profit of KRW 9.35 billion and net income attributable to controlling shareholders of KRW 11.7 billion.

Revenue fell to KRW 55 billion in 2023, down 22.2%, while operating profit dropped to KRW 3.04 billion, down 67.5%, and net income attributable to controlling shareholders fell to KRW 1.98 billion, down 83.1%.

In 2024 revenue rose 8.4% to KRW 59.6 billion, but the company swung to an operating loss of KRW 8.07 billion, and the net loss attributable to controlling shareholders reached KRW 9.09 billion.

In 2025 revenue grew 38.0% to KRW 82.2 billion, the largest of the four years, and operating profit returned to the black at KRW 1.77 billion. The net loss attributable to controlling shareholders narrowed to KRW 5.85 billion, so losses continued at a smaller scale.

Operating margin moved from 13.2% in 2022 to 5.5% in 2023, -13.5% in 2024 and 2.2% in 2025, so the revenue recovery has not restored margins to the 2022 level. Operating cash flow was KRW 11.4 billion in 2022 and KRW 13.1 billion in 2023, then -KRW 3.42 billion in 2024 and -KRW 6.06 billion in 2025.

By quarter, Q3 2025 showed revenue of KRW 23 billion, operating profit of KRW 2.57 billion and net income attributable to controlling shareholders of KRW 2.53 billion.

Q4 2025 revenue was KRW 22.8 billion, down 1.0% quarter on quarter, but operating profit fell to KRW 660 million, down 74.3%, and the company posted a net loss attributable to controlling shareholders of KRW 3.34 billion. Q1 2026 revenue was KRW 25.4 billion, up 11.3%, with an operating loss of KRW 1.54 billion.

Q2 2026 revenue rose 39.5% to KRW 35.4 billion, the largest quarter in the period shown, but operating profit was KRW 498 million while the net loss attributable to controlling shareholders was KRW 38.1 billion.

A gap that large between operating profit and the bottom line points to non-operating items, and whether they were one-off should be checked in that quarter's report notes. Q3 2026 revenue was KRW 23.8 billion, down 32.6% quarter on quarter and up 3.6% year on year.

An operating loss of KRW 2.23 billion marked a swing into loss from a year earlier, yet net income attributable to controlling shareholders reached KRW 1.13 billion, suggesting non-operating items offset the operating loss. Over the last four quarters, net losses attributable to controlling shareholders total KRW 41.9 billion.

05

Industry analysis

Vina Tech's two businesses draw on different demand pools. Supercapacitors play a role that complements secondary batteries, serving applications that need large power output over short periods.

The company argues that supercapacitors are needed to stabilize power grids alongside AI data-center power supply, so demand channels widen as data-center electricity needs grow.

KB Securities, in a March 2023 report, placed Vina Tech at about a 20% share, ranked first, of the global medium-sized supercapacitor market; that assessment dates from March 2023.

In hydrogen fuel-cell MEA, applications split across automotive, aviation and building use, and the company promotes vertical integration covering support, catalyst and MEA production as its differentiator.

Secondary-battery peers such as LG Energy Solution, Samsung SDI, Ecopro BM and L&F focus on large cells and cathode materials, so their product structures overlap with Vina Tech's only to a limited extent.

Cell and materials makers' earnings track electric-vehicle and ESS orders, while Vina Tech's track data-center and fuel-cell orders. Sector-index movements therefore offer limited guidance on the direction of Vina Tech's earnings.

In supercapacitors, competition with overseas producers on technology and price is the central variable, and in hydrogen MEA, fuel-cell system order volumes determine demand.

06

Outlook

The company has set a target of KRW 1 trillion in revenue within five years, positioning AI data centers as its new growth engine. The central route to that target is supercapacitor supply to Bloom Energy, under a contract running through 2027.

On the fuel-cell side, an aviation MEA order for MTU's FFC project came in June 2025, and a building-use fuel-cell MEA supply contract was signed with Doosan Mobility Innovation.

On capacity, the Wanju Techno Valley plant expansion has moved forward in stages, and when the second Wanju plant broke ground, a plan to invest KRW 86 billion through 2024 was announced. How the company funds further expansion will affect its shareholder structure alongside its earnings.

Revenue of KRW 35.4 billion in Q2 2026 and an operating loss of KRW 2.23 billion in Q3 show that quarterly volatility remains high.

The next checkpoints are the Q4 2026 earnings release and the 2026 annual report, and whether the operating margin rises from 2.2% in 2025 will be the gauge of how well the expansion is paying off.

Operating profit returning to the black at KRW 1.77 billion in 2025 also supports the view that the earnings base has improved.

Hydrogen fuel-cell MEA orders

Bear factors

Accumulated losses and cash flow

Operating cash flow was negative in 2024 and 2025, at -KRW 3.42 billion and -KRW 6.06 billion. Operating profit of KRW 498 million in Q2 2026 gave way to an operating loss of KRW 2.23 billion in Q3, so profitability at the operating level has swung from quarter to quarter.

Rising debt burden

Total liabilities stood at KRW 124 billion at the end of 2025, above total equity of KRW 78.2 billion. The debt-to-equity ratio climbed from 118.7% in 2022 to 158.5% in 2025, passing through 102.3% in 2023 and 109.9% in 2024.

If capacity expansion and fuel-cell investment continue on this balance sheet, financial room could narrow further.

Thin operating margin

The operating margin was only 2.2% in 2025, so a larger revenue base converted into little profit. The margin fell to -13.5% in 2024, the only year of operating loss among the four years shown. Quarterly revenue in 2026 has ranged from KRW 23.8 billion to KRW 35.4 billion, and profits have swung with it.

Risk factors

Customer and regional concentration

About 90% of revenue comes from overseas, so exchange rates and changes in overseas customers' orders feed directly into won-denominated results. If orders from a few major customers slow, the impact on earnings could be large. Trade conditions and overseas regulation could also affect export volumes.

Commercialization timing

Fuel-cell MEA orders may take time to turn into revenue, depending on development and mass-production schedules. Aviation parts may face long certification and production processes. Hydrogen demand also depends heavily on government policy and subsidies, so an order does not guarantee immediate sales growth.

Financing and shareholder dilution

Operating cash flow was negative in 2024 and 2025, so reliance on outside funding could increase. Funding capacity expansion through share issuance could dilute existing shareholders. A KB Securities report from March 2023 flagged possible overhang issues after funding for capacity expansion.

Overall view

Vina Tech is building order books in two areas: supercapacitors for data centers and hydrogen fuel-cell MEA. Annual profit returned in 2025 and revenue expanded, but the net loss attributable to controlling shareholders of KRW 5.85 billion shows losses continued.

Net income attributable to controlling shareholders turned positive in Q3 2026, yet the operating loss in the same quarter was KRW 2.23 billion, and net losses over the last four quarters total KRW 41.9 billion.

On the financial side, the debt-to-equity ratio rose to 158.5% in 2025, the highest of the four years, and operating cash flow was negative in both 2024 and 2025. Earnings-based multiples cannot be applied given the net losses of the last four quarters, and the stock trades at a premium to net assets.

Strengths include data-center and aviation or building-use orders and the Q3 2026 return to positive net income, while weaknesses include accumulated losses, debt burden and quarterly earnings volatility. The next checkpoints are the Q4 2026 results release, the 2026 annual report, and Bloom Energy supply progress.
