02

Business structure

Iljin Diamond makes synthetic industrial diamond and has been listed on the KOSPI since December 2004. According to JobKorea company information as of March 2026, diamond grit and related products account for 87% of sales, and hard metal products for 13%.

Diamond grit is a core material for abrasives and cutting and grinding tools, so demand moves with the operating rates and production flows of downstream manufacturing.

According to company listing data, the Vietnam unit (ILJIN DIAMOND VINA) handles both manufacturing and sales, while the China, Europe, US and Japan units handle sales. A Canadian sales subsidiary was established in May 2026.

The consolidated group includes Iljin Hysolus, which makes lightweight composite CNG and hydrogen tanks, with assets of KRW 342 billion as of June 2026.

Magma Tool, a seller of powder metallurgy products, is also a consolidated subsidiary, so consolidated results combine the industrial diamond core business with the hydrogen materials business. The company competes with domestic and overseas makers in synthetic diamond and hard metal markets.

Its largest shareholder and related parties held 51.59% as of October 19, 2026, and Park Sung-jin, a senior managing director, took over as CEO in March 2026.

04

Earnings analysis

Revenue fell 17.3% to KRW 154.5 billion in 2023, the smallest year in the disclosed period, and then rose 1.7% to KRW 157.1 billion in 2024 and 3.4% to KRW 162.4 billion in 2025, a two-year rise.

Operating income of KRW 3.27 billion in 2022 turned into an operating loss of KRW 4.37 billion in 2023, and the losses widened to KRW 4.64 billion in 2024 and KRW 6.79 billion in 2025, making three consecutive years of operating losses from 2023 to 2025.

The operating margin slid from 1.8% in 2022 to -2.8% in 2023, -3.0% in 2024 and -4.2% in 2025. Revenue grew in 2024 and 2025 while the loss did not narrow, so sales growth has not translated into margin improvement.

Net income attributable to controlling shareholders swung from a loss of KRW 6.71 billion in 2022 to a profit of KRW 9.99 billion in 2023, rose to KRW 12.9 billion in 2024, then fell 46.8% to KRW 6.86 billion in 2025.

Net income including non-controlling interests, at KRW 5.79 billion in 2025, is a different figure and was lower than KRW 13.6 billion in 2024. The reasons net income turned positive during operating-loss years should be traced to non-operating items and taxes, which the notes to the periodic reports break down.

Operating cash flow was KRW 1.15 billion in 2022, rose to KRW 2.48 billion in 2024, and turned to an outflow of -KRW 268 million in 2025. Quarterly results moved from an operating loss of KRW 3.62 billion in Q3 2025 to operating profit of KRW 1.03 billion in Q4 2025.

Operating profit then stood at KRW 435 million in Q1 2026 and KRW 7.05 billion in Q2 2026, a 1,519.1% quarter-on-quarter increase. Q3 2026 revenue of KRW 52.3 billion fell 4.9% from the prior quarter but rose 7.7% year on year, and operating profit of KRW 2.39 billion turned positive from a year earlier.

Net income attributable to controlling shareholders over the latest four quarters totals KRW 15.1 billion, and the Q3 2026 figure of KRW 1.71 billion fell 78.2% from the prior quarter.

05

Industry analysis

Synthetic industrial diamond is a material used in abrasives and cutting and grinding tools, and demand moves with capital spending and operating rates in downstream industries.

Downstream demand spans metal and ceramic processing, electronic component processing, and construction and stone processing, so differences in cycles across these industries show up in results.

Diamond grit accounts for 87% of sales according to JobKorea company information as of March 2026, which means the company has substantial exposure to the core demand cycle. Sales rose gradually in 2024 and 2025 after the 2023 low, which suggests the company is in an early recovery phase in revenue terms.

However, the operating margin did not improve even as sales grew, so raw material costs, selling prices and the fixed cost base appear to drive margins. On profitability, the ability to pass through costs and plant utilization are the key variables.

The hydrogen infrastructure business at the subsidiary Iljin Hysolus follows CNG and hydrogen tank markets and runs on a different cycle from the core business. The company competes with domestic and overseas synthetic diamond makers on price and quality, and an expanding overseas sales network broadens its reach.

Overall industry demand is affected by interest rates, exchange rates and capital spending cycles, so the short-term direction is difficult to pin down.

Outlook

The corporate value-up plan the company disclosed on April 22, 2026 aims to pursue stable dividend payments, taking into account its past dividend history and dividend yield at the market price.

The company said it will change the dividend process so that the dividend amount is fixed first and the record date is set afterwards. The company stated that it qualifies as a high-dividend company under the Restriction of Special Taxation Act, and its 2025 payout ratio was 62.1%.

The total dividend amount for 2025 was KRW 4.26 billion, the same as for 2024. The annual general meeting on March 19, 2026 approved the financial statements for the 22nd fiscal year and the cash dividend resolution.

On the business side, the key checkpoint is whether the infrastructure expansion at the hydrogen division of Iljin Hysolus resumes and on what timetable, since the company cited that delay as a reason for the 2025 operating loss.

Quarterly profit was KRW 7.05 billion in Q2 2026 and fell to KRW 2.39 billion in Q3 2026, so the level that continues in coming quarters is the point to watch. Q4 and full-year 2026 results will be confirmed through the 2026 annual business report.

Based on the prior year's filing cycle, that report is expected around March 2027. The Canadian sales subsidiary established in May 2026 widens the company's overseas sales footprint.
