02

Business structure

Shinsegae International is a fashion and beauty company within the Shinsegae group that is reshaping its portfolio around fashion and cosmetics. Its fashion division imports and distributes overseas brands and also runs its own labels.

Press reports say overseas fashion sales in 3Q 2026 were 3.5 times domestic fashion sales. Overseas labels such as Brunello Cucinelli, Rick Owens, UGG and Herno drove sales growth in 1Q 2026.

Its own fashion label Studio Tomboy generates about KRW 100 billion in annual sales, which is small next to the leading brands of rival fashion companies. The cosmetics division combines own brands such as Yeonjak and Bidibi with imported fragrances and luxury beauty products.

Press reports say the cosmetics share of sales rose from 23% in 2022 to 41% in 2025 and reached 43.2% in 1H 2026. Imported cosmetics sales are about 2.5 times in-house cosmetics sales, which keeps reliance on foreign brands high.

In January 2026 the company transferred its Jaju living-goods unit to a Shinsegae group home-furnishing affiliate to concentrate on fashion and beauty. Department stores accounted for 42% of 2023 sales, with large discount stores and duty-free as the other main channels. Listed peers in domestic fashion include Handsome, F&F and LF.

04

Earnings analysis

Annual sales went from KRW 1.55 trillion in 2022 to KRW 1.35 trillion in 2023 (down 12.8% year on year), KRW 1.31 trillion in 2024 (down 3.4%) and KRW 1.11 trillion in 2025 (down 15.2%). Sales fell three years in a row from 2023 through 2025, with 2022 the largest year and 2025 the smallest in the 2022–2025 period.

Operating profit fell from KRW 115.3 billion in 2022 to KRW 48.7 billion in 2023 (down 57.7%) and KRW 26.8 billion in 2024 (down 44.9%), and a KRW 11.5 billion operating loss in 2025 turned the company loss-making. Operating margin moved from 7.4% in 2022 to 3.6%, 2.1% and -1.0%.

Net income attributable to controlling shareholders fell from KRW 118.3 billion in 2022 to KRW 39.2 billion in 2023 (down 66.8%), KRW 32.5 billion in 2024 (down 17.1%) and KRW 4.12 billion in 2025 (down 87.3%).

Net income including non-controlling interests was KRW 118.8 billion in 2022, KRW 39.6 billion in 2023, KRW 32.7 billion in 2024 and KRW 4.12 billion in 2025.

In 2023 contracts with six imported brands, including Celine, ended after they announced direct entry, and fashion and lifestyle sales fell 18% that year according to a beauty trade publication. In 2025 the company said own-brand results were temporarily adjusted during the Bidibi relaunch and channel streamlining.

Quarterly, the company posted an operating loss of KRW 2.03 billion in 3Q 2025 and KRW 11.8 billion in 4Q 2025, when losses widened.

Operating profit of KRW 14.8 billion in 1Q 2026 turned positive, but 2Q 2026 profit of KRW 6.96 billion fell 53.0% from the prior quarter and 3Q 2026 profit of KRW 5.15 billion fell 26.1%, marking two straight quarterly declines. Press reports say 1Q 2026 cosmetics sales of KRW 124 billion were a quarterly record.

05

Industry analysis

Domestic fashion and beauty distribution combines imported overseas brands with the development of in-house labels. The company attributed 3Q 2026 growth to rising demand for luxury fashion, niche fragrances and luxury beauty. Imported cosmetics sales rose 20% in 1Q 2026 and 14.8% in 3Q 2026.

Growth led by imported brands tends to carry lower margins than own brands because of royalty structures, an industry source has noted, which is why global expansion of own IP is cited as a medium-term task. Duty-free was affected by slower Chinese consumption.

Bidibi once topped KRW 200 billion in annual sales on demand from Chinese shuttle buyers, but has been hit by the Chinese slowdown and a weaker duty-free channel. Department stores and large discount stores remain the main domestic channels.

Within the sector the company is compared with Handsome, F&F, LF and Youngone, and it has narrowed its focus to fashion and beauty by exiting non-core living goods.

06

Outlook

In a February 2026 report the company said it would pursue higher sales and profitability at the same time by strengthening own-brand competitiveness in global markets on a reorganized portfolio. It listed new brand development, strategic M&A and stronger digital platforms as its growth plans.

The overseas push for Yeonjak and Bidibi is described as being in a channel-expansion stage. In 3Q 2026 own-brand cosmetics sales rose 18.4%, above the 14.8% growth in imported cosmetics. One analysis notes that faster own-brand growth does not by itself change their contribution to sales.

In fashion, strengthening own labels such as Studio Tomboy and Bo is still an open task. Press reports say Studio Tomboy, IL and Man on the Bun supported 1Q 2026 results.

The next checkpoint is how 4Q 2026 operating profit compares with the KRW 11.8 billion loss in 4Q 2025, and how large own brands become relative to imported brands.

Bull factors

Cosmetics scale-up

Press reports say the cosmetics share of sales rose from 23% in 2022 to 41% in 2025 and reached 43.2% in 1H 2026. In 3Q 2026 own-brand sales including Yeonjak and Bidibi grew 18.4%, and imported cosmetics rose 14.8%. Cosmetics sales of KRW 124 billion in 1Q 2026 were reported as a quarterly record.

Overseas fashion demand

Overseas fashion brand sales reportedly grew 27.9% in 3Q 2026, with Herno and Rick Owens cited as key labels. Enfant Riche Deprimee and Bu-Oh more than doubled sales. Imported fashion sales had also risen 35.2% in 1Q 2026.

Earnings recovery path

Operating profit moved from a KRW 11.8 billion loss in 4Q 2025 to KRW 14.8 billion in 1Q 2026. 3Q 2026 operating profit of KRW 5.15 billion also swung positive from a year-earlier loss. The January 2026 transfer of the Jaju unit is cited as a factor reshaping the earnings base.

Bear factors

Annual earnings decline

Sales fell from KRW 1.55 trillion in 2022 to KRW 1.11 trillion in 2025. The 2025 operating loss of KRW 11.5 billion turned the company loss-making, and attributable net income fell 87.3% to KRW 4.12 billion. Sales declined three years in a row from 2023 to 2025, so no annual recovery has yet appeared.

Slowing quarterly momentum

After KRW 14.8 billion in 1Q 2026, 2Q profit of KRW 6.96 billion fell 53.0%. 3Q profit of KRW 5.15 billion fell another 26.1% from the prior quarter, a second straight quarterly decline. The company is profitable again, but operating profit has been lower since the 1Q peak.

Import reliance and own-brand weakness

Imported cosmetics sales are about 2.5 times in-house cosmetics sales, a high reliance on foreign brands. Press reports say own-brand sales fell 38% in 2025, and Bidibi was hit by the Chinese slowdown and weaker duty-free. Industry observers note that import-led growth can lower margins because of royalty structures.

Risk factors

China demand and duty-free channel

Bidibi once topped KRW 200 billion in annual sales on Chinese shuttle-buyer demand, but has since been hit by the Chinese slowdown and a weaker duty-free channel. The company has cut low-margin duty-free sales as part of a restructuring. Whether Chinese demand and duty-free recover remains a variable for own-brand cosmetics.

Brand contract volatility

In 2023 six imported brands including Celine ended their contracts after announcing direct entry, cutting fashion sales sharply. With overseas fashion sales at 3.5 times domestic fashion sales, the departure of a major brand increases earnings volatility. Retention of import rights is a key variable for both fashion and cosmetics.

Financial leverage burden

The debt-to-equity ratio rose from 53.4% in 2022 to 70.5% in 2025, with total liabilities of KRW 592.7 billion at end-2025. Operating cash flow was KRW 26 billion in 2024 and KRW 29.6 billion in 2025, below KRW 110 billion in 2022 and KRW 114.8 billion in 2023. Rising leverage alongside a smaller earnings base is the point to monitor.

12

Overall view

Shinsegae International is changing its business structure around fashion and cosmetics, with both its sales mix and its earnings base in transition. Sales have fallen since 2022 to KRW 1.11 trillion in 2025, and operating profit and attributable net income have also declined.

FY2025 closed with an operating loss of KRW 11.5 billion. Operating profit of KRW 14.8 billion in 1Q 2026 and KRW 5.15 billion in 3Q 2026 extended the profit run, but two straight quarterly declines since 2Q require monitoring.

The rise of cosmetics to 41% of sales and the growth of own brands Yeonjak and Bidibi are positive factors. Heavy reliance on imported brands and weakness in own brands remain negatives. A price-to-book ratio well below 1x alongside a rising debt-to-equity ratio should be read with equal weight.

Dividend capacity depends on how quickly attributable net income recovers. The next checkpoints are 4Q 2026 and full-year preliminary results, and the audited figures in the 2026 annual report.
