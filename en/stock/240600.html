02

Business structure

Yujin Technology makes precision molds, notching presses and other equipment and parts used in secondary battery production lines.

It supplies a package of equipment and parts across the battery manufacturing process, including notching presses, precision molds and cutter cartridges, from its Cheongju headquarters, while growing its automation equipment business at the Hwaseong Technical Campus.

Its main customers are domestic battery cell makers including LG Energy Solution and Samsung SDI, and the company says it has maintained long-term cooperation with both.

It runs production bases in Poland, Hungary, the United States and China, matching technical support and parts supply to customers' local production lines.

The notching equipment it unveiled in March 2026 runs stably at 250 to 300 cpm and is being developed to handle up to 400 cpm, while its notching molds keep burrs below 5 μm, according to the company.

Lead tabs are a new product line built on proprietary surface-treatment technology, but sales were only about KRW 200 million in 2025, press reports said.

The company targets KRW 50 billion in lead tab sales by 2029 and KRW 100 billion in total sales within five years by growing its automation equipment business centered on Hwaseong.

The Hwaseong plant consolidation was completed by June 2026, and the company says it cuts fixed costs by roughly KRW 2.4 to 2.5 billion a year. Sales recovery is being led by domestic mold and machinery businesses, the company said.

On competition, the company points to precision and local response capability in overseas markets as its differentiators.

04

Earnings analysis

Annual sales went from KRW 39.8 billion in 2022 to a peak of KRW 47.5 billion in 2023, a 19.3% increase, before falling to KRW 31.1 billion in 2024 (down 34.5%) and KRW 28.7 billion in 2025 (down 7.9%), marking two straight years of decline from 2024 through 2025.

Of the 2022 to 2025 period, 2023 was the largest year and 2025 the smallest, and the company attributed the 2025 drop to customers cutting equipment investment and adjusting production as electric vehicle demand slowed.

Operating profit rose from KRW 2.38 billion in 2022 to KRW 6.38 billion in 2023, a 167.5% increase, then turned into an operating loss of KRW 3.93 billion in 2024 and widened to KRW 7.78 billion in 2025.

The operating margin moved from 13.4% in 2023 to -12.6% in 2024 and -27.1% in 2025, and operating losses have continued for two consecutive years.

Net income attributable to controlling shareholders swung to a loss of KRW 1.81 billion in 2023 and reached a loss of KRW 7.77 billion in 2025, making three straight years of losses.

The 2023 swing was tied to a one-off derivative liability valuation loss of about KRW 7.1 billion, according to press reports, and was separate from operating performance.

Operating cash flow went from KRW 10.7 billion in 2023 to -KRW 1.11 billion in 2024 and -KRW 5.1 billion in 2025, while the debt ratio rose to 54.4% in 2025 from 43.2% in 2024; total equity at end-2025 was KRW 38.4 billion and total liabilities KRW 20.9 billion.

Quarterly sales rose to KRW 9.54 billion in the fourth quarter of 2025 (up 25.2% from the prior quarter) before falling to KRW 7.76 billion in the first quarter of 2026 (down 18.7%).

They then rebounded to KRW 9.6 billion in the second quarter (up 23.6%) and KRW 10.2 billion in the third quarter (up 5.8%), two straight quarters of growth, with third-quarter sales up 33.3% year on year.

The operating loss narrowed from KRW 1.46 billion in the third quarter of 2025 to KRW 793 million in the second quarter of 2026 before widening to KRW 1.14 billion in the third quarter, and the company credited Hwaseong fixed-cost savings for part of the second-quarter improvement.

The third-quarter 2026 loss attributable to controlling shareholders of KRW 2.78 billion exceeded the operating loss, suggesting non-operating items widened the loss, and the four-quarter total is KRW 7.22 billion.

05

Industry analysis

The end market is tied closely to the battery investment cycle, especially electric vehicle batteries. In 2025, electric vehicle sales in Europe fell as subsidies were cut and demand stalled, and the company said the average utilization rate of the three major Korean battery makers dropped into the 50% range.

In 2026 the company said it would expand local supply through its Ohio and Michigan subsidiaries in step with the restart of major North American battery plants.

Demand is broadening from electric vehicles toward ESS and LFP batteries, and the company is extending precision mold and equipment technology it verified domestically to its US and European subsidiaries. Supplying core parts for prismatic-cell lines in ESS production is also presented as a priority.

Demand combines new equipment orders with repeat demand from existing production lines, and the company plans to raise repeat demand to support revenue stability and profitability.

On competition, the company cites notching mold precision and lead tab surface-treatment technology as strengths, and says four overseas subsidiaries are back to normal operations. Because equipment spending depends on customers' capacity decisions, shifts in end-market conditions can pass into revenue quickly.

Outlook

The company said it expects results to improve further in the second half of 2026, citing relief from Hwaseong fixed costs, a return to profit at overseas subsidiaries and wider ESS orders.

Third-quarter 2026 sales rose to KRW 10.2 billion, but the operating loss widened to KRW 1.14 billion from the second quarter, so profit-level improvement is not yet clear on a third-quarter basis.

On orders, the company said it received a paid sample request from a new battery maker in Georgia in the United States and is preparing a cutter cartridge usage test at a battery plant in Windsor, Ontario, Canada. It also presented plans to expand parts supply through its North American subsidiaries.

For lead tabs, the company set a target of KRW 50 billion in sales by 2029 and KRW 100 billion in total sales within five years including automation equipment.

In a shareholder letter sent on July 21, 2026, the company set out four priorities: strengthening its core business, expanding investor relations, raising shareholder value and stronger management accountability. On July 13, 2026 it decided on a bonus share issue to improve stock liquidity.

The Hwaseong plant consolidation was completed by June 2026, and the company says cost savings began to show in results from July. The next checkpoints are whether the operating loss in the fourth quarter of 2026 improves from the third-quarter level and whether ESS line orders show up in the sales trend.

08

Bull factors

Recovery in quarterly sales

From a low of KRW 7.76 billion in the first quarter of 2026, sales rose to KRW 9.6 billion in the second quarter and KRW 10.2 billion in the third, two straight quarters of growth, with third-quarter sales up 33.3% year on year. The company attributes the recovery to its domestic mold and machinery businesses. Whether this sales recovery carries through to profit is the test for this bull case.

Fixed-cost restructuring and margin gains

The company says the Hwaseong plant consolidation, completed by June 2026, should cut fixed costs by roughly KRW 2.4 to 2.5 billion a year.

Gross margin for the first half of 2026 was 15.3%, up from 8.6% a year earlier, and the operating loss narrowed from KRW 1.69 billion in the first quarter to KRW 793 million in the second. The narrowing in the second quarter is consistent with the cost-savings effect the company describes.

Overseas recovery and new North American demand

In July 2026 the company said four overseas subsidiaries in the United States, Poland, Hungary and China have returned to normal operations. A paid sample request from a new battery maker in Georgia and preparations for a cutter cartridge test at a Windsor, Canada plant are cited as early signs of new demand.

Extending LFP battery mold technology verified in Korea to overseas subsidiaries is also cited as a positive factor.

Bear factors

Five straight quarters of operating losses

Operating losses have continued for five consecutive quarters from the third quarter of 2025 through the third quarter of 2026, and the third-quarter 2026 operating loss of KRW 1.14 billion was wider than KRW 793 million in the second quarter.

The third-quarter loss attributable to controlling shareholders of KRW 2.78 billion widened from a year earlier, and the four-quarter total is KRW 7.22 billion. The failure of sales growth to carry through to profit is the largest bearish factor.

EV demand slowdown and customer capex cuts

Sales fell for two consecutive years in 2024 and 2025, and the company attributes the 2025 decline to customers cutting capital spending and adjusting production. Press reports cite falling electric vehicle sales in Europe and low battery plant utilization as major causes of the earnings deterioration. Operating cash flow turning negative in 2024 and 2025 also shows exposure to customers' investment cycles.

Gap between new-business targets and results

Press reports said lead tab sales were about KRW 200 million in 2025, far below the KRW 2.5 billion target set at the IPO for 2025. Production volume was reported at around 1 million sets a month, about half the target level. Delays in the overseas expansion strategy were also cited as contributing to weaker results.

Risk factors

End-market capex cycle and customer concentration

Sales depend heavily on capital spending decisions by electric vehicle and battery makers, and in 2025 revenue fell as customers cut equipment investment. The shift toward LFP and ESS batteries is an opportunity but also requires changing a structure built around existing customers.

If investment plans at major customers such as LG Energy Solution and Samsung SDI change, revenue swings could widen.

Listing maintenance and market value threshold

Press reports say the KOSDAQ market value threshold for delisting rose to KRW 20 billion from July 2026 and is set to rise to KRW 30 billion from January 2027. Current market value is low against both thresholds, which makes listing maintenance a key risk.

In July 2026 the company issued a shareholder letter pledging to focus on maintaining its listing and restoring corporate value.

Financial capacity and cash flow

Total equity at end-2025 was KRW 38.4 billion, but operating cash flow fell to -KRW 5.1 billion in 2025 from -KRW 1.11 billion in 2024. Cash and cash equivalents were reported at KRW 6.27 billion and the order backlog at KRW 9.5 billion at the end of the first half of 2026. Whether cash cushions shrink if operating losses continue is the next item to monitor.

Overall view

Yujin Technology is a company centered on secondary battery precision molds and notching equipment, and sales have risen for two straight quarters in 2026, showing a recovery trend. Third-quarter 2026 sales were KRW 10.2 billion, up 33.3% from a year earlier.

However, operating losses have continued for five consecutive quarters since the third quarter of 2025, and the third-quarter 2026 loss attributable to controlling shareholders of KRW 2.78 billion was wider than a year earlier.

On an annual basis, sales have fallen in 2024 and 2025 after peaking in 2023, and operating profit turned into losses from 2024. The Hwaseong plant consolidation and overseas subsidiary recovery are presented as grounds for cost improvement, but the pace of profit improvement is not yet clear on a third-quarter basis.

Bullish factors include the sales recovery, fixed-cost savings and early signs of new North American and European demand, while bearish factors include continued losses, swings in end-market capital spending, listing maintenance thresholds and cash flow strain.

The two sides carry comparable weight, so whether quarterly profit returns to positive territory is the most important checkpoint.
