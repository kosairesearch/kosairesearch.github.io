02

Business structure

EXA E&C began as a specialty construction and interior company that supplied premium office partitions and systems furniture, and has since widened into cleanroom construction for advanced manufacturing sites that cannot stop operating.

Its business is organized into three divisions: the specialty construction division, which builds cleanrooms for semiconductor and display plants; the systems division, which handles system ceilings and partitions used in data centers; and the interior division.

In the first half of 2026, construction-segment revenue on a consolidated basis reached KRW 70.6 billion, up 88.7% from a year earlier, based on the semi-annual report as reported in the press.

The increase followed the ramp-up of construction at Samsung Electronics' Pyeongtaek campus and SK hynix's Yongin semiconductor cluster. Its main customers are semiconductor manufacturers, and the P5 work at Pyeongtaek was won through a contract with Samsung C&T.

Press reports credit two subsidiaries with product development and wider sales channels, and say the Vietnam unit improved on construction orders tied to a display maker and to Samsung Electronics and Samsung Electro-Mechanics.

Competitive positioning is hard to compare on disclosed figures alone, so it is best understood through the company's record of work under large general contractors.

At the end of 2025, total equity was KRW 83.9 billion, of which equity attributable to controlling shareholders was KRW 68.9 billion, and total liabilities were KRW 74.4 billion.

04

Earnings analysis

Annual figures from the regular filings show sales falling from KRW 228.1 billion in 2022 to KRW 179.9 billion in 2023, a 21.1% decline, before recovering to KRW 203.1 billion in 2024, up 12.9%, and slipping to KRW 177 billion in 2025, down 12.8%. Across 2022 to 2025, 2022 was the largest year and 2025 the smallest.

Operating profit fell from KRW 9.32 billion in 2022 to KRW 3.68 billion in 2023, down 60.5%, rose to KRW 8.51 billion in 2024, up 131.0%, and then dropped to KRW 2.51 billion in 2025, down 70.6%.

The operating margin was 4.1% in 2022, 2.0% in 2023, 4.2% in 2024 and 1.4% in 2025, so profitability moved far more than revenue did.

Net income attributable to controlling shareholders was KRW 7.54 billion in 2022, turned into a net loss of KRW 757 million in 2023, returned to profit at KRW 6.75 billion in 2024, and fell 90.6% to KRW 632 million in 2025. Net income including non-controlling interests was KRW 1.16 billion in 2025.

The quarterly pattern was more volatile. An operating loss of KRW 145 million in Q3 2025 became an operating profit of KRW 2.35 billion in Q4 2025, followed by KRW 1.04 billion in Q1 2026 and KRW 673 million in Q2 2026, before an operating loss of KRW 926 million in Q3 2026.

Sales rose from KRW 47.1 billion in Q3 2025 to KRW 52 billion in Q4 2025 and KRW 56.1 billion in Q1 2026, then declined to KRW 50.1 billion in Q2 2026 and KRW 42.4 billion in Q3 2026, down 10.0% year on year.

Press reporting on the Q2 2026 results estimated that cost of sales grew faster than revenue, which compressed the operating margin. For 2025, the filing attributes the sales decline to lower construction work at the parent company, while standalone sales at subsidiaries grew.

Operating cash flow rose from KRW 2.43 billion in 2023 to KRW 5.73 billion in 2024 and KRW 7.54 billion in 2025, above the 2025 net income of KRW 1.16 billion. The debt-to-equity ratio was 88.8% at the end of 2025, down from 102.4% in 2024.

05

Industry analysis

The company's end market is investment in semiconductor manufacturing facilities. Samsung Electronics' Pyeongtaek campus P4 and P5 and the first phase 3CUB works at SK hynix's Yongin semiconductor cluster are identified as major ongoing projects.

A May 2026 press report argued that firms with advanced industrial facility construction capabilities could benefit as large companies resume investment and conditions in semiconductors and data centers improve. The position of the industry cycle is hard to pin down with certainty.

Press coverage does consistently show that orders are concentrated in large semiconductor projects led by Samsung Electronics and SK hynix. Analytical reporting also noted that quarterly profit can swing with construction progress and cost recognition.

On data centers, the company describes preparing system ceiling work in response to AI adoption and rising cloud demand. The Honam semiconductor cluster is a project pursued by the government and major companies, and the company has said it will look for new opportunities there.

Positioning against peers is hard to measure on disclosed figures, so within the verified scope, its record of work alongside large general contractors such as Samsung C&T is presented as the differentiator. The same dependence means results hinge on the timing of orders from those contractors.

06

Outlook

In a November 2026 report, the company said it plans to secure additional orders in line with the expansion of semiconductor investment and to strengthen its project execution capacity.

In July 2026 it signed a contract with Samsung C&T for Samsung Electronics' Pyeongtaek campus P5 Ph1 interior works, Section 1, worth KRW 89 billion, with a contract period from July 2026 to October 2027. Press coverage described this as the largest single order in the company's history.

Compared with 2025 consolidated sales of KRW 177 billion, the contract value is roughly half of one year's revenue, so how revenue from the work is recognized across quarters is the next checkpoint. The company has also said it is pursuing new opportunities, including the Honam semiconductor cluster.

Press reporting in May 2026 said demand for system ceilings in data centers is rising and that commercial revenue from them is approaching. Reports describe the Vietnam unit as improving, but whether it has fully returned to profit still needs confirmation.

With operating and net losses both recorded in Q3 2026, the speed at which new orders convert into quarterly profit is the central variable. The company says it is tightening cost and profitability management by project. The conversion of backlog into revenue and the trend in cost ratios will be visible in upcoming results.

08

Bull factors

Large contract adds a revenue base

A KRW 89 billion contract for Pyeongtaek P5 Ph1 interior works, Section 1, signed in July 2026, was reported as the largest single order in the company's history. The contract period runs to October 2027, so construction revenue is structured to continue through that window.

Q1 2026 sales of KRW 56.1 billion were the highest quarter in the filings, an example of large projects lifting the revenue base.

Profit recovery since Q4 2025

Operating profit moved from an operating loss of KRW 145 million in Q3 2025 to an operating profit of KRW 2.35 billion in Q4 2025, and KRW 1.04 billion in Q1 2026. Net income attributable to controlling shareholders was KRW 3.06 billion in Q4 2025 and KRW 1.98 billion in Q1 2026, two profitable quarters in a row. The shift from loss to profit, and the recovery that followed, is the bullish point in the record.

System ceilings widen the business scope

The company is developing system ceilings for data centers as a new growth area and says customer inquiries are rising. A May 2026 press report said the product is approaching commercial revenue as AI adoption and cloud demand grow.

Operating cash flow rose from KRW 2.43 billion in 2023 to KRW 5.73 billion in 2024 and KRW 7.54 billion in 2025, a sign that core operations are generating more cash.

09

Bear factors

Two straight loss-making quarters

Q3 2026 recorded an operating loss of KRW 926 million and a net loss attributable to controlling shareholders of KRW 2.82 billion, the second straight quarter of losses since Q2 2026. Q3 2026 sales of KRW 42.4 billion fell 15.4% from the prior quarter, so revenue also declined for two quarters in a row. Profit and sales both turned down after the Q1 2026 peak, which is the main bearish point.

Thin margins relative to sales

Sales for 2025 were KRW 177 billion, down 12.8% from the prior year, and the operating margin fell to 1.4% from 4.1% in 2022. Recent quarters show cost pressure holding down margins even as sales grew, which is the bearish point.

Concentrated orders and dependence on client timing

Orders are concentrated in large semiconductor projects led by Samsung Electronics and SK hynix, so client order timing and construction progress drive results. The 2025 operating profit decline of 70.6% was attributed to lower construction work at the parent company.

A business tied to one industry's investment flow is more exposed when that investment slows, which is the bearish point.

10

Risk factors

Construction progress and cost risk

Analytical reporting noted that revenue recognition on large projects shifts quarter to quarter with construction progress and cost recognition. Operating profit fell 35.0% from the prior quarter in Q2 2026 and turned into a loss in Q3 2026, which may reflect this volatility. How the backlog is replenished after the P5 contract period ends in October 2027 is the next question.

Balance sheet and ownership risk

Total liabilities at the end of 2025 were KRW 74.4 billion against total equity of KRW 83.9 billion, a debt-to-equity ratio of 88.8%. According to a corporate data provider as of 27 July 2026, the largest shareholder group holds 20.88% of shares. The same source puts the free-float ratio at 79.12%.

Execution risk in new businesses

System ceilings for data centers and the Honam semiconductor cluster have not yet shown up in reported results, so there may be a gap between expectations and actual revenue.

Press coverage of the cumulative figures through Q3 2026 notes that consolidated operating profit was below standalone operating profit, which points to losses remaining at subsidiaries. Whether the Vietnam unit has returned to profit still needs confirmation.

Overall view

EXA E&C's sales rose to KRW 56.1 billion in Q1 2026 as large semiconductor construction work expanded, and a KRW 89 billion order in July 2026 broadened its project base. Profitability has not yet stabilized, however.

The operating margin for 2025 was 1.4%, and Q3 2026 recorded an operating loss of KRW 926 million and a net loss attributable to controlling shareholders of KRW 2.82 billion, extending the two-quarter losing streak.

Net income attributable to controlling shareholders over the latest four quarters totaled KRW 2.01 billion, a small base. On the balance sheet, the debt-to-equity ratio was 88.8% at the end of 2025, and operating cash flow of KRW 7.54 billion in 2025 exceeded net income.

Bullish points include the working relationship with large general contractors, system ceilings for data centers, and the profit recovery since Q4 2025.

Bearish points include quarterly earnings volatility from construction progress and cost swings, dependence on semiconductor investment timing, and concentrated ownership. The next checkpoints are the operating margin in the 2026 annual report, Q1 2027 results, and progress on the P5 contract.
