02

Business structure

LMS was founded in 1999 to research, develop and manufacture optical lenses and optical elements, and listed on KOSDAQ in 2007.

Its core product is prism sheets, optical films used in LCD backlight units, which made up 93.1% of product sales in a WiseReport breakdown, with diffraction gratings, wave plates and blue filters accounting for the remaining 6.9%.

By segment, the display division was 94% of revenue in Q2 2026, the optical information division 5% and the bio division 1%, according to Butler data. WiseReport said the optical information division grew as multi-camera adoption expanded, though profitability weakened on cost burdens and higher expenses.

Exports are a large share, with 86.8% of prism sheet sales and 52.3% of blue filter sales in the year ended December 2025 generated overseas. The company acquired a 40% stake in Changgang Chemical in 2019 and has three Chinese subsidiaries. Research and development spending equaled 10.5% of revenue in 2025.

WiseReport said the company holds cost and responsiveness advantages through its proprietary technology and focuses on a small and mid-sized display optics market it describes as oligopolistic.

Company profiles describe expansion into AR/VR optical components and secondary-battery protection circuits, but the revenue contribution of these new businesses is a point to track in segment disclosures and quarterly reports.

04

Earnings analysis

On a disclosed annual basis, revenue was largest in 2022 at KRW 98.9 billion and smallest in 2024 at KRW 67.7 billion. Revenue declined to KRW 75.8 billion in 2023 (down 23.4% year on year) and KRW 67.7 billion in 2024 (down 10.6%), then edged up to KRW 69.6 billion in 2025 (up 2.8%).

Operating profit of KRW 1.46 billion in 2022 turned into an operating loss of KRW 8.6 billion in 2023, narrowed to an operating loss of KRW 3.67 billion in 2024, and widened to an operating loss of KRW 9.62 billion in 2025. The operating margin was 1.5% in 2022, -11.4% in 2023, -5.4% in 2024 and -13.8% in 2025.

Net income attributable to controlling shareholders of KRW 267 million in 2022 became a net loss of KRW 13.1 billion in 2023, followed by a net loss of KRW 4.62 billion in 2024 and KRW 12.1 billion in 2025, and net income including non-controlling interests moved in the same direction.

According to Hankyung market financial data, the gross margin fell from 29.85% in 2024 to 18.33% in 2025. Revenue rose slightly while gross margin declined, which points to a larger operating loss.

Total equity at the end of 2025 was KRW 72.8 billion against total liabilities of KRW 77.5 billion, and the debt-to-equity ratio fell from 136.9% in 2022 to 90.2% in 2023 before rising to 106.3% in 2025.

Operating cash flow was KRW 10.2 billion in 2022, KRW 13.9 billion in 2023 and KRW 6.01 billion in 2024, then turned to -KRW 23.1 billion in 2025.

Quarterly, Q3 2025 revenue of KRW 17.8 billion came with an operating loss of KRW 2.66 billion, and Q4 2025 revenue rose to KRW 18.9 billion (up 6.2% from the prior quarter) with an operating loss of KRW 2.04 billion and a net loss attributable to controlling shareholders of KRW 4.23 billion.

Q1 2026 revenue reached KRW 22.3 billion (up 18.0% quarter on quarter), with an operating loss of KRW 836 million and net income attributable to controlling shareholders of KRW 3.69 billion.

Q2 2026 revenue was KRW 26.7 billion (up 19.4%), with operating profit of KRW 1.67 billion and net income attributable to controlling shareholders of KRW 3.76 billion.

Q3 2026 revenue was KRW 22.8 billion (down 14.4% from the prior quarter but up 27.9% year on year), with an operating loss of KRW 851 million and a net loss attributable to controlling shareholders of KRW 9.67 billion.

The net loss attributable to controlling shareholders over the last four quarters totaled KRW 6.45 billion, and the gap between operating and net results in several quarters makes non-operating items and income tax worth checking in the quarterly reports.

05

Industry analysis

LMS's end market centers on optical film demand for LCD backlight units, and its main product line consists of optical components for displays in smartphones, TVs and tablets. WiseReport said the display division in the 2025 fiscal year was affected by an imbalance between supply and demand and falling prices.

The same source described the division's exposure to small and mid-sized oligopolistic markets as keeping volatility low. The optical information division grew as multi-camera adoption spread, but cost burdens weakened its profitability.

When prices are falling, both unit prices and volumes are affected, so the shift from a Q2 2026 revenue rise to a Q3 2026 decline is worth separating into product volume and price effects. With 86.8% of prism sheet sales generated overseas, the company is exposed to overseas display demand and exchange-rate moves.

The company is described as holding cost and responsiveness advantages through proprietary technology, and its 2025 research and development spending equaled 10.5% of revenue.

Peers in the same electronics and components sector, such as Samsung Electro-Mechanics and LG Innotek, have broader business scopes, so direct product-level comparisons are limited. As a company concentrated on a narrow optical film product line, LMS is more directly exposed to industry cycles in its reported results.

Bull factors

Directional shift in quarterly results

Net income attributable to controlling shareholders was KRW 3.69 billion in Q1 and KRW 3.76 billion in Q2, positive in both quarters.

The appearance of profitable quarters within a loss-making year reads as one quarter of evidence for a recovery in earnings.

Concentrated product niche and cost competitiveness

WiseReport described the display division as focused on small and mid-sized oligopolistic markets, which it said kept volatility low. The company is described as holding cost and responsiveness advantages through its proprietary technology.

Because prism sheets account for 93.1% of product sales, whether the core product keeps its market position is the central variable for earnings stability.

Balance sheet and room for business expansion

The debt-to-equity ratio fell from 136.9% in 2022 to 90.2% in 2023, rose to 106.3% in 2025, and remains below its 2022 level. Total equity was KRW 72.8 billion and total liabilities were KRW 77.5 billion at the end of 2025.

If AR/VR optical components and secondary-battery protection circuits gain traction, the product mix would broaden, which is cited as a bull factor.

Risk factors

Industry and pricing risk

Prism sheets, the core product, are tied to LCD demand and unit prices, so continued declines in panel prices could push the gross margin lower. The fall in the gross margin to 18.33% in 2025 shows how this pressure has already reached reported results. How far new businesses can offset that pressure remains difficult to gauge at this stage.

Cash flow and balance sheet risk

Operating cash flow turned to -KRW 23.1 billion in 2025, and total liabilities of KRW 77.5 billion are close in size to total equity of KRW 72.8 billion. If cash generation does not recover while operating losses continue, borrowing capacity and investment funding could come under pressure.

Whether the profitable quarters in 2026 are reflected in cash flow is an item to check in the quarterly reports.

Earnings quality and currency risk

The gap between operating and net results widened sharply in several quarters, which makes the quality of earnings difficult to judge. In Q1 and Q2 2026 net income was well above operating profit or loss, while in Q3 2026 the net loss was much larger than the operating loss. With a high overseas sales share, exchange-rate movements are also an item to monitor.

Overall view

LMS is an optical film company centered on prism sheets, and an operating profit in Q2 2026 and a return to an operating loss in Q3 2026 occurred within the same year.

On an annual basis, net losses attributable to controlling shareholders continued for three consecutive years from 2023 through 2025, and the 2025 operating loss widened to KRW 9.62 billion. Operating cash flow also turned negative at -KRW 23.1 billion in 2025.

On the other side, the debt-to-equity ratio declined from 136.9% in 2022 to 106.3% in 2025, and the company has cost competitiveness based on proprietary technology and a product line in a small and mid-sized oligopolistic niche.

PER cannot be calculated and the price-to-book ratio is low, so asset value and cash flow have become the center of comparison rather than earnings. The revenue contribution of new businesses and the direction of display prices are variables that current disclosures can only partly gauge.
