Mcnex: Auto Cameras Gain Weight as Mobile Slows

Third-quarter 2026 operating profit fell sharply from a year earlier as mobile demand softened, while automotive cameras and actuators remain the main longer-term growth pillar.

02

Business structure

Mcnex designs and manufactures camera modules for smartphones and automobiles. It also designs and produces its own actuators, the drive components that handle autofocus and optical image stabilization inside the module.

According to a Daishin Securities report, mobile phone applications made up 74% of revenue and automotive and other applications 26% in Q1 2026. For the first three quarters of 2024, the split was 70% mobile and 30% automotive, so the two periods are measured on different bases and are not directly comparable.

Mobile customers include Samsung Electronics' Galaxy S and A series and Chinese smartphone makers. Automotive customers include Hyundai Motor and KIA, and the company also cites European and Chinese automakers as customers. The company says it ranks first in Korea and fifth globally in automotive camera modules.

In Korean smartphone camera modules, it says it ranks third behind LG Innotek and Samsung Electro-Mechanics. Press reports say it mass-produces more than 6 million ADAS and autonomous-driving cameras a year.

A smartphone carries two or three cameras, while a car is increasingly fitted with dozens, which underpins the automotive expansion. The company says it has invested 4% to 7% of sales in R&D for two decades.

Competition in mobile is driven by price and volume, while automotive parts face demanding reliability requirements that raise entry barriers.

04

Earnings analysis

On a DART annual-report basis, sales were KRW 1.11 trillion in 2022, KRW 932.5 billion in 2023 (down 15.9% year on year), KRW 1.06 trillion in 2024 (up 13.4%), and KRW 1.28 trillion in 2025 (up 21.0%).

After the 2023 low, sales rose in 2024 and 2025, two consecutive years of growth, and 2025 was the largest year of 2022 to 2025.

Operating profit went from KRW 10.7 billion in 2022 to KRW 18.2 billion in 2023 (up 70.3%), KRW 44.4 billion in 2024 (up 143.5%), and KRW 52 billion in 2025 (up 17.2%), rising three years in a row from 2023 to 2025. The operating margin climbed from 1.0% in 2022 to 4.2% in 2024 and eased to 4.1% in 2025.

The CEO attributes the margin gains to the rising share of high-value camera modules. Net income attributable to controlling shareholders was KRW 23 billion in 2022, KRW 27.9 billion in 2023, and KRW 63.6 billion in 2024 (up 127.8%), the peak year, before falling to KRW 51.6 billion in 2025 (down 18.9%).

Net income including non-controlling interests was KRW 51.6 billion in 2025. Quarterly operating profit peaked at KRW 11.4 billion in Q1 2026 (up 38.9% from the prior quarter), then fell to KRW 2.19 billion in Q2 (down 80.8% quarter on quarter) and KRW 2.51 billion in Q3 (down 62.7% year on year).

Q3 sales were KRW 303.5 billion... more precisely KRW 311.6 billion, up 2.7% year on year. Net income attributable to controlling shareholders fell for two consecutive quarters, from KRW 14.6 billion in Q1 to KRW 793 million in Q2 and KRW 140 million in Q3, and the last four quarters total KRW 24.4 billion.

In Q1 2026, net income attributable to controlling shareholders exceeded operating profit, which points to non-operating items; the line items in the periodic report's income notes are where the detail sits.

Kiwoom Securities cited rising memory prices that constrained component costs, an unfavorable product mix, and cost pressure as the drivers of the weak first half of 2026 in its October 2026 report.

Equity attributable to controlling shareholders accounts for the full KRW 385.5 billion of total equity at the end of 2025, with total liabilities of KRW 170.9 billion. The debt-to-equity ratio fell from 74.4% in 2022 to 44.3% in 2025.

Operating cash flow was KRW 122.3 billion in 2022, KRW 46.7 billion in 2023, KRW 82.5 billion in 2024, and KRW 69.7 billion in 2025, positive in all four years.

05

Industry analysis

Smartphone camera module volumes depend heavily on the sales volume of customers' new models. Kiwoom Securities' October 2026 report said memory price increases weighed on the mobile component market across the first half of 2026.

The same report saw the launch of new foldable smartphones as a third-quarter improvement factor, but actual Q3 operating profit of KRW 2.51 billion fell short of Kiwoom's KRW 59 million... more precisely its KRW 5.9 billion forecast. The automotive cycle is closer to an upswing.

A Daishin Securities report cited rising demand for ADAS, surround-view monitors and built-in cameras as the reason for higher automotive camera sales. The CEO said Level 3 autonomous driving requires more than ten cameras, over twice the number needed for Level 2.

Industry press forecasts robot and humanoid camera markets to grow at more than 30% a year. In Korean smartphone camera modules, LG Innotek and Samsung Electro-Mechanics rank ahead, and Mcnex ranks third. In automotive camera modules, the company says it is first in Korea and fifth globally.

Customer diversification in mobile and new automotive program wins are the variables that will shape the competitive picture.

06

Outlook

The one plan with a concrete timetable is the production base in Sri City, India. The company plans roughly KRW 30 billion of capex over three years on a 53,000 square meter site, with full-scale camera module mass production targeted for March 2027.

The company says India ranks second only to China in camera module demand and carries high tariffs on auto parts, which is why it is diversifying its supply chain.

The company targets an automotive semiconductor package business of KRW 400 billion to KRW 500 billion within ten years, and the CEO says automotive parts need two to three years to reach mass production because of reliability testing. Humanoid robot camera modules are at the sample-testing stage with customers.

Kiwoom Securities, in its October 2026 report, forecast 2026 sales of KRW 1.19 trillion (down 7%) and operating profit of KRW 25 billion (down 52%), and described the forecast as conservative. Daishin Securities described 2026 as the year the company builds its robot and humanoid technology base.

The next checkpoint is the Q4 2026 preliminary earnings release. The key questions are whether Q4 sales reflect foldable-phone component shipments and whether the operating margin recovers from the Q3 2026 level. The 2026 annual report will finalize full-year results, and the India plant schedule runs toward March 2027.

08

Bull factors

Automotive camera growth

A Daishin Securities report said automotive camera sales rose 12% year on year in Q1 2026. The company says it ranks first in Korea and fifth globally in automotive camera modules, with Hyundai Motor and KIA as key customers.

Press reports say it mass-produces more than 6 million ADAS and autonomous-driving cameras a year, which points to an established production base.

Expansion into robots and humanoids

A Daishin Securities report expects the company to combine its actuator, ToF camera and ADAS electronics technology to enter robotics. Industry press forecasts the humanoid robot camera module market to grow more than 30% a year. The company says the products are in sample testing, so the timing of revenue has not been set.

Improving balance sheet

Total liabilities at the end of 2025 were KRW 170.9 billion, against total equity of KRW 385.5 billion. Operating cash flow was positive in each of the four years from 2022 to 2025.

09

Bear factors

Mobile dependence and cost pressure

Mobile phone applications accounted for 74% of revenue in Q1 2026. Kiwoom Securities said memory price increases constrained component prices and made the product mix unfavorable, which sharply weakened first-half 2026 results.

Stagnant global smartphone shipments and intensifying price competition are also cited as burdens on profitability.

Sharp quarterly profit slowdown

Q3 2026 operating profit of KRW 2.51 billion fell 62.7% year on year, and net income attributable to controlling shareholders of KRW 140 million fell 98.5%. Q3 operating profit came in below the KRW 5.9 billion forecast Kiwoom Securities gave in its October 2026 report. Q2 operating profit had already dropped 80.8% from the prior quarter.

Thin operating margin

The operating margin was 4.2% in 2024 and 4.1% in 2025. Kiwoom Securities pointed to external variables such as exchange rates affecting costs and profitability. Revenue growth has not translated directly into margin expansion, which is cited as a bearish point.

10

Risk factors

Customer and model concentration

Results are sensitive to volumes and unit prices for specific models at key customers such as Samsung Electronics' Galaxy series. Industry analysts note that even peers supplying the same customer can diverge in results based on model volumes and pricing. Sales trends at automotive customers such as Hyundai Motor and KIA also drive automotive revenue.

Costs, currency and tariffs

Rising memory prices have translated into component price constraints and cost pressure. Analysts note that currency movements could limit the scale of any profit recovery. The India plant responds to tariff exposure, but its capex and early-stage operating costs could weigh on near-term profitability.

Timing of new businesses

The India plant is planned for mass production in March 2027, and the automotive semiconductor package business needs two to three years to reach mass production. Robot and humanoid camera products are in sample testing. Because these businesses take time to produce revenue, filling the current gap in earnings will take time.

12

Overall view

Mcnex still relies heavily on camera modules for smartphones, while widening its business into automotive cameras and actuators. Sales bottomed in 2023 and rose to KRW 1.28 trillion in 2025, and operating profit increased for two straight years through 2025.

In 2026, however, operating profit peaked in Q1 and then fell sharply in Q2 and Q3, with Q3 net income attributable to controlling shareholders at KRW 140 million.

Bullish factors include higher automotive camera sales, a first-place position in Korean automotive camera modules, plans for robot and humanoid camera products, a lower debt-to-equity ratio, and positive operating cash flow.

Bearish factors include mobile applications at 74% of revenue, component price constraints from higher memory prices, Q3 results below Kiwoom Securities' forecast, and new businesses that need years to reach mass production.

On valuation, the price-to-book ratio is below 1x, but a thinner earnings base can change how multiples read. The next checkpoints are the Q4 2026 preliminary results, the 2026 annual report, and whether the India plant begins mass production in March 2027.
