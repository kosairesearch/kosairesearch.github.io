02

Business structure

Wooyang was founded in 1992 and specializes in manufacturing and importing and exporting agricultural, marine and forestry products, fruit canned goods and ready-to-eat foods.

Its core business is B2B food ingredient importing and processing, along with the development and manufacturing of contract-produced goods, and its main customers include CJ Cheiljedang, Pulmuone, Starbucks and Ediya.

In the first half of 2025, rising demand for convenience foods and beverages was cited as the driver of improved results. Operating profit in Q2 2025 rose 151.7% from a year earlier, and media reports at the time attributed the gain mainly to strong hot dog sales.

In the first half of 2026, on a separate basis, food segment sales grew but rising cost burdens weakened profitability. Segment revenue shares are not presented here, and the discussion of the business mix is kept qualitative.

Because the customer base is tied to a limited set of large food companies and restaurant brands, order flows from those customers feed directly into revenue. Growth in convenience food and beverage demand has been the main source of volume expansion.

04

Earnings analysis

Annual revenue was KRW 163.4 billion in 2022, KRW 191.3 billion in 2023, KRW 176.3 billion in 2024 and KRW 183.8 billion in 2025. Revenue rose 17.1% in 2023, fell 7.9% in 2024 and rose 4.3% in 2025.

Operating profit moved from KRW 95.87 million in 2022 to KRW 1.3 billion in 2023, KRW 454 million in 2024 and KRW 4.67 billion in 2025, and the 2025 figure, up 928.1% from the prior year, was the largest of the four years.

The operating margin was 0.1% in 2022, 0.7% in 2023, 0.3% in 2024 and 2.5% in 2025, which reflects a thin-margin structure.

Net profit attributable to controlling shareholders was a loss of KRW 3 billion in 2022, a loss of KRW 3.92 billion in 2023 and a loss of KRW 1.31 billion in 2024, before turning to a profit of KRW 2.25 billion in 2025. Operating cash flow was KRW 10.6 billion in 2025, up from KRW 863 million in 2024.

On a quarterly basis, operating profit rose from KRW 982 million in Q3 2025 to KRW 1.7 billion in Q4 2025, dropped to KRW 978 million in Q1 2026 and fell to KRW 102 million in Q2 2026. Operating profit recovered to KRW 1.17 billion in Q3 2026.

Net profit attributable to controlling shareholders was KRW 1.43 billion in Q4 2025, then a loss of KRW 161 million in Q1 2026 and a loss of KRW 654 million in Q2 2026, before a profit of KRW 1.75 billion in Q3 2026.

The sum of the last four quarters' net profit attributable to controlling shareholders is KRW 2.37 billion. Revenue was KRW 49.3 billion in Q3 2026, up 2.7% from a year earlier, while profit recovery depended more on the cost structure than on sales growth.

05

Industry analysis

In the food and beverage sector where Wooyang operates, demand for convenience foods and beverages was the driver of improved results in the first half of 2025.

Ingredient importing and contract manufacturing depend on the procurement flows of large food companies and restaurant brands, so volumes are set by those customers.

Compared with large listed food companies such as CJ Cheiljedang, Nongshim and Orion, Wooyang is smaller in scale, and its operating margin remains low relative to revenue. Competitively, the ability to pass on costs and production efficiency separate profitability across the sector.

In the first half of 2026, revenue grew on a separate basis but cost burdens increased and operating profit declined, which shows the cost structure offsetting the benefit of demand. Where large food companies are both customers and competitors, bargaining power may be limited.

Wooyang's position rests on its B2B functions of ingredient importing, processing and contract manufacturing, and whether those functions translate into stable volumes will determine its position in the sector.

Bull factors

Operating profit in Q3 2026 was KRW 1.17 billion, up 19.4% from a year earlier. Earnings power that has moved out of the loss zone is cited as the basis for the recovery.

Demand for convenience foods and beverages

The improvement in the first half of 2025 was attributed to rising demand for convenience foods and beverages. Operating profit in Q2 2025 rose 151.7% from a year earlier. Revenue on a separate basis grew 11.8% in the first half of 2026, which expanded the food segment's scale.

Easing financial leverage

The debt-to-equity ratio declined from 276.9% in 2022 to 230.4% in 2025. Total equity at the end of 2025 was KRW 46.4 billion. As equity grew, reliance on borrowing eased in the direction of lower leverage. A low price-to-book multiple offers room for interpretation from an asset-based perspective.

Risk factors

Customer concentration

The main customers are named as CJ Cheiljedang, Pulmuone, Starbucks and Ediya. When orders concentrate on a small number of large customers, volume changes at a single customer flow directly into revenue and profit.

Shifts to in-house production or changes in trading terms at customers would affect results through the same channel. Customer-level revenue shares are not presented here, so this is treated as a structural risk.

Cost and expense pressure

In the first half of 2026, food segment sales grew, but operating profit fell as cost burdens increased. With a low operating margin, profit can disappear quickly when cost increases cannot be passed on to prices. The fall in Q2 2026 operating profit to KRW 102 million is a concrete example of this risk.

Financial structure and small-cap liquidity

The debt-to-equity ratio remains high at 230.4%. As a small-cap with a market value in the low KRW 20 billion range, the share price can move sharply after earnings releases. Small trading volumes make a stock more sensitive to institutional and foreign investor flows. Financial leverage combined with limited liquidity can amplify the share price reaction to earnings changes.

Overall view

Q3 2026 operating profit of KRW 1.17 billion and a net profit attributable to controlling shareholders of KRW 1.75 billion point in the same direction. However, the first two quarters of 2026 were losses for controlling shareholders, and Q2 operating profit fell to KRW 102 million.

The operating margin stayed between 0.1% and 2.5% over 2022 to 2025, which reflects a low ability to turn revenue into profit. The debt-to-equity ratio declined from 276.9% in 2022 to 230.4% in 2025, but total liabilities remain larger than total equity.

The price-to-book ratio is below 1x, while annual price-to-earnings ratios could not be calculated for 2022 through 2024 due to losses, so the earnings-based comparison window is short.

On the bullish side are the return to profit and the improvement in financial structure, and on the bearish side are quarterly earnings swings and cost pressure. The next checkpoints are the Q4 results in the 2026 annual report and the Q1 2027 quarterly report.
