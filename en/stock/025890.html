02

Business structure

Hankook Steel was founded in 1987 and listed in 1997 as a specialist maker of steel castings, with annual casting capacity of about 12,000 tonnes. Its products are castings for shipbuilding, industrial machinery and power generation equipment, and it also runs a scrap-iron briquetting business.

A technical analysis report from NICE Information Service, reported around June 2026, estimated 2026 segment revenue at KRW 32 billion for castings, KRW 22 billion for scrap and KRW 0.1 billion for other items, an external estimate rather than filed data.

The company says it is shrinking its lower-margin industrial machinery segment and concentrating on high-margin large castings for shipbuilding and power. In shipbuilding it has repeatedly signed supply contracts with Samsung Heavy Industries, and a contract worth KRW 2.6 billion was disclosed on August 11, 2026.

In power generation it completed first-unit gas turbine castings in May 2026 and finished a dedicated gas turbine plant in late July 2026 with capacity of up to 10 units a year.

Management plans to raise power's share of casting sales to 10% in 2026 and 20% in 2027, while cutting shipbuilding's share from about 80% to around 50%. The company says it is the only domestic maker able to produce castings above 120 tonnes, a claim it made itself.

The same report said scrap briquetting delivered up to 470 tonnes a day to electric arc furnace steelmakers in the fourth quarter of 2025.

04

Earnings analysis

Annual revenue went from KRW 54.4 billion in 2022 to KRW 53.8 billion in 2023 (down 1.0% year on year), KRW 47.3 billion in 2024 (down 12.1%) and KRW 39.9 billion in 2025 (down 15.7%), so revenue fell for three consecutive years from 2023 to 2025. Among 2022 to 2025, 2022 was the largest year and 2025 the smallest.

Operating results moved from an operating loss of KRW 518 million in 2022 to an operating profit of KRW 2.42 billion in 2023, a swing to profit, then to KRW 1.07 billion in 2024 (down 55.6%) and an operating loss of KRW 177 million in 2025, a return to loss.

The operating margin was -1.0% in 2022, 4.5% in 2023, 2.3% in 2024 and -0.4% in 2025, peaking in 2023. Net profit attributable to controlling shareholders rose to KRW 2.5 billion in 2023 (up 44.9%), fell to KRW 762 million in 2024 (down 69.5%), and turned to a net loss of KRW 291 million in 2025.

Net loss including non-controlling interests was also KRW 291 million in 2025. Operating cash flow was KRW 1.61 billion in 2022, KRW 3.63 billion in 2023 and KRW 5.48 billion in 2024, then fell to KRW 2.03 billion in 2025.

Quarterly, Q3 2025 revenue of KRW 9.78 billion with an operating loss of KRW 327 million was followed by Q4 2025 revenue of KRW 9 billion (down 8.0% quarter on quarter) and an operating loss of KRW 278 million.

Q1 2026 revenue of KRW 8.83 billion (down 1.9% quarter on quarter) was the low point, and operating profit of KRW 58.93 million marked a swing to profit. Q2 2026 revenue reached KRW 11.1 billion (up 25.5% quarter on quarter) with operating profit of KRW 153 million (up 159.3%).

Q3 2026 revenue rose to KRW 13.8 billion (up 24.3% quarter on quarter and up 40.8% year on year), marking two consecutive quarters of growth after the Q1 2026 low.

Yet Q3 2026 posted an operating loss of KRW 144 million, a return to loss, and a net loss attributable to controlling shareholders of KRW 233 million, so higher revenue did not carry through to profit.

The combined net loss attributable to controlling shareholders over the last four quarters, from Q4 2025 to Q3 2026, was KRW 404 million.

On the balance sheet, year-end 2025 total equity was KRW 33.3 billion, all attributable to controlling shareholders, total liabilities were KRW 5.03 billion, and the debt ratio fell from 61.4% in 2022 to 15.1% in 2025.

05

Industry analysis

A 2026 market report from Companies Monitor (data as of June 9, 2026) describes the shipbuilding cycle as entering a long upswing, driven by rising orders for eco-friendly vessels and offshore plants. The same source says major Korean shipbuilders have secured roughly three to four years of hull construction volume.

Casting orders follow shipbuilding contracts with a lag of about two years, and the company says large orders secured in 2023 and 2024 are now converting into casting orders from 2026. Because of this lag, the revenue effect of the shipbuilding upcycle appears well after the contracts are signed.

On the supply side, large conglomerates have scaled back their own casting operations, leaving a gap in the domestic market, and outsourcing is favored for small-batch, many-variant production.

Supply-chain shifts that restrict Chinese materials, led by the United States, have also pushed domestic shipbuilders and heavy industry firms to seek reliable local suppliers.

In power generation, demand exists for gas turbine castings, and the company expects that NORSOK certification would lift selling prices for eligible special steel grades by at least 50% per tonne versus standard castings.

On competition, the NICE Information Service report describes very few domestic makers of castings above 50 tonnes. Market-size and share figures for the domestic large-casting segment were not presented, so a share-based comparison of positioning is difficult.

06

Outlook

Order backlog rose about 30%, from 6,441 tonnes at end-2025 to 8,400 tonnes at end-June 2026.

With Samsung Heavy Industries, the company signed a shipbuilding casting supply agreement worth a total of KRW 11.7 billion in December 2025 and disclosed a KRW 2.6 billion contract on August 11, 2026, and the unexecuted volume under the supply agreement is expected to convert into individual contracts.

In July 2026 the company said it agreed with one major customer to raise supply prices. In power generation, a dedicated gas turbine plant was completed in late July 2026 with capacity of up to 10 units a year.

The company plans to keep its plant utilization on a base of 6,500 to 6,800 tonnes of shipbuilding volume a year while raising the share of higher-priced special steel to lift margins. NORSOK certification, needed for offshore plant work, is in progress with a target of completion within 2026.

The NICE Information Service report forecasts 2026 revenue of KRW 54.1 billion and expects benefits from shipbuilding orders and higher sales of premium special steel. Management also said it is reviewing mergers and acquisitions that could create synergies with shipbuilding and defense.

The next checkpoints are whether the rising backlog shows up as operating profit after the quarterly revenue gains, and how the power-share target and operating margin appear in the 2026 annual report.

Bull factors

Backlog and Pricing Improving Together

In July 2026 the company said it agreed with one major customer to raise supply prices. When volume and price rise together, the room for margin improvement on each unit of revenue grows, according to the company and press coverage.

Shift to Higher-Value Power and Special Steel

A dedicated gas turbine plant was completed in late July 2026 and can handle up to 10 units a year. The company targets power at 10% of casting sales in 2026 and 20% in 2027, and aims to obtain NORSOK certification within 2026.

The company expects certification to lift selling prices for eligible special steel grades by at least 50% per tonne versus standard castings.

Domestic Supply Gap in Large Castings

The company says it is the only domestic maker able to produce castings above 120 tonnes. Press coverage links this to a gap left by large conglomerates scaling back their casting operations, combined with restrictions on Chinese materials. Alongside this trend, Q3 2026 revenue rose 40.8% year on year.

Bear factors

Revenue Gains Not Reaching Profit

Q3 2026 revenue of KRW 13.8 billion rose 24.3% quarter on quarter, but the quarter posted an operating loss of KRW 144 million. The Q2 2026 operating profit of KRW 153 million reversed within a single quarter.

The combined net loss attributable to controlling shareholders over the last four quarters was KRW 404 million, so whether revenue growth has translated into earnings recovery has yet to be shown.

Revenue Decline History and Lower Cash Flow

Revenue fell for three consecutive years from 2023 to 2025, and the 2025 level of KRW 39.9 billion was the lowest among 2022 to 2025. Operating cash flow in 2025 was KRW 2.03 billion, down from KRW 5.48 billion in 2024.

Quarterly revenue has risen since Q2 2026, but whether this lifts the annual figure can only be confirmed in the 2026 annual report.

Execution Lag and Reliance on Shipbuilding

The plan to cut shipbuilding's share from about 80% to around 50% is still in progress, and shipbuilding orders remain the main revenue source. Casting orders lag contracts by about two years, so a downturn in the shipbuilding cycle could show up late in results. If the power-share target or NORSOK certification slips, the benefit of the higher-value shift slips with it.

Risk factors

Customer Concentration and Shipbuilding Cycle

Castings for shipbuilding are the main revenue source, and repeat contracts with Samsung Heavy Industries account for a large share. Because contracts are executed individually in sequence, the timing of confirmed volume can shift. If the shipbuilding upcycle weakens, the effect could reach casting orders after a lag of about two years.

Raw Material and Price Swings

The NICE Information Service estimate puts 2026 scrap-iron segment revenue at KRW 22 billion, so scrap price swings can move earnings. How much of the supply-price increase reaches profit depends on the cost structure. The cause of the Q3 2026 return to an operating loss cannot be pinned down from the disclosed figures alone.

Small-Cap Ownership Structure and Liquidity

Major shareholders hold 61.46% as of the November 11, 2026 disclosure, which limits the tradable share count, and the market capitalization is small. Affiliate open-market purchases and changes in the major shareholder's stake can affect supply and demand for the shares. Total equity of KRW 33.3 billion and a 15.1% debt ratio are relevant to financial stability.

12

Overall view

Hankook Steel is growing its order backlog in the narrow niche of large castings, supported by shipbuilding and power demand. The backlog of 8,400 tonnes at end-June 2026 and the supply-price agreement reached in July 2026 show a widening revenue base.

However, Q3 2026 revenue rose 40.8% year on year while the quarter still posted an operating loss of KRW 144 million, and the last four quarters total a net loss attributable to controlling shareholders of KRW 404 million.

On an annual basis, revenue fell from KRW 54.4 billion in 2022 to KRW 39.9 billion in 2025 over three consecutive years, and 2025 turned to losses at both the operating and net attributable levels. The balance sheet is comparatively sturdy, with a 15.1% debt ratio and total equity of KRW 33.3 billion at end-2025.

On the business side, the gas turbine plant, the planned rise in power's share, and the NORSOK target form the core of the higher-value shift. Still, these efforts have not yet shown up as profit in filed results, and dependence on the shipbuilding cycle and a concentrated customer base remain.

The points to check next are the fourth-quarter profit trend in the 2026 full-year results, the operating margin and power share in the 2026 annual report, and whether NORSOK certification is obtained.
