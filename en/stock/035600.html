02

Business structure

Kginicis's core business is online payment gateway (PG) services, connecting card issuers and merchants to handle payment processing, settlement and merchant screening.

According to press reports from August 2026, the company links card companies with about 190,000 merchants and is widening its scope into rental payments, VAN services and integrated overseas payments.

It operates six business segments: e-commerce and distribution, education, food service, finance, real estate leasing, and media and broadcasting.

In Q1 2026, e-commerce and distribution accounted for 70.3% of consolidated sales of KRW 381.7 billion, with segment sales of KRW 268.3 billion and operating profit of KRW 21.4 billion.

The same quarter, food service segment sales were KRW 87.8 billion and media and broadcasting sales were KRW 16.1 billion, according to reports.

Its rental service generated roughly KRW 30 billion in Q1 2026 sales, about 15% of separate-basis revenue, and it is broadening its customer base from individuals to businesses. Reports said rental transaction volume hit a quarterly high in Q2 2026.

Overseas, the company acquired a patent for a Japan payment service and is building cross-border payment and inbound purchase services for domestic merchants. The education unit KG Edu One returned to profit in Q2 2026, and the mobile payment-focused subsidiary KG Financial is also expanding. Competition hinges on cost structures negotiated with card companies and on securing large merchants.

04

Earnings analysis

Annual sales rose from KRW 1.18 trillion in 2022 to KRW 1.34 trillion in 2023 (up 14.2%) and KRW 1.36 trillion in 2025, growing for three consecutive years from 2023 to 2025. Growth slowed sharply, however, to 0.7% in 2024 and 0.3% in 2025, and 2025 was the largest year of 2022–2025.

Operating profit moved from KRW 106.5 billion in 2022 and KRW 106.3 billion in 2023 to KRW 61 billion in 2024 (down 42.6%). Reports link the 2024 decline to the TMON and WeMakePrice settlement default, with bad-debt provisions rising to KRW 49.2 billion from KRW 7 billion a year earlier.

Operating profit recovered to KRW 98.6 billion in 2025 (up 61.6%), and the company attributed much of the rebound to a turnaround at its consolidated subsidiary KG Mobilians, which had normalized losses.

The operating margin ran at 9.0% in 2022, 7.9% in 2023, 4.5% in 2024 and 7.3% in 2025, showing a recovery direction in 2025. Profit attributable to controlling shareholders peaked at KRW 77.4 billion in 2023, fell to KRW 41.2 billion in 2024, and rose 16.0% to KRW 47.9 billion in 2025.

Net profit including non-controlling interests was KRW 57.8 billion in 2025, and the gap with controlling-shareholder profit reflects minority stakes in subsidiaries. Quarterly profit attributable to controlling shareholders fell 76.7% to KRW 3.79 billion in Q4 2025 before recovering to KRW 15.3 billion in Q1 2026.

Q3 2026 operating profit was KRW 22.1 billion, down 22.0% year on year, and controlling-shareholder profit over the last four quarters (Q4 2025 to Q3 2026) totaled KRW 44.9 billion.

Consolidated cumulative sales for January to September 2026 were KRW 1.1033 trillion, up 9.5%, and cumulative operating profit was KRW 76.1 billion, down 1.7% (preliminary, announced November 5, 2026).

05

Industry analysis

Growth in Korea's electronic payment market leans heavily on large merchants and online transactions, and the company cited rising transaction volume from large and top-tier merchants along with a consumer rebound as drivers of Q1 2026 results.

In the cycle, 2024 was the year the TMON and WeMakePrice incident exposed receivable risk for PG operators, and transactions have recovered since the second half of 2025.

Card company cost adjustments and the card-fee recalculation cycle are external variables that shape fee income, and they were cited for weakness in the first half of 2025.

Reported R&D expenses fell 16.6% in 2025 from 2024, but the company says this reflects accounting reclassification and that actual technology investment continues. Some industry observers view stablecoins and overseas payments as new growth engines after the slowdown in PG volume.

In competition, the company is widening its scope by bundling card-issuer ties, VAN services and merchant networks. As rental and overseas payments grow in share, dependence on the single PG business should decline.

In sum, securing large merchants is a strength, while proving that new businesses generate profit is the next test.

06

Outlook

In its 2026–2030 corporate value-up plan, announced in August 2026, the company said it will use 50% of separate adjusted net profit for shareholder returns. The previous plan targeted a 30% return for 2024–2026, and the company reported a 46.6% return ratio for 2024.

Its ultimate goal is PBR of 1x or higher, and as of August 2026 reports said it had not yet reached that level. Treasury shares totaled 935,946 as of end-June 2026, about 3.4% of issued shares, and 735,946 of them are slated for cancellation after a board decision before September 2027.

The company did not give specific earnings guidance but said it aims for growth of more than 10% year on year. First-half 2026 separate operating profit rose 8.5% to KRW 25.8 billion, but the operating margin fell from 7.1% to 6.3%.

Overseas, it plans to expand inbound purchase payments on the back of its Japan payment service and extend cross-border trade (CBT) business to Southeast Asia in 2027. For stablecoins, reports said it is first reviewing a dollar-based business in partnership with Solana.

In non-PG areas, it plans to expand bundled rental offerings and B2B business and build settlement infrastructure linking trade payments with foreign exchange. The next checkpoint is the additional shareholder return amount, which is set after the 2026 annual financial statements are finalized in March 2027.

08

Bull factors

Higher shareholder return benchmark

The company set a 50% return benchmark for 2026–2030 on separate adjusted net profit, raised from 30% in the prior plan. Treasury shares of 735,946 are scheduled for cancellation before September 2027. It has also set a medium-term PBR target of 1x or higher, signaling a focus on capital efficiency.

Rental and B2B widen revenue sources

Rental generated roughly KRW 30 billion in Q1 2026, about 15% of separate-basis sales. First-half 2026 separate sales reached KRW 409.7 billion, up 22.8% year on year. Reports say expansion into corporate customers pushed quarterly transaction volume to a record.

Q1 rebound and subsidiary contribution

Q1 2026 consolidated sales of KRW 381.7 billion were the highest quarterly figure in the disclosed series, and operating profit rose 20.8% quarter on quarter to KRW 25.5 billion. First-half 2026 consolidated operating profit reached KRW 54.1 billion, up 9.9% year on year. Reports say subsidiary earnings gains are broadening the base of consolidated profit.

09

Bear factors

Slowing quarterly profit

Q3 2026 operating profit was KRW 22.1 billion, down 22.0% year on year, and controlling-shareholder profit fell 36.0% to KRW 10.4 billion. Sales have declined for two consecutive quarters from Q2 2026. The first-half 2026 separate operating margin narrowed from 7.1% to 6.3%.

Memory of receivable losses

Bad-debt provisions reached KRW 49.2 billion in 2024, roughly seven times the KRW 7 billion of the prior year. Receivables of KRW 26.2 billion tied to a subsidiary's partners were fully impaired, cutting profit sharply. The case shows how partner risk can materialize in the settlement structure.

Earnings volatility and debt burden

Controlling-shareholder profit fell from KRW 77.4 billion in 2023 to KRW 41.2 billion in 2024, and its 2025 recovery to KRW 47.9 billion still sits below the 2023 level. The debt ratio rose from 139.9% in 2022 to 161.8% in 2025. The operating margin dropped to 4.5% in 2024, so earnings stability remains a open issue.

10

Risk factors

Card-issuer fee and cost structure

Card company cost adjustments and the fee recalculation cycle directly affect PG fee income. Results stalled in the first half of 2025 for these reasons and were offset by higher transactions in the second half. Even if volume grows, margins can narrow when fee terms change.

Timing of new business monetization

Stablecoin work is at an early review stage with Solana, while the Japan payment service and Southeast Asia CBT expansion are planned for 2027 and beyond. Reported R&D expenses fell 16.6% in 2025, which the company attributes to accounting reclassification. New businesses may take time to contribute to consolidated profit.

Dependence on large merchants and subsidiaries

E-commerce and distribution made up 70.3% of Q1 2026 consolidated sales, so dependence on one segment is high. A decline or loss of large merchants would feed quickly into quarterly results.

Because the 2025 operating profit recovery leaned on a subsidiary's loss normalization, subsidiary performance is another swing factor for consolidated profit.

12

Overall view

Kginicis is driven by its PG core business and rental services, with consolidated sales for January to September 2026 reaching KRW 1.1033 trillion, up 9.5% (preliminary, announced November 5, 2026).

However, Q3 2026 operating profit of KRW 22.1 billion fell 22.0% year on year, confirming a slowdown in quarterly earnings. In 2024, bad-debt provisions linked to the TMON and WeMakePrice incident pushed operating profit down to KRW 61 billion, and it recovered to KRW 98.6 billion in 2025.

The company raised its 2026–2030 shareholder return benchmark to 50% and set out a treasury share cancellation plan, strengthening its return framework. The debt ratio rose to 161.8% in 2025 from 139.9% in 2022, and controlling-shareholder profit remains below the KRW 77.4 billion recorded in 2023.

Supporting factors include the expanded return framework, growth in rental and B2B, and the Q1 2026 sales rebound, while the main offsetting factors are the slowdown in quarterly profit, the history of receivable losses, and earnings volatility with debt burden.

New businesses such as stablecoins and overseas payments remain at plan or review stages, so their contribution to earnings will need to be confirmed in future disclosures.

The next checkpoints are the year-end dividend in December 2026, preliminary full-year results around February 2027, and the additional return amount after financial statements are finalized in March 2027.
