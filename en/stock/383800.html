LX Holdings: Earnings Track Subsidiary Equity-Method Income

LX Holdings' operating profit depends heavily on equity-method income from its subsidiaries. After an operating loss in the fourth quarter of 2025, operating profit stayed positive in the first three quarters of 2026, but third-quarter operating profit fell 46.0% from a year earlier.

02

Business structure

LX Holdings was spun off from the LG Group in 2021 through an instituted split and listed on the KOSPI in May of that year. It is a pure holding company that does not manufacture or sell products itself. Its income comes from dividends received from subsidiaries and trademark royalties.

In the 2025 business report's segment table, dividend income accounted for 63.2% and trademark income for 27.0% of the holding company's simple total before consolidation adjustments. Trademark income is paid by subsidiaries for using the 'LX' name and logo.

Its major affiliates are LX International, LX Semicon and LX Hausys, with stakes in the 20–50% range that are classified as associates and accounted for under the equity method.

Subsidiary businesses are split between resource and trading at LX International, semiconductors including display driver ICs at LX Semicon, building materials at LX Hausys and petrochemical materials at LX MMA.

One analysis says LX International accounts for about 70% of group revenue, and it was reported that LG Electronics was LX International's largest customer, averaging roughly KRW 6 trillion a year as of 2024.

In 2025 the group bought the LG Gwanghwamun Building in Jongno-gu, Seoul, for KRW 512 billion, adding a rental income base.

Competition is better understood at the level of each subsidiary's industry than as a comparison between holding companies, and because most subsidiaries are sensitive to global demand and raw material prices, the holding company's earnings follow the same cycle.

04

Earnings analysis

On a reported basis, consolidated revenue fell from KRW 236.9 billion in 2022 to KRW 37.9 billion in 2023, a decline of 84.0%, and then rose to KRW 40.7 billion in 2024 (up 7.3%) and KRW 41.3 billion in 2025 (up 1.5%), growing for two consecutive years from 2024 through 2025.

Operating profit was KRW 158.9 billion in 2022, KRW 73.2 billion in 2023 (down 54.0%), KRW 156 billion in 2024 (up 113.2%) and KRW 133.4 billion in 2025 (down 14.5%).

Profit attributable to controlling shareholders was KRW 170.1 billion, KRW 78.8 billion, KRW 160.3 billion and KRW 135.6 billion over the four years, and net profit was reported at the same amounts in each year.

The operating margin was 67.1% in 2022, 193.0% in 2023, 383.6% in 2024 and 323.1% in 2025, well above 100% because operating profit includes equity-method income.

In the 2025 business report, equity-method income was far larger than operating revenue, while operating expenses rose on higher personnel and other operating costs. The 2025 decline in operating profit therefore appears to reflect lower equity-method income.

Quarterly, operating profit was KRW 37.3 billion in the third quarter of 2025, and the fourth quarter swung to an operating loss of KRW 17.7 billion on revenue of KRW 9.95 billion.

First-quarter 2026 operating profit of KRW 41.6 billion turned positive, though it fell 41.7% from a year earlier, and a decline in dividends from subsidiaries was cited as the main reason for the year-on-year drop.

Second-quarter operating profit was KRW 60.3 billion, up 45.0% from the prior quarter, and third-quarter operating profit was KRW 20.1 billion, down 66.6% from the prior quarter.

Profit attributable to controlling shareholders was KRW 37.6 billion, KRW 56.6 billion and KRW 17.6 billion in the first three quarters of 2026, and the last four quarters total KRW 95.7 billion. In the third quarter revenue rose while operating profit fell, showing that revenue and profit items move differently.

The specific cause of the third-quarter decline is a matter to check in the subsidiaries' results and the 2026 business report.

05

Industry analysis

LX Holdings' earnings closely mirror the conditions of its subsidiaries, so the cycle in each subsidiary's industry is the key variable. Subsidiary results diverged in the first quarter of 2026.

Operating profit at building-materials subsidiary LX Hausys rose from KRW 7.1 billion to KRW 45.9 billion, more than six-fold, with a recovery in domestic home transactions, demand centered on remodeling and stabler PVC raw material prices cited as drivers.

By contrast, semiconductor subsidiary LX Semicon's operating profit fell from KRW 59.7 billion to KRW 20.6 billion, its operating margin dropped from 12.5% to 5.3%, and a slow display-industry recovery and weaker IT demand were named as causes.

Petrochemical-materials subsidiary LX MMA's operating profit fell from KRW 33.8 billion to KRW 14.5 billion over the same period, and weak PMMA market conditions were said to limit margin recovery.

Resources-and-trading subsidiary LX International saw revenue rise, but operating profit fell 6.8% as coal prices declined. Taken together, housing and construction-related demand is recovering while display, semiconductor and petrochemical demand is weak, so the subsidiaries sit at different points in their cycles.

Compared with other listed holding companies, LX Holdings has few operating functions of its own and relies heavily on subsidiary dividends and equity-method income. Dependence on intra-group business also remains.

Analysts have pointed to LG Group's continuing high share of sales, and the Fair Trade Commission found in 2021 that the LG-related transaction shares of LX Pantos and LX Semicon were above the industry averages.

06

Outlook

The most concrete items to watch are asset investment and funding.

The company pursued the purchase of the LG Gwanghwamun Building for KRW 512 billion, which was reported as its largest asset investment since the 2021 spin-off, and it was described as a way to secure a rental income base and consolidate key group functions in one place.

A report from October 2025 said the purchase would first be funded with existing cash equivalents, with any shortfall raised through corporate bond issuance. The same report put first-half cash and cash equivalents at KRW 312.1 billion and the external funding need at about KRW 200 billion.

Because this would be the company's first corporate bond issuance since its founding, it is a key item for gauging changes in the balance sheet.

The company has also moved to secure funds for additional share purchases in its subsidiary LX International, and it was reported to be reviewing further stake purchases in affiliates.

With its stake below a majority, the company cannot bring these affiliates into consolidation, so raising its stakes is seen as a way to strengthen control. However, timing and size are not yet fixed.

According to an August 2026 report, the holding of chairman Koo Bon-joon fell from 20.15% to 12.38%, while the shares held by his eldest son, president Koo Hyung-mo, increased, a shift interpreted as linked to the group's succession.

Because operating profit depends on equity-method income and dividends from subsidiaries, subsidiary industry conditions are the biggest variable after the fourth quarter of 2026. Whether profit stays positive in the fourth-quarter and annual preliminary results is the next reference point.

The second-quarter figure is the largest among the reported quarters since the third quarter of 2025. Improvement at a subsidiary

Operating profit at LX Hausys rose from KRW 7.1 billion to KRW 45.9 billion in the first quarter of 2026, more than six-fold. A recovery in home transactions and remodeling demand was cited as the driver. Improvement at some subsidiaries offers a degree of diversification to holding-company earnings.

Financial capacity and funding channels

Total equity at the end of 2025 was KRW 2 trillion, all attributable to controlling shareholders, and total liabilities were KRW 357.8 billion. Market commentary pointed to ample financial capacity at a debt-to-equity ratio of around 2.1% in an earlier period. The ability to access bond markets widens the options for raising stakes in subsidiaries.

Bear factors

Volatility of dividend and equity-method income

A decline in dividends from subsidiaries was cited as the main reason for the year-on-year drop in first-quarter 2026 results. In the 2025 business report, equity-method income declined from the prior year while operating expenses rose.

Holding-company profit swings sharply from quarter to quarter depending on subsidiary dividend timing and equity-method valuations.

Sharp third-quarter profit decline

Third-quarter 2026 operating profit was KRW 20.1 billion, down 66.6% from the prior quarter and 46.0% from a year earlier. Profit attributable to controlling shareholders was KRW 17.6 billion, down 53.1% year on year. Revenue rose while profit fell, showing that revenue does not translate directly into profit contribution.

Weak conditions at key subsidiaries

LX Semicon's operating profit fell from KRW 59.7 billion to KRW 20.6 billion in the first quarter of 2026, with its operating margin at 5.3%. LX MMA's operating profit fell from KRW 33.8 billion to KRW 14.5 billion over the same period.

If recovery in the semiconductor and petrochemical subsidiaries is delayed, the holding company's earnings recovery will also be constrained.

Risk factors

Intra-group trade and dependence on LG

LG Electronics is LX International's largest customer, and one analysis says LX International accounts for about 70% of group revenue. Analysts also note the LG Group's share of sales remains high, so changes in LG-related demand feed directly into subsidiary results.

In 2021 the Fair Trade Commission found that LG-related transaction shares at LX Pantos and LX Semicon were above industry averages.

Balance-sheet change and external funding

The debt-to-equity ratio rose sharply from 1.9% in 2024 to 17.9% in 2025. Reports indicated that external funding needs could grow as the KRW 512 billion Gwanghwamun building purchase overlaps with possible additional stake purchases in subsidiaries.

Because the bond issuance would be the company's first since its founding, its terms and interest rates are items to check in future disclosures.

Control structure and stake shifts during succession

As of May 2026, Koo Bon-joon and related parties held 43.82%. An August 2026 report said chairman Koo Bon-joon's stake declined while president Koo Hyung-mo's holdings increased.

Because stakes in key affiliates remain below a majority, bringing them into consolidation is difficult, and strengthening control remains an open task.

Overall view

LX Holdings is a pure holding company that relies on subsidiary dividends and equity-method income rather than its own operations, so its results reflect subsidiary conditions directly. Reported operating profit recovered sharply in 2024 after its 2023 low and eased slightly in 2025.

Operating profit stayed positive through the first three quarters of 2026 after the fourth-quarter 2025 loss, but third-quarter operating profit was down 46.0% from a year earlier.

Positive factors include the run of quarterly profit, the improvement at the building-materials subsidiary, and equity that is large relative to debt.

Negative factors include lower subsidiary dividends and volatile equity-method income, weakness at the semiconductor and petrochemical subsidiaries, and continued reliance on LG Group business.

The debt-to-equity ratio rose to 17.9% in 2025, and the Gwanghwamun building purchase and planned bond issuance are the next variables for the balance sheet. How the share price is read depends on whether one weights subsidiary earnings flow or asset value more heavily.

The next checkpoints are the preliminary fourth-quarter and full-year 2026 results, the 2026 business report, and the preliminary first-quarter 2027 results.
