02

Business structure

Aligned Genetics was founded in October 2008 and develops and sells life science research and diagnostic instruments. Its main products are automated cell counting systems, tissue clearing and imaging systems, and digital cell imaging systems, designed by combining biotechnology, optics, electronics and software.

The flagship LUNA line of automated cell counters serves pharmaceutical and biotech companies and research institutes that need fast measurement of cell count and viability. The CELENA digital cell imaging system and the tissue clearing system broaden the portfolio toward cell observation and tissue analysis.

Once instruments are installed, demand for consumables such as counting slides follows, so instrument sales and recurring revenue arise together. The company says this model, which pairs research instrument sales with higher-margin consumables, supports earnings stability.

Segment revenue shares are not included in the disclosed figures. Based on 2019 data, export shares by product ranged from roughly 81% to 97%, though that year's mix may differ from today.

On the new-business side, media have reported that the company became the exclusive manufacturer and supplier of the CosmoSort single-cell analysis instrument, and in March 2026 it presented point-of-care molecular diagnostics as a new growth engine.

Competition overlaps with other research instrument makers in cell analysis and imaging, and the company has said that combining biotech, electronics, optics and software expertise is what secures market advantage.

04

Earnings analysis

Annual revenue rose from KRW 13.8 billion in 2022 to KRW 15.2 billion in 2023 (up 10.6% year on year), KRW 15.8 billion in 2024 (up 3.7%) and KRW 16 billion in 2025 (up 1.5%), and 2025 was the largest year of 2022 to 2025.

Growth slowed each year, and the company cited external factors behind the stagnation, including the US government shutdown, tariff concerns and cuts to government research budgets.

Operating profit went from KRW 2.85 billion in 2022 to KRW 3.34 billion in 2023 (up 17.1%), the peak, then fell 16.7% to KRW 2.78 billion in 2024 and 12.1% to KRW 2.44 billion in 2025, a decline in two consecutive years. The operating margin moved from 20.7% in 2022 to 21.9% in 2023, 17.6% in 2024 and 15.3% in 2025.

Operating profit fell while revenue rose, so the margin was squeezed, and the detailed cost and selling and administrative expense lines should be checked in the business report.

Net profit attributable to controlling shareholders was KRW 2.33 billion in 2022, KRW 3.24 billion in 2023 (up 38.7%) and KRW 4.13 billion in 2024 (up 27.6%), with 2024 the largest year, before falling 29.5% to KRW 2.91 billion in 2025.

Net profit including non-controlling interests was reported at the same amounts in each year. Operating cash flow rose from KRW 2.22 billion in 2022 to KRW 3.61 billion in 2025, above the KRW 2.91 billion of 2025 profit attributable to controlling shareholders.

Total equity at the end of 2025 was KRW 37.2 billion, all of it attributable to controlling shareholders, and total liabilities were KRW 20.6 billion. Quarterly results are volatile.

From KRW 3.89 billion in revenue, KRW 545 million in operating profit and KRW 933 million in controlling-shareholder net profit in the third quarter of 2025, the fourth quarter rose to KRW 4.28 billion in revenue (up 9.9% quarter on quarter), KRW 1.11 billion in operating profit (up 103.1%) and KRW 1.54 billion in controlling-shareholder net profit (up 65.2%).

The first quarter of 2026 fell back to KRW 3.64 billion in revenue, KRW 230 million in operating profit and KRW 418 million in controlling-shareholder net profit, and the second quarter recovered to KRW 4.35 billion, KRW 644 million and KRW 1.75 billion.

The third quarter of 2026 revenue of KRW 3.97 billion rose 2.1% year on year, but operating profit of KRW 281 million fell 48.4% year on year, and the company swung to a controlling-shareholder net loss of KRW 922 million.

Controlling-shareholder net profit for the last four quarters, from the fourth quarter of 2025 to the third quarter of 2026, totals KRW 2.79 billion.

Industry analysis

The market Aligned Genetics serves is linked to pharmaceutical and biotech R&D budgets and to laboratory equipment spending at universities and research institutes. A data provider compares Aligned Genetics with the KOSDAQ medical and precision instruments industry group.

Demand for research instruments is sensitive to the timing of research funding and to policy changes, so budget cycles matter more than the general manufacturing cycle.

The company attributed its revenue stagnation to the US government shutdown, tariff concerns and cuts to government research budgets, which shows exposure to external policy variables.

On the other hand, the company says cell counting and cell imaging are used in basic experimental workflows, so replacement and expansion demand is steady.

The diagnostics market is broadening toward molecular diagnostic instruments that deliver fast results at the point of care, as demand for early cancer detection and infectious disease response grows, and Aligned Genetics is trying to enter this flow with its POCT system.

In research instruments, performance matters alongside the consumables supply chain and the customer base, and media have reported that the company serves both pharmaceutical companies and university and research institutions.

Because export shares are high, with about 81% to 97% by product in 2019, competition and currency movements can affect profitability directly. The cycle position is hard to judge with certainty, so the direction of quarterly results in 2026 and the performance of the POCT business will serve as the reference points.

Risk factors

Research budgets and demand swings

Research instrument sales are sensitive to pharmaceutical and biotech R&D budgets and to laboratory budgets at universities and research institutes. US government shutdowns and cuts to government research funding have already been cited as reasons for revenue stagnation.

If budget execution slips, instrument orders can be pushed out by a quarter or more, adding to earnings volatility.

Tariffs, currency and export dependence

Export shares by product were about 81% to 97% in 2019, so overseas sales make up a large part of revenue. Tariff changes and currency movements can directly affect export profitability and won-denominated revenue. A slowdown in sales by overseas distribution partners would flow straight through to export results.

New-business commercialization and capex burden

The POCT diagnostics business depends on regulatory approval, quality control and commercialization timing. The KRW 14 billion Gunpo property acquisition and production expansion could add strain to the asset and liability structure. With the debt ratio at 55.5%, the pace of investment will shape financial stability.
