GSE: Profit Recovered in 2025, Quarterly Swings Widen

02

Business structure

GSE is a regional city gas supplier that distributes LNG-based gas in Jinju, Sacheon and surrounding areas in South Gyeongsang Province. The company has run the city gas business since its 2010 merger with the former GSE, and city gas supply remained its main business after the IT segment was suspended in 2011.

A 2021 report described its supply area as seven cities and counties after new supply to Sancheong and Hapcheon was planned for late 2021. In that August 2021 report, GSE ranked 24th among city gas companies by supply volume, with a 0.8% national share and a 14.3% share within South Gyeongsang Province.

Revenue is driven mainly by city gas sales, and demand splits between building and industrial users. Building demand depends heavily on temperature and heating needs, and in the first quarter of 2026 lower heating demand cut building-segment sales while cost reductions improved gross profit.

Industrial demand is expected to lead gas demand growth in 2026. Its regional supply areas are often described as local monopoly markets, which underpins the franchise. Compared with city gas peers such as Samchully and Seoul City GAS, GSE is a small regional operator. Shareholders related to the largest holder hold 44.58% including related parties.

04

Earnings analysis

On a DART-filed annual basis, revenue fell from KRW 172.6 billion in 2022 to KRW 168.8 billion in 2023 (down 2.2% year on year) and to KRW 155.6 billion in 2024 (down 7.8%), before recovering to KRW 170.1 billion in 2025 (up 9.3%). 2022 was the largest year and 2024 the smallest.

Operating profit was KRW 4.63 billion in 2022 and KRW 4.38 billion in 2023 (down 5.3%), then rose to KRW 4.91 billion in 2024 (up 12.0%) and KRW 7.34 billion in 2025 (up 49.6%). It increased for two consecutive years from 2024 through 2025, and 2025 was the largest operating profit year in the period.

The operating margin moved from 2.7% in 2022 and 2.6% in 2023 to 3.2% in 2024 and 4.3% in 2025, a profitability recovery. Net profit attributable to controlling shareholders was KRW 5.9 billion in 2022, KRW 5.59 billion in 2023 (down 5.3%), KRW 3.56 billion in 2024 (down 36.2%) and KRW 3.88 billion in 2025 (up 8.8%).

The rebound in that line lagged the operating profit recovery, and non-operating items and income tax detail should be checked in the full periodic report. Net profit including non-controlling interests was reported at the same amounts: KRW 5.9 billion, KRW 5.59 billion, KRW 3.56 billion and KRW 3.88 billion.

Operating cash flow was KRW 17.8 billion in 2022, KRW 10.3 billion in 2023, KRW 14.5 billion in 2024 and KRW 12.1 billion in 2025.

The debt ratio went from 130.9% in 2022 and 126.1% in 2023 to 152.5% in 2024 and 141.4% in 2025, with total liabilities of KRW 137.3 billion and total equity of KRW 97.1 billion at the end of 2025, all attributable to controlling shareholders.

Quarterly results moved from an operating loss of KRW 1.47 billion in the third quarter of 2025 to an operating profit of KRW 1.57 billion in the fourth quarter, a swing to profit.

Revenue then peaked at KRW 71.5 billion in the first quarter of 2026, with operating profit of KRW 5.44 billion (up 246.0% from the prior quarter). In the second quarter revenue fell to KRW 36.3 billion (down 49.2%) and operating profit to KRW 1.96 billion (down 63.9%).

In the third quarter revenue was KRW 22 billion and the company posted an operating loss of KRW 1.35 billion. Net profit attributable to controlling shareholders over the last four quarters totaled KRW 3.79 billion.

Because city gas sales concentrate in the heating-season first quarter, the first-quarter peak and the third-quarter loss are best read together within one business year.

05

Industry analysis

City gas economics depend on LNG procurement costs and tariff structure, so revenue tends to move with feedstock prices and volumes. On the demand side, building heating is a business whose quarterly results swing with temperature and heating degree days.

A NICE D&B company report prepared around 2024 expected industrial gas consumption to rebound with industrial recovery and heating degree days to rise under normal-temperature assumptions. The same report described government low-carbon policy as supporting switching from other fuels to city gas.

The 2050 carbon neutrality strategy calls for a shift toward low-carbon energy supply, so whether city gas remains a transition fuel is a medium-term variable. Its supply-volume rank of 24th in 2021 shows the size gap with larger city gas utilities.

Forecasts for industrial demand growth and weaker building demand point in different directions, so the 2026 outlook depends on the demand mix. Among listed city gas companies, GSE is characterized by regional supply areas and a small capital base.

08

Bull factors

Profit recovery through 2025

Operating profit rose for two consecutive years from its 2023 low, reaching KRW 7.34 billion in 2025 (up 49.6%), the highest in the period. The operating margin improved from 2.7% in 2022 to 4.3% in 2025, which supports the case for a profitability recovery.

Cost savings and gross profit

In the first quarter of 2026, building-segment sales fell on lower heating demand, but cost reduction efforts improved gross profit. Operating profit that quarter reached KRW 5.44 billion, up 246.0% from the prior quarter, showing cost management supporting quarterly profitability.

Industrial demand and area expansion

Forecasts point to 2026 gas demand growth led by industrial natural gas, and western Gyeongnam supply-area expansion and an aerospace cluster are cited as medium-term bases. If demand less tied to building heating grows, quarterly earnings swings could narrow, which is the bullish logic.

09

Bear factors

Third-quarter 2026 swing to loss

Operating loss reached KRW 1.35 billion in the third quarter of 2026 and the net loss attributable to controlling shareholders reached KRW 1.62 billion. Revenue fell for two consecutive quarters, to KRW 36.3 billion in the second quarter and KRW 22 billion in the third. The inability of the recovery to hold at the quarterly level is the bearish case.

Dependence on building heating demand

Building demand depends on heating needs, so warmer weather or weaker heating demand reduces sales quickly. Building-segment sales fell in the first quarter of 2026 on lower heating demand. A structure whose results move with climate is a bearish factor.

Leverage and small scale

Total liabilities were KRW 137.3 billion at the end of 2025 against total equity of KRW 97.1 billion, a debt ratio of 141.4%, after reaching 152.5% in 2024. Net profit attributable to controlling shareholders fell 36.2% in 2024, and the company's small supply-volume rank of 24th (2021 report) limits its scale.

10

Risk factors

Feedstock prices and tariff structure

City gas sales and margins depend on LNG feedstock prices and tariff approval. If cost increases reach tariffs with a lag, short-term margins can be squeezed. Cost cuts protected gross profit in the first quarter of 2026, but the same effect is not assured during sharp feedstock price spikes.

Climate and heating demand

Building demand moves sharply by quarter with temperature and heating needs. Continued warming could structurally reduce heating demand, and the decline in building-segment sales in the first quarter of 2026 reflected weaker heating demand. Climate is a variable the company cannot control.

Small-cap liquidity and ownership

Shareholders related to the largest holder hold 44.58% including related parties, leaving a free float of roughly half. Holder ratios differ across data providers, so the shareholder disclosure in the periodic report is the reference. The small market capitalization can amplify trading volatility.

Overall view

GSE is a regional LNG-based city gas supplier in South Gyeongsang Province. Its 2025 filed results show operating profit rising to KRW 7.34 billion and the operating margin reaching 4.3%, a profitability recovery.

Quarterly results in 2026, however, peaked at KRW 71.5 billion of revenue in the first quarter and declined in the second and third quarters, with the third quarter returning to an operating loss of KRW 1.35 billion and a net loss attributable to controlling shareholders of KRW 1.62 billion.

Bullish factors are the annual profit recovery, forecasts of industrial gas demand growth and supply-area expansion. Bearish factors are the seasonality and temperature dependence of building demand, the third-quarter 2026 swing to loss and a 141.4% debt ratio.

Net profit attributable to controlling shareholders over the last four quarters totaled KRW 3.79 billion, so reported indicators can move sharply with quarterly results.

Beyond the fact that the share price trades at a low multiple of net asset value, earnings volatility leaves the interpretation open to several readings. The next checkpoints are full-year 2026 results and the business report reflecting fourth-quarter profit and loss, and building-segment sales in the 2026 winter.
