Asiana Airlines Nears Korean Air Merger as Losses Persist

Asiana Airlines is scheduled to be integrated into Korean AIR Lines with the combined carrier launching on December 17, 2026, while operating losses persist and attributable net income turned positive in the third quarter of 2026.

  1. 1

    Merger structure: the exchange ratio is set at 1 Korean AIR Lines share for 0.2736432 Asiana Airlines shares, with the merger date on December 16, 2026 and the integrated launch on December 17. Asiana Airlines will cease as a legal entity, and Korean AIR Lines will take over its assets, liabilities and employees.

  2. 2

    Earnings trajectory: the company recorded losses in two consecutive years, 2024 and 2025, with an operating loss of KRW 345.2 billion and a net loss attributable to controlling shareholders of KRW 283 billion in 2025. In the third quarter of 2026 the operating loss narrowed to KRW 182.4 billion and attributable net income turned to KRW 235.2 billion.

  3. 3

    Cash flow and balance sheet: operating cash flow fell from KRW 1.86 trillion in 2022 to KRW 137 billion in 2025, and total liabilities of KRW 11.4 trillion at the end of 2025 far exceed total equity of KRW 829 billion.

  4. 4

    Passenger and cargo: third-quarter 2026 passenger revenue rose 9.7% year on year and yield per passenger rose 18.1% (separate basis, announced November 12, 2026). Cargo revenue fell 32.9% over the same period, reflecting the sale of the freighter business.

  5. 5

    Next checkpoints: the December 2026 merger registration, the transition of operations and mileage programs under the combined carrier, and the 2026 annual report.

02

Business structure

Asiana Airlines is an air transport operator centered on passenger and cargo services. Although it is classified under aerospace and defense, the operations that generate its reported results are air transport.

Separate-basis passenger revenue in the third quarter of 2026 was KRW 1.27 trillion, the bulk of total revenue of KRW 1.53 trillion, and rose 9.7% year on year.

The increase reflected an expansion of short-haul routes with higher returns and additional European flights in the summer peak season, which lifted yield per passenger by 18.1%. Cargo revenue was KRW 96.6 billion, down 32.9% year on year, reflecting the August 2025 sale of the freighter business.

On the customer side, the company is expanding codeshare operations with Korean AIR Lines to broaden its sales network and increasing the places where mileage can be used. In-cabin competitiveness is being addressed through a wider rollout of Starlink-equipped aircraft and upgraded meals and lounges.

Korean AIR Lines acquired a 63.88 percent stake in December 2024, making Asiana a subsidiary, and the companies moved to the merger agreement stage in May 2026.

Korean AIR Lines, the merger partner, is the most direct point of comparison, while Jejuair, JIN AIR and Air Busan compete on short-haul routes as low-cost carriers.

Based on a November 2026 report, low-cost carrier mergers received final approval, with a combined low-cost carrier scheduled to launch in March 2027, which may also reshape the domestic low-cost market. The main lens for this business is therefore air travel demand and the cost structure.

04

Earnings analysis

On an annual basis, revenue rose from KRW 6.21 trillion in 2022 to KRW 7.62 trillion in 2023, up 22.8% year on year, and reached KRW 8.32 trillion in 2024, up 9.1%, making 2024 the largest year in the 2022 to 2025 period. Revenue fell 12.6% to KRW 7.27 trillion in 2025.

Operating profit was KRW 598.8 billion in 2022 and KRW 619.9 billion in 2023, up 3.5%, before dropping 55.5% to KRW 275.7 billion in 2024. The company then recorded an operating loss of KRW 345.2 billion in 2025, a swing into deficit.

The operating margin moved from 9.6% in 2022 and 8.1% in 2023 to 3.3% in 2024 and -4.8% in 2025. Net income attributable to controlling shareholders was KRW 110.4 billion in 2022 and KRW 113.9 billion in 2023, turned to a net loss of KRW 419.2 billion in 2024, and narrowed to a net loss of KRW 283 billion in 2025.

Net income including non-controlling interests was a net loss of KRW 413 billion in 2024 and KRW 292.5 billion in 2025.

On a quarterly basis, the operating loss was KRW 197.7 billion in the third quarter of 2025 and KRW 196 billion in the fourth quarter, narrowed to KRW 52.4 billion in the first quarter of 2026, widened to KRW 356.3 billion in the second quarter, and narrowed again to KRW 182.4 billion in the third quarter.

Third-quarter 2026 revenue of KRW 1.82 trillion rose 8.2% year on year. The combined attributable net loss for the last four quarters, from the fourth quarter of 2025 to the third quarter of 2026, was KRW 642.5 billion.

The debt ratio fell from 1,780.2 percent in 2022 to 1,240.8 percent in 2024 before rising to 1,370.2 percent in 2025.

The third-quarter swing to net profit reflected a KRW 460.4 billion foreign currency translation gain from a lower year-end exchange rate on a separate basis, which is a valuation gain distinct from operating performance.

05

Industry analysis

The cost structure of the airline industry depends heavily on fuel and exchange rates. Asiana Airlines reported that fuel costs in the third quarter of 2026 rose by KRW 147.2 billion year on year, which the company attributed to sustained high oil prices on a separate basis.

The won-dollar year-end settlement rate fell from 1,402 to 1,358 won, producing a foreign currency translation gain. The company expects the fourth quarter to enter a seasonally weak period, with continued volatility in oil prices and exchange rates.

In response, it plans to temporarily suspend some loss-making routes and expand codeshare operations with Korean AIR Lines to strengthen its sales network. On the industry structure side, the integration of the national carriers is the most significant change.

Korean AIR Lines received approvals from competition authorities in Singapore, South Korea, Australia and China in 2022, the United Kingdom in 2023, and Japan, the European Union and the United States in 2024.

Korean AIR Lines has stated that after integration it aims to strengthen Incheon International Airport's hub function and expand its global network to compete with the world's largest airlines.

In low-cost carriers, a combined carrier including JIN AIR and Air Busan has received approval and is scheduled to launch in March 2027, which may change competition on short and medium-haul routes.

Asiana's position is best read less as a standalone comparison and more in terms of how the combined network allocates short-haul high-yield and European routes.

06

Outlook

The merger schedule sets the merger date for December 16, 2026 and the integrated launch for December 17. After the merger, Asiana Airlines shares will cease to exist, and new Korean AIR Lines shares will be allocated to Asiana shareholders according to the exchange ratio.

The new shares are scheduled for listing on January 4, 2027, as disclosed in June 2026. On the operational side, the company said it has completed the standardization of its safety operating system, the expansion of its integrated control center and maintenance facilities, and the integration of crew training.

Adjustments to overlapping routes, new route expansion, and improvements to lounges and in-flight meals are also underway. The integration of mileage programs is under discussion with the Fair Trade Commission and other authorities, with an announcement to follow once finalized.

For the fourth quarter of 2026, plans include temporarily suspending some loss-making routes, expanding codeshare operations with Korean AIR Lines, and increasing the number of Starlink-equipped aircraft.

Korean AIR Lines has set the goal of strengthening Incheon hub functions and expanding its global network after integration. The next checkpoints are the direction of the fourth-quarter 2026 operating result and the operating margin disclosed in the first periodic report after the integration.

Bull factors

Improving passenger economics

Third-quarter 2026 passenger revenue rose 9.7% year on year and yield per passenger rose 18.1% on a separate basis. The company attributed this to expanded short-haul routes with higher returns and added European flights in the summer peak. Load factor also rose 1.2 percentage points, indicating better capacity utilization.

A path to easing financial burden

Korean AIR Lines said it had fully repaid the KRW 3.6 trillion in public funds that Asiana Airlines received. After the merger, Korean AIR Lines will succeed to all of Asiana's assets, liabilities, rights and obligations, and employees.

Korean AIR Lines expects the combined carrier to strengthen Incheon's hub function and expand its global network.

Quarterly loss swings

The operating loss narrowed from KRW 356.3 billion in the second quarter of 2026 to KRW 182.4 billion in the third quarter and was also smaller than a year earlier. Attributable net income turned to KRW 235.2 billion in the third quarter of 2026.

Whether the narrowing continues depends on the seasonally weak fourth quarter and oil and exchange rate movements.

Bear factors

Persistent operating losses

The operating loss has continued for five consecutive quarters, from the third quarter of 2025 through the third quarter of 2026. The company recorded an operating loss of KRW 345.2 billion in 2025 and still posted KRW 182.4 billion in the third quarter of 2026.

Fuel costs rose KRW 147.2 billion year on year on a separate basis, alongside higher maintenance and service investment and integration costs.

Balance sheet and cash flow

The debt ratio rose again from 1,240.8 percent in 2024 to 1,370.2 percent in 2025. Total liabilities of KRW 11.4 trillion at the end of 2025 far exceed total equity of KRW 829 billion. Operating cash flow fell from KRW 1.86 trillion in 2022 to KRW 137 billion in 2025, indicating weaker internal funding capacity.

Quality of profit

The third-quarter 2026 net profit reflected a KRW 460.4 billion foreign currency translation gain on a separate basis. Because operating results remained in deficit, the swing to profit is hard to read as an improvement in core operations.

Cargo revenue of KRW 96.6 billion was down 32.9% year on year, and the revenue base narrowed after the freighter business was sold.

Risk factors

Exchange rates and fuel

Fuel costs rose KRW 147.2 billion year on year in the third quarter of 2026 amid sustained high oil prices. A weaker won could generate valuation losses on foreign currency debt, and the third-quarter net profit relied on a translation gain from a lower exchange rate. If oil and exchange rates move unfavorably again, earnings volatility could widen.

Merger execution

Integrating the safety operating system requires approval for changes to operating standards, and follow-up procedures with overseas aviation authorities remain. The mileage integration plan is under discussion with the Fair Trade Commission and other authorities, and its timing is not yet fixed.

Because Asiana shares will be exchanged for Korean AIR Lines shares, any change in merger terms or schedule would directly affect shareholder value.

Demand and route competition

The fourth quarter of 2026 is a seasonally weak period, and the company plans to temporarily suspend some low-profit routes. If the combined low-cost carrier launches in March 2027, price competition on short-haul routes could intensify.

Until route adjustments with Korean AIR Lines are complete after the integration, there is a possibility of demand gaps during the reallocation of capacity.

Overall view

Asiana Airlines is scheduled to be integrated into Korean AIR Lines with the combined carrier launching on December 17, 2026, and the merger process has entered its final stage after shareholder approval and government authorization.

The company recorded losses in 2024 and 2025, and in the third quarter of 2026 the operating loss narrowed to KRW 182.4 billion while attributable net income turned to KRW 235.2 billion.

However, the third-quarter profit reflected a KRW 460.4 billion translation gain on a separate basis and should be distinguished from the five consecutive quarters of operating losses.

On the financial side, total liabilities at the end of 2025 far exceeded total equity and operating cash flow fell to KRW 137 billion, which remain key challenges. Positive factors include improved yield per passenger, full repayment of public funds, and expected network expansion from the integration.

Headwinds include high fuel costs, exchange rate volatility, shrinking cargo revenue, and the transition of mileage and operating systems during the merger. The next checkpoints are whether the merger is completed in December 2026 and the operating margin disclosed in the first periodic report after the integration.
