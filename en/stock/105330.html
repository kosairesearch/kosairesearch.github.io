02

Business structure

KNW was founded in 2001 and listed on KOSDAQ in September 2009. Its business divisions are electronic components materials, automotive components materials and semiconductor materials, with production sites in Paju and Ulsan in Korea and production facilities in China and Vietnam.

As of the first half of 2022, the largest share of consolidated revenue came from the semiconductor special gas supplied by subsidiary Fluorine Korea, at 65.0%. Automotive components and materials (interior trim) accounted for 28.9% and electronic components materials for 6.1% over the same period.

The sale of the US and Mexico subsidiaries was completed in February 2022, and their results were classified as discontinued operations. The company then pursued expansion in semiconductor materials, took part in a Fluorine Korea rights offering in July 2024 and secured a 100% stake.

In September 2023 it acquired a stake in KNW Materials. Semiconductor special gases are sensitive to changes in downstream semiconductor demand, while the automotive components business has seen revenue declines and wider operating losses when market conditions weaken.

The latest breakdown of revenue by division should be checked against the next periodic report's business-segment disclosure.

04

Earnings analysis

Annual revenue fell from KRW 99.6 billion in 2022 to KRW 95.4 billion in 2023, down 4.2% year on year, and then to KRW 78.5 billion in 2024, down 17.7%, which was the smallest year in the 2022 to 2025 period. Revenue rose 3.3% to KRW 81.1 billion in 2025, while 2022 was the largest year.

Operating profit fell from KRW 15.3 billion in 2022 to KRW 5.22 billion in 2023, down 65.9% year on year, and the company swung to an operating loss of KRW 2.29 billion in 2024. The operating loss widened to KRW 2.96 billion in 2025, and the company has posted losses for two consecutive years from 2024 to 2025.

The operating margin moved from 15.4% in 2022 to 5.5% in 2023, -2.9% in 2024 and -3.6% in 2025.

Net income attributable to controlling shareholders went from KRW 18.1 billion in 2022 to KRW 2.05 billion in 2023, down 88.6%, then KRW 3.44 billion in 2024, up 67.7%, before a net loss of KRW 5.77 billion in 2025 turned it into a loss.

At year-end 2025 the debt ratio stood at 118.8%, up from 82.0% in 2022, with total liabilities of KRW 145.1 billion and total equity of KRW 122.1 billion. Operating cash flow declined from KRW 14.6 billion in 2022 to an outflow of KRW 1.74 billion in 2025.

On a quarterly basis, revenue was KRW 19.9 billion in the third quarter of 2025, KRW 21.4 billion in the fourth quarter (up 7.5% quarter on quarter), KRW 16.6 billion in the first quarter of 2026 (down 22.3%), KRW 18.4 billion in the second quarter (up 10.7%) and KRW 22.7 billion in the third quarter (up 23.4% quarter on quarter and up 14.2% year on year).

Operating losses widened from KRW 464 million in the third quarter of 2025 to KRW 890 million, KRW 1.12 billion and KRW 1.84 billion through the second quarter of 2026, before the third quarter swung to operating profit of KRW 1.29 billion.

The net loss attributable to controlling shareholders was largest in the second quarter of 2026 at KRW 7.59 billion and narrowed to KRW 1.3 billion in the third quarter. The net loss for the last four quarters totaled KRW 11.9 billion.

Revenue has risen for two straight quarters since the first-quarter 2026 low, but a pre-tax loss that remained even after operating profit is the main hurdle to earnings recovery.

05

Industry analysis

The downstream markets behind KNW's revenue are semiconductors and automobiles. Semiconductor special gases are sensitive to changes in downstream chip demand, while automotive components and materials can see revenue fall and operating losses widen when vehicle production slows.

According to a corporate information site, consolidated revenue in the first half of 2026 fell 11.9% year on year and the operating loss grew 84.6% (as of September 5, 2026).

Revenue then rebounded to KRW 22.7 billion in the third quarter of 2026, up 14.2% year on year, but two consecutive quarters of growth is still a short run.

The company is classified under shipbuilding, yet shipbuilders such as HD Hyundai Heavy Industries and Hanwha Ocean move with order backlogs and newbuilding price cycles and do not overlap directly with KNW's end markets.

Industry comparisons are therefore better made against semiconductor materials and automotive components indicators than against shipbuilding indices. On the semiconductor side, domestic chip capital investment is the key variable, and on the automotive side, global vehicle production drives orders and utilization.

In special gas supply, customer qualification and equipment linkage act as barriers to entry. The company is classified as a mid-sized firm with production bases in Paju, Ulsan, China and Vietnam.

Outlook

A change in ownership is the largest variable for the company. On October 7, 2026, Eco Materials agreed to transfer 9,113,891 common shares of KNW (56.89%) to BGF, and the change in largest shareholder followed when payment was completed on October 12, 2026. The disclosed transfer price was KRW 81.75 billion.

Some reports at the time described a different structure, with BGF acquiring a 56.7% stake for KRW 50 billion through a third-party allotment of new shares, so the transaction was reported differently from the filing. The purpose of BGF's acquisition was presented as diversification through entry into new businesses.

The specific products and investment size of those new businesses still need to be confirmed through later disclosures. On the capital side, a capital reduction to cancel treasury shares was completed on April 27, 2026, reducing total issued common shares from 16,060,028 to 16,019,520.

The financial statements for the 25th fiscal year were approved at the annual general meeting on March 19, 2026. On the operating side, the key question is whether third-quarter operating profit continues into the fourth quarter and beyond.

It also needs to be checked whether the semiconductor special gas unit drives revenue recovery and whether losses in automotive components narrow. The next checkpoint is the operating margin and the size of the net loss attributable to controlling shareholders in the 2026 annual report.

Bull factors

Third-quarter operating profit turnaround

Operating profit in the third quarter of 2026 was KRW 1.29 billion, the first operating profit since the KRW 464 million operating loss in the third quarter of 2025. Revenue of KRW 22.7 billion rose for two straight quarters after the first-quarter 2026 low.

The shift from operating losses to profit is the main bullish point. Whether one profitable quarter becomes a trend needs to be confirmed with fourth-quarter 2026 results.

Semiconductor gas base and full subsidiary ownership

Fluorine Korea became a wholly owned subsidiary through a July 2024 rights offering and has supplied semiconductor special gases. Because this business accounted for 65.0% of consolidated revenue in the first half of 2022, a recovery in downstream chip demand could have a large effect on results. The company also has production bases in Paju and Ulsan.

Change of largest shareholder and diversification aim

With BGF becoming the largest shareholder, diversification through entry into new businesses was presented as the purpose of the acquisition. A capital reduction to cancel treasury shares was completed in April 2026, reducing issued shares.

The ongoing effort to streamline the capital structure is a positive financial factor. The scale and timing of the new businesses can be confirmed through later disclosures.

Bear factors

Two straight years of operating losses and margin decline

After an operating loss of KRW 2.29 billion in 2024, the loss widened to KRW 2.96 billion in 2025. The operating margin was -3.6% in 2025, below -2.9% in 2024. The margin fell from 15.4% in 2022 over four years, which is the starting point of the bearish case.

Revenue also declined from KRW 99.6 billion in 2022 to KRW 81.1 billion in 2025, which can weaken the ability to absorb fixed costs.

Five consecutive quarters of net losses for controlling shareholders

Net losses attributable to controlling shareholders have continued for five consecutive quarters, from the third quarter of 2025 through the third quarter of 2026. The KRW 7.59 billion loss in the second quarter of 2026 was the largest among the disclosed quarters. The four-quarter total is KRW 11.9 billion. Even with third-quarter operating profit, the pre-tax loss remained large enough to leave a net loss.

Rising financial burden and weaker cash flow

The debt ratio rose from 62.0% in 2024 to 118.8% in 2025, with total liabilities of KRW 145.1 billion. Operating cash flow shifted from KRW 14.6 billion in 2022 to an outflow of KRW 1.74 billion in 2025.

Cumulative revenue for the first three quarters of 2026 was down 3.20% year on year on a preliminary basis, announced November 5, 2026.
