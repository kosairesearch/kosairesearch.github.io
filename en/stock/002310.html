02

Business structure

Asia Paper Manufacturing was founded in 1958 and listed on the KOSPI in 1988 as a maker of corrugating base paper and corrugated boxes. Its business is split into three segments: paper, corrugated products and other.

On 2023 annual sales, the paper segment accounted for 53.5%, the corrugated segment for 45.3% and other operations for 1.2%. On a Q3 2024 basis the split was 53.8%, 45.1% and 1.1%, so the overall mix stayed broadly similar.

The paper segment centers on corrugating medium, and the company is described as holding the largest production capacity in that business. The corrugated segment makes corrugated board and boxes, and domestic sales accounted for 100% of those products on a 2023 basis.

The company runs a vertically integrated chain from base paper to board to boxes, supported by five subsidiaries. Its main raw materials are imported pulp and recycled imported waste paper, whose prices are affected by global economic conditions and exchange rates.

A February 2024 securities report cited domestic market shares of 17% in corrugating medium and 13% in board, and described the domestic corrugated market as an oligopoly led by a few major players.

Simple-aggregated segment sales in the first half of 2026 were KRW 311.9 billion for paper and KRW 268.5 billion for corrugated products, with corrugated at about 46% of the total. As a result, base paper prices and corrugated demand jointly drive earnings.

04

Earnings analysis

Annual sales went from KRW 1.02 trillion in 2022 to KRW 908.3 billion in 2023 (down 11.2% year on year), KRW 891.1 billion in 2024 (down 1.9%) and KRW 855.1 billion in 2025 (down 4.0%).

Sales have declined for three consecutive years from 2023 through 2025, with 2022 the largest year and 2025 the smallest in the 2022 to 2025 period.

Operating profit fell from KRW 109.4 billion in 2022 to KRW 87.6 billion in 2023 (down 20.0%) and to KRW 26.6 billion in 2024 (down 69.7%), the smallest year in the period. Operating profit recovered slightly to KRW 27.2 billion in 2025, up 2.4%, but remains far below 2022.

The operating margin went from 10.7% in 2022 and 9.6% in 2023 to 3.0% in 2024 and 3.2% in 2025. In 2024 operating profit fell far more than sales did, so margin compression was the core driver of the earnings decline.

Net income attributable to controlling shareholders was KRW 94.4 billion in 2022, KRW 81.1 billion in 2023 (down 14.1%), KRW 23.9 billion in 2024 (down 70.5%) and KRW 29.6 billion in 2025 (up 23.7%).

A 2025 year-end review noted that lower base paper sales pulled total sales down slightly, while lower energy costs and integration synergies improved profitability.

Quarterly results moved from operating profit of KRW 4.76 billion in Q3 2025 and an operating loss of KRW 810 million in Q4 2025 to operating profit of KRW 10 billion in Q1 2026, a swing back into the black.

Q2 2026 operating profit was KRW 12.4 billion, up 23.9% from the prior quarter, while Q3 2026 was KRW 10.3 billion, down 17.3% from the prior quarter but up 116.0% year on year. Quarterly sales were KRW 229.5 billion in Q2 2026 and KRW 229.2 billion in Q3 2026, the latter up 6.9% year on year.

Net income attributable to controlling shareholders over the last four quarters, from Q4 2025 to Q3 2026, totaled KRW 32.8 billion. Operating cash flow fell from KRW 116.9 billion in 2022 and KRW 132.9 billion in 2023 to KRW 83.1 billion in 2024 and KRW 77 billion in 2025.

05

Industry analysis

Corrugated boxes trade as intermediate goods, cut and printed to the specific production needs of each customer on a made-to-order basis. The corrugated industry is structured as an export-oriented sector, with exports larger than imports, and export value rose 10.9% in 2022 on a year-on-year basis.

The 2025 year-end review described corrugated board and box demand as stable.

Imported pulp and recycled imported waste paper, the main raw materials, are affected by global economic conditions and exchange rates, and pulp prices also respond to capacity additions and closures at major producing countries, pulp inventories and wood supply.

Lower energy costs were cited as a factor in improved profitability in 2025. Domestic market shares were presented as 17% in corrugating medium and 13% in board, and the company is classified as a major player in the domestic corrugated oligopoly.

Its vertically integrated chain, anchored by the largest corrugating medium capacity, is presented as its competitive base. Because domestic demand accounts for most of the corrugated segment, domestic packaging demand flows directly into results.

Corrugated segment simple-aggregated sales were KRW 268.5 billion in the first half of 2026, moving together with base paper supply. The industry backdrop combines stable demand with fluctuating raw material costs.

06

Outlook

In its value-enhancement plan disclosed on March 26, 2026, the company said it will keep working to improve profitability in order to secure dividend funds.

The same plan calls for cancelling treasury shares to raise per-share value and shareholder rights, and for maintaining results-linked shareholder returns and dividend stability. It also lays out new product development, product diversification and process efficiency as routes to stable earnings.

The dividend amount for fiscal 2025 was disclosed as up 10.03% from the prior fiscal year. The new plant investment was reported as a corrugated manufacturing plant with a scale of KRW 195.1 billion, targeted for completion in 2026.

Reports from 2026 put the new plant investment near KRW 200 billion, and net outflows in investing cash flow increased the financial burden versus the prior year. The same reports identified the timing of the new plant's contribution to results as the key variable for future earnings.

First-half 2026 consolidated sales were KRW 446.6 billion, up about 4.5% from KRW 427.3 billion a year earlier, but profitability did not improve in tandem. Q3 2026 sales rose 6.9% year on year, a sign of top-line recovery.

The 2026 annual report, the company's next regular filing, will show the progress of the plant investment and the full-year operating margin.

08

Bull factors

Quarterly profit back in the black

After an operating loss of KRW 810 million in Q4 2025, operating profit turned positive at KRW 10 billion in Q1 2026. Operating profit was KRW 12.4 billion in Q2 2026 and KRW 10.3 billion in Q3 2026, both above KRW 10 billion.

Shareholder return plan disclosed

The value-enhancement plan disclosed on March 26, 2026 covers profitability improvement to fund dividends, cancellation of treasury shares, and results-linked returns with dividend stability. The fiscal 2025 dividend amount was disclosed as up 10.03% from the prior year. The company said treasury share cancellation would raise per-share value.

Lower leverage and vertical integration

The debt-to-equity ratio fell from 28.7% in 2022 to 22.4% in 2025. Total equity at year-end 2025 was KRW 845.3 billion and total liabilities were KRW 189.2 billion. The vertically integrated chain from base paper to boxes is presented as a strength in sourcing and production linkage.

09

Bear factors

Sales down three straight years

Sales fell for three consecutive years from 2023 through 2025, with 2025 sales of KRW 855.1 billion below KRW 1.02 trillion in 2022. The 2025 year-end review attributed the overall decline to lower base paper sales. Q3 2026 sales rose 6.9% year on year but dipped 0.1% from the prior quarter.

Weaker margin structure

The operating margin fell from 10.7% in 2022 to 3.0% in 2024 and was only 3.2% in 2025. Operating profit declined from KRW 109.4 billion in 2022 to KRW 27.2 billion in 2025. Quarterly results have also shown a swing into an operating loss, as in Q4 2025 at KRW 810 million.

Production concentration and weaker cash flow

The Sejong plant received a partial work suspension order after a serious industrial accident, and the restart date was disclosed as undetermined. Operating cash flow fell from KRW 116.9 billion in 2022 to KRW 77 billion in 2025.

10

Risk factors

Raw materials and FX

Imported pulp and recycled imported waste paper, the main inputs for base paper, are exposed to global economic conditions and exchange rates. Pulp prices also move with capacity changes at major producers, inventories and wood supply. Lower energy costs helped profitability in 2025, but higher raw material prices could offset that benefit.

Plant concentration and safety

A serious industrial accident at the Sejong plant led to a partial work suspension order, and the restart date was disclosed as undetermined. On a 2024 basis, the affected production accounted for 39.12% of consolidated sales. Because a single plant carries a large share of output, any halt has an outsized effect on group sales.

New plant investment burden

A corrugated plant investment of KRW 195.1 billion was reported, with completion targeted for 2026. A 2026 report said net investing outflows of KRW 27 billion widened the burden versus the prior year.

If the new capacity contributes to results later than planned, depreciation and payback pressure could weigh on margins.

12

Overall view

Asia Paper Manufacturing's sales fell for three consecutive years from 2023 through 2025, and the operating margin declined to 3.0% in 2024 and 3.2% in 2025. Operating profit was disclosed at or above KRW 10 billion in each quarter from Q1 through Q3 2026, showing a recovery in quarterly profit.

Q3 2026 sales rose 6.9% year on year, but some reports say profitability did not improve in tandem in the first half. Positives include the return to positive quarterly operating profit, the shareholder return plan disclosed in March 2026 and a debt-to-equity ratio of 22.4% at year-end 2025.

Negatives include the long-running sales decline, the weaker margin structure, concentration at the Sejong plant and the burden of the new plant investment. The next checkpoints are the 2026 annual report, the 2027 annual shareholders' meeting and the Q1 2027 quarterly report.
