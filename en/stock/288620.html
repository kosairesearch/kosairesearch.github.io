02

Business structure

S-Prism was founded in 2014 and listed on KOSDAQ in 2018 as a hydrogen fuel cell specialist.

Its core business is building-use products based on polymer electrolyte membrane fuel cells (PEMFC), with an LNG-fueled lineup of roughly 1 kW to 25 kW according to the company, while a financial information site reports five models ranging from 1 kW to 50 kW, so the capacity range is described differently across sources.

For power generation, the company offers phosphoric acid fuel cells, and a system with 48% electrical efficiency is listed. Cumulative supply of building-use fuel cells since 2014 is more than 15 MW, and the company says it holds 21 domestic and foreign patents and has carried out 13 government R&D projects.

At its annual general meeting on March 27, 2026, the company changed its name from S-Fuelcell to S-Prism and set out a plan to expand from building fuel cells into distributed energy solutions that add mobility power-pack technology.

In September 2026 it announced a building energy integration business combining PEMFC, energy storage systems, building-integrated photovoltaics (BIPV) and heat pumps.

The large contracts disclosed in 2026 are a shop-fabrication and on-site installation contract for a Seoul redevelopment site with a major Korean construction company worth KRW 3.93 billion, and a KEPCO grid-stabilization PCS supply contract with a Korean counterparty worth KRW 3.67 billion.

The latter involves procuring PCS units from outside suppliers and delivering them under outsourced production, so its economics differ from self-made product sales. Business change is tracked by product line and individual contract rather than by segment revenue share.

04

Earnings analysis

On a disclosed annual basis, revenue went from KRW 46.8 billion in 2022 to KRW 32.5 billion in 2023 (down 30.5% year on year), KRW 33.7 billion in 2024 (up 3.5%), and KRW 15.8 billion in 2025 (down 53.1%), with 2022 the largest year and 2025 the smallest in the 2022 to 2025 window.

Operating income of KRW 1.19 billion in 2022 turned into an operating loss of KRW 2.48 billion in 2023, and the loss widened to KRW 4.14 billion in 2024 and KRW 15 billion in 2025, which makes three consecutive loss-making years from 2023 through 2025.

The operating margin fell from 2.5% in 2022 to -7.6% in 2023, -12.3% in 2024 and -95.1% in 2025.

Net income attributable to controlling shareholders moved from KRW 846 million in 2022 to a loss of KRW 2.34 billion in 2023, a loss of KRW 1.19 billion in 2024 (a smaller loss), and a loss of KRW 21.8 billion in 2025 (a wider loss).

A financial information site's earnings commentary attributes the 2025 deterioration to lower building fuel cell sales and higher costs, and says lower sales and higher expenses in power generation fuel cells and power plant operation widened the operating loss.

By quarter, the fourth quarter of 2025 posted revenue of KRW 5.36 billion (up 162.3% from the prior quarter) but an operating loss of KRW 8.38 billion and a parent-shareholder net loss of KRW 12.3 billion, the largest losses in the five quarters shown.

Revenue then came in at KRW 2.43 billion in the first quarter of 2026 (down 54.6% from the prior quarter), KRW 3.94 billion in the second quarter (up 62.3%) and KRW 3.12 billion in the third quarter (down 20.8%).

The second-quarter operating loss narrowed to KRW 733 million and parent-shareholder net income turned positive at KRW 568 million, although operating-level losses continued, so whether that turn reflects non-operating items is a point to check in the next periodic report.

Third-quarter revenue rose 52.8% year on year and the operating loss of KRW 907 million narrowed from a year earlier. The parent-shareholder net loss over the last four quarters totals KRW 17.5 billion.

The debt-to-equity ratio fell from 117.5% in 2022 to 98.6% in 2025, with year-end 2025 total equity of KRW 45.8 billion (all attributable to controlling shareholders) and total liabilities of KRW 45.2 billion. Operating cash flow swung from an inflow of KRW 6.99 billion in 2024 to an outflow of KRW 1.36 billion in 2025.

05

Industry analysis

On the demand side, power bottlenecks from expanding AI data centers have drawn attention. The independent research firm Valuefinder said in a September 2026 report that fuel cells, which can be built in a short period and generate power on site, are emerging as a realistic power alternative.

The same report said U.S. data center construction spending hit a record of roughly USD 75 billion in July 2026. The AI Data Center Promotion Special Act, which passed a National Assembly plenary vote, has also been cited as a tailwind.

On the building side, zero-energy building certification requirements are being phased in more broadly, and a proposed system revision that would recognize air-source heat as renewable energy is pushing building heating toward electrification, according to the company.

On competition, the company says its building fuel cell technology keeps it ahead in the market and cites cumulative supply of more than 15 MW. Still, how quickly data center demand turns into revenue is not separately visible in the disclosed contracts.

With a market capitalization below the sector average, the company sits in the small-cap group, so scale differences versus larger sector peers are a factor. The timing of any industry upturn showing up in earnings can be tracked through the revenue trend over the 2026 quarters.

06

Outlook

The company presented its building energy integration business, announced in September 2026, as the first project under its distributed energy transition roadmap.

It plans to select demonstration buildings and fund the demonstrations itself, and according to an Etoday report the target is a pilot product within two years of starting work, expanding later into a standard product with partners.

A Money Today report from the same period described a plan for a pilot product within one year, so the pilot timeline has been reported differently across outlets. Over the medium to long term, the company also plans to extend the business to systems that use clean hydrogen as fuel.

On orders, the shop-fabrication and installation contract signed in May 2026 runs until November 28, 2027, and the PCS supply contract signed in June 2026 runs until December 30, 2026.

The company describes the 2026 order as its first large project since the financial restructuring and says it expects the order to mark the start of an improvement in financial indicators.

The next regular filing, the 2026 annual report, will be the first reference point for the size of the full-year operating loss and operating cash flow.

On financing, the company bought back KRW 9.5 billion of third-series convertible bonds before maturity in October 2026, leaving a remaining principal of KRW 5.4998 billion. Several filings in October and November 2026 also reported reductions in holdings after bondholders exercised early redemption rights. Whether data center demand converts into actual orders is a point to verify in upcoming contract disclosures.

Quarterly losses narrowed

The operating loss narrowed from KRW 8.38 billion in the fourth quarter of 2025 to KRW 2.83 billion in the first quarter of 2026, KRW 733 million in the second quarter and KRW 907 million in the third quarter. Parent-shareholder net income turned positive at KRW 568 million in the second quarter of 2026. The direction change came within a run of five consecutive loss-making quarters.

AI data center and distributed energy demand

A September 2026 report said power bottlenecks from AI data center expansion are making fast-to-build fuel cells a realistic alternative. The company has made its building energy integration solution its first project. The AI Data Center Promotion Special Act has also been cited as a tailwind.

Bear factors

Multi-year losses and a wider deficit

The 2025 operating loss of KRW 15 billion was much larger than the KRW 4.14 billion loss in 2024, and the operating margin was -95.1%. The company has posted losses for three consecutive years from 2023 through 2025. The size of losses relative to revenue is cited as a bearish factor.

Shrinking revenue and quarterly volatility

Revenue declined from KRW 46.8 billion in 2022 to KRW 15.8 billion in 2025. Quarterly revenue in 2026 was KRW 2.43 billion, KRW 3.94 billion and KRW 3.12 billion, with large swings from quarter to quarter. Whether large contracts can sustain quarterly revenue depends on contract periods and delivery timing.

Funding burden and capital structure

Total liabilities at the end of 2025 were KRW 45.2 billion, close to total equity of KRW 45.8 billion. In October 2026 the company disclosed that it bought back KRW 9.5 billion of third-series convertible bonds before maturity using its own funds. Operating cash flow in 2025 was an outflow of KRW 1.36 billion.

Risk factors

Demand realization uncertainty

AI data center demand and the building energy integration business are not yet clearly visible in disclosed revenue. Pilot launch targets reported as two years in one outlet and one year in another add timing uncertainty. If contract gaps appear, quarterly revenue could fall again.

Liquidity and capital structure

Operating cash flow in 2025 was an outflow of KRW 1.36 billion, and the debt-to-equity ratio was 98.6%. Continued early redemption requests on convertible bonds could increase cash outflow pressure. In October 2026 the company bought back part of the bonds using its own funds.

Profitability of outsourced business

The PCS supply contract uses outsourced production, with units procured from outside suppliers. Its economics may differ from self-made products, so whether higher revenue translates into operating profit needs separate review. Contract-level margins cannot be derived from the filings alone.

Overall view

S-Prism's revenue fell from KRW 46.8 billion in 2022 to KRW 15.8 billion in 2025, and its operating loss widened to KRW 15 billion, while large contracts and narrower quarterly losses appeared in 2026.

Parent-shareholder net income turned positive at KRW 568 million in the second quarter of 2026, yet the operating loss of KRW 733 million in the same quarter shows operating-level losses continued.

Bullish factors are the contract wins in 2026, the year-on-year rise in third-quarter revenue, and growing distributed energy demand tied to AI data centers.

Bearish factors are three consecutive loss years from 2023 through 2025, a parent-shareholder net loss of KRW 17.5 billion over the last four quarters, a debt-to-equity ratio of 98.6% at the end of 2025, and convertible bond early redemption events.

On valuation, the P/E cannot be calculated on four quarters of net losses and the share price sits below book value per share, so asset-based and earnings-based readings diverge. How wide that gap remains depends on whether operating profit turns positive on a sustained quarterly basis.

The next checkpoints are the operating loss and operating cash flow in the 2026 annual report, and the delivery and payment collection under the PCS contract that ends on December 30, 2026.
