02

Business structure

Gold&S is an education services company that has operated after-school programs, education publishing, and the Talking Club language-institute franchise, and its business structure was substantially reshaped in January 2026 when it acquired the online language-education brand Siwonschool from co-major shareholder SJW International.

Siwonschool has accumulated 2.9 million members and applies AI-based learning programs across seven languages including English, offering online courses for TOEIC, OPIc, IELTS and other test-preparation exams.

The company also operates an offline language institute in Hongdae targeting university students and job seekers with customized curricula.

The largest shareholder is the SJW&Gold investment partnership, structured with SJW International—whose largest investor is Siwonschool founder Lee Si-won—and Goldmedallist each contributing 50%.

The CEO role was handed to Yang Hong-geol, formerly head of SJW International, right after the March 2026 annual general meeting, putting a major-shareholder-side executive in direct charge of management.

The company positions itself as an "AI-based comprehensive language platform" and has made building a premium AI learning product lineup a pillar of its marketing strategy.

The competitive landscape mixes online language-education platforms such as Yanadu and AI-driven language services with legacy offline language-institute franchises.

While the KOSDAQ sector classification still lists the company under media and entertainment, the actual center of revenue has shifted to the education services segment.

04

Earnings analysis

Annual revenue rose from KRW 9.49 billion in 2022 to KRW 9.95 billion in 2023, then declined for two straight years to KRW 9.59 billion in 2024 and KRW 9.02 billion in 2025.

Operating losses of KRW -1.14 billion (2022) and KRW -1.37 billion (2023) narrowed to KRW -0.49 billion in 2024 before widening again to KRW -1.88 billion in 2025.

Net income was positive only in 2024 at KRW 61 million, while 2022 (KRW -6.25 billion), 2023 (KRW -2.96 billion) and 2025 (KRW -1.47 billion) all posted losses.

Shareholders' equity fell for four consecutive years, from KRW 14.55 billion in 2022 to KRW 11.50 billion in 2025, while the debt ratio jumped from 22.3% in 2024 to 43.8% in 2025. Operating cash flow also flipped from positive KRW 292 million in 2024 back to negative KRW 155 million in 2025.

Quarterly performance took a markedly different path once the Siwonschool integration took hold in 2026: after revenue of KRW 2.11 billion, KRW 2.49 billion and KRW 2.22 billion in 2025 Q2 through Q4 with operating losses of KRW -0.43 billion, KRW -0.31 billion and KRW -0.55 billion, revenue jumped to KRW 7.65 billion in 2026 Q1, turning operating profit positive at KRW 0.65 billion and net income at KRW 0.73 billion.

In 2026 Q2 revenue was KRW 5.77 billion with operating profit of KRW 0.19 billion, yet net income expanded far more sharply to KRW 1.22 billion, suggesting a meaningful non-operating contribution whose sustainability should be confirmed in subsequent quarterly disclosures.

05

Industry analysis

The domestic online language-education market is in a phase of intensifying competition around AI-based learning tools and personalized curricula, with test-preparation English (TOEIC, OPIc, etc.) and everyday foreign-language segments both featuring multiple players such as Yanadu and AI-driven services.

The traditional offline language-institute franchise business is affected by a shrinking school-age population and slowing growth in the private-education market, making online and AI transition a survival strategy.

Gold&S's acquisition of Siwonschool, an online brand with 2.9 million cumulative members, can be read as an attempt to gain scale relative to its previous offline-centered operations. However, larger competitors are also expanding investment in AI learning content, leaving differentiation as an ongoing challenge.

On the regulatory side, the Korea Exchange is phasing in higher market-cap thresholds for KOSDAQ listing maintenance—KRW 20 billion in 2026, rising further to KRW 30 billion thereafter—increasing listing-maintenance pressure across small and mid-cap education and content companies. Gold&S is cited as a case directly affected by this tightened requirement.

06

Outlook

In its February 2026 value-up plan, the company laid out a 2026-2028 roadmap centered on concentrating education-business capabilities and pursuing M&A in AI education, targeting KRW 45 billion in revenue and KRW 3.2 billion in net income by 2028.

The original 2026 target of KRW 10 billion in revenue and a KRW 1.4 billion net loss has already been exceeded on the revenue side by first-half results of roughly KRW 13.4 billion, with net income also turning positive, leaving room for an upward target revision.

The financial strategy emphasizes core-business focus, identifying promising investment targets, and governance improvement, while the marketing strategy centers on building a premium AI learning product lineup and upgrading the online platform.

On shareholder returns, the company is considering measures to build dividend capacity by reducing an estimated accumulated deficit of about KRW 63 billion in 2026, including treasury-stock retirement in 2027 and transferring capital-reduction gains and capital surplus into retained earnings in 2028.

However, execution of this roadmap is intertwined with listing continuity, and the medium-term plan's effectiveness could be undermined if market capitalization does not recover first.

As of the latest reporting, the private placement and M&A floated as tools to lift market capitalization had not been confirmed as completed.

08

Bull factors

Visible Effect of Siwonschool Integration

Both Q1 and Q2 2026 revenue rose sharply year over year, with operating profit and net income turning positive in both quarters. The company attributes the improvement to integrated operation of content, marketing and IT infrastructure following the Siwonschool acquisition.

First-half revenue already exceeded the full-year target, showing early results from the business restructuring.

Concrete Medium-Term Targets

In its February 2026 value-up program, the company presented quantitative targets of KRW 45 billion in revenue and KRW 3.2 billion in net income by 2028.

It disclosed concrete implementation measures—including treasury-stock retirement and deficit reduction—alongside three strategic pillars in finance, marketing and R&D. Regardless of whether the targets are met, the plan's specificity gives the market a benchmark against which to track progress.

Shift Toward AI-Based Learning Content

The company has made building a premium product lineup around the AI-applied Siwonschool brand the core of its marketing strategy. Siwonschool applies AI learning tools across seven languages including English, offering greater scalability relative to the legacy offline language-institute business. The company is also pursuing M&A in AI education to diversify its business portfolio.

09

Bear factors

Administrative-Issue Designation and Listing Risk

Market capitalization stayed below KRW 20 billion for more than 25 trading days, leading to administrative-issue designation in July 2026. Failure to maintain market capitalization above KRW 20 billion for 45 consecutive trading days within 90 trading days of the designation could trigger a formal delisting cause.

The earlier capital reduction and share consolidation helped avoid penny-stock delisting rules but did not by themselves lift market capitalization.

Long-Term Balance-Sheet Weakening

Shareholders' equity fell for four consecutive years, from KRW 14.55 billion in 2022 to KRW 11.50 billion in 2025, while the debt ratio nearly doubled from 22.3% in 2024 to 43.8% in 2025.

The 2025 operating loss of KRW -1.88 billion actually widened from KRW -0.49 billion in 2024, and operating cash flow turned negative again. This trajectory illustrates the structural burden that had accumulated right up until the 2026 rebound.

Opacity in Net Income Composition

Operating profit in Q2 2026 was only KRW 0.19 billion while net income reached KRW 1.22 billion, suggesting a substantial contribution from non-operating items. The nature of this gap—whether one-off or repeatable—has not yet been clarified, warranting a qualitative assessment of future quarterly results.

The company's two designations as an unfaithful-disclosure entity within the past year also merit caution regarding disclosure reliability.

Risk factors

Delisting

If the market-capitalization requirement is not met within the prescribed period after administrative-issue designation, a formal delisting process could begin. The company has mentioned a private placement and M&A as potential tools to restore market capitalization, but confirmed execution has not yet been verified.

Because the KOSDAQ listing-maintenance market-cap threshold is scheduled to rise further through 2027, regulatory pressure could persist.

Governance and Related-Party Transactions

The core growth driver, the Siwonschool business, was acquired from an affiliate of the major shareholder, and the current CEO is also a major-shareholder-side figure.

This close linkage between controlling ownership and management leaves room for potential conflicts of interest with minority shareholders in future related-party transactions or funding decisions.

Internal discussions reportedly took place over whether the major shareholder would inject additional funds, which has been flagged as a governance-related risk.

Disclosure and Earnings Quality

The company has been designated an unfaithful-disclosure entity twice within the past year, and under tightened disclosure-violation delisting rules, even a single serious violation can trigger a delisting review.

If quarters like Q2 2026, where net income far exceeded operating profit, recur, market doubts about earnings sustainability could grow. Basic disclosure-risk management, such as meeting periodic report filing deadlines, also requires ongoing verification.

12

Overall view

Gold&S has shown early signs of successful business restructuring, with revenue and net income improving markedly in the first half of 2026 following the Siwonschool education business acquisition.

However, the balance-sheet weakening accumulated through 2025—declining equity and a rising debt ratio—along with the disproportionate expansion of Q2 2026 net income relative to operating profit, are points that warrant further scrutiny regarding earnings quality.

The most critical variable is the administrative-issue designation stemming from a market-capitalization shortfall; failure to meet the market-cap requirement within the prescribed period could make listing continuity itself uncertain.

The company has presented quantitative targets through 2028 along with concrete plans such as treasury-stock retirement and deficit reduction, but executing this plan is contingent on first restoring market capitalization.

The governance structure, with a major-shareholder-side figure now leading management, and the two unfaithful-disclosure designations over the past year, are additional factors to consider.

Before forming any investment judgment, it is important to continuously verify whether the listing-maintenance requirement is being met and to monitor the qualitative composition of future quarterly earnings.
