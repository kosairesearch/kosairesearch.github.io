Nexteel: Profit Recovery Amid Tariffs and Wind Investment

Nexteel's operating profit fell 36.8% in 2025, but since Q1 2026 the company has returned to quarterly profit and sales have risen for three straight quarters, putting its earnings recovery at the center of the story.

02

Business structure

Nexteel produces steel pipe. Meritz Securities said in a March 2025 report that the combined domestic capacity of its two Pohang plants and one Gyeongju plant is about 1.5 million tons.

The same report said new lines under construction at the third Pohang plant include 50,000 tons of spiral pipe and 180,000 tons of roll-bending pipe. Overseas, the company has run a 120,000-ton plant in Houston since mid-2022, and the company says most products made there are supplied to local customers.

Exports are a large share of the business: export sales of KRW 308.6 billion in Q3 2024 accounted for 72.5% of total sales. Media reports say about 90% of exports go to North America, mainly the United States.

Its product lines include electric resistance welded (ERW) pipe, a 26-inch large-diameter line running since July 2023, and cryogenic-toughness line pipe. The business is shifting from traditional oil and gas toward offshore wind substructures and energy pipe for LNG, CCUS and hydrogen.

Roll-bending equipment carries an investment of KRW 219.1 billion, and the company has hired outside talent to build its offshore wind organization. SeAH Steel and Husteel are the main peers cited in comparisons.

A FerroTimes tally for Q3 2025 found that among five specialty pipe makers, only Nexteel and Husteel were profitable.

04

Earnings analysis

Annual revenue declined from KRW 668.4 billion in 2022 to KRW 619.1 billion in 2023, KRW 552.4 billion in 2024 and KRW 551 billion in 2025, a three-year consecutive decline from 2023 through 2025. Operating profit fell from KRW 181.3 billion in 2022 to KRW 39.9 billion in 2025, a 36.8% drop in 2025 alone.

Operating margin moved from 27.1% to 25.4%, 11.4% and 7.2% over the same years. Profit attributable to controlling shareholders fell to KRW 34.8 billion in 2024 before recovering modestly to KRW 36 billion in 2025, a 3.7% increase.

Media reports said 2024 included a one-off item: a refund of U.S. anti-dumping duties of more than KRW 40 billion was recognized at once, lifting the Q2 2024 operating margin above 30%, and estimates put the underlying margin at 3% to 4% without it.

The company attributed the 2025 profit decline to the impact of steel tariffs and weak demand in downstream industries.

In quarterly terms, operating profit of KRW 5.44 billion in Q3 2025 turned into an operating loss of KRW 3.04 billion in Q4 2025, when net income attributable to controlling shareholders was a loss of KRW 3.25 billion.

Q1 2026 revenue of KRW 100.6 billion, operating profit of KRW 2.67 billion and controlling-shareholder net income of KRW 2.76 billion marked a return to profit. Q2 revenue rose to KRW 167.5 billion, up 66.4% from the prior quarter, but controlling-shareholder net income fell to KRW 449 million.

Q3 2026 revenue of KRW 199.5 billion rose 63.6% year on year and marked a third straight quarterly increase, while operating profit was KRW 2.3 billion, down 57.8% year on year. Controlling-shareholder net income was KRW 10.4 billion in Q3 2026, up 9.4% year on year, and the last four quarters totaled KRW 10.4 billion.

Because net income is well above operating profit, the non-operating items behind that gap should be checked in the notes to the periodic report. According to wisereport data, revenue in the first half of 2026 fell 19.9% year on year, operating profit fell 78.3% and net income fell 89.2%.

Operating profit for the first three quarters of 2026 adds up to about KRW 10.46 billion, roughly a quarter of the KRW 39.9 billion posted for full-year 2025.

05

Industry analysis

Domestic steel pipe demand depends heavily on construction activity. In explaining its Q3 2025 results, the company cited the seasonal off-season, a prolonged construction slump and higher raw material prices as burdens on demand and profitability. On exports, U.S. tariffs are the key variable.

As of Q3 2025, a 50% U.S. steel tariff was partly applied, and the company said that once the tariff applied in full in Q4 it would be difficult. A 2025 media report said low-priced Chinese steel had kept the industry weak for a long period, so margin pressure across domestic producers is cited as structural.

On raw materials, the company said that after anti-dumping duties were imposed on imported inputs, its use of domestic raw materials rose above 95%. New demand is seen coming from offshore wind, LNG, CCUS and hydrogen-related high-value pipe.

Hanwha Investment & Securities analysed in its Q3 2025 review that sales from new offshore wind and LNG projects would start in Q4. Against peers, Nexteel's distinguishing feature is its local production base through the Houston plant.

A 2025 media report argued that local production could be a relative benefit under tariff policy. However, the company said weak U.S. demand and rising competition make it difficult to secure new orders. Sales rose for three straight quarters from Q1 2026, but first-half 2026 revenue remained lower year on year.

06

Outlook

The next periodic report is the 2026 annual report, which will set the full-year operating margin and controlling-shareholder net income.

In July 2026 the company decided to acquire shares in pipe maker Tex-Isle for KRW 73.78 billion, and it said the aim was to strengthen global competitiveness and secure a U.S. local production and distribution base.

A KRW 50 billion seventh private convertible bond issuance was completed in June 2026, and in July 2026 the company decided to invest KRW 4 billion in a private equity partnership.

Media reported in October 2025 that the KRW 219.1 billion roll-bending line had been delayed by two years from its planned 2026 completion to 2028. The same report said sales from the line would take at least three years to materialise. Export diversification is another task.

The company said it would widen export destinations in view of weak U.S. demand and rising competition. Domestically, a 2025 report cited the company's expectation that structural pipe orders could rise as semiconductor fab construction expands.

Q3 2026 revenue of KRW 199.5 billion rose, but operating profit was KRW 2.3 billion, so the next thing to watch is whether higher sales translate into operating profit. At the annual general meeting on March 31, 2026, financial statements were approved and a dividend item was handled.

08

Bull factors

U.S. local production base

The 120,000-ton Houston plant has been operating since mid-2022 and serves as a tariff response for a company with heavy North American exposure. A 2025 media report relayed the company's view that U.S.-made products carry higher selling prices and margins. Analysts at the time also argued that the company could benefit relatively under tariff policy.

Improved financial structure

The debt-to-equity ratio fell from 106.1% in 2022 to 50.7% in 2025, and total equity at the end of 2025 was KRW 464 billion. Operating cash flow was positive in both 2024 (KRW 60.5 billion) and 2025 (KRW 23.8 billion). Quarterly profitability has returned since Q1 2026.

New business options

The 26-inch large-diameter line has run since July 2023, and the company is preparing to expand into project-based work such as offshore wind substructures. A 2025 media report said the large-diameter project team was elevated to a project division reporting directly to the CEO after outside talent was hired. If demand for large-diameter pipe materialises, the revenue base could widen.

09

Bear factors

Continued margin decline

Operating margin declined from 27.1% in 2022 to 7.2% in 2025. According to wisereport, first-half 2026 operating profit fell 78.3% year on year. Q3 2026 operating profit was only KRW 2.3 billion.

Tariff exposure and export concentration

Export sales were 72.5% of total sales in Q3 2024, and media reports say about 90% of exports go to North America. The company said in late 2025 that the full 50% U.S. tariff would make Q4 difficult. Steel tariffs were also named as a cause of the 2025 profit decline. Export diversification remains an open task.

Burden of new business investment

The KRW 219.1 billion roll-bending investment has been pushed back to 2028, and analysts expect at least three years before it generates sales. A 2025 report said the Houston plant ran at 30% to 40% utilization in 2024. The KRW 50 billion convertible bond issued in June 2026 remains a variable for the capital structure.

10

Risk factors

Tariffs and trade policy

U.S. steel tariffs affect export prices and demand and were named as a direct cause of the 2025 profit decline. If the tariff level or scope changes, North American export results could swing again. The more production is local, the more the nature of tariff exposure changes.

Execution of new businesses

The roll-bending line has already seen its completion pushed back by two years. If new orders are not secured in sufficient volume, recovery of the equipment investment could be delayed. The timing of offshore wind and LNG project sales remains the largest uncertainty in the company's earnings outlook.

Capital structure and share price volatility

A KRW 50 billion convertible bond was issued in June 2026, and conversion would increase the share count. A 2025 report noted that the share price has moved sharply on tariff news and theme-driven expectations. Conversion terms and dilution scale should be checked in future disclosures.

12

Overall view

Nexteel's reported results show revenue and operating profit both declining since the 2022 peak, with operating margin falling from 27.1% to 7.2%. The company attributed the 2025 decline to steel tariffs and weak downstream demand.

In 2026, sales have risen for three straight quarters since the return to profit in Q1, but Q3 operating profit fell to KRW 2.3 billion. Strengths cited include the Houston plant and North American export base, large-diameter pipe capacity for offshore wind, and a debt-to-equity ratio that fell from 106.1% to 50.7%.

Weaknesses include exposure to tariff policy, the delay of the roll-bending line to 2028, weaker demand, and the capital structure change from the convertible bond. Multiple readings are divided because the share price has moved while earnings have declined.

The pace of earnings recovery and the timing of new business sales are the factors seen as dividing those readings. The next checkpoints are the operating margin in the 2026 annual report, Q1 2027 results, and the completion schedule of the roll-bending line.
