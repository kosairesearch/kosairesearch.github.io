02

Business structure

anymedi builds patient-specific surgical solutions from medical imaging using 3D printing and AI, and was founded in 2016 as a spin-off from Asan Medical Center. Its main products are surgical simulators, surgical guides, and custom implants. The company listed on the Konex market in December 2022.

Based on the 2024 annual report cited in a KRX KIND technology analysis report, surgical guides were KRW 0.532 billion or 39.1% of sales, prototypes KRW 0.132 billion or 9.7%, simulators KRW 0.079 billion or 5.8%, and merchandise KRW 0.054 billion or 4.0%.

The excerpted table does not show the remaining revenue lines, so the weight of implants and other segments for 2024 could not be confirmed. In 2022, implants, including the custom nasal implant brand innofit, were the largest line at 43.2% of sales.

The report cites clinical use of more than 400 breast cancer surgical guides, more than 600 joint replacement guides, and more than 3,600 nasal implants. The company holds five devices that passed new-health-technology or innovative-health-technology assessments.

It also received KRW 2.85 billion in global collaboration project funding from the Ministry of Trade, Industry and Energy for 2023-2026 to run clinical work in the United States. A company-data platform lists Cellumed, Cubelabs, and Bioorigin among similar companies.

Its business differs in nature from large biopharma names such as Samsung Biologics and Celltrion, so they are not direct comparables.

03

Recent trends

According to the KRX KIND technology analysis report, revenue was KRW 0.66 billion in 2022, KRW 1.12 billion in 2023, and KRW 1.36 billion in 2024, a 21.0% increase in 2024.

The operating loss was KRW 6.47 billion in 2022, KRW 3.96 billion in 2023, and KRW 2.30 billion in 2024, with a net loss of KRW 3.13 billion in 2024. The same table shows a debt ratio of 618.1% for 2024.

A KODATA-based company profile shows 2025 revenue in a band of KRW 1.6 billion to under KRW 2.1 billion and an operating loss in a band of KRW 0.9 billion to under KRW 1.1 billion. These are range values, so the confirmed figures in the annual report filed on March 24, 2026 should be checked separately.

At its Konex listing, the company planned to reach break-even in the second half of 2023 and to attempt a KOSDAQ listing in the first quarter of 2024. Operating losses continued through 2024 and the shares remain on Konex as of October 2026, so those plans were not realized.

In February 2025 the company raised a KRW 1.14 billion short-term loan from Industrial Bank of Korea under a Korea Technology Finance Corporation guarantee with an investment option. On April 10, 2026 it resolved to issue 540,000 new shares to a third party to raise KRW 270 million in operating funds.

The issue price was KRW 500 against a reference price of KRW 400. On the same day, shareholder Company K Partners reported selling 176,869 shares on the open market, leaving its stake at 7.14%.

An intraday quote page on June 24, 2026 showed KRW 320, a 52-week range of KRW 218 to KRW 2,585, and a six-month change of -83.64%. On August 10 the stock was designated an investment-warning issue after a sharp rise, and it was designated again on October 2.

Trading was halted for one day on October 14 after the price rose more than 20% over two days following the designation. The October 27, 2026 close of KRW 210 is below the 52-week low of KRW 218 shown in the June 24 data.

04

Outlook

Seoul Credit Rating & Information assessed that the business benefits from digital healthcare and precision medicine policies, research funding, and national health insurance fee coverage, while regulatory approval and new-health-technology assessment procedures are complex.

The company said it completed a deep-learning image analysis and surgical-planning automation module on its orthopedic guide software platform and plans to expand into AI surgical robots. A company official said revenue grew more than 20% from 2023 despite the medical disruption that began in March 2024.

The April 2026 rights-offering filing lists research spending for AI solution development among other uses of funds. However, the revenue contribution and launch progress of the robot business could not be confirmed in this research.

One news report notes that medical AI companies should be judged on hospital adoption scale, recurring revenue, and R&D burden as well as technology. The company held an investor relations meeting on May 6, 2026.

Quarterly detail is limited under the Konex disclosure regime, so whether the operating loss narrows further is best checked in the next annual filing. Room for operating margin improvement depends on how fast hospital adoption of surgical guides and implants grows relative to the fixed-cost base.

05

Bull factors

Clinical record and certifications

The company holds five devices that passed new-health-technology or innovative-health-technology assessments. Clinical use in the hundreds to thousands of cases has been reported for breast cancer guides, joint replacement guides, and nasal implants.

The approval and insurance-listing history can be read as an entry barrier that latecomers cannot match quickly. It could support revenue conversion if hospital prescriptions expand.

Consecutive loss reduction

The operating loss shrank from KRW 6.47 billion in 2022 to KRW 3.96 billion in 2023 and KRW 2.30 billion in 2024. Over the same period revenue grew from KRW 0.66 billion to KRW 1.36 billion. Revenue growth together with cost savings points to room for improvement in business metrics. The 2025 range values also sit at a smaller operating loss than 2024.

Expansion into AI surgical robots

The company said it is developing a marker-free AI surgical robot for joint surgery. It explained that its existing custom surgical guides demonstrated high precision in shoulder replacement and other procedures in clinical work at major domestic hospitals.

Collaboration with Olympus, Pentax, and Boston Scientific on endoscopy simulator modules was also reported. If the new business delivers, revenue mix could diversify.

06

Bear factors

Small revenue and continued losses

Revenue of KRW 1.36 billion in 2024 was smaller than that year's operating loss of KRW 2.30 billion. The company posted net losses for three years in a row. The break-even target for the second half of 2023, set at listing, was not met based on reported results. The timing of a return to profit is not yet confirmed by filings.

Weak balance sheet

The 2024 debt ratio was 618.1%. A company-data platform notes the current ratio fell from 76.5% in 2023 to 16.9% in 2025. The company planned new borrowing and equity offerings and raised a KRW 1.14 billion short-term loan in February 2025. Short-term repayment capacity is weak, so reliance on further funding is high.

Dilution and holder changes

The April 2026 rights offering allotted 540,000 new shares to a third party. Filings also show rights-offering decisions in August and September 2025. Around the same time, shareholder Company K Partners sold 176,869 shares on the open market. Repeated offerings and holder changes can be read as dilution and supply pressure for existing shareholders.

07

Risk factors

Market and liquidity

A company-data platform reported a 15.00% rise on a trade of just four shares and noted that small-volume stocks can show large percentage moves on little trading. Traded value on October 27, 2026 was only KRW 185,850. Trading on Konex is thin, so transacting at a desired price can be difficult. The current market capitalization of KRW 2.0 billion compares with KRW 35.9 billion at the listing price.

When the stock was designated an investment-warning issue on August 10, 2026, the exchange noted that trading could be halted if the price rose further. It was designated again on October 2. A one-day trading halt was actually imposed on October 14. Repeated market measures can disrupt trading continuity.

Business and policy

Seoul Credit Rating & Information assessed that continued R&D investment is required amid complex procedures such as regulatory approval and new-health-technology assessment, and competition from global firms.

As in the medical disruption that began in March 2024, lower surgery volumes at large hospitals can affect demand for guides and implants. The AI surgical robot is a development-stage business, and its commercialization timeline and revenue contribution are unconfirmed. With about 35 employees as of September 2026, the organization must handle R&D and sales at the same time.

08

Overall view

anymedi is a small Konex company with a distinct business area, patient-specific surgical solutions built on 3D printing and AI, and a record of clinical use and certifications.

On reported results, revenue grew from KRW 0.66 billion to KRW 1.36 billion between 2022 and 2024, while the operating loss shrank from KRW 6.47 billion to KRW 2.30 billion.

On the other hand, a 2024 debt ratio of 618.1%, repeated small equity offerings, and an outside analysis showing a 2025 current ratio of 16.9% indicate that the financial burden remains.

In 2026 the stock received investment-warning designations and a one-day trading halt, and with traded value of only KRW 185,850 on October 27 the market is thin and prices move widely.

Confirmed 2025 results and the commercialization of the AI surgical robot were not verified in this research and are items to check in future filings. Business growth, financial stability, and the trading environment point in different directions, so readers can weigh each item for themselves.
