02

Business structure

Nexen Tire manufactures and sells passenger car and SUV tires. As of the third quarter of 2026, Europe accounted for 43% of sales, followed by North America at 21%, Korea at 18% and other regions at 18%.

Tires 18 inches and above made up 41.5% of sales, up 3.4 percentage points year on year, with the share at 32.2% in Europe and back in the 50% range in the United States.

All-weather and winter tires are the seasonal lines, and cumulative January to September 2026 sales grew 31.3% for all-weather tires and 11.7% for winter tires.

The company is widening original equipment supply, starting with the new Audi A6 using its Enpera Sport tire, and with new Kia Sorento and Hyundai Avante and Tucson models.

Production is centered on the Yangsan plant in Gyeongnam, while research and development is run from the central research institute in the Magok industrial complex in Seoul. Customers split between automakers under OE contracts and the replacement market, where distribution networks and brands matter.

Competition hinges on development capability in high-inch and all-season segments and on OE approvals. With a large share of sales in Europe, EU environmental rules and trade measures directly shape production and product supply strategy.

04

Earnings analysis

Annual sales rose from KRW 2.6 trillion in 2022 to KRW 3.19 trillion in 2025, and grew for three consecutive years from 2023 to 2025. Sales in 2025 rose 12.0% year on year and were the largest of the 2022 to 2025 period.

Operating profit moved from an operating loss of KRW 54.3 billion in 2022 to KRW 187 billion in 2023, a swing into profit. It then fell 7.9% to KRW 172.1 billion in 2024 and 1.1% to KRW 170.3 billion in 2025, declining for two consecutive years.

Operating margin went from -2.1% in 2022 to 6.9% in 2023, 6.0% in 2024 and 5.3% in 2025, so margins have eased since the 2023 peak even as sales grew.

Net income attributable to controlling shareholders moved from a loss of KRW 27.8 billion in 2022 to KRW 102.8 billion in 2023, then rose 22.9% to KRW 126.4 billion in 2024 and 19.4% to KRW 150.8 billion in 2025.

On a quarterly basis, operating profit peaked at KRW 54.2 billion in the first quarter of 2026 and then declined in the second quarter to KRW 34.3 billion and in the third quarter to KRW 18.1 billion. Third-quarter sales were KRW 814.8 billion, down 8.6% from the prior quarter but up 4.4% from a year earlier.

The cost of sales ratio rose to 74.0%, and the company attributed the pressure to higher raw material prices and ocean freight linked to the Middle East conflict, along with a stronger won.

Net income attributable to controlling shareholders went from KRW 216 million in the second quarter to a controlling-shareholder net loss of KRW 46.6 billion in the third quarter. Net income including non-controlling interests, at KRW 151.2 billion in 2025, is a different figure from controlling-shareholder net income.

The debt ratio fell from 147.1% in 2022 to 129.2% in 2025, with year-end 2025 total liabilities of KRW 2.66 trillion against total equity of KRW 2.05 trillion.

Operating cash flow rose from -KRW 99.4 billion in 2022 to KRW 351.4 billion in 2025, a cash generation trend that has held up apart from the quarterly profit swings.

05

Industry analysis

The tire industry is highly sensitive to raw material and logistics costs. The company said that higher raw material prices and ocean freight, linked to the Middle East conflict, increased its cost burden.

It also pointed to weaker market demand, which makes it hard to pass costs through to prices while expanding volume. A stronger won reduced the won-converted earnings of export-heavy companies.

High-inch and high-performance tires can carry higher value than standard models, but when raw material and freight costs rise, the margin benefit from higher sales can be limited. On trade, the EU imposed final anti-dumping duties of 4.3% to 45.3% on Chinese passenger car and light commercial tires in July 2026.

Nexen Tire, with the largest share of sales in Europe, has to factor this shift into its production locations and product-by-product supply strategy.

Environmental rules are also tightening, and the company has built a supply chain management system to address the EU Deforestation Regulation (EUDR) and the Empowering Consumers for the Green Transition directive (ECGT). On demand, new vehicle supply and seasonal tire demand are supporting sales volume.

A direct comparison with domestic peers is hard to make from disclosed figures, so differentiation currently rests on the expanding share of high-inch and all-season products.

06

Outlook

The company said it expects higher sales of high-inch, all-weather and winter tires to translate into a profit recovery once raw material and freight costs ease. Improving product mix is central to its strategy, with a stated push toward higher-value products.

On regulation, it has built a supply chain management system to respond to the EUDR and ECGT and is checking product and marketing compliance across the board. OE supply is also expanding, with supply started for the new Audi A6, Kia Sorento, and new Hyundai Avante and Tucson models.

How much these models contribute to sales will show up in upcoming results. Whether the 74.0% cost of sales ratio in the third quarter eases is the key marker for an operating profit recovery. Compared with the 5.3% operating margin in 2025, the current level is low.

The company highlighted whether raw material and logistics costs stabilize and whether mix improvement translates into higher operating profit.

On volume, the expansion of high-inch shares in Europe and North America needs to continue, and the effect of new all-weather and winter launches will show in the next season's sales.

The 2026 annual results and the business report will fix full-year figures, including the fourth quarter, and that will be the point to recheck the annual trend in mix and cost ratios.

08

Bull factors

Sales growth and a richer product mix

Sales have stayed in the KRW 800 billion range for four straight quarters, and third-quarter 2026 sales rose 4.4% year on year. Tires 18 inches and above reached 41.5% of sales, and all-weather tire sales rose 31.3% in cumulative January to September 2026. A shift toward higher-value products is the core of the bullish case.

Europe-led growth and wider OE supply

Third-quarter European sales reached KRW 349.1 billion, up 8.8% year on year, and made up 43% of total sales. New OE supply began for the new Audi A6, Kia Sorento, and new Hyundai Avante and Tucson models. A broader OE customer base supports the stability of sales volume.

Room for margin recovery if costs ease

The company said it expects the product mix benefit to lead to a profit recovery once raw material and freight costs ease. Operating margin rose to 6.9% in 2023, so a recovery path has been seen once before under more favorable cost conditions. Whether this expectation materializes is the next point to check.

Bear factors

Sharp profit drop in the third quarter

Third-quarter operating profit was KRW 18.1 billion, down 61.1% year on year and 47.4% from the prior quarter. The cost of sales ratio rose 2.6 percentage points to 74.0%. A controlling-shareholder net loss of KRW 46.6 billion marks a swing into loss and signals weaker earnings capacity.

Cost and currency pressure

The company cited higher raw material prices and ocean freight linked to the Middle East conflict, along with a stronger won, as factors weighing on profitability. A stronger won lowers the won value of overseas sales.

When costs rise faster than sales, operating profit can fall even as revenue grows, which makes the cost structure a burden.

Europe concentration and regulatory burden

With Europe at 43% of sales, regional concentration is high. The EU imposed final anti-dumping duties on Chinese passenger car and light commercial tires in July 2026, and compliance with environmental rules such as the EUDR and ECGT is expanding. Regulatory costs and trade changes could weigh on European profitability.

Risk factors

Raw materials and freight

Raw material prices and ocean freight rose together due to external factors such as the Middle East conflict, lifting the cost of sales ratio to 74.0%. If raw material and logistics costs stay high for long, the benefit of mix improvement may not reach operating profit. How far cost increases can be passed on to selling prices is the key question.

Currency and profitability

The company said a stronger won weighed on profitability. If currency direction does not change, pressure on won-converted profit could persist. Operating margin eased from 6.9% in 2023 to 5.3% in 2025, so currency and cost pressure together could slow the pace of margin recovery.

EU regulation and trade

To address the EUDR and ECGT, the company has built a supply chain management system and is checking compliance across products and marketing. With Europe at 43% of sales, compliance costs and trade measures could feed directly into results. Anti-dumping duties on Chinese tires could also reshape market structure and need monitoring.

Overall view

Nexen Tire has kept sales in the KRW 800 billion range for four straight quarters through the third quarter of 2026, with growth led by Europe and high-inch and all-weather products.

Operating profit, however, peaked at KRW 54.2 billion in the first quarter of 2026 and then declined in the second and third quarters to KRW 18.1 billion. The main drivers are a cost of sales ratio of 74.0%, higher raw material and freight costs, and a stronger won.

Sales rose for three consecutive years from 2023 to 2025, and controlling-shareholder net income grew in both 2024 and 2025. The third quarter of 2026, however, brought a controlling-shareholder net loss of KRW 46.6 billion, so the earnings direction is mixed.

Bullish factors are the product mix shift, wider OE supply and room for recovery if costs ease. Bearish factors are the cost and currency structure, concentration in Europe, and regulatory and trade burdens.

The next checkpoints are fourth-quarter and full-year 2026 results, and the cost ratio and operating margin in the 2026 business report.
