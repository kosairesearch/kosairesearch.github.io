02

Business structure

Geumhwa PSC was founded in 1981 as a plant construction contractor and entered power plant maintenance in 1995. It expanded into water treatment through a 2022 contract with Ecobit, and in February 2025 it acquired Ecobit Engineering, which was renamed Geumhwa Water Solutions.

Routine maintenance at power plants is the core business, accounting for 55.30% of sales in the Gibon-Monitor aggregation, followed by power plant equipment at 18.63%, water treatment and wastewater at 11.37%, overseas plant work at 9.02%, and other at 5.68%.

In the sales-mix series as of December 2024, domestic routine maintenance was the largest at 73.22%, with overseas plant work at 11.03% and water treatment and wastewater at 8.07%. Overhaul work, which involves stopping a generator and fully disassembling equipment for inspection, is performed on an annual schedule.

The company website lists many state-owned utility plants, including Korea South-East Power plants at Gangneung Anin and Yeongheung, Korea Southern Power's Samcheok Green Power, Korea East-West Power's Dangjin plant, and Korea West Power's Taean plant.

Private-sector plant work includes SK E&S's Hanam, Paju and Wirye plants, Byeollae Energy, and Daeryun Power's Yangju facility. Because customers are concentrated among power generators, the timing and scale of maintenance orders drive quarterly results.

In the Gibon-Monitor breakdown, overseas plant work is classified as export and the remaining segments as domestic. The workforce was 1,182 as of June 30, 2026, reflecting a labor-intensive structure.

Among listed companies in the same energy and power sector, KEPCO Plant Service & Engineering also handles power plant maintenance.

04

Earnings analysis

Annual revenue rose from KRW 248.3 billion in 2022 to KRW 337.6 billion in 2023 (up 35.9%), was nearly flat at KRW 340.2 billion in 2024 (up 0.8%), and climbed to KRW 423.4 billion in 2025 (up 24.5%).

Revenue increased for three consecutive years from 2023 to 2025, and 2025 was the largest year of 2022 to 2025 while 2022 was the smallest.

Operating profit was KRW 27.9 billion in 2022, KRW 43.5 billion in 2023 (up 55.8%), KRW 36.2 billion in 2024 (down 16.7%), and KRW 43.8 billion in 2025 (up 21.0%), with 2025 the largest year.

The operating margin rose from 11.2% in 2022 to 12.9% in 2023, then eased to 10.6% in 2024 and 10.3% in 2025, so margins did not keep pace with sales growth.

Profit attributable to owners was KRW 27.8 billion in 2022, KRW 38 billion in 2023 (up 36.7%), and KRW 40 billion in 2024 (up 5.4%), the peak year, before falling 23.6% to KRW 30.6 billion in 2025. Net income including non-controlling interests was reported at the same figures as attributable profit in each year.

The gap in 2025, when operating profit rose while net income fell, is a point to check in the non-operating items of the annual report. Operating cash flow was KRW 8.94 billion in 2022, KRW 40.2 billion in 2023, and KRW 74.6 billion in 2024, before turning to KRW -24.7 billion in 2025.

On a quarterly basis, revenue rose from KRW 93.8 billion in the third quarter of 2025 to KRW 132.1 billion in the fourth quarter (up 40.8%), with operating profit rising from KRW 3.76 billion to KRW 15.5 billion.

In the first quarter of 2026, revenue fell 35.7% to KRW 84.9 billion and operating profit fell 91.7% to KRW 1.28 billion, before the second quarter recovered to KRW 137 billion in revenue (up 61.4%) and KRW 16.8 billion in operating profit.

The third quarter of 2026 showed revenue of KRW 119.8 billion (down 12.6% from the prior quarter, up 27.7% year on year) and operating profit of KRW 11.3 billion (down 33.1% from the prior quarter, up 199.3% year on year). Profit attributable to owners totaled KRW 34.3 billion over the last four quarters.

Gibon-Monitor aggregated first-half 2026 consolidated figures as revenue up 12.4% and operating profit down 26.2% year on year, as of September 7, 2026.

05

Industry analysis

The 11th Basic Plan for Electricity Supply and Demand projects growth centered on renewable energy and LNG, hydrogen and nuclear generation.

The decline in coal-fired capacity, however, weighs on the maintenance volume base, which is why the company set minimizing the impact of lower coal-fired generation as its first value-up goal.

The coal phase-out policy and renewable expansion are raising demand for high-quality service and technical capability in plant maintenance. Tighter environmental rules and higher water-quality requirements form the demand base for the water treatment segment.

The company is targeting routine maintenance of LNG combined-cycle and nuclear plants as new order sources, placing its portfolio where the generation mix shift meets its service business.

Maintenance orders from generators tend to follow long-term contracts and regular overhaul cycles, which makes order flow relatively predictable, although volumes vary with plant types and operating schedules.

Among listed companies in the same sector, KEPCO Plant Service & Engineering also handles power plant maintenance and serves as a peer for comparison. Supportive factors include life extension of aging equipment and demand tied to LNG conversion, while the main drag is lower volume from reduced coal-fired capacity.

Outlook

In its March 2026 value-up plan, the company named LNG combined-cycle and new nuclear routine maintenance orders as its main tasks. To support this, it said it would strengthen client trust through training of specialized technical staff and responsibility-based maintenance.

As a response to policy and business environment changes, it set maintaining a stable financial structure and cash generation as a task, including management of held assets and tighter control of execution budgets at existing sites. On dividends, it aimed for stable and sustainable payouts within distributable profit.

The March 2026 general shareholders meeting approved the 45th fiscal year financial statements and the disposition of retained earnings, including a cash dividend.

Gibon-Monitor reported that the company has responded to market expansion in water treatment through large project orders and ZLD (zero liquid discharge) technology in the first half of 2026. The second-quarter 2026 results materials were posted on the IR page on September 21, 2026.

The scale and timing of new order disclosures remain to be confirmed. The next checkpoints are whether LNG combined-cycle and nuclear routine maintenance orders appear in formal disclosures, and when that volume is reflected in revenue after 2027.

Bull factors

Revenue growth on a maintenance base

Revenue grew from KRW 248.3 billion in 2022 to KRW 423.4 billion in 2025 and rose for three consecutive years from 2023 to 2025. Operating profit of KRW 43.8 billion in 2025 was the largest of 2022 to 2025.

Routine and overhaul maintenance account for a large share of sales, so volume comes in along regular maintenance cycles. Second-quarter 2026 revenue of KRW 137 billion was the highest of the last five reported quarters.

Balance sheet headroom

The debt-to-equity ratio fell from 34.4% in 2022 to 27.4% in 2025. Total liabilities were KRW 105.5 billion at the end of 2025 against total equity of KRW 385.2 billion, of which KRW 385.1 billion is attributable to owners.

Equity is far larger than liabilities, which leaves financial room through periods of business swings. The company has also named a stable balance sheet and cash generation as a task.

Value-up plan and dividend policy

In March 2026 the company disclosed a value-up plan aimed at stable and sustainable dividends within distributable profit. The cash dividend for fiscal 2025 was approved at the 2026 general shareholders meeting.

The company disclosed that it qualified as a high-dividend company under the Restriction of Special Taxation Act for the first time in 2026. Whether the dividend policy continues can be checked at the next general shareholders meeting.

Bear factors

Earnings volatility and margin erosion

The operating margin fell from 12.9% in 2023 to 10.3% in 2025. Profit attributable to owners dropped 23.6% to KRW 30.6 billion in 2025. Quarterly profit swings sharply, with first-quarter 2026 operating profit of KRW 1.28 billion, down 91.7%, before recovering to KRW 16.8 billion in the second quarter. Gibon-Monitor aggregated first-half 2026 operating profit as down 26.2% year on year.

Concern over lower coal-fired volume

The 11th Basic Plan projects growth led by renewables and LNG and nuclear generation. As coal-fired capacity shrinks, maintenance volume tied to coal plants could decline. The company itself named minimizing the impact of lower coal-fired generation as the first goal of its value-up plan. Whether the targeted new orders convert into actual volume remains to be seen.

Turn in operating cash flow

Operating cash flow moved from KRW 74.6 billion in 2024 to KRW -24.7 billion in 2025. Operating profit rose to KRW 43.8 billion in the same year, so profit and cash moved in opposite directions.

Working capital shifts or the timing of receivables collection could be contributing factors, and their scale needs confirmation in the annual report. Continued negative operating cash flow could also affect room for dividend payments.

Risk factors

Policy and regulatory risk

The coal phase-out and changes in the generation mix under the electricity supply plan alter the structure of maintenance volume. Demand for coal plant maintenance falls as coal capacity shrinks.

LNG and nuclear plant maintenance could open new opportunities, but the timing and scale of orders depend on policy schedules. Tighter environmental rules create opportunities for water treatment, while demand could shift with the scope of regulatory changes.

Customer and order concentration

Customers are concentrated among state-owned generators such as Korea South-East Power, Korea Southern Power, Korea East-West Power and Korea West Power, along with some private generators. Changes in a particular generator's maintenance schedule or operating plan directly affect quarterly results.

The swing from a 35.7% quarterly revenue decline in the first quarter of 2026 to a 61.4% increase in the second reflects this structure. Quarters without large orders can see wide earnings swings.

Cost and workforce risk

With a workforce of about 1,182, the business is labor-intensive. Higher labor costs could compress the operating margin even when maintenance volume is unchanged. Because the company has named training specialized technical staff as a task, recruitment and training costs are likely to continue. How much labor cost increases feed into quarterly margins can be checked in the next annual report.

Overall view

Geumhwa PSC combines power plant maintenance with water treatment, and its revenue grew to KRW 423.4 billion in 2025 from 2022. Operating margin, however, eased from 12.9% in 2023 to 10.3% in 2025, and profit attributable to owners fell to KRW 30.6 billion in 2025.

Quarterly results swung widely in 2026, with first-quarter operating profit of KRW 1.28 billion and second-quarter operating profit of KRW 16.8 billion in the same year.

Supportive factors include financial headroom reflected in a 27.4% debt-to-equity ratio and KRW 385.2 billion in total equity, targeted LNG and nuclear maintenance orders in response to lower coal volume, and the dividend policy.

Negative factors include the possibility of lower coal-related volume, the swing to KRW -24.7 billion in 2025 operating cash flow, and the 26.2% year-on-year drop in first-half 2026 operating profit reported by Gibon-Monitor. These two sets of factors should be weighed equally.

The next checkpoints are the full-year 2026 operating margin, the reasons behind the cash flow in the 2026 annual report, and the scale of disclosed LNG and nuclear orders. This report does not include an investment opinion or target price.
