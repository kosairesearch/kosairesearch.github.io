Core Losses Versus Lithium Sulfide Ramp-Up

ISU Specialty Chemical's revenue grew, but operating profit in its core precision chemical business fell sharply in 2025 and turned into a loss in the third quarter of 2026. 02

Business structure

ISU Specialty Chemical was spun off from ISU Chemical in May 2023, taking over the precision chemicals and solid-state battery materials business, and in 2024 it absorbed the precision chemicals unit of an affiliate, ISU Exachem.

Its main products are mercaptan-based precision chemicals such as TDM, NOM and NDM, which act as molecular weight regulators in the production of ABS and SBR.

The company says its proprietary technology gives it a cost advantage, and one brokerage analysis notes that it receives hydrogen sulfide feedstock from an ISU Chemical plant by pipeline, which keeps raw material and logistics costs low.

A 10,000-ton expansion was completed in May 2025, bringing annual TDM capacity to 30,000 tons. The new business, lithium sulfide (Li2S), is a solid electrolyte raw material for solid-state batteries, and the company has been developing sulfide-based process technology since 2020.

In September 2025 a groundbreaking ceremony was held for a commercial plant at the Onsan site in Ulsan, with an investment of about KRW 85.2 billion, and the company's plan was to build a first phase of 150 tons per year by the first half of 2026 and expand it to as much as 500 tons.

On the customer side, the company disclosed in January 2026 a TDM supply contract worth about KRW 23 billion with a CNPC-affiliated buyer, and one analysis says lithium sulfide has been under technical validation with battery customers in Korea, Japan and the United States.

In lithium sulfide, Lake Materials is known as the first company to produce more than 100 tons at home and abroad, and analysts expect competition to intensify as this company's facilities come online. In precision chemicals, LOTTE Fine Chemical is often cited as a comparison.

04

Earnings analysis

The disclosed annual figures show revenue growth alongside weaker profits. Revenue rose from KRW 117.5 billion in 2023 to KRW 332.1 billion in 2024, an increase of 182.5% over the prior year, and then to KRW 411.5 billion in 2025, up 23.9%.

It has increased for two consecutive years from 2024 through 2025, and 2025 was the largest year of the 2023 to 2025 period.

Operating profit moved from an operating loss of KRW 6.12 billion in 2023 to an operating profit of KRW 14.2 billion in 2024, which was a swing to profit, and then fell 88.6% to KRW 1.62 billion in 2025. The operating margin was -5.2%, 4.3% and 0.4% over the three years.

Net profit attributable to controlling shareholders was a net loss of KRW 6.21 billion in 2023, KRW 10.5 billion in 2024 and KRW 770 million in 2025, a 92.7% decline. Net income including non-controlling interests was -KRW 6.21 billion, KRW 10.5 billion and KRW 770 million in the same years.

One brokerage analysis attributes the sharp margin drop in 2025 to scheduled maintenance in the second quarter and lower TDM selling prices, while another point is that 2024 was the first full year after the spin-off, which limits the meaning of year-over-year comparisons.

On a quarterly basis, operating profit turned to KRW 1.63 billion in the fourth quarter of 2025 after an operating loss of KRW 1.46 billion in the third quarter, and then rose to KRW 3.48 billion in the first quarter of 2026, up 113.3% from the prior quarter, and KRW 7.74 billion in the second quarter, up 122.3%.

In the third quarter of 2026, however, the company posted an operating loss of KRW 4.49 billion, a swing back to loss, and the loss widened from the year-earlier period.

Revenue peaked at KRW 108.9 billion in the fourth quarter of 2025 and then declined for three consecutive quarters from the first quarter of 2026 through the third; the third-quarter figure of KRW 92.1 billion was down 14.8% from the prior quarter and 11.5% from a year earlier.

Net profit attributable to controlling shareholders moved from KRW 3.63 billion in the second quarter of 2026 to a net loss of KRW 3.89 billion in the third quarter, and the sum of the last four quarters is KRW 4.54 billion.

On the balance sheet, total liabilities at year-end 2025 were KRW 233.6 billion and total equity was KRW 132.9 billion, while operating cash flow fell from KRW 12 billion in 2023 to KRW 10.6 billion in 2024 and KRW 4.64 billion in 2025. The specific cause of the third-quarter 2026 loss is a point to confirm in the body of the periodic report.

05

Industry analysis

The company's two businesses sit in different cycles. TDM is a precision chemical tied to ABS and SBR demand, and one brokerage noted that TDM selling prices have entered a recovery phase. The same analysis expected sales volume to rise on the 2025 capacity expansion and higher ABS utilization.

Lithium sulfide is a nascent market whose demand depends on when solid-state battery mass production begins. Global forecasters project solid-state battery shipments of about 700 GWh by 2030.

One brokerage, in a report from early 2026, said meaningful lithium sulfide revenue could become possible from around 2030, when solid-state battery mass production is expected to begin in earnest.

On competition, Lake Materials is known as the first producer above 100 tons, and analysts expect competition to intensify as this company's investment comes online.

The company uses a continuous process for lithium sulfide and has reportedly completed joint development of its commercial process with KBR of the United States. Sourcing raw materials from group affiliates is cited as a cost advantage.

On the bear side, if commercialization does not move ahead of 2030, plant utilization and revenue recognition could be delayed.

06

Outlook

The lithium sulfide timetable the company has described comes in phases. The first phase of 150 tons per year was planned for completion by the first half of 2026, with a design that can be expanded to as much as 500 tons.

According to brokerage analysis, the company plans to raise lithium sulfide capacity to 190 tons, including pilot capacity, by the end of 2026, and a 150-ton Mother Plant was expected to start operating in the second half of 2026.

Another analysis estimates that capital expenditure per ton would fall by about 50% for a follow-on expansion of 350 tons. On funding, one analysis says the company secured a low-interest policy loan of about KRW 100 billion through the National Growth Fund, which completes financing for further expansion.

The medium-term investment plan runs through 2029. In TDM, the January 2026 supply contract with a CNPC affiliate converted a letter of intent signed in November 2025 into actual sales. The company also said it expects to mitigate the risk of TDM and NDM price swings.

Still, one research report expects lithium sulfide sales in 2026 to remain small, so the timing of revenue contribution depends on the pace of commercialization. The operating status of the Mother Plant and actual capacity figures can be checked in the 2026 annual report.

08

Bull factors

First-Mover Position in Solid-State Materials

The company broke ground on a lithium sulfide commercial plant at the Onsan site in Ulsan and aims to serve as a production hub for domestic battery makers and overseas markets including China and Japan.

With global forecasters projecting solid-state battery shipments of about 700 GWh by 2030, its position as a raw material supplier could grow alongside the market. Its continuous process and joint process development with KBR are also cited as strengths.

Room for Core Earnings Recovery

Annual TDM capacity reached 30,000 tons after the May 2025 expansion, and one brokerage saw TDM selling prices entering a recovery phase.

Operating profit rose for two consecutive quarters, to KRW 3.48 billion in the first quarter of 2026 and KRW 7.74 billion in the second, after turning to a profit in the fourth quarter of 2025. Room for the operating margin to recover again is the core bull argument.

Policy Funding and Group Supply Base

One analysis says a low-interest policy loan of about KRW 100 billion from the National Growth Fund secures funding for further expansion. The group feedstock pipeline and the TDM contract with a CNPC affiliate have also broadened the demand base. Easing funding constraints for the new business's facilities is a bull factor.

09

Bear factors

Unstable Earnings Direction

Operating profit fell 88.6% to KRW 1.62 billion in 2025, and net profit attributable to controlling shareholders fell 92.7% to KRW 770 million. The company returned to an operating loss of KRW 4.49 billion in the third quarter of 2026, and revenue has declined for three consecutive quarters.

The sum of the last four quarters' net profit attributable to controlling shareholders is only KRW 4.54 billion.

Financial Burden and Funding Needs

The debt ratio rose from 129.5% in 2023 to 175.8% in 2025. Operating cash flow fell to KRW 4.64 billion in 2025, and one analysis says core operations alone struggle to fund the roughly KRW 85.2 billion plant investment. Higher short-term borrowings add to the financial burden.

Commercialization Timing and Rising Competition

Lithium sulfide revenue is tied to the timing of solid-state battery mass production, and the prevailing view is that meaningful sales could start around 2030. If commercialization slips past 2029, plant utilization and revenue could be delayed. Lake Materials is already producing more than 100 tons, so competition for share is likely to intensify.

10

Risk factors

Technology and Commercialization Timing

The lithium sulfide business depends on solid-state battery mass production schedules and customer adoption. If customers' actual mass production slips beyond 2027, plant utilization could remain low. Whether the process scale-up and cost reduction targets are reproduced in actual production is also a point to watch.

Pricing and Demand

TDM and NDM are affected by petrochemical-linked demand for ABS and SBR and by selling prices. One analysis links the drop in the operating margin from 4.3% in 2024 to 0.4% in 2025 to a second-quarter scheduled maintenance combined with lower selling prices. The company says it expects its arrangements to mitigate the impact of price swings.

Financing and Funding

A debt ratio of 175.8% and higher short-term borrowings have reduced financial flexibility. If the plant investment grows, additional borrowing could increase. Depending on how further funding is raised, existing shareholders' stakes could be diluted.

Overall view

ISU Specialty Chemical's disclosed results show revenue expansion alongside weakening profits. Revenue rose from KRW 117.5 billion in 2023 to KRW 411.5 billion in 2025, but operating profit was only KRW 1.62 billion in 2025, and the company returned to an operating loss of KRW 4.49 billion in the third quarter of 2026.

Its core TDM business reached annual capacity of 30,000 tons after the May 2025 expansion, and operating profit rose for two consecutive quarters in the first and second quarters of 2026, which supports the bull case.

On the other side, the debt ratio rose to 175.8% and operating cash flow fell to KRW 4.64 billion in 2025, so the funding structure for plant investment remains a bear factor.

Lithium sulfide has entered the commercialization stage with a plant that broke ground in September 2025 and a capacity target for the end of 2026, but the timing of revenue depends on solid-state battery mass production schedules.

The market holds two views: that the company is a leading solid-state materials player, and that the share price is hard to explain through earnings multiples.

The next checkpoints are the capacity target at the end of December 2026, the fourth-quarter provisional results in early February 2027, and the 2026 annual report at the end of March 2027.
