02

Business structure

Founded in 1990 and listed on KOSDAQ in 2000, HK is a laser processing machine specialist whose core business is manufacturing and selling metal and non-metal material processing equipment using laser beam thermal energy, along with parts sales and service.

Its main products include high-power fiber laser cutters, bending machines, sheet-metal automation systems, and welders, and it holds the leading domestic market share in a laser processing machine market historically dominated by German and Japanese manufacturers.

The company has expanded beyond equipment sales into a 'total laser processing solution' business that combines automation systems, software, and process operation services.

Its overseas business is anchored by HK America, established in 1999, through which it has pursued the North American market for nearly three decades, alongside a Taiwan subsidiary in Taichung that has operated for about a decade.

More recently it opened a branch in Nagoya, Japan, targeting that market with a locally tailored model called FL2512, and it is expanding its sales network around Nagoya and Kyushu, regions dense with automakers and parts suppliers such as Toyota.

Its customer base has diversified to include Korea's major shipbuilders, steelmakers, semiconductor fab infrastructure companies, and auto parts makers, and it has signed a contract to supply a sheet-metal automation line for the new Chicago plant of Hyundai TransLead, the North American subsidiary of Hyundai Motor Group.

In shipbuilding, it is working with Korea's three major shipbuilders on projects to replace existing plasma cutting equipment with large-format laser cutting equipment, high-priced units exceeding one million dollars apiece that contribute to overseas revenue growth.

Overall, HK is in the process of transitioning its business model from a domestic equipment manufacturer to a comprehensive automation-and-solutions supplier, broadening its customer base across semiconductor, shipbuilding, automotive, and energy end markets both at home and abroad.

04

Earnings analysis

Annual revenue fell for two straight years, from KRW 81.4 billion in 2022 to KRW 66.6 billion in 2023 and KRW 54.0 billion in 2024, before roughly stabilizing at KRW 54.2 billion in 2025.

Operating profit shrank from a KRW 5.8 billion profit (7.1% margin) in 2022 to a KRW 0.8 billion profit (1.3%) in 2023, then turned into a KRW 2.6 billion operating loss (-4.9%) in 2024, before that loss narrowed sharply to about KRW 0.4 billion (-0.7%) in 2025, bringing the company close to breakeven.

Net income attributable to owners was KRW 4.3 billion in 2022 and KRW 1.9 billion in 2023, swung to a KRW 2.2 billion net loss in 2024, and then turned positive again with roughly KRW 4.6 billion in net income in 2025.

Quarterly results show notable volatility: in Q3 2025 revenue was KRW 11.2 billion with an operating loss of KRW 1.7 billion and a net loss of KRW 0.1 billion, whereas Q4 2025 revenue jumped to KRW 21.3 billion with an operating profit of KRW 2.6 billion and net income of KRW 5.0 billion, effectively driving the full-year result.

In Q1 2026 revenue plunged to KRW 5.1 billion and the operating loss widened to KRW 2.2 billion, yet net income remained positive at KRW 0.9 billion, and in Q2 2026 revenue recovered to KRW 14.6 billion with an operating loss of KRW 0.2 billion while net income reached KRW 7.9 billion.

A notable pattern is that net income has repeatedly exceeded operating profit by a wide margin in recent quarters, suggesting a meaningful contribution from non-operating items that operating performance alone does not explain.

Cash flow also diverged from reported earnings: in 2024, operating cash flow was a positive KRW 4.7 billion despite the net loss, while in 2025 operating cash flow turned negative at roughly KRW -0.4 billion despite the return to net profit.

The debt ratio steadily declined from 45.8% in 2022 to 31.0% in 2025, indicating an improving balance sheet. On balance, while revenue has been flat, the narrowing operating loss and return to net profit are encouraging, but the quality of earnings and quarter-to-quarter volatility warrant continued monitoring.

05

Industry analysis

The laser processing equipment market has traditionally been led by German and Japanese manufacturers, but HK has maintained the leading domestic market share within it.

The broader Korean machine tool industry is reportedly recording its highest order value in eight years, driven by a recovery in the semiconductor industry and large-scale capital investment.

Building new semiconductor fabs generates demand for precision processing of various metal materials used in piping, steel structures, and power and HVAC systems, and this is reshaping HK's order structure alongside expanding domestic and overseas semiconductor facility investment.

TSMC, the world's largest foundry operator, has reportedly approved roughly KRW 42 trillion in additional investment for advanced process and packaging upgrades and fab construction, bringing its cumulative approved capital expenditure this year to nearly KRW 150 trillion, underscoring a continuing global semiconductor investment cycle that includes Taiwan.

In shipbuilding, demand is rising both domestically and overseas to replace existing plasma cutting equipment with large fiber laser equipment, supported by the laser equipment's advantages in productivity, processing precision, and operational efficiency.

In the United States, reshoring policy is driving new and expanded production facilities among global companies, which is cited as an additional opportunity for metal processing equipment demand.

In markets where Chinese equipment adoption is restricted, there is also discussion of a potential benefit to HK given its quality and maintenance support capabilities.

That said, this demand is heavily dependent on the capital expenditure cycles of end markets such as semiconductors, shipbuilding, and automobiles, so a slowdown in those cycles could also affect the order flow.

06

Outlook

The company has stated that it expects a substantial portion of its H1 2026 order backlog of KRW 34.6 billion to be converted into revenue within the year.

Regionally, its customer base is growing quickly in the Seoul metropolitan area and Chungcheong region, where semiconductor fab investment is concentrated, and the company has said it plans to expand sales activity into the Honam region to secure new customers.

Overseas, it has said it plans to accelerate its push into the US and Japanese semiconductor markets by leveraging its reference of supplying laser processing equipment to three tier-one vendors within TSMC's Taiwan supply chain.

In Japan, it is pursuing the formal commercial launch of its locally tailored FL2512 model alongside strengthening its sales and service network around Nagoya and Kyushu.

In the United States, it has signed a contract to supply a sheet-metal automation line to the new Chicago plant of Hyundai TransLead, the North American subsidiary of Hyundai Motor Group, and the company has previously indicated that orders for large-format laser equipment from Korea's shipbuilders were expected to gain momentum starting in the second half of 2026.

The recent open-market share purchases by the major shareholder's affiliates and key executives over about a month have been interpreted as a signal of the company's confidence in future business growth.

How much of this pipeline, and at what pace, converts into actual revenue and profit will likely be the key determinant of future results.

Bull factors

Beneficiary of the Semiconductor Fab Investment Cycle

Backlog and total order intake have both risen sharply on expanded infrastructure investment tied to domestic semiconductor fab construction.

In Taiwan, the company has secured a reference for supplying equipment to tier-one vendors in TSMC's supply chain, and it plans to use this as a springboard to accelerate its push into the US and Japanese semiconductor markets.

If fab investment progresses from infrastructure construction into the equipment installation phase, additional equipment demand could follow.

Overseas Diversification and New Product Launches

The company's US customer base has surpassed 300 accounts, and a notable share of those customers are repeat buyers. In Japan, it has begun formal commercial sales of its locally tailored FL2512 model, expanding its foothold in a market with high entry barriers.

Its customer base is also broadening into end markets such as shipbuilding and automotive, which could reduce reliance on any single industry.

Improving Balance Sheet and Insider Share Purchases

The debt ratio has steadily declined from 45.8% in 2022 to 31.0% in 2025, indicating an improving balance sheet. The company also moved from a net loss in 2024 to a net profit in 2025, showing some recovery in earnings strength.

The recent open-market purchase of roughly 140,000 shares by the major shareholder's affiliates and key executives over about a month is a notable fact from an ownership-alignment perspective.

Bear factors

Stagnant Revenue and Thin Operating Margins

Revenue fell from KRW 81.4 billion in 2022 to KRW 54.0 billion in 2024 and remained around KRW 54.2 billion in 2025, showing no clear growth trend.

While the 2025 operating loss narrowed considerably, the company is still operating at a loss, and profitability improvement has not been stable, with the operating loss widening again in Q3 2025 and Q1 2026.

Whether the rising backlog translates into actual operating profit improvement needs to be confirmed in coming quarterly results.

Questions Around Earnings Quality

Net income has repeatedly and substantially exceeded operating profit in recent quarters, suggesting a meaningful portion of earnings may stem from non-operating factors. In 2025, operating cash flow was negative despite the net profit, revealing a gap between reported earnings and actual cash generation. Whether these non-recurring earnings drivers are sustainable remains uncertain.

Small-Cap Characteristics and Intensifying Competition

The company's market capitalization places it among the smaller names on KOSDAQ, which may mean limited liquidity and relatively higher share price volatility.

Competition from global rivals in Germany and Japan continues, and if the capital expenditure cycles in end markets such as semiconductors and shipbuilding slow down, the order flow could also contract.

Risk factors

Sensitivity to End-Market Cycles

The company's results are heavily influenced by capital expenditure cycles in end markets such as semiconductor fabs, shipbuilding, and automobiles.

It currently benefits from the semiconductor fab expansion tailwind, but a slowdown or delay in the global semiconductor investment cycle could slow the pace at which backlog converts into revenue.

Orders for large-format laser equipment from shipbuilders are also expected to gain momentum only from the second half of 2026, so the actual timing and scale of orders need to be monitored.

Foreign Exchange and Policy Risk in Overseas Operations

As the company operates local subsidiaries in multiple countries including the United States, Japan, and Taiwan and increases the share of overseas revenue, it is exposed to currency fluctuations and changes in trade and investment policy across those countries.

US reshoring policy is currently a favorable factor, but shifts in tariff or investment incentive policy could affect local order plans.

Earnings Quality and Cash Flow Volatility

Even though net income turned positive in 2025, operating cash flow was negative, revealing a gap between reported earnings and cash generation.

Quarterly revenue and earnings swing widely, making it difficult to draw firm conclusions from any single quarter's results, and this volatility could continue to affect financial planning and investor assessment going forward.

12

Overall view

Built on its leading position in Korea's laser processing machine market, HK is simultaneously pursuing three growth drivers: the semiconductor fab expansion cycle, equipment replacement in shipbuilding, and overseas market expansion, and its backlog and total order intake metrics show clear improvement.

Financially, the 2025 operating loss narrowed sharply and net income turned positive, but net income has repeatedly and substantially exceeded operating profit in recent quarters, so the quality of those earnings deserves close attention.

Revenue has been stagnant since 2022 and quarterly results are volatile, making whether the rising backlog translates into stable operating profit improvement a key variable for future performance.

Overseas, concrete progress is evident in the expanding US customer base, entry into TSMC's Taiwan supply chain, and the formal launch of a new product in Japan, which could reduce reliance on the domestic semiconductor investment cycle.

That said, heavy dependence on end-market capital expenditure cycles and the liquidity constraints typical of a small-cap stock are factors that should also be weighed. The Q3 report and the actual pace at which backlog converts into revenue are likely to be key tests of the company's growth narrative going forward.
