02

Business structure

HK, founded in 1990, is a laser processing equipment maker listed on KOSDAQ that was previously named Han Kwang before adopting its current corporate name.

It holds the leading domestic market share in laser machine tools, developing and producing high-power fiber laser cutters, welders, sheet-metal forming and bending machines, and smart automation solutions in-house.

Its core products are fiber laser processing machines, which are increasingly replacing CO2 lasers, supplied as mother-machine-type equipment for cutting and welding across metal-processing industries.

Its customer base spans semiconductor fab infrastructure builders, automakers and parts suppliers, shipbuilders, and energy and nuclear facility operators, and it recently signed a contract to supply a sheet-metal automation line for Hyundai TRANSLEAD's new Chicago plant, a North American subsidiary of Hyundai Motor Group.

Overseas sales are handled through HK America, a U.S. subsidiary established in 1999, and a China-based affiliate, with cumulative U.S. clients surpassing 300.

In the global laser equipment market, HK competes against established players such as Trumpf of Germany and Bystronic of Switzerland as well as lower-cost Chinese equipment makers.

The company is pursuing a transition from equipment sales toward becoming a manufacturing solutions provider that automates entire sheet-metal processing workflows, and with government support it is developing autonomous laser cutting heads and robot-based autonomous manufacturing processes.

04

Earnings analysis

Annual revenue fell from KRW 81.4 billion in 2022 to KRW 66.6 billion in 2023 (down 18.1% year over year) and KRW 54 billion in 2024 (down 18.9% year over year), before edging up to KRW 54.2 billion in 2025 (up 0.3% year over year); among 2022-2025, 2022 was the largest year and 2024 the smallest.

Operating profit dropped from KRW 5.81 billion in 2022 to KRW 842 million in 2023 (down 85.5% year over year), then turned to an operating loss of KRW 2.64 billion in 2024 (a swing to loss from the prior year) and an operating loss of KRW 387 million in 2025 (loss continued but narrowed), marking two consecutive years of operating losses in 2024 and 2025.

Controlling-interest net profit fell from KRW 4.27 billion in 2022 to KRW 1.9 billion in 2023 (down 55.6% year over year), swung to a net loss of KRW 2.25 billion in 2024, and then turned positive again to KRW 4.57 billion in 2025.

Operating margin moved from 7.1% in 2022 to 1.3% in 2023, -4.9% in 2024, and -0.7% in 2025, reflecting a slow recovery in core profitability.

On a quarterly basis, revenue was volatile, moving from KRW 12.9 billion in the second quarter of 2025 to KRW 11.2 billion in the third quarter (down 13.2% quarter over quarter), KRW 21.3 billion in the fourth quarter (up 90.2% quarter over quarter), down to KRW 5.06 billion in the first quarter of 2026 (down 76.3% quarter over quarter), and back up to KRW 14.6 billion in the second quarter of 2026 (up 188.5% quarter over quarter and up 13.1% year over year).

Operating profit reached KRW 2.62 billion in the fourth quarter of 2025 but swung to an operating loss of KRW 2.21 billion in the first quarter of 2026 (a swing to loss quarter over quarter) and an operating loss of KRW 187 million in the second quarter of 2026 (loss continued but narrowed quarter over quarter, loss continued and widened year over year), extending the operating loss streak to two consecutive quarters from the first through the second quarter of 2026.

Controlling-interest net profit, by contrast, moved from KRW 4.97 billion in the fourth quarter of 2025 to KRW 866 million in the first quarter of 2026 (down 82.6% quarter over quarter) and KRW 7.92 billion in the second quarter of 2026 (up 814.2% quarter over quarter and up 2,238.1% year over year), diverging from the operating loss trend, with the trailing four quarters from the third quarter of 2025 through the second quarter of 2026 totaling KRW 13.6 billion.

On the balance sheet, the debt-to-equity ratio declined steadily from 45.8% in 2022 to 31.0% in 2025, with total equity of KRW 63.1 billion at the end of 2025 (entirely attributable to controlling interests), while operating cash flow improved from -KRW 909 million in 2022 to KRW 1.82 billion in 2023 and KRW 4.69 billion in 2024 before reversing to -KRW 432 million in 2025, highlighting a divergence between reported earnings and cash generation.

05

Industry analysis

The global laser cutting machine market is accelerating its shift from CO2-based systems to fiber lasers, which offer better energy efficiency and lower maintenance costs, a transition that positions HK to potentially benefit.

On the demand side, capacity expansion by global foundries including TSMC is driving investment not only in advanced process and packaging equipment but also in infrastructure and manufacturing equipment tied to new fab construction.

In Korea, new fabs are concentrated in the greater Seoul and Chungcheong regions, expanding demand for laser equipment from related infrastructure builders, and the company has said it is also expanding sales efforts into the Honam region.

In the United States, manufacturing reshoring is driving new and expanded production facilities in semiconductors, shipbuilding, and energy, creating a favorable environment for metal-processing equipment demand.

In Taiwan, demand for large fiber laser systems replacing older plasma cutting equipment is rising, with quality-focused suppliers winning orders over lower-cost Chinese equipment in some cases.

At the same time, the global laser equipment market remains led by established players such as Trumpf of Germany and Bystronic of Switzerland, while Chinese manufacturers continue to expand volume through aggressive pricing, meaning competition from low-cost Chinese equipment remains an ongoing challenge for HK.

06

Outlook

The company reported that its first-half 2026 order backlog reached KRW 34.6 billion, up 141% year over year, with first-half total orders of about KRW 48.9 billion, up 59.7% from KRW 30.6 billion a year earlier, and it expects a significant portion of secured orders to be recognized as revenue within the year.

Following the completion of equipment deliveries to three Taiwan-based first-tier TSMC vendors, HK has been seeking additional order opportunities within the TSMC value chain through participation in a Taiwan trade show, and it plans to leverage this track record to pursue the U.S. and Japanese markets where TSMC is expanding production sites.

In the automotive segment, HK won a contract worth roughly USD 8.4 million (about KRW 12 billion) to supply a sheet-metal automation line for Hyundai TRANSLEAD's new Chicago plant, a North American subsidiary of Hyundai Motor Group, with the equipment reportedly priced above USD 1 million per unit.

In Japan, HK began formal sales of its locally tailored 'FL2512' equipment, targeting the sheet-metal and shipbuilding segments.

On the new-business front, the company said it is accelerating commercialization of underwater laser cutting technology aimed at the nuclear decommissioning market, and is also developing robot-based autonomous manufacturing processes with government support.

A controlling-shareholder affiliate and senior executives disclosed open-market purchases of about 140,000 shares worth roughly KRW 180 million over about one month, which the company described as a reflection of confidence in business growth and a commitment to responsible management.

Bull factors

Entry into Semiconductor Fab Supply Chain

HK completed equipment deliveries to three Taiwan-based first-tier TSMC vendors, securing a reference within the global semiconductor value chain. Building on this, the company has said it plans to expand sales into the U.S. and Japan, where TSMC is expanding production sites.

In Korea, the company says it is also gaining customers in the greater Seoul and Chungcheong regions, where new fabs are concentrated.

Rising Order Backlog and Revenue Visibility

HK's first-half 2026 order backlog rose 141% year over year to KRW 34.6 billion, and total orders increased 59.7%. The company has said it expects a significant portion of secured orders to convert into revenue within the year.

Higher-value projects, such as the automation line order for the Hyundai TRANSLEAD Chicago plant, are also increasing.

Improving Balance Sheet and Return to Profit

The debt-to-equity ratio fell steadily from 45.8% in 2022 to 31.0% in 2025, reflecting improved financial health. After a net loss in 2024, controlling-interest net profit turned positive at KRW 4.57 billion in 2025. A controlling-shareholder affiliate and executives also disclosed open-market share purchases.

Bear factors

Continued Operating Losses in Core Business

HK posted operating losses in both the first and second quarters of 2026, extending the loss streak to two consecutive quarters. Operating margin was -0.7% in 2025, marking a second consecutive year of operating losses. Despite the revenue rebound, a clear recovery in core profitability has yet to materialize.

High Quarter-to-Quarter Earnings Volatility

Quarterly results have swung sharply, with first-quarter 2026 revenue plunging 76.3% quarter over quarter to KRW 5.06 billion before rebounding 188.5% to KRW 14.6 billion in the second quarter.

Controlling-interest net profit has also alternated between profit and loss and between sharp increases and decreases across quarters. This pattern makes it difficult to conclude a trend reversal from any single strong quarter.

Competition from Low-Cost Chinese Equipment

In the global laser equipment market, Chinese manufacturers continue to expand their volume base through aggressive pricing. HK itself has identified the competitive pressure from low-cost Chinese equipment as an ongoing challenge. The company continues to face pressure to differentiate through quality and technology.

Risk factors

Downstream Industry Capex Cycle

HK's results are heavily tied to capital spending cycles in downstream industries such as semiconductor fab expansion, new automotive plants, and shipbuilding and energy facility investment. A slowdown or delay in these investment cycles could push back order intake and revenue recognition timing. The large quarter-to-quarter revenue swings are related to this dependence on investment cycles.

Foreign Exchange and Tariff Exposure from Overseas Sales

As the share of overseas revenue from the United States, Taiwan, and Japan grows, currency fluctuations could affect profitability. Changes in U.S. tariff policy or the broader trade environment are also variables that could affect price competitiveness and supply chain strategy. Operating costs at overseas subsidiaries are likewise exposed to currency movements.

Intensifying Low-Cost and Technology Competition

Continued aggressive pricing and volume expansion by Chinese manufacturers could intensify price competition. Failure to narrow the technology gap with established German and Swiss manufacturers could weaken HK's position in higher-value segments.

Delays in developing new technologies such as autonomous processing and robotic automation could pressure the company's competitiveness.

Overall view

HK's earnings structure remains intertwined: despite an annual loss in 2024 and continued operating losses through the first half of 2026, controlling-interest net profit grew sharply again in the second quarter of 2026, following a similar rebound in 2025.

On the positive side, HK's entry into TSMC's Taiwan supply chain and a 141% increase in order backlog, aligned with the semiconductor fab expansion cycle, provide evidence of top-line growth.

At the same time, operating losses persisted for two consecutive quarters from the first through the second quarter of 2026, meaning a recovery in core profitability remains a work in progress.

Diversified growth drivers—U.S. reshoring, expansion into Japan and Taiwan, and the Hyundai TRANSLEAD project—are running in parallel, but the wide swings in quarterly revenue and earnings make it premature to assert a clear trend.

Competition from low-cost Chinese equipment and dependence on downstream industry investment cycles remain variables that will require ongoing monitoring.

The next quarterly report and any further order disclosures will be important in determining whether the recent top-line growth and net profit improvement translate into a sustained recovery in core operating profitability.
