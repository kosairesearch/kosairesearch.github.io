02

Business structure

Inhwa Precision is a marine engine parts maker that produces cylinder covers, bed plates and frame boxes, core components of large marine engines, and supplies them to global engine manufacturers.

The company describes an integrated process linking welding and machining and says it maintains cooperative relationships with three major domestic engine makers.

Stock-information site Alphasquare lists the segment mix as marine engine parts at 69.15%, metal structures such as heat exchangers and pressure vessels at 19.11%, and metal forming machinery at 7.09%. The exchange classifies the company under automobiles, but most of its sales come from marine engine parts.

The company previously acquired an automotive parts maker as a subsidiary. In February 2024 it sold a 21.58% stake in an engine-maker affiliate to a Hanwha Group affiliate for about KRW 137.4 billion, giving up management control.

In air cargo, it holds a 20.86% stake in an air-cargo fund into which it invested about KRW 92.5 billion, and the estimated value of that stake was about KRW 89.1 billion as of end-June 2026.

Investment-banking sources reported in October 2026 that the company is screening acquisition targets across shipbuilding and defense, though no deal is currently in progress. Consolidated cash and short-term financial assets were about KRW 143 billion at the end of June 2026.

04

Earnings analysis

Annual revenue fell from KRW 148.4 billion in 2022 to KRW 104 billion in 2023 (down 29.9% year on year), bottomed at KRW 92.8 billion in 2024 (down 10.7%), and recovered to KRW 108 billion in 2025 (up 16.4%).

Operating profit bottomed at KRW 4.89 billion in 2023 (down 50.6%), then rose to KRW 10.3 billion in 2024 (up 110.0%) and KRW 15.5 billion in 2025 (up 51.4%).

The operating margin climbed from 4.7% in 2023 to 11.1% in 2024 and 14.4% in 2025, and in 2024 operating profit rose even as revenue declined, meaning margin improvement offset lower volume.

Net profit attributable to controlling shareholders swung from a loss in 2022 to a profit in 2023, reached KRW 40 billion in 2024, and fell to KRW 25.6 billion in 2025, a decline that is read as the absence of the 2024 one-time gain.

Equity-method losses shrank to KRW 6.4 billion in 2025 from KRW 31.2 billion in 2024, so the 2025 decline was not driven by those losses. In the third quarter of 2025, operating profit of KRW 4.87 billion coincided with a controlling-shareholder net loss of KRW 3.11 billion, with a large non-operating loss.

Quarterly revenue then rose for three straight quarters from the fourth quarter of 2025 to the third quarter of 2026, with operating profit growing for three straight quarters, while controlling-shareholder net profit dipped in the first quarter of 2026 before recovering.

The last four quarters together total KRW 39 billion in controlling-shareholder net profit. Operating cash flow was KRW 18.7 billion in 2024 and KRW 5.14 billion in 2025, so profit growth did not translate into cash growth in 2025.

05

Industry analysis

Inhwa Precision's core market is large marine engine parts, and demand tracks the ordering flow of marine engine makers. The company's growth plan targets a larger share of large, higher-value engine structures, including components for dual-fuel eco-friendly engines.

Tighter environmental rules that push diesel engines toward gas-type designs are cited as a demand backdrop. In October 2026, the price-to-book ratio fell back below 1x as the shipbuilding-equipment sector corrected, while quarterly operating profit kept growing over the same period.

Daishin Securities projected marine engine parts revenue at KRW 101.9 billion for 2026 and KRW 113.7 billion for 2027 in an October 2026 company analysis, and these are securities estimates rather than filed results. The competitive position rests on long-term relationships with engine makers.

Because the company's business is different from most automotive-listed peers, direct comparison is limited, and the timing of marine orders and engine-maker demand is the largest variable.

06

Outlook

The company's medium-term plan disclosed in October 2026 targets a consolidated operating margin of at least 15%, a debt ratio of 30% or lower, and shareholder returns of at least 50% of consolidated operating profit for 2027 through 2029.

The targets are stated as medium-term benchmarks that may change with business conditions and market circumstances. The first-half 2026 operating margin of 16.1% already sits above the 15% target level.

Daishin Securities projected 2026 operating profit at KRW 20.2 billion, up about 30%, and KRW 23.1 billion for 2027, and these are securities estimates rather than filed results. Growth investment includes facility expansion to strengthen cost competitiveness.

The air-cargo affiliate's revenue is reported at the KRW 600 billion level, so its results will weigh heavily on equity-method gains and losses. An IPO that would allow recovery of the investment has been considered, but no listing schedule has been set.

The next checkpoints are whether the planned investments are executed and whether the operating margin holds at or above the target level.

Risk factors

Industry cycle and customer concentration

Because marine engine parts are the core business, results depend on ship orders and engine-maker demand. The shipbuilding-equipment correction is already reflected in the price-to-book ratio. Concentration among three domestic engine makers means order changes at a single customer can directly affect revenue.

Investment gains and M&A volatility

Equity-method results and fund valuation changes can move controlling-shareholder net profit sharply. The IPO-based recovery of the air-cargo investment has no set timetable. The company is screening acquisitions, and a completed deal could change its financial structure and earnings profile.

Possible changes to the shareholder return policy

The 2027 to 2029 return targets are medium-term benchmarks that may change with business conditions and market circumstances. The 30%-or-below debt-ratio target is not yet met, with the ratio at 35.4% at year-end 2025.

Return amounts are linked to consolidated operating profit, so lower operating profit would reduce the return pool.
