02

Business structure

iMBC was founded in 2000 as a subsidiary of MBC and listed on KOSDAQ in 2005, operating internet broadcasting and digital content businesses. Revenue is split into service, content, advertising and commission income.

According to a financial data aggregator, service income accounted for 55% of second-quarter 2026 revenue, content for 34%, advertising for 8% and commission income for 3%. JobKorea reports commission income at 2% for the same period, so the segment weights differ by source.

Service revenue centers on producing and operating MBC's websites, and the company is pursuing digital operation outsourcing for public institutions and companies using its AI and big-data operating know-how. Content revenue comes from digital content sales, and weak VOD sales were cited as a drag on 2025 revenue.

Advertising revenue comes from free and paid services on MBC's site and from content advertising. Subsidiary I-Porter handles international parcel forwarding, a separate business from the core.

Because much of the revenue comes from producing and operating parent-affiliated channels, the revenue structure differs from that of independent content producers. Competition overlaps with web agencies, digital content platforms and advertising agencies.

04

Earnings analysis

Based on disclosed annual results, revenue fell from KRW 47.5 billion in 2022 to KRW 46.3 billion in 2023 (down 2.4% year on year), KRW 45.4 billion in 2024 (down 1.9%) and KRW 40 billion in 2025 (down 11.9%).

Revenue declined for three consecutive years from 2023 to 2025, with 2022 the largest year and 2025 the smallest in the 2022–2025 period.

Operating profit fell from KRW 2.56 billion in 2022 to KRW 1.16 billion in 2023 (down 54.8%) and KRW 635 million in 2024 (down 45.2%), then turned into an operating loss of KRW 7.63 billion in 2025. The operating margin dropped from 5.4% in 2022 to 2.5% in 2023 and 1.4% in 2024, and was -19.1% in 2025.

The company's earnings commentary attributed the decline to weaker service demand, weak VOD sales in content, lower advertising revenue and higher cost burdens.

Net profit attributable to controlling shareholders went from KRW 1.67 billion in 2022 to KRW 2.08 billion in 2023 (up 24.5%) and KRW 1.96 billion in 2024 (down 5.8%), then turned into a loss of KRW 5.36 billion in 2025. Net loss including non-controlling interests was KRW 5.43 billion in 2025.

Quarterly, an operating loss of KRW 1.18 billion in the third quarter of 2025 was followed by an operating profit of KRW 263 million in the fourth quarter, then KRW 2.63 million in the first quarter of 2026, KRW 180 million in the second and KRW 128 million in the third.

Quarterly revenue was KRW 10.1 billion, KRW 9.99 billion, KRW 9.63 billion, KRW 9.82 billion and KRW 9.22 billion across the third quarter of 2025 to the third quarter of 2026.

Operating profit has been small since 2026, and net profit attributable to controlling shareholders has fallen for three straight quarters from its peak of KRW 1.07 billion in the fourth quarter of 2025; the last four quarters total KRW 2.65 billion.

Operating cash flow went from KRW -5.03 billion in 2023 to KRW 6.69 billion in 2024 and KRW 8.59 billion in 2025. Total equity at the end of 2025 was KRW 57.4 billion, including KRW 55.5 billion attributable to controlling shareholders, with total liabilities of KRW 9.67 billion.

05

Industry analysis

In a 2025 annual comparison of 49 entertainment companies, iMBC ranked 31st by market capitalization, 34th by revenue and 43rd by operating profit.

Its operating margin of -19.06% was below the industry average of -10.99%, and its debt ratio of 16.85% was far under the industry average of 79.47%, so financial leverage is low. In the same comparison the industry average operating profit was positive, while iMBC posted a loss.

The peer group includes many artist-management and music and live-entertainment companies such as Hybe, SM Entertainment and JYP Ent., so its business mix differs from iMBC's web-operation and digital-content model.

On the demand side, short-form advertising, AI-based content production and digital operation outsourcing for public institutions are cited as growth areas, but quarterly revenue in 2026 is still declining. Competition is split among web agencies, digital content platforms and advertising agencies.

Dependence on parent-affiliated channels offers stable demand but limits external expansion. The position of the sector in its cycle cannot be determined from iMBC's results alone.

06

Outlook

The directions the company has publicly described are expanding advertising through AI, big data and short-form content, and pursuing digital operation outsourcing for public institutions and companies.

Whether these have translated into revenue is not yet visible in quarterly results, as third-quarter 2026 revenue was KRW 9.22 billion, down 8.4% year on year.

Operating profit stayed positive at KRW 128 million in the third quarter of 2026, but net profit attributable to controlling shareholders fell to KRW 493 million, the third straight quarterly decline.

The results of subsidiary I-Porter's international parcel forwarding business should be tracked separately from the core business. If new contracts are disclosed, the scale of the digital operation outsourcing business and its weight against the core can be gauged.

The next checkpoint is the operating margin and segment revenue trend in the 2026 annual report. On a quarterly basis, the direction of service, content and advertising revenue in the first-quarter 2027 report is the next checkpoint.

Whether 2026 full-year results turn profitable and whether the revenue decline stops are the practical tests of the strategy.

Bull factors

Quarterly profitability holds

Quarterly operating profit has stayed positive since the fourth quarter of 2025, from KRW 263 million to KRW 180 million and KRW 128 million in 2026. The third quarter of 2026 turned from a year-earlier loss into a profit. If the one-time costs the company cited for 2025 do not recur, the quarterly profit pattern could persist.

Balance sheet and cash flow

Total equity at the end of 2025 was KRW 57.4 billion, of which KRW 55.5 billion is attributable to controlling shareholders. Total liabilities were KRW 9.67 billion, and the debt ratio was 16.9% in both 2024 and 2025. Operating cash flow stayed positive at KRW 6.69 billion in 2024 and KRW 8.59 billion in 2025. The debt ratio is also well below the industry average.

Stated expansion directions

The company has described plans to expand advertising using AI, big data and short-form content, and to pursue digital operation outsourcing for public institutions and companies. If the outsourcing business, built on experience running MBC's websites, reaches new clients, it could broaden the service revenue base. Whether this has translated into results will depend on whether the quarterly revenue decline stops.

Bear factors

Revenue keeps shrinking

Revenue fell from KRW 47.5 billion in 2022 to KRW 40 billion in 2025, declining three years in a row from 2023. Third-quarter 2026 revenue of KRW 9.22 billion was down 8.4% year on year.

The company cited weaker service demand, weak VOD sales and lower advertising revenue, and quarterly data show whether these drivers persisted into 2026.

Small and shrinking profit base

The company posted an operating loss of KRW 7.63 billion and a net loss attributable to controlling shareholders of KRW 5.36 billion in 2025. Quarterly operating profit in 2026 has ranged from KRW 2.63 million to KRW 180 million.

Net profit attributable to controlling shareholders has fallen for three straight quarters, so the earnings base remains thin.

Dependence on parent-linked channels

Service revenue centers on producing and operating MBC's websites, so dependence on parent-linked channels is high. Content and advertising revenue also come from services built on MBC's site. If expansion to external clients is slow, the revenue structure could remain tied to a single channel and limit growth.

10

Risk factors

Delayed earnings recovery

The 2025 loss reflected lower sales and one-time costs together. If those costs recur or revenue keeps falling, the quarterly profit pattern could break down. The third-quarter 2026 profit of KRW 128 million is small and sensitive to cost changes.

Cash flow volatility

Operating cash flow was KRW -5.03 billion in 2023 before turning positive in 2024 and 2025. The swings are large, so cash generation cannot yet be considered stable. Whether cash flow holds up after the 2025 loss needs to be confirmed.

Platform and advertising shifts

Content revenue relies on VOD sales, and weak VOD sales weighed on revenue in 2025. If the advertising market reorganizes around short-form video, the structure of existing advertising revenue could also change. Whether the short-form and AI advertising push can absorb this shift remains unproven.

Quarterly operating profit has stayed positive from the fourth quarter of 2025 through the third quarter of 2026, reaching KRW 128 million in the latest quarter. Revenue was still down 8.4% year on year in the third quarter of 2026, so the decline continues.
