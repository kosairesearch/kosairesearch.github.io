02

Business structure

Kye-Ryong Construction Industrial is a residential and civil engineering contractor listed on KOSPI since January 30, 1996. On a consolidated basis for the first half of 2026, building construction accounted for 58.99% of revenue and civil engineering for 24.01%, together about 83% (Asia Today report).

On a standalone basis the building share was 70.02% and civil engineering 22.56%. Sales from residential sales projects have shrunk: according to a Nate News report, 2025 sales in that category fell from KRW 801.3 billion to KRW 229.9 billion, a 71.3% decline, and its share of revenue dropped from 25.6% to 8.0%.

The same report said public-procurement-driven revenue, including KRW 1.69 trillion of building contracts and KRW 653.7 billion of civil contracts, made up 81.2% of the total.

Main clients include the Public Procurement Service, local governments and public agencies, while private work includes logistics centers, privately co-financed public housing and street-home redevelopment projects.

According to a BNK Investment & Securities report, the company ranked 3rd in public-works orders in 2021, 5th in 2022, 3rd in 2023 and 1st in 2024.

In the Seoul housing market, large construction groups compete intensely on brand, and many of the projects the company has secured are small-scale street-home redevelopments. The order backlog stood at KRW 14.14 trillion at the end of September 2026 (BusinessPost report).

04

Earnings analysis

Annual revenue rose from KRW 2.95 trillion in 2022 to KRW 2.98 trillion in 2023 (up 0.9% year on year) and KRW 3.17 trillion in 2024 (up 6.5%), then fell to KRW 2.89 trillion in 2025 (down 8.9%). Among 2022 to 2025, 2024 was the largest year for revenue.

Operating profit fell from KRW 132.8 billion in 2022 to KRW 100.7 billion in 2023 (down 24.2%) and KRW 97.7 billion in 2024 (down 3.0%), then recovered to KRW 166.9 billion in 2025 (up 70.8%). The operating margin moved from 4.5% in 2022 to 3.4% in 2023 and 3.1% in 2024 before reaching 5.8% in 2025.

A Nate News report read the 2025 pattern as a shift toward profitability over sales volume, with profit rising even as revenue declined, and linked it to the sharp drop in residential sales and the larger share of public-procurement work.

Attributable net income was KRW 60.5 billion in 2022, KRW 47.2 billion in 2023 (down 21.9%), KRW 47.4 billion in 2024 (up 0.3%) and KRW 100 billion in 2025 (up 110.9%), rising for two consecutive years from 2024 through 2025.

Net income including non-controlling interests was KRW 97.9 billion in 2025, a separate measure from attributable net income. Operating cash flow went from negative KRW 10.1 billion in 2024 to positive KRW 209.9 billion in 2025.

Quarterly revenue was KRW 661.7 billion in Q3 2025, KRW 823.1 billion in Q4 2025 (up 24.4% quarter on quarter), KRW 670.7 billion in Q1 2026 (down 18.5%), KRW 840.1 billion in Q2 2026 (up 25.2%) and KRW 798.6 billion in Q3 2026 (down 4.9% quarter on quarter, up 20.7% year on year).

Q3 2026 operating profit of KRW 50.5 billion was up 30.5% year on year, yet attributable net income of KRW 5.88 billion was down 76.1%, widening the gap between the two measures. Over the latest four quarters, attributable net income totaled KRW 106.2 billion.

05

Industry analysis

Even amid a weak construction cycle, public procurement is supporting results. The government set the 2026 social overhead capital budget at KRW 21.1 trillion, KRW 1.6 trillion more than the prior year, and analysts said the expansion would deepen the order base for companies strong in public works (Newsway report).

The company said public-sector volume flowed into results and improved profitability even as the construction market weakened (Consumer News report).

Its major 2026 wins also concentrated in public building work, including a KRW 132.7 billion new government office in Yeoju and a KRW 84.0 billion research office project in Sejong (Global Economic report).

In the private segment, Seoul redevelopment is the competitive arena: the company secured several street-home and redevelopment projects in 2026, but competes with large builders on brand (Asia Today report). The company has moved from a regional mid-sized builder based in Daejeon toward a nationwide contractor.

Because residential sales now make up a smaller share of revenue, exposure to sales-market swings has fallen, while dependence on public tender timing and budget execution has risen.

Outlook

The company has said it will bid selectively for projects that secure profitability and concentrate resources on stable areas such as public works (Newsway report).

Major contracts disclosed in 2026 include the Eco Delta City 1BL privately co-financed public sale housing project, KRW 150.67 billion, signed on June 5, 2026 with a period running to October 31, 2031 (Nate News report).

The Nonsan Naedong 3 district apartment project, KRW 144.23 billion, was signed on June 26, 2026, with a period from October 1, 2026 to October 31, 2029.

The Second Gyeongchun National Road construction Section 1 carries a contract amount of KRW 184.75 billion, equal to a 60% stake in the total contract, with a period from September 1, 2026 to August 29, 2033 (Topstar News report).

The Daejeon Southwest Comprehensive Sports Town facility project, KRW 97.6 billion, was signed on November 5, 2026, with a period from November 10, 2026 to January 12, 2030 (Digital Today report).

The Busan BEXCO Exhibition Hall 3 project, KRW 83.8 billion, was signed on November 10, 2026, with a period from December 21, 2026 to February 4, 2031 (Topstar News report).

In Seoul, the company was selected as construction contractor for the Sinyoung 1 redevelopment, with an expected contract amount of about KRW 91.6 billion (Asia Today report). The company disclosed that contract amounts and periods may change with construction progress. The 2026 annual report, which will confirm full-year and Q4 results, is the next checkpoint.

08

Bull factors

Sustained Top-Tier Public Orders

According to a BNK Investment & Securities report, the company ranked 3rd in public orders in 2021, 5th in 2022, 3rd in 2023 and 1st in 2024. A 2026 SOC budget of KRW 21.1 trillion, confirmed by the government, was expected to widen the public-tender base (Newsway report).

Public projects carry comparatively low volatility, which the market views as supportive of medium-term revenue.

Revenue Visibility From the Backlog

The order backlog stood at KRW 14.14 trillion at the end of September 2026, about seven times 2025 annual revenue (BusinessPost report). In 2026 the company disclosed the Daejeon sports town contract of KRW 97.6 billion and the Busan BEXCO project of KRW 83.8 billion. In Seoul it was also selected for the Sinyoung 1 redevelopment, widening its housing order scope.

Stronger Earnings Base in 2026

Operating profit reached KRW 166.9 billion in 2025, up 70.8%, and the 5.8% margin was the highest of 2022 to 2025. Q2 2026 operating profit of KRW 69.0 billion rose 69.2% from the prior quarter. Operating cash flow also turned positive, at KRW 209.9 billion in 2025.

09

Bear factors

Sharp Drop in Q3 Net Income

Q3 2026 attributable net income was KRW 5.88 billion, down 76.1% year on year and 84.8% quarter on quarter. Operating profit in the same quarter rose 30.5%, widening the gap between the two measures.

Attributable net income has swung widely from quarter to quarter over the latest four quarters, a weakness in earnings stability.

Leverage and a Past Cash Outflow

The debt-to-equity ratio rose gradually from 218.9% in 2022 to 220.6% in 2024 and 225.5% in 2025. Total liabilities of KRW 2.23 trillion at the end of 2025 are more than double total equity of KRW 990.9 billion. In 2024, operating cash flow was negative KRW 10.1 billion.

Thin Margins and Shrinking Sales

The operating margin stayed in the 3% range at 4.5% in 2022, 3.4% in 2023 and 3.1% in 2024, and the 5.8% reading for 2025 covers only one year. Revenue fell 8.9% in 2025 to KRW 2.89 trillion, below the KRW 2.95 trillion of 2022. Whether margins hold as sales contract is the point to confirm in later years.

10

Risk factors

Policy and budget

The public-works base depends on SOC budget execution and tender timing. Although the 2026 budget rose from the prior year, slower execution or shifted tender schedules could change order volume and the timing of revenue recognition. A high public share means the company is directly exposed to policy changes.

Funding and contract terms

The BEXCO Exhibition Hall 3 contract has no down payment or advance payment and is paid on progress billing. The company disclosed that contract amount and period may change with construction progress.

Because cash recovery depends on progress, working capital and project funding during construction could affect the balance sheet.

Seoul redevelopment competition

Seoul redevelopment is highly competitive, with large builders competing on brand. Many of the company's Seoul housing projects are small street-home redevelopments, so if large redevelopment and reconstruction wins are delayed, the pace of housing-segment growth could be limited (Asia Today report).

Overall view

Kye-Ryong Construction Industrial has a public-procurement-led order base and an order backlog of KRW 14.14 trillion. Operating profit rose 70.8% to KRW 166.9 billion in 2025, and operating cash flow turned positive at KRW 209.9 billion.

However, Q3 2026 attributable net income fell 76.1% to KRW 5.88 billion, exposing a gap with operating profit, and the debt-to-equity ratio rose gradually to 225.5%. Supporting factors include top-tier public orders, the expanded 2026 SOC budget and a larger 2026 order intake.

Offsetting factors include volatile net income, leverage, a history of thin margins and the 8.9% revenue decline in 2025. In the housing segment, the outcome of large redevelopment bids is the item to watch. The share price trades well below book value, but whether quarterly earnings stabilize is the central question.

The next checkpoints are contract change disclosures after the BEXCO project starts and full-year results in the 2026 annual report.
