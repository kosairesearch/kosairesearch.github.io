02

Business structure

Kye-Ryong Construction Industrial is a housing and civil engineering contractor. Its 2025 sales comprised building construction contracts of KRW 1.69 trillion and civil engineering contracts of KRW 653.7 billion, with public-client-driven revenue making up 81.2% of the total.

Sales from the sale-type housing business fell 71.3% from KRW 801.3 billion in the prior year to KRW 229.9 billion, and its share of total sales dropped from 25.6% to 8.0%.

According to a BNK Investment & Securities report, the company ranked third in public works orders in 2021, fifth in 2022, third in 2023 and first in 2024.

Its 2026 awards included the Suwon Dangsu 2 public housing project for LH worth KRW 112.2 billion, the Eco Delta City 1BL public-sale housing project for Busan Urban Corporation worth KRW 150.67 billion, the Nonsan Naedong 3 housing project worth KRW 144.2 billion, and the Gochuk-dong street-type redevelopment project worth KRW 103.3 billion.

In November 2026 it disclosed a KRW 97.6 billion share of the Seonam-bu Sports Town facility in Daejeon, a 57.70% stake, and a KRW 83.8 billion BEXCO Exhibition Hall 3 project in Busan, a 35.83% stake, both ordered through the Public Procurement Service.

In the business report filed in March 2026, rebar accounted for 39.6% of raw material purchases and ready-mixed concrete for 28.6%. The company competes with large contractors in public and redevelopment markets and has said it will selectively bid for projects with secured profitability.

04

Earnings analysis

Annual sales were KRW 2.95 trillion in 2022, KRW 2.98 trillion in 2023 (up 0.9%), and KRW 3.17 trillion in 2024 (up 6.5%), before falling to KRW 2.89 trillion in 2025 (down 8.9%). Among 2022 to 2025, 2024 was the largest year for sales and 2025 the smallest.

Operating profit fell from KRW 132.8 billion in 2022 to KRW 100.7 billion in 2023 (down 24.2%) and KRW 97.7 billion in 2024 (down 3.0%), then rose to KRW 166.9 billion in 2025 (up 70.8%), the largest year in the period. The operating margin moved from 4.5% in 2022 to 3.4%, 3.1% and 5.8% in 2025.

Margins improved in 2025 even as sales declined, which analysts link to the shrinking sale-type housing business and a revenue mix centered on public clients.

Net profit attributable to shareholders was KRW 60.5 billion in 2022, KRW 47.2 billion in 2023 (down 21.9%), KRW 47.4 billion in 2024 (up 0.3%) and KRW 100 billion in 2025 (up 110.9%), rising for two consecutive years from 2024 through 2025 after the 2023 low.

Net profit including non-controlling interests was KRW 97.9 billion in 2025, below the attributable figure, which implies a loss attributable to non-controlling interests that year. Operating cash flow went from KRW -10.1 billion in 2024 to KRW 209.9 billion in 2025, turning positive.

The debt-to-equity ratio rose from 218.9% in 2022 to 225.5% in 2025, and at year-end 2025 total equity was KRW 990.9 billion, of which KRW 945.6 billion was attributable to shareholders.

On a quarterly basis, Q3 2025 sales were KRW 661.7 billion with operating profit of KRW 38.7 billion; Q4 2025 sales rose to KRW 823.1 billion (up 24.4% from the prior quarter) with operating profit of KRW 48.1 billion.

Q1 2026 sales were KRW 670.7 billion (down 18.5%) with operating profit of KRW 40.8 billion, and Q2 2026 sales of KRW 840.1 billion and operating profit of KRW 69 billion were the highest of the last five quarters on both measures.

Q3 2026 sales were KRW 798.6 billion (up 20.7% year on year) and operating profit KRW 50.5 billion (up 30.5%), yet net profit attributable to shareholders was only KRW 5.88 billion (down 76.1%), widening the gap. The last four quarters together produced KRW 106.2 billion of net profit attributable to shareholders. The cause of the gap should be checked in the quarterly report footnotes.

05

Industry analysis

To support a construction recovery, the government raised the 2026 social overhead capital budget by KRW 1.6 trillion to KRW 21.1 trillion, a factor analysts cite for broadening the order base of a contractor strong in public works.

Backlog for privately participating public housing projects rose from KRW 878 billion at end-2024 to KRW 1.619 trillion at end of Q3 2025, roughly doubling in nine months. Standalone building backlog reached KRW 3.249 trillion at end of Q3 2025, up 21.2% from end-2024.

The company moved from third place in public works orders in 2021 to first in 2024, which points to a stronger public-sector position. As the sale-type housing business shrank, the direct effect of private sales cycles on revenue has weakened.

Public-client dependence, however, makes budget execution, tender timing and bidding competition more influential on earnings. Rebar and ready-mixed concrete make up a large share of raw material purchases, so material prices and supply conditions are key cost variables.

In cycle terms, a rising public budget combined with expanding public orders differs from a recovery driven by private housing sales. Large contractors compete in the same public and redevelopment segments, so its position depends on selective bidding and profitability more than order volume alone.

06

Outlook

The company has said it will bid selectively for projects with secured profitability and focus resources on stable areas such as public works.

Its 2026 contract disclosures ran from the KRW 112.2 billion Suwon Dangsu 2 public housing project in March, through the KRW 74.1 billion Bucheon street-type redevelopment in April, the KRW 150.67 billion Eco Delta City 1BL and KRW 144.2 billion Nonsan Naedong 3 projects in June, the KRW 103.3 billion Gochuk-dong redevelopment in September, to the Daejeon sports town and Busan BEXCO projects in November.

The BEXCO Exhibition Hall 3 contract runs from December 21, 2026 to February 4, 2031, and the company has said amounts and periods may change as work progresses. The Bucheon project was signed without down payments, on a progress-payment basis.

The most recent consolidated backlog confirmed here stood at KRW 14.2 trillion at end of Q3 2025, which analysts described as roughly four and a half years of work. The confirmed 2026 public works budget is cited as a basis for expanding public orders.

With Q3 2026 net profit attributable to shareholders down to KRW 5.88 billion, whether the gap between operating and net profit narrows is the next point to watch. Preliminary Q4 2026 results are typically released in early 2027, and the 2026 annual business report is due by the end of March 2027. Operating margin and the debt-to-equity ratio should be read together across these filings.

Debt burden and receivables

The debt-to-equity ratio rose from 218.9% in 2022 to 225.5% in 2025, with total liabilities at KRW 2.23 trillion at end-2025. Construction receivables stood at KRW 165.2 billion at end-Q3 2024, up from KRW 85.9 billion at end-2023. Rising receivables make the pace of collections important for cash flow and financial soundness.

Dependence on public orders

Public-client-driven revenue at 81.2% makes the company sensitive to budget execution and tender timing. Sales fell 8.9% in 2025, and Q1 2026 sales fell 18.5% from the prior quarter. Lower exposure to private sales has been offset by greater sensitivity to policy changes and bidding competition.

Risk factors

Policy and order dependence

A high share of public orders means that budget execution and public housing tender timing feed directly into sales. Intense bidding could conflict with the stated plan to choose profitable projects. Contract amounts and periods may change as work progresses, so disclosed awards cannot be read as earnings directly.

Balance sheet and receivables

The debt-to-equity ratio was 225.5% at end-2025, above the 218.9% of 2022. Total liabilities of KRW 2.23 trillion exceed total equity of KRW 990.9 billion. Because receivables rose in an earlier period, the pace of collections and the durability of operating cash flow need monitoring.

Cost inputs and quarterly volatility

Rebar and ready-mixed concrete account for a large share of raw material purchases, so material price swings affect costs. Quarterly operating profit ranged from KRW 38.7 billion in Q3 2025 to KRW 69 billion in Q2 2026.

Net profit attributable to shareholders fell to KRW 5.88 billion in Q3 2026, making quarterly earnings harder to predict.

Overall view

Kye-Ryong Construction Industrial's reported results show operating profit and net profit attributable to shareholders rising together in 2025, even as sales declined. Shrinking the sale-type housing business and reshaping revenue around public clients, at 81.2% of sales, appears behind the 5.8% operating margin.

Public housing and redevelopment awards continued through 2026, with Daejeon and Busan projects added in November. Against Q3 2026 operating profit of KRW 50.5 billion, net profit attributable to shareholders of only KRW 5.88 billion calls for a check on how operating profit converts to net profit.
