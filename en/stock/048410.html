02

Business structure

Hyundai Bioscience was spun off from Hyundai Electronics in 2000 and is a bio-pharmaceutical and cosmeceutical company; it largely exited its IT business in 2018 and reoriented around biotech.

Its production base is a plant in Gimcheon, Gyeongbuk, and it runs R&D and manufacturing and sales through overseas joint ventures and subsidiaries in Japan and the United States. Its display business has generated virtually no revenue since 2019, so the sales base has narrowed to bio hair-care products and cosmetics.

A 2026 review still describes overseas sales of bio hair-care and cosmetics products, including Vitabrid C12, as the base, while new-drug commercialization has been slow. The core new-drug candidate is Jepty (CP-COV03), an oral niclosamide-based compound that uses a drug-delivery technology.

In Vietnam, it is in a Phase 2/3 trial for dengue fever, while CP-PCA07 for prostate cancer in Korea is preparing to enroll patients for a Phase 1 study. In October 2026, an integrated dengue and related infectious disease Phase 2/3 trial, DEN-201, was formally presented at IDWeek 2026 in Washington, D.C.

Around the same period, a plan for a Phase 2 trial in severe fever with thrombocytopenia syndrome (SFTS) was reported, with patient recruitment cited as the key issue. Clinical trials, regulatory consultations and product approval are separate procedures.

The largest shareholder group, C&Pharm and four others, holds 11.40%. The competitive field overlaps with domestic and foreign companies developing antiviral candidates at the clinical stage, but its revenue depends on cosmetics and hair-care products, which differs from large bio companies with production-based sales.

04

Earnings analysis

In the annual disclosed results, revenue rose from KRW 7.85 billion in 2022 to KRW 9.48 billion in 2023, a 20.8% increase, and then to KRW 15.1 billion in 2024, a 58.7% increase; 2024 was the largest year in the 2022 to 2025 period. Revenue fell 76.6% to KRW 3.52 billion in 2025, making it the smallest year.

The operating loss was KRW 26.4 billion in 2022 and KRW 9.79 billion in 2023, the company then posted an operating profit of KRW 791 million in 2024, and in 2025 it returned to an operating loss of KRW 18.2 billion.

The operating margin was -335.7% in 2022, -103.2% in 2023, 5.3% in 2024 and -516.3% in 2025, moving sharply with changes in the revenue base.

The net loss attributable to controlling shareholders was KRW 15.8 billion in 2022, KRW 14.5 billion in 2023, KRW 6.93 billion in 2024 and KRW 22.1 billion in 2025, a four-year run of losses. The net loss including non-controlling interests was disclosed at the same amounts for each year.

Operating cash flow was -KRW 16.5 billion in 2022, -KRW 6.01 billion in 2023, KRW 2.26 billion positive in 2024, and -KRW 17 billion in 2025, so apart from 2024 cash flowed out of operations. The debt ratio fell from 71.7% in 2024 to 18.1% in 2025.

Quarterly, revenue was KRW 1.92 billion in the fourth quarter of 2025, up 247.6% from the prior quarter, then KRW 325 million in the first quarter of 2026, down 83.1%, and KRW 462 million in the third quarter of 2026, down 16.5% year on year.

The operating loss ran for five consecutive quarters from KRW 4.62 billion in the third quarter of 2025 to KRW 5.17 billion in the third quarter of 2026, with the largest at KRW 5.54 billion in the second quarter of 2026.

The net loss attributable to controlling shareholders peaked at KRW 11.6 billion in the second quarter of 2026, and the third-quarter figure of KRW 6.84 billion narrowed from both the prior quarter and the year-earlier quarter.

The four-quarter total for the net loss attributable to controlling shareholders is KRW 28.5 billion. In the third quarter of 2026, the net loss narrowed, but the operating loss widened from the year-earlier quarter, so the operating-level deficit expanded.

05

Industry analysis

The antiviral and infectious disease treatment field targets conditions where standard therapies are limited. A professor at UC San Diego School of Medicine explained that dengue and other febrile viral diseases are hard to tell apart in their early symptoms, so early intervention with broad antivirals is needed.

For SFTS, the company's representative pointed to the absence of treatment alternatives, which supports the view that unmet need is large. In this field, clinical data and regulatory outcomes determine business value more than market size.

In terms of cycle position, the company is at an early development stage, with clinical-stage pipelines central to the business while commercial sales have shrunk.

Large bio-pharmaceutical peers with production-based sales can partly absorb clinical failure risk through product revenue, whereas Hyundai Bioscience has less revenue to absorb such risk.

Many domestic and foreign companies are developing candidates in infectious diseases, so trial design and the speed of patient enrollment are the differentiators. At IDWeek 2026, a leading antiviral researcher presented Jepty's integrated trial design and early data, which allowed external academic review.

On investor sentiment, one reading of recent trading is that a sharp rise and heavy volume show attention but do not settle the direction of earnings or fair value. In short, the company's clinical pace and regulatory discussions, more than industry conditions alone, determine its position in the sector.

Outlook

The most concrete schedule the company has disclosed is the integrated dengue Phase 2/3 trial of Jepty (DEN-201). The company plans to decide the Phase 3 dose based on dose-specific safety and efficacy data from Phase 2.

The Phase 2 data readout is therefore the largest next checkpoint, and its timing has not been disclosed. For SFTS, a plan for a Phase 2 trial has been reported, with patient recruitment identified as the key issue. CP-PCA07, a prostate cancer candidate, is preparing for patient enrollment in a Phase 1 study.

On regulation, clinical trials, regulatory consultations and product approval are separate procedures, so trial results do not translate directly into an approval timetable. On the business side, the overseas sales base for hair-care and cosmetics products remains as the existing revenue.

On reported results, the next regular checkpoint is the 2026 annual report, which will fix annual revenue, operating profit and loss, and the net loss attributable to controlling shareholders.

Fourth-quarter results are included in the annual report rather than a separate quarterly filing, so they are confirmed with the annual figures. At an external academic presentation, an outside expert presented Jepty's integrated trial design directly, which allowed independent review.

On funding, share capital rose to KRW 48.0 billion at year-end 2025, indicating external capital was raised, and any further financing can be tracked through future filings. Overall, clinical timelines and the figures in the regular reports will be the reference points for future checks.
