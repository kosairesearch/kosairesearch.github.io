02

Business structure

Hansol Paper is a paper maker whose main lines are printing paper, industrial paper (white board) and specialty paper such as thermal paper. Its largest shareholder is the group holding company Hansol Holdings, with a 30.49% stake at end-June 2026.

Thermal paper, used for receipts, labels and tickets, is a core specialty line; the company says it has the world's largest thermal paper capacity on its own count and supplies about 85% of domestic demand.

The industrial paper segment produces white board, and Eugene Investment & Securities said in a November 2, 2026 report that the segment posted Q3 2026 revenue of KRW 146.0 billion and operating profit of KRW 6.0 billion, extending a run of eight consecutive quarters of operating profit.

Daejeon plant annual capacity is about 750,000 tons, and the company estimated a 46.0% domestic white board share as of end-May 2026. Main raw materials are pulp and recovered paper.

Export mix is large: first-half 2026 exports of printing and specialty paper from the headquarters reached KRW 459.5 billion, about 1.6 times domestic sales of KRW 285.6 billion, and over 35% of printing paper exports went to North America on the company's count. Same-industry listed peers include Moorim P&P, DaelimPaper, Hankuk Paper MFG, PaperCorea and Shindaeyang Paper.

04

Earnings analysis

Annual revenue fell from KRW 2.46 trillion in 2022 to KRW 2.19 trillion in 2023 (down 10.7% year on year), then rose to KRW 2.22 trillion in 2024 and KRW 2.29 trillion in 2025, an increase in each of the last two years. 2022 was the largest year in the period and 2023 the smallest.

Operating profit fell from KRW 130.2 billion in 2022 to KRW 47.2 billion in 2023 (down 63.7%) and KRW 22 billion in 2024 (down 53.5%), then recovered to KRW 49.9 billion in 2025 (up 127.1%). The operating margin dropped from 5.3% in 2022 to 1.0% in 2024 before reaching 2.2% in 2025.

For 2024, the decline was tied to bad-debt provisions on logistics warehouses in the environment and construction segment. Eugene Investment & Securities said in a January 2025 report that those provisions reached about KRW 70 billion for the year, and that excluding them the paper segment was recovering.

The same report projected 2025 operating profit of KRW 111.1 billion, against KRW 49.9 billion actually reported.

Owner net income went from KRW 73.8 billion in 2022 to KRW 1.76 billion in 2023 (down 97.6%), a KRW 30.4 billion net loss in 2024 (a swing into deficit), and KRW 3.89 billion in 2025 (a swing back to profit).

The gap between operating profit and net income appears to reflect heavy interest costs: standalone operating profit in 2025 was KRW 44.1 billion while interest expense was about KRW 36 billion. Quarterly results are volatile.

Operating profit went from KRW 2.55 billion in Q3 2025 to KRW 7.71 billion in Q4 2025 (up 202.3% quarter on quarter), KRW 11.2 billion in Q1 2026 (up 45.8%) and KRW 50.8 billion in Q2 2026 (up 352.0%).

Drivers of the Q2 improvement cited in coverage included higher sales of white board, higher thermal paper prices after a supply disruption in Chinese thermal dye, and a tariff refund effect after US reciprocal tariffs were invalidated.

Q3 2026 swung to an operating loss of KRW 6.4 billion, and Eugene Investment & Securities cited a weaker exchange rate, inventory valuation losses and higher shipping freight as negative factors. Inventories stood at KRW 466.1 billion at end-June 2026, about 25% above year-end 2025.

According to brokerage commentary, total borrowings in Q1 2026 exceeded KRW 820 billion and operating cash flow was negative. Owner net income for the last four quarters (Q4 2025 to Q3 2026) totaled KRW 18.2 billion.

05

Industry analysis

Printing paper faces a structural headwind as paper's role as a message medium shrinks. At the October 21, 2026 National Assembly audit, CEO Han Kyung-rok pointed to this trend and said the company has expanded toward eco-friendly packaging and materials.

On the cost side, higher pulp prices and energy and logistics costs, combined with printing-paper price adjustments, are the swing factors for profit recovery.

Thermal paper exports face a US anti-dumping review: the 3rd annual review set a 0% margin, while the preliminary ruling in the 4th review, announced October 19, 2026, set 1.69%. The review covers sales from November 2024 to October 2025, and no final ruling had been issued as of November 10, 2026.

Pricing practices in printing paper became a legal issue: the Korea Fair Trade Commission said in April 2026 that six paper makers, including Hansol Paper, agreed seven times to raise printing paper prices between February 2021 and December 2024.

In board, the company is widening recovered-carton collection to secure raw material and outlets. In cosmetics materials, the wood-derived cellulose fiber Duracle is positioned as a viscosity-control ingredient.

At the March 2026 general meeting, the CEO named expanding non-commodity product lines a core task, arguing that general-purpose products alone will not sustain domestic manufacturers in global competition.

Against same-industry peers such as Moorim P&P, DaelimPaper and Hankuk Paper MFG, Hansol Paper is tilting toward thermal paper and eco-friendly materials.

06

Outlook

In late July 2026 the company said it expects its earnings recovery to continue in the second half, as major raw-material prices stabilize and eco-friendly, higher-value businesses such as nanocellulose and biodegradable packaging grow.

As medium-term targets, it presented KRW 3 trillion in revenue and KRW 300 billion in operating profit for 2030 in its June 2026 sustainability report, and the gap from current results is still wide.

At the Janggang plant, some lines stopped production after a fatal accident involving a subcontractor employee on November 11, 2026. The disclosure says a partial work-stop order was issued for forklift operations in some processes, and that post-processing became impossible, forcing a temporary halt.

It also reports no full-facility suspension order from authorities. Revenue from the affected line was KRW 271.78 billion on a 2025 annual basis, equal to 11.87% of KRW 2.29 trillion in 2025 consolidated revenue.

The estimated restart date is December 1, 2026, and the company said it will conduct safety checks and re-disclose if production resumes or a material change occurs. On materials, it is negotiating raw-material supply contracts with major cosmetics companies at home and abroad for Duracle.

On efficiency, the company was selected in October 2026 for a government-backed AI factory project for paper processes, reported at a scale of KRW 12.2 billion over four years.

On the cartel issue, a publishing industry group said in October 2026 that it maintains plans to file a joint lawsuit against six companies, including Hansol Paper, within this year. The next periodic report is the 2026 annual report, which will include Q4 2026 results.

08

Bull factors

Anchor in higher-value materials

Thermal paper has the world's largest capacity on the company's count and about 85% of domestic supply. Wood-based Duracle is in supply-contract talks with cosmetics makers, and the paper-based packaging product Protego HS launched in April 2026.

Q2 profit rebound and expected cost relief

Q2 2026 operating profit of KRW 50.8 billion rose 352.0% from Q1. Higher white board sales and thermal paper prices were cited as drivers, and the company expects raw-material prices to stabilize in the second half.

Shareholder return policy and steady industrial paper profits

The company approved an interim dividend in August 2026. The industrial paper segment stayed profitable in Q3 2026, extending eight consecutive quarters of operating profit.

09

Bear factors

Raw-material, FX and inventory pressure

In Q3 2026, a weaker exchange rate, inventory valuation losses and higher shipping costs combined to produce an operating loss, and inventories rose about 25% from year-end 2025 to KRW 466.1 billion at end-June.

Cartel-related damages and legal uncertainty

The KFTC said six paper makers including Hansol Paper agreed seven times to raise printing paper prices from February 2021 to December 2024. The company was granted full leniency-based fine exemption, but affected industries continue to seek damages, and publishers maintain plans for a joint suit this year.

Borrowing burden and interest costs

In 2025, standalone operating profit was KRW 44.1 billion against interest expense of about KRW 36 billion, and brokers said operating cash flow was negative in Q1 2026.

10

Risk factors

Raw materials, FX and logistics costs

Because raw materials exceed 40% of costs in this industry, delayed price pass-through can quickly erode operating profit. In Q1 2026 the operating profit decline was much larger than the revenue decline, and in Q3 exchange rates and freight also weighed on results.

Legal and regulatory (cartel damages, anti-dumping)

At the audit, the CEO said the company would review damages procedures but gave no compensation amount or timetable. The preliminary dumping margin of 1.69% in the 4th US anti-dumping review leaves tariff exposure until a final ruling.

Production disruption and safety

A fatal accident involving a subcontractor employee halted some lines at the Janggang plant. The affected line's 2025 revenue was KRW 271.78 billion, equal to 11.87% of annual revenue, and the disclosure notes that results of the authorities' investigation could change the outlook.

Overall view

Hansol Paper recovered to KRW 49.9 billion in operating profit in 2025 from its 2024 low, but swung back to a KRW 6.4 billion operating loss in Q3 2026, so quarterly earnings remain highly volatile.

Supporting factors include the shift toward higher-value materials such as thermal paper and Duracle, the KRW 50.8 billion operating profit in Q2 2026, the industrial paper segment's eight consecutive profitable quarters, and a 25–35% shareholder return target for 2026–2028.

Offsetting factors include raw-material and FX-driven cost swings, higher inventories at end-June 2026, KRW 843 billion in standalone borrowings and interest costs, and claims tied to the cartel case.

On an asset basis the share price sits well below book value per share, while owner net income over the last four quarters totaled KRW 18.2 billion, leaving an earnings-based multiple open to wide interpretation.

Points to confirm include the estimated restart date of December 1, 2026 at the Janggang plant, whether the joint lawsuit is filed this year, the final US anti-dumping ruling, Q4 2026 preliminary results around January 2027, and the 2026 annual report by end-March 2027.

The 2030 targets of KRW 3 trillion in revenue and KRW 300 billion in operating profit provide a benchmark for measuring the distance from current results.
