02

Business structure

Founded in 1996 and headquartered in Yongsan-gu, Seoul, Barunson Entertainment & Arts plans, produces and supplies film, drama and other video content. Its businesses span content production and rights, film investment and distribution, and other operations including office leasing.

According to FnGuide's breakdown, investment and distribution accounted for 58.32% of 2024 revenue, followed by office leasing at 19.98%, other content rights at 10.58% and drama production at 9.07%.

In the same breakdown, content production was 61.50% of 2023 revenue and film operations 28.18%, suggesting the mix has shifted from production toward investment and distribution. The category structure changed in 2024, so direct year-to-year comparison is limited.

In overseas distribution, the film 'Dont Cry Butterfly' reportedly won two awards at the Venice Film Festival. Production partnerships included a co-production with a Thai producer on 'Delete the Movie' in February 2025 and a global collaboration with an Indonesian studio in May 2025.

In June 2025 the company said it was moving generative AI-based content production into full operation. For 2025, the company said new-business exhibition revenue grew but investment costs for the new business weighed on operating results.

Competition overlaps with large producers such as CJ ENM and Studio Dragon in film and drama, but direct comparison on scale and business scope is difficult. Its revenue model differs from music and artist-management-focused peers such as HYBE, SM Entertainment and JYP Ent.

04

Earnings analysis

Annual revenue moved from KRW 26 billion in 2022 to KRW 23.7 billion in 2023 (down 9.1% year on year), KRW 9.49 billion in 2024 (down 59.9%) and KRW 11.6 billion in 2025 (up 22.3%). The largest year in 2022–2025 was 2022 and the smallest was 2024.

The operating loss was KRW 4.22 billion in 2022, KRW 4.82 billion in 2023 (losses widened), KRW 266 million in 2024 (losses narrowed) and KRW 2.28 billion in 2025 (losses widened).

The operating margin was -16.2% in 2022, -20.4% in 2023, -2.8% in 2024 and -19.6% in 2025, so the smallest loss margin came in 2024, the year of the smallest revenue. The company has recorded losses for four consecutive years from 2022 to 2025.

The net loss attributable to controlling shareholders was KRW 13.3 billion in 2022, KRW 13.3 billion in 2023, KRW 5.13 billion in 2024 and KRW 6.88 billion in 2025.

Net income including non-controlling interests, which differs from the controlling figure, was a loss of KRW 13.3 billion in 2022, KRW 13.9 billion in 2023, KRW 5.18 billion in 2024 and KRW 6.89 billion in 2025.

Quarterly, revenue of KRW 967 million in Q3 2025 rose to KRW 3.83 billion in Q4 2025, up 296.1% quarter on quarter, while the operating loss widened from KRW 724 million to KRW 1.81 billion.

Revenue was KRW 2.3 billion in Q1 2026, KRW 2.24 billion in Q2 and KRW 4.04 billion in Q3, with operating losses of KRW 109 million, KRW 259 million and KRW 352 million.

Net income attributable to controlling shareholders moved from KRW 585 million in Q3 2025 to a loss of KRW 7.23 billion in Q4 2025, then a loss of KRW 105 million in Q1 2026, a profit of KRW 417 million in Q2 and a loss of KRW 2.24 billion in Q3.

The Q4 2025 loss accounts for about 79% of the KRW 9.16 billion controlling net loss over the last four quarters. Operating cash flow was KRW -5.05 billion in 2022, KRW 300 million in 2023, KRW -2.31 billion in 2024 and KRW -6.43 billion in 2025.

At the end of 2025, total equity was KRW 58.4 billion, equity attributable to controlling shareholders was KRW 58.8 billion and total liabilities were KRW 44.5 billion, with the debt ratio rising to 76.2% from 64.9% in 2024.

05

Industry analysis

Korea's film market is seeing slow recovery in theater demand, with viewing shifting toward platforms such as OTT services. The company's own profile frames its strategy as seeking OTT growth amid weak theater attendance.

Profit for content companies tends to swing widely with a title's box office and distribution results. On a cycle view, annual revenue was smallest in 2024 and recovered in 2025, and quarterly revenue rose sharply in Q4 2025 and Q3 2026.

Quarterly volatility remains large, so one or two distribution outcomes can dominate a given quarter. Among peers in media and entertainment, the large talent agencies are centered on music and artists, while Barunson focuses on film and drama production and overseas co-production, so the business models differ.

Compared with drama-focused Studio Dragon or diversified CJ ENM, its own IP base and distribution scale are smaller, so its revenue is less anchored by large platform commissions. Co-productions in Indonesia and Thailand offer growth access to partners but also depend on local partners' production capacity.

Moving generative AI-based content production into full operation in June 2025 could change production cost structure. Whether the industry recovers will depend on whether theater attendance and OTT rights demand move together.

Large swings in quarterly revenue

Revenue rose 296.1% quarter on quarter in Q4 2025 and then fell 39.9% in Q1 2026. Annual revenue also swung from KRW 9.49 billion in 2024 to KRW 11.6 billion in 2025. Because results depend on when titles are released and distributed, quarterly revenue is hard to forecast.

Risk factors

Share price volatility risk

Shares outstanding fell from 74,439,675 to 18,609,918 after the capital reduction. A smaller float can amplify price moves for the same trading volume. Volatility has increased since trading resumed.

Title performance and new-business cost risk

Profit for content companies can swing widely with a title's box office and distribution results. In 2025, new-business revenue grew but its investment costs weighed on operating results. Until monetization is confirmed, these costs remain a factor that can widen losses.

Governance and theme-stock risk

The largest shareholder group holds 40.80% as of October 22, 2026, so control is concentrated. Past reports described the company as having been included in a political theme-stock list, and its history of exchangeable bond issuance has also been noted. Theme-driven trading can magnify price swings.
