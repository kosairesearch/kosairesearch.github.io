02

Business structure

DaedongGear is a powertrain manufacturer founded in 1973 and listed on KOSDAQ in 1991, and it is an affiliate of the Daedong group whose parent is Daedong. According to a Value Finder report, the first-half 2026 sales mix was 49.2% agricultural machinery, 43.5% automotive and 7.3% industrial.

The agricultural segment supplies drivetrain parts and transmissions, and its sales fell from KRW 180.8 billion in 2023 to KRW 114.1 billion in 2025 according to IB Tomato.

IB Tomato analysis attributes the decline in the agricultural share from 64.3% in 2023 to 49.2% in the first half of 2026 mainly to lower parent-company volumes rather than to diversification.

Customer concentration on the parent remains high: parent-related sales fell from KRW 175.5 billion in 2023 to KRW 111.1 billion in 2025, a 36.7% drop, while non-parent sales rose 3.9% over the same period, from KRW 105.7 billion to KRW 109.9 billion.

In electrified products, the main contract is an electric-vehicle reducer part supply from Hyundai Transys worth about KRW 138.5 billion.

Overseas, the company will supply three tractor gearbox models to Company A, a global No. 3 agricultural machinery maker, with expected sales of about KRW 71.8 billion over seven years. A domestic construction-equipment maker, Company D, has also contracted reducers for small excavators.

In robotics, the company is pursuing reducers and actuators, and it is pursuing a joint venture with a Chinese harmonic reducer maker for humanoid robots.

04

Earnings analysis

Annual revenue rose from KRW 242.1 billion in 2022 to KRW 281.2 billion in 2023, a 16.2% increase, and that year was the peak. Revenue then fell 8.6% to KRW 257.1 billion in 2024 and 14.1% to KRW 220.9 billion in 2025, so it declined for two consecutive years from 2024 through 2025.

Operating profit went from KRW 7.59 billion in 2022 to KRW 8.09 billion in 2023, a 6.6% increase, then fell 44.8% to KRW 4.46 billion in 2024 and 96.4% to KRW 162 million in 2025. The operating margin moved from 3.1% in 2022 to 2.9% in 2023, 1.7% in 2024 and 0.1% in 2025, the lowest of the four years.

Net profit attributable to controlling shareholders fell from KRW 4.23 billion in 2022 to KRW 2.77 billion in 2023 and KRW 1.17 billion in 2024, then turned into a net loss of KRW 4.74 billion in 2025. Total net loss for 2025 was also KRW 4.74 billion.

At the end of 2025, total equity was KRW 79.1 billion and total liabilities were KRW 190.4 billion, and the debt-to-equity ratio rose to 240.6% from 191.5% a year earlier. Operating cash flow stayed positive at KRW 9.63 billion in 2025.

Quarterly revenue went from KRW 50 billion in the third quarter of 2025 to KRW 51.1 billion in the fourth quarter, then KRW 59.3 billion in the first quarter of 2026 and KRW 67 billion in the second quarter, before falling to KRW 53 billion in the third quarter.

Operating profit was a KRW 1.65 billion profit in the second quarter of 2026 after losses in earlier quarters, but the third quarter returned to an operating loss of KRW 2.01 billion, wider than a year earlier.

Net profit attributable to controlling shareholders was a loss of KRW 2.54 billion in the third quarter of 2026, and the last four quarters total a net loss of KRW 3.39 billion.

IB Tomato analysis points to higher fixed costs from new lines running below capacity, and capital expenditure rose from KRW 7.2 billion in 2023 to KRW 11.1 billion in 2024 and KRW 25.8 billion in 2025.

05

Industry analysis

The company's industry exposure falls into three areas. First, agricultural machinery moves in step with the parent Daedong's global cycle; a Korea IR Council researcher said most of DaedongGear's agricultural sales come from the parent and that the segment fell as demand for high-horsepower tractors weakened.

The parent reported a third-quarter 2026 consolidated operating loss of KRW 39.3 billion, with North American sales down 11.7% and European sales down 69.1%.

Second, electrified parts depend on electric-vehicle demand, which has slowed temporarily in some markets, yet the company keeps adding EV parts and module contracts.

Third, robot components are at an early stage: reducer and actuator development is ongoing, but customer qualification and revenue recognition still need confirmation.

The stock rose nearly 17% in a single day on November 4, 2026 on expectations for robot reducers and electrified parts, and that move does not by itself mean earnings have followed. The company aims to raise sales outside the parent to 70% by 2030.

Competition splits between captive supply to vehicle-related customers and winning overseas agricultural customers, and the contract with Company A is described as the company's first supply agreement with an overseas agricultural machinery maker.

06

Outlook

The company's 2026 targets are revenue of KRW 270 billion, up by double digits from the prior year, and at least KRW 500 billion in new orders.

Adding the disclosed quarterly revenue for the first three quarters of 2026 gives KRW 179.3 billion, so reaching the target would require a sharp rise in fourth-quarter revenue.

New orders were KRW 262.8 billion in the first half of 2026, and the company is negotiating additional EV and agricultural supply projects worth about KRW 300 billion in the second half. If those are all contracted, annual new orders could reach about KRW 570 billion, according to the company.

Key schedule items are as follows. The Hyundai Transys EV reducer part is set for pilot production in November 2026 and mass production from April 2027, and the company expects EV-related sales of at least KRW 75 billion in 2027, about three times the 2026 level.

The tractor gearbox for Company A is in sample ordering with mass supply targeted for the second half of 2027.

On capacity, the company is building 20 new production lines, and a KRW 35 billion investment for added capacity and robotics was reported in early 2026, with a third plant on about 10,000 pyeong of idle land possibly starting by year-end 2026.

A rights offering in July 2026 raised KRW 40.3 billion, but reports say another KRW 17 billion is still needed for new equipment. Medium-term goals are KRW 1 trillion in annual revenue by 2030 and 70% of sales from outside the parent, both as stated by company management.

08

Bull factors

Backlog that could convert into sales

The cumulative order backlog from January 2024 through May 2026 totaled KRW 1.8644 trillion. New orders of KRW 262.8 billion in the first half of 2026 equal about 119% of the prior year's revenue. The Hyundai Transys EV part applies for eight years from 2027, with mass production planned for April 2027.

Early signs of customer diversification

Non-parent sales rose from KRW 105.7 billion in 2023 to KRW 109.9 billion in 2025. The company signed its first supply contract with an overseas agricultural machinery maker and a reducer contract with a construction-equipment maker. Management has said it aims to raise the non-parent share of sales to 70% by 2030.

Expansion into robot components

The company is pursuing robot core components such as reducers and actuators. It is working toward a joint venture with a Chinese harmonic reducer maker for humanoid robots, and its 2026 corporate value-enhancement plan lists robotics as a growth pillar. Expansion could widen if customer qualification and mass-production yields are confirmed.

Bear factors

Losses continue to persist

Net loss attributable to controlling shareholders turned into a loss of KRW 4.74 billion in 2025. The third-quarter 2026 operating loss of KRW 2.01 billion was wider than a year earlier. The last four quarters total a net loss of KRW 3.39 billion attributable to controlling shareholders.

Dependence on parent's agricultural cycle

Agricultural machinery sales fell from KRW 180.8 billion in 2023 to KRW 114.1 billion in 2025. Parent-related sales dropped 36.7% from KRW 175.5 billion to KRW 111.1 billion over the same period. The company's revenue continues to move with the parent's agricultural cycle.

Financial strain and further funding needs

The debt-to-equity ratio was 240.6% at the end of 2025. Total borrowings reached KRW 129.6 billion in the first half of 2026, up from KRW 79.7 billion at the end of 2023. A KRW 40.3 billion rights offering in July 2026 was completed, but reports say KRW 17 billion is still needed for new equipment.

10

Risk factors

Equipment ramp-up and mass-production timing

The Hyundai Transys EV part is planned for pilot production in November 2026 and mass production in April 2027. If the 20 new lines do not reach utilization on schedule, fixed costs could keep pressing on earnings. A delay in customer quality approval would also push back revenue recognition.

Parent and agricultural cycle

The parent Daedong reported a third-quarter 2026 consolidated operating loss of KRW 39.3 billion, with lower sales in North America and Europe. If demand for high-horsepower tractors keeps weakening, deliveries of high-horsepower powertrain parts could stall. Reliance on the parent remains high even though the agricultural share has fallen.

EV demand and funding

The EV market is going through a demand slowdown, so volumes under electrified contracts could fluctuate. With capital spending continuing and self-generated cash weak, further borrowing or equity raises may be needed. Worse financing conditions could disrupt delivery of the contracted orders.

12

Overall view

DaedongGear has built an order backlog of KRW 1.8644 trillion and booked KRW 262.8 billion in new orders in the first half of 2026, but earnings have been weak, with 2025 operating profit of KRW 162 million and a net loss of KRW 4.74 billion attributable to controlling shareholders.

The second-quarter 2026 operating profit was followed by an operating loss of KRW 2.01 billion in the third quarter, so converting orders into profit is still being confirmed quarter by quarter.

The business mix is moving toward lower agricultural dependence and more electrified and overseas customers, but reliance on parent-company sales and the agricultural cycle remains significant. Finances carry a debt-to-equity ratio of 240.6%, alongside a July 2026 rights offering and continuing equipment funding needs.

Robot components are at an early stage, and customer qualification and revenue recognition still need confirmation. The next checkpoints are whether 2026 order targets are met, the start of EV-part mass production in April 2027, and the operating margin and debt ratio in the 2026 annual report.
