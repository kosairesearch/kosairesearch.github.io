02

Business structure

SuNAM is a high-temperature superconducting wire manufacturer founded in 2004 and listed on KOSDAQ in 2020.

It uses its proprietary RCE-DR process to produce superconducting wire for power cables, fault current limiters and fusion reactors, and it also develops and delivers non-insulated superconducting magnet systems and rotating machines.

According to WiseReport's product breakdown, superconducting wire manufacturing equipment accounts for about 69%, second-generation high-temperature superconducting wire for about 24%, and merchandise for about 7%. Sales are order-based, so revenue moves with the timing of project awards.

Listed customers include Korean power and cable companies alongside Siemens, Nexans, Airbus, NASA and Commonwealth Fusion System, and the products serve cable, fault-current-limiter and magnet applications. The company runs RCE-DR, IBAD and PLD production equipment.

In power-grid applications it positions itself as a global core supplier on critical current performance and price. In magnet applications it has finished developing PLD-based new products and is widening supply mainly to fusion research institutes. Its magnet business has been described as less competitive than rivals, which weighed on results.

04

Earnings analysis

Annual results show large swings in revenue. Revenue went from KRW 6.36 billion in 2022 to KRW 6.39 billion in 2023 (up 0.4% year on year), the largest year in 2022–2025, and then to KRW 1.41 billion in 2024 (down 78.0%), the smallest year. Revenue reached KRW 3.39 billion in 2025, up 140.9% year on year.

Delayed superconducting equipment orders from 2024 were recognized in 2025, which drove that rebound. The rebound did not translate into smaller losses.

Operating losses were KRW 2.4 billion in 2022, KRW 3.09 billion in 2023 (losses widened), KRW 4.47 billion in 2024 (losses widened) and KRW 3.86 billion in 2025 (losses narrowed), a loss in each of the four years from 2022 to 2025.

Operating margins were -37.7% in 2022, -48.4% in 2023, -317.8% in 2024 and -113.9% in 2025. The very negative 2024 figure reflected a revenue base that fell to KRW 1.41 billion.

Net losses attributable to controlling shareholders were KRW 2.12 billion in 2022, KRW 11.3 billion in 2023, KRW 3.94 billion in 2024 and KRW 3.98 billion in 2025. The 2023 loss was more than three times the operating loss of KRW 3.09 billion.

Net income including non-controlling interests was recorded as the same loss amount in each year. The debt-to-equity ratio fell from 123.5% in 2022 to 40.1% in 2023 and rose again to 70.4% in 2025. Total liabilities at the end of 2025 were KRW 6.85 billion and total equity was KRW 9.74 billion.

Operating cash flow was -KRW 2.03 billion in 2024 and -KRW 1.91 billion in 2025.

Quarterly revenue was KRW 958 million in Q3 2025, KRW 1.04 billion in Q4 2025 (up 8.6% quarter on quarter), KRW 453 million in Q1 2026 (down 56.4%), KRW 1.2 billion in Q2 2026 (up 164.1%) and KRW 790 million in Q3 2026 (down 34.1% quarter on quarter and down 17.6% year on year).

Operating losses ran in all five quarters from Q3 2025 to Q3 2026. The Q3 2026 operating loss of KRW 656 million narrowed from both the prior quarter and the year-earlier quarter, while the controlling-shareholder net loss of KRW 761 million widened year on year. The controlling-shareholder net loss over the last four quarters totaled KRW 4.56 billion.

05

Industry analysis

SuNAM's downstream demand comes from three areas. The first is fusion energy: the company has a contract with the UK's UKIFS for the STEP fusion project. In magnet applications, it is expanding supply mainly to fusion research institutes.

The second is power cables for AI data centers, where the company says it is strengthening superconducting cable cooperation with US-based VEIR, Inc. The third is the power grid and aerospace, with Korean utilities and cable makers alongside Siemens, Nexans, Airbus and NASA on its customer list.

Within the same machinery and equipment sector, Doosan Enerbility supplies large power generation equipment, whereas SuNAM is a specialized superconducting wire supplier with much smaller order sizes. SuperOx is also named alongside the customer list.

The position in the cycle is hard to quantify, but several order disclosures in the second half of 2026 suggest demand is turning into contracts. Individual contract sizes remain in the hundreds of millions of won, so the timing of larger project awards largely determines revenue swings.

In magnet applications, PLD-based products align with fusion research demand, but how far they differ from competing products rests largely on the company's own description.

08

Bull factors

Revenue rebound and a run of order disclosures

Three single-sales supply contracts were disclosed between July and October 2026, worth KRW 430 million, KRW 660 million and KRW 367,493,250. The last one covers supply from its own production to the United States. A continued order flow is the core support for a steadier revenue base.

Expanding fusion and AI-cable applications

The company has a contract with UKIFS for the STEP fusion project and is strengthening superconducting cable cooperation with VEIR for AI data centers. Its customer list includes Commonwealth Fusion System and Siemens. Broadening from power cables to fusion and data-center uses diversifies the sources of product demand.

Narrowing operating losses

The operating loss for Q3 2026 was KRW 656 million, down from KRW 1.26 billion in Q4 2025. The 2025 operating loss of KRW 3.86 billion was narrower than the KRW 4.47 billion in 2024. The debt-to-equity ratio fell from 123.5% in 2022 to 70.4% in 2025. Shrinking operating losses can be read as an early sign of earnings improvement.

Bear factors

Four straight years of losses and cumulative deficits

The company recorded a loss in each year from 2022 to 2025, and the controlling-shareholder net loss reached KRW 11.3 billion in 2023. Operating cash flow was also negative in both 2024 and 2025. The loss structure has not changed even as revenue has risen.

Volatile order-based revenue

Because sales are order-based, revenue moves with project timing. Quarterly revenue was KRW 453 million in Q1 2026, down 56.4% quarter on quarter, and KRW 1.2 billion in Q2 2026, up 164.1%. Such large swings make earnings harder to project.

Competitive weakness in magnet applications

The company has been described as less competitive than rivals in magnet applications, which weighed on results. Although new magnet products have been developed, the data available does not quantify their differentiation from competing products. Share count growth is also a possibility, which keeps dilution a point to monitor.

Risk factors

Order execution risk

Individual contracts are worth hundreds of millions of won, so a delay in one or two large orders can swing quarterly revenue sharply. Q3 2026 revenue of KRW 790 million also reflected order timing.

Contract periods run into the first half of 2027, so how execution timing feeds into reported results needs to be tracked.

Technology and product competition risk

Magnet applications have been described as less competitive than rivals. It is uncertain whether PLD-based new products will translate into wider supply to fusion research institutes. In power-grid applications, price and performance competition with overseas suppliers is likely to continue.

Financial and shareholding risk

Operating cash flow was negative in both 2024 and 2025. Payment is made within 30 days after invoicing following delivery, so cash collection can lag. As of a report dated April 20, 2026, the largest shareholder and related parties held 8.35%, and the largest shareholder reported a reduction of 170,000 shares.

Overall view

SuNAM supplies superconducting wire on an order basis. Revenue rose 140.9% to KRW 3.39 billion in 2025, a sign of recovery. Still, it posted losses in each year from 2022 to 2025, and operating losses have run for five straight quarters through Q3 2026.

The Q3 2026 operating loss narrowed to KRW 656 million, but the controlling-shareholder net loss widened year on year. The controlling-shareholder net loss over the last four quarters totaled KRW 4.56 billion.

Three supply contracts disclosed between July and October 2026 and partnerships in fusion and AI data-center applications support the bull case. Order-timing revenue swings, weaker competitiveness in magnet applications and negative operating cash flow remain the bear points.
