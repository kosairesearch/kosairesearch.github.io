Sonokongco: Revenue Rebounds on Auto Sales, Operating Losses Persist

Sonokongco swung to a net profit attributable to controlling shareholders in 2025 and its revenue grew sharply in the first half of 2026, yet operating losses have run for five consecutive quarters through the third quarter of 2026, leaving its earnings structure still unproven.

02

Business structure

Sonokongco is a distributor centered on character toys, game software distribution and PC bang operations. According to a securities information site, its revenue mix is 93.48% character toys and packaged games, 6.48% PC bang distribution commissions and 0.04% character licensing income.

Its toy sales previously relied on in-house characters held by a related company, but after that arrangement ended in 2021 the business shifted to importing and selling products from global toy makers.

In April 2024 Mattel notified the company that their domestic distribution relationship would end, effective October 1, 2024. Mattel toys accounted for KRW 12.7 billion, or 26.96% of the standalone annual revenue of KRW 47.1 billion in 2023.

The company said it had signed distribution agreements with other global toy makers such as Jazwares and Moose Toys to fill the gap. In June 2025 HK Mobility, an auto distribution company, became the largest shareholder, and used-car sales and car rental operations were subsequently added to consolidated results.

Consolidated revenue for the first half of 2026 was KRW 81.8 billion, up 249% from KRW 23.4 billion a year earlier, and media reports attributed most of the increase to the auto business.

The toy segment faces structural pressure from a shrinking child population and the spread of Chinese-made toys, so competitive positioning is the central variable there.

04

Earnings analysis

Consolidated revenue fell from KRW 66.7 billion in 2022 to KRW 50.3 billion in 2023, a 24.5% decline, and then to KRW 32 billion in 2024, a 36.4% decline, before recovering to KRW 97 billion in 2025, up 202.9%. The company attributed the 2025 revenue increase mainly to the inclusion of a subsidiary in consolidation.

Operating losses were KRW 5.99 billion in 2022, KRW 9.5 billion in 2023, KRW 9.45 billion in 2024 and KRW 6.68 billion in 2025, which makes 2022 through 2025 four consecutive years of operating losses.

The operating margin worsened from -9.0% in 2022 to -18.9% in 2023 and -29.5% in 2024, then improved to -6.9% in 2025, and the deepest loss relative to sales came in 2024, the year with the smallest revenue.

Net profit attributable to controlling shareholders went from a loss of KRW 7.65 billion in 2022 and KRW 11.9 billion in 2023 to a loss of KRW 9.56 billion in 2024, then turned to a profit of KRW 826 million in 2025.

Net profit including non-controlling interests was KRW 808 million in 2025, a different figure from the controlling-shareholder number, and the company cited derivative valuation and trading gains as a factor behind that increase.

Quarterly operating losses widened from KRW 1.89 billion in the third quarter of 2025 to KRW 2.93 billion in the fourth quarter, narrowed to KRW 734 million in the first quarter of 2026, then widened again to KRW 2.62 billion in the second quarter and KRW 3.04 billion in the third quarter.

Third-quarter 2026 revenue was KRW 39.6 billion, up 12.4% year on year, while the controlling-shareholder net loss of KRW 6.45 billion marks a second consecutive quarterly loss.

Based on the third-quarter 2026 quarterly report, revenue for the first three quarters of 2026 was reported at KRW 121.4 billion with an operating loss of KRW 6.4 billion. The combined controlling-shareholder net loss for the last four quarters was KRW 842 million.

05

Industry analysis

The domestic toy market is in a structural contraction. Citing data from a toy industry cooperative, one report said the market shrank from KRW 2.032 trillion in 2019 to KRW 2.012 trillion in 2021.

Lower birth rates, a shrinking child population, weaker consumer sentiment amid high prices and the shift of spending toward video content were cited as drivers. Statistics Korea data show the number of doll and toy manufacturers fell from 461 in 2005 to 80 in 2019.

In this environment, distribution agreements with global brand owners have become the mainstream model, and Sonokongco has followed that path. Among peers, LEGO Korea was reported to have posted strong results, while a rival with its own intellectual property has also grown its sales.

Companies with owned IP are generally viewed as having less earnings volatility than pure distributors. Sonokongco's revenue fell from KRW 129.3 billion in 2016 to KRW 66.7 billion in 2022, which materially reduced its standing in the industry. Competition has also intensified as Chinese-made toys spread.

Its expansion into used-car sales and car rental, outside toy distribution, is an unusual diversification within the sector.

06

Outlook

The company has pointed to expanding its toy lineup toward adult collectors. In an August 2025 report, its chief executive said that broadening the customer base from young children to adult collectors and securing stable supply of Nintendo Switch 2 units would improve results.

The same report quoted him agreeing that the existing model of distributing overseas IP has lost competitiveness. The auto business has become the main driver of the 2026 revenue increase now that used-car sales and rental fees are reflected in results.

A media report said used-car sales reached KRW 2.5 billion in the first half of 2026, already above the KRW 900 million recorded for the full year 2025. Revenue growth has not yet been matched by profit, so the profitability of mobility and other new businesses is seen as a key task.

In 2023 the company added battery business to its articles of incorporation, but whether that business has contributed to results should be checked in upcoming periodic reports.

The company's stated goal for the toy segment was a return to profit by 2026, and the 2025 and 2026 disclosures do not yet show that target being met. The key points to watch are whether the auto business can offset operating losses and whether the toy segment's operating loss narrows.

08

Bull factors

Revenue recovery and auto business consolidation

Revenue reached KRW 97 billion in 2025, up 202.9%, and first-half 2026 consolidated revenue was reported at KRW 81.8 billion, up 249% from a year earlier. Used-car sales and rental operations drove most of the increase, and used-car sales in the first half of 2026 already exceeded full-year 2025 sales.

The arrival of new revenue sources while toy distribution shrinks is read as a sign of diversification. Whether this growth has translated into better profitability still needs separate confirmation.

Return to profit in 2025 and narrower losses

The 2025 operating loss of KRW 6.68 billion narrowed from KRW 9.45 billion in 2024, and the operating margin improved from -29.5% to -6.9%. Net profit attributable to controlling shareholders moved from a loss of KRW 9.56 billion in 2024 to a profit of KRW 826 million in 2025.

The company cited derivative valuation and trading gains as a factor in net profit growth, and that item should be distinguished from operating improvement. The year shows a move from loss to profit at the net level alongside a smaller operating loss.

Equity base maintained at end-2025

Total equity at the end of 2025 was KRW 49.4 billion, of which KRW 48.6 billion was attributable to controlling shareholders. Concerns about capital impairment were raised around 2024 because of an expanding accumulated deficit, which makes the 2025 equity level relevant for financial stability.

Total liabilities were KRW 70.6 billion, larger than equity, but the equity base itself remains in place. The reasons for the change in equity can be traced in the equity movement section of the periodic report.

Bear factors

Five consecutive quarters of operating losses

Operating losses have run for five consecutive quarters from the third quarter of 2025 through the third quarter of 2026.

The third-quarter 2026 operating loss of KRW 3.04 billion widened from KRW 1.89 billion a year earlier, and the first three quarters of 2026 were reported with a cumulative operating loss of KRW 6.4 billion. Operating losses persisting while revenue grows is the most significant weakness. Controlling-shareholder net losses have also run for two consecutive quarters since the second quarter of 2026.

Structural shrinkage of the core toy distribution business

Revenue fell from KRW 129.3 billion in 2016 to KRW 66.7 billion in 2022 and then to KRW 32 billion in 2024. Mattel toys had accounted for 26.96% of standalone annual revenue in 2023, and that distribution relationship ended in October 2024.

A shrinking child population and the spread of Chinese-made toys continue to weigh on the toy market. Whether new global brand agreements have fully replaced the lost sales is not yet visible in the disclosed figures.

Higher leverage and cash outflow

The debt-to-equity ratio rose from 105.1% in 2024 to 143.0% in 2025. Operating cash flow moved from a KRW 374 million inflow in 2022 to outflows of KRW -1.21 billion in 2023, KRW -2.71 billion in 2024 and KRW -6.28 billion in 2025.

The largest operating cash outflow came in 2025, the year the company returned to a controlling-shareholder profit, which points to a gap between reported earnings and cash. Whether this outflow is linked to the auto business expansion should be checked in the notes of the periodic report.

Risk factors

Business restructuring and change in major shareholder

A change of chief executive in late 2023 was read as a sign that the toy business was facing structural difficulty, and in June 2025 the largest shareholder changed to an auto distribution company.

As the business shifts from toy distribution toward cars and rentals, investment in the existing toy operations could decline. Frequent changes in business direction reduce the predictability of earnings. Whether the change in control leads to better results remains to be confirmed.

Financial structure and cash flow

Total liabilities at the end of 2025 were KRW 70.6 billion, larger than total equity of KRW 49.4 billion. With the debt-to-equity ratio at 143.0% and the operating cash outflow widened to KRW -6.28 billion, financial room is a concern.

Borrowing terms and maturities cannot be assessed from the available data, so the borrowings schedule in the periodic report is needed. If cash generation does not support the profit turnaround, financial flexibility could remain limited.

Dependence on overseas IP and currency exposure

The toy business depends on licenses for foreign brands, so revenue can drop sharply when a contract ends, as the Mattel termination showed. For weak results in 2023, the company cited higher cost of sales from a strong dollar. Exchange rate moves and license terms can together weigh on profitability.

Overall view

Sonokongco is experiencing two changes at once: a return to a controlling-shareholder profit in 2025 and a sharp revenue increase in the first half of 2026. The revenue growth centers on used-car sales and rental rather than toys, reflecting a business restructuring as toy distribution has contracted.

On the other hand, operating losses ran for four consecutive years from 2022 through 2025 and for five consecutive quarters through the third quarter of 2026. The debt-to-equity ratio rose to 143.0% at the end of 2025 and the operating cash outflow widened.

Equity of KRW 49.4 billion at the end of 2025 supports financial stability, but because the last four quarters were net losses, earnings-based valuation comparisons are difficult.

The strengths are the revenue recovery and the narrowing of 2025 losses, and the weaknesses are continuing operating losses and cash outflows.

The next checkpoints are the preliminary fourth-quarter 2026 results and the 2026 annual report, with the central question being whether the auto business can reduce operating losses.
