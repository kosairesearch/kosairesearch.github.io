02

Business structure

CU Tech (376290) is a surface-mount technology (SMT) electronic components maker that listed on KOSDAQ on October 8, 2021. Its core products are FPCA (flexible printed circuit assembly) and RFPCA modules for smartphone displays.

In the first quarter of 2026, product sales of KRW 41.9 billion made up 96.6% of KRW 43.3 billion in total consolidated sales, with processing and other revenue making up the rest.

Product sales were split into smartphone displays at KRW 19.0 billion (44.0%), notebook and tablet displays at KRW 14.6 billion (33.7%), automotive displays at KRW 6.7 billion (15.4%), and electrical and electronic products at KRW 1.6 billion (3.7%).

Smartphone display parts remain the largest category, while automotive displays have become the third-largest, a sign of a more diversified product mix. The company attributed its 2025 decline to lower global smartphone demand, which cut sales and profitability in its smartphone SMT business.

SMT lines require costly equipment such as SPI, mounters, AOI and X-ray inspection tools, along with materials and process control know-how.

Listed peers in the same electronic components sector include LG Innotek, Samsung Electro-Mechanics and Korea Circuit, but product mix and revenue scale differ widely, so direct comparison is limited. Restar Corporation is the largest shareholder, and in February 2026 the company decided to lend money to it.

04

Earnings analysis

Annual sales fell from KRW 216.4 billion in 2022 to KRW 172.9 billion in 2023 (down 20.1% year on year), then rose to KRW 284.2 billion in 2024 (up 64.3%), the largest year of 2022 to 2025, before dropping to KRW 216.5 billion in 2025 (down 23.8%).

Operating profit peaked at KRW 11.5 billion in 2024 (up 429.3%) and bottomed at KRW 2.17 billion in 2023 (down 52.8%). The 2025 operating profit of KRW 5.42 billion (down 52.8%) was less than half the 2024 level. Operating margin was 2.1% in 2022, 1.3% in 2023, 4.0% in 2024 and 2.5% in 2025, with 2024 the highest.

Profit attributable to controlling shareholders was KRW 3.82 billion, KRW 919 million, KRW 13.6 billion and KRW 1.36 billion for 2022 to 2025, and net profit including non-controlling interests was reported at the same levels for each year.

Operating cash flow was KRW 8.81 billion in 2022 and KRW 2.98 billion in 2023, turned to a net outflow of KRW 5.22 billion in 2024, and recovered to KRW 9.67 billion in 2025. The company attributed the 2025 decline to lower smartphone demand, which reduced sales and profitability in its smartphone SMT business.

On a quarterly basis, third-quarter 2025 sales were KRW 48.4 billion with operating profit of KRW 2.09 billion. Fourth-quarter 2025 sales rose to KRW 66.7 billion (up 37.7% from the prior quarter) and operating profit to KRW 2.78 billion (up 33.2%).

First-quarter 2026 sales fell to KRW 43.4 billion (down 34.9%) with operating profit of KRW 1.72 billion (down 38.0%), marking the low point, and sales rose in the second and third quarters of 2026.

Third-quarter 2026 operating profit, however, dropped to KRW 934 million (down 49.5% from the prior quarter and 55.3% year on year), leaving operating margin below 2%, and the company swung to a net loss attributable to controlling shareholders of KRW 5.66 billion.

First-quarter 2026 controlling-shareholder net profit of KRW 4.04 billion exceeded operating profit of KRW 1.72 billion, which suggests non-operating items had a large effect on the gap between the two measures.

Controlling-shareholder net profit over the last four quarters, from the fourth quarter of 2025 to the third quarter of 2026, totaled KRW 2.28 billion.

05

Industry analysis

CU Tech's core end market is smartphone display parts, so swings in smartphone set demand flow directly into its sales. The company cited lower global smartphone demand as the cause of the 2025 decline, which makes a recovery in end-market demand the largest variable for revenue.

Sales rose in the second and third quarters of 2026, but operating profit fell over the same period, so the revenue recovery has not yet translated into higher profitability. Automotive displays made up 15.4% of first-quarter 2026 sales, while notebook and tablet displays accounted for 33.7%.

Automotive parts typically go through qualification and long supply agreements before mass production, so new entry takes time. SMT lines depend on costly inspection and placement equipment and on materials and process control know-how, which keeps capital needs significant.

In the same electronic components sector, listed companies such as LG Innotek, Samsung Electro-Mechanics and Isupetasys operate with different product mixes and revenue scales, so metrics are hard to compare side by side.

Whether sales keep recovering after the first-quarter 2026 low will serve as the benchmark for the industry cycle.

Bull factors

Two consecutive quarters of growth after the first-quarter low are a fact bulls can point to if smartphone demand turns. How much of that revenue recovery reaches operating profit remains to be seen.

Product mix diversifying

Automotive display sales reached KRW 6.7 billion in the first quarter of 2026, or 15.4% of total sales by product. Building a position beyond smartphone parts spreads demand across more end markets over time.

Whether automotive revenue grows large enough to change the earnings structure is a question for annual disclosures.

Balance sheet capacity and shareholder returns

At the end of 2025 the debt-to-equity ratio was 12.7%, with total liabilities of KRW 14.5 billion and total equity of KRW 114.4 billion. Operating cash flow swung from a KRW 5.22 billion outflow in 2024 to KRW 9.67 billion in 2025.

Reliance on smartphone demand

Related-party lending to largest shareholder

The KRW 21.96 billion loan to the largest shareholder, decided in February 2026, is not small relative to total equity of KRW 114.4 billion at the end of 2025. Because it is a transaction with the largest shareholder at a 4.6% rate, it is a point to examine for cash flow and governance. Repayment and any change in terms will appear in later periodic disclosures.

Risk factors

Automotive product ramp-up

Expanding automotive displays is central to the company's product diversification. Automotive parts typically pass through qualification and long supply agreements before mass production, so delays in new customer approvals could push back the diversification effect.

In that case, the smartphone-centered structure would persist for longer and the improvement in profitability could also be delayed.

Equipment investment burden

SMT lines require costly equipment such as SPI, mounters, AOI and X-ray inspection tools. Meeting new product volumes, including automotive, may require adding such equipment, and the cost would hit earnings first through depreciation and funding needs.

If investment runs ahead of the pace of sales recovery, fixed-cost pressure from equipment spending could increase.

Working capital in sales recovery

Sales rose in the second and third quarters of 2026, yet operating cash flow turned to a net outflow of KRW 5.22 billion in 2024, when sales peaked. Higher sales can grow working capital such as receivables and inventory, so cash generation may lag profit.

Whether operating cash flow, which recovered to KRW 9.67 billion in 2025, holds up will depend on working capital movements.

Overall view

CU Tech's sales peaked in 2024 at KRW 284.2 billion, with operating profit of KRW 11.5 billion, then fell to KRW 216.5 billion in 2025. After a first-quarter 2026 low, third-quarter sales rose to KRW 57.5 billion.

Third-quarter operating profit, however, fell to KRW 934 million, the company swung to a net loss of KRW 5.66 billion attributable to controlling shareholders, and controlling-shareholder net profit over the last four quarters totaled KRW 2.28 billion.

The weaknesses are a 44.0% share from smartphone displays, dependence on smartphone demand, and a KRW 21.96 billion related-party loan to the largest shareholder.

Automotive displays at 15.4% signal diversification, but whether they become large enough to change the earnings structure is a question for full-year results.
