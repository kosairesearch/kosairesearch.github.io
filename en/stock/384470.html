Overseas Growth and Recurring Revenue Narrow Losses

02

Business structure

Coreline Soft is a medical AI software company that analyzes chest CT images, and its flagship product is the lung cancer screening solution in the AVIEW LCS family.

The company promotes a One CT, Multi-Disease strategy that analyzes lung nodules, emphysema and COPD, coronary artery calcification (CAC), and interstitial lung abnormalities from a single chest CT scan.

It has taken part in Korea's national lung cancer screening since 2019, and the company says it has secured lung cancer screening contracts for nine consecutive years.

Cumulative revenue for January to September 2026 of KRW 5.22 billion split into KRW 2.4 billion domestic and KRW 2.8 billion overseas, and the overseas share rose from 38% in 2024 and 40% in 2025 to 53.7%. Germany is the center of overseas business.

Low-dose CT lung cancer screening for high-risk groups has been carried out within the statutory health insurance system there since April 2026. As of November 2026, the company said it had contracted with 142 of roughly 310 expected participating facilities and that 50 of them were operating the software.

Revenue comes from implementation sales recognized at installation and recurring sales from subscription and pay-per-use models. In January to September 2026, implementation sales were about KRW 850 million and software recurring revenue was KRW 2.32 billion.

Distribution extends beyond direct hospital sales to B2B deals with global medical device companies, AI platforms, and national or multi-institution projects.

In Japan, the company received PMDA approval in September 2026 and in October 2026 signed a supply and linkage agreement for AVIEW CAC with teleradiology company Doctor Net.

In Korea, it was selected as a solution supplier for 9 of 17 regional responsibility hospitals in a Ministry of Health and Welfare AI-based clinical system support program.

In competition, lung nodule detection overlaps with players such as Infervision, while in risk assessment the company works with US-based Optellum to connect functions. The company says it holds 12 FDA 510(k) clearances and more than 20 US patents.

04

Earnings analysis

Annual revenue under the disclosed figures was KRW 4.09 billion in 2022, KRW 4.1 billion in 2023 (up 0.2% year on year), KRW 3.93 billion in 2024 (down 4.2%), and KRW 4.28 billion in 2025 (up 9.0%). Among 2022 to 2025, 2025 was the largest year and 2024 was the smallest.

Operating loss widened from KRW 10.2 billion in 2022 to KRW 11.7 billion in 2023 and KRW 13.8 billion in 2024, then narrowed to KRW 13.4 billion in 2025. The operating margin improved from -248.4% in 2022, -284.3% in 2023 and -350.7% in 2024 to -313.9% in 2025.

Net loss attributable to controlling shareholders was KRW 10.2 billion in 2022, KRW 14.1 billion in 2023, KRW 14.1 billion in 2024 and KRW 14.5 billion in 2025, and the company recorded a loss in each of those four years from 2022 to 2025.

Net loss including non-controlling interests was KRW 10.2 billion, KRW 14.1 billion, KRW 14.1 billion and KRW 14.5 billion over the same years.

Operating cash flow was negative KRW 8.78 billion in 2022, negative KRW 10.3 billion in 2023, negative KRW 10.8 billion in 2024 and negative KRW 11.6 billion in 2025, so the outflow grew each year.

The debt-to-equity ratio was 157.5% in 2022, 35.5% in 2023, 573.2% in 2024 and 124.5% in 2025, with large swings across years. At year-end 2025, total equity was KRW 18.2 billion, all of it attributable to controlling shareholders, and total liabilities were KRW 22.6 billion.

Quarterly revenue was KRW 975 million in Q3 2025, KRW 1.33 billion in Q4 2025 (up 36.2% from the prior quarter), KRW 1.3 billion in Q1 2026 (down 2.1%), KRW 1.59 billion in Q2 2026 (up 22.0%) and KRW 2.33 billion in Q3 2026 (up 46.9%).

Operating loss was KRW 3.59 billion in Q3 2025, KRW 2.93 billion in Q4 2025, KRW 3.1 billion in Q1 2026, KRW 2.37 billion in Q2 2026 and KRW 2.22 billion in Q3 2026, which is five consecutive quarters of losses since Q3 2025.

Net loss attributable to controlling shareholders was KRW 3.7 billion, KRW 3.25 billion, KRW 3.22 billion, KRW 3.72 billion and KRW 2.44 billion across the same five quarters, and the four quarters from Q4 2025 to Q3 2026 total KRW 12.6 billion.

The company said cumulative operating loss for January to September 2026 of KRW 7.69 billion was down 26.9% year on year, and that about 80% of the roughly KRW 2.82 billion improvement came from higher revenue rather than cost cuts.

Cumulative revenue for the first nine months still includes about KRW 850 million of implementation sales, which differ in nature from recurring revenue.

05

Industry analysis

Lung cancer screening with low-dose CT is expanding as a national program. The company argues that even after clinical evidence is established, lung cancer screening takes a long time to settle into national health systems, and that the global rollout is only now beginning.

In Germany, screening within the statutory health insurance system began in April 2026, and participating medical institutions have started preparing in earnest.

Screening requires first and second reads, follow-up, comparison with prior images and quality control, which raises the importance of workflow tools that link hospitals and support continuous operation over stand-alone reading software.

The center of gravity in medical imaging AI is also shifting from individual algorithm performance toward connection with existing PACS and RIS systems and modular workflows.

In the United States, a new HCPCS code, G0680, was created in 2026 for billing of coronary artery calcification analysis, so added risk analysis from chest CT has begun to enter a separate billing framework.

On the competitive side, Infervision and others operate in lung nodule detection, while Optellum is active in risk assessment. Coreline Soft has focused on the detection stage and, through its work with Optellum, chosen to connect risk assessment and follow-up management as well.

The company cites its experience applying AI in real clinical workflows in two different settings, Korea's national lung cancer screening and Germany's statutory health insurance-based screening, as a strength.

06

Outlook

The following facts are confirmable from company statements.

About half of the German contracts secured in the first half of 2026 have been reflected in revenue, and the company said the rest will be recognized sequentially from the second half of 2026 onward, depending on system build-out, integration, and the start of screening and usage.

In May 2026 the company was selected for a 2026 first-round multi-ministry advanced medical device R&D program, receiving support of KRW 2.2 billion over three years.

The project targets an automated lung nodule detection solution for cardiac CT, called AVIEW IPN, with the aim of obtaining FDA 510(k) clearance through an overseas clinical study.

The company also presented an AI-based lung disease analysis workflow platform at the World Conference on Lung Cancer (WCLC 2026) in September 2026. In the United States, it renewed a subscription contract with 3DR Labs for a fourth year in June 2026 and said AVIEW annual usage at that customer rose 60%.

In August 2026 it said it was pursuing supply of its AVIEW Lung Nodule CAD software through Optellum's LungOS platform. In Japan, the key checkpoint is whether the Doctor Net AVIEW CAC agreement translates into more analyzed cases and more participating institutions.

The company also said it will expand into imaging biomarkers and quantitative analysis for drug trials. However, the timing of US clearance and the pace of German contract go-live have not been disclosed in specific terms, so they remain plans until they appear in reported results.

08

Bull factors

Overseas-led growth

Cumulative overseas revenue for January to September 2026 was KRW 2.8 billion, up 152.3% year on year and 53.7% of total revenue. Of the KRW 2.26 billion total revenue increase, KRW 1.69 billion came from overseas. The company said new German customer contracts rose from 10 in 2025 to a cumulative 77 by July 2026.

Rising recurring revenue share

Software recurring revenue rose 79.0% year on year to KRW 2.32 billion in the first nine months of 2026, and its share of software sales rose from 45.0% to 55.0%. Subscription and pay-per-use revenue builds as medical institutions operate the software and examination volumes accumulate. The company said standalone recurring revenue in Q3 2026 was KRW 950 million, up 99% year on year.

Revenue-driven loss narrowing

Q3 2026 operating loss of KRW 2.22 billion narrowed from KRW 3.59 billion in Q3 2025, and the company said this was its lowest quarterly level since listing. According to the company, about 80% of the profit improvement came from higher revenue rather than cost reduction. Management said it expects operating leverage to grow as business scale expands.

09

Bear factors

Cumulative losses and cash outflows

Net loss attributable to controlling shareholders has run for four consecutive years from 2022 to 2025, and the 2025 figure of KRW 14.5 billion was the largest in that period. Operating cash outflow widened from negative KRW 8.78 billion in 2022 to negative KRW 11.6 billion in 2025.

Despite improving earnings trends, bears point out that the company is still some distance from generating cash.

Contract-to-revenue lag

The company said only about half of the contracts it secured in the first half of 2026 have been reflected in revenue. The remainder depends on system build-out, integration, and the timing of screening start and usage.

Usage-based contracts grow revenue only as examination volumes increase, so a rise in contract count does not translate directly into revenue.

Overseas concentration and quarterly volatility

Quarterly revenue fell 2.1% to KRW 1.3 billion in Q1 2026 from the prior quarter, and the quarterly series does not yet show a long run of steady growth. With the overseas share at 53.7%, results are more exposed to screening policy and contract go-live pace in specific countries such as Germany. Policy changes in individual countries could increase earnings volatility.

10

Risk factors

Policy and reimbursement

German lung cancer screening runs within the statutory health insurance system, so changes to coverage or screening criteria could affect contract go-live and usage.

In Korea, AI-based lung nodule detection and lung cancer prediction technology has been designated as an evaluation-pending new medical technology, so the timing of formal reimbursement inclusion is not set. Regulatory differences across countries are a key variable for the pace of overseas expansion.

Shifting platform competition

Medical imaging AI competition is moving from algorithm performance toward integration with existing PACS and RIS systems and modular workflow offerings. If partnerships with platform companies lag, detection-stage technology could be displaced in hospital purchasing choices.

The division of roles between lung nodule AI vendors such as Infervision and risk-assessment vendors such as Optellum is also a variable.

Financial capacity

Total liabilities of KRW 22.6 billion at year-end 2025 exceed total equity of KRW 18.2 billion, and operating cash outflows continue. Net loss attributable to controlling shareholders for the four quarters from Q4 2025 to Q3 2026 totals KRW 12.6 billion.

If the narrowing of losses does not continue or funding conditions change, the pace of overseas build-out and clinical investment could be affected.

12

Overall view

Coreline Soft recorded KRW 2.33 billion in Q3 2026 revenue, the highest quarterly figure in the disclosed series, and overseas sales and recurring revenue grew together, which shows the business mix shifting.

Operating loss narrowed from KRW 3.59 billion in Q3 2025 to KRW 2.22 billion in Q3 2026, and the company said most of the improvement came from higher revenue.

However, net loss attributable to controlling shareholders has continued for four consecutive years from 2022 to 2025, and total liabilities exceed total equity at year-end 2025.

The operating cash outflow has also widened every year since 2022, so it has not yet been confirmed that the earnings improvement is turning into cash generation. Only about half of the German contracts have been reflected in revenue, so contract go-live and rising usage are the key variables for upcoming results.

On valuation, an earnings-based multiple cannot be calculated and price-to-book is well above 1x, so how the market reflects business improvement is the central interpretive question.

Strengths are overseas growth and the recurring revenue structure, while weaknesses are cumulative losses, cash outflows, and the lag between contracts and revenue. The next checkpoint is the full-year operating margin and operating cash flow in the 2026 annual report.
