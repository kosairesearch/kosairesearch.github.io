02

Business structure

HD Construction Equipment launched on January 1, 2026 as a combined company after absorbing HD Hyundai Infracore through a merger.

The combined company has domestic production bases in Ulsan, Incheon and Gunsan, plus overseas bases in India, China, Brazil and Norway, and its parent HD Hyundai has described it as a comprehensive construction equipment group with annual revenue of about KRW 8 trillion.

Its main products are excavators, wheel loaders, articulated dump trucks (ADT) and compact equipment, with excavators sold under the Hyundai and DEVELON brands. Engines are also a core business, covering an industrial engine lineup, hydrogen engines and high-performance battery packs.

A Samsung Securities report from April 2026 said the construction equipment segment accounts for 84% of group revenue and named the engine business and the parts and service (AM/PS) business as growth drivers.

By region, demand from resource-rich countries in the Middle East, Africa and South America and a high share of emerging markets are defining features. North America is the market where the company has stressed share gains, and it is a battleground among global construction equipment brands.

As of October 2, 2026, the largest shareholder group held 37.49%. Segment-level revenue can be checked in the regular business report.

04

Earnings analysis

Annual revenue rose from KRW 3,515.6 billion in 2022 to KRW 3,825.0 billion in 2023 (up 8.8% year on year), fell to KRW 3,438.1 billion in 2024 (down 10.1% year on year), and then recovered to KRW 3,776.5 billion in 2025 (up 9.8% year on year). Over 2022-2025, the largest year was 2023 and the smallest was 2024.

Operating profit peaked at KRW 257.2 billion in 2023 (up 50.8% year on year) from KRW 170.6 billion in 2022, then fell to KRW 190.4 billion in 2024 (down 26.0% year on year) and KRW 170.9 billion in 2025 (down 10.3% year on year), declining for two consecutive years from 2024 to 2025.

The operating margin was 4.9% in 2022, 6.7% in 2023, 5.5% in 2024 and 4.5% in 2025; in 2025, revenue grew 9.8% yet operating profit fell 10.3%, so the margin declined.

Net income attributable to controlling shareholders was KRW 111.5 billion in 2022 and KRW 139.4 billion in 2023 (up 25.0% year on year), followed by KRW 100.0 billion in 2024 (down 28.3% year on year) and KRW 99.4 billion in 2025 (down 0.6% year on year).

Net income including non-controlling interests was KRW 87.0 billion in 2025, a figure on a different basis from net income attributable to controlling shareholders. Operating cash flow decreased from KRW 318.2 billion in 2023 to KRW 166.0 billion in 2025.

The debt ratio stayed low, moving from 106.8% in 2022 to 86.6% in 2025; at the end of 2025, total equity was KRW 1,930.3 billion (KRW 1,805.2 billion attributable to controlling shareholders) and total liabilities were KRW 1,672.4 billion.

On a quarterly basis, revenue of KRW 954.7 billion and operating profit of KRW 55.8 billion in Q3 2025 were followed by KRW 947.3 billion and KRW 33.4 billion in Q4 2025 (operating profit down 40.2% quarter on quarter), and then rose sharply in Q1 2026 to revenue of KRW 2,304.9 billion (up 143.3% quarter on quarter) and operating profit of KRW 190.7 billion.

Operating profit was KRW 248.9 billion in Q2 2026 and KRW 157.7 billion in Q3 2026 (down 36.6% quarter on quarter). Net income attributable to controlling shareholders plunged from KRW 173.9 billion in Q1 2026 and KRW 180.4 billion in Q2 2026 to KRW 1.92 billion in Q3 2026 (down 98.9% quarter on quarter).

The sum of net income attributable to controlling shareholders over the most recent four quarters is KRW 378.6 billion.

05

Industry analysis

Demand from end markets is diverging by region. In its October 14, 2026 report, Daishin Securities said high interest rates and the continuing Middle East conflict linked to the US-Iran war would delay new construction equipment orders.

The same report said North American dealer inventory has been held at four to five months and that non-residential investment demand remains firm outside the Middle East and Africa.

In an April 28, 2026 report, Samsung Securities said global construction equipment demand rebounded from 2025 and that North American inventory, which had been heavier than at rivals, has returned to normal levels.

Firm raw material prices were cited as supporting demand from resource-rich countries in the Middle East, Africa and South America. In an April 2026 report, Kiwoom Securities said the Middle East accounts for 10% of construction equipment exports and expected reconstruction demand to grow once hostilities end.

A January 2026 press report cited expectations that AI data center expansion in the United States would lift construction demand.

On positioning, the company's high exposure to emerging markets is viewed as favorable for raw materials and currency, while the weaker Middle East and Africa demand and currency effects were the main reasons Daishin gave for expecting third-quarter results to fall short of consensus. Competition with Doosan Bobcat and other global brands in North America continues.

The company's president said the new models would be used to raise North American market share and advance toward global top-tier status. The engine lineup shown includes the DA11 electronic ultra-small 1.1-liter engine, the DX05 and DX08 engines for the new models, and the DX22 engine for ultra-large excavators.

In a July 10, 2026 report, Shinhan Investment Corp. said it expected quarterly seasonality to flatten after the merger and pointed to parts AM/PS and engine business growth as important factors.

In an October 14, 2026 report, Daishin Securities said the impact of the Middle East conflict would weigh on results through the end of 2026. The same report had expected third-quarter 2026 revenue of KRW 2.292 trillion and operating profit of KRW 165 billion, both below consensus.

Disclosed third-quarter revenue was KRW 2.09 trillion and operating profit KRW 157.7 billion. The next checkpoints are the fourth-quarter and full-year 2026 results, including whether earnings recover after the sharp drop in third-quarter profit attributable to owners.

Bear factors

Order delays from high rates and the Middle East conflict

In an October 14, 2026 report, Daishin Securities cut its target from KRW 200,000 to KRW 180,000, citing delayed new orders from high interest rates and the Middle East conflict linked to the US-Iran war.

The same report expected third-quarter results to fall short of consensus, mainly because of weaker Middle East and Africa demand and currency effects. The impact was expected to weigh on results through year-end.

Sharp third-quarter profit drop and reported pre-tax loss

Third-quarter 2026 net profit attributable to owners was KRW 1.92 billion, down 95.6% year on year, a large gap from operating profit of KRW 157.7 billion.

Press coverage of the provisional results released on October 26, 2026 reported a KRW 4 billion pre-tax loss, compared with a KRW 57.1 billion pre-tax profit a year earlier. The gap between operating and net profit is a bearish point that needs explaining.

A run of target price cuts

KB Securities cut its target by 26.5%, from KRW 245,000 to KRW 180,000, on June 30, 2026. Shinhan Investment Corp. lowered its target to KRW 190,000 on July 10, 2026. Daishin Securities cut its target again on October 14, 2026. All three kept their ratings unchanged, and the reductions were tied to order timing and Middle East risks.

10

Risk factors

Geopolitics and interest rates

If the Middle East conflict linked to the US-Iran war continues, demand from the Middle East and Africa may keep shrinking. Daishin Securities noted that a high-rate environment could delay construction investment and push back new orders.

Because the Middle East accounts for about 10% of construction equipment exports, a prolonged conflict is a downside factor for the earnings outlook.

Currency and raw material swings

Currency moves have at times helped profitability, but Daishin Securities cited currency effects as one of the main reasons for the third-quarter shortfall. Firm raw material prices support demand from resource-rich countries, but they can also raise costs.

A high share of emerging markets means quarterly results can swing with regional currencies and commodity prices.

Post-merger comparability and earnings volatility

Because the 2026 merger took effect on January 1, prior-year figures are based on the standalone pre-merger company, which limits growth comparisons.

Net profit attributable to owners moved from KRW 173.9 billion in the first quarter to KRW 180.4 billion in the second and KRW 1.92 billion in the third, showing large quarter-to-quarter swings. Whether the merger benefits persist and how volatile earnings remain are the key risks to monitor.

Overall view

HD Construction Equipment's merger on January 1, 2026 lifted quarterly revenue into the KRW 2 trillion range, and press reports said its operating margin rose into double digits in the second quarter of 2026.

Annual results declined for two consecutive years after the 2023 peak, and the 2025 operating margin fell to 4.5%. Third-quarter 2026 operating profit rose to KRW 157.7 billion, but net profit attributable to owners dropped to KRW 1.92 billion, leaving earnings quality as the central open issue.

North American inventory normalization, engine business growth and Middle East and Africa reconstruction demand are cited as positives. High interest rates, order delays tied to the Middle East conflict and currency effects are cited as negatives, and brokers cut their target prices between June and October 2026.

The next checkpoints are Middle East effects through late December 2026, fourth-quarter 2026 preliminary results, and the 2026 annual business report due by March 31, 2027.
