Report overview

Loss-making turnaround bets hinge on earnings recovery

02

Business structure

ISAAC Engineering is an industrial automation and smart factory specialist founded in 2006. It has supplied process automation and control systems mainly for semiconductor, steel and energy customers, and its core model combines OT engineering with IT to cover design, build, installation and maintenance.

Major customers reportedly include SK hynix and POSCO. In the second quarter of 2026, revenue was split into 'goods' at 54%, industrial automation solutions at 26%, digital factory solutions at 17% and extra-high-voltage predictive diagnosis solutions at 2%, according to a Butler data compilation.

The business runs along four lines. The first is industrial automation and a manufacturing AI platform, Ultivis AX, which was shown with integrated monitoring and AI agents at AW 2026 in March 2026.

The second is power infrastructure, including substation predictive diagnosis and maintenance through the affiliate ISAAC Greedian. The third is battery recycling through a joint venture founded in 2024, in which ISAAC holds a 40% stake, handling pretreatment of spent batteries.

The fourth is overseas AIoT platform sales through an Indonesian subsidiary set up in 2025. Listed IT and software peers include Samsung SDS, LG CNS and POSCO DX Company, while press coverage describes ISAAC as a mid-sized engineering company.

04

Earnings analysis

On the basis of the regulatory filings, annual revenue was KRW 59.7 billion in 2022, KRW 100.5 billion in 2023, KRW 68.5 billion in 2024 and KRW 79 billion in 2025, with 2023 as the largest year in the period. Revenue grew 68.4% in 2023, fell 31.9% in 2024 and recovered 15.4% in 2025. Profit followed a different path.

Operating profit fell from KRW 2.36 billion in 2022 to KRW 134 million in 2023, a 94.3% decline, then turned into an operating loss of KRW 4.54 billion in 2024. The 2025 operating loss widened to KRW 7.18 billion, making 2024 and 2025 two consecutive years of operating losses.

The operating margin slid from 4.0% in 2022 to -9.1% in 2025. FnGuide data also show that the cost of sales ratio rose after 2022, which points to cost pressure as one reason for the margin decline.

Profit attributable to controlling shareholders was KRW 2.07 billion in 2022, turned into a loss of KRW 65.82 million in 2023 and widened to a loss of KRW 8.62 billion in 2025, a three-year run of losses from 2023.

Net loss including non-controlling interests was KRW 8.91 billion in 2025, a different figure from the controlling-shareholder measure. At the end of 2025 the debt ratio stood at 124.1%, up from 67.0% in 2024, with total equity of KRW 34.6 billion and total liabilities of KRW 43 billion.

Operating cash flow swung from an inflow of KRW 8.17 billion in 2024 to an outflow of -KRW 3.72 billion in 2025.

Quarterly, operating profit of KRW 271 million in the third quarter of 2025 gave way to an operating loss of KRW 3.58 billion in the fourth quarter, then narrowed to KRW 2.52 billion and KRW 1.47 billion in the first and second quarters of 2026 before widening to KRW 2.67 billion in the third quarter.

Revenue was KRW 14.1 billion, KRW 18.4 billion and KRW 17.3 billion in the first three quarters of 2026.

Mid-2025 press coverage quoted the company saying that industrial automation weakened on delayed and reduced investment in semiconductors, steel and batteries, and that its industrial software and power predictive diagnosis businesses had not reached breakeven.

05

Industry analysis

Demand in the industrial automation segment is tied to capital spending cycles in semiconductors, steel and batteries. A July 2025 press report said reduced and delayed investment in these industries weakened the core segment, and that investment recovery began turning into orders from the second quarter of 2025.

The same report put cumulative orders at KRW 5.02 billion as of July 2025. Reported revenue since then has moved considerably, at KRW 14.1 billion, KRW 18.4 billion and KRW 17.3 billion in the first three quarters of 2026, so it is not yet clear that order recovery has translated into steadier sales.

In power infrastructure, a March 2026 press report pointed to rising strain on domestic grids from data centers, AI and renewable energy. An investment industry source said grid construction could become the largest infrastructure investment field over the next decade.

The company's AC/DC hybrid transmission technology earned a runner-up award in a Korea Electric Power Corporation contest, but it was submitted under an individual engineer's name, and the company said it has filed a patent and is pursuing commercialization.

In battery recycling, raw material access and sales channels are seen as key advantages, and under the joint structure the partner provides technology and sales networks while ISAAC handles equipment investment and production operations.

Compared with listed IT peers Samsung SDS, LG CNS and POSCO DX Company, the company is described in press coverage as a mid-sized automation engineering firm.

06

Outlook

The company has set 2026 as the turning point for an earnings turnaround, pairing expansion of industrial automation into manufacturing AI and power infrastructure with the scaling back of some business units to build a profit-focused structure, according to a Money Today/The Bell report.

A company official said the core business base would be largely in place during 2026 and that the company expects to enter a full-scale growth track from 2027. The recycling joint venture generated KRW 12 billion of revenue in 2025 and is targeting about KRW 20 billion in 2026.

The same report put the venture's first-quarter 2026 revenue at KRW 6.4 billion, and the company said it plans to complete equipment setup by October 2026 to raise yields. If the venture's results improve, they would flow into ISAAC's profit through equity-method income.

On the manufacturing AI side, Ultivis AX-based AI agents and integrated monitoring were shown at AW 2026 in March 2026. In power infrastructure, an affiliate announced an MOU with US-based Centurion on a data-center integrated power infrastructure project on March 25, 2026.

Because it is only an MOU, the timing of any revenue contribution will need confirmation in later filings. The AC/DC transmission technology is moving from patent filing toward productization and construction standardization.

A shareholder meeting on May 26, 2026 considered an articles amendment adding renewable energy and power sales businesses to the corporate purpose.

08

Bull factors

Commercial potential of AC/DC hybrid transmission

The AC/DC hybrid transmission technology, which earned a runner-up award in a Korea Electric Power Corporation contest, adds DC lines to existing transmission towers.

Press coverage highlighted that transmission capacity could rise without new sites, shortening design and permitting periods and lowering construction costs. The company said it has filed a patent and is preparing market entry into domestic transmission infrastructure and public demonstration projects.

Recycling JV revenue growth and equity-method income

The affiliate recycling JV generated KRW 12 billion of revenue in 2025 at its Jincheon site in Chungbuk, and its 2026 target is about KRW 20 billion. Press coverage reported KRW 6.4 billion of revenue in the first quarter of 2026 alone.

Normal operating margins for spent-battery pretreatment are reportedly around 10%, and analysts say better JV results could lift equity-method income.

Revenue recovery and order flow

Annual revenue rose 15.4% to KRW 79 billion in 2025, and second-quarter 2026 revenue increased 30.9% from the prior quarter. A July 2025 press report said orders picked up after the second quarter of 2025, with cumulative orders at KRW 50.2 billion. The company also said investment recovery in semiconductors and steel was feeding into orders.

09

Bear factors

Persistent operating and net losses

Operating losses have run for four consecutive quarters from the fourth quarter of 2025 through the third quarter of 2026, and the third-quarter 2026 operating loss widened to KRW 2.67 billion.

Losses attributable to controlling shareholders have continued for five straight quarters since the third quarter of 2025, totaling KRW 12.3 billion over the last four quarters. The operating margin fell from 4.0% in 2022 to -9.1% in 2025.

Rising debt and weaker cash flow

The debt ratio jumped to 124.1% at the end of 2025 from 67.0% in 2024. Total liabilities of KRW 43 billion exceed total equity of KRW 34.6 billion, of which KRW 34.4 billion is controlling-shareholder equity.

Operating cash flow moved from an inflow of KRW 8.17 billion in 2024 to an outflow of -KRW 3.72 billion in 2025.

Convertible bond dilution and major shareholder pledges

Convertible bonds of KRW 10 billion were disclosed in 2025, maturing on September 1, 2030, with a conversion window from September 1, 2026 to August 1, 2030. Shares issued on conversion would equal 15.32% of total shares.

As of February 2026, the largest shareholder and related parties together held 56.42%, and the filings show pledge contracts with securities firms covering part of those holdings.

10

Risk factors

Financial stability

The debt ratio stood at 124.1% at the end of 2025, with total liabilities of KRW 43 billion exceeding total equity of KRW 34.6 billion. Continued operating losses would erode equity further and raise the debt burden.

With operating cash flow turning to an outflow in 2025, how working capital is funded is the next point to watch.

Dilution and major shareholder pledges

Conversion of the bonds could issue shares equal to 15.32% of total shares, diluting existing holders. The largest shareholder disclosed pledge contracts covering part of its holdings in October 2025 and February 2026. Any disposal of pledged shares or new pledge agreements could affect free float and trading supply.

New-business execution and sector cyclicality

Industrial automation revenue is sensitive to capital spending in semiconductors, steel and batteries. Manufacturing AI, power infrastructure and recycling are still small in revenue or at MOU and target stage, so losses could persist if planned sales and profitability do not materialize.

Because recycling sits in a joint structure, its contribution through equity-method income may be limited in size and timing.

12

Overall view

ISAAC Engineering is building growth pillars in manufacturing AI, power infrastructure and battery recycling on top of its industrial automation base.

In the filed results, revenue peaked at KRW 100.5 billion in 2023 and recovered to KRW 79 billion in 2025, but operating losses ran for two consecutive years from 2024 to 2025 and for four consecutive quarters from the fourth quarter of 2025.

Third-quarter 2026 revenue fell 14.3% year on year, and losses attributable to controlling shareholders totaled KRW 12.3 billion over the last four quarters.

Supporting factors include the patent filing for AC/DC hybrid transmission technology, the revenue target and possible equity-method gains from the recycling joint venture, and the company's stated expectation of entering a growth track from 2027.

Constraining factors include a debt burden reflected in the 124.1% debt ratio, operating cash flow turning negative, dilution risk from KRW 10 billion of convertible bonds, and pledge contracts covering part of the largest shareholder's stake.

On valuation measures, the P/E cannot be calculated because of net losses and the price-to-book ratio is below 1x, so business results and book equity need to be read side by side.

The next checkpoints are the filing deadline for the 2026 annual report on March 31, 2027 and the first-quarter 2027 report deadline on May 15, 2027. Whether the operating margin moves out of loss territory in those filings will be the main gauge of how the new businesses are performing.
