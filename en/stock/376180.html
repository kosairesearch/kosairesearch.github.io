Core Water-Purifier Business Stalls, Food Waste Appliance Is the Swing Factor

02

Business structure

Founded in 2002, Picogram is a specialist manufacturer of water purifiers and purifier filters that listed on KOSDAQ in November 2021. Its business is split between OEM/ODM filter and finished-product supply to domestic and overseas purifier brands, and direct sales of its own brand, Pureal, direct-flow purifiers.

Domestic customers include Kyowon, SK Magic, Chungho Nais and Daelim, while overseas it maintains long-standing supply relationships with global brands such as Rainwater and Brondell. As of the second quarter of 2024, 52.5% of total revenue came from overseas, making exports more than half of the business.

The company produces its core carbon-block filter material in-house and holds its signature one-touch Plug-and-Play (PnP) self-replaceable filter technology.

More recently it has pursued food-waste appliances, branded Pureal CleanAs, built on condensing technology, as a new growth axis; the product converts odor and moisture generated during food-waste processing into water and discharges it internally.

The company has also set up a Bio, Beauty & Healthcare (BB&H) division, including the cosmeceutical brand CELLINstem, to diversify further.

The domestic water-purifier market is a crowded red ocean with more than a hundred competitors, and Picogram has carved out a niche with outright-purchase sales in a market otherwise dominated by rental models.

04

Earnings analysis

Consolidated revenue in 2025 came to KRW 30.87 billion, down from KRW 32.14 billion in 2024, and operating profit swung to a loss of KRW 704 million from a profit of KRW 2.84 billion in 2024. Net income attributable to owners also reversed from KRW 7.13 billion in 2024 to a loss of KRW 1.10 billion in 2025.

Operating margin deteriorated steadily, from 11.3% in 2022 to 5.5% in 2023, 8.8% in 2024, and -2.3% in 2025.

On a quarterly basis, after revenue of KRW 9.94 billion and operating profit of KRW 503 million in Q2 2025, the loss widened in the second half with Q3 revenue of KRW 6.28 billion and an operating loss of KRW 362 million, followed by Q4 revenue of KRW 6.18 billion and an operating loss of KRW 1.05 billion.

Q1 2026 revenue was KRW 6.60 billion (down year on year) with operating profit of KRW 32 million, essentially breakeven, before Q2 2026 showed a clear rebound with revenue of KRW 8.44 billion, operating profit of KRW 1.20 billion, and owners' net income of KRW 2.39 billion.

Still, the trailing four quarters (Q3 2025 through Q2 2026) combined show a small operating loss of roughly KRW 178 million, so whether the Q2 improvement becomes a sustained trend will require confirmation in subsequent quarters.

Net income has at times swung more than operating profit, suggesting non-operating items such as derivative valuation gains or losses tied to convertible bonds may have influenced the bottom line.

Operating cash flow in 2025 was KRW 2.02 billion, similar to KRW 2.02 billion in 2024, indicating cash generation held up relatively well despite the accounting loss.

05

Industry analysis

The domestic water-purifier market is mature and crowded with more than a hundred competitors; large appliance makers have defended share through rental-centric business models, while Picogram has maintained a niche strategy of outright-purchase sales and B2B supply.

The carbon-block filter material segment has historically been dominated by a small number of players, and Picogram has pursued in-house material production to secure cost competitiveness.

Overseas, the company exports filters and purifiers to more than 40 countries, having broadened its customer network across the United States, Europe and Asia.

In the newer food-waste appliance segment, the domestic market is estimated at roughly KRW 1 trillion, and in Japan demand has been rising amid tightening environmental regulation, including food recycling legislation.

That said, the broader home-appliance market tends to see longer replacement cycles for consumers during periods of high inflation and high interest rates, leaving companies with heavy domestic reliance more exposed to demand swings.

Multiple competitors, including large appliance makers, have already entered the food-waste appliance market, so how well Picogram's condensing-technology differentiation is received will be a key factor shaping the competitive landscape.

Outlook

The company has stated that it built a dedicated food-waste appliance production line with annual capacity of 60,000 units at its Bupyeong, Incheon plant in the second half of 2025, and began full-scale operation and sales from 2026.

In August 2025, a company official said the goal was to secure roughly KRW 50 billion in incremental revenue from the food-waste appliance business, a substantial addition relative to the company's historical annual revenue scale, making progress toward that target a key variable for future earnings.

Development of a compact food-waste appliance aimed at one- and two-person households has reportedly been completed, with additional product launches planned.

Reports indicate the company drew interest from North American buyers at the CES 2026 exhibition, suggesting continued overseas marketing activity to build out a global distribution network.

On the financial side, early redemptions of the convertible bonds issued in 2023 have continued through 2025 and 2026, and an August 2026 disclosure on further acquisition and retirement of the remaining balance suggests dilution concerns tied to the bonds have largely been addressed.

A key point to watch will be whether the contribution from food-waste appliance sales becomes clearly visible in the next regular disclosure, the Q3 2026 report.

Bull factors

Q2 Earnings Turnaround

Q2 2026 revenue of KRW 8.44 billion rose sharply from KRW 6.60 billion in the prior quarter, and operating profit jumped to KRW 1.20 billion, breaking a string of losses through the second half of 2025. Owners' net income also reached KRW 2.39 billion, the strongest among recent quarters.

The rebound followed weak quarters and its durability needs confirmation, but the magnitude of the single-quarter improvement is notable.

Food-Waste Appliance Business Launch

The Pureal CleanAs food-waste appliance, based on condensing technology, has a 60,000-unit annual production line and moved into full-scale production and sales from 2026. The company has set a target of securing roughly KRW 50 billion in incremental annual revenue from this business. Participation in global exhibitions such as CES has also broadened contact with overseas buyers.

Easing Financial Overhang

The debt ratio fell from 81.3% in 2024 to 49.5% in 2025, and the convertible bonds issued in 2023 were progressively redeemed early through 2025-2026, culminating in an August 2026 disclosure on retiring the remaining balance.

The reduction in potential dilution tied to the bonds can be viewed as a positive for the future stability of per-share metrics.

Bear factors

Widening Annual Earnings Volatility

2025 consolidated revenue fell to KRW 30.87 billion from the prior year, and operating profit swung to a loss of KRW 704 million from a profit of KRW 2.84 billion in 2024. Operating losses were also recorded in both Q3 and Q4 of 2025, widening the swing in results. Despite the Q2 2026 improvement, the trailing four-quarter cumulative operating result remains a modest loss.

Core Business Revenue Stagnation

Annual revenue has remained stuck in a roughly KRW 29.3–32.1 billion range for several years, from 2022 to 2025. Q1 2026 revenue of KRW 6.60 billion was disclosed as lower than the year-earlier period. With growth in the core purifier and filter business stalled, reliance on the new business is increasing.

Governance Change and Small-Cap Characteristics

In August 2026, the CEO gifted 3 million common shares to his three daughters, reducing his own stake from 42.78% to 26.62%, though the family's combined stake was maintained at around 56%.

The effect of this ownership restructuring on future management control or shareholder policy bears watching, and the small market capitalization also means liquidity and share price volatility can be elevated.

Risk factors

Demand and Industry Risk

The home-appliance market tends to see longer replacement cycles during periods of high inflation and high interest rates, exposing the company to demand slowdowns.

Expanding outright-purchase sales within a rental-dominated domestic purifier market may face limits, and the high share of overseas revenue also leaves the company sensitive to currency and overseas economic swings.

New Business Execution Risk

The food-waste appliance business has a stated target of roughly KRW 50 billion in incremental revenue, but actual sales performance and the timeline for achieving this have not yet been fully confirmed in disclosed quarterly results.

Expanding the product lineup and building overseas distribution may require sustained marketing spend, and there remains a possibility that the company's differentiation is not well received relative to competitors.

Financial and Governance Risk

The convertible bonds issued in 2023 saw repeated put-option exercises and early redemptions throughout 2025-2026 as the share price traded below the conversion price and refixing floor, a process that has continuously drawn down internal cash.

Governance changes, including the founder family's share gifting, are underway, and as a small-cap stock, trading volume and share price volatility can be relatively elevated.

Overall view

Picogram's core water-purifier and filter business has remained range-bound in revenue for several years, and full-year operating results swung to a loss in 2025 before showing a clear improvement in both revenue and operating profit in the second quarter of 2026.

The food-waste appliance business, pursued as a new growth driver, has entered full-scale production with a 60,000-unit annual line, and the company has set a target of roughly KRW 50 billion in incremental annual revenue, making its future contribution worth watching.

On the financial side, continued early redemption of the 2023 convertible bonds has lowered the debt ratio and eased potential dilution concerns, though this has also required sustained use of internal cash, which merits attention.

Ownership structure changed through the founder family's share gifting, though the family's combined stake was maintained.

The trailing four-quarter cumulative operating result remains a modest loss, so whether the Q2 improvement represents a temporary rebound or a trend reversal will need further confirmation through upcoming quarterly results.

For investment judgment, it appears important to weigh both the stagnation in the core business and the execution track record of the new business together.
