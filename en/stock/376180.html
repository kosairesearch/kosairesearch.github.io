Picogram: Filter Core Plus New Lines, With Profits Swinging Both Ways

02

Business structure

Picogram, founded in 2002 and listed on KOSDAQ in 2021, is a living-environment appliance maker whose roots lie in one-touch filter cartridges and water purifiers it builds directly. Beyond finished units, it manufactures carbon block water-filter materials and performs secondary processing on them.

Product sales made up most of first-half 2026 revenue, at roughly KRW 13.52 billion against about KRW 1.14 billion of merchandise sales. In early 2025 the company said about 60 percent of total sales were exports, of which roughly 30 percent came from North America.

In the US it produces under OEM and ODM contracts for pipe and fire-protection maker WATTS and for Brondell, a bidet and air purifier maker, and it sells large-capacity water purifiers directly on Amazon.

To meet tighter environmental rules it completed a condensing-type smart heating food waste disposer in 2024 and is marketing it under the Clean-us brand. Its cosmetics line centers on the B2B brand CELLINstem, with a push to strengthen distribution through agents in Vietnam.

Company disclosures cite 186 patents as of 2025. In 2024 it supplied heat-compressed carbon block automation equipment and technology to a Chinese manufacturer and has since been positioned to earn royalties on that partner's own product sales.

04

Earnings analysis

Annual revenue went from KRW 29.4 billion in 2022 to KRW 27.7 billion in 2023, down 5.7 percent year on year, before rising to KRW 32.1 billion in 2024, up 16.0 percent, which was the largest year of 2022 to 2025. Revenue then came to KRW 30.9 billion in 2025, down 4.0 percent.

The company attributed the 2023 revenue drop to lower overseas sales amid a global economic slowdown. Operating profit fell from KRW 3.33 billion in 2022 to KRW 1.53 billion in 2023, down 54.1 percent, as costs rose for new businesses including the CELLINstem cosmetics line.

It recovered to KRW 2.84 billion in 2024, up 85.9 percent, before turning to an operating loss of KRW 704 million in 2025. The operating margin read 11.3 percent, 5.5 percent, 8.8 percent and then -2.3 percent in 2025.

Controlling-shareholder net income rose from KRW 3.2 billion in 2022 to KRW 4.45 billion in 2023, up 39.0 percent, and to KRW 7.13 billion in 2024, up 60.0 percent, although non-operating items such as gains on valuation of convertible bond derivatives contributed to those gains.

In 2025 the controlling-shareholder result swung to a net loss of KRW 1.1 billion. The debt-to-equity ratio rose from 64.9 percent in 2022 to 92.3 percent in 2023, then fell to 49.5 percent in 2025, when total equity stood at KRW 39.9 billion and total liabilities at KRW 19.8 billion.

Operating cash flow declined from KRW 4.46 billion in 2022 to KRW 2.02 billion in 2025. Quarterly results show an operating loss of KRW 362 million in the third quarter of 2025 and KRW 1.05 billion in the fourth quarter, followed by a first-quarter 2026 operating profit of KRW 31.54 million.

The second quarter of 2026 delivered KRW 1.2 billion, up 3,714.2 percent from the prior quarter, and the third quarter returned to an operating loss of KRW 103 million, with a controlling-shareholder net loss of KRW 1.89 billion. Controlling-shareholder net income over the last four quarters totaled KRW 2.17 billion.

05

Industry analysis

Press reports describe South Korea's food waste disposer market as roughly KRW 1 trillion in size, and the company is entering it to broaden its home appliance sales.

Tighter environmental rules have raised certification requirements for household units, and the company said stricter Ministry of Environment certification added costs in 2025.

In filter materials, higher activated carbon prices have pushed filter makers that used granular carbon toward carbon block, a shift the company cited behind its first-half 2026 carbon block sales growth.

Carbon block is a core material in water filters, and Picogram emphasizes its heat-compressed production technology, covered by patents in Korea and the US. On the export side the US is a market with OEM and ODM demand, while China involves equipment supply and a technology royalty arrangement.

Demand for water purifiers and household appliances tends to track consumer sentiment, which remains sensitive to prices and interest rates in Korea. Picogram sits in the position of absorbing material-switching demand from OEM customers in filters.

Its food waste disposer business is still in an early entry phase, and its market share has not yet been verified through disclosed data.

06

Outlook

Press reports say the company plans to add equipment investment in 2026 to expand carbon block capacity in response to rising demand, while a September 2026 company profile describes that investment as under review, so the scale and timing remain open.

The next checkpoint for the investment is the equipment item in the 2026 annual business report. The cosmetics business is pushing to strengthen distribution through agents centered in Vietnam to raise the overseas revenue share and improve profitability.

In North America, the company co-founded Picogram USA, Inc. with a local US partner in May 2026 and holds a 50 percent stake, aiming to target North American brand markets and expand OEM and ODM work.

Management has said it intends to strengthen profitability in its existing water purifier and filter business while widening its portfolio into carbon block materials, North America, cosmetics and home appliances to build a mid- to long-term growth base.

Press coverage credits the Clean-us food waste disposer's marketing and premium positioning with helping improve profitability. In an early 2025 report, the company said it would receive royalties from a Chinese manufacturer's use of its technology starting in 2025.

08

Bull factors

First-half 2026 turned profitable

Operating profit of KRW 31.54 million in the first quarter of 2026 and KRW 1.2 billion in the second quarter added up to a first-half operating profit. Press reports put the operating margin moving from -3.8 percent to about 8.2 percent, with lower selling and administrative costs cited as the main driver. Compared with the loss-making 2025 period, the cost structure shows an improving direction.

Rising carbon block demand

A September 2026 company profile says higher activated carbon prices have led customers to switch from granular carbon to carbon block, expanding supply. The company plans equipment investment to raise capacity for that demand.

Because carbon block is a core filter material, it links directly to the existing filter business and could become a structural pillar.

Overseas OEM and royalty base

In early 2025 the company said about 60 percent of its sales were exports. It produces under OEM and ODM contracts for WATTS and Brondell in the US, and it added a structure in which a Chinese manufacturer pays technology royalties after supplying heat-compressed carbon block equipment. A broader overseas customer base could cushion slower domestic consumption.

09

Bear factors

2025 loss swing and third-quarter relapse

The company swung to an operating loss of KRW 704 million and a controlling-shareholder net loss of KRW 1.1 billion in 2025, which it attributed to higher selling costs, including advertising for new product launches and commission fees.

The third quarter of 2026 again showed an operating loss of KRW 103 million and a controlling-shareholder net loss of KRW 1.89 billion, so profitability has not held steadily quarter to quarter.

Revenue decline and quarterly pullback

Press reports put first-half 2026 revenue down 18.3 percent from a year earlier. Annual revenue in 2025 came to KRW 30.9 billion, down 4.0 percent.

Third-quarter 2026 revenue rose 10.4 percent from a year earlier, but it fell 17.8 percent from the prior quarter to KRW 6.94 billion, so the durability of the recovery is not yet proven.

New-business costs and a thin free float

Costs for new businesses such as the cosmetics line pulled down operating profit in 2023, and advertising-led selling costs rose again in 2025. Such spending weighs on profit until the new lines produce results.

With the largest shareholder and related parties holding a mid-50 percent stake as of the June 2025 change, the free float is limited, which can weigh on trading liquidity.

10

Risk factors

Cost of portfolio expansion

Pursuing food waste disposers, cosmetics and a North American subsidiary at the same time puts pressure on profit through advertising and startup costs. Both the 2023 operating profit decline and the 2025 rise in selling costs traced back to this pattern. If the new lines fail to convert into revenue, operating losses could recur.

Overseas demand and trade exposure

With about 60 percent of sales from exports, results are heavily exposed to overseas economic conditions and trade rules. In 2023 revenue fell when weaker global demand cut overseas sales. Order swings from US customers and currency moves could hit revenue and profit at the same time.

Input costs and capex burden

Higher activated carbon prices expand demand for carbon block but also raise input costs, cutting both ways for margins. The planned capacity expansion adds investment that could weigh on the balance sheet. The debt-to-equity ratio stood at 49.5 percent at the end of 2025, after exceeding 90 percent in 2023.

Overall view

Picogram swung to an annual loss in 2025 and then posted operating profit in the first two quarters of 2026, but the third quarter returned to an operating loss of KRW 103 million and a controlling-shareholder net loss of KRW 1.89 billion.

Revenue peaked at KRW 32.1 billion in 2024 and eased to KRW 30.9 billion in 2025, and press reports say first-half 2026 revenue fell from a year earlier. Bullish factors include carbon block demand driven by higher activated carbon prices, an export-heavy OEM and royalty base, and first-half 2026 operating profit.

Bearish factors include new-business costs, volatile quarterly profits, the 2025 loss and the third-quarter relapse, and a limited free float. Valuation needs to be read against a small earnings base of KRW 2.17 billion in controlling-shareholder net income over the last four quarters and a price-to-book ratio below 1.
