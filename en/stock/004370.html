Overseas growth meets cost pressure at Nongshim

02

Business structure

Nongshim sells mainly ramen, along with snacks and beverages, and its core brands include Shin Ramyun, Neoguri and Yukgaejang.

In the third quarter of 2026, on a standalone basis, noodle sales rose 3% as non-soup noodles such as Shin Ramyun Rose gained, while snack and beverage sales fell 4.1% and 2.6%, according to coverage of the November 13, 2026 disclosure.

On a consolidated basis, third-quarter external sales of the domestic entity were KRW 633.2 billion (up 4.6%) and those of overseas subsidiaries were KRW 296.4 billion (up 11.4%), giving an overseas share of 31.9%.

Among overseas units, the U.S. subsidiary grew sales 14.3% to KRW 139.9 billion as sales increased at major retail channels such as Costco and Walmart. The China unit rose 21.7% to KRW 53.6 billion after expanding its product range at snack specialty stores and membership warehouse clubs.

The European unit's sales fell 6.4% to KRW 22.2 billion because its UK business was switched to direct exports. For January to September 2026, the overseas share of consolidated sales reached 39.4%, up 2.6 percentage points.

In overseas markets, Samyang Foods has kept growing with its Buldak brand, which has intensified competition in K-ramen. Nongshim's chairman has also stated a goal of overtaking Japanese makers to become the number one ramen brand in the U.S. market. Domestically, the noodle portfolio partly offsets the declines in snacks and beverages.

04

Earnings analysis

Annual revenue rose from KRW 313.1 billion in 2022 to KRW 351.4 billion in 2025, with growth slowing from 9.0% in 2023 to 0.8% in 2024 and 2.2% in 2025.

Operating profit grew from KRW 112.2 billion in 2022 to KRW 212.1 billion in 2023, an 89.1% increase, then fell 23.1% to KRW 163.1 billion in 2024 and recovered 12.8% to KRW 183.9 billion in 2025. Across 2022 to 2025, 2023 was the largest year and 2022 the smallest for operating profit.

The operating margin was 6.2% in 2023, 4.7% in 2024 and 5.2% in 2025, so 2025 did not return to the 2023 level. Net profit attributable to controlling shareholders rose 48.0% to KRW 171.9 billion in 2023, fell 8.5% to KRW 157.3 billion in 2024 and rose 8.2% to KRW 170.1 billion in 2025.

Net profit including non-controlling interests was KRW 157.6 billion in 2024 and KRW 170.1 billion in 2025, and it is reported as a separate figure from profit attributable to controlling shareholders.

The debt-to-equity ratio edged up from 31.1% in 2022 to 35.0% in 2025, with total equity at KRW 2.83 trillion and total liabilities at KRW 990.9 billion at year-end 2025. Operating cash flow went from KRW 159.7 billion in 2022 to KRW 327.2 billion in 2023 and KRW 268.7 billion in 2025.

On a quarterly basis, operating profit fell from KRW 54.4 billion in the third quarter of 2025 to KRW 33.4 billion in the fourth quarter, then peaked at KRW 67.4 billion in the first quarter of 2026.

It then declined to KRW 59.3 billion in the second quarter and KRW 49.7 billion in the third quarter, two consecutive quarterly declines, while third-quarter revenue fell 2.8% from the prior quarter.

Coverage of the November 13, 2026 disclosure attributed the margin pressure to palm oil and other raw material prices, freight and energy costs, and selling and administrative expenses that rose from KRW 205.7 billion to KRW 223.5 billion.

Net profit was also hit by foreign-exchange-related items, which swung from a KRW 1.3 billion gain to a KRW 12.9 billion loss. Profit attributable to controlling shareholders over the latest four quarters totals KRW 176 billion.

05

Industry analysis

Competition in K-ramen exports has intensified as Samyang Foods continues to grow overseas with its Buldak brand.

Nongshim started operating its export-dedicated plant in Busan's Nongsan area, raising domestic export ramen capacity from 700 million to 1.2 billion units a year, and reported that global capacity including overseas plants will reach 6.2 billion units.

The company said artificial intelligence was applied across the production process, raising productivity by 17.6% versus the previous lines. The biggest industry headwind is raw materials and logistics costs.

Higher palm oil and other ingredient prices, freight and energy costs weighed on third-quarter profitability, and Nongshim attributed this to rising export-related costs. Greater currency volatility also led to foreign-exchange losses that weighed on net profit.

In the U.S., sales are expanding through large retailers such as Costco and Walmart, while in China, growth is being supported by wider distribution through snack specialty stores and membership warehouse clubs.

In cycle terms, the company is in an investment phase in which overseas capacity expansion and sales channel growth are advancing together while cost increases press on margins.

Against peers, Samyang Foods is cited as a leader in overseas brand growth, whereas Nongshim is responding with a noodle-centered domestic business and overseas capacity additions. In Europe, the switch to direct exports has reduced reported sales, leaving distribution structure changes as an open issue.

06

Outlook

Nongshim has set a target of KRW 5.4 trillion in global ramen sales and a 60% overseas sales share by 2030, as reported in November 2026. Given that the overseas share was 39.4% for January to September 2026, the target assumes a substantial rise in the overseas mix.

The Busan Nongsan export plant plans to export to more than 80 countries by next year, covering nearly every continent except the U.S. and China, where the company already has plants.

In Europe, after establishing a sales subsidiary, the company announced plans to expand Shin Ramyun sales through major retailers in the UK, Germany and France. Entry into South America, Africa and Oceania has also been presented as a plan.

In the U.S., Nongshim America appointed a new head and created a chief sales officer role, according to November 2026 reports.

In May 2026, the U.S. holding subsidiary set up the local content company NM STUDIOS, with an investment of about KRW 3.08 billion, and the company said the venture remains at the preparation stage without a specific plan.

The next checkpoint is how raw material and currency costs flow through to the operating margin after the fourth quarter of 2026.

08

Bull factors

Rising overseas sales mix

Third-quarter external sales of overseas subsidiaries rose 11.4% to KRW 296.4 billion, led by the U.S. and China units at 14.3% and 21.7% growth.

Per the quarterly report, U.S. operating profit for January to September rose 119.3% from a year earlier, which is cited as evidence that overseas operations are adding to profit.

Expanding overseas production capacity

With the Busan Nongsan export plant in operation, domestic export ramen capacity rose from 700 million to 1.2 billion units a year. Including overseas plants, global capacity reaches 6.2 billion units a year. The company has set a target of KRW 5.4 trillion in global ramen sales and a 60% overseas share by 2030.

Profit recovery and cash generation

Operating profit fell to KRW 163.1 billion in 2024 and then rose 12.8% to KRW 183.9 billion in 2025. Operating cash flow in 2025 was KRW 268.7 billion, and total equity at the end of 2025 was KRW 2.83 trillion. The debt-to-equity ratio edged up to 35.0% at the end of 2025.

09

Bear factors

Slower third-quarter profitability

Third-quarter operating profit fell 8.6% to KRW 49.7 billion, and the operating margin narrowed from 6.2% a year earlier to 5.3%, according to coverage of the November 13, 2026 disclosure. Operating profit has declined for two consecutive quarters since peaking at KRW 67.4 billion in the first quarter of 2026. Profit attributable to controlling shareholders fell 41.3% to KRW 29.7 billion in the third quarter.

Cost, currency and expense pressure

Palm oil and other raw material prices, freight and energy costs all rose. Foreign-exchange-related results swung to a KRW 12.9 billion loss in the third quarter, which pulled down net profit. Selling and administrative expenses rose to KRW 223.5 billion, and advertising costs increased to KRW 29.3 billion.

Weak domestic snacks, beverages and Europe

On a standalone basis, third-quarter snack sales fell 4.1% and beverage sales fell 2.6%. European unit sales fell 6.4% to KRW 22.2 billion, as UK sales moved to direct exports and dropped out of the unit's reported sales. Competition in overseas K-ramen has intensified, and Samyang Foods' growth is cited as a point of comparison.

10

Risk factors

Cost and currency

Palm oil and other raw materials, freight and energy costs all rose in the third quarter. Wider currency swings turned foreign-exchange-related results into a loss. How much of the cost increase can be passed on to prices will determine the pace of margin recovery.

Execution of overseas expansion

The Nongsan export plant and the expansion of overseas subsidiaries carry investment burdens, and the 2030 target assumes a large rise in the overseas share. The U.S. unit has seen management change, with its chief executive who took office in December 2024 leaving after 22 months. Whether capacity additions translate into sales growth remains to be confirmed.

Slowdown in the domestic portfolio

Domestic snack and beverage sales fell 4.1% and 2.6% in the third quarter. Noodle-led domestic growth offsets part of this, but noodle sales grew only 3%. A slowdown in domestic demand could weaken the cash generation needed to fund overseas expansion.

12

Overall view

Nongshim's revenue has grown to the mid-KRW 3 trillion range since 2022, while the operating margin declined from 6.2% in 2023 to 5.2% in 2025.

In the third quarter of 2026, revenue rose 6.7% on overseas subsidiary growth, but higher palm oil, freight and currency costs combined to reduce operating profit and profit attributable to controlling shareholders.

Two consecutive quarterly declines in operating profit since the first-quarter 2026 peak are the clearest current fact. On the other side, the overseas share rose to 39.4% for January to September 2026, and the Nongsan export plant and the U.S. and China units broaden the medium-term base.

With the price-to-book ratio below 1x, the valuation read depends on how fast overseas margins recover and how long cost pressure lasts. The next checkpoints are the full-year operating margin in the 2026 annual report, the overseas sales share in the first quarter of 2027, and progress toward the 2030 target.
