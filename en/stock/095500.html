02

Business structure

Mntechco develops and produces prism sheets and diffusion films for LCD backlight units, built on three-dimensional optical design, fine nano-patterning and polymer material technologies. TV optical films are the core business, and the company's profile describes it as the world's number one maker of TV optical films.

Beyond optical films it also handles noise filters, touchscreen films, retroreflective films and battery materials. The battery materials push began in February 2022, when it acquired a cathode additive maker that is now its battery-materials subsidiary.

As of 2023 press reports, the company ran cathode additive and lithium hydroxide processing operations. In the first quarter of 2023, battery materials made up 34% of revenue, displays 31%, and noise filters for white goods about 20%.

Because these shares are from 2023, the latest segment mix should be checked against the periodic filings. On ownership, the company is the largest shareholder of a listed affiliate with a 51.39% stake, and as of 2023 reports it held 85% of its battery-materials subsidiary.

Optical films are applied mainly in premium large-size TVs, so results track TV demand and panel utilization directly. In competition, it is compared with large listed electronic components companies, but it is presented as a leader in the specific niche of optical films.

Because displays and battery materials carry different weights, which segment is supporting revenue is the key to reading the business structure.

Earnings analysis

Annual results moved in different directions for sales and profit. Revenue rose to KRW 490.3 billion in 2022... Revenue peaked at KRW 694 billion in 2023, up 41.5% from the prior year, then fell 10.3% to KRW 622.3 billion in 2024 and 13.5% to KRW 538.5 billion in 2025, a two-year decline from 2024 through 2025.

Operating profit was KRW 24.9 billion in 2024, up 81.4%, the largest year in the 2022 to 2025 span, and dropped 15.8% to KRW 20.9 billion in 2025. The operating margin rose from 2.0% in both 2022 and 2023 to 4.0% in 2024 and 3.9% in 2025, holding near 4% even as sales shrank.

Net income attributable to controlling shareholders moved from a net loss of KRW 7.9 billion in 2022 to a net loss of KRW 1.21 billion in 2023, a smaller loss, then turned positive at KRW 22.1 billion in 2024 before falling 37.4% to KRW 13.8 billion in 2025.

Net income including non-controlling interests was KRW 13.6 billion in 2025, a different figure from the controlling-shareholder line. Press coverage of the 2025 results cited a slowdown in downstream industries that reduced affiliate earnings, along with lower financial income.

Operating cash flow swung from KRW -23.9 billion in 2022 to KRW 65 billion in 2023, KRW -3.39 billion in 2024 and KRW 65 billion in 2025. At the end of 2025 the debt ratio was 95.5%, with total equity of KRW 336.9 billion, of which KRW 299.3 billion belonged to controlling shareholders.

On a quarterly basis, operating profit of KRW 4.4 billion in the third quarter of 2025 turned into an operating loss of KRW 861 million in the fourth quarter, then recovered to KRW 8.46 billion in the first quarter of 2026, the peak of the last five quarters.

Operating profit then fell to KRW 2.6 billion in the second quarter, down 69.3% from the prior quarter, and to KRW 296 million in the third quarter, down 88.6% quarter on quarter and 93.3% year on year, a two-quarter decline.

Net income attributable to controlling shareholders went from KRW 9.81 billion in the first quarter of 2026 to a net loss of KRW 9.61 billion in the second quarter and KRW 12.5 billion in the third. In the first quarter, that net income exceeded operating profit, which suggests non-operating items were significant. The breakdown of those items is a point to check in the periodic filings.

05

Industry analysis

Optical films are components in LCD backlight units that spread light evenly and direct it, and they are used mainly in premium large-size TVs. A 2023 press report presented Mntechco as a leader in optical films for LCD TVs.

The same report said that as the LCD market weakened, the company chose battery materials as its next growth engine. Through the third quarter of 2025, average utilization of its optical film lines fell and cut sales, while gross profit improved and helped defend profitability.

The 2025 annual disclosure also cited a slowdown in downstream industries as the reason for lower affiliate earnings. The display-side cycle depends on TV demand and a recovery in panel utilization, so it is too early to pin down where it stands.

In battery materials, lithium hydroxide and cathode additives are the main products, and the company has said that a growing battery market is widening its opportunities. Battery materials are sensitive to raw material prices and customer demand, so their earnings drivers differ from those of optical films.

Within the electronic components sector, larger listed companies such as Samsung Electro-Mechanics and LG Innotek operate on a much bigger scale than Mntechco, which combines optical films with battery materials.

Competitive intensity in optical films depends on large-size TV panel demand and pricing, so utilization trends in that business are the key indicator.

Outlook

The starting point for the outlook is the trend in 2026 quarterly results.

Operating profit fell from KRW 8.46 billion in the first quarter to KRW 2.6 billion in the second and KRW 296 million in the third, while net income attributable to controlling shareholders moved from a KRW 9.81 billion profit in the first quarter to net losses of KRW 9.61 billion in the second and KRW 12.5 billion in the third.

The medium-term plan leans toward battery materials. A 2023 press report described a plan to expand annual lithium hydroxide capacity from 14,000 tons to 40,000 tons, with KRW 33 billion invested in the expansion.

At the same time, press coverage said the company was pursuing a factory construction project in Quebec, Canada, with completion initially targeted for 2025. A 2024 report said an affiliate planned to build precursor and nickel sulfate plants in Saemangeum, with total investment of KRW 800 billion over 2024 to 2028.

These are targets set at the time of reporting, so their current progress should be checked in capital expenditure and property, plant and equipment movements in the periodic filings. On the optical film side, the checkpoint is a recovery in TV demand and utilization.

Third-quarter 2026 sales of KRW 112.2 billion were down 11.5% from a year earlier. How much battery materials can support earnings will show up in results after the new capacity actually operates.

Risk factors

Downstream demand risk

If LCD TV demand and optical film utilization do not recover, the sales decline could extend. The 2025 results disclosure cited a slowdown in downstream industries. Sales were KRW 106.7 billion in the second quarter of 2026 and KRW 112.2 billion in the third.

Expansion and financial burden

Capital spending on battery materials adds to the financial burden. Press reports from 2023 and 2024 described a KRW 10 billion loan secured by shares of a listed affiliate. A disposal of 2,158,828 registered common shares for KRW 28 billion was disclosed on January 30, 2026.

Overhang from affiliate share issuance

A June 2026 report noted that newly issued shares from a rights offering at a listed affiliate were entering the market, raising supply concerns. Mntechco is the largest shareholder of that affiliate with a 51.39% stake. Share price volatility could increase while the ownership structure is being reshaped.
