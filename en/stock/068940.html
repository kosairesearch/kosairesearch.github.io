02

Business structure

Cellfie Global was founded in 1998 and listed on KOSDAQ in 2010 as a smart card manufacturer. It produces credit, debit and transit cards as well as ID cards, local currency cards and high-pass cards, and supplies domestic banks and card issuers.

The company holds certifications from VISA, Mastercard and JCB, which serve as prerequisites for overseas card issuance contracts. Its subsidiary ICK International Inc. conducts overseas sales to financial institutions in the Americas.

The company also secured proprietary card-related software, including COS and SCMS, to offer a total credit card solution. Based on the 2026 Q2 revenue mix compiled by a third-party data aggregator, products accounted for 99% of revenue and other items for 1%.

Because revenue depends mainly on card product sales, results are tied to card issuance volumes and the ordering timing of banks and card issuers. Competition is shaped by certification status, security software capability and delivery track record.

Market data sites list entry into PG (payment gateway) services as the reason for inclusion in an e-payment theme, making payment service expansion another variable. A single-sales supply contract was disclosed in September 2026, and the counterparty and amount should be checked in the original DART filing.

Earnings analysis

Within 2022 to 2025, 2022 was the largest year and 2024 the smallest.

The operating loss widened to KRW 3.43 billion in 2025, and the company has recorded losses for two consecutive years from 2024 to 2025.

Operating margin moved from 9.5% in 2022 to -9.7% in 2025. Net income attributable to controlling shareholders was KRW 9.96 billion in 2022 and KRW 235 million in 2023 (down 97.6%), then a net loss of KRW 1.83 billion in 2024 and a net loss of KRW 10.6 billion in 2025.

The company attributed the 2025 change mainly to unusual increases in selling, general and administrative and other expenses linked to legal and related costs. 05

Industry analysis

Cellfie Global makes smart cards, so its demand base and profit structure differ from large component makers in the same electronics sector classification. Demand comes from issuers such as banks, card companies and local governments, whose orders depend on card replacement cycles and new issuance plans.

The shift toward mobile and simple payments is a structural variable for physical card demand. Transit, local currency and high-pass cards, by contrast, carry public or quasi-public issuance demand that provides a base of volume.

The domestic smart card manufacturing market includes many small and mid-sized makers, so pricing, delivery timing and certification status are the competitive points. With 2025 revenue of KRW 35.5 billion, the company is more accurately compared with small card manufacturers than with large component makers.

Its overseas sales to financial institutions in the Americas, backed by VISA, Mastercard and JCB certifications, set it apart from its domestic-centered peers. The share and size of overseas revenue should be confirmed from the business segment information in DART periodic reports.
