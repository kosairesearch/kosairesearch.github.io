Long-term gas charters underpin earnings, but Q3 2026 swung to a loss

02

Business structure

KSS Line is a marine transport company whose core business is gas carriers that move LPG, ammonia and VCM. Based on Q3 2025, gas carriers accounted for 86% of sales, product carriers 7.9% and chemical carriers 6.2%.

For January through September 2026, one report put the gas segment at 84.3% of sales and 79.5% of operating profit, with gas vessel utilization at 96.32%. Within gas carriers, very large gas carriers (VLGCs) are the core asset, and a 2023 research report described the fleet of 15 VLGCs as sixth largest globally.

Earnings rely heavily on long-term time charters, with 88% of 2024 sales from gas carriers, mostly under contracts of two to five years. The customer base centers on refiners and petrochemical companies, including GS Caltex, S-Oil, SK Enmove, Methanex, Shell and BASF as named in a 2023 report.

Chemical carriers, which transport products such as BZ and SM, form another part of sales. In competition, HMM focuses on container ships and Pan Ocean on bulk carriers, which distinguishes KSS Line's gas-focused position.

KSS Line is reported to be the only domestic ammonia shipping company, which is cited as a differentiator. As of a July 2024 report, the fleet numbered 34 vessels.

04

Earnings analysis

Annual results show sales and operating profit growing together.

Sales went from KRW 445.4 billion in 2022 to KRW 472.6 billion in 2023 (up 6.1% year on year), KRW 517.9 billion in 2024 (up 9.6%) and KRW 561.4 billion in 2025 (up 8.4%), rising for three straight years from 2023, with 2025 the largest year in the 2022 to 2025 period.

Operating profit went from KRW 69.5 billion in 2022 to KRW 88.4 billion in 2023 (up 27.1%), KRW 104.1 billion in 2024 (up 17.8%) and KRW 110.3 billion in 2025 (up 6.0%), also rising for three straight years from 2023.

Operating margin was 15.6% in 2022, 18.7% in 2023, 20.1% in 2024 and 19.7% in 2025, rising after 2022 and easing slightly in 2025.

Profit attributable to controlling shareholders followed a different path: KRW 45.5 billion in 2022, KRW 17 billion in 2023 (down 62.5%), KRW 57.4 billion in 2024 (up 236.9%) and KRW 36.6 billion in 2025 (down 36.3%), with 2024 the largest year and 2023 the smallest.

Net profit including non-controlling interests was KRW 45.5 billion, KRW 17 billion, KRW 57.4 billion and KRW 36.6 billion for the same years. The gap between operating profit and net profit reflects financing costs, and one market data site put interest expense at roughly KRW 17.8 billion per quarter as of Q2 2026.

The balance sheet improved: the debt ratio fell from 308.4% in 2022 to 293.8%, 257.3% and 255.3% in 2025, with total liabilities of KRW 1.39 trillion and total equity of KRW 544.3 billion at end-2025.

Operating cash flow was KRW 112.6 billion in 2022, KRW 106.9 billion in 2023, KRW 119.3 billion in 2024 and KRW 154.7 billion in 2025. Quarterly results were volatile.

Sales were KRW 142.7 billion in Q3 2025 with operating profit of KRW 28.8 billion, then KRW 147.4 billion in Q4 2025 (up 3.3% quarter on quarter) with operating profit of KRW 29.7 billion.

Q1 2026 sales fell to KRW 139.8 billion (down 5.2% quarter on quarter), while profit attributable to controlling shareholders rose to KRW 63.5 billion, which the company attributed to a disposal gain from selling one VLGC and FX translation gains.

Q2 2026 sales were KRW 159.9 billion (up 14.4%) and operating profit KRW 32 billion (up 48.2%), but controlling-shareholder profit fell to KRW 17 billion.

Q3 2026 sales were KRW 151.6 billion (up 6.2% year on year), operating profit KRW 26.7 billion (down 7.1%), and the company swung to a controlling-shareholder net loss of KRW 10.8 billion.

Controlling-shareholder profit over the last four quarters totals KRW 83.3 billion, and the Q1 2026 figure makes up a large share of that total, so the four-quarter sum should not be read as recurring operating earnings.

05

Industry analysis

Demand for the company's core cargo depends on seaborne shipments of LPG and ammonia. A brokerage report cited in press coverage expects VLGC supply to rise 8% year on year in 2026.

That supply growth can be read as a pressure point on freight rates and fleet profitability, so the cycle position is hard to call and freight levels and new orders are the points to watch.

On ammonia, KSS Line is described as the only domestic ammonia shipper, and one analysis expects vessel demand to rise after 2027 as the ammonia market develops. Among listed domestic shipping companies, HMM focuses on containers and Pan Ocean on bulk, so direct comparison narrows to gas carrier operators.

Korea Line and Heung-A Shipping also have different cargo mixes. KSS Line's strengths are a structure that absorbs part of freight volatility through long-term charters and high fleet utilization.

For January through September 2026, gas vessel utilization was 96.32%, product carriers 99.19% and chemical carriers 98.90%. High utilization does not fully offset weaker freight rates, so utilization and freight should be read together.

06

Outlook

The most concrete contract fact is the extension of VLGC charters. According to a brokerage report cited in press coverage, the renewed contracts total KRW 826.4 billion, or about KRW 201.3 billion a year, equal to 36% of 2025 sales.

One VLGC contract previously due to end in 2026 and six due in 2028 were reported as extended by two to five years.

On January 29, 2026, the company disclosed a long-term time charter with B International Shipping & Logistics DMCC worth KRW 80.9 billion, equal to 15.63% of recent sales, with an annualized value of about KRW 27 billion.

The contract period is listed as starting April 10, 2028, so the revenue contribution begins after that date. Ammonia transport is the main new business, and one analysis expects vessel demand to rise after 2027 as the market develops, though new vessel deliveries or long-term ammonia contracts remain items to confirm.

On the financing side, vessel acquisitions have raised borrowings, and total liabilities of KRW 1.39 trillion at end-2025 will shape financing costs. The next annual report will show whether financing costs and profit attributable to controlling shareholders have stabilized.

Bull factors

Earnings visibility from long-term charters

Renewed charters for seven VLGCs disclosed in early 2026 total KRW 826.4 billion, equal to 36% of 2025 sales on an annualized basis. A KRW 80.9 billion long-term charter signed in January 2026 is set to start in April 2028. Most gas carrier sales sit under two- to five-year contracts, which partly cushions freight swings.

Three years of rising operating profit and margin

Operating profit rose for three straight years from 2023 to reach KRW 110.3 billion in 2025. Operating margin improved from 15.6% in 2022 to 19.7% in 2025. Q2 2026 operating profit of KRW 32 billion rose 48.2% quarter on quarter.

Balance sheet repair and cash generation

The debt ratio fell from 308.4% in 2022 to 255.3% in 2025. Operating cash flow rose to KRW 154.7 billion in 2025 from KRW 112.6 billion in 2022. Ammonia transport is cited as a new growth area, with demand expected to rise after 2027 according to one analysis.

Bear factors

Q3 net loss and non-operating cost burden

Q3 2026 operating profit was KRW 26.7 billion, yet the company posted a KRW 10.8 billion net loss attributable to controlling shareholders. The gap comes from non-operating items, including financing costs. Interest expense was reported at roughly KRW 17.8 billion per quarter as of Q2 2026.

Reliance on one-off gains and quarterly swings

Profit attributable to controlling shareholders in Q1 2026 of KRW 63.5 billion included a vessel disposal gain and FX translation gains. That quarter makes up a large share of the KRW 83.3 billion four-quarter total, so the total alone does not show earnings power. Controlling-shareholder profit fell 36.3% in 2025 to KRW 36.6 billion.

VLGC supply growth and freight pressure

Added supply could put pressure on freight rates and fleet profitability. Gas carriers make up a high share of sales, so freight changes have a large effect.

10

Risk factors

New business execution risk

Ammonia transport is cited as a growth area, but whether KSS Line will add new vessels or sign long-term ammonia contracts has not yet been confirmed. Even if demand is expected to rise after 2027, failing to convert it into transport contracts would delay the new business's contribution to earnings.

Vessel delivery timing or contract terms below expectations could narrow the contribution from this growth axis.

Customer concentration and renewal risk

A large share of sales is tied to long-term time charters with refiners and petrochemical companies, so demand changes at a single customer could reach earnings quickly. Major customers named include GS Caltex, S-Oil, SK Enmove, Methanex, Shell and BASF.

When the extended VLGC charters approach their end dates, renewal decisions and terms will determine future earnings. Reduced activity or contract adjustments at key customers could increase earnings volatility.

Contract start timing risk

The long-term time charter with B International Shipping & Logistics DMCC is listed to start on April 10, 2028, so its revenue contribution will appear only after that date. The KRW 80.9 billion contract equals 15.63% of recent sales.

If the start is delayed or contract terms change during performance, the revenue growth expected from this charter could be pushed back accordingly.

Overall view

KSS Line's sales and operating profit rose for three straight years from 2023 through 2025, supported by long-term gas carrier charters, while operating margin eased slightly to 19.7% in 2025 from 20.1% in 2024.

Profit attributable to controlling shareholders fell to KRW 36.6 billion in 2025 from KRW 57.4 billion in 2024, moving in a different direction from operating profit. In 2026, quarterly controlling-shareholder results swung from KRW 63.5 billion in Q1 to KRW 17 billion in Q2 and a KRW 10.8 billion net loss in Q3.

The Q1 figure included a vessel disposal gain and FX translation gains that are unlikely to recur. Operating profit stayed positive at KRW 26.7 billion in Q3, so core operating profitability and non-operating cost burden should be read separately.

Strengths include the long-term charter base and expected ammonia demand, while weaknesses include rising VLGC supply and financing costs. The debt ratio fell to 255.3%, but total liabilities of KRW 1.39 trillion keep the borrowing structure a key variable.

The next checkpoints are the Q4 2026 provisional results and the controlling-shareholder profit and non-operating items in the 2026 annual report.
