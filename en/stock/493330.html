02

Business structure

GFI is a fire-safety specialist for energy storage systems (ESS) founded in 2014, and it listed on KOSDAQ on January 14, 2026.

Its flagship is DI-KIT, a direct-injection suppression device for ESS battery packs, and it also supplies suppression sheets and thermal-runaway prevention pads built on its in-house microcapsule technology.

According to the company, DI-KIT detects gas from a battery module within one to three seconds and sprays extinguishing agent directly onto the ignited cell or single module. The same system also applies to battery-based power infrastructure such as uninterruptible power supplies, not just ESS.

Within the segments, DI-KIT sales in the first nine months of 2025 reached KRW 10 billion, about 2.7 times the year-earlier level, while fire-protection construction and design orders added KRW 7.2 billion.

The customer base is anchored by Samsung SDI, and a September 1, 2026 disclosure covered DI-KIT supply to Samsung SDI's North American ESS site, which press reports say runs through 2027. Its UL9540A safety certification and verified track record for overseas sales are cited as its main entry barriers.

04

Earnings analysis

GFI's annual results show revenue growth and a swing in bottom-line profit moving in opposite directions. Revenue rose from KRW 24.2 billion in 2024 to KRW 45 billion in 2025, up 85.4% year on year, and operating profit increased from KRW 5.49 billion to KRW 6.75 billion, up 23.0%.

Profit attributable to owners, however, moved from KRW 5.35 billion in 2024 to a net loss of KRW 3.42 billion in 2025, and net income moved from KRW 5.21 billion to a net loss of KRW 3.58 billion. The operating margin fell from 22.7% to 15.0%, as revenue growth far outpaced operating profit growth.

Operating cash flow dropped from KRW 5.35 billion to KRW 3.25 billion. At the end of 2025, total equity stood at KRW 53 billion, all of it attributable to owners, and total liabilities at KRW 12.1 billion, lifting the debt-to-equity ratio from 16.5% to 22.8%.

Quarterly results began with the fourth quarter of 2025, when revenue was KRW 17.9 billion, operating profit KRW 3.3 billion, and profit attributable to owners a net loss of KRW 7.28 billion.

In the first quarter of 2026, revenue was KRW 19.5 billion, operating profit KRW 2.9 billion, and profit attributable to owners KRW 3.04 billion, a swing to profit from the prior quarter.

Second-quarter revenue fell 20.2% to KRW 15.5 billion, while operating profit edged up 1.8% to KRW 2.95 billion and profit attributable to owners was KRW 2.25 billion.

Third-quarter revenue rose 7.2% to KRW 16.7 billion, operating profit fell 5.6% to KRW 2.79 billion, and profit attributable to owners was KRW 2.25 billion. The last four quarters together added up to KRW 259 million in profit attributable to owners, with the fourth-quarter loss pulling the total down.

For the first nine months of 2025, the company attributed its operating profit decline to higher marketing costs for B2C online sales and initial investment costs at its Chinese subsidiary.

05

Industry analysis

Demand for ESS fire safety is being driven by rising power needs from AI data centers and the expansion of renewable energy. According to press reports, global ESS shipments grew in 2025 as demand for managing power volatility increased along these two trends.

A domestic brokerage report from January 2026 analyzed that GFI's DI-KIT, backed by safety certification and a track record for overseas sales, could benefit directly from ESS expansion. Because DI-KIT also applies to battery-based power infrastructure such as UPS, its addressable demand could extend beyond ESS.

Fire-safety products track battery installation volumes, so customers' ESS build-out cycles drive revenue. The North American ESS build-out appears to be expanding, but the pace and timing depend on customer orders.

The company says it is broadening its customer base from its Samsung SDI-centered track record toward data centers and markets in Europe and Southeast Asia. In China, it is pushing to expand suppression products for ESS and secondary batteries. Overall, the demand backdrop is favorable, but converting that demand into revenue depends on the customer mix.

Outlook

In September 2026, alongside the North American supply contract with Samsung SDI, the company said it aims to add major customers by year-end to diversify its customer base. In the same period it said it would accelerate entry into global markets including Europe and Southeast Asia.

In its half-year disclosure in August 2026, it said revenue from the North American ESS fire-suppression installation business would begin in the third quarter, but it has not separately disclosed how much that business contributes to quarterly sales.

New products such as thermal-runaway prevention pads are seeing expanding supply. The company is also pursuing expanded suppression products aimed at new demand sources such as data centers. Third-quarter 2026 revenue was KRW 16.7 billion, up 7.2% from the prior quarter, while operating profit fell 5.6%.

How much the North American installation revenue feeds into the operating margin is therefore the next point to check. Most of the plans the company has stated are directional expansions, and the only dated items are the year-end customer target and the Samsung SDI supply period running through 2027. The 2026 annual business report, the next regular filing, should show the revenue mix and overseas share.

Bull factors

ESS demand and customer references

Global ESS shipments grew in 2025, and rising AI data-center power demand is expanding the need for battery-based power infrastructure.

GFI disclosed on September 1, 2026 a contract to supply DI-KIT to Samsung SDI's North American ESS site, and its UL9540A certification and verified track record are cited as strengths for overseas sales. Because its products apply to both ESS and UPS, the addressable demand base is broad.

Top-line growth and a return to profit

Revenue reached KRW 45 billion in 2025, up 85.4%, while operating profit rose 23.0%. The first quarter of 2026 returned to profit attributable to owners at KRW 3.04 billion on revenue of KRW 19.5 billion, and third-quarter revenue of KRW 16.7 billion was higher than in the second quarter.

Quarterly sales holding within the range of KRW 15.5 billion to KRW 19.5 billion also supports the top-line picture.

Product expansion and overseas plans

Supply of new ESS safety products such as thermal-runaway prevention pads is expanding, and DI-KIT sales in the first nine months of 2025 were about 2.7 times the year-earlier level. The company said revenue from its North American ESS suppression installation business would begin in the third quarter. Plans to broaden sales into Europe, Southeast Asia and China were also presented.

Risk factors

Customer and order concentration

A Samsung SDI-centered structure is a strength for references, but quarterly revenue could wobble if a key customer's ESS installation schedule slips. The company has not separately disclosed how much the North American installation business contributes to quarterly revenue, which limits visibility. Its diversification target is set for the end of 2026.

Overseas expansion costs and execution

The company said higher marketing costs for B2C online sales and initial investment costs at its Chinese subsidiary reduced operating profit in the first nine months of 2025. Expanding into Europe, Southeast Asia and North America at once could raise staffing and funding burdens. How these costs flow through to the operating margin is the central question.

Certification and regulatory changes

Overseas sales depend on safety certifications such as UL9540A, so changes to certification standards or local rules could add steps to product deployment. Certification timelines often determine when overseas sales can begin. Certification schedules and the start of overseas sales should be confirmed through disclosures.

Quarterly revenue stayed between KRW 15.5 billion and KRW 19.5 billion through the third quarter of 2026, and the Samsung SDI North American supply contract and overseas expansion plans are presented as its growth drivers.

On the other side, the company recorded a net loss attributable to owners in 2025, and profit attributable to owners over the last four quarters totals only KRW 259 million.
