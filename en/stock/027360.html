02

Business structure

AJU IB Investment operates as a specialized technology financing company and general partner of private equity funds, running venture capital (VC) funds and private equity (PE) strategies side by side.

According to its filings, its core business covers investments in and loans to technology-based businesses, factoring and leasing, and the formation, operation and management of investment partnerships.

Its activities span accelerator (AC) work for startups, VC investments in companies at the so-called death valley stage, and PE investments aimed at buyouts and restructuring.

Revenue consists of management fees from fund operations, performance fees tied to fund results, and valuation and disposal gains on investee assets, and a November 2026 report said swings in valuation gains on listed portfolio holdings are shaking quarterly results.

According to the business report filed in March 2026, fee income fell to roughly KRW 20.3 billion in 2025 from roughly KRW 23.1 billion in 2024.

Overseas investments run through its U.S. subsidiary Solasta Ventures, whose Boston unit focuses on bio companies and whose Silicon Valley unit, Solasta Nexus, invests in AI, robotics, cloud and autonomous driving businesses.

In 2023 the company reportedly invested in SpaceX, led by Elon Musk, through a special purpose company set up via Solasta Ventures. A June 2025 report said 19 of its locally invested companies had listed on Nasdaq, and the company has been introduced as a large venture capital firm listed on KOSDAQ since 2018.

04

Earnings analysis

On an annual basis, operating profit was KRW 4.85 billion in 2022, KRW 17.7 billion in 2023 (up 265.9% year on year), KRW 8.39 billion in 2024 (down 52.7%), and KRW 11.9 billion in 2025 (up 41.7%). 2023 was the largest year in the 2022 to 2025 period.

Profit attributable to controlling shareholders was KRW 2.04 billion in 2022, KRW 16.6 billion in 2023 (up 714.2%), KRW 8.32 billion in 2024 (down 49.9%), and KRW 8.32 billion in 2025, unchanged from the prior year.

Net income including non-controlling interests was KRW 2.04 billion, KRW 16.6 billion, KRW 8.32 billion and KRW 8.32 billion over the same years and should be read separately from profit attributable to controlling shareholders.

The 2025 operating profit increase was attributed to higher valuation and disposal gains from rising values at investee companies, according to a February 2026 report.

The debt-to-equity ratio declined from 53.3% in 2022 to 37.2% in 2023, 30.9% in 2024 and 26.8% in 2025, and at year-end 2025 total equity was KRW 266.9 billion, of which KRW 249.6 billion belonged to controlling shareholders, against total liabilities of KRW 71.4 billion.

Operating cash flow was KRW 9.83 billion in 2022, KRW 43 billion in 2023 and KRW 24.1 billion in 2024, then turned negative at -KRW 1.14 billion in 2025.

By quarter, operating profit went from KRW 2.33 billion in Q3 2025 to KRW 5.69 billion in Q4 2025 (up 144.3% quarter on quarter), KRW 8.05 billion in Q1 2026 (up 41.5%), and KRW 38.1 billion in Q2 2026 (up 373.8%).

Q3 2026 instead produced an operating loss of KRW 18.6 billion, a swing into loss from the prior year, with a net loss attributable to controlling shareholders of KRW 14.4 billion.

A November 16, 2026 report attributed the third-quarter results to lower unrealized valuation gains on listed portfolio holdings, a KOSDAQ correction, a price decline in SpaceX and a weaker dollar.

As a result, profit attributable to controlling shareholders over the last four quarters (Q4 2025 to Q3 2026) totaled KRW 24.9 billion, with the concentration of earnings in Q2 2026 widening the spread between quarters.

05

Industry analysis

AJU IB Investment's results depend more on the price trend of KOSDAQ-listed companies and the exit environment than on the broader venture investment cycle.

An August 2026 report cited weak KOSDAQ index performance and shrinking investor sentiment as a double burden, while a November 2026 report pointed to the KOSDAQ slump and a tight exit market as key variables.

On the other side, a steady stream of portfolio company listings and rising stakes in high-quality assets were seen as positives for first-half results. Because exit timing is tied to listing schedules, the IPO market creates a lag in quarterly results.

A February 2026 report noted that more liquidity from KOSDAQ revitalization measures could benefit the venture investment ecosystem, and some industry observers say AJU IB Investment could also benefit.

Among listed venture capital firms, the company is described as a large manager running accelerator, venture and private equity strategies together, with two U.S. offices investing in bio and AI technology companies.

Because SpaceX is its prominent holding and listing expectations affect its valuation, single-asset events weigh heavily on results relative to the sector cycle.

Outlook

The company attributed the third-quarter 2026 decline to lower valuation gains driven by market swings and described it as a temporary fluctuation. According to a November 2026 report, the company said the profit built up in the first half and its high profitability are being maintained.

In the same report, it said it plans to form new funds worth KRW 335 billion within the year to widen its management fee base and to build earnings capacity around AI industry transformation and a portfolio centered on global top-tier companies.

A February 2026 report analyzed that SpaceX's corporate value and expectations for its initial public offering affect the valuation of the stake.

The same report carried an observation that the return multiple on the SpaceX investment could exceed 10 times, but this was an outside observation cited by the press rather than an official company forecast. The report also said the stake size and investment amount have not been disclosed.

The 2025 year-end dividend was set at a higher per-share amount than in 2024, and total dividends rose. The next checkpoints are whether the new fund formation is completed by the end of 2026 and the annual operating profit and profit attributable to controlling shareholders in the 2026 business report.

Whether the Q3 2026 loss is offset by first-half profit on an annual basis will indicate the company's earnings capacity.

Bull factors

Drivers of first-half profit

First-half 2026 operating profit totaled KRW 46.2 billion, combining Q1 at KRW 8.05 billion and Q2 at KRW 38.1 billion, and an August report described it as a record. Higher performance fees and the recognition of SpaceX valuation gains were cited as the main drivers.

Supporters point to the fact that profit came from fund results and large asset valuations. Even after the Q3 loss, the first-half cumulative figure remains a record by press accounts.

Planned expansion of managed assets

In a November 2026 report, the company said it plans to form new funds worth KRW 335 billion within the year. The new funds were presented as a way to broaden the management fee base. The company emphasized a portfolio centered on AI industry transformation and global top-tier companies. Supporters cite the point that a larger fund base enlarges the management fee base alongside it.

Balance sheet and shareholder returns

The debt-to-equity ratio fell from 53.3% in 2022 to 26.8% in 2025, and total equity at year-end 2025 was KRW 266.9 billion. The 2025 year-end dividend rose from the prior year, and total dividend payments increased. The company has paid annual year-end dividends since 2019, showing continuity in shareholder returns. Supporters see the dividend increase alongside lower borrowing burden as evidence of financial strength.

Swing into a Q3 loss

Q3 2026 produced an operating loss of KRW 18.6 billion and a net loss attributable to controlling shareholders of KRW 14.4 billion, a swing into loss from the prior year.

The company described the decline in valuation gains as a temporary fluctuation, but the structure shows operating results can move sharply when valuation gains fall. The gap between KRW 38.1 billion in Q2 operating profit and the Q3 loss shows how wide quarterly swings can be. Bears treat this volatility as the central risk.

Earnings volatility and market dependence

A November 2026 report attributed the lower Q3 valuation gains to the KOSDAQ correction, a price decline in SpaceX and a weaker dollar. A structure in which results depend on the prices of major holdings is the core bear argument.

Cash flow and fee income

The decline in fee income needs to be checked alongside the scale of fund operations. Bears point to the weaker cash generation.

Risk factors

Market and exit risk

KOSDAQ weakness and a tight exit market constrain both the valuation of listed holdings and the timing of exits from held assets. If listing schedules slip, the recognition of performance fees can also be delayed. An August 2026 report pointed to weak KOSDAQ index performance and shrinking investor sentiment.

Valuation swings and single-asset risk

Valuations of large unlisted assets such as SpaceX feed heavily into quarterly results, and profit and loss can shift sharply with corporate value assessments and listing timing. A Q3 2026 report cited the SpaceX price correction as a factor behind the decline. A pattern in which the valuation changes of specific assets drive quarterly profit is recurring.

Fund formation and fee risk

If the planned KRW 335 billion of new fund formation within the year does not proceed as planned, the expansion of the management fee base will slow. Fee income fell to roughly KRW 20.3 billion in 2025 from roughly KRW 23.1 billion in 2024. The timing and size of fund formation are the items to check through the end of 2026.
