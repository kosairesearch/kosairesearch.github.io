02

Business structure

WooGeneB&GCO is a KOSDAQ-listed maker of animal medicines and vaccines. According to the 2025 annual report filed in March 2026, domestic sales accounted for 82% of 2025 revenue and exports for 18%.

The company says its penicillin-based injectable Antipen SM and the Supertia and Denagard series have established themselves as prescription treatments in Korea.

It supplies products through more than 20 major distribution channels domestically and is widening its overseas sales network in the United States, Hong Kong and Southeast Asia.

In vaccines, the porcine epidemic diarrhea vaccine IMMUNIS PED-M is the lead export item, with its first shipments to Thailand recorded in December 2023.

In October 2023 the company launched a combined swine fever and swine erysipelas vaccine, and in November 2024 it signed a supply contract with the Ministry of Agriculture, Food and Rural Affairs for a lumpy skin disease vaccine.

Media reports for the first half of 2026 said PED-M shipments to Thailand, adjuvant sales in South America and Russia/CIS, and growth of a swine respiratory treatment in Southeast Asia contributed to higher sales.

The subsidiary OSP, in which the company took a stake in December 2019, has seen its private-brand business expand, which helped profits. Because shipments depend on domestic livestock disease outbreaks and government stockpiling, quarterly revenue swings tend to be large. Overseas expansion also depends on obtaining product approval in each country first.

04

Earnings analysis

Annual revenue rose from KRW 43.7 billion in 2022 to KRW 57.9 billion in 2023 (up 32.5%), the largest year in the 2022–2025 span, and then fell two straight years to KRW 55.7 billion in 2024 (down 3.9%) and KRW 54.8 billion in 2025 (down 1.6%).

Operating profit was KRW 1.16 billion in 2022, the largest year, and declined to KRW 714 million in 2023 (down 38.2%) and KRW 295 million in 2024 (down 58.6%), the smallest year. In 2025 operating profit rose 8.1% to KRW 319 million, but the operating margin was only 0.6%, against 2.6% in 2022.

The margin path ran 2.6% in 2022, 1.2% in 2023, 0.5% in 2024 and 0.6% in 2025. Controlling shareholders' net income of KRW 850 million in 2022 turned into a controlling net loss of KRW 1.18 billion in 2023, widened to a net loss of KRW 5.22 billion in 2024, and narrowed to a net loss of KRW 1.77 billion in 2025.

Net income including non-controlling interests was a net loss of KRW 3.58 billion in 2025, and the gap with the controlling figure suggests non-controlling holders absorbed part of the loss.

The debt ratio climbed from 41.7% in 2022 to 70.5% in 2025, and operating cash flow was KRW 2.64 billion, KRW 1.18 billion, KRW 6.7 billion and KRW 3.87 billion across 2022 to 2025.

Quarterly, revenue of KRW 13.2 billion and an operating loss of KRW 769 million in Q3 2025 gave way to Q4 2025 revenue of KRW 13.5 billion (up 2.0% quarter on quarter) and operating profit of KRW 459 million, a swing to profit.

Q1 2026 revenue was KRW 14.3 billion (up 6.1%), operating profit KRW 705 million (up 53.6%), and the controlling net loss narrowed to KRW 23.78 million. Q2 2026 revenue reached KRW 15.5 billion (up 8.3%), operating profit KRW 1.38 billion (up 95.6%), and controlling net income KRW 1.15 billion, a return to profit.

Q3 2026 revenue fell to KRW 12.7 billion (down 18.1% quarter on quarter and down 4.1% year on year), with an operating loss of KRW 144 million and a controlling net loss of KRW 946 million. The controlling net losses over the last four quarters (Q4 2025 to Q3 2026) total KRW 2.59 billion.

05

Industry analysis

The animal medicine market is strongly shaped by livestock disease outbreaks and government disease-control policy. Demand for swine vaccines is steady at the farm level, but timing of outbreaks and government supply volumes can make quarterly revenue volatile.

The company's results appear to reflect this pattern: revenue rose in Q1 and Q2 2026 but fell 18.1% quarter on quarter in Q3. The export markets the company has named are Southeast Asia, Latin America, and Russia and the CIS.

Overseas sales depend on local product approval, so the speed of approvals is a key variable for export growth. The company has said exports to markets such as Vietnam and Mexico would expand once PED-M approval is completed there.

Although classified under biotech and pharmaceuticals, the company differs from listed firms centered on human medicines because most of its revenue depends on the livestock market. Benchmarking against animal health peers therefore explains its results better than comparing it with human drug developers.

Because it combines in-house vaccines with animal medicines, margins shift with product mix. The operating margin of 0.6% in 2025 can be read in this context of structurally thin margins. The company names new vaccines and its pet food business as its next growth drivers.

06

Outlook

The company said it will focus its resources in the second half of 2026 on launching high value-added vaccines and new bio projects. It also cited global expansion of its premium pet food business and a new vaccine lineup as growth drivers.

It said PED-M exports would increase once approvals are completed in markets such as Vietnam and Mexico. No approval timing has been disclosed, so it is difficult to pin down when such exports would appear in results.

On the financing side, the fifth convertible bond series was fully repaid in March 2026, reducing the overhang of potential sales. The largest shareholder side disclosed open-market purchases of company shares worth about KRW 1 billion.

A margin loan held by a former CEO was fully repaid, lowering risk tied to the largest shareholder's stake. At the March 2026 annual shareholder meeting, amendments to the articles, the re-election of an executive director and the appointment of an outside director were approved.

A company official said the business should gradually be re-rated as profitability recovers from the recent improvement. That remark reflects the company's expectation, while the disclosed figures show a return to an operating loss in Q3 2026.

The next checkpoint is the 2026 annual report, which will show Q4 2026 results and the full-year operating margin. The timing of new product launches and overseas approvals should be checked against company disclosures and news reports once dates are confirmed.

The largest shareholder side holds 30.91% of shares, and an outside director resigned in March 2026, changing the board composition.
