02

Business structure

Woory Industrial Holdings became a holding company in 2015, when its automotive parts business was split off into the subsidiary Woory Industrial (215360). The holding company manages subsidiaries and investments, and its main income comes from investment and brand fees and rental income.

Woory Industrial makes automotive HVAC parts, including HVAC actuators, coolant and PTC heaters, control head units and clutch coil assemblies. Its 2022 annual report describes the subsidiary as a second-tier supplier to vehicle makers, with major Korean customers and export customers including DENSO and BEHR.

Overseas subsidiaries in China, India, Thailand and Slovakia are consolidated, so their results feed into group revenue. Some overseas units produce locally, while others receive parts from the Pyeongtaek plant in semi-knocked-down form and also get tooling from Woory Industrial.

As of 28 October 2026, the holding company held 40.33% of Woory Industrial. A March 2024 research note described two chief executives as holding the core stakes in the ownership structure.

Same-sector listed holding companies include SK Square, Hanjinkal and Lotte, but their subsidiaries differ, which limits direct comparison. Because customer concentration is high, earnings track vehicle production volumes and supplier selection by vehicle makers.

04

Earnings analysis

Revenue rose from KRW 495.5 billion in 2022 to KRW 575.3 billion in 2023 (up 16.1% year on year), KRW 645.3 billion in 2024 (up 12.2%) and KRW 705 billion in 2025 (up 9.3%), increasing for three consecutive years from 2023 through 2025.

Operating profit swung from an operating loss of KRW 19.8 billion in 2022 to KRW 21.3 billion in 2023, fell to KRW 15 billion in 2024 (down 29.3%) and recovered to KRW 34.2 billion in 2025 (up 127.9%). Operating margin was -4.0% in 2022, 3.7% in 2023, 2.3% in 2024 and 4.9% in 2025, the highest of the four years.

Attributable net income moved from an attributable net loss of KRW 18.3 billion in 2022 to KRW 15.2 billion in 2023, fell to KRW 1.33 billion in 2024 (down 91.2%) and rose to KRW 19 billion in 2025 (up 1,328.1%).

For 2025, the company cited company-wide cost reductions from a new management system and higher sales as the main drivers, as reported on 12 February 2026. Operating cash flow went from KRW 1.39 billion in 2022 to KRW 10.1 billion in 2023 and KRW 52 billion in 2024, then eased to KRW 45.3 billion in 2025.

The debt ratio rose from 116.5% in 2022 to 143.7% in 2023 and 146.8% in 2024, then fell to 141.0% in 2025. At the end of 2025, total liabilities were KRW 298.3 billion and total equity was KRW 211.6 billion, of which attributable equity was KRW 141.4 billion.

By quarter, operating profit fell from KRW 9.92 billion in 3Q 2025 (attributable net income of KRW 9.2 billion) to KRW 6.86 billion in 4Q 2025, down 30.8% quarter on quarter.

It recovered to KRW 8.63 billion in 1Q 2026 (up 25.7%) but dropped to KRW 1.46 billion in 2Q 2026 (down 83.1%), when attributable results turned to a loss of KRW 134 million. In 3Q 2026, revenue was KRW 166 billion, down 5.1% year on year, and operating profit was KRW 4.12 billion, down 58.5% year on year.

The attributable net loss of KRW 4.24 billion in 3Q 2026 was a swing from a year-earlier profit, and it marks the second consecutive loss-making quarter after 2Q 2026.

Attributable net income across the last four quarters (4Q 2025 to 3Q 2026) totals KRW 5.29 billion, a small figure next to the KRW 19 billion attributable net income for 2025.

The cost structure and business-segment drivers behind the losses since 2Q 2026 are items to check in the notes and segment disclosures of the periodic reports.

Industry analysis

Automotive HVAC parts are used in both combustion-engine and electric vehicles, and in EVs cabin heating and battery thermal management shape the product mix. The subsidiary's PTC heaters and HVAC actuators are core components of these climate systems.

As a second-tier supplier to vehicle makers, however, its end demand depends on vehicle production volumes and the awards of higher-tier suppliers. Overseas production is spread across China, India, Thailand and Slovakia, so regional differences in vehicle demand flow into group results.

Quarterly operating profit moved widely in 2026, from KRW 8.63 billion in 1Q to KRW 1.46 billion in 2Q and KRW 4.12 billion in 3Q, so demand and cost factors both need monitoring.

Among same-sector holding companies, SK Square, Hanjinkal and Lotte hold subsidiaries in different industries, which limits direct comparison with an auto parts group.

The holding discount describes a holding company's market value trading below the value of its subsidiaries, and clarity of the ownership structure and subsidiary results affect its size.

The link to electrification can be gauged from the product mix, but the revenue amount tied to EVs has to be checked in the segment information of the periodic reports.

Risk factors

Customer concentration and vehicle demand

Because the subsidiary is a second-tier supplier to vehicle makers, changes in production plans pass directly into revenue. Concentration among domestic customers means one customer's volume adjustment can move results. Shifts in parts content during the EV transition could also affect demand for existing products.

Overseas units and currency

Results from overseas subsidiaries in China, India, Thailand and Slovakia flow into consolidated earnings, so local currencies and local competition are variables. Wide gaps among overseas units can add volatility to group earnings. Some overseas units rely on parts from the Pyeongtaek plant, so domestic utilization also matters.

Ownership structure and liquidity

In a holding structure, subsidiary stakes and concentrated executive ownership are intertwined, so changes in governance can affect the shareholder base. As a small-cap stock, trading can be volatile. The size of the holding discount also depends on the ownership structure of the subsidiary.
