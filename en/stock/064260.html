Payment volume grows, losses persist at Danal

02

Business structure

Danal is an integrated payment company founded in 1997, and it highlights that it commercialized the world's first mobile phone payment service in 2000.

Its core commerce payment business consists of mobile phone micropayments, credit card payment gateway (PG) services, gift certificate, account and barcode payments, and identity verification services.

In 2019 it opened offline mobile phone payments together with Samsung Pay, and the company said it plans a full revamp of its offline mobile payment service Batong within this year.

Its subsidiary Danal Entertainment is expanding intellectual property business from content distribution into concerts, exhibitions and merchandise.

The digital asset segment is stablecoin payment and settlement infrastructure, where the company says major domestic banks handle issuance and remittance while Danal handles merchant payments and settlement, and the proof of concept and platform are complete.

Overseas, the company says it supports payments in more than 200 countries and 23 currencies across e-wallets, overseas carrier billing and overseas credit cards.

By segment, commerce revenue for the first nine months of 2025 was KRW 147.7 billion, down 13% year on year, and digital content revenue was KRW 13.8 billion, down 43.2%. In competition, mobile phone payment is Danal's own niche, but PG and simple payments overlap with large platforms and card companies.

Unlike NAVER and Kakao in the same IT and software sector, whose platform advertising income is large, Danal's revenue is more directly tied to payment transaction volume.

04

Earnings analysis

On a filed basis, annual revenue went from KRW 294.9 billion in 2022 to KRW 295.5 billion in 2023, an increase of 0.2% that marked the peak, before falling to KRW 260.9 billion in 2024 (down 11.7%) and KRW 225.4 billion in 2025 (down 13.6%). Revenue fell two years in a row from 2024 to 2025.

Of the 2022 to 2025 period, 2023 was the largest year for revenue and 2025 was the smallest.

Operating profit moved from an operating loss of KRW 2.76 billion in 2022 to operating profit of KRW 3.5 billion in 2023, a swing to profit, then fell 60.2% to KRW 1.39 billion in 2024 and recovered 65.9% to KRW 2.31 billion in 2025.

The operating margin was -0.9% in 2022, 1.2% in 2023, 0.5% in 2024 and 1.0% in 2025, so core profitability stayed near 1% of revenue.

Net loss attributable to controlling shareholders was KRW 15.3 billion in 2022 and KRW 27.9 billion in 2023, then turned to a KRW 7.25 billion profit in 2024 before swinging back to a KRW 63.7 billion loss in 2025.

Net income including non-controlling interests was a KRW 33.8 billion loss in 2022, a KRW 40.6 billion loss in 2023, a KRW 2.92 billion profit in 2024 and a KRW 68.4 billion loss in 2025; the gap from the controlling-shareholder figures reflects non-controlling interests.

Operating cash flow went from negative KRW 16.3 billion in 2024 to positive KRW 14.3 billion in 2025. The debt ratio fell from 160.5% in 2023 to 141.2% in 2025, when total equity was KRW 307.6 billion (KRW 302.8 billion attributable to controlling shareholders) and total liabilities were KRW 434.4 billion.

Quarterly, the controlling-shareholder net loss of KRW 35.7 billion in the third quarter of 2025 narrowed to KRW 9.54 billion in the fourth quarter, and the first quarter of 2026 returned to a KRW 2.9 billion profit.

Losses then returned, with a KRW 7.65 billion loss in the second quarter and a KRW 32.6 billion loss in the third quarter of 2026, making two quarters of losses in a row, and third-quarter 2026 operating profit of KRW 149 million fell 91.7% from the prior quarter.

The preliminary nine-month 2026 figures announced on November 13, 2026 (revenue of KRW 174.6 billion and operating profit of KRW 3.1 billion) reflect higher payment volume and a smaller net loss, according to the company.

05

Industry analysis

Demand in the payment industry depends on online and offline transaction volume, and high interest rates and external uncertainty weigh on consumer spending.

In its announcement of the first nine months of 2026 results, the company said growth in online and offline payment coverage and higher transaction volume in commerce, culture and leisure lifted revenue.

By contrast, commerce segment revenue for the first nine months of 2025 fell 13% year on year, and Dealsite analyzed that the core revenue source slowed after the TMON and WeMakePrice incident. This means the pace of recovery in the core commerce segment will decide earnings.

The biggest variable for the industry now is institutionalization of digital assets. The Digital Asset Basic Act government bill, which Newspim and other outlets reported is likely to be announced in November 2026, together with the stablecoin legislation track, will define the scope of bank-linked payment models.

Overseas, the company says it is developing cross-border payment business models with companies in Mongolia, the United States and Singapore. Competitively, Danal has its own mobile payment niche, but its revenue structure differs from NAVER and Kakao, whose platform advertising and content income are large.

Because revenue is directly tied to payment transaction volume, whether volume recovers will determine how sensitive earnings are.

06

Outlook

The company plans to add bank counter top-ups, linkage of simple payments for overseas users, and multilingual ATM location guidance within this year. It plans to expand card issuance channels centered on airports and other major arrival points, aiming for full-scale revenue generation from 2027.

The offline mobile payment service Batong will be fully revamped within this year, switching to a QR code scan at stores without a separate app and simplifying identity verification and sign-up.

Because the stablecoin business expands in line with the domestic legislative timetable, the content of the Digital Asset Basic Act government bill will set the scope of the business.

Subsidiary Danal Entertainment aims to return to profit this year while broadening its intellectual property business into concerts, exhibitions, pop-up stores and merchandise.

The company indicates that revenue contribution from new services should show up in results from 2027, so until then the core payment transaction volume and operating margin are the baseline for results.

The 2026 annual business report is scheduled for submission in March 2027, when full-year 2026 results including the fourth quarter and whether the subsidiary returned to profit will be confirmed. First-quarter 2027 results will be disclosed in the quarterly report in May of the same year.

08

Bull factors

Revenue growth from payment volume

In preliminary nine-month 2026 results announced on November 13, 2026, the company reported revenue of KRW 174.6 billion and operating profit of KRW 3.1 billion and said the top line grew year on year. The first quarter of 2026 also returned to a controlling-shareholder profit of KRW 2.9 billion.

The company cited wider online and offline payment coverage and higher commerce, culture and leisure transaction volume as drivers.

Early position in stablecoin infrastructure

The company says it has completed a proof of concept and platform for a structure in which banks handle stablecoin issuance and remittance while Danal handles payments and settlement.

A service linked with a global company has entered commercialization, and cross-border payment models with companies in Mongolia, the United States and Singapore are in progress. The company plans to expand related services once the Digital Asset Basic Act government bill is announced.

Improving financial structure

The debt ratio fell from 160.5% in 2023 to 141.2% in 2025. Of total equity of KRW 307.6 billion at the end of 2025, KRW 302.8 billion is attributable to controlling shareholders.

09

Bear factors

Continued losses for controlling shareholders

The controlling-shareholder net loss swung to KRW 63.7 billion in 2025, and the last four quarters total KRW 46.9 billion. The third quarter of 2026 saw a KRW 32.6 billion loss attributable to controlling shareholders, with operating profit of only KRW 149 million. Losses in two consecutive quarters are a key bearish factor.

Revenue decline and slowing commerce

Annual revenue fell two years in a row from 2024 to 2025. Commerce segment revenue for the first nine months of 2025 fell 13% year on year, and digital content segment revenue fell 43.2%. Dealsite reported that the main revenue source slowed after the TMON and WeMakePrice incident.

Thin margins and financial burden

The operating margin was only 1.0% in 2025, so profitability relative to revenue is thin. Total liabilities of KRW 434.4 billion exceed total equity of KRW 307.6 billion, and the debt ratio is 141.2%.

Because derivative valuation losses were reported as a factor in net losses, volatility in non-operating items is also a burden.

10

Risk factors

Regulatory and policy risk

The stablecoin business depends on the Digital Asset Basic Act government bill and the legislative timetable. If the timing or content changes, the scope of bank-linked payment and settlement models and the timing of monetization could shift.

The company has said it is preparing services within the institutional framework, so the legal framework is a precondition for the business.

Earnings volatility

Derivative valuation gains and losses and non-operating items can move net income sharply, regardless of operating profit. In the third quarter of 2026, operating profit was KRW 149 million while the controlling-shareholder net loss was KRW 32.6 billion, a wide gap. If that gap varies from quarter to quarter, the net loss trend becomes harder to read.

Dependence on commerce transaction volume

Commerce payments, the center of revenue, are exposed to consumer spending and high interest rates. Commerce segment revenue for the first nine months of 2025 fell 13%, and the digital content segment fell 43.2%.

Whether the subsidiary returns to profit and new channels such as Batong can offset slowing transaction volume is the key question.

12

Overall view

Danal preliminarily announced that payment volume growth lifted revenue for the first nine months of 2026 and narrowed its net loss, but on a filed basis revenue fell two years in a row from 2024 to 2025 and the controlling-shareholder net loss swung to KRW 63.7 billion in 2025.

The last four quarters total KRW 46.9 billion in controlling-shareholder losses, and the second and third quarters of 2026 mark two quarters of losses in a row. On the other hand, operating cash flow turned positive at KRW 14.3 billion in 2025, and the debt ratio fell from 160.5% in 2023 to 141.2% in 2025.

Bullish factors are the recovery in core transaction volume and completion of stablecoin payment infrastructure, while bearish factors are an operating margin of around 1%, falling commerce revenue, and non-operating volatility such as derivative valuation gains and losses.

The next checkpoints are the content of the Digital Asset Basic Act government bill, the Batong revamp and bank counter top-ups within this year, and whether the subsidiary returns to profit as confirmed in the 2026 annual report.

Because valuation is in a net-loss phase, earnings-based metrics are hard to compare, and asset-based comparison is more suitable for factual checks. With bullish and bearish factors coexisting, the key is to confirm which way both trends move in upcoming disclosures.
