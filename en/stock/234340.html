Report overview

Record Cumulative Profit on Payments; Net Income Recovering

Hecto Financial posted record cumulative results through the third quarter of 2026, and net income attributable to controlling shareholders, which fell for three straight years through 2025, has been positive every quarter since the first quarter of 2026.

02

Business structure

Hecto Financial is a fintech company that runs simple cash payments, payment gateway (PG) services, virtual accounts and overseas settlement. One media report described it as the only PG-focused fintech in Korea that offers simple cash payments, credit cards and mobile payments together.

PG revenue reached KRW 79.8 billion in 2024, up 26.9% from the prior year and leading growth, while simple cash payment revenue fell 30.3% to KRW 32.9 billion and virtual account revenue fell 1.8% to KRW 23.4 billion.

In 2025, PG revenue rose to KRW 92.2 billion, or 49.18% of total revenue, and simple cash payment revenue grew 26.4% to KRW 41.5 billion. In the first quarter of 2026, revenue mix was PG 42.8%, simple cash payments 31.64%, virtual accounts 9.88% and other revenue 10.31% (KRW 5.9 billion).

The company says growth in Naetongjang Payment, a proprietary member-based service with high margins, lifted simple cash payment margins in the third quarter of 2026.

Fee structures differ by business: in 2025, fee costs were KRW 13.5 billion, or 32.5%, of simple cash payment revenue of KRW 41.5 billion, while PG fee costs reportedly reach about 80% of PG revenue.

A September 2025 company analysis classified Hecto Financial as a late entrant in PG, in a market where large PG operators hold most of the share. Customers are merchants and the company's own members, and the company cited new merchants and higher transaction volumes as growth drivers in the third quarter of 2026.

Overseas operations began with cross-border settlement in 2023 and have expanded into small-value personal overseas remittances.

04

Earnings analysis

Annual revenue rose from KRW 129.3 billion in 2022 to KRW 153.1 billion in 2023 (up 18.4% year on year), KRW 159.3 billion in 2024 (up 4.1%) and KRW 187.4 billion in 2025 (up 17.7%), growing for three consecutive years from 2023 through 2025, with 2025 the largest year of 2022 to 2025.

Operating profit fell from KRW 12.8 billion in 2022 to KRW 12.6 billion in 2023 (down 2.1%), then recovered to KRW 13.3 billion in 2024 (up 5.7%) and KRW 15.6 billion in 2025 (up 17.2%), rising for two consecutive years from 2024 through 2025.

The operating margin fell from 9.9% in 2022 to 8.2% in 2023 and was 8.3% in both 2024 and 2025.

Net income attributable to controlling shareholders fell from KRW 14 billion in 2022 to KRW 11.3 billion in 2023 (down 19.4%), KRW 9.71 billion in 2024 (down 14.0%) and KRW 9.27 billion in 2025 (down 4.5%), three consecutive years of decline, which diverged from the rise in operating profit in 2024 and 2025.

In 2025, net income attributable to controlling shareholders (KRW 9.27 billion) exceeded net income including non-controlling interests (KRW 8.82 billion).

The debt-to-equity ratio rose from 134.8% in 2022 to 159.5% in 2023, 198.3% in 2024 and 196.6% in 2025, and total liabilities of KRW 352.1 billion at the end of 2025 exceeded total equity of KRW 179.1 billion.

Operating cash flow rose from KRW 371 million in 2022 to KRW 26.9 billion in 2023, KRW 64.5 billion in 2024 and KRW 64.4 billion in 2025, and in 2024 and 2025 it was well above that year's operating profit.

Quarterly revenue rose from KRW 45.8 billion in the third quarter of 2025 to KRW 51.2 billion in the fourth quarter (up 11.8% from the prior quarter) and KRW 57.5 billion in the first quarter of 2026 (up 12.3%), then fell to KRW 50.1 billion in the second quarter (down 12.8%) before recovering to KRW 52.6 billion in the third quarter (up 4.9% quarter on quarter and up 14.9% year on year).

Operating profit moved from KRW 4.52 billion in the third quarter of 2025 and KRW 3.57 billion in the fourth quarter (down 21.1%) to KRW 9.14 billion in the first quarter of 2026 (up 156.2%), then returned to the mid-KRW 4 billion range with KRW 4.61 billion in the second quarter (down 49.6%) and KRW 4.8 billion in the third quarter (up 4.1%).

Net income attributable to controlling shareholders turned to a net loss of KRW 445 million in the fourth quarter of 2025, turned positive at KRW 7.35 billion in the first quarter of 2026, and was KRW 4.13 billion in the second quarter (down 43.7%) and KRW 6.04 billion in the third quarter (up 46.2% quarter on quarter and up 50.3% year on year).

Net income attributable to controlling shareholders over the most recent four quarters totaled KRW 17.1 billion. Cumulative revenue of KRW 160.2 billion through the third quarter of 2026 (up 17.6% year on year) and operating profit of KRW 18.5 billion (up 54.4%) are preliminary figures announced on November 9, 2026.

05

Industry analysis

Korea's payment market is expanding as e-commerce and simple payments drive transaction growth, and Hecto Financial has shifted its revenue mix from account-based services toward non-account-based services within this trend.

A 2025 report showed the share of non-account-based services rising from 20.1% in 2021 to 40.2% in 2024. An August 2026 report said simple cash payment revenue reached KRW 29.7 billion in the first half of 2026, or 27.6% of total revenue of KRW 107.6 billion.

The PG market has many participants, with large PG operators holding most of the share, and Hecto Financial is classified as a late entrant. PG revenue in the first half of 2026 was KRW 48.2 billion, up only KRW 400 million from KRW 47.8 billion a year earlier.

By contrast, third-quarter 2026 PG revenue was KRW 25.0 billion, up 14.1% year on year, so quarterly growth has been uneven. In simple payments, the company competes with platform operators such as kakaopay.

In overseas settlement, it is expanding small-value remittances using a foreign exchange license, and cross-border settlement demand is linked to stablecoin-based payments.

Because PG and simple payments account for most revenue, fee structures and the pace of merchant acquisition in these two businesses will shape its position in the industry.

06

Outlook

At its third-quarter 2026 results, the company said it will keep expanding its global business and building next-generation payment infrastructure, including stablecoin and AI agent payments.

A March 2026 report said the company planned to set up overseas subsidiaries and launch stablecoin-based payment and settlement services and overseas remittances. The timing of those launches was not specified in the available sources.

The company joined an ecosystem around x402, an AI payment protocol, and its AI agent payments are described as being at the proof-of-concept and technology build-out stage. The company has not disclosed that commercial AI payment revenue contributed to third-quarter 2026 results.

Cross-border settlement partnerships with overseas platforms are expanding, and other revenue, which includes cross-border settlement, rose 42.0% year on year to KRW 8.0 billion in the third quarter of 2026. Within simple cash payments, the mix of higher-margin proprietary member-based services continues to grow.

The next items to track are how much overseas settlement contributes to results and whether stablecoin and AI payment trials move into commercial services. The 2026 annual report will show whether other revenue and overseas operations are disclosed as a larger share of the business.

08

Bull factors

Record cumulative results and margin gains

Cumulative revenue through the third quarter of 2026 reached KRW 160.2 billion, up 17.6% year on year, and operating profit reached KRW 18.5 billion, up 54.4%. These are preliminary figures announced on November 9, 2026, and they are the highest cumulative results on record.

The cumulative operating margin was about 11.5%, roughly 2.7 percentage points above about 8.8% a year earlier. Operating profit growth outpacing revenue growth is cited as a bullish point, showing expansion and margin improvement together.

Higher-margin proprietary member services

Within simple cash payments, the share of Naetongjang Payment, a proprietary member-based service, has grown, lifting profitability. In 2025, fee costs were KRW 13.5 billion, or 32.5%, of simple cash payment revenue of KRW 41.5 billion, while PG fee costs reportedly reach about 80% of PG revenue.

Simple cash payment revenue in the third quarter of 2026 was KRW 12.0 billion, up 12.5% year on year. Analysts note that as services with a lower cost structure take a larger share, their contribution to profit could expand.

Overseas settlement and new payment rails

Other revenue, which includes cross-border settlement, was KRW 8.0 billion in the third quarter of 2026, up 42.0% year on year. The company has outlined plans for overseas subsidiaries and stablecoin-based payment, settlement and remittance services, according to a March 2026 report.

It is widening its business through small-value overseas remittances using a foreign exchange license. Operating cash flow was KRW 64.5 billion in 2024 and KRW 64.4 billion in 2025, well above operating profit in each of those years, which is a cash generation strength.

09

Bear factors

Net income to controlling shareholders down three straight years

Net income attributable to controlling shareholders fell from KRW 14 billion in 2022 to KRW 11.3 billion in 2023, KRW 9.71 billion in 2024 and KRW 9.27 billion in 2025, three straight years of decline. Operating profit rose in 2024 and 2025 over the same period, so the two measures moved in opposite directions.

The fourth quarter of 2025 recorded a net loss attributable to controlling shareholders of KRW 445 million. Quarterly profit has been positive in 2026, and whether annual net income breaks the downward trend will show in the 2026 annual report.

PG growth slowed; virtual accounts declining

The small increase in first-half PG revenue is the basis for the slowdown. Virtual account revenue fell to KRW 11.3 billion from KRW 11.6 billion over the same comparison. Virtual account revenue had also declined 1.8% in 2024.

In contrast, third-quarter 2026 PG revenue rose 14.1% year on year to KRW 25.0 billion, so quarterly momentum has been uneven. Whether PG growth holds steady across quarters is the point to watch on this bearish side.

High leverage relative to equity

The debt-to-equity ratio rose from 134.8% in 2022 to 159.5% in 2023, 198.3% in 2024 and 196.6% in 2025. Total liabilities at the end of 2025 were KRW 352.1 billion, above total equity of KRW 179.1 billion. Controlling shareholders' equity accounts for KRW 171.2 billion of that total. The debt-to-equity ratio has stayed above 190% since 2024, which is a financial weakness.

Risk factors

Client attrition and reliance on simple payments

A 2026 report said that in 2021, simple payments made up over 40% of revenue and supported a double-digit operating margin, but after some clients left, revenue declined and the annual operating margin stayed in single digits through 2025. The case shows how losing specific clients can directly hit profitability. The recovery in simple payment margins depends on keeping clients in this segment.

Fee structure differences across businesses

Bank fees account for a large share of service costs in simple cash payments, whose 2025 cost ratio was 32.5%, well below the roughly 80% fee cost ratio reported for PG. Because profitability differs sharply across businesses, PG revenue growth may not translate into proportional profit growth.

PG was the largest segment at 42.8% of revenue in the first quarter of 2026. Changes in fee terms or bank linkage costs could affect margins by business.

Timing of new-business commercialization

Overseas settlement, stablecoin payments and AI agent payments are at the proof-of-concept and technology build-out stage, and the company has not disclosed commercial revenue from them in the third quarter of 2026.

Analysts and the company point to how much overseas settlement contributes to results and whether stablecoin and AI payment trials become commercial services as the key items to watch. Costs from expanding new businesses, and the timing gap before they show up in results, could weigh on short-term profit.

Overall view

Hecto Financial recorded its highest cumulative revenue and operating profit through the third quarter of 2026, driven by growth in its core payment business and expansion of higher-margin simple cash payments.

Cumulative operating profit of KRW 18.5 billion was up 54.4% year on year, and the preliminary cumulative operating margin of about 11.5% compares with about 8.8% a year earlier.

However, net income attributable to controlling shareholders fell for three consecutive years from KRW 14 billion in 2022 to KRW 9.27 billion in 2025, and the fourth quarter of 2025 recorded a net loss of KRW 445 million.

Profit has been positive in every quarter since the first quarter of 2026, and the most recent four quarters total KRW 17.1 billion. On the financial side, the debt-to-equity ratio was 196.6% in 2025, with total liabilities of KRW 352.1 billion above total equity of KRW 179.1 billion.

Overseas subsidiaries, stablecoin-based payment and settlement, and AI agent payments remain at the planning or proof-of-concept stage, and their contribution to results has not yet appeared in disclosures.

The next point to confirm is whether annual net income attributable to controlling shareholders and the operating margin in the 2026 annual report continue the quarterly trend.
