Pulp Prices and Fines Keep Moorim Paper in Losses

Moorim Paper posted a shareholder net loss for five straight quarters through the third quarter of 2026, and pulp prices and regulatory fines are the key swing factors for its next results.

02

Business structure

Moorim Paper is a pulp, paper and paperboard maker listed on KOSPI in 1990, and together with its pulp affiliate Moorim P&P it forms one of the main listed paper arms of the Moorim Group. The company runs a vertical structure from its own forests through pulp production to printing paper and specialty industrial paper.

According to a corporate analysis report, Moorim P&P supplies roughly 30% of the pulp that Moorim Paper uses as its main raw material, and in 2011 the company completed a integrated pulp-and-paper plant with capacity of about 550,000 tons of printing paper.

The same report put overseas sales at about 47% of revenue in 2022, with exports to more than 100 countries including North America, Europe, Southeast Asia and the Middle East.

Its domestic market share was about 47.6% in industrial paper and about 23.1% in printing paper, so exports weigh more heavily in printing paper. On products, the company has widened its portfolio into higher-value items such as thermal paper used for receipts, tickets and lottery tickets.

In the first half of 2026, paper-segment operating profit fell 92%, but a KRW 23.8 billion improvement in the wholesale segment and roughly a fourfold rise in financial-segment operating profit cushioned consolidated earnings, according to EBN.

The company attributed the decline to a temporary halt in production during construction of an expanded eco-friendly recovery boiler, and it said it is also developing bioplastics using bio-derived ingredients such as pulp.

04

Earnings analysis

Annual sales moved from KRW 1.4 trillion in 2022 to KRW 1.32 trillion in 2023 (down 5.5% year on year) and then recovered to KRW 1.38 trillion in 2024 (up 4.7%). Sales fell to KRW 1.26 trillion in 2025 (down 8.6%), the smallest year in the 2022–2025 window.

Operating profit ran from KRW 96.3 billion in 2022 to KRW 67.8 billion in 2023 (down 29.6%), KRW 89.4 billion in 2024 (up 31.9%), and KRW 5.95 billion in 2025 (down 93.3%). The operating margin over the same years was 6.9%, 5.1%, 6.5% and 0.5%, so 2025 saw a sharp drop.

Operating profit fell far more than sales did, which points to pulp price declines and fixed-cost pressure squeezing profit.

Shareholder net income went from KRW 15.2 billion in 2022 to KRW 3.47 billion in 2023 (down 77.1%), rose to KRW 40.7 billion in 2024 (up 1,071.4%), and turned into a shareholder net loss of KRW 15.4 billion in 2025. Including non-controlling interests, the 2025 net loss was KRW 23.2 billion.

Quarterly, operating profit in the first quarter of 2026 was KRW 159 million, a swing from an operating loss in the prior quarter, and the second quarter rose to KRW 28 billion.

However, the second-quarter shareholder net loss widened to KRW 39.5 billion, and reports tied this to the recognition of a Fair Trade Commission fine. Third-quarter 2026 sales were KRW 286.2 billion (down 7.1% year on year), with an operating loss of KRW 15.5 billion that was larger than a year earlier.

Operating cash flow in 2025 was KRW 19 billion, down sharply from KRW 182.1 billion in 2024.

Shareholder net losses have now run for five straight quarters from the third quarter of 2025 through the third quarter of 2026, and the four quarters from the fourth quarter of 2025 to the third quarter of 2026 total KRW 77.9 billion.

05

Industry analysis

FnGuide summarized that printing paper demand keeps shrinking as digital technology spreads, and the domestic market remains flat.

In an April 2026 report, a Korean credit rating agency expected that weak supply-demand conditions for printing paper at home and abroad, along with rising fixed costs such as labor, would limit the recovery in earnings.

On raw materials, the decline in international pulp prices was cited as a main cause of the 2025 earnings drop.

In the first quarter of 2026, higher raw material prices including pulp weighed on results, and in the third quarter a fall in printing paper selling prices combined with higher prices for some raw materials, according to News1.

A September 2026 Newspim report said international pulp prices were still falling and called pulp a key variable for second-half profitability. External factors include higher shipping costs and oil price volatility linked to tensions in the Middle East, as a company official noted in the first quarter of 2026.

Because the company produces pulp and paper in an integrated system, a rise in pulp prices would tend to lift profitability noticeably. The rating agency's researcher, however, said that even a moderate pulp rebound could be limited by weak printing paper supply, higher wood chip prices and fixed-cost burdens.

In cycle terms, whether pulp prices rebound is the central question, while several reports agree that demand for printing paper has not yet shown a clear turn. EBN compared the company with rival Hansol Paper when it pointed out the decline in core paper profit.

06

Outlook

A company official said a rebound in pulp prices would lift profitability because the company produces pulp and paper together.

The same official said that once the new eco-friendly recovery boiler is fully running, energy production efficiency should improve and cut costs by roughly KRW 50 billion a year on an EBITDA basis.

A Korean credit rating agency expected 2026 operating profitability to improve year on year, citing a pulp price rebound, lower production costs from the new boiler, and higher utilization at its pulp and paper facilities.

The same report said weak printing paper supply and higher fixed costs would limit the recovery, and that although the large capex cycle has ended, the debt burden will not ease sharply in the short term.

In the first quarter of 2026, strong exports to North America and a weaker-won effect partly improved results from the prior quarter. On the fine, the Moorim group companies challenged the penalty and a price reconsideration order in court, and the request to suspend the price order was granted.

The final payment and amount of the fine may change depending on litigation. The affiliate Moorim P&P's operating loss widened to KRW 10.8 billion in the first half of 2026 from KRW 10.3 billion a year earlier, and Newspim named that affiliate's results as a key variable for the second half.

The 2026 annual report, due by the usual filing deadline, will be the first full disclosure of fourth-quarter 2026 results and the final reflection of the fine.

08

Bull factors

Operating profit recovery in the second quarter

Operating profit rose to KRW 28 billion in the second quarter of 2026 from KRW 159 million in the first quarter, and sales reached KRW 337.7 billion. Reports tied the improvement partly to strong North American exports and a weaker won.

Integrated pulp-and-paper model benefits from a pulp rebound

A Korean credit rating agency expected 2026 operating profitability to improve year on year, factoring in a pulp rebound and higher utilization. This view depends on the direction of raw material prices.

Planned cost savings from the recovery boiler

The company expects that the new eco-friendly recovery boiler could cut costs by about KRW 50 billion a year on an EBITDA basis once fully running. EBITDA fell to KRW 95.5 billion in 2025 from KRW 184.4 billion in 2024, so whether this saving materializes is an important test. The savings figure is the company's own estimate.

Bear factors

Five straight quarters of shareholder net losses

Shareholder net losses have run for five straight quarters from the third quarter of 2025 through the third quarter of 2026, totaling KRW 77.9 billion over the last four quarters. The third quarter of 2026 brought an operating loss of KRW 15.5 billion, larger than a year earlier.

Operating profit has turned positive in only one or two quarters, which weighs on the case for a sustained recovery.

Regulatory fines and balance sheet strain

Reports say Moorim Paper was fined KRW 28.6 billion while affiliate Moorim P&P was fined KRW 57.5 billion. An April 2026 analysis said reflecting the combined fines would lift the debt ratio to 290.4%. The debt ratio was 270.6% at the end of 2025 and borrowings dependence was 61.1%.

Printing paper demand and fixed-cost pressure

FnGuide noted that printing paper demand has shrunk as digital technology spreads and the domestic market has stalled. A Korean credit rating agency said weak printing paper supply and higher fixed costs such as labor would limit the earnings recovery. Even if pulp prices rebound, demand-side constraints remain a bearish factor.

Risk factors

Legal and regulatory risk

The Moorim group companies challenged the fine and a price reconsideration order, and the request to suspend the price order was granted. The final payment and amount of the fine depend on the litigation outcome. The financial burden could still change depending on how these cases are resolved.

Raw material and logistics risk

Pulp prices were the main cause of the 2025 earnings decline, and in the third quarter of 2026 higher prices for some raw materials coincided with lower printing paper selling prices. Shipping costs and oil price volatility linked to Middle East tensions were cited by a company official as external uncertainties. A rating agency also pointed to higher wood chip prices as a cost burden.

Financial and credit risk

In April 2026 a Korean credit rating agency lowered the outlook on Moorim Paper's senior unsecured bonds to negative from stable. Reports said interest expense exceeded KRW 40 billion in 2025, and analysts said operating profit struggled to cover financing costs. Industry observers warned that a further downgrade could raise borrowing costs and deepen the financial burden.

Overall view

Moorim Paper's operating profit fell to KRW 5.95 billion in 2025 and it swung to a shareholder net loss of KRW 15.4 billion, with shareholder losses running for five straight quarters through the third quarter of 2026.

Operating profit of KRW 28 billion in the second quarter of 2026 signaled a recovery, but the shareholder net loss widened to KRW 39.5 billion that quarter as the fine was reflected, and the third quarter returned to an operating loss of KRW 15.5 billion.

Bullish factors include the profit upside from the integrated pulp-and-paper model if pulp prices rebound, the planned cost savings from the recovery boiler, and a rating agency's expectation of improved operating profitability in 2026.

Bearish factors include stagnant printing paper demand, fixed-cost pressure, a debt ratio of 270.6% and borrowings dependence of 61.1%, and uncertainty from the fine litigation. The price-to-book ratio sits well below 1x, but with losses across the last four quarters no earnings-based multiple can be computed.

The next checkpoints are the 2026 annual report and how the fines are reflected there, and whether the operating margin improves in the first-quarter 2027 report. The litigation outcome and any change in the credit rating outlook remain key variables for the balance sheet.
