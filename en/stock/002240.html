Kiswire: Earnings Recovery After Impairment Hit

02

Business structure

Kiswire, founded in 1945, is Korea's largest specialty wire rope and high-carbon steel wire producer, organized around two main divisions: Rope and Wire.

In the first quarter of 2026, external customer revenue was split between the Rope division at KRW 87,688 million (20.3%), the Wire division at KRW 319,745 million (73.9%), and other operations at KRW 25,277 million (5.8%).

The Rope division produces wire rope (W/R) and related products supplied to shipbuilding, crane, and construction applications, while the Wire division produces steel wire (S/W), PC steel strand, and bead wire (B/W) mainly supplied to automotive tire and parts makers.

Operating income for the same quarter was KRW 3,156 million for Rope, KRW 559 million for Wire, and KRW 3,330 million for other operations, totaling KRW 7,045 million. Notably, the Wire division, despite generating the largest share of revenue, contributed the smallest share of operating income.

The company estimates its domestic market share at roughly 56% in Rope and 49% in Wire as of the first quarter of 2026, keeping both divisions in leading industry positions. Utilization rates for the same quarter stood at a relatively high 92.8% for Rope and 95.2% for Wire.

Because a large share of output is exported, currency movements and the performance of overseas production subsidiaries significantly affect consolidated results. Within the broader Kiswire group of affiliates, the company serves as the core listed entity.

04

Earnings analysis

Annual revenue declined from KRW 2.07 trillion in 2022 to KRW 1.79 trillion in 2023 (down 13.7%) and KRW 1.74 trillion in 2024 (down 2.8%), before rebounding to KRW 1.81 trillion in 2025 (up 4.0%).

Operating profit peaked at KRW 133.5 billion in 2022, fell to KRW 56.3 billion in 2023 (down 57.8%) and KRW 26.1 billion in 2024 (down 53.6%), then improved to KRW 40.7 billion in 2025 (up 56.0%).

The company attributed the operating profit increase to higher selling prices at domestic and overseas units that lifted revenue, while explaining that net income declined due to recognition of a property, plant and equipment impairment.

Net income attributable to controlling interests indeed fell for three consecutive years, from KRW 81 billion in 2022 to KRW 71.5 billion in 2023 (down 11.8%), KRW 33.7 billion in 2024 (down 52.9%), and KRW 9.61 billion in 2025 (down 71.5%), while net income including non-controlling interests shrank from KRW 92.3 billion in 2022 to KRW 4.6 billion in 2025.

The PP&E impairment recognized during fiscal 2025 totaled approximately KRW 154.7 billion, concentrated mostly in machinery.

By quarter, controlling-interest net profit swung to a loss of KRW 25.9 billion in the fourth quarter of 2025, then turned profitable at KRW 23.5 billion in the first quarter of 2026 and continued improving to KRW 29.1 billion in the second quarter (up 24.1% quarter-on-quarter, up 431.6% year-on-year).

Operating profit fell from KRW 20.4 billion in the second quarter of 2025 to KRW 11.2 billion in the third quarter (down 45.1%) and KRW 871 million in the fourth quarter (down 92.2%), rebounded to KRW 7.04 billion in the first quarter of 2026 (up 709.2%), then eased again to KRW 5.34 billion in the second quarter (down 24.2% quarter-on-quarter, down 73.8% year-on-year).

The trailing four-quarter sum of controlling-interest net profit (third quarter of 2025 through second quarter of 2026) was KRW 48.6 billion, maintaining a profitable trend despite sharp quarterly swings.

Operating cash flow rose from KRW 108.7 billion in 2022 to KRW 182.4 billion in 2023, then eased to KRW 123.3 billion in 2024 and KRW 117 billion in 2025, remaining consistently positive and offsetting much of the impact of non-cash impairment charges.

05

Industry analysis

In 2025, Korea's wire industry struggled with weak downstream demand and growing Chinese imports, though wire rope demand rebounded and performed relatively well even as Chinese import share in the domestic wire rope market exceeded 40%, squeezing domestic sales.

The industry expects that even if construction activity partially recovers in 2026, overall wire rope production and sales will decline as shipbuilding output falls roughly 10% year-on-year.

By contrast, Korea's three major shipbuilders posted record-high combined operating profit in the first quarter of 2026 on the back of strong order backlogs, with the shipbuilding sector described as entering its strongest upcycle in 13 years.

However, there is typically a lag between strong new orders and actual increases in vessel construction volume, so the impact on Rope division demand may not be immediate.

The tire industry, a key end market for the Wire division, is supported by a global tire cord market expected to grow at roughly 7% annually starting 2026, sustaining medium-term demand.

Competitively, Kiswire maintains leading domestic market shares in both Rope and Wire, but continues to face pricing pressure from lower-cost overseas suppliers.

06

Outlook

For 2026, Kiswire budgeted KRW 6.972 billion for new and expansion capital investment aimed at boosting capacity and the mix of higher value-added products, having executed KRW 3.522 billion through the first quarter.

On the dividend front, the company decided on a fiscal 2025 year-end cash dividend totaling KRW 9.444 billion (a dividend yield of 1.7% at the time of announcement).

The 2025 payout ratio rose to 98.3% due to the sharp decline in net income, with the total dividend amount edging up from KRW 8.746 billion paid for fiscal 2024.

In addition, the company stated in a March 2026 value-up plan that it would pursue a dividend policy aligned with the high-dividend company criteria under the Restriction of Special Taxation Act—either a 40% payout ratio, or a 25% payout ratio combined with a 10% year-on-year increase in total dividend payments.

The company also passed an amendment to its articles of incorporation at its annual general meeting establishing a legal basis for interim dividends, reflecting changes to the Commercial Act. The next scheduled regulatory filing is the third-quarter report, due November 16, 2026.

Given the business's sensitivity to currency movements and the performance of overseas subsidiaries, the sustainability of the earnings recovery will likely depend significantly on the normalization of profitability at those overseas production units.

Bull factors

Improving Operating Profit Trend

In 2025, revenue rose to KRW 1.81 trillion (up 4.0%) and operating profit rose to KRW 40.7 billion (up 56.0%), breaking a two-year decline. Controlling-interest net profit stayed positive for two straight quarters in 2026 at KRW 23.5 billion and KRW 29.1 billion.

The trailing four-quarter sum of controlling-interest net profit reached KRW 48.6 billion, well above the full-year 2025 figure of KRW 9.61 billion.

Solid Balance Sheet and Stronger Shareholder Return Policy

The debt-to-equity ratio declined from 46.6% in 2022 to 35.6% in 2025, remaining at a stable level. Operating cash flow consistently exceeded KRW 100 billion each year. In March 2026, the company outlined a strengthened dividend policy direction based on the high-dividend company criteria as part of a value-up plan.

Dominant Domestic Position in Both Rope and Wire

As of the first quarter of 2026, the company's estimated domestic market share was about 56% in Rope and 49% in Wire, keeping both divisions in leading positions. Utilization rates were also relatively high at 92.8% for Rope and 95.2% for Wire, indicating room for production efficiency.

The medium-term growth outlook for the global tire cord market is also supportive of demand for the Wire division.

Bear factors

Net Income Volatility from PP&E Impairment

2025 net income collapsed year-on-year to KRW 4.6 billion, and controlling-interest net profit fell for a third straight year to KRW 9.61 billion. The company cited impairment recognition as a key cause, with total PP&E impairment during fiscal 2025 reaching approximately KRW 154.7 billion. If such non-recurring losses recur, net income volatility could widen again even as operating profit improves.

High Quarter-to-Quarter Earnings Volatility

Operating profit swung widely, falling from KRW 20.4 billion in the second quarter of 2025 to KRW 871 million in the fourth quarter, jumping to KRW 7.04 billion in the first quarter of 2026, then falling again to KRW 5.34 billion in the second quarter.

Controlling-interest net profit posted a loss of KRW 25.9 billion in the fourth quarter of 2025 before returning to profitability only in 2026. This volatility is largely attributed to the structure's sensitivity to overseas subsidiary performance and one-off items.

Rising Low-Cost Chinese Supply and Shipbuilding Output Concerns

Chinese import share in the domestic wire rope market has exceeded 40%, continuing to erode domestic sales. For 2026, the industry expects overall wire rope production and sales to decline as shipbuilding output falls roughly 10% year-on-year, even if construction demand partially recovers.

While shipbuilders' order backlogs remain healthy, the lag before this translates into actual construction volume means the pace of Rope division demand recovery could be slow.

10

Risk factors

Currency and Overseas Subsidiary Risk

Both the Wire and Rope divisions have high export exposure and operate numerous overseas production subsidiaries, so currency fluctuations and the performance of those subsidiaries significantly affect consolidated results.

In the past, declining sales at domestic and overseas units have been cited as a cause of weak annual performance. If currency moves unfavorably or overseas demand weakens, consolidated profitability could deteriorate again.

Downstream Demand Risk

The Rope division depends heavily on shipbuilding and construction, while the Wire division depends on the automotive tire industry, exposing the company directly to cyclical swings in those end markets.

With 2026 shipbuilding output projected to decline year-on-year and the pace of construction recovery uncertain, both revenue and utilization could be affected if downstream demand recovers more slowly than expected.

Risk of Recurring Asset Impairment

During fiscal 2025, an impairment of approximately KRW 154.7 billion was recognized on PP&E, mainly machinery, sharply reducing net income.

If overseas production subsidiaries fail to normalize profitability or equipment efficiency declines further, additional asset impairment cannot be ruled out, which could keep widening net income volatility even as operating profit improves.

12

Overall view

Kiswire saw both revenue and operating profit rise in 2025, breaking a two-year decline, but net income and controlling-interest net profit moved in the opposite direction, falling sharply due to a PP&E impairment charge.

Entering 2026, controlling-interest net profit stayed positive for two consecutive quarters, lifting the trailing four-quarter total to KRW 48.6 billion, though operating profit itself softened again in the second quarter, underscoring continued quarter-to-quarter volatility.

Structurally, both the Rope and Wire divisions retain leading domestic market positions, but face common pressures from low-cost Chinese imports, cyclicality in downstream shipbuilding and construction demand, and volatility in overseas subsidiary earnings.

On the balance sheet side, the company maintains a stable base, with the debt-to-equity ratio down to the mid-30% range and consistently positive operating cash flow.

While the company outlined a strengthened dividend policy direction in 2026 based on high-dividend company criteria, the fact that the 2025 payout ratio spiked to 98.3% amid the net income collapse is worth monitoring when assessing future dividend sustainability.

The next quarterly filing (due November 16, 2026), the risk of further impairment charges, and the actual pace of demand recovery in shipbuilding and construction will likely be key variables shaping the earnings trajectory ahead.
