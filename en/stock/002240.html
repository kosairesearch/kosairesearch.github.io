Wire Leader Faces a Profit Recovery Test

02

Business structure

Kiswire makes wire rope and specialty wire, and its business is split into two segments, wire rod and wire rope. As of the end of September 2023, the revenue mix was 36.4% wire rope, 58.1% wire rod and 5.5% other.

A 2025 press report instead put wire rod at more than 70% of total sales, so the mix depends on the period and the basis used, and the latest segment revenue should be checked in the periodic filings.

Among wire rod products, bead wire and steel cord used as tire reinforcement form the core, and the company classifies this business under its rubber segment internally. The wire rope segment serves industrial demand from construction, bridges and energy.

Kiswire is described as the domestic leader in production capacity and sales of specialty wire. As of the end of the third quarter of 2025, about 86% of total sales came from exports, with the United States, Europe, China and Japan cited as key markets.

Its share of the domestic wire rod market is about 44%, which is effectively an oligopoly. In 2024 the company sold KRW 54.2 billion of products to the Swiss joint venture VEROPE AG. A KOSDAQ-listed affiliate that makes Cu-Fe bimetal wire listed in June 2025.

04

Earnings analysis

Based on the annual figures in the periodic filings, sales were KRW 2.07 trillion in 2022, fell to KRW 1.79 trillion in 2023 (down 13.7% year on year) and KRW 1.74 trillion in 2024 (down 2.8%), then rose to KRW 1.81 trillion in 2025 (up 4.0%). 2022 was the largest year and 2024 the smallest.

Operating profit went from KRW 133.5 billion in 2022 to KRW 56.3 billion in 2023 (down 57.8%) and KRW 26.1 billion in 2024 (down 53.6%), then recovered to KRW 40.7 billion in 2025 (up 56.0%).

The operating margin was 6.4% in 2022, 3.1% in 2023, 1.5% in 2024 and 2.3% in 2025, so the 2025 profit rebound did not bring margins back to 2022 levels.

Kiswire's 2022 improvement was temporary, as a COVID base effect and stronger sales to the United States and Europe lifted results before profits again fell short of market expectations.

Profit attributable to controlling shareholders fell from KRW 81 billion in 2022 to KRW 71.5 billion in 2023 (down 11.8%), KRW 33.7 billion in 2024 (down 52.9%) and KRW 9.61 billion in 2025 (down 71.5%), declining for three straight years from 2023.

Net income including non-controlling interests was KRW 4.6 billion in 2025, a different figure, and the gap reflects non-controlling interests. Operating cash flow was KRW 108.7 billion in 2022, KRW 182.4 billion in 2023, KRW 123.3 billion in 2024 and KRW 117 billion in 2025, above KRW 100 billion each year.

Quarterly sales were KRW 441.5 billion in the third quarter of 2025, KRW 449.4 billion in the fourth quarter (up 1.8% quarter on quarter), KRW 432.7 billion in the first quarter of 2026 (down 3.7%), KRW 483.1 billion in the second quarter (up 11.7%) and KRW 461.8 billion in the third quarter of 2026 (down 4.4% quarter on quarter, up 4.6% year on year).

Operating profit fell from KRW 11.2 billion in the third quarter of 2025 to KRW 871 million in the fourth quarter (down 92.2%), then recovered to KRW 7.04 billion in the first quarter of 2026 and KRW 5.34 billion in the second quarter, before an operating loss of KRW 3.99 billion in the third quarter of 2026.

Profit attributable to controlling shareholders was a KRW 25.9 billion loss in the fourth quarter of 2025, then KRW 23.5 billion and KRW 29.1 billion profits in the first and second quarters of 2026, and a KRW 24.5 billion loss in the third quarter of 2026. The last four quarters together total KRW 2.13 billion. The causes of the quarterly swings should be read from the segment detail in the periodic reports.

05

Industry analysis

The wire market Kiswire serves is effectively an oligopoly in Korea. According to a Maeil Business report, its share of the domestic wire rod market is about 44%. Because the center of gravity is overseas rather than domestic, export results are a useful gauge of the industry cycle.

Wire rod export sales, before intragroup eliminations, were KRW 1.27 trillion in 2022, KRW 1.03 trillion in 2023, KRW 979.4 billion in 2024 and KRW 1.02 trillion for the first three quarters of 2025, staying near the KRW 1 trillion level.

In its 2023 results commentary, the company said that global economic uncertainty and oversupply in steel made a sharp market recovery hard to expect. A Bloter headline framed Chinese low-price competition as hitting export markets.

On trade policy, a 2026 April market report said steel shares rose on expectations for a US tariff revision, with Kiswire up 8% that day. Within steel, Kiswire combines specialty wire and wire rope production, the largest domestic capacity by the company's own account.

Its exposure to price competition from Chinese producers and to swings in tire reinforcement demand tied to auto output remains. Whether wire rod pricing or export volume drove margins since 2022 can be traced in the segment profit data of the periodic filings.

08

Bull factors

Domestic oligopoly base

A domestic wire rod share of about 44% is a strength in the production base. Producing tire reinforcement and industrial wire rope together widens the business scope. Still, the operating margin has declined since 2022, so market share has not translated directly into profitability.

Profits in early 2026

Operating profit was KRW 7.04 billion in the first quarter of 2026 and KRW 5.34 billion in the second. Profit attributable to controlling shareholders turned positive at KRW 23.5 billion in the first quarter and rose to KRW 29.1 billion in the second, up 24.1% quarter on quarter.

The third-quarter operating loss interrupts this, so whether the early-2026 pattern continues is the open question.

Financial capacity

Total equity at the end of 2025 was KRW 1.96 trillion, and the debt ratio was 35.6%, down from 46.6% in 2022. Operating cash flow was KRW 117 billion in 2025 and above KRW 100 billion each year. Cash generation holding up while profit shrank is cited as a financial strength.

09

Bear factors

Margin compression and a loss

The operating margin fell from 6.4% in 2022 to 3.1% in 2023 and 1.5% in 2024, then edged up to 2.3% in 2025. An operating loss of KRW 3.99 billion in the third quarter of 2026 shows the margin recovery has not held quarter to quarter. Profit attributable to controlling shareholders declined for three straight years from 2023.

Price competition

After a temporary improvement in 2022, results have consistently fallen short of market expectations, according to press coverage. Reports also point to Chinese low-price competition pressuring export markets. If steel oversupply persists, room to raise selling prices may stay limited.

Export and affiliate concentration

About 86% of sales came from exports as of the end of the third quarter of 2025. Sales to specific overseas and affiliated buyers are also material, and those transactions are estimated at about 16% of standalone sales. A change in buyer volumes or exchange rates could widen swings in results.

Risk factors

Ownership and governance

As of October 16, 2026, the affiliate's stake stood at 10.06%. The affiliate committed to selling down its stake in stages within five years as part of its listing review. Sales could affect the tradable share count, so ownership-change filings should be tracked. The bonus issue will also raise total shares outstanding to 30 million.

Exchange rate and tariff exposure

With about 86% of sales from exports, exchange rate and tariff policy changes affect sales and margins directly. Steel shares rallied in April 2026 on tariff revision hopes, but policy can change, so this remains a source of volatility. Overseas subsidiary results and export pricing should be tracked each quarter.

Cost pass-through and margins

If raw material cost increases are not passed through to selling prices in time, margins narrow further. With the operating margin at 2.3%, a delay in cost pass-through could widen losses.

Whether the third-quarter 2026 operating loss stemmed from cost factors should be confirmed in the detailed items of the periodic report.

12

Overall view

Kiswire has a large share of the domestic specialty wire market, an export-driven business, and a financial base of a 35.6% debt ratio and KRW 1.96 trillion in total equity.

On the other side, the operating margin fell from 6.4% in 2022 to 2.3% in 2025, and profit attributable to controlling shareholders declined for three straight years.

The profits in the first and second quarters of 2026 can be read as a recovery signal, but whether the third-quarter operating loss is temporary or a trend is hard to tell from quarterly data alone.

Strengths include the oligopoly-like share and financial capacity, while weaknesses include margin compression and export and price-competition pressure. On the ownership side, the staged sale by the affiliate and the share count change from the bonus issue are items to track.
