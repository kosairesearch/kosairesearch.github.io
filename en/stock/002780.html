02

Business structure

Chinhung International is a general contractor that wins projects from clients and recognizes revenue as construction progresses.

Its business spans housing, urban redevelopment, public civil engineering and industrial facilities, and reported revenue is grouped into public civil works, public building works and private building works.

In the nine months through Q3 2025, private building revenue fell from KRW 401.5 billion to KRW 238.2 billion, the main reason total revenue declined, while public civil works and public building works rose 1.12% and 54.30%.

Private building work includes redevelopment and reconstruction, officetels, mixed-use towers and apartment contracts. Largest shareholder Hyosung Heavy Industries is both an affiliate and a major contract counterparty, with a KRW 49.37 billion factory contract in Changwon signed in October 2025 as a typical example.

In urban redevelopment, Seoul small-scale housing projects stand out, with new urban redevelopment awards of about KRW 422 billion as of October 2026. The company uses the Harrington brand for residential projects.

In public works it is shifting from comprehensive bidding toward technical bidding, and a May 2026 report cited about KRW 80 billion added from an Incheon road turnkey project. New awards totaled KRW 1,152.2 billion in 2024.

Among construction peers, Chinhung is a mid-sized contractor smaller than the large builders, and it is expanding public, industrial and redevelopment work to reduce reliance on housing sales.

04

Earnings analysis

Annual revenue and profit moved in different directions. Revenue rose from KRW 628.9 billion in 2022 to KRW 759.4 billion in 2023, up 20.7%, then fell 4.4% to KRW 726.2 billion in 2024 and 20.6% to KRW 576.4 billion in 2025. Among 2022 to 2025, 2023 was the largest year and 2025 the smallest.

Operating profit held at KRW 50.7 billion in 2022 and KRW 51.7 billion in 2023, up 2.1%, before turning to an operating loss of KRW 4.75 billion in 2024. The 2025 operating loss widened to KRW 23 billion, and 2024 and 2025 were consecutive years of operating losses.

Operating margin fell from 8.1% in 2022 and 6.8% in 2023 to -0.7% in 2024 and -4.0% in 2025. Owner net income fell 8.6% to KRW 45.3 billion in 2023 and 95.2% to KRW 2.18 billion in 2024, then became a KRW 28.4 billion loss in 2025. Net income in 2025 was a loss of KRW 28.4 billion, the same figure as the owner loss.

In quarterly terms, revenue in Q4 2025 reached KRW 187 billion, up 18.9% from the prior quarter, yet the company posted an operating loss of KRW 26.7 billion. Q1 2026 revenue was KRW 183.6 billion with operating profit of KRW 11.1 billion, a swing back to profit.

Q2 2026 operating profit was KRW 507 million, down 95.4% from the prior quarter. Q3 2026 revenue reached KRW 212 billion, up 34.8% year on year, and operating profit was KRW 4.7 billion, up 107.4% year on year, with owner net income of KRW 14.6 billion.

The owner net loss over the latest four quarters totaled KRW 2.03 billion. The Q1 improvement was attributed to revenue recognition from redevelopment and public works awarded in the prior year.

For Q3, the company said early completion settlement at a Uijeongbu mixed-use project and cost-reduction activities contributed. Such settlement effects tend to be one-off, so whether the profit level holds in later quarters needs separate checking.

Operating cash flow was negative at KRW -53.6 billion in 2024 and KRW -26.3 billion in 2025, two consecutive years of outflows. The debt ratio stood at 133.7% at end-2025, above 102.6% a year earlier.

05

Industry analysis

The domestic construction sector faces a combination of a slowing housing market, rising construction costs and a weaker project financing market. A June 2026 report said mid-sized builders are seeking steady work outside housing, including public projects, industrial facilities and urban redevelopment.

Chinhung has moved in line with this trend by winning power infrastructure and industrial facility contracts. HVDC technology is used for long-distance, high-capacity power transmission, and investment in related equipment is rising with the spread of AI data centers and renewable energy.

In urban redevelopment, small-scale housing projects in older low-rise neighborhoods in Seoul are increasing, and the company said it will select new sites based on project feasibility and profitability. Government plans to expand corporate rental housing have also been cited as a possible policy tailwind.

On the other hand, a 2025 report pointed to weak sales in the private housing market. Because private sales risk has not clearly disappeared, the effect of reducing the share of housing sales on earnings stability is worth watching.

In competition, Chinhung is contesting technical public bids and redevelopment awards alongside other mid-sized builders. As mid-sized companies pursue similar strategies to move away from housing-heavy models, competition for awards is intensifying.

The pace of sector recovery and the intensity of bidding competition are the external variables that will shape this company's order performance.

06

Outlook

The company's target is KRW 1 trillion in new contract awards. At a January 2026 partner event it set a goal of reaching KRW 1 trillion in new awards for a third consecutive year, and by Q3 2026 cumulative awards exceeded KRW 900 billion by the company's own tally.

Backlog at the end of Q3 is estimated in the high KRW 3 trillion range. Disclosed additions include the KRW 332.3 billion Daesa-dong 1st District redevelopment in September and the KRW 85.2 billion Myeongjang-dong small-scale housing project in October.

New sites that started construction include Yeosu Hakdong and Busan Oncheon-dong mixed-use projects and the Changwon HVDC factory, while the Seongnam Jung-1 redevelopment and Yeongju Hyucheon-dong residential project are progressing as existing sites.

The Daesa-dong 1st District covers 1,080 households, with a contract period ending on the last day of the 40th month from actual construction start. The Jeongneung-dong small-scale housing project covers 209 households with a construction period of 32 months from actual start.

Whether a higher share of progress-billing projects, as the company has described, supports cash flow stability is the next point to watch. Q3 2026 operating margin was 2.2%, improved from about 0.2% in Q2, and the preliminary nine-month operating margin through September 2026 was 2.6%.

Whether the margin holds at this level in the next annual report, and how fast backlog converts to revenue, will be the benchmarks for the earnings trend. Cost control on large new sites and the durability of settlement effects also merit attention.

Bull factors

Order Recovery and Large Contracts

A KRW 332.3 billion Daesa-dong 1st District redevelopment contract was disclosed in September 2026. By the company's own tally, cumulative awards through Q3 2026 exceeded KRW 900 billion. Conversion of the order backlog into revenue is the basis for the bullish case on revenue continuity.

Quarterly Profit Recovery

Operating profit turned positive in Q1 2026 at KRW 11.1 billion, and Q3 operating profit reached KRW 4.7 billion, up 107.4% year on year. Q3 operating margin rose to 2.2% from about 0.2% in Q2. Compared with the operating loss of KRW 26.7 billion in Q4 2025, the direction of quarterly profit has changed.

Diversification and Possible Policy Tailwinds

Awards are broadening into public technical bids, redevelopment and power infrastructure such as HVDC. A report noted possible policy benefits from the government's plan to expand corporate rental housing. The company is also raising the share of progress-billing projects, which is cited as relevant to cash flow.

Bear factors

Annual Losses and Earnings Volatility

Operating losses were KRW 4.75 billion in 2024 and KRW 23 billion in 2025, two consecutive years of operating losses, and the 2025 owner net loss was KRW 28.4 billion. After an operating loss of KRW 26.7 billion in Q4 2025, Q2 2026 operating profit fell to KRW 507 million. Large quarterly swings mean whether profitability persists still needs confirmation.

Revenue Decline and Cash Outflows

Revenue fell from KRW 759.4 billion in 2023 to KRW 576.4 billion in 2025, a 20.6% decline in 2025. The debt ratio rose to 133.7% at end-2025 from 102.6% in 2024.

Contingent Liabilities and Weak Sales Burden

As of end-Q3 2025, PF contingent liabilities totaled 15 cases worth KRW 1,732.5 billion. At the same point, 11 defect-related lawsuits with the company as defendant carried claims of KRW 26.8 billion. A 2025 report flagged weak housing sales and selling and administrative expense pressure as challenges.

10

Risk factors

Ownership and Largest Shareholder

Largest shareholder Hyosung Heavy Industries holds a 48.19% stake, and a possible sale has been repeatedly raised. In a May 2026 report, the company said its largest shareholder is conducting various strategic reviews but no specific decision has been finalized.

If an ownership change materializes, it could affect contract relationships and market assessment, so disclosure developments need monitoring.

Cost and Settlement Volatility

The company said early settlement at the Uijeongbu mixed-use project contributed to Q3 operating profit. Settlement gains are largely one-off and may not recur in later quarters. As the Q2 2026 operating profit of KRW 507 million shows, project-level cost control can shift quarterly profit sharply.

Dependence on Large Awards and Project Execution

With large sites such as Daesa-dong 1st District starting construction, execution progress and completion schedules will determine the pace of revenue recognition. The company plans to increase progress-billing work, but some small-scale housing projects are on a sale-based payment structure. The speed of backlog conversion and any construction delays are the next items to watch.

Overall view

Chinhung International's revenue has declined for two consecutive years since peaking in 2023, and it recorded operating losses in both 2024 and 2025. In 2026, quarterly profit recovered from KRW 11.1 billion operating profit in Q1 to KRW 4.7 billion in Q3.

On the order side, large projects such as the KRW 332.3 billion Daesa-dong 1st District redevelopment were disclosed in September 2026, and cumulative awards by the company's count exceeded KRW 900 billion.

On the other hand, the debt ratio rose to 133.7% at end-2025, and operating cash flow was negative in both 2024 and 2025. The price-to-book ratio sits in a range well below net asset value, while PER cannot be calculated due to the net loss over the latest four quarters.

The largest shareholder's sale review, one-off settlement effects and contingent liabilities cut both ways in the outlook. The next points to check are whether full-year 2026 operating profit closes in the black and the debt ratio and cash flow in the 2026 annual report.
