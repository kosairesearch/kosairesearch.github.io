02

Business structure

Piolink, founded in 2000, makes network and security equipment, centered on ADC (application delivery controllers) that distribute server load, and also sells web application firewalls, cloud-managed security switches (TiFRONT), hyper-converged infrastructure (HCI) and security services.

The company says it has held the top position in domestic ADC for nearly a decade (ZDNet Korea interview, July 2025). For the second quarter of 2026, the revenue mix was 63% network equipment and 37% security services and maintenance, according to Butler's company data.

A JobKorea tally as of March 2026 showed 61% and 39%, so the split varies with each data provider's classification. Customers are split roughly 60:40 between public and private sectors (ZDNet Korea), and Mirae Asset Securities' April 2026 field note put the public sector at about 60% of revenue.

Because public tenders are a large share, budget timing and large project cycles directly drive quarterly results. Overseas sales are concentrated in Japan: the company said about 90% of 2024 overseas sales came from Japan, and overseas revenue reached KRW 10.1 billion in 2025 (Mirae Asset Securities).

At its shareholder meeting, the company said HCI revenue rose from roughly KRW 800 million in 2024 to about KRW 3.0 billion in 2025. The zero-trust product TiFRONT ZT was also shown at a 2026 event.

On competition, the company argues that most of its rivals are overseas vendors, which gives it an edge in domestic public projects (shareholder meeting, March 2026).

04

Earnings analysis

The disclosed annual figures show revenue and profit moving in different directions. Revenue went from KRW 61.6 billion in 2022 to KRW 59 billion in 2023 (down 4.2% year on year) and KRW 58.3 billion in 2024 (down 1.2%), then recovered to KRW 65.6 billion in 2025 (up 12.6%), making 2025 the largest year of 2022–2025.

Operating profit fell from KRW 11.4 billion in 2022 to KRW 9 billion in 2023 (down 21.1%) and KRW 2.59 billion in 2024 (down 71.2%), the smallest year, before rebounding to KRW 5.4 billion in 2025 (up 108.4%). Operating margin moved from 18.5% in 2022 to 15.3%, 4.4% and 8.2% in 2025.

Net income attributable to controlling shareholders fell from KRW 12.1 billion in 2022 to KRW 5.41 billion in 2024 and rose to KRW 7.52 billion in 2025 (up 39.1%). Net income including non-controlling interests moved in the same direction.

For the nine months of 2025, the company attributed the lower operating profit to upfront spending on people and R&D for future businesses such as HCI and zero trust (November 2025 disclosure coverage).

For the 2025 recovery, the company said overseas and security businesses were the main drivers (February 2026 disclosure coverage). At year-end 2025, total equity was KRW 84.8 billion, the debt ratio was 17.8%, and operating cash flow was KRW 11 billion. Quarterly results swung far more.

Revenue of KRW 14.7 billion and operating profit of KRW 521 million in the third quarter of 2025 became revenue of KRW 24.2 billion and operating profit of KRW 6.44 billion in the fourth quarter (up 1,136.2% from the prior quarter).

First-quarter 2026 revenue was KRW 12.8 billion (down 47.1% from the prior quarter), with an operating loss of KRW 1.48 billion. Second-quarter 2026 revenue was KRW 15.8 billion with an operating loss of KRW 132 million, a narrower loss.

Third-quarter 2026 revenue was KRW 18.8 billion (up 27.7% year on year) and operating profit was KRW 2.6 billion (up 398.8% year on year), a return to profit. Revenue rose for two consecutive quarters from the second quarter to the third quarter of 2026.

Net income attributable to controlling shareholders over the last four quarters, from the fourth quarter of 2025 to the third quarter of 2026, totaled KRW 7.46 billion. The first-quarter 2026 net loss attributable to controlling shareholders of KRW 1.31 billion also shows how volatile quarterly profit has been.

05

Industry analysis

Demand for security and network equipment is supported by a steady stream of cyber intrusions. In 2025, several companies and public bodies suffered system hacks and large personal-data leaks, and the industry read this as a potential earnings driver for information-security companies (News Works, 2025).

On the public side, demand for disaster recovery (DR) rose after the 2025 fire at the National Information Resources Service data center.

Management said its equipment was in the burned facility, so replacement demand came first, and it expects further DR-related revenue as the government builds out DR systems (shareholder meeting, March 2026). In virtualization, changes in foreign solutions are opening demand for domestic alternatives.

Companies and public agencies have reviewed foreign virtualization infrastructure as licensing and business-policy changes raised costs (Edaily, August 2026). The company also said it is targeting the gap left after VMware, the market leader, was acquired by Broadcom (shareholder meeting, March 2026).

Public projects also move in cycles. According to the company, school-network projects have historically surged in five-year rounds and then declined after completion, and it believes 2026 is the point where that cycle returns.

Taken together, the sequence so far is a low in 2024, recovery in 2025, and expectations for large project restarts in 2026. On competition, the argument is that most rivals are overseas vendors, which the company says helps domestic players in public procurement.

Companies such as Ahnlab and Samsung SDS operate with different business mixes, so direct comparisons are limited. Whether domestic substitution demand converts into orders will show up in company disclosures and contract sizes.

06

Outlook

Management is optimistic about a recovery in 2026. At the March 2026 shareholder meeting, it said the fifth phase of the school-network project would restart and lift revenue significantly, and that it expects growth in line with last year. It set a target of doubling HCI revenue in 2026.

First-half 2026 revenue was KRW 28.6 billion, up 7% year on year, and the company said some public budget execution and tenders were delayed by the regional elections in the first half (announced August 2026).

The company expects related projects to normalize from the third quarter, with large public and private projects to follow. Third-quarter 2026 revenue did rise to KRW 18.8 billion, and operating profit turned to KRW 2.6 billion.

Because quarterly results move with public tender timing, the key point to watch is when the fifth-phase school-network work and DR budgets are recognized as revenue. Overseas, the company is pursuing Japan exports alongside Southeast Asia expansion.

For 2025, it set a Southeast Asia revenue target 30% above the prior year (InsideVina, 2025). It also says it will strengthen market leadership in line with government plans to expand AI infrastructure investment (February 2026 coverage).

The targets management has given are mostly directional, such as doubling HCI, so they should be checked against confirmed annual results. The 2026 annual report, the next regular filing, will show whether confirmed revenue and operating margin match management's descriptions.

08

Bull factors

Restart of large public projects

Management said it expects revenue to rise as the fifth phase of the school-network project restarts and DR budgets are executed after the 2025 National Information Resources Service fire (shareholder meeting, March 2026).

First-half 2026 revenue of KRW 28.6 billion, up 7% year on year, is consistent with that direction. The bull case is that normalized public tenders could widen the revenue recovery after the third quarter of 2026.

Signs of margin recovery

According to a ThinkPool earnings flash, the operating margin in the fourth quarter of 2025 was 27%, up 11 percentage points from a year earlier. Operating profit of KRW 2.6 billion in the third quarter of 2026 marked a return to profit and rose 398.8% year on year. Annual operating margin recovered from 4.4% in 2024 to 8.2% in 2025.

Overseas and HCI growth

Per Mirae Asset Securities, overseas revenue rose 24.7% to KRW 10.1 billion in 2025, driven by Japan-centered exports. The company said HCI revenue rose to about KRW 3.0 billion in 2025 and set a target of doubling it in 2026. Rising demand for alternatives to foreign virtualization products is also a favorable trend for the company.

09

Bear factors

Quarterly losses and lumpy profit

Operating losses were KRW 1.48 billion in the first quarter of 2026 and KRW 132 million in the second, so the core business was loss-making in both first-half quarters. The return to profit in the third quarter is a single data point.

Fourth-quarter 2025 operating profit of KRW 6.44 billion exceeded the full-year 2025 figure of KRW 5.4 billion, which shows profit concentrated in certain quarters.

R&D burden and reliance on non-operating income

According to a News Works report from 2025, cumulative R&D expense for the first nine months was KRW 8.5 billion, more than 20% of revenue. The same report noted that the net profit at the time came from financial income and tax benefits despite an operating loss. It questioned whether the company can sustain this cost structure.

Concentration in domestic and public demand

Cumulative exports for the first nine months of 2025 were KRW 6.5 billion, about 16% of revenue, and more than 80% of sales were still domestic (News Works, 2025).

With the public sector at roughly 60% of revenue, delayed budget execution feeds directly into results, as the first-half 2026 tender delays around the regional elections showed.

10

Risk factors

Public budgets and tender timing

With roughly 60% of revenue from the public sector, quarterly results swing with budget execution and tender timing. In the first half of 2026, the company said some public tenders were delayed by the regional elections. The business has also repeatedly seen revenue fall after large projects end.

Profitability and cost structure

R&D and headcount investment make up a large fixed-cost base, so profit moves sharply when revenue dips. The KRW 1.48 billion operating loss in the first quarter of 2026 is an example. If HCI and zero-trust investments do not convert into revenue, the cost burden could persist.

Concentration in overseas markets

Overseas revenue is concentrated in Japan, so it is exposed to demand, regulation and currency shifts in a single market. Overseas share was about 16% on a cumulative basis for the first nine months of 2025, still a low proportion. Whether Southeast Asia targets translate into revenue still needs to be confirmed.

Overall view

Piolink recovered in 2025 after its 2024 operating-profit low, and its operating profit returned to a profit in the third quarter of 2026.

On a disclosed basis, revenue rose from KRW 61.6 billion in 2022 to KRW 65.6 billion in 2025, but operating profit fell from KRW 11.4 billion in 2022 to KRW 2.59 billion in 2024 before recovering to KRW 5.4 billion in 2025.

Bullish factors are the expected restart of public projects, growth in HCI and Japan exports, and improving quarterly profit since the fourth quarter of 2025.

Bearish factors are the dependence on public budgets and tender timing, loss-making quarters such as the first quarter of 2026, a heavy fixed-cost base for R&D, and a domestic-heavy revenue mix.

Valuation readings are mixed, with the multiple above its own three-year average but below peers, and earnings-based multiples are sensitive to the last four quarters.

Total equity of KRW 84.8 billion and a debt ratio of 17.8% point to a light debt burden, but operating cash flow moved from a loss in 2023 to KRW 11 billion in 2025, so the durability of cash generation also matters.

The next checkpoints are fourth-quarter 2026 results, the operating margin in the 2026 annual report, and when fifth-phase school-network and DR budgets are recognized as revenue.
