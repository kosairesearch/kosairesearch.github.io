02

Business structure

Dongwon Metal was listed on KOSPI on November 10, 1995, and operates in the manufacture of new automotive parts. It was founded in 1971 as a maker of vehicle body parts, and some records show it under the earlier name Dongwon Metal. Its main revenue comes from door frame products.

It supplies automakers on an OEM basis, with Hyundai Motor, KIA and KG Mobility among its customers. Revenue mix differs by source: one shows automotive parts at 95% and steel pipes at 5% on a first-quarter 2026 revenue basis, while another shows automotive parts at 80.40% and steel pipes at 19.60%.

Products are widening into body parts made by roll forming and battery base plates for hybrid and electric vehicles, with the SX2 EV and SG2 EV listed as applications.

Production has been reorganized around the Yesan plant, which began full operation in June 2025 after the Dunpo, Gunsan and Cheonan plants were consolidated into it. Overseas, MES systems were built at the Czech and Alabama, U.S. subsidiaries. R&D headcount was 46 as of June 2026.

By revenue, the company ranks 52nd among 137 listed automotive parts companies on an annual basis.

In a body parts market where large parts makers and mid-sized processors compete, its scale sits in the middle or below, and because sales track automaker production volumes, customer production plans strongly shape order volumes.

Earnings analysis

Annual revenue rose from KRW 538.6 billion in the fiscal year ended March 2023 to KRW 659.5 billion in the fiscal year ended March 2026, and it has grown for three consecutive years from the fiscal year ended March 2024 through the fiscal year ended March 2026.

Revenue of KRW 626.2 billion in the fiscal year ended March 2025 was up 10.2% year on year, and revenue in the fiscal year ended March 2026 rose 5.3%. The operating margin moved from 6.6% in the fiscal year ended March 2023 to 6.3%, 6.7% and then 5.3% in the fiscal year ended March 2026.

Operating profit of KRW 35 billion in the fiscal year ended March 2026 fell 16.6% and was the smallest of the four years, while KRW 42 billion in the fiscal year ended March 2025 was the largest.

Net profit attributable to owners reached KRW 39 billion in the fiscal year ended March 2026, up 84.5%, the largest of the four years. The gap between falling operating profit and rising net profit may reflect non-operating items and tax effects, which the annual report notes should be checked.

Operating cash flow rose from KRW 59.9 billion in the fiscal year ended March 2023 to KRW 107.7 billion in the fiscal year ended March 2026. The debt ratio fell from 443.8% to 254.9%, and at the end of March 2026 total liabilities were KRW 458.1 billion against total equity of KRW 179.7 billion.

Quarterly operating profit fell from KRW 8.45 billion in the third quarter of 2025 to KRW 6.06 billion in the first quarter of 2026, the low point, then rose for two consecutive quarters to KRW 14.4 billion in the second quarter and KRW 35.2 billion in the third quarter of 2026.

05

Industry analysis

Demand for automotive parts is tied to automakers' production and sales volumes, so the industry cycle moves with customers' production plans. Because the main customers are Hyundai Motor, KIA and KG Mobility, production adjustments by domestic automakers translate quickly into order volume changes.

On the eco-friendly vehicle side, battery frame parts for hybrid and electric vehicles align with the direction of the product portfolio. Demand for electric vehicles, however, depends on automakers' model plans and regional policies, so a single indicator is not enough to place the company on the cycle.

Within the sector, the company ranks 38th of 137 listed automotive parts companies by annual operating margin. In the same data set, the sector average operating margin is negative, which points to weak profitability across the industry.

The company's operating margin of 5.3% for the fiscal year ended March 2026 is above that average. In competition, large parts makers and regional processors coexist in structural body parts. As overseas plants take a larger share, local trade policy and exchange rate changes affect costs and profit more.

Bull factors

Operating profit recovery

Operating profit in the third quarter of 2026 was KRW 35.2 billion, up 316.5% from a year earlier. It has risen for two consecutive quarters since the first-quarter 2026 low, and this operating-level recovery is the main bullish point cited.

Eco-friendly vehicle parts portfolio

The company handles battery base plates for hybrid and electric vehicles alongside roll-formed body parts. Named electric vehicle applications connect the product line to actual production models, which supports the bullish case that electrification could expand demand for battery frame parts.

Balance sheet and cash generation

The debt ratio fell from 443.8% in the fiscal year ended March 2023 to 254.9% in the fiscal year ended March 2026. Operating cash flow rose from KRW 59.9 billion to KRW 107.7 billion over the same period, even as operating profit fell in the latest year, which is a cash generation strength. Whether lower borrowing dependence continues will determine how durable this strength is.

Bear factors

Supply is concentrated among automakers including Hyundai Motor, KIA and KG Mobility. Production changes or model discontinuations at customers pass directly into volume and profit. Operating profit fell 16.6% in the fiscal year ended March 2026, which can be read alongside this volume sensitivity as a bearish factor.

Weak quarterly net profit

Net profit attributable to owners in the third quarter of 2026 was KRW 5.78 billion, down 41.3% from a year earlier and 58.5% from the prior quarter. Operating profit rose sharply in the same quarter to KRW 35.2 billion, so the failure of the operating recovery to reach net profit is the bearish point.

Financial leverage and overseas guarantees

As overseas investment grows, guarantee exposure and borrowing burden rise together, which is the bearish side.

Risk factors

Customer production swings

A change in automaker production plans directly affects volume and utilization. With concentrated customers, a model discontinuation or production shift at one customer shows up in results at once.

Revenue rose 22.2% in the third quarter of 2026, yet net profit fell, showing that revenue growth does not always carry through to profit. Customer-level volume shares and production plan changes are the key points to monitor.

Overseas investment and guarantee burden

Production systems are in place at the Czech and Alabama, U.S. subsidiaries, and a guarantee has been decided for a Georgia overseas affiliate. If utilization or borrowing terms worsen at overseas units, the guarantee exposure could weigh on parent-level finances.

Local trade conditions and exchange rates also affect overseas profitability. Changes in guarantee limits and overseas funding are the items to watch.

Shareholding changes and flows

A disclosure dated October 7, 2026 showed that major shareholder Lee Seung-jae's holdings rose by 30,520 shares. Over the period from September 7 to October 12, 2026, foreign investors were net sellers of about 186,115 shares.

In a thinly traded stock, shifts in major shareholder holdings and foreign flows can widen price swings. Ownership disclosures and flow trends are the items to watch.
