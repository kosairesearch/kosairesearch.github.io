02

Business structure

KR Motors started as a two-wheeler manufacturer and distributor listed on KOSPI. In March 2026 it secured 100% of an auto precision parts maker, and on September 30, 2026 it absorbed that subsidiary through a merger.

After the merger the company changed its name in October 2026 to put its auto-parts business first, and it said it will move its head office to Daejeon.

The parts business uses cold-forging equipment and related technology to produce brake caliper pistons for automotive brake systems, along with electric drive components and chassis parts for braking and driving systems.

The merged subsidiary's reported 2025 revenue was about KRW 79.2 billion, a scope that differs from the KRW 13.8 billion in the listed company's own 2025 filing. Because consolidation starts from the acquisition date, the 2026 quarterly revenue scale changed from the second quarter.

The company plans to scale back two-wheeler manufacturing toward distribution and to make auto parts its core business. After the merger the parts unit keeps separate profit-and-loss management and its own production, quality, development and delivery systems.

On March 24, 2026 the subsidiary's board decided to sell its entire stake in a China-based unit. Press reporting in March 2026 said two-wheeler weakness had kept the company in the red. Listed companies in the same auto sector include Hyundai Mobis, HL Mando, Hyundai WIA, SNT Motiv and Sungwoo Hitech.

04

Earnings analysis

On the basis of the filed results, annual revenue was KRW 117.1 billion in 2022, KRW 78.4 billion in 2023 (down 33.0% year on year), KRW 16 billion in 2024 (down 79.6%) and KRW 13.8 billion in 2025 (down 13.8%). 2022 was the largest year and 2025 the smallest in the 2022–2025 window.

Operating losses were KRW 6.23 billion in 2022, KRW 17.9 billion in 2023 (losses continued and widened year on year), KRW 4.94 billion in 2024 (losses continued and narrowed) and KRW 2.89 billion in 2025 (losses continued and narrowed). Operating margins were -5.3%, -22.8%, -30.8% and -20.9% from 2022 to 2025.

Net loss attributable to owners of the parent was KRW 6.68 billion in 2022, KRW 15.9 billion in 2023, KRW 11.4 billion in 2024 and KRW 1.9 billion in 2025.

Net loss including non-controlling interests was KRW 13 billion, KRW 21 billion, KRW 14.2 billion and KRW 1.9 billion over the same years, a different measure from the owners' figure. Press reports tied the 2023 loss widening to weakness in the two-wheeler business.

At the end of 2025 total equity was KRW 43.7 billion, all attributable to owners of the parent, and total liabilities were KRW 50.5 billion. The debt-to-equity ratio moved from 272.7% in 2022 to 518.5% in 2023, 161.2% in 2024 and 115.7% in 2025.

Operating cash flow was negative KRW 2.41 billion in 2022, negative KRW 8.76 billion in 2023, negative KRW 5.15 billion in 2024 and negative KRW 6.57 billion in 2025.

Quarterly revenue was KRW 3.41 billion in Q3 2025, KRW 2.93 billion in Q4 2025 (down 14.0% from the prior quarter), and KRW 2.56 billion in Q1 2026 (down 12.9%).

It then jumped to KRW 26.3 billion in Q2 2026 (up 931.2% from the prior quarter) and was KRW 24.9 billion in Q3 2026 (down 5.4% from the prior quarter, up 631.2% year on year). A press report said first-half revenue rose 286.4% year on year, which it attributed to the auto parts acquisition.

Operating results were an operating loss of KRW 1.26 billion in Q3 2025, an operating loss of KRW 623 million in Q4 2025 (losses narrowed), an operating loss of KRW 670 million in Q1 2026 (losses widened), an operating profit of KRW 435 million in Q2 2026 (turned positive), and an operating loss of KRW 958 million in Q3 2026 (turned negative; losses narrowed year on year).

Net income attributable to owners was a loss of KRW 2.67 billion in Q3 2025, a profit of KRW 2.23 billion in Q4 2025 (turned positive), KRW 19.4 billion in Q1 2026 (up 767.5%), KRW 9.5 billion in Q2 2026 (down 51.0%) and a loss of KRW 2.3 billion in Q3 2026 (turned negative).

The last four quarters total KRW 28.8 billion. Press reports put the bargain purchase gain at KRW 21.5 billion in Q1 2026 and an additional KRW 6.6 billion in Q2, or KRW 28.1 billion in total, so the four-quarter total includes a one-time item and differs from operating earnings.

05

Industry analysis

Demand for auto parts is set by finished-vehicle production volumes and by negotiations over part prices. Brake caliper pistons, the core product, are used in braking systems and move with the replacement cycles of vehicle platforms.

Across the industry, the shift to electrification is changing the mix of braking and driving components and remains central to the debate. Cold forging shapes metal at near room temperature to raise strength and precision, and suppliers with this capability typically carry large equipment investments.

The acquired parts business focuses on a narrow range of components, so its product portfolio is narrower than that of diversified parts makers. Hyundai Mobis and HL Mando are large suppliers tied to major automakers' supply chains.

Hyundai WIA, SNT Motiv and Sungwoo Hitech are listed peers in drivetrain, power transmission or body parts. Within that group, KR Motors' parts unit is a smaller specialist in brake-system forgings.

Two-wheeler manufacturing has tended to swing with domestic demand and distribution conditions, and the company says it is scaling that business back toward distribution. Its results therefore depend on two factors at once: demand for auto parts and the wind-down of the two-wheeler operations.

06

Outlook

Through the merger and the name change, the company has formalized a restructuring centered on precision auto parts. Two-wheeler manufacturing is being scaled back toward distribution, and parts are being developed as the core business.

A related company said it is exploring medium- to long-term synergies between the parts capability and the group's existing vehicle manufacturing, sales and distribution operations.

On March 24, 2026 the subsidiary's board decided to sell its entire stake in a China-based unit, which reflects a broader pruning of overseas assets. Press reports said a contract was signed in October 2026 to sell the Changwon plant site and buildings for KRW 47.5 billion.

Proceeds were used to repay convertible bonds first, and the redeemed bonds were fully retired. Press reports also said private equity firms still need to sell 2.4 million common shares, about 12.2% of the company, and no sale date has been set.

The 2026 annual report, the next periodic filing, will show the full-year effect of the acquisition. Full-year 2026 results will combine Q4 with the second-quarter profit and third-quarter loss, and whether that pattern continues is the main point to watch.

Bull factors

Revenue scale changed with the parts acquisition

Quarterly revenue reached KRW 26.3 billion in Q2 2026 and KRW 24.9 billion in Q3 2026, up from KRW 2.93 billion in Q4 2025. After the subsidiary was brought in, the parts business became the center of revenue. Operating profit turned positive in Q2 2026.

Track record of narrowing operating losses

Operating loss narrowed from KRW 17.9 billion in 2023 to KRW 2.89 billion in 2025. The operating margin improved from -30.8% in 2024 to -20.9% in 2025. The losses have been smaller since their 2023 peak.

Balance sheet improvement and bond repayment

The debt-to-equity ratio fell from 518.5% in 2023 to 115.7% in 2025. Press reports said proceeds from the Changwon plant sale were used to repay convertible bonds first, and the bonds were fully retired. Total liabilities at the end of 2025 were KRW 50.5 billion.

Bear factors

Return to losses in Q3 2026

Operating results swung to an operating loss of KRW 958 million in Q3 2026 from a profit in Q2. Revenue also fell 5.4% from the prior quarter. The profit in Q2 did not carry into the following quarter.

Quarterly earnings supported by one-time gains

Q1 2026 net income attributable to owners of KR Motors, KRW 19.4 billion, reflected a bargain purchase gain of KRW 21.5 billion reported in the press. The four-quarter total of KRW 28.8 billion also includes this one-time item. Q3 2026 showed a net loss attributable to owners of KRW 2.3 billion.

Overhang from private equity holdings

Press reports said two private equity firms still need to sell 2.4 million common shares, about 12.2% of the company. No sale timing has been set in those reports. Such a block could weigh on the share supply if sold.

Risk factors

Accounting adjustment risk

Press reports put the bargain purchase gain at KRW 28.1 billion after a fair value review, with an upward revision from the earlier estimate. Reported earnings can move with accounting treatment and revaluation outcomes. This item is separate from operating trends.

Business transition risk

The wind-down of two-wheeler operations and the sale of an overseas subsidiary's stake are changing the consolidation scope and revenue mix. Costs and profit swings from this transition flow directly into quarterly results.

Cash flow risk

Operating cash flow was reported as negative KRW 2.41 billion in 2022, negative KRW 8.76 billion in 2023, negative KRW 5.15 billion in 2024 and negative KRW 6.57 billion in 2025. The negative flow continued in 2025. The effect of bond repayment and share sales on liquidity is a point to track quarter by quarter.

Overall view

KR Motors is moving from a two-wheeler-centered loss structure toward precision auto parts. On the filed figures, revenue fell from KRW 117.1 billion in 2022 to KRW 13.8 billion in 2025, and the operating loss narrowed to KRW 2.89 billion in 2025.

Quarterly revenue grew in 2026 to KRW 26.3 billion in Q2 and KRW 24.9 billion in Q3, and operating profit turned positive in Q2. Operating results then turned to a KRW 958 million loss in Q3.

The Q1 2026 net income includes a press-reported bargain purchase gain, so earnings figures are best read alongside operating results. Lower leverage and the repayment of convertible bonds are balance sheet improvements.

The sale of 2.4 million shares held by private equity firms and the costs of the business transition remain open variables. Strengths and weaknesses are both present, so the next points to check are full-year 2026 results and the operating margin in the 2026 annual report.
