02

Business structure

SP Systems was founded in 1988 and is headquartered in Yangsan-si, South Korea, manufacturing and supplying industrial automation equipment and logistics automation systems built around its proprietary high-speed gantry robots.

The company's business is organized into gantry robots and automation peripherals, smart factory (ICT) solutions, and secondary battery and automotive parts manufacturing; according to job platform JobKorea's data as of March 2026, gantry robots and automation peripherals accounted for 47% of sales, smart factory solutions 41%, battery components 8%, and automotive parts 4%.

Its major customers are Hyundai Motor and affiliated Hyundai Group companies, GM Korea, and Nexen Tire, with a core focus on automotive engine component production lines, while the company has recently expanded into secondary batteries, electric vehicles, shipbuilding, and construction.

Its product lineup includes conrod, crankshaft and camshaft handling, cylinder head and block handling, transmission case handling, and pallet head handling, as well as conveyor, buffer, lifting, inspection, and valve body production solutions serving the automotive, machine tool, display, tire, and test measurement automation sectors.

The company originally focused on logistics automation for the display industry before leveraging accumulated technology to broaden into automotive and secondary battery customers.

As part of its secondary battery growth push, it incorporated an affiliate holding related patents into the group in 2017, and it now operates seven affiliated companies.

In the industrial automation and robotics space, SP Systems competes with listed peers covering overlapping segments such as SFA Engineering, Yuilrobotics, and Robotis, and its secondary battery automation order flow is tied closely to the capital spending pace of downstream battery makers.

04

Earnings analysis

Annual revenue rose from KRW 53.1 billion in 2022 to KRW 60.5 billion in 2023 (up 13.8% year on year) and KRW 71 billion in 2024 (up 17.5%), before declining to KRW 61.6 billion in 2025 (down 13.3%); among 2022-2025, 2024 was the largest year and 2022 the smallest.

Operating profit of KRW 3.39 billion in 2022 turned into an operating loss of KRW 304 million in 2023, the loss widened to KRW 2.8 billion in 2024, and narrowed to KRW 764 million in 2025, marking three consecutive years of operating losses from 2023 through 2025.

Controlling-interest net income followed a similar path, moving from a profit of KRW 1.75 billion in 2022 to a loss of KRW 806 million in 2023, widening to a loss of KRW 2.12 billion in 2024 and further to KRW 6.18 billion in 2025, also three consecutive years of losses.

Net income including non-controlling interests stayed positive at KRW 2.75 billion in 2022 and KRW 419 million in 2023 before swinging to a net loss of KRW 2.2 billion in 2024 and KRW 5.29 billion in 2025. The operating margin fell from 6.4% in 2022 to -0.5% in 2023, -3.9% in 2024, and -1.2% in 2025.

Quarterly revenue moved from KRW 15.3 billion in Q2 2025 to KRW 15.9 billion in Q3 2025 (up 3.8% quarter on quarter), KRW 15 billion in Q4 2025 (down 5.6%), and fell sharply to KRW 10.8 billion in Q1 2026 (down 27.9%) before partially recovering to KRW 11.9 billion in Q2 2026 (up 10.0% quarter on quarter but down 22.3% year on year).

Operating income turned positive at KRW 1.86 billion in Q4 2025 before reverting to a loss of KRW 1.59 billion in Q1 2026, then narrowing to a loss of KRW 184 million in Q2 2026, marking two consecutive quarters of operating losses from Q1 through Q2 2026.

Controlling-interest net income widened to a loss of KRW 3.94 billion in Q4 2025, narrowed to a loss of KRW 952 million in Q1 2026, then turned to a profit of KRW 251 million in Q2 2026 versus both the prior quarter and the same quarter a year earlier; the trailing four quarters (Q3 2025-Q2 2026) still show a cumulative controlling-interest net loss of KRW 5.05 billion.

In contrast to the reported losses, operating cash flow swung from KRW 3.71 billion in 2022 to negative KRW 4.47 billion in 2023, KRW 1 billion in 2024, and KRW 4.98 billion in 2025, showing improved cash generation in 2025 despite the accounting loss.

05

Industry analysis

SP Systems' revenue is sensitive to the capital expenditure cycles of downstream industries such as automotive, secondary batteries, and displays.

The secondary battery industry suffered a downturn through 2025 amid slowing electric vehicle demand, but analysts note the industry has moved past last year's downturn and is entering a gradual recovery phase, with the trend of slowing EV demand and downward earnings revisions beginning to ease.

The recovery is seen as being driven less by EV growth itself and more by a European EV recovery and high growth in North American energy storage systems (ESS), with ESS emerging as a new growth axis for batteries as AI data center power demand rises.

At the same time, a more cautious view holds that the secondary battery industry sits at an inflection point between a prolonged structural downturn and a new growth phase, with slowing EV demand risking utilization pressure and order concentration.

On October 19, 2026, reports noted that secondary battery equipment stocks moved higher together on expectations of production line expansion and next-generation battery equipment investment by battery makers, illustrating how sensitive the equipment and automation segment's share prices are to battery capex expectations.

A separate theme concerns robotics and humanoid development: following Samsung Electronics' incorporation of Rainbow Robotics as a subsidiary, joint robot development between the two has advanced, and brokerages occasionally cite industrial automation companies such as SP Systems alongside other names when discussing potential supply chain beneficiaries.

In the industrial robotics and automation market, SP Systems' business overlaps partly with companies such as SFA Engineering and Yuilrobotics, but given its relatively smaller scale, its results tend to hinge heavily on the investment decisions of a limited number of large customers.

In robotics and automation, continued progress in the Samsung Electronics-Rainbow Robotics collaboration has kept market attention on the related supply chain, though it remains unconfirmed whether this translates into actual orders for SP Systems.

Overall, the direction of results from the second half of 2026 onward will depend on whether the earnings improvement seen in the second quarter continues and whether secondary battery and automotive customers resume capital spending.

Bull factors

Second-Quarter Earnings Improvement Signal

Controlling-interest net income turned positive at KRW 251 million in Q2 2026 versus both the prior quarter and the same period a year earlier, while the operating loss narrowed to KRW 184 million, continuing a loss-reduction trend.

Revenue also rose 10.0% quarter on quarter, partially recovering from the sharp Q1 decline. Whether this trend continues into Q3 and beyond is the next item to watch.

Secondary Battery Recovery Expectations

Brokerages have suggested the secondary battery industry is entering an early recovery phase after last year's downturn, citing a European EV recovery and expanding North American ESS demand as key drivers.

For SP Systems, which generates battery component and battery-related robotics revenue, a resumption of downstream customer capex could be a positive variable, though the timing and pace of recovery remain uncertain.

Robotics and Automation Theme Linkage

Following Samsung Electronics' incorporation of Rainbow Robotics as a subsidiary and the advancement of joint humanoid robot development between the two, brokerage reports have at times mentioned SP Systems alongside other names as a potential supply chain beneficiary.

This can draw market attention to the company as a holder of industrial automation technology, though whether it has translated into actual orders has not been specifically confirmed.

Bear factors

Widening Revenue Volatility

2025 revenue fell 13.3% year on year to KRW 61.6 billion, and Q1 2026 revenue dropped sharply by 27.9% quarter on quarter. Although Q2 partially recovered, revenue was still down 22.3% year on year, reaffirming a business structure where results hinge on the investment timing of a few large customers.

Three Consecutive Years of Losses

Both operating income and controlling-interest net income posted losses for three consecutive years from 2023 through 2025, with the controlling-interest net loss actually widening from KRW 806 million in 2023 to KRW 6.18 billion in 2025.

It remains too early to judge whether the Q2 2026 return to profit is a temporary occurrence or a sustained improvement.

Cumulative Net Loss Still Negative

The cumulative controlling-interest net loss over the trailing four quarters (Q3 2025-Q2 2026) reached KRW 5.05 billion. A single quarter's return to profit is not sufficient to conclude the earnings structure has fully improved, and continuity over the coming quarters needs to be confirmed.

Risk factors

Customer Concentration Risk

Revenue from the automotive and secondary battery segments is tied to the capex schedules of a small number of large customers, so delays or cutbacks in customer investment can directly affect orders and revenue. The 27.9% quarter-on-quarter revenue decline in Q1 2026 illustrates this exposure.

Secondary Battery Downturn Risk

Analysts note the secondary battery industry is exposed to utilization pressure and order concentration risk stemming from slowing EV demand. If the industry recovery is delayed beyond expectations, additional downward pressure could be exerted on battery component and related robotics revenue.

Financial Structure Risk

The debt-to-equity ratio rose from 46.3% in 2023 to 52.5% in 2025, and years of losses have reduced the scale of the controlling interest compared with earlier periods. A delay in the recovery of profitability could reignite market concerns about the financial structure.

Overall view

SP Systems swung from a profit in 2022 to three consecutive years of operating and controlling-interest net losses from 2023 through 2025, before showing a sign of earnings improvement with a return to controlling-interest net profit in Q2 2026.

However, the trailing four-quarter cumulative controlling-interest net loss still stands at KRW 5.05 billion, so whether a single quarter's profit marks a sustained turn requires further confirmation.

Revenue peaked at KRW 71 billion in 2024 before declining sharply through 2025 and Q1 2026, then partially rebounding in Q2, reflecting notable quarter-to-quarter volatility.

On the industry side, gradual secondary battery recovery expectations and the Samsung Electronics-Rainbow Robotics-driven robotics theme are both in play, though neither has been confirmed to translate into actual orders for the company.

Financially, operating cash flow improved to KRW 4.98 billion in 2025 even as the debt-to-equity ratio trended higher, warranting a combined look at earnings, cash flow, and financial structure.

Confirming the persistence of the earnings improvement through the next periodic report will be an important factor to watch going forward.
