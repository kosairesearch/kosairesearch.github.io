02

Business structure

Kook Soon Dang is a liquor company specializing in traditional Korean liquor, including makgeolli and Baekseju. Makgeolli is the core category, and its rice makgeolli, first launched in 1996, is cited as the company's flagship product.

At the 2026 Korea Liquor Awards, three makgeolli products, including 1000 Billion Lactic Acid Bacteria Makgeolli, Kook Soon Dang Rice Makgeolli and Bakbongdam Rice Makgeolli, won category grand prizes, according to the company.

Its Hoengseong brewery produces makgeolli, yakju, fruit liquor and general distilled spirits, and the company buys all of the Seolgaengmi rice variety, a brewing-dedicated rice used for Baekseju, under contract farming with local growers. On the sales side, collaborations with food and restaurant brands stand out.

The company says cumulative sales of makgeolli products made with brands such as Jolipong, Babambar and Chilsung Cider exceed about 10 million units. In October 2026, it launched a limited-edition rice makgeolli chewing candy, Makju, with Crown Confectionery.

The company exports traditional liquor to 65 countries and received the industry's first '7 million dollar export tower' award in 2021. Industry analysis describes the domestic liquor market as centered on beer and soju, leaving the yakju and takju segments with only modest growth.

In terms of competition, Kook Soon Dang has focused on widening points of contact for makgeolli consumers through partnerships with food brands.

04

Earnings analysis

Annual results show revenue shrinking and operating losses appearing first, followed by a swing to a controlling-shareholder net profit in 2025.

Revenue fell from KRW 74.6 billion in 2022 to KRW 70.5 billion in 2023 (down 5.5% year on year), KRW 68.8 billion in 2024 (down 2.4%) and KRW 67.5 billion in 2025 (down 1.9%), marking three consecutive years of decline from 2023 to 2025. Of the years 2022 through 2025, 2022 was the largest and 2025 the smallest.

Operating profit fell from KRW 9.15 billion in 2022 to KRW 4.46 billion in 2023 (down 51.2%), and turned into an operating loss of KRW 2.28 billion in 2024. The operating loss narrowed to KRW 832 million in 2025, and the two years from 2024 to 2025 were both loss years.

The operating margin moved from 12.3% in 2022 to 6.3% in 2023, then -3.3% in 2024 and -1.2% in 2025.

Net income attributable to controlling shareholders was a loss of KRW 4.64 billion in 2022 and a loss of KRW 3.9 billion in 2023, before turning to a profit of KRW 1.37 billion in 2024 and rising to KRW 6.24 billion in 2025, up 354.3% year on year.

Net income including non-controlling interests was KRW 6.25 billion in 2025. Because net income rose sharply while the company posted an operating loss, non-operating items appear to have played a large role.

Separate-basis figures also show dividend income and gains on financial asset disposals as large items in 2025 financial income. Quarterly results began with revenue of KRW 18.8 billion, operating profit of KRW 197 million and controlling-shareholder net income of KRW 3.18 billion in the third quarter of 2025.

In the fourth quarter of 2025, revenue fell to KRW 14.6 billion (down 22.1% quarter on quarter), the company swung to an operating loss of KRW 1.19 billion, and controlling-shareholder net loss was KRW 803 million. On a separate basis, selling expenses rose sharply from the previous quarter in that period.

Revenue recovered to KRW 17.5 billion in the first quarter of 2026 (up 19.5% quarter on quarter), with operating profit of KRW 35.39 million, and controlling-shareholder net income was KRW 1.07 billion.

In the second quarter of 2026, revenue was KRW 14.7 billion (down 15.8% quarter on quarter), the operating loss was KRW 1.43 billion, and controlling-shareholder net income was KRW 779 million.

Third-quarter 2026 revenue was KRW 15.9 billion (up 8.3% quarter on quarter, down 15.1% year on year), but the operating loss widened to KRW 1.63 billion.

Operating losses have continued for two straight quarters from the second to the third quarter of 2026, and the third quarter moved from an operating profit of KRW 197 million a year earlier to a loss.

The controlling-shareholder net loss of KRW 3.65 billion in the third quarter was larger than the operating loss, which suggests non-operating items had a large effect.

The cumulative controlling-shareholder net loss over the last four quarters, from the fourth quarter of 2025 to the third quarter of 2026, totals KRW 2.61 billion.

05

Industry analysis

Industry analysis says the domestic liquor market faces a combination of pressures, including an economic slowdown, weaker demand for premium liquor and tighter regulation. Within a consumption structure centered on beer and soju, the yakju and takju segments are described as showing modest growth but weak results.

Makgeolli's alcohol content, traditionally around 6%, has fallen to about 5%. In line with this shift, Kook Soon Dang is positioning makgeolli as a drink that people with a lower alcohol tolerance can enjoy comfortably.

To move beyond the image of makgeolli as a cheap, familiar drink, the company says it is focusing on raw material differentiation, brewing process upgrades and package design. The aim is to reposition makgeolli from a drink consumed in volume to a premium fermented drink valued for its quality.

Overseas, the company uses a staged approach, starting with low-barrier products such as fruit makgeolli and then linking consumers to original makgeolli and yakju.

In Korea, it works with food and beverage brands, including Lotte Chilsung Beverage's Chilsung Cider and Binggrae's Babambar, to reach Generation Z consumers.

The company cites the sweep of grand prizes across its fresh makgeolli, pasteurized makgeolli and traditional liquor lines at the 2026 Korea Liquor Awards as evidence of product competitiveness.

06

Outlook

The company identifies its business priorities as raising the brand value of existing products, strengthening product competitiveness and expanding distribution channels. Makju, launched in October 2026, is a limited-edition product sold through large discount stores, e-commerce platforms and the company's own stores.

The company says it will decide whether to produce more after reviewing consumer response and consulting with Crown Confectionery.

Makju combines the company's fermentation technology with candy manufacturing technology and uses rice makgeolli fermented three times, made from 100% domestic rice with in-house cultured koji and yeast.

The company says it will keep working with food and beverage partners and plans to expand collaborations beyond food and restaurants into a wider range of industries, and that it is currently discussing new tie-ups with several sectors.

Overseas, it maintains an export base in 65 countries and uses a staged approach that leads with low-barrier products such as fruit makgeolli.

Because the collaboration products are mainly limited-run releases, whether additional production happens will determine how much these products contribute to revenue after 2026, and the company's follow-up announcements will show this.

Risk factors

Regulation and lower alcohol content

Industry analysis says the liquor sector faces pressure from an economic slowdown, weaker demand for premium products and tighter regulation. The shift in makgeolli alcohol content from around 6% to about 5% could change unit prices and margin structure.

The company is responding with premiumization, and how these price shifts affect profitability will show up in quarterly filings.

Risk that collaboration gains are one-off

Cumulative sales of about 10 million units from collaboration products rely heavily on limited-run releases. Makju is a limited-edition product, and any additional production depends on consumer response and talks with Crown Confectionery. If collaborations remain short-lived campaigns, they may not translate into a steady base of recurring revenue.

Continued operating losses and selling costs

Operating losses have continued since the second quarter of 2026, so the timing of a turnaround in profitability is not yet visible. The third quarter of 2026 combined a revenue decline with a wider operating loss.

On a separate basis, selling expenses jumped in the fourth quarter of 2025 compared with the prior quarter, which can be read as a sign of rising non-production cost pressure.

Overall view

Kook Soon Dang is a makgeolli-centered traditional liquor company whose revenue fell for three consecutive years, from KRW 74.6 billion in 2022 to KRW 67.5 billion in 2025.

Operating losses occurred in both 2024 and 2025, though the 2025 loss narrowed, and net income attributable to controlling shareholders was KRW 6.24 billion. In 2026, operating losses have continued for two straight quarters, and the third-quarter loss widened to KRW 1.63 billion.

On the balance sheet, total liabilities at the end of 2025 were KRW 19.2 billion with a debt-to-equity ratio of 8.0%, against total equity of KRW 239.1 billion. Collaboration products, award wins and an export base in 65 countries are cited as brand assets.

At the same time, falling revenue and quarterly losses mean that whether profitability can recover remains unconfirmed.
