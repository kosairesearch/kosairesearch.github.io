Report overview

Sungmoon Electronics: Whether the earnings recovery can hold

Sungmoon Electronics makes metallized films for capacitors. Its operating profit has risen for two straight years since the 2023 low, but a net loss attributable to controlling shareholders in the third quarter of 2026 makes earnings durability the next test.

    Revenue reached KRW 53.7 billion in 2025, the largest year in the 2022–2025 period, and grew in both 2024 and 2025 after the 2023 low.

  2. 2

  3. 3

  5. 5

02

Business structure

Sungmoon Electronics was founded in 1980 and listed in 1990 as a specialist in metallized films for film capacitors. Its core product is metallized film for capacitors, produced in zinc (Zn) and aluminum (Al) deposited variants.

Based on the company's own profile, the product line holds a 20% share of the global market and a 70% share of the domestic market. The business is organized around two pillars: metallized film for capacitors and the DM business.

Its main customers are film capacitor manufacturers, and in 2024 exports made up 97% of sales against 3% domestic. Overseas operations include a Qingdao subsidiary in China and an Indian subsidiary established in 2025.

The company says the Qingdao unit has posted consistent profits and has become a key earnings source within the group. The product direction is toward higher-value films for solar, wind, ESS and EV applications.

In the value chain, the company holds a 25% stake in an Anhui Province capacitor affiliate, bought for about KRW 5.5 billion in 2021, through which it plans to supply EV capacitors.

It also took a 25% stake in a Jiangsu Province film maker in 2025, and the company says that plant can reduce reliance on outside suppliers of PP film. On competition, first-quarter 2026 revenue rose on new export orders, but commentary notes that intensifying competition pressured profitability.

04

Earnings analysis

Annual revenue fell from KRW 50 billion in 2022 to KRW 45.1 billion in 2023, a 9.8% decline, then rose to KRW 48.1 billion in 2024 (up 6.7%) and KRW 53.7 billion in 2025 (up 11.7%). Revenue grew in 2024 and 2025 after the 2023 low, and 2025 was the largest year of the 2022–2025 period.

The 2025 filing attributed the gains to higher average exchange rates and higher thin-film sales volume. Operating profit fell from KRW 2.47 billion in 2022 to KRW 514 million in 2023, a 79.2% decline, then recovered to KRW 1.1 billion in 2024 (up 114.8%) and KRW 1.67 billion in 2025 (up 51.7%).

Operating margin was 4.9% in 2022, 1.1% in 2023, 2.3% in 2024 and 3.1% in 2025, so 2025 remained below 2022.

Profit attributable to controlling shareholders was KRW 2.04 billion in 2022, a net loss of KRW 1.65 billion in 2023 after turning to a loss, KRW 2.71 billion in 2024 after turning to profit, and KRW 2 billion in 2025, down 26.0%.

In 2025 operating profit rose while controlling-shareholder profit fell, and net income including non-controlling interests of KRW 2.02 billion differs from that figure. The debt-to-equity ratio moved from 78.5% in 2022 to 65.9% in 2023, 82.5% in 2024 and 89.0% in 2025.

Operating cash flow rose every year, from KRW 1.06 billion in 2022 to KRW 3.27 billion, KRW 4.42 billion and KRW 7.23 billion in 2025.

On a quarterly basis, the company moved from an operating loss of KRW 0.38 million in the third quarter of 2025 to operating profit of KRW 368 million in the fourth quarter, then KRW 670 million in the first quarter of 2026 and KRW 980 million in the second quarter, before dropping 24.5% to KRW 740 million in the third quarter.

Revenue peaked at KRW 17.4 billion in the second quarter of 2026 and was KRW 16.1 billion in the third quarter, down 7.4% from the prior quarter.

In the third quarter of 2026 operating profit was positive while the net loss attributable to controlling shareholders was KRW 637 million, and the gap sits in items below operating profit, which the quarterly report details. Profit attributable to controlling shareholders over the last four quarters totaled KRW 1.91 billion.

Industry analysis

One analysis projects the film capacitor market to grow at more than 5.8% a year, driven by rising demand from electric and hybrid vehicles. The company points to film development for eco-friendly inverter systems and EV chargers as new opportunities.

Based on the company's profile, its share is 20% globally and 70% domestically, a notably strong domestic position. However, the first-quarter 2026 commentary noted that intensifying competition weighed on profitability.

On inputs, the company has relied on outside suppliers of PP film, and it says the Jiangsu plant could reduce that dependence once running. New export orders from markets such as India lifted first-quarter 2026 revenue.

06

Outlook

The most concrete company timeline is the start of full operation at the Jiangsu film plant, which the company says is slated for the fourth quarter of 2027. Once it runs, in-house PP film sourcing would reduce outside dependence, according to the company.

The company also said, based on cost competitiveness improvements, that operating profit is expected to enter a full growth phase from late 2027. This view comes from a June 2026 report of company remarks, and no formal numerical guidance was identified.

In the same report, the company said it is reviewing direct investment and a joint venture with a foreign firm that holds source technology in next-generation materials. The timing and scale of that venture were not disclosed.

Through its Anhui affiliate, the company plans to supply EV capacitors to domestic and foreign carmakers. Higher-value product mix and the Qingdao subsidiary's continued profits are cited as drivers of improvement. The Indian subsidiary, established in 2025, is in an early stage of overseas expansion.

The next checkpoints are the Jiangsu plant timing, the outcome of the joint venture review, and the operating margin trend after 2027.

Overall view

Sungmoon Electronics has grown revenue to KRW 53.7 billion in 2025 on the strength of its film share and overseas units, and operating profit has risen for two years since the 2023 low.

However, operating margin was 3.1% in 2025, still below 4.9% in 2022, and the third quarter of 2026 showed positive operating profit alongside a KRW 637 million net loss attributable to controlling shareholders.

The company says operating profit should enter a growth phase from late 2027, once the Jiangsu plant runs and raw-material sourcing is in-house. That is management's view and is not yet confirmed in reported results. Leverage rose to 89.0% in 2025, so capital headroom depends on the pace of profit recovery.

The stock trades below book value, but with small earnings the multiple is sensitive to quarterly results. The next checkpoints are the 2026 annual report, the Jiangsu plant timeline, and the operating margin in the first quarter of 2027.
