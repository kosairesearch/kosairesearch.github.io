Hanil Iron & Steel: Testing Whether Quarterly Profits Can Hold

Hanil Iron & Steel's revenue declined for three straight years through 2025, yet net income attributable to controlling shareholders turned positive in the second and third quarters of 2026. After two years of losses in 2024 and 2025, whether this turnaround reflects a durable recovery is the next question to track.

02

Business structure

Hanil Iron & Steel, founded in December 1957, processes and distributes steel products such as steel plates and section steel, and also handles construction materials. Its main items include steel plates, shapes, coil cutting and shot blasting.

Based on second-quarter 2026 revenue, the segment mix was wholesale and trading 64%, primary metal manufacturing 29%, construction material rental 4% and plate cutting services 3%, according to a third-party company information site.

The mix shows that distribution and trading, rather than production facilities, sit at the center of revenue. According to the company profile, Hanil is an official dealer of Hyundai Steel Company, sourcing and processing hot-rolled and cold-rolled sheet to customer specifications.

It operates coil centers in Pohang and Dangjin, using proximity to large steel mills to reduce logistics costs. Its end markets are described as automotive, construction and shipbuilding. Because supply is concentrated in Hyundai Steel Company, supply terms and price changes feed directly into margins.

Dongkuk Steel Mill, SeAH Steel and Daehan Steel, which have a larger weight in pipe and section production, operate different business models from Hanil Iron & Steel. Revenue in 2025 was KRW 196.4 billion.

04

Earnings analysis

On a reported basis, annual revenue went from KRW 255.2 billion in 2022 to KRW 216.3 billion in 2023 (down 15.3% year on year), KRW 205.8 billion in 2024 (down 4.8%) and KRW 196.4 billion in 2025 (down 4.6%). 2022 was the largest year in the period and 2025 the smallest.

Operating profit moved from KRW 4.03 billion in 2022 to KRW 4.86 billion in 2023 (up 20.5%), then fell to KRW 667 million in 2024 (down 86.3%) before recovering to KRW 3.18 billion in 2025 (up 376.7%). 2023 was the largest operating profit year in the period and 2024 the smallest.

Operating margin was 1.6% in 2022, 2.2% in 2023, 0.3% in 2024 and 1.6% in 2025. For the 2025 improvement, the company said it had improved its operating profit structure through cost management, according to its preliminary results announced on February 6, 2026.

Net income attributable to controlling shareholders moved from a loss of KRW 1.51 billion in 2022 to a profit of KRW 740 thousand in 2023, a swing back to a loss of KRW 5.26 billion in 2024, and a narrower loss of KRW 554 million in 2025.

Because operating profit was positive in 2025 while a loss remained at the controlling-shareholder level, the gap points to a drag from items below operating profit. Net income including non-controlling interests, a different measure, was a loss of KRW 436 million in 2025.

Operating cash flow fell every year, from KRW 21.4 billion in 2022 to KRW 5.72 billion in 2023, KRW 3.28 billion in 2024 and KRW 592 million in 2025.

On a quarterly basis, operating profit in Q4 2025 was KRW 25.98 million, down 98.2% from the prior quarter, and rose to KRW 2.86 billion in Q2 2026, up 516.5% from the prior quarter. Q3 2026 operating profit was KRW 1.51 billion, down 47.0% from the prior quarter but up 6.3% year on year.

According to a news report citing the quarterly filing, cumulative revenue for January to September 2026 was KRW 187.1 billion and operating profit was KRW 4.83 billion.

05

Industry analysis

Distribution- and processing-focused steelmakers' earnings depend heavily on supplier pricing and demand conditions.

In a January 13, 2026 report on Hyundai Steel Company, Mirae Asset Securities expected that, after anti-dumping measures on hot-rolled coil and the depletion of customer inventories, price increases would translate into an improved spread between selling and raw material prices.

That view concerns Hyundai Steel Company's steel segment, but for a dealer, price trends at its supplier flow through to margins. End demand depends on automotive, construction and shipbuilding, so investment and orders in those sectors determine volumes.

Revenue declining for three consecutive years through 2025 shows that demand was weak during the period. According to press reports, the company focused on expanding profitability amid a demand slump.

Compared with steelmakers and pipe producers in the sector, Hanil Iron & Steel runs a smaller-scale asset base and differentiates through its dealer and processing capabilities.

Quarterly revenue held at KRW 61.9 billion in Q1 2026, KRW 61.8 billion in Q2 and KRW 63.4 billion in Q3, a pattern that suggests volumes did not fall sharply.

06

Outlook

Quarterly revenue rose 24.8% year on year in the third quarter of 2026, pointing to a recovery in volumes after the first half. Cumulative operating profit for January to September 2026 of KRW 4.83 billion already exceeds full-year 2025 operating profit of KRW 3.18 billion, so the profit base is larger than in 2025.

Quarterly operating profit is volatile, however, moving from KRW 463 million in Q1 2026 to KRW 2.86 billion in Q2 and KRW 1.51 billion in Q3. The filings alone do not separate how much of the Q2 swing came from costs versus selling prices.

If cost management gains persist, operating margin improvement could appear in upcoming reports. If steel prices weaken again, dealer margins could come under pressure.

Net income attributable to controlling shareholders turned positive in both Q2 and Q3 of 2026, so the key question is whether profit holds on a full-year basis. The next checkpoints are the 2026 preliminary annual results and the 2026 annual report, which will also include the fourth quarter of 2026.

Dividends for fiscal 2026 are subject to shareholder approval, and that decision will show the level of shareholder return.

Bull factors

Quarterly profit turnaround and earnings recovery

Net income attributable to controlling shareholders turned positive in Q2 2026 after a loss in the prior quarter, and reached KRW 4.1 billion in Q3, turning positive from a year-earlier loss. Q3 operating profit of KRW 1.51 billion rose 6.3% year on year.

Cumulative operating profit for January to September 2026 of KRW 4.83 billion exceeds full-year 2025 operating profit of KRW 3.18 billion.

Share price low relative to net assets

With the price-to-book ratio below 1x, the share price is low relative to asset value. Book value attributable to controlling shareholders was KRW 161.2 billion at the end of 2025, and market capitalization is smaller than that. The stock trades at a discount to the sector on this measure, which forms the valuation argument for this case.

Dealer-based volumes and room for spread improvement

As an official Hyundai Steel Company dealer operating coil centers in Pohang and Dangjin, the company sources hot-rolled and cold-rolled sheet on a stable basis. In a January 13, 2026 report on Hyundai Steel Company, Mirae Asset Securities expected price increases to translate into a better spread. That trend could extend to improved distribution margins.

Bear factors

Revenue down for three consecutive years

Revenue declined for three consecutive years from 2023 through 2025, ending at KRW 196.4 billion in 2025, below the KRW 255.2 billion of 2022. Quarterly revenue has returned to the KRW 61 billion to 63 billion range in 2026, but whether the annual trend has turned will be visible only in full-year 2026 results. Operating margin fell to 0.3% in 2024 during this period.

Financial burden and shrinking operating cash flow

The debt-to-equity ratio rose from 94.3% in 2022 to 103.6% in 2025. Operating cash flow fell from KRW 21.4 billion in 2022 to KRW 592 million in 2025. Total equity was KRW 165.1 billion at the end of 2025, so the pace at which earnings add to equity is slow.

Thin operating margin and quarterly volatility

Operating margin was low at 0.3% in 2024 and 1.6% in 2025. Quarterly operating profit swung from KRW 25.98 million in Q4 2025 to KRW 2.86 billion in Q2 2026. Net loss attributable to controlling shareholders reached KRW 5.26 billion in 2024, so a small earnings base leaves results open to sharp swings.

10

Risk factors

Raw material and price volatility

Steel distribution margins depend on supply prices from Hyundai Steel Company and on steel market conditions. If raw material costs rise ahead of selling prices, margins narrow. The drop in operating margin to 0.3% in 2024 shows how quickly such pressure can reach reported results.

Supplier and customer concentration

Being an official Hyundai Steel Company dealer gives supply stability, but dependence on one supplier means changes in its terms flow directly through to the company. A slowdown in automotive, construction or shipbuilding demand would hit volumes quickly.

Press reports describe the company focusing on profitability amid a demand slump, which underlines how much end-market conditions matter.

Balance sheet and cash generation

The debt-to-equity ratio stands at 103.6%, and operating cash flow fell to KRW 592 million in 2025. Whether cash generation keeps pace with the swing to quarterly profit remains an open question.

Because a controlling-shareholder loss remained in 2025 despite operating profit, the quality of earnings also needs to be checked.

Overall view

Hanil Iron & Steel is a steel company centered on distributing and processing steel plates and shapes, built on its status as an official dealer of Hyundai Steel Company. In the reported figures, revenue declined for three consecutive years from 2023 through 2025, and operating margin stayed between 0.3% and 2.2%.

Net losses attributable to controlling shareholders followed in 2024 and 2025. Operating profit rose to KRW 3.18 billion in 2025, yet a controlling-shareholder loss remained, and the debt-to-equity ratio rose to 103.6%.

In 2026, net income attributable to controlling shareholders was positive in the second and third quarters, and cumulative operating profit for January to September of KRW 4.83 billion exceeds full-year 2025 operating profit.

Strengths include the quarterly swing to profit, a share price low relative to net assets and dealer-based volumes. Weaknesses include the revenue decline, thin operating margins and financial burden.

The next checkpoints are the 2026 preliminary annual results and the 2026 annual report, and the key question is whether quarterly profits carry into full-year results.
