02

Business structure

Chorokbaem Media is a content producer whose core business is producing and distributing television dramas. It has concentrated on drama production since its founding in 1998, and it is run by co-CEOs Kim Dong-jun and Yoon Dong-hyun.

Its main product is drama content, and revenue is recognized per title as broadcast scheduling and OTT supply contracts are executed, so quarterly revenue can swing widely with delivery timing and title performance. The company is also expanding into AI-based video content beyond drama.

The 2026 Korea AI Content Festival (KAiCF), announced on February 12, 2026, accepted entries from February to July 2026 and drew 1,066 submissions across advertising (628), music video (255), short-form drama (136) and mid-form drama (47) categories.

The awards ceremony was held on August 6, 2026, and the company said all winners would be offered chances for collaboration, investment, partnerships or hiring. In competition, the company faces Studio Dragon and CJ ENM as large drama producers, while broadcasters such as SBS shape supply through their own scheduling. At 2025 revenue of KRW 172.2 billion, its scale is far smaller than those of the large operators.

04

Earnings analysis

On a disclosed basis, revenue rose from KRW 192.5 billion in 2022 to KRW 241.3 billion in 2023, a 25.3% increase over the prior year, which marks the peak. It then fell 12.9% to KRW 210.3 billion in 2024 and 18.1% to KRW 172.2 billion in 2025, a two-year decline, and 2025 was the smallest year of 2022 to 2025.

Operating profit went from KRW 5.59 billion in 2022 to an operating loss of KRW 297 million in 2023, a swing into loss, and the operating loss widened to KRW 15.4 billion in 2024. In 2025 operating profit was KRW 2.83 billion, with an operating margin of 1.6%.

Net loss attributable to owners narrowed from KRW 45.3 billion in 2022 and KRW 42.2 billion in 2023 to KRW 4.02 billion in 2024, and turned into net profit attributable to owners of KRW 2.82 billion in 2025.

However, net profit including non-controlling interests was KRW 45.8 billion in 2024, which moved in the opposite direction from both the operating loss and the net loss attributable to owners, so the non-controlling interest and non-operating items behind that gap should be checked in the annual report notes.

On a quarterly basis, the fourth quarter of 2025 showed revenue of KRW 35 billion and operating profit of KRW 1.5 billion, but the first quarter of 2026 returned to an operating loss of KRW 797 million.

Operating losses of KRW 507 million in the second quarter and KRW 483 million in the third quarter extended the run to three consecutive quarters, and third-quarter 2026 revenue of KRW 24 billion was down 51.0% year on year.

Net profit attributable to owners of KRW 3.29 billion in the third quarter of 2026 moved to profit, but it diverged from the operating loss, so the items behind it need checking.

Net profit attributable to owners over the last four quarters, from the fourth quarter of 2025 to the third quarter of 2026, totaled KRW 8.29 billion.

05

Industry analysis

Domestic drama production is structured so that broadcaster scheduling and OTT platform supply jointly create demand. As the number of platforms grows, demand for outsourced production continues, but per-episode rates and contract terms tend to be set by the bargaining power of platforms and large producers.

Mid-sized producers face large swings in revenue, because the success or failure of a single title can strongly determine annual results.

The pattern of revenue peaking in 2023 and then falling for two consecutive years through 2025 raises the question of whether company-specific scheduling gaps or industry conditions are the larger factor.

According to the company, generative AI is spreading across the entire production process, from planning and story structuring to image and video compositing, voice synthesis and post-production.

This shift could change production cost and timing structures, so it carries both opportunity and risk for smaller producers. The company is responding by using its AI video festival to identify creators with AI production capability.

Against large drama producers such as Studio Dragon and CJ ENM, the gap in business scale and supply structure is wide. Reading its position precisely requires financial and scheduling data on a comparable basis, which is a point to verify in the next annual report.

In the fourth-quarter 2026 results, the first checkpoint is whether the operating loss continues for a fourth straight quarter or turns to a profit.

Bull factors

Earnings swing from loss to profit

In 2025, both operating profit and net profit attributable to owners turned positive, reversing the direction of earnings. Net profit attributable to owners was positive in the fourth quarter of 2025 and the third quarter of 2026, with a four-quarter total of KRW 8.29 billion. On the bull side, the evidence is that earnings power has moved toward recovery.

Low debt burden and sizeable equity

Total liabilities were KRW 86 billion, and the debt-to-equity ratio of 29.8% followed a fall from 49.1% in 2022 to 26.0% in 2024 before rising slightly. A light debt load can be read as room to absorb gaps in the production schedule.

Bear factors

Two years of revenue decline and a sharp quarterly drop

Revenue peaked at KRW 241.3 billion in 2023 and then fell in 2024 and 2025 for two consecutive years. Third-quarter 2026 revenue of KRW 24 billion was down 51.0% year on year, and it had already dropped from KRW 35 billion in the fourth quarter of 2025 to KRW 24.6 billion in the first quarter of 2026.

Given that quarterly revenue moves with scheduling, whether the revenue base can recover is the core of the bearish case.

Continued operating losses and mixed profit signals

Operating losses of KRW 797 million, KRW 507 million and KRW 483 million in the first through third quarters of 2026 mean operating losses have run for three consecutive quarters.

The third-quarter net profit attributable to owners of KRW 3.29 billion moved in the opposite direction from the operating loss, so whether that profit came from core operations needs confirmation. Until operating-level recovery is shown, the durability of earnings remains in question.

Concentrated largest stake and governance review

The largest shareholder holds 39.33%, so decision-making is heavily dependent on that one holder. After trading resumed, press coverage described the removal of risks tied to the former largest shareholder as a first step toward normalization, but also reported that earnings and subsidiary status remain under review. On the bearish side, this is the first point raised.

Risk factors

Earnings volatility and scheduling dependence

Drama revenue swings sharply from quarter to quarter depending on scheduling and delivery timing. A typical example is the drop from KRW 35 billion in the fourth quarter of 2025 to KRW 24.6 billion in the first quarter of 2026, a 29.8% fall in one quarter. If new title deliveries fall into a gap, the earnings recovery could reverse.

Non-operating items and non-controlling interest uncertainty

Net profit including non-controlling interests of KRW 45.8 billion in 2024 moved in the opposite direction from the operating loss of KRW 15.4 billion and the net loss attributable to owners of KRW 4.02 billion.

When the gap between total net profit and net profit attributable to owners is large, judging the quality of earnings becomes difficult. Until the composition of this gap is confirmed in the annual report notes, the item remains a risk.

Ownership change and shareholder concentration

Because the stake is concentrated in one holder, a change in ownership could shift management direction and voting dynamics at the same time.

Resuming trading was part of clearing risks linked to the former largest shareholder, but press reports said transparency in management and subsidiary oversight still need monitoring. The effect of ownership changes on strategic continuity is also a point to watch.

Overall view

Chorokbaem Media turned both annual operating profit and net profit attributable to owners positive in 2025, and its debt-to-equity ratio remains low at 29.8%. On the other side, revenue has fallen for two years since peaking in 2023, and third-quarter 2026 revenue was down 51.0% year on year.

Operating losses have run for three quarters in 2026, so whether the swing to profit is one-off or lasting remains unresolved. Net profit attributable to owners over the last four quarters totaled KRW 8.29 billion, so the earnings direction is toward recovery, but the earnings base is small relative to revenue.

The composition of non-controlling interests and non-operating items is complex, as the gap between 2024 net profit and the net loss attributable to owners shows, so the quality of earnings needs verification.

A price-to-book ratio well below 1x shows a discount to net assets, but the short profit history limits multi-year judgment. The next checkpoints are full-year operating profit in the 2026 annual report and the fourth-quarter 2026 results. This report does not present an investment opinion or a target price.
