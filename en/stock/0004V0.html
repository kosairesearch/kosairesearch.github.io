02

Business structure

NBR Motion develops and manufactures roller-type rolling elements used in automobiles, heavy equipment and robots, and listed on KOSDAQ in January 2026 after a merger with Mirae Asset Venture SPAC 3. Its 2025 sales mix was tapered rollers 61%, steel balls 30% and bearing races 9%.

In the first half of 2026, tapered rollers accounted for 62.6% and steel balls for 36.7%, together more than 99% of sales. Bearing race sales fell to about KRW 10 million in the half-year after a change of subcontractor led to customer approval procedures.

Its 2025 end-market mix was 65% passenger vehicles and 35% robots and wind power. The company is a tier-two supplier that supplies components to global bearing module makers such as Schaeffler, SKF and NSK, which then sell complete units to vehicle, industrial equipment and robot customers.

In competition, Japanese companies have long dominated high-precision bearings, and press coverage describes NBR Motion as the only domestic tapered roller maker.

New products include silicon nitride ceramic balls for EV reducers, which completed product development in 2024, and CRB rollers, G3-grade parts for medical and precision equipment, and ceramic rollers for semiconductor equipment, which are in development.

In robotics, coverage reported an order from Nabtesco for precision rolling elements for RV reducers, with initial supply under way.

04

Earnings analysis

On a DART-filed basis, annual sales fell from KRW 63.8 billion in 2024 to KRW 63.1 billion in 2025, down 1.2% year on year. The operating loss narrowed from KRW 4.1 billion in 2024 to KRW 3.93 billion in 2025, and the operating margin improved slightly from -6.4% to -6.2%.

Over the same period, the net loss attributable to controlling shareholders widened from KRW 7.59 billion to KRW 12.2 billion, and the net loss including non-controlling interests followed the same trend. The company attributed the 2025 results to merger-related and listing-related one-time costs.

Press coverage put the one-time merger costs at about KRW 7 billion, mostly accounting entries, with the cash-basis loss at about KRW 1 billion. The operating loss also reflected about KRW 2.5 billion of inventory valuation allowances booked conservatively under listed-company rules.

Gross profit, however, turned from a loss in 2024 to a profit in 2025, and operating cash flow moved from -KRW 4.79 billion in 2024 to KRW 6.91 billion in 2025.

By quarter, Q4 2025 sales were KRW 15.2 billion, the operating loss was KRW 4.32 billion and the net loss attributable to controlling shareholders was KRW 11.9 billion. In Q1 2026 sales were KRW 15.1 billion, down 0.7% quarter on quarter, and the operating loss was KRW 1.23 billion.

In Q2 2026 sales rose 14.4% to KRW 17.2 billion and the operating loss narrowed to KRW 1.13 billion. In Q3 2026 sales fell 7.9% to KRW 15.9 billion and the operating loss widened to KRW 1.48 billion. The company said the cost burden from setting up new equipment and higher R&D spending were temporary factors.

05

Industry analysis

The core of this market is the localization of high-precision bearings. Korea Investment & Securities said in a February 2026 report that tariff policy could shift outsourced volumes from China back to Korea.

The same report rated the company's technology at the top SP level within the G5 grade and said capacity in Miryang and Changwon would absorb replacement demand for Japanese products.

Shinhan Investment Corp. said in February 2026 coverage that completing in-house ceramic ball materials could cut costs by 40% versus Japan. On the demand side, end markets are broadening from passenger vehicles to robots, aerospace and wind power.

In an August 2026 report, a researcher at the Korea IR Association said bearing component demand is diversifying into EVs, robots and aerospace. In competition, global bearing makers such as NSK, SKF and Schaeffler are both customers and incumbents in high-precision markets.

In robotics, large-cap names such as Doosan Robotics and Rainbow Robotics lead sector sentiment, and coverage notes that NBR Motion's small market capitalization makes its share price respond strongly to flows.

On the capacity cycle, Shinhan Investment Corp. noted that production capacity is saturated, so continued capital spending is a precondition for growth.

06

Outlook

The company said it would complete equipment investment and process improvements in the first half of 2026 to expand capacity, and build new orders and sales growth on that base (March 2026 coverage).

In a February 2026 report, Korea Investment & Securities expected capacity to rise by more than 20% once the expansion is complete. In August 2026 coverage, brokers said the expansion effect should appear from late Q3 and that annual profitability could turn positive in 2027.

The same coverage cited a product mix shift toward tapered rollers, partial recovery in bearing race sales and initial ceramic ball revenue as profit drivers.

As of February 2026, Korea Investment & Securities projected 2027 sales of KRW 91.1 billion, up 30.7%, an operating profit of KRW 8.6 billion and an operating margin of about 9.4%. The same report expected full-scale orders from global passenger vehicle makers from 2027.

Coverage in August 2026 said ceramic ball sales could begin in 2027 and cylindrical roller mass production could start in 2028, according to a Korea IR Association researcher.

On financing, August 2026 coverage said a private CB of up to KRW 15 billion was being prepared, while September 2026 coverage reported a decision to issue KRW 17 billion, so the reported size differs by article. The next checkpoint is whether the Q4 2026 results and the 2027 expansion effect show up in sales.

Bull factors

Localization and cost competitiveness

Its position as the only domestic tapered roller maker, and its focus on localizing high-precision bearings long dominated by Japanese companies, are cited as strengths. Shinhan Investment Corp. said in February 2026 coverage that in-house ceramic ball materials could cut costs by 40% versus Japan. The same coverage said this could widen market share.

Expanding order backlog

The order backlog rose to KRW 91.1 billion at end-June 2026 from KRW 84.7 billion at end-2025, and total orders reached KRW 123.2 billion. Half-year coverage said this laid a foundation for growth in scale. Whether the backlog converts into sales after capacity expansion is the next point to watch.

Expansion into robotics and advanced industries

Demand is diversifying into robotics, aerospace and EVs, and coverage reported a Nabtesco order for precision rolling elements and parts supply for Tesla robotaxis.

Ceramic balls completed product development in 2024, and a forecast says full-scale orders from global passenger vehicle makers could start in 2027 after commercialization in 2026.

Bear factors

Continued losses and wider net loss

The net loss attributable to controlling shareholders widened to KRW 12.2 billion in 2025, and operating losses were recorded in both 2024 and 2025.

Operating losses have continued for four straight quarters from Q4 2025 to Q3 2026, and the cumulative net loss attributable to controlling shareholders over those quarters is KRW 18.5 billion. The company calls the cost burden temporary, but the continuation of quarterly losses remains to be confirmed.

Stagnant sales and lower race revenue

Sales declined from KRW 63.8 billion in 2024 to KRW 63.1 billion in 2025, and Q3 2026 sales were KRW 15.9 billion. Bearing race sales were about KRW 5.9 billion in 2025 but fell to about KRW 10 million in the first half of 2026. Stagnant sales are overlapping with cost pressure from new equipment setup.

Funding needs and dilution risk

Cash and cash equivalents fell to KRW 1.7 billion at end-June 2026 from KRW 12.9 billion at end-2025, and first-half operating cash flow was a net outflow of KRW 4.5 billion. August 2026 coverage said a private CB was being prepared with a conversion price not adjusted for share price declines. Concerns were raised before listing that sales by financial investors could increase share price volatility.

10

Risk factors

Customer and contract structure

Passenger vehicles account for 65% of end demand as of 2025, so the business depends on vehicle demand and customer approval procedures. As the bearing race case shows, a change of subcontractor that delays final customer approval can sharply reduce sales. As a tier-two supplier, the company has limited direct control over end-customer demand.

Dilution and financing

Coverage reported that the CB terms do not adjust the conversion price for share price declines. Conversions would increase the share count and could dilute existing shareholders. Coverage also said the CB coupon and guaranteed yield were both set at 0%.

Post-listing trading flows

Concerns were raised that sales of shares by financial investors after listing could increase volatility. On the first trading day in January 2026, turnover was KRW 186.4 billion while institutions were net sellers. Foreign ownership was about 1.7% on the first trading day.

12

Overall view

NBR Motion is a KOSDAQ-listed company that emphasizes domestic tapered roller production and the localization of high-precision bearings, and it listed through a SPAC merger in January 2026. On a DART-filed basis, 2025 sales declined slightly to KRW 63.1 billion, and the operating loss narrowed to KRW 3.93 billion.

However, the net loss attributable to controlling shareholders widened to KRW 12.2 billion, and losses have continued for four straight quarters from Q4 2025 to Q3 2026.

Quarterly operating losses were KRW 1.13 billion in Q2 2026 and KRW 1.48 billion in Q3 2026, so no sign of a return to profit appears in filed results yet. Strengths include the KRW 91.1 billion order backlog at end-June 2026 and new business options in ceramic balls and robotics.

Weaknesses include stagnant sales, lower cash and funding needs through CB issuance. Broker views differ in timing and assumptions: as of February 2026, Korea Investment & Securities forecast a 2027 operating profit of KRW 8.6 billion, while August 2026 coverage cited brokers expecting annual profitability in 2027.

On valuation, no P/E can be calculated, the company pays no dividend, and the price-to-book ratio is around 1x. The next checkpoints are Q4 and full-year 2026 results, the 2026 annual report, and whether 2027 orders and sales materialize.
