02

Business structure

NVR Motion, founded in 2011, is a precision drive-component maker whose core products are taper rollers and steel balls used as rolling elements in bearings. It listed on KOSDAQ on January 14, 2026 through a merger with Mirae Asset Vision SPAC 3.

Its flagship taper rollers and steel balls supply roughly 65% to internal-combustion and electric-vehicle markets and about 35% to heavy equipment, trucks and other markets. As of first-half 2026, taper rollers accounted for 62.6% of revenue and steel balls 36.7%, together making up more than 99% of total sales.

By contrast, bearing races, which generated KRW 5.9 billion in revenue in 2024, fell to roughly KRW 10 million in first-half 2026 revenue as a subcontractor change went through final customer approval.

Its customer base includes domestic automakers and heavy-equipment makers, the Iljin Bearing Group (which approved mass production of wheel bearings in August 2025), and GM's Austin plant in North America (which awarded a G5 steel ball project in July 2025).

The company holds a subsidiary, NVR Roller, which produces taper rollers.

It has recently expanded its product lineup to include silicon nitride ceramic balls (localization begun in 2021, development completed in 2024), cylindrical roller bearings (CRB) for robots, and G3-grade products for medical and precision equipment, aiming to diversify its end markets beyond automotive into robotics, semiconductors and aerospace.

As of end-June 2026, total order value stood at KRW 123.2 billion and the order backlog at KRW 91.1 billion, up from KRW 84.7 billion at end-2025, including volumes extending beyond 2027.

04

Earnings analysis

Consolidated revenue in 2025 was KRW 63.1 billion, down 1.2% from KRW 63.8 billion in 2024. The operating loss narrowed from KRW 4.1 billion in 2024 to KRW 3.93 billion in 2025, with the operating margin improving slightly from -6.4% to -6.2%.

The attributable net loss, however, widened from KRW 7.59 billion in 2024 to KRW 12.2 billion in 2025, with one-off costs tied to the SPAC merger and an inventory valuation reserve cited as contributing factors.

On a quarterly basis, after posting revenue of KRW 15.2 billion with an operating loss of KRW 4.32 billion and an attributable net loss of KRW 11.9 billion in Q4 2025, the company reported Q1 2026 revenue of KRW 15.1 billion (down 0.7% quarter on quarter) with an operating loss of KRW 1.23 billion (loss narrowed), followed by Q2 2026 revenue of KRW 17.2 billion (up 14.4% quarter on quarter) with an operating loss of KRW 1.13 billion (loss narrowed) — marking three consecutive quarters of operating and attributable net losses from Q4 2025 through Q2 2026.

The sum of attributable net losses over the trailing four quarters (Q1-Q4 2025) was KRW 12.2 billion, matching the full-year figure.

On the balance sheet, the debt ratio fell sharply to 150.9% at end-2025 from 316.7% at end-2024, with total equity of KRW 34.4 billion (entirely attributable to the parent) against total liabilities of KRW 51.9 billion.

Cash flow also improved, with operating cash flow turning to KRW 6.91 billion in 2025 from -KRW 4.79 billion in 2024.

According to media reports, however, first-half 2026 revenue held roughly flat year on year at KRW 32.3 billion, while rising cost of sales and selling, general and administrative expenses pushed operating results from a KRW 300 million profit a year earlier into a loss of about KRW 2.4 billion.

05

Industry analysis

The market for rolling elements used in bearings — rollers and balls — has long been dominated in its highest-precision segments by Japanese and German makers, leaving Korea reliant on imports.

NVR Motion has positioned itself as the sole domestic producer of taper rollers and has pursued localization of high-precision G3-to-G5 grade steel balls and ceramic balls.

The end-market mix is clearly shifting from traditional internal-combustion and EV drivetrains toward robotics, humanoid machines, semiconductor equipment and aerospace.

In particular, the rolling elements used in humanoid robot joints — which integrate motors, reducers, bearings and sensors — are emerging as a critical component, with global projects such as Tesla's Optimus and Boston Dynamics' next-generation Atlas cited as demand drivers.

Within the same machinery sector, names such as Rainbow Robotics, Doosan Robotics, Robotis, Yuilrobotics and SPG are often discussed alongside NVR Motion, though the company sits further upstream in materials and components rather than finished robotics systems.

Management has publicly stated a strategy of broadening its customer base beyond automakers and heavy-equipment makers into components for robot reducers and ceramic rollers for semiconductor equipment.

Even so, the new business lines have yet to reach full-scale production volume, and a substantial share of existing revenue still depends on automotive and heavy-equipment bearing components, leaving sensitivity to cyclical swings in those end markets.

06

Outlook

The company plans to allocate KRW 13.5 billion of the proceeds from its September 2026 convertible bond issuance to facility investment, with KRW 10 billion earmarked for G3 ultra-precision products and a ceramic bearing assembly line, and the remainder for expanding and automating steel ball and taper roller production and building a CRB roller mass-production line.

KRW 3.5 billion is allocated to repaying high-interest debt, reducing the financial burden alongside the capacity build-out.

As part of the investment, the company is reportedly consolidating its Miryang production line into Changwon and pushing process automation to improve manufacturing cost and operating efficiency simultaneously.

In robotics and mobility, the company has received an order for precision rolling elements from Japan's Nabtesco and is supplying initial volumes, and it reportedly signed a contract through a German bearing-module OEM to supply bearing components for Tesla's third-generation driving unit worth roughly KRW 8.7 billion cumulatively over 2026-2028.

Mass production of EV reducer components for Ford, GM and Audi is reported to begin in stages across 2026 and 2027.

Korea Investment & Securities, in a 2026 report, expected 2026 performance to remain broadly similar to 2025 but projected 2027 revenue of KRW 91.1 billion (up 30.7% year on year) and operating profit of KRW 8.6 billion (up 267.6% year on year, 9.4% operating margin).

This is a brokerage forecast, however, and actual results may diverge depending on the timing of facility ramp-up and the pace of new-business commercialization.

08

Bull factors

Narrowing Losses and a Cash Flow Turnaround

The 2025 operating loss narrowed from the prior year and operating cash flow turned positive at KRW 6.91 billion. Operating and attributable net losses have continued to shrink each quarter since Q4 2025. The debt ratio also fell sharply from 316.7% to 150.9%, supporting the case for balance sheet improvement.

Entry into the Robotics and Mobility Value Chain

News of entry into the robotics and mobility supply chain has continued, including supply as a tier-2 vendor for Tesla's robotaxi and an order from Japan's Nabtesco for rolling elements.

New products such as ceramic balls and CRB rollers could serve as a foothold for diversifying beyond an automotive-centered end market into robotics, semiconductors and aerospace. The order backlog also rose to KRW 91.1 billion at end-June 2026 from end-2025.

Room for Cost Structure Improvement through Capital Investment

A significant portion of the proceeds from the KRW 17.0 billion convertible bond is slated for automation equipment and production-line consolidation. Consolidating the Miryang line into Changwon is described as able to improve both manufacturing cost and operating efficiency. The company has stated that its contribution margin rises sharply once revenue surpasses a certain threshold.

09

Bear factors

Stagnant Revenue and a Widening Net Loss

2025 revenue fell 1.2% year on year to KRW 63.1 billion, and the attributable net loss widened to KRW 12.2 billion from the prior year. A sharp decline in bearing race revenue due to delayed approval of a subcontractor change also weighed on top-line recovery. Revenue stagnation could persist until new business lines reach full scale.

Dilution Pressure from Convertible Bonds

If the KRW 17.0 billion convertible bond issued in September 2026 were fully converted, it could add new shares equal to 18.1% of the current share count. Including existing unconverted convertible bonds and warrants, potential dilution reaches 26.1% of outstanding shares.

Concerns about overhang were already highlighted early in the listing as financial investors sold down their stakes.

Cost Burden from Capacity Expansion

According to media reports, rising cost of sales and SG&A expenses in first-half 2026 pushed operating results from a profit a year earlier into a loss. New equipment set-up and higher R&D spending could weigh on profitability in the near term.

If the shift to mass production in new businesses is delayed, the timing of revenue recovery relative to the investment outlay could also be pushed back.

10

Risk factors

Financial and Dilution Risk

A sizable amount of mezzanine instruments, including convertible bonds and warrants, means dilution could materialize if conversion is exercised as the stock rises. The history of financial investors exiting quickly after listing also suggests the potential for further share supply.

While the debt ratio has improved, the need for additional funding to sustain capacity investment cannot be ruled out.

Customer and End-Market Concentration Risk

Most revenue is concentrated in two products, taper rollers and steel balls, which remain heavily dependent on automotive and heavy-equipment end markets. The first-half 2026 bearing race case demonstrated that delays in customer approval processes can directly affect revenue. The new robotics and ceramic ball businesses are reported to not yet have reached large-scale mass production.

New Business Execution Risk

New businesses such as ceramic balls, CRB rollers and rolling elements for robots are reported to still be in early supply or development stages.

Whether supply contracts with global automakers and robotics companies will expand further remains unconfirmed in some areas, so the timing of any earnings impact could vary with execution speed.

If facility ramp-up, including the integrated Changwon production line, does not proceed as planned, the expected cost efficiency gains could be delayed.

Overall view

NVR Motion posted a slight revenue decline and a widening attributable net loss in 2025, yet also showed signs of balance sheet improvement, including a narrower operating loss, a turn to positive operating cash flow, and a lower debt ratio.

Through 2026, quarterly operating losses and attributable net losses have narrowed for three straight quarters, and the company is using a KRW 17.0 billion convertible bond to fund ultra-precision and ceramic bearing facilities while also repaying debt.

In robotics and mobility, news of value-chain entry — including supply for Tesla's robotaxi program and an order from Nabtesco — points to a concrete attempt to diversify beyond an automotive-centered business structure.

Still, most revenue remains concentrated in two products, taper rollers and steel balls, and in automotive and heavy-equipment end markets, while potential equity dilution from convertible bonds and warrants remains an overhang.

The timing of new businesses reaching large-scale production and whether the capital investment delivers its intended cost benefits will likely be key variables shaping the earnings path ahead.

Readers should monitor the periodic reports to be disclosed after Q3 2026 and any further contract disclosures to assess whether the loss-narrowing trend continues.
