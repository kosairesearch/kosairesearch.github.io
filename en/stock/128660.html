An earnings base sensitive to aluminum prices

PJ Metal's annual operating profit and net income attributable to controlling shareholders rose in 2025, but a swing to an operating loss in Q3 2026 showed again how sensitive its earnings are to raw-material prices.

02

Business structure

PJ Metal was established in 2010 as a spin-off from Aldex and manufactures nonferrous metal products (cite 1-1). It produces aluminum deoxidizers used in steelmaking and billets supplied to extrusion companies, using aluminum scrap as its raw material (cite 1-5).

In Q1 2024, aluminum deoxidizers and related products generated KRW 31.0 billion, or 59.95% of revenue, while billets generated KRW 20.7 billion, or 40.05% (cite 9-13). Its main customer is POSCO's Gwangyang Works, to which it supplies deoxidizers as the core product (cite 6-14).

A company profile page describes the firm as sharing the domestic deoxidizer market with POSCO M-Tech (cite 6-15). Aluminum scrap is purchased both domestically and overseas (cite 3-3), and the company has extended into aerospace material recycling by securing airframe scrap under sourcing contracts (cite 5-6).

The 51%-owned subsidiary is pursuing recycling of lead-acid batteries and is preparing EV battery recycling (cite 9-6, 6-9, 6-10). The Q1 2024 mix is the oldest segment data here, so the latest segment shares should be rechecked in the business report. Diversifying recycled-material suppliers is a task the company itself lists (cite 4-6).

04

Earnings analysis

On a reported annual basis, revenue fell from KRW 269 billion in 2022 to KRW 229.3 billion in 2023 (down 14.8%), then rose to KRW 254.5 billion in 2024 (up 11.0%) and KRW 349 billion in 2025 (up 37.1%). 2025 was the largest year of the 2022 to 2025 period.

Operating profit went from KRW 10.4 billion in 2022 to KRW 6.01 billion in 2023 (down 42.0%), the smallest year, and then rose for two consecutive years to KRW 9.17 billion in 2024 and KRW 12.2 billion in 2025.

Operating margin, however, stayed at 3.9% in 2022, 2.6% in 2023, 3.6% in 2024 and 3.5% in 2025, so higher revenue did not translate into margin gains.

Net income attributable to controlling shareholders peaked at KRW 14 billion in 2023 (up 142.6%), bottomed at KRW 2.82 billion in 2024 (down 79.9%), and recovered to KRW 7.63 billion in 2025 (up 170.0%).

In 2024, total net income of KRW 1.05 billion was below the controlling-shareholder figure, which implies that non-controlling interests recorded a loss.

For 2025, total net income of KRW 8.46 billion was attributed by the company mainly to higher revenue from aluminum prices and better profitability at a subsidiary (cite 13-4).

Operating cash flow moved from KRW 4.06 billion in 2023 to -KRW 256 million in 2024 and -KRW 17.8 billion in 2025, so cash generation weakened in a year when profit rose.

The debt-to-equity ratio climbed to 113.3% at the end of 2025 from 81.3% a year earlier, with total liabilities of KRW 87.4 billion and total equity of KRW 77.1 billion, of which KRW 73.8 billion belongs to controlling shareholders.

Quarterly operating profit went from KRW 2.44 billion in Q3 2025 to KRW 5.89 billion, KRW 7.93 billion and KRW 11.8 billion in Q2 2026, before Q3 2026 turned to an operating loss of KRW 2.92 billion.

Controlling-shareholder net income in the same quarter was still KRW 1.32 billion, which points to non-operating items, and their composition is a point to check in the business report notes.

05

Industry analysis

Because PJ Metal's raw material and products are both aluminum, the international aluminum price is the largest external variable. A March 2026 report said that a supply halt at a major smelter linked to Middle East tensions pushed international prices up to a four-year high (cite 5-3).

In the same report, DS Investment estimated, based on past results, that operating profit rises by roughly 18 to 22 percent when the LME price increases 10% quarter on quarter, through inventory gains (cite 5-7). This is a brokerage estimate, and actual quarterly results depend on cost and inventory flows.

End demand spans steel, construction, automotive and aerospace. First-half 2025 data showed that US protectionism and a strong dollar weakened emerging-market demand, while a domestic construction slump and weak auto exports compounded the drag (cite 1-3).

On the steel cycle, analysts expected profitability to improve alongside a steel-industry recovery (cite 7-5). Competitively, the domestic deoxidizer market is described as shared with POSCO M-Tech (cite 6-15).

Recycled-material sourcing is also a cost variable, and the company's plan to respond early to raw-material price swings reflects that exposure (cite 15-5).

06

Outlook

On March 27, 2026, the company disclosed a value-up plan targeting 5% average annual revenue growth and a 5% operating margin over 2026 to 2028 (cite 15-3, 15-8). The levers it cites are early responses to raw-material price swings and diversifying recycled-material suppliers (cite 15-5).

The plan also includes boosting revenue through export market development and new steel grade development (cite 15-6), and developing higher-value products and niche markets (cite 15-8). Environmental and safety investment for greener sites is part of the plan (cite 15-4).

On shareholder returns, the payout ratio for fiscal 2025 was 55.28%, and the company changed its dividend procedure to fix the dividend amount before setting the record date (cite 4-8, 4-10).

The subsidiary's battery recycling business has been presented as a future growth engine (cite 6-10), and the subsidiary was preparing recycling operations as of 2025 (cite 1-8). A 5% operating margin target is above the 3.5% reported for 2025, so progress will be measured on annual margins.

Whether the Q3 2026 operating loss is temporary or structural will shape the route to the target. The 2026 annual business report, the next regular filing, is the first checkpoint for annual operating margin and segment information.

Bull factors

Inventory gains from rising aluminum prices

Rising international aluminum prices create inventory gains on held stock. The company attributed higher 2025 revenue mainly to aluminum price increases (cite 13-4). A brokerage estimate put the operating profit sensitivity at roughly 18 to 22 percent for each 10% quarterly rise in the LME price (cite 5-7). Reported 2025 results show that the price upswing did lift profit.

Value-up plan and dividends

The company disclosed a target of 5% average annual revenue growth and a 5% operating margin for 2026 to 2028 (cite 15-3). Its fiscal 2025 payout ratio was 55.28% (cite 4-8). It also announced a procedure change that fixes the dividend amount before setting the record date (cite 4-10). The targets and shareholder return plan were published in formal disclosures.

Recycled feedstock and new business lines

The company plans to diversify recycled-material suppliers to lower costs (cite 15-5). Its subsidiary is preparing battery recycling and is presented as a future growth engine (cite 6-10). Sourcing aerospace scrap under contracts has extended the company into aerospace material recycling (cite 5-6). A broader feedstock base can make the business more resilient to raw-material swings.

Bear factors

Swing to operating loss and volatile quarterly profit

Q3 2026 revenue was KRW 103.4 billion, down 15.8% from the prior quarter, and operating profit swung to a loss of KRW 2.92 billion. That compares with KRW 11.8 billion in operating profit in Q2 2026, showing wide quarterly variation.

Controlling-shareholder net income of KRW 1.32 billion in the same quarter depended on items outside operations, which makes the core business signal weaker. The business report will provide the cause of the operating loss.

Thin operating margin and weaker cash flow

From 2022 to 2025, the operating margin stayed between 2.6% and 3.9%. Operating cash flow was -KRW 17.8 billion in 2025 and -KRW 256 million in 2024. The debt-to-equity ratio rose to 113.3% in 2025, with total liabilities of KRW 87.4 billion. Weaker cash generation in a year of higher profit is a negative signal.

Raw-material prices and demand softness

First-half 2025 data described weakened emerging-market demand due to US protectionism and a strong dollar (cite 1-3). A domestic construction slump and weak auto exports also reduced sales capacity (cite 1-7). The company itself said it must respond early to raw-material price swings (cite 15-5). If prices fall, inventory gains can turn into losses.

Risk factors

Raw-material and price risk

Scrap feedstock and aluminum product prices move together, so price swings can sharply shift profit. The company has said it will respond early to raw-material price changes and diversify recycled-material suppliers (cite 15-5). Whether price and inventory movements contributed to the Q3 2026 operating loss remains a point to check.

Balance sheet and cash-flow risk

The debt-to-equity ratio rose from 81.3% in 2024 to 113.3% in 2025, with total liabilities of KRW 87.4 billion at year-end 2025. Operating cash flow was -KRW 17.8 billion in 2025. If cash does not follow reported profit, working-capital pressure can rise. The components of the cash flow statement are a point to check in the business report.

Customer concentration and industry risk

Heavy reliance on POSCO's Gwangyang Works ties results to the steel cycle (cite 6-14). In first-half 2025, a domestic construction slump and weak auto exports compounded the drag (cite 1-3). With the domestic deoxidizer market shared with POSCO M-Tech (cite 6-15), the pace of new demand matters. Export market development is one part of the company's plan and a test of demand diversification.

12

Overall view

PJ Metal makes aluminum deoxidizers and billets, and its results recovered in 2025 with revenue of KRW 349 billion and net income attributable to controlling shareholders of KRW 7.63 billion. Still, the operating margin stayed in the mid-3% range, and operating cash flow was -KRW 17.8 billion in 2025.

An operating loss of KRW 2.92 billion in Q3 2026 turned the quarter negative, and because controlling-shareholder net income was still positive, the mix of non-operating items is a key point to verify.

Supporting factors include inventory gains when aluminum prices rise, the 2026 to 2028 value-up targets, and a 55.28% payout ratio for fiscal 2025. Constraining factors include sharp quarterly swings, a higher debt-to-equity ratio, and exposure to raw-material prices and steel, construction and auto demand.

On valuation, the price-to-book ratio is below 1 times, but because earnings are volatile, multiples should be read alongside the quarterly trend. The next checkpoint is the 2026 annual business report, where the cause of the operating loss and progress toward the 5% margin target will be central.
