Operating turnaround, but financial gains swing the bottom line

Fursys returned to operating profit in Q3 2026 with KRW 6.15 billion, yet a KRW 21 billion net loss attributable to controlling shareholders shows how financial valuation swings now drive its bottom line.

02

Business structure

Fursys is a furniture maker centered on office furniture, with product lines that include office furniture, chairs, desks and cabinets. Of Q3 2026 revenue of KRW 97.1 billion, product sales were KRW 86.9 billion, up 13.7% year on year.

Interior construction sales over the same period rose from KRW 3.1 billion to KRW 9.3 billion, more than tripling. Domestic plants are located in Ansung and Chungju 1 and 2, with first-half 2026 utilization at 71.7 percent for Chungju 1 and 65.0 percent each for Ansung and Chungju 2.

Overseas, the Dong Nai Province plant in Vietnam produces sofas, chairs and wooden furniture and ran at 85.0 percent utilization in the same period, above the domestic plants. Product sales account for most revenue, with interior construction playing a supporting role.

Business press reports describe competition in the domestic office furniture market as intensifying.

Fursys operates alongside its sister brand within the Fursys Group, and both units were reported to face weak domestic business and persistent losses at overseas subsidiaries as of Q3 2025, with overseas units in North America, China and Vietnam all loss-making.

Fursys therefore reads as two drivers: domestic office furniture demand and utilization at its overseas production base.

04

Earnings analysis

On the reported annual figures, revenue moved from KRW 381.3 billion in 2022 to KRW 362.9 billion in 2023 (down 4.8 percent), then rose to KRW 385.7 billion in 2024 (up 6.3 percent), which was the largest year of 2022 to 2025, before falling to KRW 358.2 billion in 2025 (down 7.1 percent), the smallest year of the period.

Profit followed a different path. Operating profit went from KRW 32.7 billion in 2022 to KRW 35.2 billion in 2023 (up 7.9 percent), the peak, then fell to KRW 21.5 billion in 2024 (down 39.0 percent) and KRW 5.74 billion in 2025 (down 73.3 percent), declining two years in a row from 2024 to 2025.

Operating margin moved from 8.6 percent in 2022 and 9.7 percent in 2023 to 5.6 percent in 2024 and 1.6 percent in 2025. Although 2024 was the largest revenue year, margin compression made the profit decline far steeper than the revenue decline.

Net profit attributable to controlling shareholders rose from KRW 5.88 billion in 2022 to KRW 66.9 billion in 2023 (up 1,038.3 percent), then fell to KRW 47.7 billion in 2024 (down 28.7 percent) and KRW 35.6 billion in 2025 (down 25.4 percent).

The 2023 jump in net profit, which exceeded operating profit of KRW 35.2 billion, reads as a result of non-operating items. Net profit including non-controlling interests was reported as KRW 5.88 billion, KRW 66.9 billion, KRW 47.7 billion and KRW 35.6 billion for 2022 through 2025.

At the end of 2025, total equity of KRW 597.7 billion was entirely attributable to controlling shareholders, with total liabilities of KRW 72.5 billion and a debt ratio of 12.1 percent.

Operating cash flow was positive in all four years, at KRW 26.4 billion in 2022, KRW 50 billion in 2023, KRW 20.5 billion in 2024 and KRW 28.2 billion in 2025.

Quarterly, operating profit swung from a KRW 2.01 billion loss in Q3 2025 to a KRW 2.42 billion profit in Q4 2025, then to a KRW 2.07 billion loss in Q1 2026 and a KRW 5.42 billion loss in Q2 2026, before returning to a KRW 6.15 billion profit in Q3 2026.

Revenue rose from KRW 80.1 billion in Q3 2025 to KRW 92.1 billion in Q4 2025 and KRW 97.6 billion in Q1 2026, dipped to KRW 87.6 billion in Q2 2026, and recovered to KRW 97.1 billion in Q3 2026.

In Q2 2026, net profit attributable to controlling shareholders was KRW 31.7 billion despite the operating loss, so non-operating items supported earnings in that quarter. The sum of net profit attributable to controlling shareholders over the last four quarters was KRW 25.7 billion.

05

Industry analysis

Demand for domestic office furniture is strongly tied to corporate investment and the property market. Business press analysis from Money in pointed to a property downturn and other external factors delaying a recovery in domestic furniture demand.

The same report noted that Fursys standalone EBITDA fell to KRW 15.3 billion in Q3 2025, with an EBITDA margin of 6.6 percent. A report on the August 2026 earnings release tied the profit decline to higher raw and subsidiary material prices.

Competition in the domestic market is intensifying, so price defense and cost control are both required. Overseas, the company expanded into the United States, China and Vietnam, yet losses at Fursys overseas subsidiaries totaled KRW 5.8 billion through Q3 2025.

The 13.7 percent rise in Q3 2026 product sales is confirmed, but one quarter alone is not enough to judge a domestic demand recovery.

Compared with peers in the textile, fashion and lifestyle sector, where many companies are apparel-focused, Fursys combines office furniture manufacturing with overseas production bases, so its earnings drivers differ.

Vietnam utilization running above the domestic plants suggests overseas production could be linked to export demand.

06

Outlook

The key medium-term item is the expansion of Fursys production base in Vietnam. The second plant in Dong Nai Province is under construction from March 2026 to March 2027, with a total floor area of 88,704 square meters. After completion, annual capacity is expected to rise from about 1 million to about 2 million units.

The company plans to start a third expansion phase at the local production site in 2028 and to strengthen local product development and R&D. In the first half of 2026 it injected about KRW 15.1 billion more into the Vietnam unit, following about KRW 15.4 billion in the first half of 2025.

High utilization of 85.0 percent at the Vietnam plant is cited as the rationale for expansion. However, Vietnam subsidiary revenue fell 12.8 percent to KRW 25.9 billion in the same period, and the unit swung to a loss.

On the operating side, the question is whether revenue growth and the Q3 2026 operating profit can be sustained.

Financial assets measured at fair value through profit or loss were about KRW 181.3 billion at the end of Q3 2026, and the company attributed the drop in financial income to lower valuations of its held financial products.

The direction of financial gains therefore remains the variable that determines the direction of net profit attributable to controlling shareholders.

08

Bull factors

Core sales and operating profit improving together

According to a Newsway report, Q3 2026 revenue of KRW 97.1 billion rose 21.3 percent year on year, while selling and administrative expenses fell from KRW 28.4 billion to KRW 26.1 billion over the same period. Product sales of KRW 86.9 billion grew 13.7 percent, and interior construction sales more than tripled. As a result, operating profit of KRW 6.15 billion replaced a prior-year operating loss.

Balance sheet headroom

Total equity at the end of 2025 was KRW 597.7 billion against total liabilities of KRW 72.5 billion, a debt ratio of 12.1 percent. The debt ratio has declined from 15.2 percent in 2022 to 12.6 percent in 2023, 12.2 percent in 2024 and 12.1 percent in 2025.

Financial assets measured at fair value through profit or loss were about KRW 181.3 billion at the end of Q3 2026.

Vietnam production base and utilization

Utilization at the Vietnam plant was 85.0 percent in the first half of 2026, above 65.0 percent at Ansung, 71.7 percent at Chungju 1 and 65.0 percent at Chungju 2. The company plans to build a second plant to lift annual capacity from about 1 million to about 2 million units. It also injected about KRW 15.1 billion more into the Vietnam unit in the same period.

09

Bear factors

High volatility from financial income

Financial income in Q3 2026 fell to negative KRW 32.2 billion, a KRW 48.1 billion decline from a year earlier, which turned pre-tax profit into a loss. A company official attributed the main cause to lower valuations of held financial products.

The KRW 21 billion loss attributable to controlling shareholders despite operating profit shows how much earnings depend on financial variables.

Declining profitability trend

Annual operating margin declined from 8.6 percent in 2022 and 9.7 percent in 2023 to 5.6 percent in 2024 and 1.6 percent in 2025. Operating profit of KRW 5.74 billion in 2025 is far below the 2023 peak of KRW 35.2 billion.

The company attributed the consolidated first-half 2026 operating loss of KRW 7.49 billion to higher raw material costs combined with spending.

Overseas losses and investment burden

Through Q3 2025, Fursys overseas subsidiaries posted losses of KRW 5.8 billion, and reports said the North American, Chinese and Vietnamese units were all loss-making. In the first half of 2026 the Vietnam unit swung to a loss, with revenue of KRW 25.9 billion down 12.8 percent. If the capacity expansion does not translate into profit, the investment burden could accumulate.

10

Risk factors

Sustainability of quarterly operating profit

Q3 2026 operating profit of KRW 6.15 billion came after operating losses of KRW 2.07 billion in Q1 2026 and KRW 5.42 billion in Q2 2026. Since Q3 2025, quarterly operating results have alternated between profit and loss, so the direction has not been stable.

Whether the Q3 profit carries into Q4 2026 and Q1 2027 has not yet been confirmed. If profitability holds for only one or two quarters, judging the recovery in full-year 2026 operating profit could take longer.

Recovery in domestic office furniture demand

Domestic office furniture demand depends heavily on corporate investment and the property market. Analysts say a property downturn is delaying a recovery in domestic furniture demand. Product sales growth in Q3 2026 is confirmed, but one quarter alone is not enough to judge a demand recovery.

If the recovery stays slow, the Ansung and Chungju plants may continue to carry spare production capacity at their current utilization levels.

Second Vietnam plant construction schedule

The second Vietnam plant in Dong Nai Province, with a total floor area of 88,704 square meters, is scheduled to be under construction until March 2027. If completion slips, the plan to lift annual capacity from about 1 million to about 2 million units would also be delayed.

The planned start of the third expansion phase in 2028 could also shift with the plant's schedule. Construction delays would push back the timing when the capacity gains show up in results.

12

Overall view

Fursys returned to operating profit in Q3 2026 with revenue of KRW 97.1 billion and operating profit of KRW 6.15 billion.

However, financial income deteriorated to negative KRW 32.2 billion, producing a KRW 21 billion loss attributable to controlling shareholders, so the direction of earnings now depends more on financial asset valuations than on core operations.

On an annual basis, operating profit peaked at KRW 35.2 billion in 2023 and declined two years in a row through 2025, when the operating margin was 1.6 percent. The balance sheet is comparatively light, with a debt ratio of 12.1 percent at the end of 2025, and operating cash flow was positive in all four years.

The Vietnam production base is expanding toward completion of the second plant in March 2027, supported by 85.0 percent utilization, while weak domestic demand, cost pressure and overseas losses remain negative factors.

Because both strengths and weaknesses are present, the next checkpoints are the operating margin in the 2026 annual report and the direction of financial income in Q1 2027.
