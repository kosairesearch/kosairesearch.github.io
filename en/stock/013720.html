Report overview

Balance-sheet restructuring and continued losses in an engine-parts business

Cheongbo is an auto engine-parts maker whose automotive-parts segment accounts for 99% of its business. It restructured its balance sheet in 2026 through asset sales, long-term borrowing and a capital reduction, yet net losses attributable to controlling shareholders have run for five straight quarters.

02

Business structure

Cheongbo was founded in 1978 to make automotive parts and castings and has been listed on KOSDAQ since 1996.

According to the company profile, its casting and precision machining technology, built up over more than half a century, supports engine parts for the commercial-vehicle and powertrain markets, including valve tappets and chambers.

Its product lineup includes valve tappets, valve seat rings, valve guides, rocker arms, insert chambers and ladder frames. Automotive parts account for 99% of its business segments.

Press reports said tappets and housings made up about 50% and about 49% of product sales in 2025, so these two items account for most of product revenue.

Named key customers include HD Construction Equipment, a construction-equipment engine customer, and Daimler Truck, which is moving to next-generation engines for medium and heavy trucks.

The North American agricultural-equipment market is also described as having finished inventory adjustment and entered a recovery phase.

At its June 2026 extraordinary shareholder meeting, the company added defense-products development and manufacturing, aircraft and unmanned aircraft maintenance, missile development and MRO maintenance services to its business purposes.

The same agenda removed most of its earlier blockchain, crypto-asset, AI and drone-related purposes. The company's Korean name changed to Cheongbo in June 2026, and its English name changed to CHEONGBO INDUSTRIAL CO., LTD.

On November 10, 2026, the board resolved to absorb its wholly owned energy subsidiary through a small-scale merger.

04

Earnings analysis

Annual revenue fell from KRW 36 billion in 2022 to KRW 31.4 billion in 2023 (down 12.9% year on year) and KRW 24.4 billion in 2024 (down 22.0%), then edged up to KRW 24.9 billion in 2025 (up 1.9%). Within 2022–2025, 2022 was the largest year and 2024 the smallest.

Operating profit rose from KRW 393 million in 2022 to KRW 787 million in 2023 (up 100.1%) and KRW 1.41 billion in 2024 (up 78.8%), then fell to KRW 762 million in 2025 (down 45.9%). The operating margin was 1.1% in 2022, 2.5% in 2023, 5.8% in 2024 and 3.1% in 2025.

The highest margin came in 2024, the year revenue declined, which points to a change in the earnings structure; the reasons for the 2025 margin decline cannot be isolated from the disclosed figures alone.

The net loss attributable to controlling shareholders was KRW 29.7 billion in 2022, KRW 16.6 billion in 2023, KRW 14.8 billion in 2024 and KRW 12.2 billion in 2025, a loss-making run of four consecutive years with the loss narrowing after 2023. Net loss including non-controlling interests shows the same yearly figures.

Net losses persisted in 2023–2025 despite positive operating profit, which suggests non-operating items and interest costs weighed on the bottom line.

Quarterly, operating profit of KRW 456 million in the third quarter of 2025 turned into an operating loss of KRW 267 million in the fourth quarter, and the first quarter of 2026 posted an operating loss of KRW 504 million.

Operating profit returned to KRW 1.01 billion in the second quarter of 2026 but fell to KRW 135 million in the third quarter, down 86.7% from the prior quarter and 70.5% from a year earlier.

The net loss attributable to controlling shareholders was largest in the fourth quarter of 2025 at KRW 9.13 billion, narrowed to KRW 738 million in the first quarter of 2026, then widened to KRW 2.25 billion in the second quarter and KRW 2.15 billion in the third. The last four quarters total KRW 14.3 billion.

Revenue went from KRW 6.8 billion in the third quarter of 2025 to KRW 5.41 billion in the fourth quarter (down 20.5% quarter on quarter), recovered to KRW 6.59 billion and KRW 7.99 billion in the first and second quarters of 2026, and was KRW 7.27 billion in the third quarter (down 9.0% quarter on quarter, up 6.9% year on year).

At the end of 2025, total equity was KRW 58.4 billion, all of it attributable to controlling shareholders, and total liabilities were KRW 26.4 billion. The debt ratio was 74.4% in 2022, 59.9% in 2023, 35.2% in 2024 and 45.2% in 2025.

Operating cash flow was KRW -1.34 billion in 2022, then KRW 5.65 billion in 2023, KRW 4 billion in 2024 and KRW 2.72 billion in 2025, staying positive after 2023.

The first-quarter 2026 inflows came from a KRW 10.5 billion stake sale and KRW 17 billion in new long-term borrowing, which are one-off in nature rather than operating results. On a separate-financial-statement basis, the debt ratio rose from 40.6% to 60.7% in the same quarter.

05

Industry analysis

Given its business structure, the relevant peer group is automotive parts rather than media and entertainment. End markets are construction-equipment engines (HD Construction Equipment), medium and heavy truck engines (Daimler Truck) and agricultural equipment.

According to an information service's commentary on the first half of 2026, stronger demand for engines at HD Construction Equipment and a stronger order base from the newly completed Gunsan plant contributed to revenue growth.

The same commentary cited the completion of inventory adjustment in North American agricultural equipment and its move into a recovery phase as another driver. Next-generation engine production at Daimler Truck was presented as supporting medium- and long-term demand for medium and heavy truck engine parts.

Taken together, the first half of 2026, when stronger demand from HD Construction Equipment overlapped with the end of agricultural-equipment inventory adjustment, was described as a recovery period.

The addition of defense and aviation MRO business purposes reads as an attempt to widen demand beyond engine parts, but related revenue is not yet separated in the disclosed results. Revenue of KRW 24.9 billion in 2025 remains below the KRW 36 billion recorded in 2022.

Because automotive parts account for nearly all of the business, results move closely with engine-parts demand. On competition, the company presents its casting and precision machining technology in commercial-vehicle and powertrain parts as its strength.

The confirmed near-term event is the absorption merger of the energy subsidiary. The merger contract date is November 13, 2026, and the shareholder record date is November 26, 2026. According to an information service, HD Construction Equipment is developing hydrogen engines with mass production targeted for 2026.

Daimler Truck's next-generation engine production was presented as a medium- and long-term pillar of demand for medium and heavy truck parts. The completion of the Gunsan plant has widened the production base, and its effect should appear in full-year 2026 results and subsequent quarterly revenue trends.

Defense, aviation and MRO business purposes were added at the June 2026 shareholder meeting, and when and how much revenue they generate should become visible in future periodic reports.

On financing, long-term borrowing of KRW 20 billion at the end of the first quarter of 2026 (separate-statement basis) and a remaining balance of KRW 7.5 billion on the 13th convertible bond series (as of the June 17, 2026 disclosure) could bring interest and repayment burdens, making the trend in non-operating items an important checkpoint.

On equity, how the accumulated deficit of KRW 74.2 billion at the end of the first half of 2026 changes in the next periodic report is the key item to track. Full-year 2026 results will be fixed in the 2026 annual report, which is generally due within 90 days after fiscal year-end, around late March 2027.

Bull factors

Wider demand from construction-equipment and truck engine customers

Commentary from an information service cites stronger engine demand at HD Construction Equipment and the completed Gunsan plant as strengthening the order base. North American agricultural-equipment inventory adjustment is described as finished, with the market in recovery.

Daimler Truck's next-generation engine production was presented as supporting medium- and long-term demand for medium and heavy truck parts. These points come from that commentary.

Margin recovery and a return to operating profit in Q2 2026

The operating margin rose from 1.1% in 2022 to 5.8% in 2024, the highest level in the disclosed period. Operating profit turned positive at KRW 1.01 billion in the second quarter of 2026. The bullish case treats this as evidence of a recovery in core earnings.

Operating profit then fell to KRW 135 million in the third quarter, but the quarter-level return to profit remains a fact.

Improved short-term liquidity indicators

According to a Deal Site report, the current ratio on a separate-statement basis improved from 155% to 374% at the end of the first quarter of 2026. Operating cash inflow was KRW 800 million in the same period, and cash and cash equivalents rose by KRW 16.2 billion.

Asset sales and borrowing drove the change, but the bullish case cites the easing of short-term repayment pressure. The KRW 10.5 billion stake sale and KRW 17 billion borrowing were used to secure liquidity.

09

Bear factors

Five straight loss-making quarters and renewed losses

Net losses attributable to controlling shareholders have continued for five straight quarters since the third quarter of 2025. The fourth quarter of 2025 was the largest at KRW 9.13 billion. The last four quarters total KRW 14.3 billion, above the KRW 12.2 billion annual loss for 2025. The bearish case points to the lack of a sustained quarterly improvement in earnings.

Higher borrowing and convertible bond repayment pressure

Long-term borrowings rose from KRW 3 billion at the end of 2025 to KRW 20 billion at the end of the first quarter of 2026 on a separate-statement basis. The debt ratio also rose from 40.6% to 60.7%.

A balance of KRW 7.5 billion remains on the 13th convertible bond series after a June 2026 partial repurchase, and some observers expect the repayment burden to continue. The bearish view holds that liquidity filled by asset sales is one-off.

Operating profit setback and accumulated deficit

Operating profit in the third quarter of 2026 was KRW 135 million, down 70.5% from a year earlier. The accumulated deficit reached KRW 74.2 billion at the end of the first half of 2026, up from KRW 71.2 billion at the end of 2025.

The capital reduction offset accumulated losses on the equity side and was not an operating recovery. The bearish case holds that core earnings are not keeping pace with the equity recovery.

Risk factors

Financial and liquidity

Borrowing and convertible bond repayment are overlapping, which raises the weight of interest costs and cash-flow management. A private fund's payment on a CB balance was delayed for more than 14 months, and the company decided to retire that CB.

In June 2026 the company bought back KRW 1 billion of the 13th series early for KRW 1.19 billion in cash. Further repurchases or cancellations could add to funding needs.

Shareholder structure and share-price volatility

A registered director, Bae Jin-han, reported holding 3,978,042 shares, or 6.54%, as of June 26, 2026. On July 31, 2026, the stock was designated for one day as an investment-caution issue on price and volume criteria. After the capital reduction, the number of listed shares changed to 12,165,628 from July 28, 2026. Changes in the ownership structure and float may add to share-price volatility.

Business concentration and new-business execution

Most revenue comes from automotive engine parts, and the company's name has changed three times since 2021. Blockchain, AI and drone-related business purposes added in June 2025 were removed within a year.

Whether the defense and aviation business and the energy-subsidiary merger translate into results remains uncertain. A history of frequent changes in direction makes it harder to judge continuity in new businesses.

Overall view

Cheongbo's core business is making auto engine parts, and it has posted losses for four consecutive years from 2022 to 2025.

The net loss attributable to controlling shareholders narrowed to KRW 12.2 billion in 2025, but in 2026 quarterly operating profit fell to KRW 135 million in the third quarter after a positive second quarter, and net losses have run for five straight quarters.

On financing, asset sales of KRW 10.5 billion and KRW 17 billion in long-term borrowing improved liquidity, but long-term borrowing and the debt ratio rose and the accumulated deficit grew to KRW 74.2 billion.

The bullish evidence on the business side is wider demand from HD Construction Equipment, the Gunsan plant and Daimler Truck's production. The bearish evidence covers continued losses, borrowing burden, accumulated deficit and the operating profit setback.

Defense, aviation and MRO businesses and the energy-subsidiary merger are variables that have not yet shown up in results.

The next checkpoints are full-year 2026 results and the operating margin and net loss trend in the 2026 annual report, due around late March 2027, along with the remaining level of long-term borrowing.
