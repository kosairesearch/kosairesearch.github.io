Report overview

Capital Overhaul and Convertible Bonds Amid Engine-Parts Rebound

Cheongbo is carrying out a major capital and business overhaul-including an 80 percent capital reduction, a corporate name change, and new convertible bond issuance-even as second-quarter 2026 engine-parts revenue rebounded while the net loss widened.

  1. 1

    First-half 2026 consolidated revenue rose 14.8 percent year on year, but operating profit fell 11.0 percent and the net loss widened 51.8 percent.

  2. 2

  3. 3

  4. 4

  5. 5

02

Business structure

Cheongbo was founded in 1978 as an auto-parts and casting manufacturer and listed on KOSDAQ in 1996, producing commercial-vehicle and powertrain components using casting and precision-machining technology built up over more than five decades.

Its core products are engine parts such as valve tappets and chambers, with HD Hyundai Construction Equipment and other construction- and agricultural-machinery OEMs as key customers.

In the first half of 2026, expanded engine-segment demand from HD Hyundai Construction Equipment, a strengthened order base following completion of a new Gunsan plant, and the end of inventory adjustment in the North American agricultural-machinery market followed by a recovery phase are cited as contributors to revenue growth.

During the first half of 2026 the company held an extraordinary shareholders' meeting that reverted its corporate name from THE CUBE& to Cheongbo, while simultaneously resolving a capital reduction without compensation and a reduction of statutory reserves to offset accumulated deficits.

In the same process, the company decided to take an equity stake in power-construction firm Daeyeon ENC in addition to its core engine-parts manufacturing, in an apparent attempt at business diversification.

Daeyeon ENC is a specialist in transmission and distribution construction, and its semiconductor and industrial-plant segment was disclosed as a new target market with no confirmed order track record yet.

Cheongbo is thus at a transitional stage in which its traditional role as an engine and powertrain parts supplier and a search for new businesses through capital restructuring and equity investment are proceeding in parallel.

Classified under the auto-parts sector, the company competes with domestic and overseas suppliers of commercial-vehicle and construction-equipment engine parts.

04

Earnings analysis

Consolidated revenue fell from KRW 36.02 billion in 2022 to KRW 31.36 billion in 2023 and KRW 24.45 billion in 2024, then edged up to KRW 24.91 billion in 2025. The operating margin improved from 1.1 percent in 2022 to 2.5 percent in 2023 and 5.8 percent in 2024, before slipping back to 3.1 percent in 2025.

The debt ratio declined from 74.4 percent in 2022 to 59.9 percent in 2023 and 35.2 percent in 2024, then rose again to 45.2 percent in 2025. The net loss narrowed from KRW 29.70 billion in 2022 to KRW 16.63 billion in 2023 and KRW 14.78 billion in 2024, but a loss of KRW 12.18 billion continued in 2025.

On a quarterly basis, revenue improved from KRW 6.32 billion with KRW 0.29 billion operating profit in the second quarter of 2025 to KRW 6.80 billion with KRW 0.46 billion operating profit in the third quarter, before revenue fell to KRW 5.41 billion and an operating loss of KRW 0.27 billion was recorded in the fourth quarter.

The fourth-quarter 2025 net loss reached KRW 9.13 billion, far exceeding the operating loss, which is interpreted as reflecting sizable non-operating loss items.

In the first quarter of 2026, revenue rose to KRW 6.59 billion yet the company posted an operating loss of KRW 0.50 billion and a net loss of KRW 0.74 billion.

In the second quarter of 2026, revenue reached KRW 7.99 billion, the largest in the last five quarters, and operating profit turned positive at KRW 1.01 billion, but the net loss widened to KRW 2.25 billion.

Summed over the trailing four quarters from the third quarter of 2025 through the second quarter of 2026, revenue totaled KRW 26.79 billion and operating profit KRW 0.70 billion, while the net loss reached KRW 13.20 billion.

05

Industry analysis

The auto engine and powertrain parts industry in which Cheongbo operates is closely tied to the capital-spending and inventory cycles of commercial-vehicle, construction-equipment, and agricultural-machinery OEMs.

In the first half of 2026, expanded engine-segment demand from HD Hyundai Construction Equipment and a strengthened order base following the start-up of a new Gunsan plant served as the main drivers of the revenue recovery.

The North American agricultural-machinery market is assessed as having completed its inventory adjustment and entered a recovery phase.

Over the medium to long term, HD Hyundai Construction Equipment is developing a hydrogen engine targeting mass production in 2026, and Daimler Truck is also preparing to mass-produce a next-generation engine, feeding expectations of solid demand for heavy-duty truck engine parts.

This also implies, however, a high degree of dependence on specific OEM and heavy-equipment customers, meaning results can be sensitive to changes in those customers' production plans or order timing.

At the same time, the company is seeking new demand from the semiconductor and industrial-plant sectors through its equity stake in a power-construction firm, though this remains at an early stage with no order track record yet.

Within the sector, Cheongbo is a small KOSDAQ-listed company that is relatively disadvantaged in revenue scale and financial strength compared with larger auto-parts suppliers.

Subscription and payment for the newest convertible bond are scheduled for October 8, making it a point to check whether the Daeyeon ENC stake acquisition is completed as planned.

In the core business, HD Hyundai Construction Equipment's hydrogen-engine mass-production timeline and Daimler Truck's next-generation engine demand remain medium-to-long-term revenue variables.

Whether new investee Daeyeon ENC can secure actual orders in the semiconductor and industrial-plant segments will also determine the success of the diversification effort.

On the financial side, with recent bank borrowings totaling roughly KRW 17.0 billion, how repayment and borrowing terms are managed also bears watching.

Bull factors

Core Business Demand Recovery

Second-quarter 2026 revenue reached KRW 7.99 billion, the highest of the last five quarters, and operating profit turned positive at KRW 1.01 billion.

Expanded engine demand from HD Hyundai Construction Equipment, the start-up of the new Gunsan plant, and the completion of North American agricultural-machinery inventory adjustment are seen as contributors to the recovery. Despite quarter-to-quarter volatility, core-business revenue itself shows an upward trend.

Medium-to-Long-Term Engine Parts Demand Base

HD Hyundai Construction Equipment is developing a hydrogen engine targeting 2026 mass production, and Daimler Truck is also preparing to mass-produce a next-generation engine, feeding expectations of solid medium-to-long-term demand for heavy-duty truck engine parts.

Such customer product transitions could translate into additional order opportunities for engine-parts suppliers like Cheongbo.

Attempted Balance-Sheet Cleanup

The company cleaned up its book-value capital structure through an 80 percent capital reduction and a cut in statutory reserves to offset accumulated deficits, and as a result the current ratio at the end of the first half rose sharply to 346 percent from 160 percent at the end of the prior year.

However, it should be noted that this came alongside asset sales and increased borrowing as part of the broader restructuring.

Bear factors

Widening Net Loss

The net loss in the first half of 2026 grew 51.8 percent year on year, and in the fourth quarter of 2025 the net loss reached KRW 9.13 billion, far exceeding the operating loss. Despite revenue recovery, the bottom-line loss has repeatedly widened rather than narrowed.

Convertible Bond Dilution Overhang

The combined balance of the 14th, 15th, and newest convertible bond series is KRW 15.1 billion, with potential issuable shares equal to 26.91 percent of currently outstanding shares. If conversion requests accelerate, the resulting increase in shares outstanding could weigh on per-share metrics.

Governance Concerns

According to one media report, Cheongbo newly lent a total of KRW 5.0 billion to management, a subsidiary, and an investment partnership during the same half-year in which it pursued a capital reduction aimed at improving its financial structure.

The report also noted that the specific terms and repayment plans of these fund disbursements are not fully explained by the disclosures alone.

Risk factors

Financial Soundness

Accumulated losses remain large, with unappropriated deficit of roughly KRW 63.6 billion on a separate basis and total deficit of about KRW 74.2 billion on a consolidated basis, while bank borrowings have recently grown to a total of around KRW 17.0 billion. If losses continue, the possibility of again approaching capital-impairment thresholds cannot be ruled out.

Market Alerts and Volatility

Cheongbo was designated an investment-caution issue twice, on July 31 and August 4, 2026, with one instance attributed to concentrated trading in a small number of accounts whose top-10 buy-involvement ratio reached 57.82 percent. The short-term volatility typical of small, low-liquidity stocks warrants attention.

New Business Uncertainty

The equity investment in Daeyeon ENC, funded through convertible bond proceeds, begins with no confirmed order track record in the targeted semiconductor and industrial-plant markets.

Synergies with the existing transmission-and-distribution construction business and the timing of any actual revenue contribution have not yet been confirmed.

Overall view

Cheongbo continues its core engine and powertrain parts supply business dating back to 1978, while in 2026 simultaneously pursuing a major capital and business overhaul that includes reverting its corporate name, an 80 percent capital reduction, and an equity investment funded by new convertible bonds.

The second quarter of 2026 showed joint improvement in revenue and operating profit, signaling a recovery in the core business, yet the net loss widened in the same quarter, and the fourth quarter of 2025 saw a net loss far exceeding the operating loss.

While the capital reduction and reduction of statutory reserves cleaned up the book-value capital structure, the accumulated deficit itself did not shrink, and governance-related questions remain open given increased borrowing and media reports on related-party lending.

The convertible bond balance represents a potential dilution factor exceeding a quarter of outstanding shares, and repeated investment-caution designations make share-price volatility another point to monitor.

Going forward, key items to confirm include whether core profitability is sustained in the mid-November third-quarter report, progress on the new Daeyeon ENC investment following the October convertible bond payment, and further clarification of related-party transactions.
