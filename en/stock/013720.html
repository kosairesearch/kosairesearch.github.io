02

Business structure

Cheongbo Industrial Co., Ltd., founded in 1979 and listed on KOSDAQ in 1993, is a specialized manufacturer of automotive engine and transmission parts, producing core engine components such as valve tappets, valve seat rings, and valve guides using precision casting methods.

Its product mix is concentrated, with tappets accounting for roughly KRW 6.9 billion, about 50% of product sales, and housings accounting for roughly KRW 6.7 billion, about 49%, together comprising nearly all product revenue.

The company supplies global automotive and engine OEM customers under platform-based supply relationships and has stated that it was newly adopted for components on Daimler Truck's next-generation engine platform.

The auto parts industry structure means that once a component is adopted for a platform, stable revenue tends to follow for an extended period after development.

Since CEO Oh Kyung-won took office in 2021, the company has changed its name multiple times, from Chungbo Industrial to CBI in June 2021, to The Cube& in June 2025, and back to Cheongbo in June 2026.

Around the same time, it dropped business objectives added in 2025 such as blockchain, AI, gaming, and drones, replacing them with defense, aerospace, drone development, and missile maintenance and repair (MRO) objectives; the AI initiative had involved the younger brother of Kakao's founder but failed to secure investment and produced no results.

In 2026, the company sold its entire 1 million shares in GB Innovation for KRW 10.5 billion and raised KRW 17 billion in long-term borrowings, combining asset sales and external borrowing to shore up liquidity.

In the process, however, payment on convertible bonds held by a private fund known as 'The Cube Fund No.1' was delayed for more than 14 months, disrupting the company's planned cash recovery.

04

Earnings analysis

Annual revenue fell from KRW 36 billion in 2022 to KRW 31.4 billion in 2023 (down 12.9% year-on-year) and KRW 24.4 billion in 2024 (down 22.0%), before recovering slightly to KRW 24.9 billion in 2025 (up 1.9%).

Operating profit improved from KRW 393 million in 2022 to KRW 787 million in 2023 (up 100.1%) and KRW 1.41 billion in 2024 (up 78.8%), but then declined to KRW 762 million in 2025 (down 45.9%), with the operating margin also falling from 5.8% in 2024 to 3.1% in 2025.

The controlling shareholder net loss narrowed each year from KRW 29.7 billion in 2022 to KRW 16.6 billion in 2023, KRW 14.8 billion in 2024, and KRW 12.2 billion in 2025, but the company remained in the red for four consecutive years.

On a quarterly basis, revenue bottomed at KRW 5.41 billion in Q4 2025 before rising for two straight quarters, to KRW 6.59 billion in Q1 2026 (up 21.8%) and KRW 7.99 billion in Q2 2026 (up 21.3% quarter-on-quarter and 26.4% year-on-year).

Operating profit remained negative with losses of KRW 267 million in Q4 2025 and KRW 504 million in Q1 2026, before swinging to a profit of KRW 1.01 billion in Q2 2026, up 250.9% year-on-year.

In contrast, the controlling shareholder net loss persisted for five consecutive quarters from a loss of KRW 624 million in Q2 2025 to a loss of KRW 2.25 billion in Q2 2026, with the loss ballooning to KRW 9.13 billion in Q4 2025, a divergence from the operating profit trend.

The cumulative controlling shareholder net loss over the last four quarters (Q3 2025 through Q2 2026) reached KRW 13.2 billion, suggesting that non-operating items had a significant effect on net results separate from the quarterly operating recovery.

Operating cash flow moved from negative KRW 1.34 billion in 2022 to KRW 5.65 billion in 2023 and KRW 4 billion in 2024, before slipping to KRW 2.72 billion in 2025, indicating that cash generation also moderated alongside profit volatility.

05

Industry analysis

The automotive engine and transmission parts industry in which Cheongbo operates is heavily influenced by global vehicle production cycles and exchange rates, with demand for internal combustion and hybrid engine components expected to coexist with the electrification transition for some time.

The company has stated that its export-oriented business structure benefits from a high-exchange-rate environment in terms of profitability.

Platform-based supply contracts tend to generate long-term revenue once a component is adopted, but conversely, delays in new platform adoption or reduced vehicle production volumes can increase revenue volatility.

As a small specialized parts maker with revenue concentrated in specific items such as tappets and housings, the company is relatively sensitive to demand shifts in those product categories.

Separately, in 2026 the company added defense, aerospace, unmanned aerial vehicle, and missile maintenance-related business objectives alongside its core auto parts business, though this remains an early-stage diversification effort with no confirmed orders or revenue contribution to date.

South Korea's defense and aerospace industry is a distinct, high-barrier sector driven by government budgets and export contracts, and the relevance or execution capability of this new segment relative to the existing auto parts business has not yet been demonstrated.

Outlook

The company has stated that mass production supply of two core components newly adopted for Daimler Truck's next-generation MDEG engine platform is set to expand from June 2026, and whether this contributed to the Q2 2026 revenue and operating profit rebound warrants further confirmation in upcoming quarterly results.

Management has said it has continued profitability-focused management through production process efficiency gains, cost reduction, and flexible plant operations.

On the balance sheet side, the company completed a 5-to-1 capital reduction to offset accumulated deficits in July 2026, and combined asset sales with long-term borrowing to improve short-term liquidity metrics, though accumulated deficits have continued to grow.

In October 2026, the company decided to issue a new KRW 1 billion private convertible bond for the stated purpose of funding the acquisition of securities in another company, and the specific target and purpose require confirmation through further disclosure.

The defense and aerospace new business has not yet disclosed any orders or revenue contribution, making its actual commercialization and pace a point to watch going forward.

Whether the unresolved convertible bond payment issue tied to The Cube Fund No.1 has been fully settled is also a matter that requires confirmation.

Bull factors

Quarterly Rebound and New Overseas OEM Orders

Revenue and operating profit both improved in Q2 2026, up 21.3% quarter-on-quarter with a swing to operating profit, marking two consecutive quarters of improvement from the Q4 2025 trough.

The company stated that two core components were newly adopted for Daimler Truck's next-generation engine platform, with mass production supply expanding from June 2026.

Since the auto parts industry structure tends to generate long-term stable revenue once a platform adoption is secured, actual revenue conversion from this new adoption could broaden the base of the core business.

Balance Sheet Restructuring to Address Accumulated Deficits

In July 2026, the company completed a 5-to-1 capital reduction to offset deficits, attempting to address concerns over capital impairment.

It raised KRW 10.5 billion from the sale of its GB Innovation stake and KRW 17 billion through long-term borrowing to secure liquidity, and the debt-to-equity ratio stood at 45.2% at year-end 2025, below the 74.4% level in 2022. Operating cash flow remained positive at KRW 2.72 billion in 2025.

Diversification Attempt to Expand the Business Portfolio

At its June 2026 shareholders meeting, the company added defense product development and manufacturing, and unmanned aerial vehicle and missile-related development and maintenance (MRO) as new business objectives. The company stated the purpose was to pursue new business entry and diversification. This can be interpreted as an attempt to move away from a revenue structure concentrated solely in auto parts.

Bear factors

Four Straight Years of Controlling Shareholder Net Losses and Non-Operating Volatility

The company posted a controlling shareholder net loss every year from 2022 through 2025, and the loss widened to KRW 9.13 billion in Q4 2025, diverging from the operating profit trend.

The cumulative controlling shareholder net loss over the last four quarters reached KRW 13.2 billion, making it difficult to conclude that the swing to operating profit alone signals an improvement in net results.

The fact that non-operating swings have had a larger effect on net income than operating performance complicates the interpretation of future results.

Credibility Concerns from Frequent Name Changes and Business Objective Additions

Since 2021, the company's name has changed four times, from Chungbo Industrial to CBI to The Cube& and back to Cheongbo, and business objectives added in 2025 such as blockchain, AI, gaming, and drones were replaced with defense and aerospace objectives in less than a year.

A figure involved in the earlier AI initiative resigned after only a month, raising questions about the continuity of new business pursuits. This history is a point worth considering when assessing the feasibility of newly added business objectives.

Revenue Base Contraction and Concentration Risk

Annual revenue declined from KRW 36 billion in 2022 to KRW 24.4 billion in 2024, and the 2025 rebound to KRW 24.9 billion amounted to only a 1.9% increase.

Product revenue is concentrated in just two items, tappets and housings, meaning demand shifts at a specific customer or vehicle platform can have an outsized effect on overall results. The more-than-14-month delay in convertible bond payment from a private fund illustrates uncertainty in the company's funding plans.

Risk factors

Balance Sheet Risk

Accumulated deficits have continued to grow even after year-end 2025, and the burden of interest expense could increase as long-term borrowings expand. While the capital reduction and asset sales improved short-term liquidity metrics, these gains stemmed from financing and investing activities rather than operations. If additional borrowing or asset sales recur, market assessment of financial soundness could shift.

New Business Execution Risk

No concrete orders or revenue contribution have been confirmed for the newly added defense, aerospace, unmanned aerial vehicle, and missile maintenance business objectives.

Given the precedent of previously added AI and blockchain objectives being withdrawn within a year, whether this new set of objectives will translate into actual business requires further confirmation.

The sector has high entry barriers and regulatory requirements, and synergy or execution capability relative to the existing auto parts business has not been demonstrated.

Share Supply and Trading Risk

The stock was designated an investment caution issue on July 31, 2026 due to a sharp price and volume swing, indicating significant short-term volatility. The large reduction in outstanding shares from the deficit-offsetting capital reduction is also a factor that could affect share supply and demand.

Given the ongoing structure of convertible bond issuance and repayment, the possibility of additional share supply in the future should also be kept in mind.

Overall view

Cheongbo is undergoing three major changes simultaneously in 2026 on top of its traditional auto engine and transmission parts business: a capital reduction to offset deficits, a corporate name change, and the addition of defense and aerospace business objectives.

On the earnings side, revenue and operating profit improved for two consecutive quarters from the Q4 2025 trough through Q2 2026, and a new order from Daimler Truck has been confirmed, but the controlling shareholder net result has remained in the red for five consecutive quarters, tracing a different path from the operating profit trend.

The balance sheet shows improved short-term liquidity metrics following the capital reduction, asset sales, and long-term borrowing, but accumulated deficits have continued to grow, alongside funding uncertainties such as the long-delayed convertible bond payment from a private fund.

The addition of defense and aerospace business objectives remains at an early stage with no confirmed orders or revenue contribution, and the precedent of previous AI and blockchain initiatives being withdrawn within a year is worth keeping in mind.

The history of being designated an investment caution issue, along with frequent name and business objective changes, are governance and volatility factors to weigh alongside the financials.

Upcoming quarterly results and the concrete progress of the new business initiatives are likely to be the key variables in future assessment.
