02

Business structure

HFR is a KOSDAQ-listed telecom equipment maker whose sales fall into two segments, wired and wireless. The wired segment covers subscriber network equipment and similar products, and it accounted for 92% of sales in Q2 2026 according to the data aggregator Butler.

The wireless segment, which covers optical transmission and in-building equipment, made up 8% over the same period. Because most sales depend on wired equipment, the wireless share remains small.

As an equipment business tied to telecom infrastructure spending, quarterly sales can swing sharply with the timing of orders and deliveries. Indeed, quarterly sales rose from KRW 25.4 billion in Q3 2025 to KRW 48.3 billion in Q4 2025 and then fell to KRW 16.3 billion in Q1 2026, a wide range of movement.

The debt-to-equity ratio at year-end 2025 was 77.0%, and owner equity of KRW 142.4 billion accounted for most of total equity of KRW 158.6 billion. Sales of KRW 366.3 billion in 2022 were the largest year in the 2022 to 2025 span, and the following three years saw sales shrink.

Listed peers in the same telecom sector include Solid, Hyundai Futurenet and Coweaver, which serve as reference points for industry comparison. Segment profitability and customer sales concentration are items to check in the next annual report.

04

Earnings analysis

Annual sales fell from KRW 366.3 billion in 2022 to KRW 164.2 billion in 2023, down 55.2% year on year, then to KRW 157.1 billion in 2024 (down 4.3%) and KRW 142 billion in 2025 (down 9.6%), declining for three consecutive years from 2023 to 2025.

Operating profit went from KRW 90.2 billion in 2022 to an operating loss of KRW 8.42 billion in 2023, a swing into the red; the loss widened to KRW 20.2 billion in 2024 and then narrowed to KRW 1.58 billion in 2025. The operating margin moved from 24.6% in 2022 to -5.1% in 2023, -12.9% in 2024 and -1.1% in 2025.

Owner net income dropped from KRW 68.3 billion in 2022 to KRW 1.84 billion in 2023, down 97.3% year on year, and turned into an owner net loss of KRW 18.6 billion in 2024. The 2025 owner net loss was KRW 309 million, smaller than the year before.

Net income including non-controlling interests was a net loss of KRW 49.27 million in 2025, a figure that differs from the owner net loss, and the gap appears to come from non-controlling interests.

Operating cash flow went from KRW 109.5 billion in 2022 to negative figures of -KRW 34.6 billion in 2023 and -KRW 5.91 billion in 2024 before turning positive at KRW 9.55 billion in 2025.

On a quarterly basis, Q3 2025 sales of KRW 25.4 billion came with an operating loss of KRW 3.12 billion and an owner net loss of KRW 4.18 billion. Q4 2025 sales rose to KRW 48.3 billion, up 90.2% from the prior quarter, and operating profit reached KRW 507 million, a swing to profit.

Q1 2026 sales fell 66.4% quarter on quarter to KRW 16.3 billion, with an operating loss of KRW 5.72 billion and an owner net loss of KRW 8.09 billion. Q2 2026 sales recovered to KRW 24 billion, up 47.3% from the prior quarter, but the operating loss widened to KRW 7.48 billion.

Q3 2026 sales reached KRW 35.8 billion, up 40.7% year on year, the operating loss narrowed to KRW 2.11 billion, and owner net income of KRW 1.53 billion marked a swing to profit from a year earlier.

An operating loss alongside positive owner net income in Q3 suggests that non-operating items and non-controlling interests played a large role in that quarter.

Industry analysis

Sales in the telecom equipment industry often move with telecom operators' network investment, so where the spending cycle stands largely shapes earnings direction.

HFR's sales peaked at KRW 366.3 billion in 2022, fell for three consecutive years from 2023 to 2025, and rose in Q2 and Q3 2026 after the Q1 2026 low, a pattern that reads as an early stage of recovery after a decline.

Mirae Asset Securities named the company the biggest beneficiary of a generation transition in a coverage initiation report dated May 22, 2026. The same report presented it as a top pick within the telecom equipment sector and described the recovery from losses as having begun.

That assessment reflects the May 2026 view and may differ from later quarterly results. Listed peers in the same telecom sector include Solid, Hyundai Futurenet, Coweaver and Wiable, and comparisons within the sector need to account for business mix and sales scale.

The direction of industry demand can first be checked through telecom operators' investment plans and equipment order flows. What kind of equipment replacement demand the generation transition creates should be read together with the company's product mix, and segment details can be gauged in the next annual report.

Wired equipment accounted for 92% of sales in Q2 2026 according to Butler, so demand shifts in one segment feed directly into total results. If the wireless segment grows slowly, the pace at which the business mix changes may also be slow.

Quarterly earnings volatility

Because quarter-to-quarter swings are large, a single quarter is hard to use for judging the trend. Since profit direction changes from quarter to quarter, continuity needs to be confirmed.

Financial structure and cash flow
