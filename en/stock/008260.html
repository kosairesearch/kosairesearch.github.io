02

Business structure

NI Steel is a mid-sized steel company listed on the KOSPI in 1975, centered on composite steel beams and columns for construction, and it also runs steel rental and construction businesses.

Composite beams and columns are structural systems that combine the strengths of steel and concrete, and the company says they can cut steel use by more than 20%.

Based on a securities report covering the first half of 2023, segment sales mix was 43.1% structural steel products, 16.7% traded structural steel products, 24.9% steel rental and 15.3% construction.

Traded products involve buying H-beams and selling them to construction companies, while the construction business covers works such as D-Deck and steel curtain walls.

Production is based at the Dangjin plant, where Dangjin No. 2 was completed in October 2015 and the expansion of Dangjin No. 1, a plant specialized in shaped section steel, was finished in July 2016. Dangjin Nos. 1 and 3 were separated and completed in July 2023.

The rental business started in January 2019 with system scaffolding rental. Its main customers are construction companies in the civil engineering and building markets, so earnings are tied to the flow of construction orders.

On the input side, cyclical swings in downstream steel demand pass through to costs, which is the central variable for profitability. As of June 2026, related parties of the largest shareholder held 59.00%, of which Moonbae Steel held 39.72%.

04

Earnings analysis

On an annual basis, sales rose from KRW 355.4 billion in 2022 to KRW 382.9 billion in 2023, a 7.7% increase, and operating profit rose from KRW 67.7 billion to KRW 70.7 billion, up 4.3%. In 2024, sales fell 28.1% to KRW 275.3 billion and operating profit fell 47.4% to KRW 37.1 billion.

In 2025, sales declined 9.9% to KRW 247.9 billion and operating profit declined 18.9% to KRW 30.1 billion, the second straight year of decline for both measures. Profit attributable to controlling shareholders went from KRW 50.2 billion in 2023 to KRW 25.7 billion in 2024 and KRW 19 billion in 2025.

The operating margin fell from 19.1% in 2022 to 18.5% in 2023, 13.5% in 2024 and 12.1% in 2025. The debt ratio rose from 86.1% in 2024 to 90.9% in 2025.

Operating cash flow was positive in each year from 2022 to 2025, at KRW 42.4 billion, KRW 105.8 billion, KRW 78.4 billion and KRW 39.9 billion, but the 2025 figure was less than half the 2023 level.

On a quarterly basis, sales were KRW 65.2 billion and operating profit was KRW 8.2 billion in Q3 2025, then KRW 65.8 billion and KRW 7.71 billion in Q4 2025, a 5.9% quarter-on-quarter decline in operating profit.

Q1 2026 marked the low point, with sales of KRW 56.6 billion (down 14.0% from the prior quarter) and operating profit of KRW 6.5 billion (down 15.7%).

Market data aggregators attributed the weakness in that period to higher costs stemming from cyclical swings in downstream civil engineering and construction and upstream steel. Q2 2026 recovered, with sales of KRW 76.2 billion (up 34.7% quarter on quarter) and operating profit of KRW 7.91 billion (up 21.7%).

Q3 2026 sales were KRW 73.5 billion, down 3.6% from the prior quarter but up 12.7% year on year, while operating profit was KRW 9.3 billion, up 17.6% quarter on quarter and 13.5% year on year.

Securities market commentary read the quarter as a period in which sales growth stalled but profitability was defended through price adjustments and cost management.

Profit attributable to controlling shareholders rose for two consecutive quarters after the Q1 2026 low of KRW 3.85 billion, reaching KRW 4.73 billion in Q2 and KRW 5.65 billion in Q3. Over the last four quarters, from Q4 2025 to Q3 2026, that profit totaled KRW 18.9 billion.

Industry analysis

NI Steel's demand is tied to the civil engineering and construction markets, so construction orders and project volumes are the starting point for the industry outlook.

Securities market commentary on Q3 2026 described an environment of continuing uncertainty in downstream steel, in which sales growth stalled but profitability was held through price adjustments and cost management. Cost swings from upstream steel inputs were cited as a pressure on profitability in Q1 2026.

A market data provider pointed to the expanding potential market for composite beam and column conversion methods as a positive factor. In the structural materials market, steel purchase prices and construction support capability are the variables that shape competition.

Hyundai Steel Company and Dongkuk Steel Mill, both in the same steel and metals sector, have larger shares of general-purpose steel output, while NI Steel focuses on building structural products such as composite beams, columns and steel rental.

As a result, margin swings from raw material prices and construction activity may differ by product mix. The decline in the operating margin from 19.1% in 2022 to 12.1% in 2025 can be read as the combined effect of industry pressure and cost movements.

It is difficult to pin down where the industry stands in its cycle, so the pace of construction demand recovery and the cost trend are the next points to watch.

Cost pressure and downstream dependence

Risk factors

Concentrated production site

Production is concentrated at the Dangjin plant, and Dangjin Nos. 1 and 3 were separated and completed in July 2023. Because shaped section steel and composite beam and column production are tied to a single site, a halt in equipment could immediately disrupt shipments and construction delivery schedules.

Maintaining utilization at Dangjin No. 2, completed in October 2015, and the expanded Dangjin No. 1, finished in July 2016, is a precondition for the quarterly profit recovery. If equipment maintenance or shutdowns run long, the operating profit increase that has continued through Q3 could reverse.

Ownership structure and free float

As of June 2026, related parties of the largest shareholder held 59.00% of the company. Of this, Moonbae Steel accounts for 39.72%. With a high share of closely held stock, the number of shares available for trading can be limited, which can reduce liquidity. In a disclosure dated September 1, 2026, the company said it had disposed of 1,001,150 treasury shares.

Whether the new method market expands

The expanding market for composite beam and column conversion methods and the rise in steel rental sales are outlooks from a market data provider. If these do not translate into orders and revenue, the growth case weakens. Adoption depends on construction companies' design decisions and project conditions. With quarterly sales stalled, the speed at which this outlook is confirmed matters.
