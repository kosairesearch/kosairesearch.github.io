Orders grow, but Q3 2026 profitability weakens

02

Business structure

HL Mando designs and manufactures braking, steering and suspension systems along with ADAS (advanced driver assistance systems), making it a Tier 1 automotive chassis supplier.

In the 2024 annual report, regional sales before intercompany eliminations were KRW 3.84 trillion in Korea, KRW 2.30 trillion in China, KRW 1.52 trillion in the United States, KRW 900.3 billion in India and KRW 1.66 trillion in other regions, with Korea the largest.

Electrified products made up 64% of sales in 2024, overtaking mechanical parts, and the share reached 66% in Q2 2026. Management credits high-value products such as the electronic brake (IDB2) and motor-driven power steering (R-EPS) with protecting profitability.

Hyundai Motor Group accounts for close to half of sales, followed by Chinese local automakers, a North American EV maker and GM. Of cumulative new orders through September 2026, Hyundai Motor and Kia accounted for 40%, Chinese automakers 34%, Indian automakers 12% and North American automakers 10%.

Per the company's Q3 2026 regional figures, Korea sales were KRW 716.5 billion (down 8.6% year on year), China KRW 494.7 billion (down 1.7%), the Americas KRW 634.4 billion (up 0.4%), Europe KRW 192.5 billion (up 3.1%) and India KRW 282.5 billion (up 30.8%).

Some analysts view its customer base as more diversified than other domestic Tier 1 suppliers. The subsidiary HL Clemove leads development of next-generation products such as high-performance computers (HPC).

04

Earnings analysis

Per the DART periodic filings, annual revenue rose from KRW 7.52 trillion in 2022 to KRW 9.45 trillion in 2025 and grew for three consecutive years from 2023 to 2025. Growth was 11.7% in 2023, 5.4% in 2024 and 6.9% in 2025.

Operating profit peaked at KRW 358.8 billion in 2024, up 28.5%, before easing 0.5% to KRW 357.1 billion in 2025. Operating margin was 3.3% in 2022, 3.3% in 2023, 4.1% in 2024 and 3.8% in 2025, declining after 2024.

Owner net income peaked at KRW 135.6 billion in 2023 and then fell two years in a row, to KRW 129.9 billion in 2024 (down 4.2%) and KRW 100 billion in 2025 (down 23.0%). Net income including non-controlling interests fell in 2025 as well, from KRW 158.2 billion to KRW 122.8 billion.

The debt ratio dropped from 163.8% in 2024 to 148.2% in 2025, the lowest of the four years. Operating cash flow moved from -KRW 13.3 billion in 2022 to KRW 429.4 billion in 2023 and KRW 720.9 billion in 2025.

Quarterly operating profit was KRW 94.2 billion in Q3 2025 and KRW 79.7 billion in Q4 2025, recovered to KRW 93.6 billion in Q1 2026 and KRW 107 billion in Q2 2026, then fell to KRW 51.5 billion in Q3 2026. Q3 2026 revenue of KRW 2.32 trillion was roughly flat, but owner results swung to a KRW 47.1 billion net loss.

The company cited higher semiconductor and raw material costs and rising R&D, and news reports also noted a foreign-exchange swing to a KRW 61.4 billion loss.

05

Industry analysis

Demand in this industry tracks automaker production and sales. Hyundai Motor and Kia's global sales reached 7,302,451 units in 2024, up 6.7% from the prior year, according to a report carried in early 2025.

In 2026, production disruptions in Korea reduced Korean sales, and weaker domestic demand in China hit sales to Chinese local automakers. Chinese local makers are expanding exports to find growth, but analysts note HL Mando does not supply the main exporters, which limits its benefit.

Semiconductors are the biggest cost variable. Kiwoom Securities noted that memory price increases tied to ADAS production have not been passed through to customers in time, and that it sees little basis for a near-term change in bargaining power.

In India, Hyundai Motor Group and local automakers are expanding output, demand is rebounding, and EV policy support is building, according to an iM Securities report from November 2025. On competition, its customer base is more diversified than that of other domestic Tier 1 suppliers.

Electrified products were a high share of Q3 2026 new orders, so the shift toward electrification continues on the order side. The sector is in a phase where electrification is expanding while input costs are rising.

06

Outlook

The company said it will focus on meeting its annual order target, since large projects are concentrated in Q4 2026. Of the KRW 13 trillion annual target, KRW 7.6 trillion had been booked through September, and the remainder depends on large Q4 awards.

In the first half of 2026, it secured brake calipers, motor-driven steering and ADAS supply for Hyundai Motor and Kia's new North American models. In China, it expanded orders for braking products such as the IDB2, electronically controlled suspension and high-performance computers.

On new businesses, it unveiled an actuator at CES 2026 and is widening its robotics component activities. In the United States, it is pursuing a local production setup in line with demands to localize robot supply chains, and Heungkuk Securities viewed this positively for medium- to long-term growth.

Heungkuk Securities, however, expects cost pressure to persist through Q4. Price-linked items should see cost recovery after a lag in customer settlements, while PCB and plastic items still require further negotiation. R&D spending is also expected to stay high in the short term as new businesses expand.

The next checkpoints are whether the annual order target is met by the end of December 2026 and how cost recovery progresses in Q4 2026.

08

Bull factors

Electrified orders underpin the top line

Electrified products made up 76% of cumulative new orders through September 2026. The company has booked 59% of its KRW 13 trillion annual target, and the backlog stands at KRW 58 trillion. Supply for Hyundai Motor's new North American programs covering brake calipers, electric steering and ADAS adds to the order base.

Growth in India and Europe

India sales reached KRW 282.5 billion in Q3 2026, up 30.8% and a quarterly high. India sales also rose 17.7% to KRW 265.7 billion in Q2 2026. European sales increased 3.1% to KRW 192.5 billion. Higher supply to Hyundai Motor, Kia and local Indian automakers was cited as the driver.

Expansion into robotics and new businesses

HL Mando unveiled an actuator at CES 2026 and is widening its robotics component business. It is also pursuing US production capacity to meet demand for localized robot supply chains. Heungkuk Securities viewed entry into the US robotics market positively for medium- to long-term growth. The contribution of these new businesses to earnings has not yet appeared in clear figures.

09

Bear factors

Sharp Q3 profit decline

Q3 2026 operating profit fell 45.3% to KRW 51.5 billion, and the operating margin dropped 1.9 percentage points from 4.1% in Q3 2025 to 2.2%. Brokers described the result as falling well short of market operating profit forecasts. Owner net income swung to a KRW 47.1 billion net loss. The foreign-exchange swing to a KRW 61.4 billion loss also weighed on net income.

Delayed memory cost pass-through

Analysts say the structure exposed by the Q3 results is that memory price increases are not being passed to customers in time. Heungkuk Securities estimated the Q3 cost burden at about KRW 42 billion and noted R&D spending rose KRW 23.5 billion year on year. It views the burden as likely to persist through Q4 because PCB and plastic items still need negotiation.

Slower sales in Korea and China

Korea sales fell 8.6% to KRW 716.5 billion in Q3 2026, reflecting lower domestic production and some production disruptions at Hyundai Motor and Kia. China sales dropped 1.7% to KRW 494.7 billion, and sales to Chinese local OEMs fell 16% according to a Heungkuk Securities report. Weak domestic demand in China has reduced near-term growth visibility there.

10

Risk factors

Cost and settlement risk

Even when memory prices rise, profitability is hit if customer settlements lag. The operating margin fell to 2.2% in Q3 2026. If the recovery lag stretches, margin pressure could continue after Q4 2026. R&D spending is also expected to stay elevated in the short term as new and next-generation products expand.

Customer concentration risk

Hyundai Motor and Kia accounted for 40% of cumulative new orders through September 2026. A 2024 report on 2023 results cited Hyundai Motor Group affiliates as about 48% of sales. Domestic production disruptions or North American sales swings pass through quickly to results. Customer diversification is under way, but dependence on Hyundai Motor Group remains high.

Currency and FX-gain/loss risk

Reports said the foreign-exchange result swung to a KRW 61.4 billion loss in Q3 2026. It had been a KRW 16.4 billion gain in Q3 2025. The line sits apart from operating profit but can move net income. With most sales generated outside Korea, exposure to exchange-rate moves is large.

What to watch next

  1. End of December 2026

    This is the point to check how far cumulative orders reach against the KRW 13 trillion annual target. The company said large projects are concentrated in Q4. Whether the target is met is the benchmark for the electrified order trend.

  2. Early 2027

    This is when Q4 2026 provisional results are expected. The checkpoints are whether the operating margin recovers from 2.2% in Q3 and whether memory cost settlements with customers have progressed. Heungkuk Securities expects cost pressure to persist through Q4.

  3. By the end of March 2027

    This is the deadline for the 2026 annual report (DART periodic filing). Annual revenue, operating profit, owner net income and the year-end 2026 debt ratio will be disclosed as confirmed figures. The gap between Q4 2026 provisional and confirmed numbers is also a point to check.

12

Overall view

HL Mando is an electrified-focused automotive parts maker spanning braking, steering, suspension and ADAS, and sales rose for three consecutive years from 2023 to 2025, reaching KRW 9.45 trillion in 2025. Operating margin rose from 3.3% in 2022 to 4.1% in 2024 before easing to 3.8% in 2025, and fell to 2.2% in Q3 2026.

Strengths include cumulative new orders of KRW 7.6 trillion through September 2026 (59% of the annual target), a rising electrified share and growth in India and other emerging markets.

Weaknesses include delayed pass-through of memory and other chip costs to customers, weak production and demand in Korea and China, and a worse foreign-exchange result. Owner net income fell two years in a row after its 2023 peak and swung to a net loss in Q3 2026.

Brokers cut target prices after the Q3 results, and some lowered their ratings. Which side carries more weight depends on whether order growth or cost pressure is viewed as dominant.

The next checkpoints are meeting the order target by the end of December 2026, cost recovery progress in Q4 2026, and the 2026 annual report due by the end of March 2027.
