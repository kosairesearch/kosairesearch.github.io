02

Business structure

Daewonkangup makes automotive springs and seats and supplies them to vehicle makers. Product mix figures differ by source: one data set shows springs at 85.10% and seats at 14.00%, while a value-chain classification shows Q1 2026 sales at 54.4% for the chassis segment and 43.6% for the interior and seat segment.

Springs include suspension coil springs that carry vehicle load and precision springs used inside engines and transmissions, while the seat business makes complete seats and seat mechanism parts. Hyundai Motor and KIA are the largest customers, accounting for 45.6% of Q2 2026 revenue.

In 2022 their share of consolidated revenue was 54.4%, and as sales to customers outside the Hyundai group grew, the group's share fell below half. Non-captive sales rose 28.2% in the first half of 2026.

Overseas, the company supplies coil springs and stabilizer bars to global automakers including GM, Chrysler, Volkswagen, BMW and Tesla. Its nine production sites span South Korea (Cheonan, Changwon, Hamyang) and six countries including the United States, India, Poland, Russia and Mexico.

Exports made up 67.9% of consolidated revenue in 2025. A 2024 Kyobo Securities report said Hyundai and KIA account for over 90% of spring product share, with 70% of supply volume routed through Hyundai Mobis. Drive-motor cores are described as moving into full-scale mass production at a subsidiary.

04

Earnings analysis

Annual disclosed revenue went from KRW 1.02 trillion in 2022 to KRW 1.11 trillion in 2023 (up 8.0%), KRW 1.37 trillion in 2024 (up 23.6%) and KRW 1.6 trillion in 2025 (up 16.9%), rising for three straight years from 2023, with 2025 the largest year of 2022 to 2025.

Operating profit climbed from KRW 22.1 billion in 2022 to KRW 59.2 billion in 2023 (up 168.3%), fell to KRW 22.6 billion in 2024 (down 61.9%), and recovered to KRW 47 billion in 2025 (up 108.3%), with 2023 the largest year.

Operating margin stood at 2.2% in 2022, 5.3% in 2023, 1.6% in 2024 and 2.9% in 2025, so margins stayed in low single digits even as revenue grew.

Net income attributable to controlling shareholders was KRW 18.9 billion in 2022, KRW 30.6 billion in 2023, KRW 23.6 billion in 2024 and KRW 36.4 billion in 2025, with 2025 the largest year.

Net income including non-controlling interests was KRW 46 billion in 2025 and should be read separately from the controlling-shareholder figure.

The debt ratio rose from 69.0% in 2022 and 66.0% in 2023 to 91.2% in 2024 and 98.5% in 2025, when total liabilities were KRW 719.1 billion against total equity of KRW 730.2 billion. Operating cash flow grew from KRW 56.2 billion in 2022 to KRW 111.2 billion in 2025.

On a quarterly basis, an operating loss of KRW 3.39 billion in Q3 2025 turned into operating profit of KRW 15.4 billion in Q4 2025, then rose to KRW 23.3 billion in Q1 2026 and KRW 38.5 billion in Q2 2026 (up 65.4% quarter on quarter), before falling to KRW 23.6 billion in Q3 2026 (down 38.7%).

In Q3 2026 net income attributable to controlling shareholders fell more steeply (down 69.5% quarter on quarter) than operating profit. Over the last four quarters, from Q4 2025 to Q3 2026, that net income totaled KRW 84.2 billion.

A warranty expense of KRW 19.4 billion tied to a North American engine valve spring recall was booked in 2025, according to a Korean IR association value-up report.

05

Industry analysis

Demand depends heavily on production and sales at Hyundai Motor and KIA, which accounted for 45.6% of Q2 2026 revenue, so the automakers' utilization drives the company's volumes.

A Korean IR association cited the growth of Hyundai group hybrid sales, the expansion of non-captive seat supply and operating leverage from lower fixed costs as 2026 margin drivers.

A brokerage analysis quoted in a 2023 Daily Invest report pointed to rising automaker utilization from the second half of 2022 as the driver of earnings improvement at that time.

The utilization-led upcycle of that period has passed, and customer diversification and product mix are now cited as the main sources of improvement. In springs, Hyundai and KIA hold over 90% share, so existing customer relationships act as a barrier to entry while dependence on a few automakers remains high.

Exports at 67.9% of revenue also reflect a broad supply base to overseas automakers such as GM, Stellantis, Volkswagen and BMW.

Among 137 auto parts companies tracked by alphasquare, the company ranked 25th in 2025 revenue and 74th in operating margin, placing it in the middle of the group, where the industry average operating margin was negative.

06

Outlook

A Korean IR association value-up report projects 2026 revenue of about KRW 1.8 trillion, up 10.7% from KRW 1.6 trillion in 2025, and operating profit of KRW 110.6 billion, up 135.3%.

The same report expects gross margin to rise from 10.1% in 2025 to 12.6% in 2026, SG&A ratio to fall from 7.2% to 6.4%, and the company-wide operating margin to reach 6.2%.

Cited drivers include seat supply to non-captive customers, operating leverage, ASP gains, lower fixed labor costs, drive-motor core mass production at a subsidiary, and higher hybrid sales at the Hyundai group. The same report also flagged FX risk.

These projections are external estimates, not disclosed results, and the report did not present a target price. Disclosed results already cover Q1 to Q3 2026, so the remainder of the annual forecast depends on Q4 2026.

The next periodic filing is the 2026 annual business report, which will fix the full-year operating margin and debt ratio. The Q1 2027 results will then show how much non-captive sales and FX effects are reflected.

08

Bull factors

Expanding non-captive customer base

A Korean financial news report said non-captive sales rose 28.2% in the first half of 2026. The Hyundai group's share of total sales fell from above half to below half. A sales base that was concentrated in one group is becoming more diversified, which can reduce the impact of production swings at any single automaker. Expanded non-captive seat supply was cited as a core driver of 2026 margin improvement.

Drive-motor core business

Mass production of drive-motor cores at a subsidiary was described as moving into full swing. A Korean IR association cited the business as one growth driver. The product line was described as higher value-added, which could widen margin improvement. A Korean financial news report noted, however, that its contribution to earnings remains limited for now.

Operating profit recovery

Operating profit in 2025 rose 108.3% to KRW 47 billion. Quarterly operating profit climbed from KRW 15.4 billion in Q4 2025 to KRW 23.3 billion in Q1 2026 and KRW 38.5 billion in Q2 2026.

A Korean IR association projects 2026 operating profit of KRW 110.6 billion, up 135.3%, citing operating leverage and a lower SG&A ratio. This is an external estimate rather than a disclosed result.

09

Bear factors

FX and export exposure

Exports at 67.9% of 2025 consolidated revenue make profitability sensitive to exchange-rate moves. A Korean financial news report estimated that a 10% drop in the exchange rate would cut pre-tax profit by KRW 6.5 billion.

Foreign-currency receivables and deposits build up on the balance sheet, so earnings swing with the direction of the rate. Whether the 2026 improvement materializes also depends on FX, which is a bearish point.

Q3 2026 profit decline

Operating profit in Q3 2026 was KRW 23.6 billion, down 38.7% from the prior quarter. Net income attributable to controlling shareholders was KRW 7.54 billion, down 69.5% quarter on quarter. That figure has declined for two consecutive quarters, in Q2 and Q3 2026.

With the 2025 operating margin at 2.9%, a small share of revenue converts into profit, which is a bearish factor.

Warranty costs and balance sheet strain

A Korean IR association value-up report flagged a KRW 19.4 billion warranty expense in 2025 tied to a North American engine valve spring recall. Over the same period the debt ratio rose from 69.0% in 2022 to 98.5% in 2025. With liabilities growing alongside assets, financial room narrows if earnings weaken. A recurrence of warranty costs could slow the pace of margin improvement.

10

Risk factors

FX and export dependence

Exports account for 67.9% of 2025 consolidated revenue. Foreign-currency assets and receivables make pre-tax profit move with the exchange rate. The estimated KRW 6.5 billion pre-tax hit from a 10% rate drop makes the direction of FX important. The company cannot control this variable.

Concentration on automaker customers

Hyundai Motor and KIA accounted for 45.6% of Q2 2026 revenue. Lower production or utilization at these two automakers would hit both sales and plant loading. Higher hybrid sales is a supportive factor, but order volumes can shift if automaker production plans change. How far non-captive growth reduces this dependence is the key thing to watch.

Quality warranty and financial leverage

A KRW 19.4 billion warranty expense tied to a North American recall was booked in 2025. The debt ratio stood at 98.5% in 2025, up from 69.0% in 2022. With total liabilities of KRW 719.1 billion, borrowing burden could grow if earnings fall. Whether new warranty costs emerge should be checked in quarterly results.

Overall view

Daewonkangup is a parts supplier of automotive springs and seats to automakers including Hyundai Motor and KIA. Revenue has risen for three straight years from 2023 to KRW 1.6 trillion in 2025, and operating profit grew 108.3% to KRW 47 billion.

However, the operating margin was only 2.9% in 2025, so the margin structure remains thin. Net income attributable to controlling shareholders peaked in Q1 2026 and then declined for two consecutive quarters, and Q3 operating profit also fell from the prior quarter.

Bullish factors include non-captive customer growth (non-captive sales up 28.2% in the first half of 2026), drive-motor core mass production, and a projected 2026 operating profit of KRW 110.6 billion from a Korean IR association.

Bearish factors include FX sensitivity with exports at 67.9% of revenue, dependence on Hyundai Motor and KIA (45.6% of Q2 2026 revenue), a KRW 19.4 billion recall warranty expense in 2025, and a debt ratio that rose to 98.5%. The weight of these factors does not yet point in one direction.

On valuation, market capitalization sits below controlling-shareholder equity, and the dividend yield is below the industry average. This report does not present an investment opinion or target price.

The next checkpoints are the Q4 and full-year operating margin in the 2026 annual report, and how much non-captive sales and FX effects show up in Q1 2027 results.
