02

Business structure

KB Star Reit is KB Financial Group's first listed REIT, managed by KB Asset Management, and was listed on the KOSPI in October 2022.

The core holding is North Galaxy Towers in Brussels, Belgium, where the Belgian building management authority occupies 99% of the leasable area on behalf of end-users including the Belgian finance ministry, and the asset represents roughly 77% of total assets.

The lease runs for an ultra-long 27-year term from December 2004 to November 2031, with rent adjusted annually in line with Belgium's Health Index.

The REIT also holds the Samsung Electronics Europe HQ building in Chertsey, UK, as well as domestic assets including Citibank Center in Gwanghwamun, Signature Tower in Euljiro, and preferred units linked to Yeouido Finance Tower acquired in December 2025.

The company pursues a "multi-asset, ONE FIRM" strategy that combines varied property types within a single vehicle rather than segregating them into sector-specific REITs, and it runs a semiannual dividend cycle with fiscal settlements in January and July, unlike most peers.

KB Financial Group serves as an anchor investor, and affiliates such as KB Securities, KB Kookmin Bank, and KB Capital have participated in capital raises to provide financial support.

Within the same sector, the company competes alongside domestic-asset-focused REITs such as SK Reit, Lotte Reit, and Shinhan Alpha REIT, as well as JR Global Reit, another overseas-office-heavy vehicle.

04

Earnings analysis

Revenue fell 12.0% from KRW 32.5 billion in 2022 to KRW 28.6 billion in 2023, then rose for two consecutive years to KRW 29.8 billion in 2024 (up 4.2%) and KRW 30.4 billion in 2025 (up 2.1%).

Operating profit, however, swung from a KRW 11.6 billion profit in 2022 to an operating loss of KRW 21.4 billion in 2023, and the loss widened for three straight years to KRW 26.8 billion in 2024 and KRW 72.4 billion in 2025.

The operating margin accordingly deteriorated sharply from 35.8% in 2022 to -75.0% in 2023, -90.1% in 2024 and -238.4% in 2025, which the company has attributed mainly to impairment charges on its overseas investment properties.

Net loss attributable to controlling shareholders began at KRW 8.69 billion in 2022, widened to KRW 55.4 billion in 2023, narrowed to KRW 31.8 billion in 2024, then widened again to KRW 99.4 billion in 2025, marking four consecutive years of losses from 2022 through 2025.

Net income including non-controlling interests matched the controlling-shareholder figures in every year, indicating minimal non-controlling interest impact.

Operating cash flow, by contrast, moved opposite to the net-loss trend, turning from -KRW 6.32 billion in 2022 to KRW 5.3 billion in 2023, KRW 13.7 billion in 2024 and KRW 13.8 billion in 2025, consistent with impairment being a non-cash item.

Over the trailing four quarters (Q3 2025 through Q2 2026), cumulative net loss attributable to controlling shareholders totaled KRW 99.9 billion.

KB Star Reit disclosed on March 18, 2026 that consolidated operating income for the half-year from August 2025 to January 2026 turned positive, explaining that the improvement reflected a reduced impact from investment property impairment booked in the prior fiscal year, though this half-year figure is preliminary and the consolidated net loss for the same period was reported at roughly KRW 10.2 billion.

05

Industry analysis

Prolonged high interest rates and an economic slowdown have made prime office transactions in Europe scarce, leading appraisers to apply conservative assumptions due to a lack of comparable deals.

This environment has spread valuation pressure across Korea-listed REITs holding overseas offices in Belgium and the UK, and in 2026 JR Global Reit, which owns Finance Tower in Brussels, triggered a cash trap after breaching its loan-to-value covenant and entered court receivership, heightening market caution toward similarly structured vehicles.

KB Star Reit is compared structurally to JR Global Reit because its core asset, North Galaxy Towers in Brussels, accounting for about 77% of total assets, sits in the same district as JR Global Reit's Finance Tower, and both face the fallout from the weak local office market and declining asset values.

According to the July 2025 business report, North Galaxy Towers' LTV stood at 65%, with headroom against the 68.5% cash-trap trigger, and the appraisal value rebounded in January 2026, which analysts cite as a point of difference.

Still, applying the company's own more conservative valuation standard puts the LTV at 72%, already above the cash-trap threshold. The domestic office market has looked comparatively stable, with Yeouido Finance Tower, one of the REIT's holdings, reported to carry a vacancy rate below 1%.

Peers with heavier domestic weighting such as SK Reit, Lotte Reit, Shinhan Alpha REIT and ESR Kendall Square REIT are relatively insulated from overseas rate and currency swings, a positioning contrast frequently raised against KB Star Reit's overseas-heavy structure.

06

Outlook

For 2026, the company has set lease renewal negotiations for North Galaxy Towers, extending the contract from 2031 to 2040, as a top priority, with a KB Asset Management official confirming the talks are ongoing.

At the same time, the REIT continues to pursue domestic prime office acquisitions to reduce overseas concentration, and management has indicated it would consider assets in Seoul's Gangnam business district to fill a current gap in that submarket.

Financially, refinancing of the North Galaxy Towers senior loan is a key variable; the loan, originated at a fixed 1.2% rate during the pandemic-era low-rate period, is estimated to come due around 2027 accounting for an extension option, and analysts have flagged that a reset to rates in the 3-4% range could structurally raise interest costs.

To address this preemptively, a KRW 150 billion rights offering was resolved in February 2026, and in June 2026 the sub-REIT entity holding the Brussels asset carried out an additional roughly KRW 29.5 billion capital raise used to manage a foreign-exchange hedge rollover.

The company has also outlined a mid-term 2026-2030 "One-KB" collaboration plan involving sale-and-leaseback structuring of group facilities and joint acquisition of core assets leveraging the affiliate network, with longer-term ambitions to expand into asset classes such as hotels and data centers.

These plans remain at an early stage without confirmed scale or timing, and their execution will need to be verified through future disclosures.

08

Bull factors

Repeated Group-Level Capital Support

KB Financial Group resolved a KRW 150 billion rights offering in February 2026, with affiliates including KB Securities and KB Kookmin Bank participating, followed by an additional roughly KRW 29.5 billion capital raise through the sub-REIT in June 2026, and KB Capital also made a fresh investment after a four-year gap.

This pattern of preemptive action, taken before any credit event materialized, has been contrasted with JR Global Reit, a similarly structured overseas-office REIT that defaulted on short-term notes and entered receivership.

Long-Term Leases and Positive Operating Cash Flow

North Galaxy Towers carries a lease that cannot be terminated early through 2031, with 99% of tenants being Belgian government bodies.

Even as net losses persisted from 2023 to 2025, operating cash flow stayed positive at KRW 5.3 billion, KRW 13.7 billion and KRW 13.8 billion respectively, suggesting much of the loss stemmed from non-cash impairment. The long-term lease structure limits near-term vacancy risk.

Portfolio Diversification via Domestic Acquisitions

Following the December 2025 acquisition of preferred units linked to Yeouido Finance Tower, the REIT has progressively secured domestic prime offices including Citibank Center in Gwanghwamun and Signature Tower in Euljiro.

The company has indicated it aims to expand into areas such as Gangnam to diversify its geographic exposure. Increasing the domestic share within a structure currently weighted toward overseas assets is expected to reduce sensitivity to currency and rate swings.

09

Bear factors

Concentration in a Single Overseas Asset

A single asset, North Galaxy Towers, accounts for roughly 77% of total assets, meaning its value swings largely determine the REIT's overall results.

The net book value has declined about 19% versus acquisition cost, and the Samsung Electronics Europe HQ building in the UK has also seen its book value fall below acquisition cost.

The precedent of JR Global Reit, a similarly structured vehicle, entering receivership after breaching its loan-to-value covenant has heightened market caution toward assets in the same district.

Refinancing Rate Burden Around 2027

North Galaxy Towers' senior loan carries a fixed 1.2% rate secured during the pandemic era, and refinancing is expected around 2027 once the extension option is factored in.

Analysts have noted that if the rate resets to the 3-4% range, annual interest expense could rise substantially on a structural basis, pressuring dividend funding. This would represent a recurring fixed-cost increase rather than a one-time charge, with lasting implications for mid-term cash flow.

Elevated Leverage and Dilution

The debt-to-equity ratio climbed sharply from 95.6% in 2022 to 298.7% in 2025, reflecting both equity erosion from accumulated losses and higher borrowing tied to asset acquisitions.

The KRW 150 billion rights offering in February 2026 intended to counter this involved dilution of existing shareholders, and additional capital raises at the sub-REIT level have followed. While repeated recapitalization supports financial stability, it can weigh on per-share value through dilution.

10

Risk factors

Overseas Property Valuation Risk

Subdued transaction activity in the European commercial real estate market across Belgium and the UK has led appraisers to apply conservative assumptions. Further declines in appraisal value could trigger additional impairment charges that weigh on operating results.

Analysts have also noted that under the company's own conservative valuation standard, the LTV could exceed the cash-trap threshold.

Interest Rate and Currency Risk

Refinancing of North Galaxy Towers' senior loan is expected around 2027, and depending on the new rate terms, interest expense could rise structurally. Holding euro-denominated assets and liabilities has required recurring foreign-exchange hedge settlement payments, and further costs have been flagged as possible. This variable is directly tied to dividend funding capacity.

Liquidity and Covenant Risk

In 2022 the company already faced a cash-trap scare when an appraisal decline pushed LTV above the covenant threshold, which was resolved at the time through additional capital injections used to repay debt.

JR Global Reit, which holds an asset in the same district, later entering receivership after a cash trap was triggered illustrates the structural risk that dividends can be suspended upon covenant breach.

While repeated rights offerings have served as a financial buffer, the possibility of needing further capital cannot be ruled out.

Overall view

KB Star Reit has posted four consecutive years of net losses attributable to controlling shareholders from 2022 through 2025, with the operating loss also widening for three straight years from 2023 to 2025.

Revenue, however, has risen for two consecutive years since bottoming in 2023, and operating cash flow has stayed positive despite the net losses, suggesting much of the shortfall stems from non-cash impairment charges.

The key variable remains North Galaxy Towers in Brussels, which represents about 77% of total assets, with the outcome of lease renewal negotiations and the senior loan refinancing expected around 2027 likely to shape future cash flow and dividend capacity.

KB Financial Group has repeatedly injected capital through rights offerings since early 2026 to buffer the balance sheet, though this has involved dilution of existing shareholders, and the debt-to-equity ratio had risen to 298.7% by the end of 2025.

The cash-trap and receivership case of JR Global Reit, which holds an asset in the same Brussels district, is cited as a reference point for structural risk, though reports indicate the official loan-to-value ratio has so far remained below the cash-trap trigger.

Ahead of any investment decision, it is important to review the fiscal year 8 annual report expected around late October 2026 along with follow-up disclosures on the lease renewal and refinancing.
