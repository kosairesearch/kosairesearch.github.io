02

Business structure

KB Star REIT is a perpetual listed REIT that invests in prime offices in Korea and abroad, managed by KB Asset Management and entrusted to KB Kookmin Bank. It listed on the KOSPI market in October 2022 and is the first listed REIT of the KB Financial Group.

The portfolio holds five assets, and North Galaxy Towers in Belgium accounts for about 77% of total assets, which makes the portfolio heavily tilted toward overseas offices. The asset is praised for high rents that support profitability, but it is also exposed to external variables such as exchange rates.

Other holdings include Samsung Europe HQ in the UK and the domestic offices Citibank Center, Signature Tower and Yeouido Finance Tower. The company is strengthening its domestic asset weighting, with core offices in the Seoul metropolitan area named as the target for new investment.

The rights issue decided in February 2026 was planned to repay debt incurred while acquiring new assets. The largest shareholder is KB Asset Management with 27.38%, and the other major holders are mostly financial institutions.

Rental income is the main revenue source, and competition comes from domestic office-focused listed REITs and REITs holding overseas assets.

04

Earnings analysis

On a reported basis, revenue rose from KRW 28.6 billion in the fiscal year ended July 2023 to KRW 29.8 billion in the fiscal year ended July 2024 and KRW 30.4 billion in the fiscal year ended July 2025, reaching KRW 35.6 billion in the fiscal year ended July 2026.

Revenue in the fiscal year ended July 2026 grew 17.1% year on year and was the largest year in the July 2023 to July 2026 span. Over the same span, operating losses were KRW 21.4 billion, KRW 26.8 billion, KRW 72.4 billion and KRW 6.9 billion, so the company has posted operating losses for four straight years.

Operating margins were -75.0%, -90.1%, -238.4% and -19.4%, with the widest loss in the fiscal year ended July 2025.

The company said that impairment losses on investment property, recognized as operating expenses in the prior fiscal year, were the main factor behind that deterioration, according to a March 18, 2026 disclosure report.

Net loss attributable to controlling shareholders was KRW 55.4 billion, KRW 31.8 billion, KRW 99.4 billion and KRW 58.9 billion, and the net loss including non-controlling interests was reported at the same amounts.

In the half-year from August 2025 to January 2026, consolidated operating profit turned to a surplus of KRW 255 million, based on preliminary figures reported on March 18, 2026, although financing costs left a net loss of KRW 10.2 billion.

Operating cash flow fell from KRW 5.3 billion and KRW 13.7 billion to KRW 13.8 billion and then to KRW 2.81 billion in the fiscal year ended July 2026. The debt ratio climbed from 125.5% to 306.6%, showing that losses and higher borrowing have moved together.

05

Industry analysis

REIT earnings are sensitive to interest rates and asset valuations. The biggest financing variable for the company is the timing of refinancing the loan on North Galaxy Towers.

That loan was raised during the ultra-low rate period of the pandemic, and although it matured in May 2026, the one-year extension option makes 2027 the effective point for resetting the interest rate.

A press estimate puts the additional annual interest burden at roughly KRW 9.3 billion to KRW 13.4 billion, and if the increase is assumed to be KRW 10 billion, distributable cash would fall by about 30%.

European office rents are high, which helps profitability, but the assets are also exposed to exchange rates and other external variables. In one listed-company data set for the real estate sector, the company's debt ratio ranked near the top of its industry group.

Compared with domestic office-focused REITs, the high share of overseas assets is both a point of difference and a source of burden. Within the sector, the difference is better described by overseas rent levels and the borrowing structure than by asset size.

06

Outlook

When the rights issue was decided in February 2026, the company said the debt ratio would fall from 364% to 182% (interview with the head of REITs at KB Asset Management).

After the final issue price was set, the amount raised dropped from about KRW 150.8 billion to about KRW 105.4 billion, and operating funds were cut from KRW 25.0 billion to KRW 1.0 billion. The debt repayment allocation was reduced from KRW 120.0 billion to KRW 102.0 billion.

However, the debt ratio at the end of July 2026 was reported at 306.6%, so the decline planned before the rights issue does not appear in the reported figures. Acquisitions of domestic assets are proceeding as planned, with core Seoul-area offices as the target for new investment.

In June 2026 the company disclosed that it would use rights issue proceeds to acquire 100% of KB Star Multi Asset No. 1. Borrowings to partly repay existing debt were disclosed in August 2026, and borrowings to repay the 9th electronic short-term bonds were disclosed in October 2026.

A press report from April 2026 said the company planned cash dividends of about KRW 25.6 billion despite the losses. The next checkpoints are the operating margin and debt ratio in the next periodic report.

08

Bull factors

Revenue growth and a narrower operating loss

Revenue in the fiscal year ended July 2026 reached KRW 35.6 billion, up 17.1%, and the operating loss narrowed to KRW 6.9 billion from KRW 72.4 billion. The operating margin improved from -238.4% to -19.4%.

A March 2026 disclosure report indicated that consolidated operating profit turned positive in the half-year to January 2026, based on preliminary figures.

Domestic office additions and a strong sponsor base

The company has said it will raise the weight of domestic offices and named core Seoul-area offices as the target for new investment. The largest shareholder is KB Asset Management with 27.38%, and affiliation with the KB Financial Group provides a financing base. The 2026 rights issue created funds for debt repayment, which eased part of the borrowing burden.

A regular semi-annual dividend structure

A report from April 2026 said the company planned cash dividends of KRW 25.6 billion despite losses. Dividends are generally paid on a semi-annual cycle with reference dates in late January and late July. The commitment to maintain payouts gives existing holders a predictable cash-flow pattern.

Bear factors

Four straight years of losses and a persistent net loss

The net loss attributable to controlling shareholders was KRW 58.9 billion in the fiscal year ended July 2026, extending a run of losses that began in the fiscal year ended July 2023. Net loss including non-controlling interests was reported at the same amount.

Operating cash flow fell to KRW 2.81 billion in the fiscal year ended July 2026 from KRW 13.8 billion a year earlier.

A high debt ratio and reliance on borrowing

The debt ratio reached 306.6%, far above 125.5% in the fiscal year ended July 2023. The company had said the February 2026 rights issue would cut the ratio from 364% to 182%, but the ratio at the end of July 2026 stood at 306.6%. Press reports also said part of the dividend funding is tied to refinancing borrowings for maturing debt.

Overseas asset concentration and exposure to rates and currency

North Galaxy Towers accounts for about 77% of total assets, so a change in its value has a large effect on the whole company. The asset has a history of impairment charges and reversals. Its euro-denominated structure exposes the company to exchange rates, and refinancing of the loan is due around 2027.

10

Risk factors

Refinancing and interest rates

The loan on North Galaxy Towers was raised at ultra-low rates during the pandemic, and after the maturity extension the rate resets around 2027. A press estimate puts the added annual interest cost at roughly KRW 9.3 billion to KRW 13.4 billion. Higher rates would feed directly into distributable cash and the size of the net loss.

Dividend funding and dilution

The 2026 rights issue increased the share count, but the final amount raised was about KRW 105.4 billion, below the original plan. Press coverage said the market is weighing the possibility of dilution in per-share dividends.

Because dividends are paid despite losses and rely on borrowing and new equity, changes in funding structure could affect dividend continuity.

Overseas asset values and currency

Overseas office values move with discount rates and interest rates, and past revaluations have pushed operating results up and down. Euro and pound-denominated assets mean that won exchange rates affect both earnings and asset values. As domestic acquisitions proceed, this weighting may shift.

12

Overall view

KB Star REIT's fiscal year ended July 2026 showed improvement on the operating side, with revenue of KRW 35.6 billion, up 17.1%, and an operating loss of KRW 6.9 billion.

However, the net loss attributable to controlling shareholders of KRW 58.9 billion extended a four-year run of losses, and the debt ratio of 306.6% is far above the level of the fiscal year ended July 2023.

Operating cash flow fell to KRW 2.81 billion, which makes the balance between borrowing and dividends more important. Strengths include revenue growth, the narrower operating loss, the sponsor base within the KB Financial Group and the plan to add domestic offices.

Weaknesses include about 77% concentration in overseas assets, which brings exchange-rate and interest-rate exposure, the refinancing of the North Galaxy Towers loan around 2027, and a debt load that the rights issue did not reduce as planned.

The share price is below book value on a price-to-book basis, which may also reflect the market's view of the losses and the borrowing structure.

The next checkpoints are the November 13, 2026 maturity of short-term borrowing, the quarter ending October 2026, the late January 2027 dividend reference date and the refinancing of the North Galaxy Towers loan.
