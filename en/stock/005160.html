Dongkuk Industries: Nickel-Plated Steel Ramp-Up Is the Test

Dongkuk Industries has recorded operating losses for five consecutive quarters amid weak demand for its core cold-rolled special steel, while nickel-plated steel supply for North American 46-series cells begins with commercial production in Q4 2026 and normalization is targeted for 2H 2027.

02

Business structure

Dongkuk Industries produces cold-rolled special steel used in automotive precision parts and, through subsidiaries, runs wind structure and refractory businesses.

On 2023 consolidated revenue, cold-rolled steel accounted for about 49.0% and color-coated steel for about 30.0%, so the two product lines together came close to 80%. The renewable energy segment accounted for about 12.0% and construction for about 9.0%.

The core of the renewable energy segment is Dongkuk Structures & Construction Company, a subsidiary that makes wind turbine towers, which recorded revenue of KRW 110.2 billion and operating profit of KRW 10.2 billion in Q3 2025.

In August 2023 the company acquired the only domestic ferro-tungsten producer and entered tungsten materials used in defense and cutting tools.

Its growth business, nickel-plated steel, was built at a Pohang plant with about KRW 130 billion of investment, completed in September 2024, and is used as casing material for cylindrical secondary batteries.

The cold-rolled business is sensitive to automotive demand, and the company cited weakness across steel, construction and home appliances as the reason for the 2025 revenue decline. The company's strategy is to keep cold-rolled special steel as a stable base while developing nickel-plated steel as a new growth pillar.

04

Earnings analysis

Annual revenue fell from KRW 860.5 billion in 2022 to KRW 757 billion in 2023, a 12.0% decline, rebounded to KRW 790.3 billion in 2024 (up 4.4%), and then dropped to KRW 727.8 billion in 2025 (down 7.9%). Among 2022–2025, 2022 was the largest year and 2025 the smallest.

Operating profit went from KRW 1.18 billion in 2022 to an operating loss of KRW 27.1 billion in 2023, turned to a profit of KRW 12 billion in 2024, and then fell 41.4% to KRW 7.03 billion in 2025.

An analyst report attributed the 2023 loss to large losses in the renewable energy segment, where new installations were delayed by North American price increases and interest rate hikes.

Net profit attributable to controlling shareholders went from KRW 6.46 billion in 2022 to a net loss of KRW 5.11 billion in 2023, then a net loss of KRW 6.31 billion in 2024 and KRW 9.99 billion in 2025, so losses widened.

Over the same period, net income including non-controlling interests was a net loss of KRW 11.1 billion in 2025, narrower than the KRW 29.6 billion loss in 2024, and the company explained this by lower non-operating expenses from reduced construction-related provisions.

The gap between the two measures reflects non-controlling interests, so the widening of the controlling-shareholder loss in 2025 should be read alongside it.

By quarter, revenue was KRW 175.9 billion in Q3 2025, KRW 151.8 billion in Q4 2025 (down 13.7% from the prior quarter), KRW 167 billion in Q1 2026, KRW 163.4 billion in Q2 2026 and KRW 168.3 billion in Q3 2026.

Operating losses were KRW 4.99 billion in Q3 2025, KRW 3.32 billion in Q4 2025, KRW 4.57 billion in Q1 2026, KRW 855 million in Q2 2026 and KRW 945 million in Q3 2026, which is five consecutive quarters of losses.

The Q3 2026 operating loss was narrower than a year earlier, while the Q1 2026 loss widened from the prior quarter. Consolidated depreciation was KRW 14.4 billion in Q3 2025, up 22% from KRW 11.8 billion in Q3 2024, with machinery depreciation from the nickel-plated steel line cited as the main driver.

05

Industry analysis

Cold-rolled steel, the core business of Dongkuk Industries, depends on demand for automotive parts and is therefore sensitive to the automotive cycle. In explaining 2025 results, the company cited a broad downturn across steel, construction and home appliances as the main reason for lower sales and profit.

The nickel-plated steel strategy is a response to the long-term decline in internal combustion vehicle demand, as the company seeks to broaden its customer base in secondary battery casing materials.

The company expects cylindrical battery applications to extend beyond electric vehicles to drones, robots and power tools. However, EV market growth has slowed, and a report noted battery makers shifting part of their orders toward energy storage systems.

A securities analysis from April 2024 said there are only about five major nickel-plated steel producers worldwide. The same analysis pointed to the supply competitiveness of tin-plate base sheet as a key driver of margins, and the company said it plans to source the base material from POSCO initially.

A steel industry official cited the company as one with well-established business diversification and said it could withstand financial pressure better than single-business peers.

06

Outlook

The company has completed quality approval for 46-series nickel-plated steel at a key North American customer and said commercial production begins in Q4 2026. It also plans to produce and supply about 1,600 tons combined of casing material for North American secondary batteries and pipe material for India.

The company expects quarterly supply of 46-series material for North America to rise to about 4,000 tons from Q1 2027. Quality approval for 46-series material at two German premium automakers is at the final approval stage, and product testing is under way with global battery cell makers.

The company is preparing supply to match domestic battery makers' mass production schedules in 1H 2027 and is negotiating with a large Chinese cell maker with a 2027 mass production target.

The company expects the profit structure of the nickel-plated steel business to improve in earnest from 2H 2027, with normalization expected between 2H 2027 and 1H 2028.

Current annual capacity is 80,000 tons, which can be raised to 130,000 tons with additional plating equipment, and the company expects an expansion around 2029. Development of materials for 21-series batteries is also under way.

The company disclosed a corporate value-enhancement plan in March 2026, and a JobPost report said the shareholder return target and portfolio improvement must be verified through actual dividends, investment execution and a recovery in profitability.

08

Bull factors

North American 46-series supply begins

The company has received quality approval from a key North American customer for nickel-plated casing material for 46-series cylindrical batteries and plans to supply that customer exclusively.

After commercial production in Q4 2026, the company expects North American supply to reach about 4,000 tons per quarter from Q1 2027. Annual capacity is 80,000 tons today and can be expanded to 130,000 tons with additional plating equipment.

Improving balance sheet trend

The debt ratio was 85.1% at end-2025, down from 93.1% in 2024, and total equity was KRW 471 billion. Operating cash flow moved from -KRW 18.6 billion in 2024 to KRW 90.1 billion in 2025. Equity attributable to controlling shareholders was KRW 370.7 billion.

Profit contribution from the wind subsidiary

Dongkuk Structures & Construction Company recorded Q3 2025 revenue of KRW 110.2 billion and operating profit of KRW 10.2 billion, with operating profit up from KRW 7.4 billion in Q3 2024.

A steel industry official said diversification would let the company absorb the financial burden of delayed nickel-plated steel ramp-up better than single-business peers. This segment's profit partly offsets losses in the core business.

09

Bear factors

Five consecutive quarters of losses

The company has posted losses for five consecutive quarters from Q3 2025 through Q3 2026, and net losses attributable to controlling shareholders total KRW 22.4 billion over the latest four quarters. The Q3 2026 controlling-shareholder net loss was KRW 5.81 billion, wider than a year earlier. The operating margin stayed low at 1.0% in 2025.

Depreciation and delayed commercialization

Depreciation tied to the nickel-plated steel facilities rose 22% to KRW 14.4 billion in Q3 2025 from KRW 11.8 billion a year earlier. A November 2025 IB Tomato report estimated at least KRW 5 billion a year in additional depreciation under a zero residual value assumption. The fixed-cost burden during the delay in customer certification and mass production was reflected in profit.

Core downturn and lower wind orders

The company cited a broad downturn across steel, construction and home appliances for the 2025 revenue decline. Wind tower backlog fell to KRW 14.2 billion in 1H 2025 from KRW 78.9 billion in 1H 2024, and Dongkuk Structures & Construction Company temporarily halted wind tower production in Q4 2025. Consolidated revenue for 1H 2026 was KRW 330.4 billion, down 17.4% year on year.

10

Risk factors

Mass production and customer certification delays

Even after the plant was completed in September 2024, commercialization of nickel-plated steel took longer than expected due to slower EV market growth and customer qualification procedures. Certification for 46-series material at two German automakers is at the final approval stage but not yet complete. If North American volumes do not ramp as planned, the 2H 2027 normalization target could slip.

Raw material supply and margins

A securities analysis from April 2024 said the supply competitiveness of tin-plate base sheet, the raw material for nickel-plated steel, would determine margins. The company plans to source base sheet from POSCO initially, and in-house sourcing was mentioned as a possible future option.

In cold-rolled steel, the direction of raw material spreads is seen as a key factor for whether the earnings recovery continues.

Shifts in battery demand structure

A November 2025 report noted that the cylindrical battery market is growing but that battery makers are shifting some orders toward energy storage systems. The company itself cited slower EV market growth as a reason for the commercialization delay. Observers expect demand to rise gradually, so the pace of volume expansion may depend on customer plans.

12

Overall view

Dongkuk Industries combines its core cold-rolled special steel business, a wind subsidiary and the new nickel-plated steel business. At end-2025 total equity was KRW 471 billion, and the debt ratio of 85.1% was lower than in 2024, while operating cash flow turned positive at KRW 90.1 billion.

Annual results show 2025 revenue of KRW 727.8 billion, operating profit of KRW 7.03 billion and a net loss attributable to controlling shareholders of KRW 9.99 billion, continuing a streak of losses that began in 2023.

Quarterly operating losses have continued for five consecutive quarters from Q3 2025, and the Q3 2026 operating loss of KRW 945 million was smaller than a year earlier.

Strengths include 46-series quality approval in North America, commercial production starting in Q4 2026 and the plan to expand quarterly supply to about 4,000 tons from Q1 2027. Weaknesses include five straight quarters of losses, depreciation burden, lower wind orders and the possibility of certification delays.

Both sets of factors depend on whether the company's plans turn into shipments and profit, and the next checkpoints are Q4 2026 shipments, the 2026 annual report and Q1 2027 results.
