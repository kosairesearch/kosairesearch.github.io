IT Infrastructure Monitoring Leader Expands Into AI Amid Earnings Volatility

Brainzcompany has maintained a long-standing position in Korea's public-sector IT infrastructure monitoring market while expanding into AI and cloud platform business through its subsidiary Aprica, even as its quarterly earnings show wide swings.

02

Business structure

Founded in 2000, Brainzcompany developed and sold IT infrastructure integrated management solutions before listing on KOSDAQ in August 2021.

Its core is Enterprise Management Software (EMS) that integrates servers, networks, and databases onto a single platform, complemented by Application Performance Monitoring (APM), log management, Security Information and Event Management (SIEM), and IT Service Management (ITSM) solutions.

Its flagship product is Zenius EMS, built around a point-solution strategy that has reinforced its market position. According to a 2024 report, revenue was split roughly into 34.0% solutions, 33.0% maintenance, and 33.0% hardware/goods sales.

Its main customers are government agencies, financial institutions, and large corporations, and it holds the top share in the public-sector integrated monitoring segment. Customers with relationships exceeding five years account for 59.8% of its client base, reflecting a business model with strong lock-in effects.

In 2022 it acquired Aprica, which brought cloud-native infrastructure management and AI development management capabilities, expanding into Cloud Management Portal (CMP) and Machine Learning Operations (MLOps) businesses.

However, hardware-related goods sales carry thin margins, meaning profitability leverage depends on growing the share of higher-margin solution and maintenance revenue.

04

Earnings analysis

2025 consolidated revenue came to KRW 25.09 billion, down from KRW 26.59 billion in 2024, while operating profit fell from KRW 3.60 billion to KRW 2.11 billion, pulling the operating margin down from 13.5% to 8.4%.

Net income attributable to owners actually rose slightly, from KRW 4.80 billion in 2024 to KRW 5.08 billion in 2025, diverging from the operating profit trend.

By quarter, 2025Q2 revenue was KRW 5.37 billion with a thin operating profit of KRW 0.07 billion, before revenue slipped to KRW 4.23 billion in 2025Q3 alongside an operating loss of KRW 0.76 billion.

Revenue then surged to KRW 11.82 billion in 2025Q4, delivering operating profit of KRW 4.73 billion and owner net income of KRW 5.01 billion, concentrating a large share of annual results in the final quarter.

The same pattern recurred in 2026: 2026Q1 revenue of KRW 4.06 billion came with an operating loss of KRW 1.22 billion, though owner net income stayed positive at KRW 0.31 billion, and 2026Q2 swung back to an operating profit of KRW 0.40 billion on revenue of KRW 5.52 billion.

Based on regulatory filing data, cumulative 2025 nine-month revenue fell 14.7% year-on-year and the operating loss widened 47.9%, but gross profit actually increased, indicating an underlying profitability improvement.

This pattern illustrates how shifts in low-margin hardware sales mix and project revenue-recognition timing can cause large swings in quarterly results.

Over the trailing four quarters (2025Q3–2026Q2), combined owner net income reached KRW 6.84 billion, recovering to a level above full-year 2025 owner net income of KRW 5.08 billion.

05

Industry analysis

Korea's IT infrastructure monitoring market is closely tied to public-sector cloud migration policy.

In March 2026 the government formally approved a roadmap to migrate all public-sector information systems to the cloud by 2030, with the conversion rate at only 42.4% as of that date, leaving a substantial gap to the target.

The Ministry of Interior and Safety earmarked KRW 54.8 billion for private-cloud migration of public information systems and KRW 271.6 billion for building dual-operation systems for key information systems in its 2027 budget proposal, planning to prioritize 50 administrative and public information systems for sequential migration through the first quarter of 2027.

As public cloud-native transformation projects move into the construction phase, competition for contracts among IT service firms such as Metanet Digital, ITCEN group affiliates, and Orchestro has intensified.

Meanwhile, the government's 2026 AI-related budget spans 738 programs across 41 ministries totaling roughly KRW 9.9 trillion, with the Ministry of Science and ICT accounting for more than half, reflecting expanding AI infrastructure investment.

However, budgets dedicated specifically to cloud industry promotion have relatively shrunk alongside the expansion of AI service budgets, signaling a shift in policy resource allocation from infrastructure toward applied services.

Against this backdrop, Brainzcompany is positioned to connect cloud and AI transition demand to integrated monitoring and operations management solutions, leveraging its top share in the public-sector monitoring segment and a large base of long-term customers.

06

Outlook

Brainzcompany continues to pursue the public-sector IT operations management market by strengthening Zenius EMS's ability to monitor on-premise, public and private cloud, and Kubernetes environments within a single system.

It has recently upgraded the product to integrate monitoring of GPU and container resources—expanding due to public-sector AI adoption—alongside existing server and network assets.

In service management, Zenius ITSM supports 13 management processes aligned with the Ministry of Interior and Safety's standard operating procedures, helping standardize IT operations at public institutions.

Subsidiary Aprica is expanding its AI transformation (AX) platform supply across finance, bio, and public sectors, anchored by its AI model/LLM development and operations platform Cheetah and its agent-building platform Serengeti AI Agent Studio, including a case of building an AI-OCR platform for an insurance company.

Aprica has been selected as a supplier for the AI voucher support program run by the Ministry of Science and ICT and NIPA for six consecutive years from 2021 through 2026, supplying Cheetah, Serengeti, and its closed-network-specialized RAG solution Gazelle.

The company's next regular disclosure is the 2026 third-quarter report, with a statutory filing deadline of November 16, 2026 under the Capital Markets Act.

Given the seasonality concentrating revenue and profit in the fourth quarter, the scale and timing of public-sector budget execution in the second half of 2026 are likely to be a key variable shaping the annual results.

08

Bull factors

Dominant Position in Public-Sector Monitoring With High Customer Loyalty

Brainzcompany holds the top share in the public-sector integrated monitoring segment, and customers with relationships exceeding five years make up 59.8% of its client base.

The government's push to fully migrate public information systems to the cloud by 2030 supports potential ongoing demand for integrated monitoring and operations management. This strong customer lock-in underpins the stability of its maintenance revenue.

AI and Cloud Business Expansion via Aprica

Subsidiary Aprica is expanding supply of its full-lifecycle AI management platforms—Cheetah, Serengeti AI Agent Studio, and Gazelle RAG—into finance, bio, and public-sector clients.

It has secured a stable policy-support channel by being selected as a supplier for the government AI voucher program for six consecutive years from 2021 through 2026. Accumulating real-world implementation references, such as an AI-OCR build for an insurance company, is also a positive sign.

Policy Tailwind From Expanding Government AI and Cloud Budgets

The Ministry of Interior and Safety earmarked KRW 54.8 billion and KRW 271.6 billion respectively for cloud migration and dual-operation system construction of public information systems in its 2027 budget proposal.

The government's 2026 AI-related budget grew sharply to roughly KRW 9.9 trillion across 738 programs at 41 ministries, broadening the base for AI infrastructure investment. This policy funding flow widens the potential demand base for integrated monitoring and AI operations management solutions.

Bear factors

Simultaneous Decline in 2025 Revenue and Margins

2025 consolidated revenue fell to KRW 25.09 billion from KRW 26.59 billion in 2024, and operating profit dropped sharply to KRW 2.11 billion from KRW 3.60 billion, pulling the operating margin down from 13.5% to 8.4%.

Cumulative nine-month 2025 revenue also fell 14.7% year-on-year while the operating loss widened 47.9%. This is attributable to shifts in the mix of low-margin hardware sales and differences in the timing of large-project revenue recognition.

Quarterly Earnings Volatility and Seasonality

The company posted operating losses in 2025Q3 and 2026Q1, while revenue surged to KRW 11.82 billion in 2025Q4 with operating profit of KRW 4.73 billion, illustrating very wide quarter-to-quarter swings.

This seasonality stems from a business structure where results depend heavily on public-sector budget execution timing, making it difficult to judge annual trends from a single quarter. Given the high share of hardware sales in the business mix, revenue growth does not always translate directly into profit growth.

Intensifying Competition in Cloud and AI Transformation

As public cloud-native transformation projects are formally awarded, numerous IT service and cloud companies including Metanet Digital, ITCEN group affiliates, Orchestro, and Innogrid are competing for contracts.

Since server and other hardware sales carry thin margins, profit leverage may be limited unless the share of solutions and maintenance revenue expands. In the AI platform market, large cloud providers and specialized startups have also entered in force, making Aprica's continued differentiation a key question.

10

Risk factors

Earnings Volatility Risk

The clear seasonal concentration of revenue and earnings in the fourth quarter creates significant quarterly volatility. Operating losses can occur in specific quarters, as seen in 2025Q3 and 2026Q1. This volatility can complicate short-term interpretation of quarterly results by the market.

Public Budget and Policy Dependence Risk

A significant portion of revenue is tied to the timing and scale of public-sector budget execution, so shifts in government budget priorities can directly affect results. Indeed, budgets dedicated to cloud industry promotion have been observed to shrink relatively as AI service budgets expand.

Delays in budget allocation or changes in project ordering timelines could be an additional source of quarterly variation.

Profitability Pressure From Subsidiary Expansion

Expanding the AI and cloud businesses of subsidiaries such as Aprica may increase personnel and infrastructure investment costs.

There is a gap between consolidated net income and net income attributable to owners due to non-controlling interests, meaning subsidiary earnings swings can affect the owners' share of profit.

Given the high proportion of hardware sales in the business mix, revenue growth does not necessarily translate into profitability improvement.

12

Overall view

Brainzcompany has maintained a stable position in Korea's IT infrastructure integrated management market, underpinned by its top share in public-sector monitoring and a high proportion of long-term customers.

In 2025, both revenue and operating margin declined year-on-year, yet owner net income rose slightly, and through 2026 the company has swung between quarterly losses and profits while the trailing four-quarter combined profit recovered to a level above the full annual results.

The seasonality that concentrates a large portion of results in the fourth quarter's public-sector budget execution period is a factor requiring caution when interpreting quarterly figures.

Expansion of AI and cloud platform business through subsidiary Aprica is aligned with policy tailwinds from the government's public-sector cloud migration drive (targeting 2030, with a 42.4% conversion rate as of March 2026) and growing AI budgets.

However, intensifying competition around cloud-native transformation projects, the share of low-margin hardware sales, and potential shifts in public budget policy are variables that warrant balanced consideration.

Investors can track the upcoming 2026 third-quarter report, the degree of fourth-quarter earnings concentration, and confirmation of the 2027 public-sector budget to assess the sustainability of the company's profit recovery.
