Public-sector IT monitoring software: quarterly profit turn and open questions

02

Business structure

Brainzcompany builds software that monitors and manages IT infrastructure from a single console. Its flagship product, Zenius, supports both on-premises and cloud environments and monitors servers, networks, applications and Kubernetes resources in an integrated way.

The company said it is focusing on Zenius EMS and ITSM to expand in the public-sector IT operations market, and it has supplied public bodies including the National Pension Service, the National Health Insurance Service, the Korea Meteorological Administration and the Ministry of Education.

Sales also run through the marketplaces of seven major domestic cloud providers, including KT Cloud, NCP and SCP, where the product is offered on a subscription basis. In March 2025 the company said it had listed on AWS Marketplace in 2024 and was pursuing a listing on Azure Marketplace.

On a breakdown compiled by Butler for the second quarter of 2026, solution sales made up 97% of revenue and product sales 3%. Product sales include hardware such as servers, and one securities analysis argued that this low-margin category limits operating leverage unless solution and maintenance revenue grows.

A cloud and AI subsidiary handles the company's AI and cloud business and offers Serengeti, a cloud management and operations platform, and Cheetah, an AI development platform. Private-sector customers include financial, defense and medical institutions.

The public-sector cloud transition and wider disaster recovery deployment are raising demand for integrated monitoring, but the company competes for public tenders with large system integrators.

The company explained that revenue concentrates in the fourth quarter, when inspections and customers' budget execution cluster at year-end.

04

Earnings analysis

On an annual basis, revenue and operating profit did not move in the same direction. Revenue rose from KRW 18.3 billion in 2022 to KRW 25.8 billion in 2023, up 41.5% year on year, and reached KRW 26.6 billion in 2024, the largest year in the 2022–2025 period, before falling to KRW 25.1 billion in 2025, down 5.6%.

Operating profit was largest in 2022 at KRW 4.78 billion, fell 30.2% to KRW 3.34 billion in 2023, rose 7.8% to KRW 3.6 billion in 2024, and fell 41.4% to KRW 2.11 billion in 2025, the smallest year in the period.

The operating margin dropped from 26.2% in 2022 to 12.9% in 2023, edged up to 13.5% in 2024 and fell to 8.4% in 2025. The company attributed the main driver of the 2025 operating profit decline to a larger operating loss at a subsidiary.

Net income attributable to owners was KRW 5.04 billion in 2022, KRW 5.03 billion in 2023 (down 0.2%), KRW 4.8 billion in 2024 (down 4.5%) and KRW 5.08 billion in 2025 (up 5.8%), so it moved less than operating profit.

Net income including non-controlling interests was KRW 4.56 billion in 2025, and the gap with owner-attributable income comes from the non-controlling interests share.

Operating cash flow was KRW 5.12 billion in 2022, KRW 3.85 billion in 2023, KRW 5.24 billion in 2024 and KRW 5.76 billion in 2025, the highest of the four years even though 2025 operating profit fell.

Quarterly, the third quarter of 2025 had revenue of KRW 4.23 billion and an operating loss of KRW 761 million, and the fourth quarter brought revenue of KRW 11.8 billion, up 179.3% from the prior quarter, and operating profit of KRW 4.73 billion, a swing to profit.

The first quarter of 2026 recorded revenue of KRW 4.06 billion, down 65.7% from the prior quarter, and an operating loss of KRW 1.22 billion, a swing back to loss. The second quarter of 2026 had revenue of KRW 5.52 billion, up 35.8%, and operating profit of KRW 403 million, a turn to profit.

The third quarter of 2026 had revenue of KRW 5.8 billion, up 5.1% from the prior quarter and 37.0% from a year earlier, and operating profit of KRW 499 million, a turn to profit from a year-earlier loss.

However, the third quarter also posted an attributable net loss of KRW 418 million, and of the KRW 6.38 billion attributable net income over the last four quarters, KRW 5.01 billion came from the fourth quarter of 2025, which accounts for most of the total.

05

Industry analysis

The public-sector IT infrastructure management market is changing as the government pushes public information systems toward cloud and expands disaster recovery.

As public IT environments shift to distributed setups in which on-premises systems, clouds, public data centers and DR centers coexist, the need for management frameworks that cover integrated monitoring, incident response, preventive inspection and standardized operating procedures is growing.

The public sector's move to AI is also adding GPU and container resources, which raises demand for integrated monitoring. Brainzcompany is strengthening Zenius EMS and ITSM in line with this shift.

On the distribution side, besides on-premises integrated management products, the company has widened its subscription channels by listing on the marketplaces of major domestic cloud providers.

On competition, large system integrators and cloud providers meet in the same public tenders, so the company's position in any large project has to be checked deal by deal. On revenue mix, product sales are only 3% of revenue, which suggests a solution-centered structure is already largely in place.

Because government budgets are executed toward year-end, the cycle is better read on an annual basis than through individual quarters.

Outlook

The company named strengthening expertise and proactive response as its priorities for 2026. On technical support, it plans to maintain technical manuals and expand internal seminars so that customers can rely on its support services.

On products, it is upgrading storage and GPU monitoring functions to meet the needs of major customers such as the Korea Meteorological Administration.

In September 2026 the company said it is strengthening its push into the public-sector IT operations market, centered on Zenius EMS and ITSM, in line with the cloud transition and wider disaster recovery of public information systems.

Its CEO said the company will use its deployment experience and integrated operating capabilities to support stable IT operations at public agencies and expand its public-sector presence.

In AI, it plans to widen the use of AI Agent technology through collaboration with its subsidiary and to build toward an integrated observability platform with real-time analysis and predictive response.

Public tenders tend to cluster around customers' year-end budget execution, so whether the fourth-quarter concentration continues in 2026 is the next check on results. Overseas, the company has listed on AWS Marketplace and is pursuing a listing on Azure Marketplace.

Bull factors

Improving quarterly trend

Revenue rose in the second and third quarters of 2026 after a first-quarter operating loss of KRW 1.22 billion. Third-quarter operating profit of KRW 499 million turned positive from a year-earlier loss, and revenue reached KRW 5.8 billion, up 37.0% year on year. The improving direction is cited as a bull point. The improvement, however, rests on only two quarters.

Policy tailwind and product fit

The government's push to move public information systems to cloud and expand disaster recovery aligns with demand for Zenius's integrated monitoring.

The company says it has built a deployment record with public customers including the National Pension Service, the National Health Insurance Service, the Korea Meteorological Administration and the Ministry of Education.

Managing on-premises and cloud resources together is positioned as a differentiator in distributed public IT setups.

Balance-sheet capacity

Total equity at the end of 2025 was KRW 54.1 billion, with KRW 53.6 billion attributable to owners, and total liabilities were KRW 6 billion. The debt-to-equity ratio was 11.1% in 2025. Operating cash flow of KRW 5.76 billion in 2025 was the highest of 2022–2025. This financial capacity widens the room for product investment and expansion.

Bear factors

Shrinking profitability

The operating margin fell from 26.2% in 2022 to 8.4% in 2025. Operating profit of KRW 2.11 billion in 2025 dropped 41.4% year on year, and the company cited a larger operating loss at a subsidiary as the main driver.

Unless the subsidiary's results improve, recovering the core business's profit alone may not restore profitability.

Quarterly losses and profit concentration

The first quarter of 2026 swung to an operating loss of KRW 1.22 billion. In the third quarter of 2026, operating profit was positive, but the company posted an attributable net loss of KRW 418 million.

Of the KRW 6.38 billion attributable net income over the last four quarters, KRW 5.01 billion came from the fourth quarter of 2025, so profit is concentrated in a single quarter, which makes the direction of the next quarter harder to gauge.

Low-margin product sales

Product sales are only 3% of revenue, but one securities analysis noted that low-margin server sales limit operating leverage unless solution revenue grows. Revenue fell 5.6% to KRW 25.1 billion in 2025. Whether solution revenue grows fast enough to offset that decline is central to the bear case.

Risk factors

Overseas marketplace expansion

The company is pursuing a listing on Azure Marketplace after its AWS Marketplace listing, aiming to widen its subscription sales channel overseas.

Unlike public tenders tied to year-end budget execution, marketplace subscriptions can arise throughout the year, so a successful listing could ease the concentration of quarterly revenue.

However, the timing of the listing and the scale of overseas subscriptions are not yet confirmed, and a delay would also postpone this effect.

Dependence on public budgets and policy

Because key customers are public agencies, results depend on budget timing and policy direction. If the pace of the public cloud transition changes or budgets shrink, demand for the products could also shift.

Competing for tenders alongside large system integrators means revenue can be affected by individual award outcomes.

AI platform expansion execution

The company plans to widen the use of AI Agent technology through collaboration with its subsidiary and to build an integrated observability platform with real-time analysis and predictive response.

This extends Zenius monitoring into AI operations, in line with the growth of GPU and container resources in the public sector. However, if development pace and customer adoption fall short of plan, the investment may not translate into revenue.

Overall view

Brainzcompany's core business is public-sector IT infrastructure management software. On disclosed results, operating profit has shrunk since 2022, while 2026 quarterly data show revenue growth and a return to operating profit.

Full-year 2025 revenue was KRW 25.1 billion, operating profit KRW 2.11 billion and the operating margin 8.4%.

In the third quarter of 2026, revenue reached KRW 5.8 billion and operating profit KRW 499 million, both improved year on year, but the attributable net loss of KRW 418 million in the same quarter means a recovery at the net income level is not yet visible.

Strengths include the Zenius-centered integrated management product structure, financial capacity with total equity of KRW 54.1 billion and liabilities of KRW 6 billion, and the policy push toward distributed public IT.

Weaknesses include earnings volatility from the fourth-quarter concentration, lower profitability from the subsidiary's operating loss, and margin pressure when product sales take up a larger share.

On valuation, the share price sits below book value attributable to owners, so asset-based comparisons are possible, but on earnings the quarterly swings are large enough that a single multiple is hard to read.

The next checkpoints are whether fourth-quarter 2026 revenue again concentrates at year-end, the full-year 2026 preliminary results, and whether the operating margin recovers in the 2026 annual report.
