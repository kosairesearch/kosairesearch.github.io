02

Business structure

Haein was founded in 1960 as a construction-machinery and engine specialist and listed on the KOSPI market in 1988. The company holds dealer arrangements with overseas brands including Caterpillar (CAT), Jungheinrich and Vermeer, supplying engines, generator sets and construction equipment.

The business divides into equipment and parts sales, engine and generator supply, and maintenance services, with equipment rental, used-equipment trading and renewable energy added on top. Equipment, parts and engines form the two main pillars of revenue across several sources.

Maintenance and replacement-part demand after delivery supports the repeatability of sales. In engines, the company secured distribution rights for the MaK medium-speed engine and is extending into marine engines.

The power segment is widening into standby generators for data centers and marine generators and propulsion engines. Major customers include domestic industrial firms in construction, shipbuilding and automotive, while the main marine customers are the domestic navy and coast guard.

Because the dealer position for a specific overseas brand is the core asset, whether supply contracts are maintained determines business continuity. Maintenance services are provided at the Cheonan No. 1 plant, and the company has three unlisted affiliates.

04

Earnings analysis

On a reported basis, revenue rose from KRW 178 billion in 2022 to a peak of KRW 276.1 billion in 2023, up 55.1% year on year, before falling 9.9% to KRW 248.8 billion in 2024 and 2.9% to KRW 241.7 billion in 2025, a second straight annual decline.

Among 2022 to 2025, 2023 was the largest revenue year and 2022 the smallest. Operating profit moved the other way, from KRW 2.68 billion in 2022 to KRW 9.14 billion in 2023 (up 240.8%), KRW 14.2 billion in 2024 (up 54.9%) and KRW 17.6 billion in 2025 (up 24.0%), rising three years in a row from 2023 through 2025.

Profit growing while revenue shrank means margin improvement, not sales volume, drove the earnings recovery. The operating margin rose every year, from 1.5% in 2022 to 3.3%, 5.7% and 7.3% in 2025.

Net income attributable to controlling shareholders swung from a loss of KRW 2.07 billion in 2022 to KRW 7.4 billion in 2023, a turn to profit, then KRW 7.27 billion in 2024 (down 1.8%) and KRW 14.2 billion in 2025 (up 95.7%).

Annual net income was reported at the same amounts as net income attributable to controlling shareholders in each year.

Operating cash flow went from KRW 6.55 billion in 2022 to an outflow of KRW -1.81 billion in 2023 and KRW -21.3 billion in 2024, two consecutive years of outflow, before turning to KRW 46.1 billion in 2025.

At the end of 2025, total equity was KRW 127.6 billion, all of it attributable to controlling shareholders, and total liabilities were KRW 123 billion.

Quarterly results moved from KRW 56.5 billion of revenue and KRW 2.07 billion of operating profit in the third quarter of 2025 to KRW 52.6 billion of revenue (down 7.0% quarter on quarter) and KRW 10.2 billion of operating profit (up 392.4%) in the fourth quarter.

In the first quarter of 2026, revenue fell 23.3% to KRW 40.4 billion and operating profit fell 87.2% to KRW 1.3 billion, before the second quarter recovered to KRW 64.2 billion of revenue (up 58.9%) and KRW 7.1 billion of operating profit (up 445.9%).

In the third quarter of 2026, revenue was KRW 43 billion (down 33.0% quarter on quarter and down 24.0% year on year), and the company posted an operating loss of KRW 2.43 billion and a net loss attributable to controlling shareholders of KRW 198 million.

Net income attributable to controlling shareholders over the last four quarters totals KRW 17.3 billion, and two quarters account for most of it.

Such large quarter-to-quarter swings suggest sensitivity to delivery timing, but the cause of the third-quarter loss should be checked in the segment discussion of the annual report.

05

Industry analysis

The construction-machinery business moves closely with construction activity. In the first half of 2026, a construction slowdown and foreign-exchange uncertainty weighed on demand for construction equipment and engines, and standalone revenue fell 21.2%.

Over the same period, sales of engines tied to AI data centers and defense rose, lifting standalone operating profit 58.7%.

Standby generators for data centers are cited for growth potential as power-stability demand rises, and Hana Securities pointed to growth in the market for generators of 3.9 MW and above as a point to watch.

The same report noted that data-center standby-generator market share and the B2B structure are not disclosed, which makes earnings estimates difficult. In marine, expectations for growth in naval vessel MRO work are being raised in the market.

The competitive position rests on supplying Caterpillar engines to build out generator engineering, so the status of the engine supply line is central. Even if construction-equipment conditions weaken, whether maintenance and parts demand cushions the decline should be checked against equipment sales trends.

The most direct gauge of the cycle is equipment sales and engine shipments, both traceable through segment revenue in periodic reports. Domestic navy and coast guard remain the main marine generator and propulsion-engine customers, but growth with foreign navies has also been raised as a possibility.

Outlook

Consolidated operating profit for the first half of 2026 was reported at KRW 8.4 billion, up 58.7% year on year, and the power-energy segment's operating profit rose 62.3% to KRW 5.5 billion.

Most of the first-half profit came from the second quarter's KRW 7.1 billion, so the third-quarter swing to a loss is the key variable for the second half. A KRW 46.4 billion contract with Hyundai E&C for standby generators at a Pohang AI data center, covering 61.5 MW across 20 units, was reported as disclosed.

When that contract is recognized in revenue and at what scale will be visible in the revenue breakdown of upcoming periodic reports. In marine generators and propulsion engines, growth opportunities with foreign navies beyond the domestic navy and coast guard have been raised.

Expectations tied to naval vessel MRO work are also being noted in the market. In construction equipment, sales depend on whether construction activity recovers, so whether the third-quarter 2026 revenue decline is temporary or a trend should be judged from subsequent quarterly sales.

The debt-to-equity ratio fell to 96.4% at the end of 2025 from 2022, but operating cash flow moved from KRW -21.3 billion in 2024 to KRW 46.1 billion in 2025, so the stability of cash generation is the next point to watch.

The 2026 annual report, the next periodic filing, will show full-year operating margin and fourth-quarter results.

08

Bull factors

Power segment growth

The company said power-energy segment operating profit rose 62.3% to KRW 5.5 billion in the first half of 2026. A KRW 46.4 billion standby-generator contract for a Pohang AI data center was reported, and expansion of generator engineering built on Caterpillar engines is planned. If data-center power demand continues, this segment could broaden the earnings base.

Improving profitability

The operating margin rose each year, from 1.5% in 2022 to 3.3% in 2023, 5.7% in 2024 and 7.3% in 2025. Over the same period the debt-to-equity ratio fell from 122.6% to 96.4%, and total equity at the end of 2025 was KRW 127.6 billion. Profit rising while revenue shrank points to a genuine improvement in profitability.

Room to expand in marine engines and MRO

The main marine generator and propulsion-engine customers are currently the domestic navy and coast guard, but growth with foreign navies has been raised by securities analysts. Expectations for naval vessel MRO demand are also being discussed in the market. Securing distribution rights for the MaK medium-speed engine provides a base for expansion in marine engines.

Bear factors

Continued revenue decline

Revenue peaked at KRW 276.1 billion in 2023 and then fell for two straight years in 2024 and 2025. Third-quarter 2026 revenue was KRW 43 billion, down 24.0% year on year. A smaller revenue base weakens the absorption of fixed costs, which can magnify swings in profit.

Volatile quarterly profit

Operating profit was KRW 1.3 billion in the first quarter of 2026, KRW 7.1 billion in the second and an operating loss of KRW 2.43 billion in the third, a wide spread between quarters.

Two quarters make up most of the KRW 17.3 billion of net income attributable to controlling shareholders over the last four quarters, so profit is concentrated in a few periods. The drivers of the third-quarter swing to a loss are a point to check in the periodic report.

Construction slowdown and cash flow

A construction slowdown and foreign-exchange uncertainty are cited as weighing on demand for construction equipment and engines. Operating cash flow was an outflow of KRW -1.81 billion in 2023 and KRW -21.3 billion in 2024, two consecutive years. It turned to KRW 46.1 billion in 2025, but whether one year of improvement holds needs further confirmation.

Risk factors

Customer and platform concentration

Defense and aviation parts and data-center power equipment may depend on a few customers or specific platforms, so delayed contracts could increase earnings swings. Because data-center standby-generator market share is not disclosed, the durability of order volume is hard to gauge from outside.

Raw materials, FX and cost pressure

Construction equipment and engines can be affected by foreign-exchange swings, creating pressure on both costs and demand. If raw-material, labor and energy costs cannot be passed through to prices, margins narrow, and lower plant utilization raises the burden of fixed costs.

Shareholder structure and free float

The disclosed stake held by the largest shareholder and related parties is 22.47% as of July 2025, and treasury shares are 20.64% as of October 2025. With a limited number of shares trading in the market, price swings can widen when volume is thin.

Overall view

Haein is a Caterpillar dealer supplying construction equipment, engines and generators, and operating profit rose for three straight years to KRW 17.6 billion in 2025. Revenue fell for two straight years after peaking in 2023, while the operating margin rose from 1.5% in 2022 to 7.3% in 2025.

The debt-to-equity ratio fell to 96.4% and operating cash flow recovered to KRW 46.1 billion in 2025. Yet in the third quarter of 2026, revenue of KRW 43 billion and an operating loss of KRW 2.43 billion showed quarterly volatility again.

Strengths include demand for data-center power equipment, profit growth in the power-energy segment and the potential expansion of marine engines. Weaknesses include weaker construction-equipment demand amid the construction slowdown, reliance on a few customers and operating cash outflows in 2024.

Because the two sides stand against each other, the next checkpoint is the full-year operating margin and fourth-quarter results in the 2026 annual report. If revenue recovery is confirmed in the first-quarter 2027 report, it will be possible to gauge whether the third-quarter 2026 loss was a one-off factor or a trend.
