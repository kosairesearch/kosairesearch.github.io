02

Business structure

Dongkuk Refractories & Steel is a materials company whose core products are shaped and unshaped refractories and advanced ceramics used in steel and non-ferrous metal industries.

The business runs in three segments, Refractories, Steel and IT, and an aggregated breakdown by Q2 2026 revenue put Refractories at 70%, Steel at 22% and IT at 9%. Refractories are consumable materials used in steelmaking, so orders move with customers' operating rates.

The Steel segment produces and supplies steel products such as single and square pipes and HGI steel, serving construction and industrial demand. Major customers include the group company Dongkuk Steel Mill and other domestic steelmakers, and stable dealings with these steel customers form the base of the business.

Overseas, the company runs subsidiaries and a joint venture in China, giving it a local supply base. The IT segment operates through subsidiaries, and larger orders there were cited as a contributor to 2025 earnings.

In rare earths, the company is described as having a supply cooperation for rare earth refining equipment with Australian rare earth company ASM. On technology, it holds 20 patents.

The competitive picture is one of high customer concentration, since refractory demand depends on steel facility investment and operating rates.

04

Earnings analysis

Annual revenue fell from KRW 118.2 billion in 2022 to KRW 108.4 billion in 2023 (down 8.3% year on year), then rose to KRW 110.4 billion in 2024 (up 1.8%) and KRW 110.7 billion in 2025 (up 0.3%), an increase in both 2024 and 2025. 2022 was the largest revenue year in the reported period and 2023 the smallest.

Operating profit moved from KRW 4.21 billion in 2022 to KRW 2.08 billion in 2023 (down 50.4%) and KRW 1.14 billion in 2024 (down 45.4%), making 2024 the smallest year.

FY2025 operating profit of KRW 3.38 billion rose 196.9% year on year, and the company pointed to cost reduction and larger orders at its steel and IT subsidiaries as the main reasons for the gain. The operating margin went from 3.6% in 2022 to 1.9% in 2023 and 1.0% in 2024 before recovering to 3.1% in 2025.

Profit attributable to controlling shareholders fell from KRW 3.71 billion in 2022 to KRW 474 million in 2023 (down 87.2%), rebounded to KRW 1.63 billion in 2024 (up 243.8%), then dropped to KRW 581 million in 2025 (down 64.4%).

In 2025 operating profit rose while profit attributable to controlling shareholders fell, so the gap between the two widened. Net income including non-controlling interests was KRW 1.73 billion in 2024 and KRW 579 million in 2025, close to the controlling-shareholder figure in 2025.

At end-2025, total equity was KRW 82.1 billion, of which equity attributable to controlling shareholders was KRW 79 billion, and total liabilities were KRW 43.7 billion.

Operating cash flow went from KRW 6.57 billion in 2022 to KRW 6.23 billion in 2023 and KRW 5.61 billion in 2024, then rose to KRW 6.73 billion in 2025.

By quarter, Q3 2025 revenue was KRW 28.6 billion, operating profit KRW 1.24 billion and profit attributable to controlling shareholders KRW 637 million; in Q4 revenue fell to KRW 26 billion (down 8.8% quarter on quarter), operating profit to KRW 167 million (down 86.6%), and the company swung to a controlling-shareholder net loss of KRW 442 million.

The cause of the Q4 drop cannot be pinned down from the figures alone.

Q1 2026 revenue was KRW 27.8 billion (up 6.9%), operating profit KRW 565 million (up 238.6%), and controlling-shareholder net income KRW 85.95 million, a return to profit; Q2 revenue was KRW 30.1 billion, operating profit KRW 893 million (up 58.1%) and controlling-shareholder net income KRW 437 million, extending the recovery.

Q3 2026 revenue was KRW 27.1 billion (down 5.2% year on year), operating profit KRW 738 million (down 40.7%) and controlling-shareholder net income KRW 198 million (down 68.9%), a renewed decline.

05

Industry analysis

Refractory demand is tied to steel production and facility investment, so steel-sector conditions feed through into orders. Dongkuk Steel Mill's Q3 2026 revenue was KRW 932.28 billion, up 21.20% year on year, and its operating profit was KRW 29.88 billion, up 22.02%.

Its cumulative operating profit for the first nine months also rose sharply over the prior-year period. Over the same quarter, Dongkuk Refractories & Steel's revenue fell 5.2%, so a recovery at its steel customer did not translate directly into higher revenue for this company.

The cycle position reads as a stretch where customer profit recovery signals and this company's Q3 revenue decline point in different directions. Chinese subsidiaries add exposure to Chinese steel demand.

The service classifies the company under chemicals, but its business is closer to steel materials, and most listed chemical peers are centered on petrochemicals or battery materials, so their end markets differ.

Rare earth refining equipment cooperation is presented as a direction for expansion, but no separate figure appears in the disclosed segment mix. The 2025 profit gain attributed to cost reduction shows that cost structure drives the margin.

Bull factors

Operating profit recovery and cost improvement

FY2025 operating profit was KRW 3.38 billion, up 196.9%, and the operating margin rose from 1.0% in 2024 to 3.1%. The pattern reads as cost-structure gains flowing into results. Operating cash flow also rose to KRW 6.73 billion in 2025 from 2024, strengthening cash backing for earnings.

Gradual balance-sheet improvement

The debt ratio declined every year, from 59.3% in 2022 to 56.5% in 2023, 55.8% in 2024 and 53.3% in 2025. Total liabilities of KRW 43.7 billion compare with total equity of KRW 82.1 billion at end-2025. Quarterly revenue held around the KRW 27 to 30 billion range from Q3 2025 through Q2 2026.

Captive group customer base and diversification

As a member of the Dongkuk Steel Mill group, the company has volume anchored by internal customers. Dongkuk Steel Mill's Q3 2026 operating profit rose 22.02% year on year.

Chinese subsidiaries and a joint venture give it a channel to overseas demand, and the rare earth refining equipment cooperation with an Australian company is presented as a business axis separate from steel.

Bear factors

Thin operating margins

The operating margin slid from 3.6% in 2022 to 1.9% in 2023 and 1.0% in 2024, and reached only 3.1% in 2025. Q3 2026 operating profit fell 40.7% year on year, so the quarterly recovery has been uneven. With a thin margin, cost and order swings pass straight through to earnings.

Volatile profit to controlling shareholders

Profit attributable to controlling shareholders fell from KRW 3.71 billion in 2022 to KRW 474 million in 2023 and was KRW 581 million in 2025. The company swung to a controlling-shareholder net loss in Q4 2025, and Q3 2026 profit of KRW 198 million was down 68.9% year on year. Even the last four quarters combined leave the earnings base thin.

Flat revenue and customer concentration

Revenue went from KRW 118.2 billion in 2022 to KRW 110.7 billion in 2025 and fell 5.2% year on year in Q3 2026. Because it is sensitive to steel-sector conditions and group orders, a drop in customer volume moves revenue quickly. New businesses such as rare earths do not yet show a revenue contribution in the reported figures.

Risk factors

Customer and sector risk

If steel facility investment and operating rates weaken, refractory orders decline. High customer concentration means changes in group orders reach revenue quickly, and the Q3 2026 revenue decline is an example of this pattern in quarterly results. A turn in steel-sector conditions changes the company's revenue path in the same direction.

Cost and margin risk

With operating margins running between 1% and 3%, raw material and energy cost swings move profit sharply. Because the 2025 recovery leaned on cost reduction, a failure to sustain cost control could reverse it. The drop in Q4 2025 operating profit to KRW 167 million illustrates this sensitivity.

Execution and related-party risk

Rare earth refining cooperation and overseas businesses such as the Chinese subsidiaries do not yet show a revenue timeline, so their realization is uncertain. A high share of dealings with group affiliates means trading terms and volume allocation can shift results. As a small-cap stock, its share price can also move widely.

12

Overall view

Dongkuk Refractories & Steel is a materials company whose core refractory business is tied to steel customers, and FY2025 operating profit rose 196.9%, pointing to an earnings recovery.

However, the operating margin was still only 3.1% in 2025, and Q3 2026 operating profit fell 40.7% year on year, so the durability of the recovery needs checking every quarter.

The most visible bear factor is the thin earnings base, with profit attributable to controlling shareholders totaling only KRW 280 million over the last four quarters. On the other side, the debt ratio fell every year from 2022 and total equity of KRW 82.1 billion at end-2025 supports financial stability.

The return to profit in Q1 2026 and the improvement in Q2 read as early signs of a core business recovery, while the Q3 decline shows those signs remain uneven. Revenue has been flat, from KRW 118.2 billion in 2022 to KRW 110.7 billion in 2025, and dependence on steel-sector conditions and group orders is high.

On valuation, the small earnings base makes the multiple sensitive to earnings swings, and quarter-end price-to-book ran between 0.5x and 0.7x in the past. The next checkpoints are the 2026 full-year operating margin, whether Q4 2026 swings to a loss, and the trend in Q1 2027.
