Genematrix Turns Profitable in Q2 on Export Growth

Genematrix swung to both operating and controlling-interest net profit in the second quarter of 2026, but given the company's history of quarter-to-quarter swings, whether this marks a sustained trend remains to be confirmed.

02

Business structure

Genematrix was founded in 2000 and listed on KOSDAQ in 2009 as a molecular diagnostics specialist. Its proprietary C-Tag multiplex molecular diagnostics core technology underpins the NeoPlex product line, which simultaneously detects multiple pathogens in a single test and forms the core of revenue.

The diagnostics portfolio broadly covers infectious disease categories including sexually transmitted infections (STI), human papillomavirus (HPV), respiratory infections (RI), and tuberculosis/non-tuberculous mycobacteria (TB/NTM).

In biochemical diagnostics, the company offers the HiSens syphilis diagnostic product, which it says has recorded double-digit annual sales growth driven by academic marketing efforts at home and abroad.

Revenue is weighted most heavily toward product sales, with merchandise sales and other revenue making up the remainder.

To expand overseas distribution, Genematrix signed a global supply agreement with European in-vitro diagnostics leader ELITechGroup in August 2024 for worldwide sales of its molecular diagnostics products.

In April 2026, it signed a memorandum of understanding with global IVD solutions company Zybio to build an integrated molecular diagnostics platform combining reagents and instruments.

Beyond its core IVD business, the company is independently developing GMAI-02, a next-generation hepatitis A vaccine candidate, representing an expansion from diagnostics into a drug pipeline.

04

Earnings analysis

Annual revenue fell 12.5% from KRW 11.2 billion in 2022 to KRW 9.81 billion in 2023, then rose for two consecutive years to KRW 10 billion in 2024 (up 2.1% year-on-year) and KRW 10.6 billion in 2025 (up 6.2% year-on-year).

Over the same period, the operating loss widened from KRW 1.52 billion in 2022 to KRW 2.55 billion in 2023, before narrowing for two straight years to KRW 1.64 billion in 2024 and KRW 679 million in 2025, extending a run of four consecutive annual operating losses.

Controlling-interest net loss narrowed sharply from KRW 8.11 billion in 2022 to KRW 895 million in 2023, widened again to KRW 3.75 billion in 2024, then narrowed to KRW 1.83 billion in 2025.

On a quarterly basis, the operating loss widened for four straight quarters, from KRW 90.72 million in Q2 2025 to KRW 109 million in Q3, KRW 191 million in Q4, and KRW 327 million in Q1 2026, before Genematrix swung to an operating profit of KRW 393 million in Q2 2026 on revenue of KRW 2.91 billion (up 15.6% quarter-on-quarter and 8.1% year-on-year), turning positive versus both the prior quarter and the year-earlier period.

Controlling-interest net profit also turned positive in Q2 2026 at KRW 689 million versus both comparison periods, following a KRW 266 million profit in Q3 2025, reflecting a pattern of recurring quarterly swings.

The cumulative controlling-interest net loss over the trailing four quarters (Q3 2025 through Q2 2026) was KRW 190 million, narrowing to a level close to breakeven on an annualized basis.

Operating cash flow turned positive at KRW 1.29 billion in 2025, an improvement from outflows of KRW 1.02 billion in 2022, KRW 2.35 billion in 2023, and KRW 200 million in 2024. The operating margin bottomed at negative 26.0% in 2023 before gradually narrowing to negative 16.4% in 2024 and negative 6.4% in 2025.

05

Industry analysis

The molecular (in-vitro) diagnostics market is moving through a transition period following the COVID-19 boom, with demand shifting toward a broader range of infectious disease testing including respiratory illness, sexually transmitted infections, and tuberculosis.

The European Union has tightened its in-vitro diagnostic device regulation from the former CE-IVDD to CE-IVDR, raising requirements for technical documentation and clinical evaluation—a barrier for smaller diagnostics firms but a differentiator for those that obtain certification.

Genematrix completed CE-IVDR registration for its NeoPlex product line and its tuberculosis/non-tuberculous mycobacteria diagnostic product during 2025 and 2026, broadening its footprint across Europe, the Middle East, and Southeast Asia.

The global IVD market is a mix of large multinational players such as Roche and Abbott alongside smaller domestic and international molecular diagnostics specialists, and Genematrix is pursuing a strategy of supplementing its distribution reach through partnerships with automated-platform providers such as ELITechGroup and Zybio.

By market capitalization, the company sits within the small-cap KOSDAQ bio-diagnostics segment, with a scale gap in R&D and marketing resources relative to large global diagnostics companies.

However, its proprietary C-Tag core technology, which enables simultaneous detection of multiple pathogens, has recently been expanded via a U.S. patent to support detection of up to 15 targets simultaneously, beyond the previous five-target limit of conventional real-time PCR, which is viewed as a technical differentiator.

06

Outlook

The company has pointed to expanding overseas exports of its NeoPlex product line as the key driver behind its return to profitability in Q2 2026, stating that it expects this trend to continue.

Whether the ELITechGroup supply agreement and the Zybio integrated-platform collaboration translate into visible revenue contribution will be a key point to watch going forward.

On the drug pipeline side, the next-generation hepatitis A vaccine candidate GMAI-02 was selected for the Ministry of Health and Welfare's vaccine commercialization support program for essential immunization self-sufficiency, securing clinical project funding support of KRW 3.112 billion.

The company has confirmed clinical trial sites and plans to begin Phase 1 trials in the second half of 2026, after which it intends to pursue Investigational New Drug (IND) approval for Phase 2 based on the safety and immunogenicity results from Phase 1.

On the financing side, a convertible bond settled on October 6, 2026 had its stated use of proceeds amended from facility investment to funds for acquiring equity in another company, suggesting the company may be exploring external investment or inorganic growth options.

Having received Phase 1 IND approval from the Ministry of Food and Drug Safety in the first half of 2025 based on accumulated non-clinical data, the vaccine program is now moving into the clinical stage in earnest.

That said, vaccine development typically requires many years across successive clinical phases before commercialization, a factor also worth weighing.

Bull factors

The company has cited expanded exports of its NeoPlex multiplex molecular diagnostics line as the key driver of this improvement. Whether this momentum continues in subsequent quarters is a key point to watch.

Expanding Overseas Partnerships

Genematrix signed a global supply agreement with ELITechGroup in August 2024 and an MOU with Zybio in April 2026 to build an integrated molecular diagnostics platform.

In 2025 and 2026 it completed CE-IVDR registration for its NeoPlex and TB/NTM diagnostic products, strengthening its foothold for entering the European market. Whether these partnerships and certifications translate into actual overseas revenue growth is an important factor to monitor.

Narrowing Losses and Improved Cash Flow

The annual operating loss narrowed for two consecutive years, from KRW 2.55 billion in 2023 to KRW 1.64 billion in 2024 and KRW 679 million in 2025. Operating cash flow also turned positive at KRW 1.29 billion in 2025, breaking from the cash outflow pattern seen in 2022–2024.

The cumulative controlling-interest net loss over the trailing four quarters also narrowed to KRW 190 million, approaching breakeven.

Bear factors

Recurring Quarterly Volatility

Genematrix posted a controlling-interest net profit of KRW 266 million in Q3 2025, then swung back to a net loss of KRW 923 million in Q4, with losses continuing into Q1 2026. Operating losses also widened for four consecutive quarters from Q2 2025 through Q1 2026 before turning positive in Q2. A single quarter's return to profit alone may not be sufficient to confirm a sustained trend reversal.

Balance Sheet Strain and Convertible Bonds

The debt-to-equity ratio rose steadily from 31.9% in 2022 to 56.1% in 2025. Convertible bonds issued in January 2025 (the 1st and 3rd tranches, KRW 5 billion each) and an additional convertible bond settled in October 2026 remain outstanding, leaving potential for share dilution if conversion rights are exercised.

In February 2026, the 2nd tranche convertible bond was extinguished through conversion, and similar conversion events could recur going forward.

Early-Stage Drug Pipeline

The next-generation hepatitis A vaccine candidate GMAI-02 is only entering Phase 1 trials in the second half of 2026, an early stage of development that will require multiple further phases, including Phase 2 and Phase 3, over an extended period before commercialization.

Additional financing needs cannot be ruled out depending on clinical results. Compared with the company's existing diagnostics business, drug development carries greater uncertainty in determining ultimate success or failure.

Risk factors

Financial / Liquidity

The debt-to-equity ratio has trended upward from 31.9% in 2022 to 56.1% in 2025, and the outstanding balance of convertible bonds leaves room for an increase in share count.

The convertible bond settled in October 2026 had its stated use of proceeds changed from facility investment to funds for acquiring equity in another company, warranting further clarification on the intended use of funds.

While operating cash flow turned positive in 2025, whether this represents a stable pattern needs to be confirmed through additional quarterly results.

R&D / Regulatory Approval

The next-generation hepatitis A vaccine candidate GMAI-02 is in an early stage ahead of Phase 1 trial initiation in the second half of 2026, with significant uncertainty around subsequent steps such as Phase 2 IND approval.

Costs and time burdens associated with meeting tightened overseas regulatory standards such as the EU's CE-IVDR may continue to arise. Delays in regulatory approval timelines for new products across different countries could also affect the timing of revenue expansion.

Market / Competition

The global IVD market features competition between large multinational players and numerous small and mid-sized molecular diagnostics companies, and the relatively small-scale Genematrix may be at a disadvantage in terms of R&D and marketing resources.

As the export share of revenue grows, currency fluctuations could affect profitability. If collaboration outcomes with partners such as ELITechGroup and Zybio fall short of expectations, the pace of overseas revenue expansion could slow.

Overall view

Following a period of declining revenue and heavy losses in 2022–2023, Genematrix has shown a recovery trajectory with two consecutive years of revenue growth and a narrowing operating loss in 2024–2025.

In Q2 2026, both operating profit and controlling-interest net profit turned positive, with export growth in the NeoPlex line showing up in the numbers, but given the four consecutive quarters of widening operating losses that preceded it, the sustainability of this trend needs to be verified through further quarterly results.

Overseas partnerships with ELITechGroup and Zybio and expanded CE-IVDR certifications could serve as a foundation for export-driven growth.

At the same time, the rising debt-to-equity ratio, potential dilution from outstanding convertible bonds, and the long-term uncertainty of the early-stage hepatitis A vaccine pipeline are factors that warrant balanced consideration.

The next quarterly report, the status of GMAI-02's Phase 1 trial initiation, and the specific use of the convertible bond proceeds are likely to serve as important reference points going forward.
