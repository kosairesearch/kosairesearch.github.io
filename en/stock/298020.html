Spandex Supply Recovery, Uneven Quarterly Profits

02

Business structure

Hyosung TNC operates a fiber segment centered on spandex, along with its PTMG raw material, nylon and polyester yarns, and a trading segment that handles chemicals and steel products. The company is reported to have held the top global share in spandex for several years.

In an April 2025 report, the fiber segment was described as about 45% of revenue but 71% of operating profit. Production sites are located in China, Vietnam, India, Turkey and Brazil. The company's in-house PTMG production is viewed as a strength in managing raw material cost swings.

Its share in China is in the 20% range, and above 60% in Asia excluding China. The fiber brands were consolidated into CREORA and regen. The trading segment uses an overseas branch network to deal in steel and chemicals. Diversification into specialty gases such as NF3 and tire reinforcement materials is under way. Lycra, with a 6–7% spandex share, is cited as a notable competitor.

04

Earnings analysis

On a reported basis, annual revenue went from KRW 8.88 trillion in 2022 to KRW 7.53 trillion in 2023 (down 15.3% year on year), then to KRW 7.78 trillion in 2024 (up 3.3%) and KRW 7.69 trillion in 2025 (down 1.0%). The largest year in 2022–2025 was 2022, and the smallest was 2023.

Operating profit rose from KRW 123.6 billion in 2022 to KRW 213.4 billion in 2023 (up 72.7%) and KRW 270.7 billion in 2024 (up 26.9%), then fell to KRW 251.5 billion in 2025 (down 7.1%). Operating margin was 1.4%, 2.8%, 3.5% and 3.3% for 2022 through 2025.

Controlling-shareholder net income was KRW 93.2 billion in 2023 and KRW 134.5 billion in 2024, then KRW 10.8 billion in 2025 (down 92.0%). Net income including non-controlling interests was KRW 38.2 billion in 2025, a different figure.

The debt ratio rose to 193.3% at the end of 2025, and operating cash flow fell from KRW 654.9 billion in 2024 to KRW 456.1 billion in 2025. On a quarterly basis, operating profit was KRW 44.7 billion in Q4 2025 with a controlling-shareholder net loss of KRW 30 billion.

It then turned to operating profit of KRW 86.2 billion and controlling-shareholder net income of KRW 39 billion in Q1 2026. Q2 2026 revenue was KRW 2.42 trillion, operating profit KRW 188.8 billion and controlling-shareholder net income KRW 95.5 billion.

Q3 2026 revenue was KRW 2.13 trillion and operating profit KRW 110.2 billion. NH Investment & Securities estimated that the average spandex price in Q2 2026 rose 19.3% from the prior quarter. Reports put the Q2 2026 fiber segment operating profit at KRW 165.2 billion and trading and other at KRW 23.6 billion.

05

Industry analysis

The spandex market is moving from a period of oversupply led by China toward a more balanced supply-demand position. NH Investment & Securities estimated new spandex capacity in 2026 at about 30,000 tons, far below roughly 170,000 tons in the prior year. In the same analysis, demand was projected to grow 6–8% a year.

Analysts have pointed to Chinese producers cutting output and reducing inventories as a driver of the price rebound. NH put China's spandex utilization at 85% as of early April 2026. Lycra exited Chapter 11 protection in May 2026, and NH said this reduced the share-gain expectations tied to a weaker rival.

Production sites near demand centers in Turkey, Vietnam, India and Brazil are cited as having benefited from shipping disruptions tied to Middle East conflict. In China, rising inventory days have raised concerns about a short-term peak-out. Its key raw material, BDO, is coal-based, which is viewed as lowering procurement risk.

06

Outlook

NH Investment & Securities expects 2026 earnings to be front-loaded, with supply-demand conditions remaining firm into 2027. The same analysis noted no new spandex capacity in 2026 and about 125,000 tons expected in 2027, with utilization projected to rise from 77.5% in 2026 to 78.6% in 2027.

Second-half prices are expected to fall on seasonal weakness and softer raw material costs, prompting a 10.6% cut to the 2026 operating profit estimate. In a June 18, 2026 report, NH lowered its target price from KRW 730,000 to KRW 540,000 and kept its Buy rating.

The same report removed a 10% premium on the target multiple that had reflected the rival's restructuring. The company's medium-term plan is to use spandex cash generation to grow nylon and polyester profitability and build specialty gases. Specialty gas products are planned to rise from six to fifteen by 2029.

Whether nylon and polyester stay in profit after turning positive in Q2 2026 is the next thing to watch. Third-quarter operating profit fell from the prior quarter, so fourth-quarter pricing is the key variable.

08

Bull factors

Supply Balance and Limited Capacity

New spandex capacity in 2026 is estimated at about 30,000 tons, well below roughly 170,000 tons in the prior year. NH estimated that even with about 125,000 tons of 2027 additions, utilization would rise from 77.5% to 78.6% after demand growth. This estimate was presented as evidence of a firm spandex cycle.

Cost Edge and Production Footprint

Direct production of the PTMG raw material is viewed as a strong buffer against raw material swings. Plants near demand in Turkey, Vietnam, India and Brazil benefited relatively during shipping disruptions. Analysts also note that coal-based BDO lowers procurement risk.

Quarterly Profit Recovery

Controlling-shareholder net income turned positive at KRW 39 billion in Q1 2026, and operating profit rose to KRW 188.8 billion in Q2 2026, with controlling-shareholder net income of KRW 95.5 billion. Reports put Q2 fiber segment operating profit at KRW 165.2 billion. Nylon and polyester also moved into profit.

Bear factors

Second-Half Price Decline Outlook

NH expects second-half spandex prices to fall on seasonal weakness and softer raw material costs, cutting its 2026 operating profit estimate by 10.6%. The report framed this as a short-term pause. If second-half prices fall more than expected, the profit recovery could be narrower.

Residual Oversupply and Peak-Out Concerns

Industry participants point out that even without new capacity, accumulated excess supply and weaker global textile demand keep supply in the lead. In China, rising inventory days have fueled concerns about a short-term peak-out and a structural slump. If these concerns materialize, the sustainability of the 2026 profit recovery could weaken.

Balance Sheet Burden and Earnings Volatility

The debt ratio rose to 193.3% at the end of 2025 from the end of 2024. Controlling-shareholder net income fell 92.0% to KRW 10.8 billion in 2025. Q3 2026 operating profit fell 41.6% from the prior quarter, so quarterly earnings volatility remains high.

Risk factors

Renewed Chinese Supply

About 125,000 tons of capacity is expected in 2027. If it outpaces demand growth, utilization and prices could come under renewed pressure. Whether Chinese producers' output cuts and inventory reductions hold is also a point to watch. Renewed expansion by Chinese rivals could limit the price rebound.

Raw Materials and Logistics

Global shipping disruption tied to Middle East conflict and swings in petrochemical-based raw material prices are cited as profit variables. If logistics flows between overseas plants change, the size of the benefit could shift. Raw material procurement and freight costs need to be tracked together.

Financial Burden and Expansion Execution

The debt ratio rose to 193.3% at the end of 2025 from 159.6% at the end of 2024. The plan to expand specialty gas products to 15 by 2029 is in execution. The investment burden could slow the pace of the earnings recovery.

Overall view

Hyosung TNC's recent trend reflects a structure in which improving spandex supply-demand balance and price recovery lifted profit. On a reported basis, operating profit fell to KRW 251.5 billion in 2025 from 2024, but recovered to KRW 188.8 billion in Q2 2026.

In Q3 2026 it fell to KRW 110.2 billion, so quarterly volatility remains high. Strengths include the global number-one share, in-house PTMG production and limited 2027 capacity additions.

Weaknesses include the expected second-half price decline, residual Chinese oversupply and peak-out concerns, and a higher debt ratio. NH presented a KRW 540,000 target price in June 2026 and lowered its 2026 operating profit estimate in the same report.

The next checkpoints are the Q4 2026 preliminary results and the 2026 annual report, along with Chinese utilization and the start-up of 2027 capacity.
