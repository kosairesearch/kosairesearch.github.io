02

Business structure

Hanssak was founded in 1992, converted to a corporation in 2000, and listed on KOSDAQ in 2023 as a developer and supplier of application software. Its core business is network-separation (cross-domain transfer) solutions that connect isolated networks securely, and the company treats this line as its cash cow.

Product revenue rose from 65% of sales in 2023 to 69.1% in 2024 and 94.6% on a cumulative basis through the third quarter of 2025, while low-margin resale of third-party products shrank to 8.3% in 2024.

The lineup now includes cross-domain solutions (CDS), integrated password management, system access control, and SSL visibility products, and its BlueKeen VA SSL visibility solution earned a Grade 1 GS certification.

At a partner event held in late May 2026, the company unveiled six new products, including a new SecureGate 4.0 release, CDS, one-way transfer, integrated access control, password management, and SSL visibility.

Customers are concentrated in the public sector, defense, and finance, and the company cites references from more than 2,300 customers.

The domestic network-separation market grew after mandatory network separation was introduced for public institutions in 2007 and for financial institutions in 2013, and one rival shares the market with Hanssak.

Two group affiliates provide virtual desktop, AI and cloud infrastructure, and security monitoring services alongside Hanssak's consulting and solution supply.

A securities report compiled first-quarter 2026 segment figures showing maintenance revenue of KRW 1.5 billion, up 8% year on year, and product revenue of KRW 1.8 billion, down 36%.

04

Earnings analysis

Annual revenue rose from KRW 21.9 billion in 2022 to KRW 24.1 billion in 2023, a 10.0% increase, then fell 14.8% to KRW 20.5 billion in 2024 before rising 28.0% to KRW 26.3 billion in 2025. Among 2022 to 2025, 2025 was the largest year and 2024 the smallest.

Operating profit was KRW 1.85 billion in 2022 and KRW 2.63 billion in 2023, up 41.9%, before turning to an operating loss of KRW 2.71 billion in 2024. The 2025 operating loss of KRW 1.95 billion continued the losses but was smaller.

Profit attributable to controlling shareholders fell from KRW 3.23 billion in 2023 to KRW 125 million in 2024, a 96.1% decline, and then became a net loss of KRW 2.67 billion in 2025. Net loss including non-controlling interests, a different measure, was KRW 2.76 billion in 2025.

The operating margin moved from 8.5% in 2022 and 10.9% in 2023 to -13.2% in 2024 and -7.4% in 2025. Research spending rose from KRW 1.5 billion in 2020 to KRW 5.2 billion in 2024, and its share of revenue climbed from 9.99% to 25.49%, adding pressure on margins.

In 2024, public-sector revenue also fell sharply as government fiscal policy tightened. Management attributed part of the revenue delay to certification requirements for key products.

Operating cash flow went from KRW 2.66 billion in 2022 and KRW 867 million in 2023 to -KRW 1.17 billion in 2024 and -KRW 332 million in 2025.

On a quarterly basis, the company posted KRW 11.4 billion of revenue and an operating profit of KRW 742 million in the fourth quarter of 2025, then revenue of KRW 3.52 billion and an operating loss of KRW 1.81 billion in the first quarter of 2026.

Second-quarter 2026 revenue was KRW 7.57 billion with an operating profit of KRW 808 million, and third-quarter 2026 revenue was KRW 6.58 billion, up 20.6% year on year, with an operating loss of KRW 503 million. Net loss attributable to controlling shareholders over the last four quarters totaled KRW 1.38 billion.

In an August 2026 press release, the company said it had returned to profitability on both a standalone and consolidated basis in the second quarter.

05

Industry analysis

Demand for network separation was built on mandatory separation rules introduced for public and financial institutions in 2007 and 2013.

The market is now in a transition toward the National Network Security Framework (N2SF), which replaces physical separation with logical separation and redefines traditional network-transfer products as CDS.

The company's chief executive said the final N2SF guideline at the end of 2025 should accelerate adoption of zero trust, SSL visibility, and CDS in public and defense settings.

One securities report noted that some public orders have been delayed during initial data-classification work and that related projects are likely to resume after detailed government guidelines are issued.

The government plans to extend its cybersecurity disclosure system to listed companies more broadly from 2026, and industry commentary expects corporate security spending to rise after recent large breaches. Fiscal policy has also shifted from tightening toward expansion.

Public-sector revenue dropped in 2024 under tighter budgets, and product revenue shrank year on year in the first quarter of 2026. Relative to the cycle, 2026 is the year in which the demand narrative must show up in reported revenue.

The company positions itself as one of the two main players in network separation and is expanding into finance and enterprise markets, but its contribution from those segments is not yet visible in filings.

06

Outlook

The company has said it expects its results to improve in 2026, and its chief executive stated that even under conservative assumptions double-digit growth is achievable. These are management statements rather than figures confirmed in filings.

The second-quarter 2026 return to profit is consistent with the company's announcement, but the third quarter again showed an operating loss. The six new products and the push into finance and enterprise markets are the company's stated expansion directions.

At group level, it plans to offer a full-stack integrated security offering covering consulting, infrastructure build-out, solution supply, and operations management. The chief executive said that certification for core products has been completed, which creates conditions for revenue growth.

In AI, the company developed a demand-forecasting solution based on an Army logistics support model and plans to add log-based anomaly detection to access-control products, although the revenue contribution from these AI offerings is not yet visible in filings.

The next filing to check is the 2026 annual report, which will show whether the operating margin moved from the -7.4% recorded for 2025. The first-quarter 2027 quarterly report will show whether new-product revenue offset the year-on-year decline in product sales.

Whether public-sector orders resume after the N2SF guideline is also a key variable. Management's outlook should be tested against product revenue trends in the periodic reports.

08

Bull factors

Revenue shifting to in-house products

Product revenue rose from 69.1% of sales in 2024 to 94.6% on a cumulative basis through the third quarter of 2025. Resale of third-party products fell to 8.3% in 2024, reducing exposure to lower-margin sales. The chief executive described the shift as a deliberate focus on core business.

The broader lineup around the network-separation core, including CDS, password management, access control, and SSL visibility, supports the change.

Policy-driven demand expansion

The spread of N2SF is redefining network transfer as CDS, which could turn product segmentation into orders. The government plans to extend cybersecurity disclosure rules to listed companies broadly from 2026. Fiscal policy has also moved from tightening toward expansion.

These policy shifts underpin the company's growth case, and whether they show up in revenue must be confirmed in periodic reports.

Whether quarterly profit repeats

Operating profit of KRW 742 million in the fourth quarter of 2025 and KRW 808 million in the second quarter of 2026 marked two profitable quarters. The company announced a return to profitability on both standalone and consolidated bases in the second quarter of 2026.

The third-quarter 2026 operating loss of KRW 503 million was smaller than in earlier loss quarters. Whether profitable quarters repeat is the next test.

Bear factors

Annual losses and weaker cash flow

The 2025 operating loss was KRW 1.95 billion, and the net loss attributable to controlling shareholders was KRW 2.67 billion. Operating cash flow was negative in both 2024 and 2025, at -KRW 1.17 billion and -KRW 332 million. The operating margin of -7.4% in 2025 improved from -13.2% in 2024 but remains negative. Net losses attributable to controlling shareholders over the last four quarters totaled KRW 1.38 billion.

Revenue decline in the first quarter of 2026

First-quarter 2026 revenue fell 69.0% from the prior quarter to KRW 3.52 billion, and the operating loss widened to KRW 1.81 billion. A securities report compiled product revenue for the same quarter down 36% year on year. Quarterly swings are large, so one profitable quarter is hard to read as a trend. The third quarter of 2026 still showed an operating loss.

R&D burden and reliance on public orders

R&D spending grew from KRW 1.5 billion in 2020 to KRW 5.2 billion in 2024, reaching 25.49% of revenue. In 2024, tighter budgets cut public-sector revenue sharply, combining a revenue decline with a swing to loss. Some public orders are also delayed during N2SF data-classification work, which is a near-term variable. Because cutting R&D could weaken product competitiveness, the cost structure is hard to change quickly.

Risk factors

Policy and procurement timing risk

The detailed N2SF guideline and the restart of public procurement have no confirmed dates. If orders slip into 2027 or later, the revenue recovery expected after the second half of 2026 could be delayed. Certification and procurement registration have historically delayed sales of new products.

Financial capacity and earnings volatility

Controlling-shareholder equity was KRW 32.7 billion within total equity of KRW 34.2 billion at the end of 2025. Total liabilities were KRW 15.1 billion, a debt-to-equity ratio of 44.2%. Operating cash flow was negative in both 2024 and 2025, so prolonged losses would reduce financial room. Large quarterly swings also leave the timing of an annual turnaround uncertain.

Share price volatility and trading restrictions

On April 16, 2026, the stock was designated a caution-level issue after rising more than 60% over five trading days, and it was later designated a warning-level issue. At warning and risk levels, trading can be suspended.

Price swings can grow larger than the earnings news would explain, so price-based assessments can be distorted by volatility.

Overall view

Hanssak's 2026 performance has alternated between profit and loss at the quarterly level. The profitable second quarter of 2026 and the fourth quarter of 2025 are linked by the company to its shift toward in-house products.

Still, the third-quarter 2026 operating loss, the sharp first-quarter 2026 revenue decline, and negative operating cash flow in 2024 and 2025 remain. Revenue recovered to KRW 26.3 billion in 2025, but continued operating losses mean an annual turnaround is not yet confirmed in filings.

Supporting factors include the N2SF transition, wider disclosure rules, a higher product share of revenue, and a record of profitable quarters. Offsetting factors include R&D costs, delayed public orders, and large swings in quarterly results.

The share price went through caution and warning designations in 2026, and the company is small within its industry by market value. Management speaks of improvement in 2026, which should be read apart from reported results.

The next checkpoints are revenue recovery and the operating-margin trend in the 2026 annual report and the first-quarter 2027 report.
