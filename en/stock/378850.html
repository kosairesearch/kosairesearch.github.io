02

Business structure

Hwaseung R&A was spun off in February 2021 from Hwaseung Corporation and runs a single business in automotive rubber parts. Its main products are sealing products that block road noise and rainwater and fluid (hose) products used in oil and coolant systems. Its key customers are Hyundai Motor, KIA, GM and Volkswagen.

A May 2024 Hana Securities report described the domestic-to-export mix as roughly 4:6. The report also said synthetic rubber, the main raw material, is sourced through affiliates Hwaseung Networks and Hwaseung Materials. The company says it holds the number one domestic share in both sealing and fluid products.

It is strengthening development of parts for EVs and hydrogen vehicles, and its sealing parts and battery thermal-management hoses are tied to the quieter-cabin demands of EVs. Hwaseung Corporation remained the surviving entity after the split, while the auto parts business moved to the newly formed Hwaseung R&A.

Because the business is tied to automaker production, vehicle output and customer model mix drive sales directly. With exports close to half of sales, currency moves affect both revenue and non-operating gains and losses.

04

Earnings analysis

Annual sales rose from KRW 657.9 billion in 2022 to KRW 714.6 billion in 2025, increasing for three straight years from 2023 through 2025. Growth slowed from 3.5% in 2023 to 3.9% in 2024 and 1.1% in 2025.

Operating profit went from KRW 15.5 billion in 2022 to KRW 31.8 billion in 2023, up 105.0% and the peak year, then declined for two straight years to KRW 31.3 billion in 2024 and KRW 27.1 billion in 2025. The operating margin rose from 2.4% in 2022 to 4.7% in 2023, then eased to 4.4% in 2024 and 3.8% in 2025.

The company attributed the 2025 operating profit decline to higher costs. Attributable net income rose from KRW 12.7 billion in 2022 to KRW 24.2 billion in 2023 and KRW 32.4 billion in 2024, then fell 43.8% to KRW 18.2 billion in 2025.

The company said changes in non-operating items, including FX gains and losses, also weighed on 2025 net income. Net income including non-controlling interests was reported at the same amounts in each year.

Quarterly operating profit fell from KRW 10.3 billion in Q3 2025 to KRW 3.66 billion in Q4 2025, then recovered to KRW 9.06 billion in Q1 2026 and KRW 10.6 billion in Q2 2026.

Q3 2026 sales were KRW 195.9 billion, up 12.2% year on year, but operating profit was KRW 2.08 billion, down 79.7%, and attributable results turned to a net loss of KRW 10.8 billion. Because the bottom-line drop was larger than the operating decline, non-operating items and taxes may have contributed.

Operating cash flow was KRW 31.3 billion in 2022 and KRW 51.9 billion in 2023, turned negative at KRW -7.84 billion in 2024, and returned to KRW 49.9 billion in 2025. The debt ratio declined from 356.0% in 2022 to 187.6% in 2025. Across 2022 to 2025, the largest sales year was 2025 and the smallest was 2022.

Industry analysis

Hwaseung R&A's sales move directly with automaker production. A data provider's company commentary dated September 6, 2026 said higher global sales by the Hyundai Motor Group and rising demand for eco-friendly vehicles lifted automotive parts sales.

The same commentary said demand for sealing parts and battery thermal-management hoses rose on EV quietness requirements. A 2025 analysis, by contrast, cited slower EV market growth, intensifying price competition and reduced subsidies as pressure on automaker profitability.

It also pointed to Chinese EV penetration globally and expansion in Southeast Asia as possible long-term growth drivers. A May 2024 Hana Securities report noted that production concentrated in Korea limits growth and flagged a debt ratio in the mid-200% range as a discount factor.

On competition, the company emphasizes technology and price competitiveness and claims the number one domestic share in sealing and fluid products. Comparable overseas market-share data was not available, so the competitive picture rests on qualitative assessment.

Thermal management is a field where several listed auto parts companies compete, including thermal specialists such as Hanon Systems. Because customers are concentrated among domestic and global automakers, their production plans and model transitions largely determine orders and sales.

Risk factors

Customer concentration

Customers are concentrated among automakers such as Hyundai Motor and KIA, so their production plans and model transitions directly determine sales. A cut in output for specific models or a delay in EV transition timing could weigh on sealing and hose demand. Pass-through of automaker price-cut pressure to parts pricing is another risk.

FX and non-operating items

With exports close to half of sales, currency moves affect both revenue and non-operating gains and losses. The company said non-operating swings, including FX-related items, contributed to the 2025 net income decline.

Non-operating effects may also have played a part in the Q3 2026 net loss, which keeps quarterly net income volatile.

Reliance on affiliated raw material supply

A May 2024 Hana Securities report said synthetic rubber is sourced through affiliates Hwaseung Networks and Hwaseung Materials. A change in specialty rubber prices or affiliate supply terms could raise cost burdens. The debt ratio has come down from the mid-200% range, but borrowing-related pressure remains.
