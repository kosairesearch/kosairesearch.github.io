02

Business structure

Dhautoware is an automotive electronics supplier founded in 1979 that has expanded from in-vehicle infotainment (AVN) into a broader range of products. After renaming itself in 2023, it completed Gwangju and Mexico plants in 2024 to build a global production network.

Per a securities-information report on company data, display and D-Audio products account for more than 65% of sales.

The company says its product mix is shifting from AVN toward future-car core parts such as integrated vehicle controllers (BDC, CCU, DCU), integrated EV charging controllers (ICCU) and connected controllers.

Its customer base centers on Hyundai Motor Group, under a seven-year long-term contract signed in June 2023 for core autonomous and connected-car parts, running through December 2032.

For Kia, it is supplying and relocating equipment for overseas CKD plants in Pakistan and Malaysia, with related sales of about KRW 700 million across Pakistan, Malaysia and Slovakia reported in April 2026.

Its automation division supplies turnkey production-line equipment, and a Stellantis automation project at a Morocco plant was expected to add about KRW 23 billion in sales.

On the production side, the Gwangju plant was reorganized into a domestic hub with SMT equipment and automated assembly lines, and the Pyeongtaek plant was moved there to consolidate output. The Mexico unit began mass production after building its vehicle-controller line.

Competition is decided platform by platform in OEM sourcing, against large electronics-component makers in the automotive electronics market.

04

Earnings analysis

On the filed annual numbers, sales were KRW 381.8 billion in 2022, KRW 393.1 billion in 2023 (up 3.0% year on year), KRW 371.9 billion in 2024 (down 5.4%) and KRW 494.4 billion in 2025 (up 32.9%). Within 2022 to 2025, 2025 was the largest year and 2024 the smallest. Profit did not move in step with sales.

Operating profit was KRW 2.32 billion in 2022, KRW 2.78 billion in 2023 (up 19.6%) and KRW 1.9 billion in 2024 (down 31.7%), before turning into an operating loss of KRW 4.92 billion in 2025, a swing from profit to loss. Operating margins were 0.6% in 2022, 0.7% in 2023, 0.5% in 2024 and -1.0% in 2025.

The company cited Mexico plant capacity expansion and higher sales as the main drivers of 2025 revenue growth.

It also said that running two plants at once during the Pyeongtaek-to-Gwangju relocation raised fixed costs and temporarily lowered production efficiency, and that an accounting impairment test on cash-generating units was recognized as assets grew.

Profit attributable to controlling shareholders was a net loss of KRW 4.31 billion in 2022, a net profit of KRW 691 million in 2023 (a swing to profit), a net loss of KRW 2.4 billion in 2024 (a swing to loss) and a net loss of KRW 23.5 billion in 2025 (losses continued and widened).

The company has posted losses in two consecutive years, 2024 and 2025. By quarter, operating profit turned positive at KRW 2.03 billion in the fourth quarter of 2025, but the controlling-shareholder net loss in that quarter widened to KRW 24.6 billion.

The first quarter of 2026 recorded sales of KRW 135.4 billion, operating profit of KRW 3.16 billion and controlling-shareholder net profit of KRW 5.24 billion.

The second quarter showed an operating loss of KRW 809 million but a controlling-shareholder net profit of KRW 2.47 billion, which is a gap that points to non-operating items.

The third quarter showed sales of KRW 128.8 billion (down 4.3% year on year), an operating loss of KRW 471 million and a controlling-shareholder net loss of KRW 16.6 billion.

Over the last four quarters, from the fourth quarter of 2025 to the third quarter of 2026, controlling-shareholder net losses totaled KRW 33.5 billion. Operating cash flow was positive in all four years, at KRW 17.1 billion in 2022, KRW 7.32 billion in 2023, KRW 10.1 billion in 2024 and KRW 10.2 billion in 2025.

The debt-to-equity ratio, however, rose from 133.5% in 2022 to 154.9% in 2023, 372.7% in 2024 and 561.9% in 2025.

05

Industry analysis

Demand for automotive electronics rises with the spread of electrification, connectivity and autonomous functions, but it is sensitive to OEM production volumes and policy.

The company said that large orders at its Mexico plant were meaningful at a time when U.S. tariff policy raised concern about a downturn in the auto parts market. Its stated reason for building the Mexico plant was to respond to North American protectionism and tariff risk.

Its position rests on being designated a localized supplier for Hyundai Motor Group in June 2023. Dependence on one vehicle group, however, remains a concentration risk. A June 2026 report said the company disclosed at a KRX investor session orders for about 44.47 million future-car parts through 2034.

Among peers such as Samsung Electro-Mechanics and LG Innotek, the key variable is the ability to win orders platform by platform.

Autonomous driving and AI-based vehicle technology work through collaboration with a group affiliate, which has applied an AI vision system to body-shop automation equipment supplied to domestic customers.

In production operations, the company was selected under a Ministry of SMEs and Startups program to develop an AI-based production management platform, with a total budget of about KRW 3.7 billion over two years. Extending the model from the Gwangju plant to the Mexico hub is under review.

Where the industry stands in its cycle is too early to call, and OEM output and North American tariff policy are the largest variables.

06

Outlook

The company said it would use 2026 to show results from production-infrastructure investment and portfolio restructuring carried out from 2023 to 2025.

It has not published many numerical targets; the figures it has offered are an expected annual fixed-cost saving of about KRW 5 billion from consolidating production in Gwangju and the cumulative order volume.

The Mexico unit has reached stable mass production, and additional capacity is being secured to meet new North American project demand. Beyond the long-term Hyundai contract, the company says it will secure additional controller-part sales from 2027 for five years, as reported in 2025.

Recognition of about KRW 23 billion in sales from the Stellantis Morocco project is also an expected item, per an April 2026 report.

On the share side, a five-for-one reverse stock split is being pursued, which would change the number of issued shares to 9,756,244 from 48,781,224; the company says paid-in capital would not materially change.

The company has said it will expand its AI-based vehicle electronics and next-generation autonomous driving businesses to strengthen future growth drivers. Whether this translates into earnings depends on whether operating margins stay in positive territory.

08

Bull factors

Signs of margin recovery

Operating profit of KRW 3.16 billion in the first quarter of 2026 marked a return to profit, which the company attributed to a richer mix of high-value automotive electronics and cost reduction.

Operating profit had already turned positive at KRW 2.03 billion in the fourth quarter of 2025, so the operating turn has appeared across two quarters. Whether the operating margin can move from -1.0% in 2025 into positive territory is the test for this factor.

Large order backlog

A June 2026 report said the company disclosed orders for about 44.47 million future-car parts through 2034. On top of a seven-year Hyundai Motor Group contract from 2023 running through December 2032, additional controller-part sales are slated to begin in 2027 over five years. This widens the visibility of future sales.

Fixed-cost restructuring

With the Pyeongtaek relocation finished, production is consolidated in Gwangju, and annual fixed-cost savings of about KRW 5 billion are expected to become visible. The company described the dual-plant costs and lower production efficiency that weighed on 2025 results as temporary factors. The Mexico unit has reached stable mass production, leaving room for improvement in the production structure.

09

Bear factors

Two consecutive quarterly operating losses

Operating losses of KRW 809 million in the second quarter and KRW 471 million in the third quarter mean two consecutive quarters in the red. Third-quarter sales fell 4.3% year on year. Whether the first-quarter profit carries into following quarters can only be checked in upcoming results.

Higher debt burden

The debt-to-equity ratio climbed from 133.5% in 2022 to 561.9% in 2025. Total liabilities at the end of 2025 of KRW 297.2 billion are more than five times total equity of KRW 52.9 billion. Borrowing grew alongside asset investment, which leaves less room to absorb changes in interest rates and repayment conditions.

Gap between operating and net results

Third-quarter 2026 operating loss was KRW 471 million, while the controlling-shareholder net loss reached KRW 16.6 billion. In the second quarter the pattern ran the other way, with a controlling-shareholder profit despite an operating loss. The composition of these gaps is something to check in the next periodic report.

10

Risk factors

Balance sheet

A debt-to-equity ratio of 561.9% and total equity of KRW 52.9 billion leave limited room to absorb changes in interest rates and borrowing conditions. Operating cash flow was positive at KRW 10.1 billion in 2024 and KRW 10.2 billion in 2025, but repayment burden should be assessed separately. The maturity profile of the debt is something to check in the next periodic report.

North American tariffs and customer concentration

Expanding production in Mexico exposes the company to changes in North American tariff policy, and the company itself has pointed to concern about a downturn in auto parts demand from U.S. tariff policy.

Concentration on a few OEM customers, including Hyundai Motor Group, means production adjustments by key customers could flow straight through to results.

Ramp-up execution and impairment

Fixed costs and lower production efficiency during the Gwangju relocation and the Mexico ramp-up weighed on 2025 results. Because an impairment test on cash-generating units was recognized as assets grew, further impairments cannot be ruled out. The pace and yield of new lines will largely determine how fast results recover.

Overall view

On a filed annual basis, Dhautoware's sales grew to KRW 494.4 billion over 2022 to 2025, but its operating margin stayed around 1%, and 2025 swung to an operating loss of KRW 4.92 billion.

Operating profit of KRW 3.16 billion in the first quarter of 2026 marked a return to profit, but operating losses followed in the second and third quarters, and controlling-shareholder net losses totaled KRW 33.5 billion over the last four quarters.

Strengths include the long-term Hyundai Motor Group contract, the disclosed order volume of about 44.47 million future-car parts, the completed Pyeongtaek relocation with expected fixed-cost savings, and stabilizing Mexico production.

Weaknesses include operating losses in the second and third quarters, the size of the third-quarter controlling-shareholder net loss, a debt-to-equity ratio that rose to 561.9%, and exposure to North American tariffs and concentration on OEM customers.

On valuation, PER cannot be calculated, the price-to-book ratio is below 1x, and market capitalization sits below total equity. Whether that level provides a cushion on net assets has to be read together with the debt structure.

The next points to confirm are the operating margin in the 2026 annual report, operating results for the first quarter of 2027, the effective date of the reverse stock split, and how new supply volumes starting in 2027 are reflected in sales.
