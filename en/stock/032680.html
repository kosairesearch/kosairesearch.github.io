Narrowing Losses, but Financing and Listing Tests Loom

02

Business structure

Softcen, founded in 1988 and listed on KOSDAQ in 1997, is an IT infrastructure and solutions company that runs four business lines. The IT Infrastructure division covers server and storage distribution, new material systems and inspection equipment.

The IT Solutions division runs mobile services aimed at Chinese consumers, and the company said it provided more than 5 billion value-added services a year in 2025 as demand for advertising messages and message authentication grew.

Since June 2025 the company has pursued the supply of core equipment for ultra-thin solar glass and BIPV (building-integrated photovoltaic) production lines, and it also supplies flexible OLED and ultra-thin foldable cover glass process systems and secondary battery X-ray inspection equipment.

Its consolidated subsidiaries include Softcen Shenzhen, which focuses on smart education solutions, and Softcen Kangsu, which covers IT infrastructure and display new materials. Server and storage deployment and maintenance are the core of demand in the IT infrastructure business.

In competition, the company overlaps in infrastructure with large IT service providers such as Samsung SDS, LG CNS and Hyundai Autoever, but its mix of equipment deliveries sets it apart from them.

Its results depend on a mix of IT infrastructure orders, traffic in Chinese mobile services and delivery timing for display and battery equipment.

04

Earnings analysis

Annual revenue fell for three straight years, from KRW 61.1 billion in 2023 to KRW 59.9 billion in 2024 and KRW 54.8 billion in 2025, and the 8.5% decline in 2025 was larger than the 1.9% decline in 2024. Among 2022 to 2025, 2022 was the largest year by revenue.

The operating margin moved from 22.2% in 2022 to -2.3% in 2023 and reached -0.4% in 2025. Operating losses were KRW 1.42 billion in 2023, KRW 1.38 billion in 2024 and KRW 244 million in 2025, and the 2025 loss was the smallest of the three years.

The company attributed the 2025 narrowing to lower selling and administrative expenses after a deep restructuring. It also said that the termination of an overseas export contract reduced contract assets and inventory, and that gains on derivative valuation declined.

Net losses attributable to controlling shareholders were KRW 11.5 billion in 2023, KRW 3.47 billion in 2024 and KRW 2.15 billion in 2025, a deficit each year that narrowed. Net loss including non-controlling interests was KRW 987 million in 2025, a different figure from the controlling-shareholder loss.

Operating cash flow stayed positive at KRW 7.15 billion in 2023, KRW 1.27 billion in 2024 and KRW 2.8 billion in 2025, and the debt ratio fell from 56.8% in 2023 to 40.6% in 2025. At the end of 2025, total equity was KRW 66.4 billion, of which KRW 50.9 billion belonged to controlling shareholders.

On a quarterly basis, revenue bottomed at KRW 10.5 billion in Q1 2026 and then rose to KRW 13.2 billion in Q2 and KRW 17.2 billion in Q3, but Q3 revenue was still down 6.1% from a year earlier.

Operating profit turned positive at KRW 162 million in Q1 2026, reached KRW 467 million in Q2, and fell to KRW 263 million in Q3, which was 43.6% lower than the prior quarter. Net loss attributable to controlling shareholders widened to KRW 2.3 billion in Q3 2026, extending the losses to three straight quarters.

In Q4 2025, the company recorded an operating loss of KRW 576 million but a net profit attributable to controlling shareholders of KRW 1.2 billion, so non-operating items had a large effect. The combined net loss attributable to controlling shareholders over the last four quarters was KRW 1.65 billion.

05

Industry analysis

Demand for servers and storage is driven by expanding investment in cloud computing and artificial intelligence, and Softcen's IT Infrastructure division is positioning itself for this trend.

The growth of Chinese mobile internet networks has increased demand for advertising messages and message authentication, which is a supportive backdrop for the IT Solutions division.

Foldable smartphones are expected to reach mass adoption with the launch of a foldable iPhone by Apple in 2026, which links to demand for ultra-thin cover glass process equipment. Secondary battery X-ray inspection equipment depends on production line investment and quality inspection demand from battery customers.

In solar, the BIPV production line equipment business is tied to the expansion of building-integrated solar installations, but because segment revenue is not disclosed in the regular reports, it is hard to separate how much the new materials equipment business contributes to results.

On competition, Samsung SDS, LG CNS and Hyundai Autoever are much larger, with big system integration and cloud businesses.

Unlike them, Softcen mixes equipment deliveries with Chinese mobile services, so although it sits in the IT services and software category, its earnings swings look closer to hardware delivery timing.

After revenue declines from 2023, signs of recovery appeared from Q2 2026, but the filings alone do not show whether the recovery came from IT infrastructure orders or equipment orders.

Outlook

Results in 2026 show a recovery in revenue from Q2 alongside a slowdown in profit in Q3. Q3 2026 revenue of KRW 17.2 billion rose 30.2% from the prior quarter but was 6.1% lower than a year earlier, so the recovery has not yet returned revenue to the prior-year level.

Because the company said the termination of an overseas export contract reduced contract assets and inventory in 2025, whether equipment order backlog rebuilds is a key variable for revenue.

The ultra-thin solar glass and BIPV equipment business has been in progress since June 2025, and the company said it set up a joint venture and took an equity stake to complete vertical integration in foldable components.

On the capital side, a capital increase decision was announced in June 2026, the results of a third-party allotment capital increase followed in August 2026, and an extraordinary shareholders meeting approved amendments to the articles of incorporation on October 30, 2026.

Preferred Share No. 1 received a notice on October 7, 2026 about possible delisting because its market capitalization fell short, and this decision is a key variable for 2026. The next periodic report, the 2026 annual report, is due by late March 2027, when Q4 and full-year 2026 results will be finalized.

Whether the operating margin in that report improves from -0.4% in 2025 is the next checkpoint. The revenue trend in China and the results of overseas subsidiaries are also variables to watch.

Bull factors

Continued Narrowing of Losses

Operating losses narrowed from KRW 1.42 billion in 2023 to KRW 1.38 billion in 2024 and KRW 244 million in 2025, and the company attributed the 2025 improvement to lower selling and administrative expenses. The operating margin also moved from -2.3% in 2023 to -0.4% in 2025. The narrowing trend in losses is cited as a supportive factor.

Revenue Recovery From Q2 2026

Revenue bottomed at KRW 10.5 billion in Q1 2026 and then rose to KRW 13.2 billion in Q2 and KRW 17.2 billion in Q3, a two-quarter run of gains. Operating profit was KRW 467 million in Q2 2026, and it also turned positive at KRW 162 million in Q1 2026. These figures can be read as early signs of recovery in the core business.

New Materials Equipment and Vertical Integration Options

The company has pursued the supply of core equipment for ultra-thin solar glass and BIPV production lines since June 2025. The foldable smartphone market is expected to reach mass adoption with Apple's foldable iPhone in 2026, and the company said a joint venture and equity stake completed its vertical integration.

If these businesses turn into revenue, they could broaden a structure that is currently centered on IT infrastructure.

Bear factors

Losses Widen Again in Q3

Operating profit in Q3 2026 was KRW 263 million, down 43.6% from the prior quarter and 30.4% from a year earlier. The net loss attributable to controlling shareholders widened to KRW 2.3 billion, extending losses for three straight quarters from Q1 2026.

The combined net loss attributable to controlling shareholders over the last four quarters was KRW 1.65 billion.

Revenue Decline and Contract Termination

Revenue fell for three consecutive years from 2023 to 2025 and was still down 6.1% from a year earlier in Q3 2026. The company's explanation that an overseas export contract termination reduced contract assets and inventory in 2025 points to high volatility in order-driven revenue. The recovery has lasted two quarters but has not yet returned revenue to the prior-year level.

Listing Requirements and Dilution Burden

Preferred Share No. 1 received repeated notices about possible administrative issue designation and delisting due to shortfalls in listed share count and market capitalization. In July 2026, Softcen Preferred was designated as a stock under investment caution after a sharp price rise.

Capital-raising disclosures in June and August 2026 and repeated disclosures on convertible bond acquisitions have raised concerns about a rise in share count.

Business Mix and Export Dependence

Results from the Chinese mobile service business and Chinese subsidiaries may also be affected by exchange rates and local regulation, which is a risk to monitor.

Overall view

Softcen narrowed both its operating loss and its net loss attributable to controlling shareholders in 2025, and revenue has risen for two straight quarters since the Q1 2026 low.

However, operating profit fell from the prior quarter in Q3 2026 and the net loss attributable to controlling shareholders widened to KRW 2.3 billion, extending losses for three straight quarters.

Revenue fell for three consecutive years from 2023 and was still lower than a year earlier in Q3, so top-line recovery has not yet reached the prior-year level.

The business mixes IT infrastructure, Chinese mobile services, and equipment for ultra-thin solar glass and foldable components, so growth drivers are varied, but the revenue contribution of each business is not shown separately in the disclosed results.

In 2026 the company carried out a share consolidation, a treasury share cancellation, capital increases and convertible bond disclosures in quick succession, and Preferred Share No. 1 faces a listing requirement shortfall.

Strengths include the narrowing of losses and the revenue recovery since Q2, while weaknesses include the Q3 earnings setback, the decline in revenue, and dilution and listing issues tied to financing.

The next checkpoints are the exchange's decision on the listing requirements for Preferred Share No. 1 and the operating margin in the 2026 annual report.
