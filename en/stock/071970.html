02

Business structure

HD-Hyundai Marine Engine specializes in low-speed marine engines for ships. The company was previously known as STX Engine, and its name changed after the HD Hyundai group acquired a controlling stake.

Since joining the group in 2024, it has been able to use the global sales network and customer base of group shipbuilding affiliates. Within the group, HD Hyundai Heavy Industries handles large marine propulsion engines while HD-Hyundai Marine Engine covers small and mid-sized ones.

Its product line includes diesel engines alongside LNG and LPG dual-fuel engines, and it has recently expanded into 80-bore tanker engines. Revenue is split between engines and parts; in the first quarter of 2026, engine revenue was KRW 109.2 billion and parts revenue was KRW 24.3 billion.

The engine segment margin improved 1.5 percentage points from the prior quarter to 24.2% in that quarter, while the parts margin fell 8.0 percentage points quarter on quarter but stayed in the 20% range. Customers include Chinese shipyards and trading firms as well as Korean shipbuilders.

In 2024, Xiamen Xiangyu group transactions totaled KRW 90.7 billion, or 28.74% of revenue, and Daehan Shipbuilding accounted for 10.6%. Of 19 new contracts from January to September 2026, 14 were China-bound, and the production utilization rate was 64.6% in 2024.

04

Earnings analysis

Annual revenue rose from KRW 179.3 billion in 2022 to KRW 245 billion in 2023 (up 36.6%), KRW 315.8 billion in 2024 (up 28.9%), and KRW 402.4 billion in 2025 (up 27.4%), and grew for three consecutive years from 2023 through 2025.

Operating profit increased from KRW 11.3 billion in 2022 to KRW 75.9 billion in 2025, with 2025 up 128.7% and the largest year in the 2022–2025 period. The operating margin moved from 6.3% to 7.3%, 10.5% and 18.9%, so margin gains outpaced revenue growth.

Net profit attributable to owners rose from KRW 14.1 billion in 2022 to KRW 165.1 billion in 2025, and total net profit including non-controlling interests was reported at the same amount in each year from 2022 to 2025.

Operating cash flow moved from -KRW 20.4 billion in 2022 to KRW 180.6 billion in 2025, turning from negative to strongly positive alongside profit growth.

Total equity at the end of 2025 was KRW 488.9 billion and total liabilities were KRW 325.8 billion, with the debt-to-equity ratio falling from 116.7% in 2022 to 66.6% in 2025.

Quarterly, operating profit rose from KRW 20.3 billion in the third quarter of 2025 to KRW 27.9 billion in the fourth quarter, while fourth-quarter net profit attributable to owners of KRW 112.1 billion far exceeded operating profit.

In the first quarter of 2026, revenue reached KRW 133.5 billion and operating profit KRW 32.6 billion, but net profit attributable to owners fell 74.9% quarter on quarter to KRW 28.1 billion, and a securities analysis cited the base effect of a one-time bad-debt provision reversal in the prior quarter and a temporary drop in turbocharger sales as margin headwinds.

In the second quarter of 2026, revenue of KRW 128.1 billion and operating profit of KRW 31.3 billion edged lower, yet net profit attributable to owners rose to KRW 31.9 billion, and it then increased again to KRW 46.5 billion in the third quarter, marking two consecutive quarters of growth.

The operating margin in the third quarter of 2026 was 28.5%, up 4.1 percentage points from the prior quarter, and Sisajournal reported that quarterly revenue and operating profit were record highs for a quarter.

Net profit attributable to owners over the last four quarters (fourth quarter of 2025 through third quarter of 2026) totaled KRW 218.6 billion.

05

Industry analysis

Demand from the shipping market comes from three directions. First, a shipbuilding upswing, with Middle East conflict lifting demand for tankers and more customers inquiring about marine engines.

Second, replacement demand for eco-friendly engines, as the International Maritime Organization pushes ahead with a shipping carbon tax and shipowners hurry to swap aging engines for greener ones; the company's LNG and LPG dual-fuel engines fit this replacement cycle.

Third, AI data center power generation, where the International Energy Agency projects global data center electricity consumption rising from 415 TWh in 2024 to 945 TWh in 2030.

The company connects to this demand mainly by supplying turbochargers for HD Hyundai Heavy Industries' power generation engines rather than the engines themselves. The cycle is at a high point, but some observers warn that the shipbuilding boom has begun to slow.

Within the group, HD Hyundai Heavy Industries covers large engines while this company covers small and mid-sized ones. Chinese shipbuilders are gaining influence in the global newbuilding market, yet reports say they still rely on Korean marine engines because of technical limits.

Since China-bound shipyard volumes now form the core of its orders, the direction of Chinese newbuilding orders is a key variable for the company's order flow.

06

Outlook

On orders, first-half 2026 new contracts totaled 15 deals worth KRW 672.7 billion, 12 of them from Chinese shipyards.

Cumulative new orders through September 2026 were reported at KRW 870 billion, 18.4% above the full-year 2025 total of KRW 734.7 billion, while another outlet put the third-quarter cumulative at KRW 957.7 billion, so the figures diverge.

On capacity, the company is investing KRW 65.3 billion to optimize production facilities. On technology, a new license agreement with WinGD in October 2026 for two-stroke propulsion engine technology adds to its existing license, giving a base to produce both two-stroke engine lines.

In November 2026, it signed a KRW 36.1 billion engine supply contract with Samsung Heavy Industries running through June 20, 2029, the 22nd marine engine order it has disclosed this year.

At the group level, expanding land-based power generation engines has led to projections that the group's engine business could approach KRW 10 trillion in annual revenue by 2030. The next checkpoints are whether full-year 2026 new orders exceed the 2025 full-year total and whether the China-bound share declines.

Quarterly revenue depends on engine utilization and delivery volumes, so how quickly orders convert into revenue is the central variable.

08

Bull factors

Earnings quality improvement shows in the numbers

The operating margin rose from 6.3% in 2022 to 18.9% in 2025, and reached 28.5% in the third quarter of 2026, up 4.1 percentage points from the prior quarter. Third-quarter 2026 operating profit grew 197.2% year on year, showing operating leverage. Engine segment margins holding in the low-to-mid 20% range support the case for structural improvement.

Order growth and wider customer mix

First-half 2026 new contracts totaled 15 deals worth KRW 672.7 billion, which one analysis said exceeds the company's full-year 2025 revenue. A KRW 36.1 billion supply contract with Samsung Heavy Industries was signed in November 2026. Group affiliate orders have also been secured steadily since 2024, widening the customer base beyond China.

Link to the group's power generation engine business

Rising AI data center demand for power generation engines links the company to group growth through turbochargers supplied for HD Hyundai Heavy Industries' power generation engines. The group's engine business is growing on two fronts, marine propulsion and land-based power.

The October 2026 license agreement with WinGD also widened the base for two-stroke propulsion engine production.

09

Bear factors

Large swings in quarterly profit

Net profit attributable to owners fell 74.9% quarter on quarter to KRW 28.1 billion in the first quarter of 2026. In the fourth quarter of 2025, net profit attributable to owners of KRW 112.1 billion far exceeded operating profit of KRW 27.9 billion, a wide gap between the two. Because quarterly results swing sharply, a single quarter is not enough to read the trend.

Concentration on Chinese customers

Of 19 new contracts from January to September 2026, 14 were China-bound. In 2024, Xiamen Xiangyu group transactions were the largest revenue source at 28.74% of the total. A decline in Chinese newbuilding orders, or stronger in-house engine capability at Chinese yards, could shift the order flow.

Concern over a slowing shipbuilding upswing and margin factors

Some observers warn that the shipbuilding upswing has begun to slow. The first-quarter 2026 margin decline was attributed to the base effect of a one-time bad-debt reversal and lower turbocharger sales.

Because profit gains came partly from favorable external variables such as the exchange rate, a reversal in those variables could weigh on margins.

10

Risk factors

Customer and regional concentration

A high share of China-bound orders means results may react sharply to Chinese newbuilding orders and the circumstances of local shipyards. With a single Chinese group accounting for 28.74% of 2024 revenue, a change in one customer's orders could show up clearly in quarterly sales. Diversifying toward domestic and group-affiliate volume is the company's ongoing task.

Shipbuilding cycle and pace of eco-friendly replacement

Marine engine orders depend on the shipbuilding cycle, and replacement demand for eco-friendly engines depends on the timing and strength of rules such as a shipping carbon tax. If regulation slips or newbuilding orders slow, the pace of new contracts could fall. Conversely, earlier replacement demand could lift orders, so both variables need to be read together.

Capacity investment execution and exchange rates

The KRW 65.3 billion production optimization must proceed on schedule for the company to meet the higher order volume. Added capacity raises fixed costs, so a drop in utilization could weaken profitability. Whether margin gains from the favorable exchange-rate period persist also remains an open uncertainty.

Overall view

HD-Hyundai Marine Engine grew revenue, operating profit and net profit attributable to owners every year from 2022, and its operating margin improved from 6.3% to 18.9%.

In the third quarter of 2026, revenue of KRW 211.4 billion and operating profit of KRW 60.3 billion marked the largest quarter in the disclosed period.

Orders have grown with a heavy China-bound tilt, and cumulative orders through September 2026 point toward exceeding the prior full-year total, with group power generation demand and eco-friendly replacement demand as backdrop.

On the other side, quarterly profit swings are large, and the high China-bound share leaves the company sensitive to order changes from a few customers and one region. Concerns about a slowing shipbuilding upswing and external variables such as the exchange rate also remain.

On valuation, the multiples moved into a higher range after the 2025 profit surge, and the company has paid no dividends. The next checkpoints are the full-year 2026 order tally, the preliminary fourth-quarter 2026 results, and the full-year operating margin in the 2026 annual report.
