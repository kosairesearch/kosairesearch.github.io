Report overview

Record Backlog, but Earnings Volatility Remains the Test

Taihan Cable & Solution's backlog reached a record KRW 3.83 trillion at the end of the first quarter of 2026, but net losses attributable to controlling shareholders in the first and second quarters and a sharp drop in third-quarter operating profit leave earnings stability as the main open question.

02

Business structure

Taihan Cable & Solution manufactures power cables and extra-high-voltage and submarine cables, and its center of gravity is shifting from general-purpose wire to EHV and submarine cable.

Yuanta Securities put the EHV and submarine cable share of 2025 sales at 19.1% and projected it would rise to 21.5% in 2026 and 26.2% in 2027, though these are forecasts. The higher share of high-margin products is cited as the basis for margin improvement.

The materials division passes copper price moves directly into revenue, and KB Securities estimated that higher copper prices lifted that division's sales in the second quarter of 2026. Overseas orders account for much of the backlog.

KB Securities reported in April 2026 a backlog split of 16% Korea, 31% Asia and 24% North America. Recent awards include a 400kV and 230kV EHV cable system for the Singapore power authority, a 132kV transmission line in Scotland and a 154kV turnkey grid project for a solar park in Haenam.

Yuanta Securities said the Singapore projects totaled about KRW 1 trillion over the last two years. Domestically, the company won a KRW 146.3 billion turnkey contract for KEPCO's 500kV HVDC East Coast to Seoul East project (EP2 phase). Vertical integration is also under way.

The Dangjin No. 1 submarine cable plant is operating and the No. 2 plant is under construction. The Scandi Connector vessel was agreed in May 2026, delivered in July and renamed Taihan Connector on November 2, 2026. In July 2025 the company acquired Taihan Ocean Works, a subsea construction firm.

On the competitive side, LS Cable & System's affiliate in the LS Group completed a fifth submarine cable plant in July 2025 that expanded its HVDC cable capacity more than fourfold.

04

Earnings analysis

Annual results show sales growth came first. Sales rose from KRW 2.45 trillion in 2022 to KRW 2.84 trillion in 2023 (up 16.1% year on year), KRW 3.29 trillion in 2024 (up 15.7%) and KRW 3.64 trillion in 2025 (up 10.5%), increasing for three consecutive years from 2023 to 2025.

Operating profit rose from KRW 48.2 billion in 2022 to KRW 79.8 billion in 2023 (up 65.7%), KRW 115.2 billion in 2024 (up 44.3%) and KRW 128.6 billion in 2025 (up 11.7%), but the growth rate slowed each year.

The operating margin went from 2.0% in 2022 to 2.8% in 2023, 3.5% in 2024 and 3.5% in 2025, so it has been flat since 2024. Sales growth outpaced margin expansion.

Net profit attributable to controlling shareholders rose from KRW 20.6 billion in 2022 to KRW 70.6 billion in 2023 (up 243.1%), edged down to KRW 70.4 billion in 2024 (down 0.2%) and recovered to KRW 84.2 billion in 2025 (up 19.6%).

In 2024, operating profit grew 44.3% while this figure fell slightly, so non-operating items, taxes and the allocation to non-controlling interests need to be read together. Net profit including non-controlling interests was KRW 89.9 billion in 2025, above the KRW 84.2 billion attributable to controlling shareholders.

Operating cash flow was -KRW 46.6 billion in 2022, KRW 30.7 billion in 2023, KRW 6.75 billion in 2024 and -KRW 171.3 billion in 2025, a sharp deterioration in 2025.

The debt-to-equity ratio rose to 114.0% in 2025 from 76.6% in 2024, and at year-end 2025 total liabilities of KRW 1.86 trillion exceeded total equity of KRW 1.63 trillion. Quarterly results were more volatile.

Sales went from KRW 855 billion in the third quarter of 2025 to KRW 1.01 trillion in the fourth quarter (up 18.0% quarter on quarter), KRW 1.08 trillion in the first quarter of 2026 (up 7.3%), KRW 1.2 trillion in the second quarter (up 10.6%) and KRW 1.18 trillion in the third quarter (down 1.9% quarter on quarter, up 37.6% year on year).

Operating profit rose from KRW 43.4 billion in the fourth quarter of 2025 (up 47.0% quarter on quarter) to KRW 60.4 billion in the first quarter of 2026 (up 39.2%) and KRW 60.8 billion in the second quarter (up 0.7%), then fell to KRW 30.9 billion in the third quarter (down 49.3% quarter on quarter, up 4.5% year on year).

Asia Today reported the first-quarter 2026 result was the highest quarterly figure since 2010. Over the same period, net results attributable to controlling shareholders swung from a KRW 7.38 billion loss in the first quarter to an KRW 88.4 billion loss in the second quarter, which diverged from operating profit.

The gap between KRW 60.8 billion of second-quarter operating profit and the KRW 88.4 billion loss sits below the operating line, in non-operating items or taxes, and the quarterly income statement is where that should be traced.

The cumulative net loss attributable to controlling shareholders over the last four quarters is KRW 21.9 billion.

05

Industry analysis

The demand drivers for cable makers are EHV transmission and offshore wind. The company explains that offshore wind needs long-distance transmission between generation sites and the shore, which raises demand for HVDC submarine cable.

Taihan Cable & Solution has said demand for HVDC submarine cable is growing fast as offshore wind requires long-distance transmission. In the cycle, orders are rising alongside raw material prices. KB Securities noted copper prices were up 39% year on year in the second quarter of 2026.

Higher copper prices lift revenue but add cost and working-capital pressure at the same time. In competition, the LS Group is the closest benchmark. Taihan Cable & Solution said it would focus on European markets to meet global competition with LS Cable & System and others.

Domestic competition is also intense, as LS Marine Solution signed a KRW 156.9 billion subsea cable installation contract with Hanwha Ocean for the Sinan Ulsan offshore wind project.

Policy finance supports the buildout, with the Export-Import Bank of Korea providing a total of KRW 550 billion in support for the new submarine cable plant and vessel acquisition.

Yuanta Securities expects sales and profit growth to continue through 2027 on the back of a higher EHV and submarine cable mix and capacity expansion.

Taihan Cable & Solution ranks near the top in backlog size and facility expansion, but relative to the LS Group, which has both cable production and installation capacity, its installation capability is still being built out.

06

Outlook

The starting point for orders is the KRW 3.83 trillion backlog at the end of the first quarter of 2026. Taihan Cable & Solution plans to complete the Dangjin No. 2 submarine cable plant, which broke ground in September 2025, in 2027.

The No. 2 plant will house production lines for HVDC cable up to 640kV and HVAC cable at 400kV, along with a 187-meter vertical continuous extrusion tower. The company expects submarine cable production capacity to grow to about five times the current level once the No. 2 plant is operating.

The timing of large projects also matters. KEPCO plans to complete the first phase of the West Coast Energy Highway, the Saemangeum to Seohwaseong HVDC project, by 2030, finish basic design this year and tender the submarine cable work in early 2027.

The company plans to pursue domestic and overseas grid projects, including the West Coast Energy Highway, on the strength of the No. 2 plant and its vessels. Installation capacity now rests on two vessels. PALOS carries 4,400 tonnes of cable and Taihan Connector carries 7,000 tonnes.

The company said its two vessels form the only offshore wind CLV fleet in Korea. Yuanta Securities expects the EHV and submarine share to reach 26.2% in 2027, so whether that share actually rises is the next checkpoint.

Short-term variables include the technology leak investigation involving submarine cable and a block sale by a major shareholder.

08

Bull factors

Record backlog

The backlog at the end of the first quarter of 2026 reached a record KRW 3.83 trillion, up 4.5% from the prior quarter. KB Securities reported in April 2026 that the overseas orders already in hand are expected to be recognized as sales over two to three years.

The backlog is roughly the size of 2025 sales of KRW 3.64 trillion, which supports the view that revenue visibility is high.

Higher share of high-value products

Yuanta Securities expects the EHV and submarine cable share to rise from 19.1% in 2025 to 21.5% in 2026 and 26.2% in 2027, and said a higher mix of high-margin products should translate into better profitability. Margin improvement is the variable that could break the flat 3.5% operating margin seen since 2024.

Vertical integration and installation capacity

The company expects its submarine cable production capacity to grow to about five times the current level once the Dangjin No. 2 plant is operating. With its own vessels and a subsea installation subsidiary, it can handle design, production, transport and installation in-house.

Export-Import Bank of Korea financing totaling KRW 550 billion supports the build-out of production and installation infrastructure.

09

Bear factors

Volatile quarterly earnings

After a KRW 7.38 billion net loss attributable to controlling shareholders in the first quarter of 2026, the second quarter posted a KRW 88.4 billion loss, making two straight loss quarters.

Third-quarter operating profit fell to KRW 30.9 billion, down 49.3% from the prior quarter, and net profit attributable to controlling shareholders of KRW 33 billion was down 16.5% year on year. The cumulative net loss attributable to controlling shareholders over the last four quarters is KRW 21.9 billion.

Balance sheet and cash flow strain

The debt-to-equity ratio rose to 114.0% at year-end 2025 from 76.6% in 2024, with total liabilities of KRW 1.86 trillion exceeding total equity of KRW 1.63 trillion. Operating cash flow deteriorated to -KRW 171.3 billion in 2025.

With facility investment and vessel acquisitions continuing, whether that cash flow improves is the core bearish concern.

Legal risk and intensifying competition

Yuanta Securities pointed to short-term variables including the technology leak investigation into submarine cable and a block sale by a major shareholder. KB Securities listed litigation with competitors among its risk factors in a July 2026 report.

The LS Group's HVDC cable capacity has more than quadrupled since its fifth plant was completed, which could intensify bidding competition.

Risk factors

Financial and cash flow risk

In 2025, the debt-to-equity ratio rose and operating cash flow was negative. As orders grow, cash goes out first for raw materials and production, so whether order growth turns into cash generation is the key question. If borrowing and facility spending run together, the financial burden can persist even when profits improve.

Legal and governance risk

The technology leak investigation into submarine cable and a block sale by a major shareholder were flagged as short-term variables. A block sale can move the supply and demand of shares and add to share price volatility. The outcome of the investigation and the timing of the share transaction should be tracked through disclosures.

Earnings volatility, raw materials and competition

Operating profit fell to KRW 30.9 billion in the third quarter of 2026, down 49.3% from the prior quarter, and the direction of net results attributable to controlling shareholders changed from quarter to quarter. Copper prices lift revenue but also raise cost pressure. If bidding competition with the LS Group intensifies, margin improvement could slow.

12

Overall view

Taihan Cable & Solution's annual sales grew from KRW 2.45 trillion in 2022 to KRW 3.64 trillion in 2025, and operating profit rose to KRW 128.6 billion, but the operating margin has held at 3.5% in both 2024 and 2025.

The backlog reached a record KRW 3.83 trillion at the end of the first quarter of 2026, and a rising share of EHV and submarine cable creates the structure that could lift margins.

On the other side, net losses attributable to controlling shareholders in the first and second quarters of 2026 and the lower third-quarter operating profit have followed, while the 114.0% debt-to-equity ratio and -KRW 171.3 billion operating cash flow remain financial burdens.

Strengths in order visibility and weaknesses in earnings volatility and cash flow carry comparable weight at this stage.

Market valuation appears in the price-to-book premium and in analyst target prices, and the evidence to support it will come from the Dangjin No. 2 plant starting up in 2027, the outcome of the West Coast HVDC tender and the cash flow figures in the 2026 annual report.
