02

Business structure

Inchon City GAS is a city gas supplier founded in 1983 that exclusively supplies more than 800,000 households across Incheon's Bupyeong, Gyeyang, Seo, Geomdan, Yeongjong and Ganghwa areas and parts of Gimpo.

The company buys all of its required volume from KoreaGas, adjusts supply pressure at district regulating stations, and sells to end users through its pipeline network.

Retail tariffs are set by adding a retail supply cost to the wholesale price, and the retail supply cost is approved once a year by Incheon City and Gyeonggi Province. The business is concentrated in one segment, city gas sales, so all revenue comes from gas supply.

In the first half of 2025, fuel-cell power generation accounted for 27.88% of sales volume, industrial use 17.53%, and transport use (CNG refuelling) 2.91%. A corporate information site lists residential use as the largest share at 50.0% of total sales volume.

Fuel-cell demand rests on minimum-volume supply agreements with Korea Western Power and Korea Southern Power and a supply contract with Horang Energy, a project company that operates a hydrogen fuel-cell plant on the company's site.

Samchully is the other city gas operator in Incheon, and in the 2026 construction plan Inchon City GAS was allocated 16,867 households (construction cost of about KRW 15.1 billion) against 5,273 for Samchully.

04

Earnings analysis

On a disclosed basis, annual sales rose from KRW 728.5 billion in 2022 to KRW 781.3 billion in 2023 (up 7.3% year on year), KRW 943.6 billion in 2024 (up 20.8%), and KRW 963 billion in 2025 (up 2.1%), growing for three consecutive years from 2023. Within 2022 to 2025, 2025 was the largest year for sales.

Sales growth rests on volume, and 2025 gas sales volume rose 7.2% year on year to 1.151 billion cubic meters according to the operating report at the shareholders' meeting.

Operating profit moved from KRW 8.05 billion in 2022 to KRW 16.5 billion in 2023 (up 105.2%) and KRW 19.4 billion in 2024 (up 17.6%), the largest year in the period, before easing to KRW 19.1 billion in 2025 (down 1.4%).

The operating margin rose from 1.1% in 2022 to 2.1% in 2023, then held in the 2% range, reaching 2.0% in 2025, which shows that higher sales have not translated into a wider margin.

Profit attributable to controlling shareholders rose from KRW 12.2 billion in 2022 to KRW 19.3 billion in 2024 and was KRW 19.2 billion in 2025 (down 0.5%), and net income including non-controlling interests was reported at the same levels.

Operating cash flow was KRW 14.7 billion in 2022, KRW 38.9 billion in 2023, KRW 36 billion in 2024 and KRW 39.1 billion in 2025, well above net income from 2023 onward.

Quarterly, Q3 2025 recorded an operating loss of KRW 1.58 billion and a net loss attributable to controlling shareholders of KRW 519 million, but Q4 2025 turned to operating profit of KRW 11.1 billion.

Sales peaked at KRW 319.5 billion in Q1 2026, then fell to KRW 196.5 billion in Q2 and KRW 191.4 billion in Q3, two consecutive quarterly declines. Q3 2026 sales rose 37.7% year on year, yet operating profit fell to KRW 591 million, down 82.1% from the prior quarter.

A securities information site reported that first-half 2026 standalone sales fell 9.9% year on year while operating profit rose 6.6%, attributing the sales decline to lower winter heating demand.

05

Industry analysis

The city gas industry depends heavily on the wholesale price of LNG, the feedstock. Reports say the prolonged Middle East conflict has pushed up international LNG wholesale prices and put pressure on city gas wholesale tariffs.

According to KoreaGas's natural gas tariff information, the average wholesale price for city gas use was KRW 20.5886 per MJ, up 9.8% from January 2026.

Over the same period the residential wholesale tariff stayed frozen at KRW 20.8495 per MJ, while industrial, transport and power generation tariffs rose sharply, and the wholesale tariff for cogeneration use rose 30.6% from January.

On the retail side, Incheon had kept city gas tariffs frozen since 2023, but reports say rising feedstock costs made a tariff increase likely. Incheon's tariff is described as the second lowest in the country, and the residential usage charge has been frozen for three years since 2022.

On demand, Incheon was the only one of the six major metropolitan areas where population grew from 2012 to 2021. Residential demand, however, is described as flat, as new housing is offset by population decline and efficiency gains.

Commercial and industrial demand is growing, supported by large-building GHP systems, absorption chillers and carbon-neutral policy. Supply areas are split among operators, and Inchon City GAS was allocated more new households than Samchully in the 2026 construction plan.

06

Outlook

At its 2026 New Year ceremony the company set four management priorities: sustained value creation, faster response to the energy transition, supply chain management, and stronger customer trust. CEO Jung Jin-seo said the company would lead the changing energy landscape through renewable energy projects.

On March 16, 2026, the company made a voluntary disclosure of a corporate value-up plan. The company has said it will reinvest profits from new demand development, including fuel cells and hydrogen production bases, into easing tariff burdens and expanding facilities in areas without gas supply.

Under the construction plan, Inchon City GAS is set to supply 16,867 households in 2026, installing 28,499 meters of pipeline, and 11,359 households in 2027, installing 23,927 meters. Expanding the supply area widens the sales base, so the pace of execution of this plan is the next item to track.

On the 2026 earnings trend, Q2 operating profit fell 52.5% from the prior quarter and Q3 fell a further 82.1%, so the profit base is shrinking. On tariffs, the feedstock linkage system allows adjustments every two months or monthly by use category.

A 2026 report raised the possibility of an August increase, and whether and by how much it was applied should be confirmed through later tariff notices and disclosures.

Bull factors

Exclusive supply area and rising volume

The company supplies city gas exclusively across Incheon, with more than 800,000 households as customers. Sales volume in 2025 rose 7.2% year on year to 1.151 billion cubic meters, and sales have grown for three straight years from 2023. A feedstock linkage system allows changes in cost to flow through to tariffs.

Stronger balance sheet

Operating cash flow in 2025 was KRW 39.1 billion, above that year's net income. Cash generation underpins both facility investment and dividends.

Fuel-cell and renewable-linked demand

In the first half of 2025, fuel-cell power generation made up 27.88% of sales volume. The 2027 construction plan includes supply to 11,359 households. The company emphasized the pace of its renewable energy business among its 2026 management priorities.

09

Bear factors

Consecutive quarterly profit declines

Operating profit has declined for three straight quarters since the Q4 2025 peak. Q3 2026 operating profit was KRW 591 million, down 82.1% from the prior quarter, and profit attributable to controlling shareholders was KRW 2.15 billion. Sales have risen while profit has not kept pace.

Thin operating margin

The operating margin was 2.1% in 2023, 2.1% in 2024 and 2.0% in 2025. Operating profit of KRW 19.1 billion in 2025 was small against sales of KRW 963 billion. A cost swing can easily move profit because the margin is thin.

Tariff regulation and cost pressure

Retail city gas tariffs require approval from Incheon City and Gyeonggi Province. Rising LNG wholesale prices in 2026 added pressure on wholesale tariffs, while the residential wholesale tariff stayed frozen. If tariff increases are delayed, cost burdens can flow directly into margins.

10

Risk factors

Feedstock and policy risk

The company buys all of its gas from KoreaGas, so wholesale price changes flow directly into costs. Retail tariffs need approval from Incheon City and Gyeonggi Province, and the size of feedstock-linked adjustments depends on policy decisions.

A prolonged swing in LNG prices driven by Middle East tensions could change the timing and size of tariff changes and therefore the profit path.

Weather and seasonal demand risk

City gas demand depends heavily on heating needs, so quarterly results swing with the seasons. A securities information site attributed lower first-half 2026 standalone sales to reduced winter heating demand. A warmer-than-normal season could pressure both sales and profit.

Investment burden risk

Reports say the company continues to invest in supply facilities at a scale above its operating profit each year. Because it prioritizes public-interest management, expanding areas without supply and stabilizing tariffs, investment burdens could slow the pace of profitability improvement. Larger construction plans would increase cash outlays and could affect dividend capacity.

Overall view

Inchon City GAS has grown sales for three straight years from 2023 through 2025, supported by its exclusive supply area and rising volume, and its debt ratio fell to 88.4% in 2025. However, the operating margin stayed in the 2% range, from 1.1% in 2022 to 2.0% in 2025, so higher sales have not widened margins.

Operating profit and profit attributable to controlling shareholders have declined for three consecutive quarters since Q1 2026 after the Q4 2025 turn to profit, and profit attributable to controlling shareholders over the last four quarters totaled KRW 22.3 billion.

A securities information site reported that first-half 2026 sales fell on lower winter heating demand while operating profit rose. LNG wholesale price trends, the possibility of a tariff increase and the burden of facility investment are the main variables for margins.

Valuation reads in terms of earnings scale relative to the industry and share price relative to net assets, and the next checkpoints are the full-year operating margin in the 2026 annual report and quarterly profit in Q1 2027.
