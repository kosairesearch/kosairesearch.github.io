02

Business structure

Invenia is a display panel equipment maker founded in 2001 and listed on KOSDAQ in 2005. Its core product is the dry etcher used in TFT-LCD and OLED panel production, and the business has been expanding from etchers toward Chinese customer diversification and OLED-related equipment.

Most revenue is generated overseas, and contracts with Chinese panel makers largely determine results.

A contract with Chuzhou HKC Display Technology, disclosed on January 2, 2026, is worth about KRW 13.2 billion, and a contract with Fuzhou BOE Optoelectronics Technology, disclosed on June 23, 2026, is worth KRW 8.68 billion.

A KRW 19.8 billion contract signed with LG Display in 2020 saw its delivery date pushed from June 2022 to December 2025, followed by repeated year-end extension filings. The order backlog fell from KRW 102.3 billion in 2021 to KRW 55.5 billion in 2024 and stood at KRW 45.7 billion in the third-quarter 2025 report.

Because the portfolio is concentrated in display equipment, the company is pursuing semiconductor process parts and atmospheric plasma cleaners to broaden its lineup.

04

Earnings analysis

Annual revenue was KRW 56.9 billion in 2022, KRW 22.7 billion in 2023 (down 60.1% year on year), KRW 21.8 billion in 2024 (down 3.8%), and KRW 18.4 billion in 2025 (down 15.7%), a three-year decline running from 2023 through 2025. The largest year in the 2022-2025 window was 2022, and the smallest was 2025.

Operating losses widened from KRW 12.7 billion in 2022 to KRW 14.1 billion in 2023, then narrowed to KRW 10.9 billion in 2024 and KRW 8.61 billion in 2025, marking four consecutive years of losses. Operating margin moved from -22.3% in 2022 to -62.3% in 2023 and then improved to -49.7% in 2024 and -46.7% in 2025.

One press account linked the earlier narrowing of losses to cost reduction.

Net losses attributable to controlling shareholders were KRW 1.01 billion in 2022, KRW 17.5 billion in 2023, KRW 10 billion in 2024 and KRW 12.7 billion in 2025, and net income including non-controlling interests was reported at the same annual amounts.

The debt ratio rose from 116.7% in 2022 to 323.5% in 2025, when total equity was KRW 15.7 billion, all attributable to controlling shareholders, against total liabilities of KRW 50.7 billion.

Operating cash flow was KRW 4.6 billion in 2022, -KRW 3.97 billion in 2023, -KRW 1.03 billion in 2024 and KRW 589 million in 2025.

Quarterly revenue rose from KRW 1.26 billion in the third quarter of 2025 to KRW 11.9 billion in the fourth quarter, an 846.0% increase on the prior quarter, and the company said a November 2025 supply contract secured KRW 6.3 billion of revenue.

Revenue peaked at KRW 14 billion in the first quarter of 2026 and then fell to KRW 2.62 billion in the second quarter and KRW 2.44 billion in the third quarter, two consecutive quarterly declines.

Operating profit of KRW 191 million in the first quarter turned into an operating loss of KRW 2.65 billion in the second quarter and KRW 1.13 billion in the third.

Press reports attribute the gap between the first-quarter operating profit and net income of KRW 2.21 billion to non-operating foreign-exchange items and a one-time miscellaneous gain.

The cumulative net loss attributable to controlling shareholders over the last four quarters, from the fourth quarter of 2025 through the third quarter of 2026, was KRW 6.62 billion.

05

Industry analysis

Demand in the downstream market comes from Chinese panel makers expanding LCD lines and converting to OLED. Press reports say large players such as BOE and CSOT have stepped up OLED conversion and large-area LCD investment, lifting demand for dry etchers.

A June 2026 report, however, noted that the global display equipment market has paused for now, while investment demand for OLED in mid-to-large IT and automotive applications is expected to rise over the medium term.

Revenue reached KRW 182.1 billion in 2017 before the company's sales shrank, according to press analysis. Press accounts also tie the decline to LG Display cutting capex in the mid-2010s amid price pressure from Chinese makers.

The company says its large-area dry etcher for 10.5G TV production is shared globally between Invenia and Japan's TEL.

Customer requirements are shifting from standard specifications to process conditions set for each customer, and the company is developing a 6-sided version of its 8.6-generation dry etcher and a hybrid system combining ECCP and ICP.

The current position is centered on display equipment, while semiconductor parts and cleaners are still at the expansion stage. Outside China, press reports describe cooperation talks with Indian government bodies to build a display supply chain.

06

Outlook

The company declared 2026 the start of a return to profitability and of entry into new global markets, announced on January 6, 2026.

After an operating profit in the first quarter of 2026, operating losses continued in the second and third quarters, and the company said that if follow-on orders continue, results this year would grow sharply.

The company said it had completed specification discussions and verification for an 8.6-generation dry etcher supply to a Chinese customer, and expects this contract to exceed a previously secured KRW 19.5 billion order.

The same customer is reviewing a new investment of about KRW 100 billion in 8.6-generation lines, according to a company official. The company expects large order opportunities to continue from the second half of 2026 through the first half of 2027.

The BOE contract runs from June 22, 2026 to February 28, 2027, with supply to China. In India, the company plans to expand step by step from a local subsidiary and customer support center to an R&D center and production base, and internally expects a full earnings contribution from 2028.

On funding, the rights offering was reduced from KRW 13.06 billion to KRW 6.84 billion, and the planned use of operating funds was cut from KRW 9.064 billion to KRW 2.84 billion.

The company has mentioned possible sales of its Paju headquarters and a Goyang building, as well as borrowing from its largest shareholder, if funding falls short. How much the semiconductor parts and cleaner businesses contribute can first be checked in the segment revenue table of the 2026 annual report.

Bull factors

Large Chinese order pipeline

Contracts with Chuzhou HKC Display Technology worth about KRW 13.2 billion in January 2026 and with BOE worth KRW 8.68 billion in June 2026 were disclosed. The company said it completed specification discussions and verification for an 8.6-generation dry etcher supply.

When large contracts were recognized as revenue, quarterly sales surged in the fourth quarter of 2025 and the first quarter of 2026.

Improved cost structure

Press reports put first-quarter 2026 cost of sales at KRW 11.1 billion and gross profit at KRW 2.9 billion. This contrasts with the first quarter of 2025, when cost of sales exceeded revenue and produced a gross loss above KRW 1 billion.

Press analysis attributes the return to operating profit to revenue recognition from China-related orders combined with cost improvement.

Technology differentiation and new market expansion

The company says global mass-production results for large-area dry etchers for 10.5G TV production are split between Invenia and TEL. It is strengthening customer-specific responses through 6-sided expansion and hybrid system development.

In India, it is pursuing a local subsidiary and customer support center to diversify its order base beyond China.

Bear factors

Renewed losses and revenue decline

Annual operating losses continued from 2022 through 2025, while revenue fell from KRW 56.9 billion to KRW 18.4 billion. Operating losses persisted in the second and third quarters of 2026.

Quarterly revenue dropped from KRW 14 billion in the first quarter of 2026 to KRW 2.44 billion in the third quarter, showing a sharp gap once large contract revenue was recognized.

Balance sheet and funding capacity

The debt ratio was 323.5% at the end of 2025, with total liabilities of KRW 50.7 billion and total equity of KRW 15.7 billion. Press reports say the first rights offering price was set at KRW 855, cutting the raise from KRW 13.06 billion to KRW 6.84 billion and reducing planned operating funds. Operating cash flow turned positive only in 2025.

Customer concentration and order volatility

The order backlog fell from KRW 102.3 billion in 2021 to KRW 55.5 billion in 2024 and stood at KRW 45.7 billion in the third-quarter 2025 report. A KRW 19.8 billion contract with LG Display in 2020 had its delivery date extended multiple times. Because revenue depends heavily on when Chinese customers place orders, quarterly results swing widely.

Risk factors

Listing status and disclosure risk

Trading was halted on March 23, 2026 after an administrative-issue designation event occurred. On July 15, 2026, the exchange gave notice of potential designation as an unfaithful disclosure company for failing to disclose such events at the internal closing stage, with the triggering date given as February 11, 2026.

The exchange's decision deadline was August 10, 2026, so the final designation and penalty points should be checked in DART filings.

Exchange rate and Chinese investment cycle

Press reports say most revenue is generated overseas, and that high exchange rates produced foreign-currency translation gains. If the exchange rate moves the other way, non-operating income could reverse.

If Chinese customers scale back capex or price competition in bidding intensifies, orders could be delayed and revenue gaps could widen.

Funding and financial burden

Press reports put the impairment-to-capital ratio at 63% on a consolidated basis in the third quarter of 2025, and analysts said it would be hard to lower without a meaningful return to profit in the fourth quarter.

As the rights offering shrank, funding pressure increased, and the company has mentioned possible sales of its Paju headquarters and a Goyang building, as well as borrowing from its largest shareholder.

Overall view

Invenia is a dry etcher maker for display panel lines whose revenue fell from KRW 56.9 billion in 2022 to KRW 18.4 billion in 2025 with operating losses in each year.

Press reports attribute the first-quarter 2026 return to operating profit to revenue recognition from China-related orders combined with cost improvement, and that quarter's net income included one-time items.

Operating losses returned in the second and third quarters, and the cumulative net loss attributable to controlling shareholders over the last four quarters was KRW 6.62 billion.

Bullish factors include the Chinese large-customer order pipeline, large-area dry etcher technology, and expansion efforts into markets such as India.

Bearish factors include the renewed losses and falling revenue, the 323.5% debt ratio and shrinking equity at the end of 2025, and listing-related issues tied to administrative-issue designation and disclosure penalties.

The order backlog of KRW 45.7 billion in the third quarter of 2025 is below KRW 102.3 billion in 2021, so whether large orders convert into revenue is the most important point to track.

The next checkpoints are the fourth-quarter 2026 preliminary results, disclosure of the 8.6-generation contract, completion of the BOE contract, and the 2026 annual report. This report is provided for information only and includes no buy or sell opinion or target price.
