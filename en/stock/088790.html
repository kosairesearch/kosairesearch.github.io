Jindo: Seasonal Fur Sales, Uneven Quarterly Profits

Jindo's fur-centered business is highly seasonal. Operating profit recovered to KRW 3.98 billion in 2025, but operating losses in the second and third quarters of 2026 keep quarterly results volatile.

02

Business structure

Jindo, listed on KOSPI and established through a 2006 spin-off, manufactures and wholesales and retails fur garments. Its premium fur brand Jindo Fur is supported by a young casual line, a mid-to-low-priced line, a women's apparel line and an accessories brand.

Sales run through department stores, outlets, dealers, home shopping and online channels. According to FnGuide, as of December 2025 fur garments accounted for about 89.4% of sales, ready-to-wear apparel about 8.2% and rental income about 2.4%.

JobKorea put the fur garment share at about 96% as of March 2026, so the figures differ by reporting date and basis. The design institute's new-product planning and quality control are described as the basis for the company's leading position in the Korean fur market.

Despite intensifying competition among department store tenants, the company has reportedly kept cost-of-sales increases small through brand power, according to a securities information site's earnings commentary. The cost-of-sales ratio fell from 51.86% in 2022 to 49.01% in 2025 (FnGuide).

The accessories brand plans to expand department store counters at Lotte, Hyundai and Shinsegae. The 2030 mid-term plan names a higher share of high-margin products through the Sobrin line and lower distribution commissions as core tasks. Demand for fur garments depends on season and temperature, so seasonality is built into the business model.

04

Earnings analysis

On a disclosed basis, sales went from KRW 63.4 billion in 2022 to KRW 56.4 billion in 2023 (down 11.1% year on year) and KRW 44.2 billion in 2024 (down 21.7%), before rebounding to KRW 47.6 billion in 2025 (up 7.8%). 2022 was the largest year and 2024 the smallest among 2022 to 2025.

Operating profit went from KRW 6.09 billion in 2022 to KRW 3.76 billion in 2023 (down 38.3%) and KRW 1.14 billion in 2024 (down 69.6%), then recovered to KRW 3.98 billion in 2025 (up 248.3%). Operating margins were 9.6%, 6.7%, 2.6% and 8.4% from 2022 to 2025.

In 2025 sales rose 7.8% while operating profit rose 248.3%, which points to cost structure improvement rather than volume growth. FnGuide's data show the cost-of-sales ratio falling from 51.20% in 2024 to 49.01% in 2025, consistent with that reading.

Profit attributable to controlling shareholders was KRW 4.57 billion in 2022, KRW 4.39 billion in 2023 (down 4.0%), KRW 910 million in 2024 (down 79.3%) and KRW 4.11 billion in 2025 (up 351.2%).

Operating cash flow was KRW 8.58 billion in 2022, KRW 8.07 billion in 2023 and KRW 3.83 billion in 2024, recovering to KRW 7.58 billion in 2025. The debt ratio ranged from 15.6% to 25.6% over 2022 to 2025, at 24.0%, 15.6%, 24.9% and 25.6%. Quarterly results show a strong seasonal pattern.

Sales were KRW 7.38 billion in the third quarter of 2025 with an operating loss of KRW 280 million, then jumped to KRW 21.1 billion in the fourth quarter (up 186.5% from the prior quarter) with operating profit of KRW 4.35 billion.

The first quarter of 2026 brought sales of KRW 24.8 billion and operating profit of KRW 4.13 billion (down 5.0% from the prior quarter). In the second quarter sales fell to KRW 3.72 billion (down 85.0%) and the company swung to an operating loss of KRW 426 million.

Third-quarter sales of KRW 7.02 billion rose 89.0% from the prior quarter but fell 4.8% from a year earlier, while the operating loss widened to KRW 549 million.

Profit attributable to controlling shareholders turned positive at KRW 555 million in that quarter, and the gap between the operating loss and this profit is a point to verify in the 2026 annual report. Profit attributable to controlling shareholders over the last four quarters totaled KRW 7.84 billion.

05

Industry analysis

Jindo's core product, fur garments, depends heavily on temperature and seasonal purchasing. Sales concentrate in autumn and winter and shrink in spring and summer, a pattern visible in the surge in fourth-quarter 2025 sales and the sharp drop in second-quarter 2026 sales.

Companies in the same textiles, fashion and lifestyle sector, such as women's apparel and casual brands, differ in materials and seasonal structure, so margins and multiples are hard to compare directly. In department store channels, competition among tenants has intensified, as noted in earnings commentary.

On distribution, commission costs are a key variable for profitability, and the company has set distribution reform and commission cuts as execution tasks.

On raw materials, the company plans to strengthen cost competitiveness through direct sourcing and early procurement of inputs, a direction consistent with the lower cost-of-sales ratio in 2025.

Management has cited improving returns in the non-fur business as a task, which suggests that much of current profit still depends on the fur business. The company is also reviewing new businesses to diversify sales.

No public forward market indicator that would place the industry in its cycle has been confirmed, so seasonal demand and department store channel competition are the main variables for earnings.

In competitive terms, the company is described as strengthening price competitiveness on the back of its premium brand recognition in the Korean fur market.

06

Outlook

The mid-term plan disclosed on 1 April 2026 targets sales of KRW 95.1 billion-plus from the fur business and an operating margin of 12%-plus by 2030. Against an operating margin of 8.4% in 2025, the company must lift profitability further to reach the target.

Execution tasks include optimizing fixed-cost resources, expanding the share of high-margin products through the Sobrin line, distribution reform and lower commissions, SCM optimization through direct sourcing and early procurement of inputs, and expanded investment in the non-fur business.

The company stated that it will maintain a minimum per-share dividend for the business years 2026 and 2027.

At the extraordinary shareholders' meeting on 8 October 2026, amendments to the articles, including a new basis for interim dividends, and a capital reserve reduction were voted down, while a revision to the executive retirement pay rules passed.

At the same meeting, the proposal to elect one auditor was not put to a vote. A September 2026 report said the company signed a trust-based share buyback contract of up to KRW 1.5 billion with KB Securities.

A late-October 2026 disclosure showed the chairman's holdings rising by 74,888 shares and his stake increasing by 0.60 percentage points, from 9.36% as of the 22 October 2026 record date.

Fourth-quarter 2026 results have not yet been disclosed in a periodic report, so whether full-year 2026 operating margin is on the 2030 target path is a point for the 2026 annual report.

Bull factors

Profit recovery and a margin target

Operating profit for 2025 was KRW 3.98 billion, up 248.3%, and the operating margin climbed to 8.4% from a 2.6% low in 2024. The company targets an operating margin of 12%-plus by 2030. The bull case is that fixed-cost optimization and a higher share of high-margin products leave room for margin gains. The path to the target is only starting.

Financial capacity and cash flow recovery

Total equity was KRW 111.5 billion at the end of 2025 and the debt ratio was 25.6%. Operating cash flow recovered from KRW 3.83 billion in 2024 to KRW 7.58 billion in 2025. With a comparatively light debt load, the company has financial room to absorb losses in off-season quarters, which is the bullish reading.

Shareholder return policy and a larger insider stake

The company said it will keep a minimum per-share dividend for the business years 2026 and 2027. A trust-based share buyback contract of up to KRW 1.5 billion with KB Securities was reported in September 2026.

A disclosure on 30 October 2026 showed the chairman's holdings rising by 74,888 shares, a 0.60 percentage point increase in his stake.

Bear factors

Consecutive quarterly losses and a wider loss

Operating losses of KRW 426 million in the second quarter and KRW 549 million in the third quarter of 2026 mark two consecutive loss-making quarters. Third-quarter sales of KRW 7.02 billion fell 4.8% year on year, and the operating loss widened from KRW 280 million a year earlier.

The bearish view is that profits in the fourth quarter and first quarter came from concentrated seasonal sales, so fixed costs in off-season quarters amplify volatility.

Limited sales recovery

Sales fell from KRW 63.4 billion in 2022 to KRW 44.2 billion in 2024 and rebounded to KRW 47.6 billion in 2025, still below the 2022 level. The 2025 operating margin of 8.4% recovered but remains under 9.6% in 2022. The bearish concern is that the recovery rests more on margin than on top-line growth.

Department store competition and non-fur execution

Competition among department store tenants has intensified, according to earnings commentary. The company has set commission cuts and better non-fur returns as tasks, which implies that these have not yet been resolved in the current earnings structure.

The rejection of the interim dividend amendment at the October 2026 extraordinary meeting also remains a shareholder-side variable.

Risk factors

Seasonality and inventory burden

Fur garment earnings concentrate in autumn and winter sales, so quarterly revenue swings sharply. Fourth-quarter 2025 sales of KRW 21.1 billion rose 186.5% from the prior quarter, while second-quarter 2026 sales of KRW 3.72 billion fell 85.0%. A weak season could bring inventory pressure and operating losses at the same time.

Channel and cost structure

Under a department store-centered channel, competition among tenants and commission costs weigh on profitability. The company has said it will pursue commission cuts and distribution reform to reach its 2030 targets. Execution results should be checked in the cost structure of the 2026 annual report.

Governance and shareholder meeting agendas

At the 8 October 2026 extraordinary meeting, amendments including an interim dividend basis and a capital reserve reduction were voted down, and the auditor election was not put to a vote. Even with a minimum dividend policy, how shareholder agendas are handled bears on whether the dividend policy can be carried out. Whether these items return to a future meeting is a matter to check.

Overall view

Jindo recovered to KRW 3.98 billion in operating profit in 2025, moving away from the 2024 low, but the 2025 operating margin of 8.4% remains below 9.6% in 2022. Quarterly results in 2026 show the seasonal concentration of sales in clear form.

Operating profit of KRW 4.13 billion in the first quarter of 2026 and the fourth-quarter 2025 profit carried earnings, while operating losses continued in the second and third quarters of 2026.

Profit attributable to controlling shareholders over the last four quarters totaled KRW 7.84 billion, but because profit clusters in certain seasons, durability needs further confirmation.

On the financial side, equity was KRW 111.5 billion and the debt ratio 25.6% at the end of 2025, so the debt burden is comparatively light, and operating cash flow recovered to KRW 7.58 billion.

The company has set sales of KRW 95.1 billion-plus and an operating margin of 12%-plus for 2030, with high-margin product expansion and lower distribution costs as core tasks.

On shareholder returns, the minimum dividend policy, the trust-based buyback and the chairman's higher stake are positives, while the items voted down in October 2026 remain a variable for policy execution.

The points to check are the operating margin in the 2026 annual report and whether losses persist in the first-quarter 2027 quarterly report.
