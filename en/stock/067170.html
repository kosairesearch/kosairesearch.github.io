Narrower losses, but volatile quarterly profits at Autech

Autech narrowed its 2025 operating loss to KRW 2.26 billion, but third-quarter 2026 operating profit fell to KRW 415 million and the last four quarters carried KRW 17 billion in cumulative net losses attributable to controlling shareholders, so whether earnings can hold is the next thing to track.

02

Business structure

Autech is a specialty vehicle maker founded in 1991 and listed on KOSDAQ in 2003. Its main revenue comes from the specialty vehicle segment, covering ambulances, automotive parts and refrigerated trucks.

Its product lineup includes negative-pressure ambulances, welfare vehicles, cancer-screening medical vehicles, special logistics vehicles, medical equipment and automotive parts.

It expanded into heating, cooling and air-conditioning through its 2011 acquisition of Autech Carrier, and its main subsidiaries now include Autech Carrier and Autech Carrier Refrigeration.

In the first quarter of 2025, specialty vehicles benefited from stronger demand for negative-pressure ambulances and welfare vehicles, while the HVAC business saw lower sales and cost pressure.

In 2024 the company signed a PBV conversion contract with KIA, and in 2025 it set up a dedicated conversion center in Hwaseong. In March 2026 it said it would scale up PBV conversion work on the KIA partnership, emphasizing an integrated role from design and parts development through production.

In HVAC, a large order to supply high-efficiency air-cooled heat pump chillers to a data center in Gangseo, Seoul, has been reported. Orders in specialty vehicles are driven by public procurement, while HVAC orders depend on private facility investment and are seasonal with inventory effects. The two businesses therefore follow different order patterns.

04

Earnings analysis

Annual revenue fell from KRW 1.02 trillion in 2022 to KRW 959.7 billion in 2023 (down 5.9% year on year), KRW 909.2 billion in 2024 (down 5.3%) and KRW 852.1 billion in 2025 (down 6.3%), a three-year decline from 2023 through 2025.

The operating loss went from KRW 19.1 billion in 2022 to KRW 9.77 billion in 2023, widened to KRW 14.9 billion in 2024, and narrowed to KRW 2.26 billion in 2025. The operating margin was -1.9% in 2022, -1.0% in 2023, -1.6% in 2024 and -0.3% in 2025.

Net loss attributable to controlling shareholders was KRW 32.5 billion in 2022, KRW 17.2 billion in 2023, KRW 23.2 billion in 2024 and KRW 7.41 billion in 2025, while the net loss including non-controlling interests was KRW 7.96 billion in 2025, a different figure.

Operating cash flow moved from -KRW 35.2 billion in 2022 to KRW 22.5 billion in 2023, then KRW 933 million in 2024 and KRW 6.2 billion in 2025. The debt ratio was 330.2% in 2022, 273.8% in 2023, 323.4% in 2024 and 275.2% in 2025.

At the end of 2025, total liabilities were KRW 373.3 billion, total equity KRW 135.7 billion, and equity attributable to controlling shareholders KRW 104.5 billion. Quarterly, revenue was KRW 256.5 billion with operating profit of KRW 4.73 billion in the third quarter of 2025.

In the fourth quarter of 2025 revenue fell to KRW 143.5 billion (down 44.1% from the prior quarter) and the company swung to an operating loss of KRW 10.2 billion.

In the first quarter of 2026 revenue was KRW 163 billion (up 13.6% from the prior quarter) with an operating loss of KRW 4.18 billion and a net loss attributable to controlling shareholders of KRW 10.1 billion.

In the second quarter of 2026 revenue rose to KRW 251 billion (up 54.0% from the prior quarter), with operating profit of KRW 5.11 billion and net profit attributable to controlling shareholders of KRW 1.37 billion, a swing to profit.

In the third quarter of 2026 revenue was KRW 242.6 billion (down 5.4% year on year), operating profit fell to KRW 415 million (down 91.2% year on year) and net profit attributable to controlling shareholders was KRW 522 million (up 202.6% year on year).

Over the last four quarters, the net loss attributable to controlling shareholders totaled KRW 17 billion. Revenue held near the second-quarter level in the third quarter, yet operating profit fell sharply, so quarterly profitability has moved more than sales volume.

05

Industry analysis

Autech's two business lines are ambulances and specialty vehicles on one side and heating, cooling and air-conditioning through subsidiaries on the other, so a single industry cycle does not explain its results.

Specialty vehicle orders depend on public procurement, while HVAC orders depend on private facility investment and are seasonal. PBV, a demand driver on the vehicle side, is tied to automakers expanding purpose-built models, and Autech sits in KIA's PBV ecosystem handling design, parts and production.

Data-center cooling links the company to AI infrastructure demand. An independent research house said in April 2026 that Autech was pursuing NVIDIA certification for next-generation AI data-center cooling solutions, and that completing it could open a route into the global AI infrastructure supply chain.

In an AlphaSquare comparison of six listed automotive-sector companies for 2025, Autech had the highest debt ratio and a low-ranking operating margin. Hyundai Motor, KIA, Hyundai Mobis and Hanon Systems operate on very different scales and models, so direct comparison is limited.

Competitive position depends on public-bid capability in specialty vehicles and on facility-order and local production capacity in HVAC. Revenue has declined for three years, but quarterly revenue of more than KRW 240 billion in the second and third quarters of 2026 points to a possible top-line recovery.

Whether that top-line recovery converts into operating profit is the central industry question, given third-quarter operating profit of KRW 415 million.

Outlook

The first measure of the PBV strategy is how much PBV-related revenue appears in the next periodic report. The company has been reported as targeting 250,000 global sales by 2030, following its dedicated Hwaseong conversion center set up in 2025.

In HVAC, a large order to supply data-center chillers in Gangseo, Seoul, has been reported as a new order example. NVIDIA certification is still in progress, and its completion is cited as a potential turning point for entering the global AI infrastructure supply chain.

After a swing to operating profit in the second quarter of 2026, a sharp fall in third-quarter operating profit means whether profit holds quarter to quarter is the near-term point to watch. Insider holdings also changed.

A disclosure from April 20, 2026 showed executive director Shin-hyung Kang at a 6.05% stake, and a May 7 disclosure showed vice president Wook Kang at 6.17%. These are disclosed facts, and their meaning is best read alongside the earnings trend.

On revenue, whether the KRW 251 billion of the second quarter and KRW 242.6 billion of the third quarter hold will depend on the seasonality of HVAC and the timing of specialty vehicle orders.

The next verifiable checkpoint is the 2026 annual report, due by the end of March 2027, which will set full-year operating and net results, including the fourth quarter.

Risk factors

Order and demand concentration

Specialty vehicle orders depend on public procurement, while HVAC orders depend on private facility investment and seasonality. The PBV business relies on KIA contract volumes, so changes in orders can flow directly into results. Dependence on a few customers adds to quarterly volatility.

Financial burden

Total liabilities at the end of 2025 were KRW 373.3 billion, and equity attributable to controlling shareholders was KRW 104.5 billion. Operating cash flow was KRW 6.2 billion in 2025, positive but small. If net losses continue, borrowing costs and financial headroom could come under pressure at a 275.2% debt ratio.

Governance and shareholding changes

At the March 27, 2026 annual general meeting, the approval of the director compensation limit was voted down. Executive holdings were disclosed as increasing in April and May 2026. Both the shareholder vote and the ownership changes are facts to track together.

Overall view

Autech narrowed its operating loss from KRW 19.1 billion in 2022 to KRW 2.26 billion in 2025, and the operating margin improved from -1.9% to -0.3%. Revenue, however, has declined for three straight years from 2023 through 2025, and the debt ratio remained high at 275.2% at the end of 2025.

After a swing to operating profit in the second quarter of 2026, third-quarter operating profit fell to KRW 415 million, so whether quarterly profit holds is still unconfirmed. On the business side, PBV conversion work for KIA and data-center cooling orders appear to be the new lines.

Progress on NVIDIA certification and how much PBV volume reaches revenue are the key variables for the business shift. On valuation, the last four quarters of net losses leave no earnings-based multiple, and the price-to-book ratio is below 1x.

The next checkpoints are the 2026 annual report, due by the end of March 2027, and the Q1 2027 quarterly report.
