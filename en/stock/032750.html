Samjin: Q3 2026 Profit Drop Puts Remote-Control Core in Focus

02

Business structure

Samjin is an electronics component maker that produces TV remote controls and smart-home IoT products on an ODM or OEM basis and supplies them mainly to Samsung Electronics. A 2022 technical analysis report described the company as a specialist in smart-home electronic components centered on remote controls and IoT.

By product segment, remote controls accounted for 69% of Q2 2026 revenue, followed by speakers at 9%, communication modules at 9%, other products at 9%, and merchandise at 4%, according to a corporate data compilation. Because remote controls dominate, TV and appliance set demand and customer order flow drive revenue.

The IoT business generates sales mainly from hubs and sensors for smart homes, and the company is developing communication modules as a core device, according to a financial information site.

Production covers design of mechanics, circuits and software, plastic injection molding and PCB assembly, according to the FnGuide company profile. Consolidated subsidiaries include production units in Thailand and Qingdao, China, and a sales unit in Singapore.

R&D spending equaled about 2.7% of revenue in 2025, according to FnGuide. Because finished remote controls are priced and volumed by customer orders, supply relationship stability and cost competition are central to profitability.

04

Earnings analysis

Revenue on a reported basis rose from KRW 155.1 billion in 2022 to KRW 156.4 billion in 2023, up 0.8% year on year, and to KRW 159.2 billion in 2024, up 1.8%, before falling to KRW 148.3 billion in 2025, down 6.8%. Among 2022 to 2025, 2024 was the largest year and 2025 the smallest.

Operating profit grew from KRW 1.66 billion in 2022 to KRW 1.89 billion in 2023, up 14.0%, and to KRW 7.25 billion in 2024, up 284.1%, then fell to KRW 5.82 billion in 2025, down 19.7%. The operating margin moved from 1.1% in 2022 and 1.2% in 2023 to 4.6% in 2024 and 3.9% in 2025.

In 2024, revenue grew only modestly while the margin rose sharply, so margin improvement rather than sales scale drove the earnings recovery.

Net income attributable to controlling shareholders was KRW 1.9 billion in 2022, KRW 116 million in 2023 (down 93.9%), KRW 9.05 billion in 2024 (up 7,694.4%) and KRW 3.89 billion in 2025 (down 57.0%).

In 2023 operating profit rose while attributable net income fell sharply, which suggests non-operating items weighed on results; the detailed line items need to be checked in the annual business report.

Operating cash flow moved from negative KRW 8.03 billion in 2022 to KRW 19.3 billion in 2023, then KRW 12.7 billion in 2024 and KRW 7.95 billion in 2025.

By quarter, revenue peaked at KRW 43.8 billion in Q4 2025, up 58.1% from the prior quarter, while operating profit was only KRW 647 million, down 36.6%, and the company posted a net loss attributable to controlling shareholders of KRW 564 million.

In Q1 2026 revenue was KRW 35.5 billion, down 18.9% quarter on quarter, operating profit was KRW 1.36 billion, up 110.9%, and net income attributable to controlling shareholders was KRW 2.21 billion, a return to profit.

Q2 2026 revenue was KRW 29.1 billion, operating profit KRW 787 million and attributable net income KRW 1.05 billion. Q3 2026 revenue was KRW 26.9 billion, down 3.1% year on year, operating profit KRW 307 million, down 69.9%, and attributable net income KRW 43 million, down 97.2%. Operating profit as a share of revenue fell from about 3.8% in Q1 2026 to about 1.2% in Q3 2026.

05

Industry analysis

Samjin's downstream demand is directly tied to the TV and appliance business of Samsung Electronics. Samsung reported combined VD and DA revenue of KRW 13.9 trillion and an operating loss of KRW 0.2 trillion for Q3 2026.

In the same company, the DS division's memory business posted record quarterly revenue and operating profit as it met AI demand. In this divergent setting, where semiconductors boomed while set businesses posted losses, remote-control demand is most practically gauged through set shipment volumes.

Samsung's DX division cited rising component costs among the difficult operating conditions. For a component supplier, how much of the cost increase can be passed through to prices determines margins, and with the operating margin down to about 1%, Samjin's earnings are highly sensitive to cost changes.

Remote controls are a fairly mature product whose volumes follow TV shipments and specification changes. Non-remote products, including IoT hubs, sensors and communication modules, made up 31% of Q2 2026 revenue and represent the newer growth area tied to smart-home expansion.

Compared with listed peers such as Samsung Electro-Mechanics and LG Innotek, Samjin is smaller and more dependent on a limited customer base. Its R&D spending equaled about 2.7% of 2025 revenue, according to FnGuide.

Outlook

The most direct checkpoint is whether the revenue decline continues into Q4 2026. Revenue fell from KRW 43.8 billion in Q4 2025 to KRW 26.9 billion in Q3 2026.

The operating margin dropped from about 3.8% in Q1 2026 to about 1.2% in Q3 2026, and whether the Q1 2026 return to profit and peak operating profit reflected one-off factors should be checked in the line-item detail of the annual business report.

Q4 2026 results will be included in the 2026 annual business report, which will fix the full-year picture. On the new-business side, expansion of the IoT smart-home product range and development of communication modules are under way, according to FnGuide.

On the customer side, Samsung's Q4 2026 results will serve as a reference for TV and appliance set volumes. Because Samsung's VD and DA division posted an operating loss in Q3 2026, a recovery in set demand would bear on remote-control order flow.

The next periodic report is the 2026 annual business report, and given that the 2025 annual report was disclosed on March 12, 2026, filing around March 2027 is the next confirmation date.

After that, the Q1 2027 quarterly report will show whether revenue rebounds from the KRW 26.9 billion level and whether the operating margin moves above 1%.

08

Bull factors

Balance sheet has room

Total equity was KRW 110.4 billion and total liabilities KRW 17.8 billion at end-2025, a debt ratio of 16.1%. The debt ratio fell from 24.3% in 2022 to 18.3% in 2024. Operating cash flow turned from negative KRW 8.03 billion in 2022 to positive in each year since, reaching KRW 7.95 billion in 2025.

Margin recovery

The operating margin rose from 1.1% in 2022 to 4.6% in 2024 and was 3.9% in 2025. Operating profit in 2024 reached KRW 7.25 billion, up 284.1% year on year. Even after the 2025 decline, the margin stayed above its 2022 and 2023 levels.

Non-remote product mix

Products other than remote controls accounted for 31% of Q2 2026 revenue, covering speakers, communication modules, and smart-home hubs and sensors. Ongoing development of communication modules points to an intent to widen the IoT product range.

Bear factors

Three straight quarterly declines

Revenue fell from KRW 43.8 billion in Q4 2025 to KRW 35.5 billion in Q1 2026, KRW 29.1 billion in Q2 and KRW 26.9 billion in Q3. Q3 revenue was down 7.6% quarter on quarter and 3.1% year on year. The continued quarterly decline weakens the basis for a recovery in the top line.

Weakening earnings power

Q3 2026 operating profit was KRW 307 million, down 69.9% year on year, and net income attributable to controlling shareholders was KRW 43 million, down 97.2%. The operating margin fell from about 3.8% in Q1 2026 to about 1.2% in Q3. In Q4 2025 the company had posted a net loss attributable to controlling shareholders of KRW 564 million.

Concentration and customer weakness

Remote controls made up 69% of Q2 2026 revenue, so results are closely tied to TV set demand. Samsung's VD and DA division, a key customer segment, posted combined revenue of KRW 13.9 trillion and an operating loss of KRW 0.2 trillion in Q3 2026. When the customer's set business is loss-making, component order volumes can come under pressure.

10

Risk factors

Customer concentration

Samjin depends heavily on orders from a small number of customers, including Samsung Electronics. A reduction in orders or a change in supply relationships could move revenue and profit together. Because remote controls dominate sales, a drop in set shipments translates quickly into lower results.

Cost and currency pressure

Samsung's DX division cited rising component costs among difficult operating conditions and said currency movements weighed on its component business. Samjin is similarly exposed to raw material and currency swings, and delayed cost pass-through could pressure its margin. With the operating margin near 1%, even small cost changes feed heavily into profit.

Earnings volatility and market size

Net income attributable to controlling shareholders swung from KRW 116 million in 2023 to KRW 9.05 billion in 2024 and KRW 3.89 billion in 2025. Non-operating items have sometimes outweighed operating profit, so operating profit alone does not explain earnings. The small market capitalization can also lead to larger share price swings.

Overall view

Samjin is an electronics component maker whose results are tied to set volumes at downstream customers such as Samsung Electronics, with remote controls as its core ODM and OEM business.

Annual revenue ranged between KRW 148.3 billion and KRW 159.2 billion from 2022 to 2025, and operating profit peaked at KRW 7.25 billion in 2024 before falling to KRW 5.82 billion in 2025. Total equity of KRW 110.4 billion and a debt ratio of 16.1% reflect financial capacity.

On the other hand, revenue has declined for three consecutive quarters since the Q4 2025 peak, and Q3 2026 operating profit fell to KRW 307 million with attributable net income of KRW 43 million.

Attributable net income over the last four quarters totals KRW 2.73 billion, about 30% of the KRW 9.05 billion recorded in 2024. A price-to-book ratio well below 1x shows the share price sits low relative to assets, and how it is read depends on whether earnings declines persist.

The next checkpoints are the Q4 2026 results in the 2026 annual business report and the Q1 2027 quarterly report.
