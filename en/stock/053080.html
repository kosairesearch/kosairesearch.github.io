K-Ensol: Order Recovery Against Project Cost Pressure

K-Ensol swung to an operating loss of KRW 62 billion in 2025 and returned to operating profit in Q3 2026, yet its net loss attributable to controlling shareholders over the last four quarters totals KRW 63.8 billion.

02

Business structure

K-Ensol was founded in 1989 as Wonbang Tech and changed its name to K-Ensol in May 2023. It designs and builds cleanrooms and dry rooms. By segment, dry rooms made up 50%, industrial cleanrooms 36%, bridge construction 11%, and bio cleanrooms 4% of revenue in Q2 2026.

Dry rooms are facilities that control temperature and humidity in battery production space, and the company has supplied them to Hyundai Motor Group and SK On's U.S. plants. Industrial cleanrooms center on process facilities for semiconductor fabs such as Samsung Electronics and SK hynix.

A brokerage analysis of the first half of 2024 noted that dry rooms (51.5%) overtook industrial cleanrooms (30.6%) for the first time. Bridge construction is a segment whose results swing with SOC budget levels. Bio cleanrooms are a newer business tied to rising CDMO capacity demand.

The company is also preparing a liquid-immersion cooling business for AI data centers, supplying samples with Submer and running cooling performance tests. Liquid-cooling revenue is not disclosed as a separate business line.

Because profitability depends largely on cost ratios of large projects, the 2025 loss also started from that point.

04

Earnings analysis

On a disclosed basis, revenue rose from KRW 388.8 billion in 2022 to KRW 417.4 billion in 2023, an increase of 7.4%, and reached KRW 579.2 billion in 2024, up 38.7%. It then fell 20.0% to KRW 463.5 billion in 2025. Of the 2022 to 2025 period, 2024 was the year with the largest revenue.

Operating profit was KRW 22 billion in 2022, KRW 18.5 billion in 2023 (down 15.9%), and KRW 26.4 billion in 2024 (up 43.1%), before turning into an operating loss of KRW 62 billion in 2025. Operating margin moved from 5.6% in 2022 and 4.4% in 2023 to 4.6% in 2024 and -13.4% in 2025.

The company cited construction losses from higher cost ratios on major projects and one-off charges, including bad-debt provisions, as the main reasons for the 2025 decline.

For the first nine months of 2025, a financial data provider attributed the drop to slower semiconductor cleanroom recovery and display weakness, weaker dry-room orders from the EV slowdown, and lower SOC budgets hitting the bridge business.

Net profit attributable to controlling shareholders was KRW 15.9 billion in 2022, KRW 11.3 billion in 2023, and KRW 13.6 billion in 2024, then a net loss of KRW 54.3 billion in 2025. Net profit including non-controlling interests, a separate figure, was a net loss of KRW 54.4 billion in 2025.

Operating cash flow swung from KRW 40.2 billion in 2022 to -KRW 9.36 billion in 2023, KRW 19.2 billion in 2024, and -KRW 35.9 billion in 2025. Quarterly, the Q4 2025 operating loss of KRW 67.5 billion was larger than the full-year 2025 operating loss, so the loss was concentrated in one quarter.

In Q1 2026 the company returned to a small operating profit of KRW 576 million on revenue of KRW 115.2 billion. In Q2 2026 revenue reached KRW 137 billion, up 18.9% from the prior quarter, but the company posted an operating loss of KRW 5.55 billion, and the disclosure again cited cost overruns on major projects.

Q3 2026 revenue was KRW 121.9 billion, down 11.0% from the prior quarter and 1.2% year on year, with operating profit of KRW 6.84 billion.

05

Industry analysis

End demand for K-Ensol spans semiconductor cleanrooms, battery dry rooms, bio cleanrooms, and data-center cooling. In 2026 the semiconductor upcycle and expanding AI data-center investment are lifting cleanroom demand. Yongin semiconductor cluster construction and higher CDMO capacity orders are also cited as drivers.

On the battery side, slower EV demand and delayed battery investment have weakened dry-room orders and profitability. A 2024 brokerage report expected order backlogs to grow on semiconductor capex expansion in 2025 to 2026, yet cleanroom revenue also declined in 2025.

That gap can be read as the difference between order timing and revenue recognition. In cycle terms, the semiconductor side is in recovery while the battery side is in a longer investment adjustment.

Against competitors, K-Ensol was described in a 2024 report as a strong player in both semiconductor and battery cleanroom construction. Because large projects can move quarterly results through cost ratios, order size alone does not show profitability. The liquid-cooling market is still early, and that revenue is not reported as a separate business.

06

Outlook

K-Ensol's growth drivers fall into three areas. First, in semiconductor cleanrooms, a 2024 brokerage report's project schedule lists a large Nam-sa, Yongin fab program starting construction in 2026 and running through 2042.

Second, in liquid cooling, the company is supplying data-center samples with Submer and running cooling performance tests. Whether this becomes recognized revenue will show up in future order and revenue disclosures. Third, in bio cleanrooms, the company is building its order backlog to meet CDMO capacity demand.

In the near term, the question is whether the Q3 2026 return to operating profit continues. In Q2 2026, cost overruns on major projects produced a construction loss, and operating profit recovered in Q3. Whether cost ratios stabilize will drive the direction of quarterly results.

The short-term borrowing increase of KRW 45 billion decided in July 2026 was disclosed as securing operating funds. Whether that borrowing funds operations or expansion will be separated in future financial statements and cash-flow items. Recovery in dry rooms depends on when battery investment resumes.

Bull factors

Semiconductor and data-center cleanroom demand

Semiconductor upcycle and expanding AI data-center investment are lifting demand for cleanrooms, which is the main bullish factor. Yongin cluster construction and rising CDMO orders are cited as additional drivers for industrial and bio cleanroom revenue.

Q3 2026 operating profit of KRW 6.84 billion, up 88.3% year on year after returning to profit, can be read as an early sign of recovery.

Rising order intake

According to a 2024 brokerage report, new orders for cleanrooms and dry rooms rose from KRW 359.4 billion in 2021 to KRW 437.6 billion in 2022 and KRW 737.4 billion in 2023. The same report gave revenue recognition periods of three to six months for cleanrooms and one to one and a half years for dry rooms. How far post-2023 orders have carried into 2026 revenue is the test for this bullish case.

Liquid-cooling option for AI data centers

K-Ensol is supplying liquid-immersion cooling samples for AI data centers with Submer and running performance tests. Extending its cleanroom design and construction capabilities into data-center facilities is the growth option.

Exclusivity scope and the split of installation and maintenance revenue are not public, so the timing of any revenue contribution is uncertain.

Bear factors

2025 losses and cost burden

K-Ensol posted an operating loss of KRW 62 billion and a net loss attributable to controlling shareholders of KRW 54.3 billion in 2025. The losses trace back to higher cost ratios on major projects, which fed through to construction losses and bad-debt provisions.

Q2 2026 still produced an operating loss of KRW 5.55 billion and a net loss attributable to controlling shareholders of KRW 6.58 billion, bringing the last four quarters to KRW 63.8 billion in combined net losses.

Weaker dry-room profitability

Dry rooms accounted for 50% of revenue in Q2 2026, making them the largest segment. Stagnant EV demand and delayed battery investment have weakened their profitability. Through the first nine months of 2025, slower EV demand had also softened dry-room orders. Weakness in the largest segment slows the pace at which company-wide earnings can recover.

Rising leverage

The debt-to-equity ratio rose from 101.4% in 2022 to 212.9% in 2025. Total liabilities at year-end 2025 reached KRW 263.8 billion, above total equity of KRW 123.9 billion. Operating cash flow was -KRW 35.9 billion in 2025, and in July 2026 the company decided to add KRW 45 billion in short-term debt.

10

Risk factors

SOC budget and bridge segment risk

Bridge construction accounted for 11% of revenue in Q2 2026, and its results swing with SOC budget levels. In 2025, lower SOC budgets weighed on bridge results. If SOC budgets shrink or tender timing slips, bridge orders and revenue could fall further.

Movements in this segment could affect company-wide revenue separately from the recovery in semiconductor cleanrooms and dry rooms.

Bio CDMO order risk

Bio cleanrooms are a newer business at 4% of Q2 2026 revenue and are tied to CDMO capacity expansion. The company is building its order backlog to meet this demand. If CDMO expansion plans are delayed or orders concentrate among a few clients, bio orders may not build up as expected.

Because the segment is small, its earnings contribution is limited, but an order gap could slow the expansion of this growth axis.

Liquid-cooling commercialization risk

Liquid-immersion cooling is at the stage of supplying samples with Submer and running performance tests for AI data centers. If performance validation fails or does not lead to commercial contracts, the timing of revenue contribution could slip.

Because liquid-cooling revenue is not disclosed as a separate business, the size of any contracts is hard to verify externally. If the business does not move into commercial production, development effort may not translate into earnings.

Overall view

K-Ensol designs and builds semiconductor cleanrooms, dry rooms, and bio cleanrooms, and went from a revenue peak in 2024 to an operating loss in 2025. Q3 2026 brought operating profit and a rise in quarterly revenue, while net profit attributable to controlling shareholders has alternated between profit and loss.

Bullish factors include semiconductor and AI data-center demand, expanding bio orders, and the order growth through 2023. Bearish factors include rising cost ratios and bad-debt pressure on major projects, weaker dry-room profitability, and a debt-to-equity ratio of 212.9% alongside more short-term borrowing.

Liquid cooling is at the sample-supply stage, and its revenue contribution is not yet visible. With combined net losses of KRW 63.8 billion over the last four quarters, the P/E ratio cannot be calculated.
