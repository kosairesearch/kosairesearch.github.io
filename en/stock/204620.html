Report overview

Inbound Tourism Strength Meets an Overseas Expansion Test

Global Tax Free, Korea's leading tax refund operator, has seen both revenue and profit improve from 2022 through 2025 on the back of rising inbound foreign tourism, and its next growth phase now hinges on whether it can secure footholds in overseas refund markets such as Japan, Thailand and Singapore.

02

Business structure

Global Tax Free's core business is operating tax refund counters, refunding value-added tax on purchases made by foreign tourists at affiliated merchants in Korea and earning a commission on the refunded amount.

As of December 2023 the company held roughly 60-65% share of Korea's domestic tax refund market, making it the dominant player ahead of competitors such as Global Blue and Cube Refund.

Its key affiliated merchants include Olive Young and three major domestic department store chains, linking foreign tourist spending growth directly to its revenue.

Overseas, the company operates through local subsidiaries in four countries: Singapore, where it runs the sole central refund counter at Changi Airport; Japan; and France, through Simplytaxfree, acquired in 2018.

In the first half of 2026, the consolidated revenue mix by region was 88.6% Korea, 9.4% Singapore and 2.0% Japan, with the domestic business still dominant.

To expand overseas, the company has partnered with Planet, the world's second-largest tax refund operator, to pursue the Japanese market, planning to leverage Planet's luxury brand network including Gucci, Balenciaga and Saint Laurent along with roughly 400,000 merchant locations.

On the governance side, the controlling shareholder changed to GTF Holdings, which holds a 16.26% stake, in March 2026. Revenue is tied to a commission rate reportedly around 30% of the refunded VAT amount, making tourist arrivals and per-capita spending the key drivers of performance.

04

Earnings analysis

Consolidated revenue rose from KRW 49.4 billion in 2022 to KRW 93.4 billion in 2023 (up 89.2% year on year), KRW 129.7 billion in 2024 (up 38.9%) and KRW 154.1 billion in 2025 (up 18.8%), marking three consecutive years of growth from 2023 through 2025.

Operating profit swung from an operating loss of KRW 2.72 billion in 2022 to operating profit of KRW 14.8 billion in 2023, then rose to KRW 21.8 billion in 2024 (up 47.3%) and KRW 29 billion in 2025 (up 33.1%), two consecutive years of growth from 2024 through 2025.

Net income attributable to controlling shareholders stayed in the red with a net loss of KRW 11.2 billion in 2022 and a narrower net loss of KRW 7.99 billion in 2023, before turning positive at KRW 5.58 billion in 2024 and surging to KRW 34.7 billion in 2025 (up 522.5%).

Net income including non-controlling interests followed a similar path, from a net loss of KRW 14.3 billion in 2022 and KRW 7.99 billion in 2023 to KRW 6.25 billion in 2024 and KRW 35.5 billion in 2025.

The operating margin improved every year, from -5.5% in 2022 to 15.8% in 2023, 16.8% in 2024 and 18.8% in 2025, while operating cash flow turned from negative (-KRW 10 billion in 2022, -KRW 8.09 billion in 2023) to positive KRW 14.5 billion in 2024 and KRW 40.5 billion in 2025.

On a quarterly basis, revenue bottomed at KRW 29.7 billion in the fourth quarter of 2025 (down 34.0% quarter on quarter) before rising for two straight quarters to KRW 35.5 billion in the first quarter of 2026 (up 19.4%) and KRW 46.9 billion in the second quarter of 2026 (up 32.2% quarter on quarter, up 4.2% year on year).

Through 2026, quarterly profit swung more as the base effect from the sunset of the cosmetic-surgery VAT refund scheme fed through, with first-quarter 2026 operating profit falling to KRW 5.82 billion (down 21.2% quarter on quarter) before rising to KRW 9.04 billion in the second quarter (up 55.3% quarter on quarter, up 9.4% year on year).

Daol Investment & Securities noted that the sunset created a base-effect drag equivalent to 19% of prior-year revenue, but expansion in top categories such as cosmetics and department stores plus new categories drove revenue growth that exceeded this drag.

Net income attributable to controlling shareholders over the trailing four quarters (third quarter 2025 through second quarter 2026) totaled KRW 36.7 billion.

05

Industry analysis

From January to August 2026, foreign visitors to Korea reached 15.048 million, up 21.6% year on year, while foreign tourists' card spending in the tourism sector rose 48.5% to KRW 14.018 trillion over the same period, with spending growth more than double the rate of visitor growth.

Visitor arrivals in 2025 totaled 18.937 million, up 8.2% from 2019 and above pre-pandemic levels, with the Chinese share of arrivals falling from 34.4% in 2019 even as growth in Japanese, Taiwanese and American visitors drove the overall increase.

Chinese tourists, who account for 41% of total commission revenue, generated per-capita commission revenue that rose steadily from KRW 7,009 in the second quarter of 2024 to KRW 9,094 in the second quarter of 2025 and KRW 9,423 in the second quarter of 2026.

As of December 2023, Korea's domestic tax refund market was an oligopoly in which Global Tax Free held a dominant share, with Global Blue and Cube Refund splitting the remainder.

Overseas, Japan is set to fully implement a post-departure refund system from November 1, 2026, which SK Securities estimated could create a new tax refund market worth KRW 300 billion to KRW 600 billion annually.

Thailand, where foreign visitor numbers are roughly double Korea's, is seen as having significant growth potential given its still-nascent tax refund infrastructure. Singapore is viewed as a candidate to expand beyond store and airport refund operations into a central system covering refund approval and settlement.

With structural growth in the domestic market unfolding alongside regulatory change overseas, the industry's direction is shaped jointly by tourist inflow trends and government decisions on systems and tenders in each country.

06

Outlook

The company stated it expects 2026 to be a "quantum jump" year driven by overseas market bid participation and regulatory changes abroad.

Japan is scheduled to shift its consumption tax exemption method from point-of-purchase exemption to a post-departure refund system starting November 2026, and the company is working through its Japanese subsidiary alongside Planet to secure local merchants.

In Thailand, the company was included among qualified candidates in a pre-qualification bid for refund counter operations in November 2025 and submitted the required documents, with management at the time stating it expects at least KRW 10 billion in annual operating profit if selected as the sole operator.

In Singapore, a tender for selection of a refund system operator by the national tax authority was expected to be announced in the first quarter of 2026, with the company's Singapore subsidiary, the operator of the central refund counter at Changi Airport, seen as a leading candidate.

In a report dated May 18, 2026, SK Securities estimated the company's 2026 revenue at KRW 172 billion and operating profit at KRW 32.6 billion, projecting the operating margin would rise to around 19%.

The same report forecast that earnings leverage from overseas expansion would intensify from 2027 onward, further boosting growth momentum.

When announcing its 2025 results, the company set a goal of becoming a top-tier global tax refund player within a few years through large-scale expansion in Japan and overseas tenders in Singapore and Thailand.

Whether these overseas milestones proceed as planned, and whether domestic peak-season consumption continues to offset the base-effect drag, will be the key swing factors for future results.

08

Bull factors

Structural growth in inbound tourism

Foreign visitors to Korea rose 21.6% year on year from January to August 2026, while foreign tourism spending grew 48.5% over the same period, outpacing visitor growth.

Per-capita commission revenue from Chinese tourists has also risen steadily, from KRW 7,009 in the second quarter of 2024 to KRW 9,423 in the second quarter of 2026. The simultaneous improvement in both visitor volume and per-capita spending supports the underlying strength of the domestic refund business.

Entry opportunities in overseas refund markets

Japan is set to fully implement a post-departure refund system from November 2026, which could open a new market estimated at KRW 300 billion to KRW 600 billion annually. Thailand is also seen as having significant growth potential given its still-early-stage refund infrastructure relative to its tourist volume.

Multiple overseas growth paths, including the Planet partnership and bid participation in Singapore and Thailand, are being pursued concurrently.

Improving balance sheet

The debt ratio fell from 53.7% in 2023 to 31.6% in 2025, while operating cash flow turned from -KRW 8.09 billion in 2023 to KRW 40.5 billion in 2025.

In addition, consolidated retained earnings turned positive during the first half of 2026, and debt-free cash reserves have continued to build, expanding financial flexibility. Profit quality appears to be increasingly backed by cash generation.

09

Bear factors

Volatility from the base-effect drag

First-quarter 2026 operating profit fell 21.2% quarter on quarter due to the base-effect drag from the sunset of the cosmetic-surgery VAT refund scheme at the end of 2025. Daol Investment & Securities estimated this sunset created a base-effect drag equivalent to 19% of prior-year revenue. The widening gap between peak- and off-season quarterly results is a point worth monitoring.

Uncertainty in overseas bids

The main bid process for Thailand's refund counter operator selection is underway following pre-qualification, but the final outcome has not yet been confirmed. Singapore's tender for a refund system operator is similarly subject to timing and decisions controlled by its government.

The fact that much of the overseas growth story depends on decisions made by other countries' governments is a source of uncertainty.

Sensitivity of tourism demand to external factors

Inbound tourist volume and spending are influenced by exchange rates, regional travel preferences and bilateral relations, all of which are outside the company's control. There have been past instances where monthly revenue slowed as Chinese tourist preferences shifted toward competing destinations such as Japan. A convergence of such external factors could again widen quarterly earnings volatility.

10

Risk factors

Regulatory and policy risk

As seen with the sunset of the cosmetic-surgery VAT refund scheme, government changes to refund eligibility, limits or tax rates directly affect revenue and the commission structure.

Overseas, business scope and profitability in Japan, Thailand and Singapore remain fluid until each country finalizes its system design and bid conditions. The timing and scale of such regulatory changes are difficult to fully anticipate in advance.

Currency and geopolitical risk

A substantial portion of revenue is tied to foreign tourist spending denominated in other currencies and converted into won, so exchange rate swings can affect commission revenue. Geopolitical variables, such as diplomatic tensions or safety-related restrictions on group travel, can also influence tourist inflows. These factors are difficult for the company to control through its own operating strategy alone.

Intensifying competition risk

Domestically, competition over merchant networks and commission rates with existing rivals such as Global Blue persists, while overseas, large operators including global leader Global Blue and second-ranked Planet may jointly participate in bids in various countries.

New entrants, including payment and fintech firms moving into refund services, are a longer-term variable that could reshape the competitive landscape. Intensifying competition could create downward pressure on commission rates.

12

Overall view

Global Tax Free saw revenue, operating profit and net income attributable to controlling shareholders all improve from 2022 through 2025, with operating cash flow also turning from negative to positive as its balance sheet stabilized.

Through 2026, quarterly revenue rose for two consecutive quarters after bottoming in the fourth quarter of 2025, even amid the base-effect drag from the cosmetic-surgery VAT refund sunset, though quarterly profit swings widened compared with before.

The full rollout of Japan's post-departure refund system and the bid outcomes in Thailand and Singapore stand out as the key variables that will shape the next phase of overseas expansion.

However, because these overseas tenders depend on decisions by foreign governments, their timing and outcome cannot be determined with certainty at this point.

A cash-rich, debt-free balance sheet and the shift to positive retained earnings are notable developments worth watching alongside potential changes in shareholder-return policy. Ahead of any investment decision, monitoring the upcoming quarterly earnings release and the overseas bid results will be important.
