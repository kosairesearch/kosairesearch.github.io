02

Business structure

Founded in 2006 and listed on KOSDAQ, Koiz has focused on LCD protective and optical films along with functional materials built on prism-coating and light-guide-plate (LGP) molding technology. In 2007 the company set up a production subsidiary in Nanjing, China, to serve overseas volume.

Its headquarters relocated to Eonyang, Ulju County, Ulsan in July 2024. Since 2018 the company has established two subsidiaries as part of a diversification push and has run development organizations in the greater Seoul area and Ulsan.

Since 2023 it has pursued a new metal-oxide (cathode material) business, securing new sites and expanding operations toward this goal. Its core LCD-related optical materials business appears to have faced pressure from the maturing display industry and shifts among customers.

The company has stated that customer testing for the new business is underway despite weakness in the existing business, but acknowledged uncertainty over when this would translate into sales.

A detailed segment-level revenue breakdown could not be confirmed from disclosed filings, so this description remains qualitative.

04

Earnings analysis

Consolidated revenue rose from KRW 9.70 billion in 2022 to KRW 16.39 billion in 2023 and KRW 20.73 billion in 2024, before falling sharply back to KRW 12.65 billion in 2025.

The operating loss had been narrowing each year, from KRW 7.21 billion in 2022 to KRW 4.10 billion in 2023 and KRW 3.17 billion in 2024, but then widened sharply to KRW 8.07 billion in 2025. Net loss also expanded, from KRW 2.65 billion in 2024 to KRW 11.04 billion in 2025.

This combination of falling revenue and widening losses appears linked to the divestiture of a Chinese partner's business unit alongside U.S. tariff issues.

By quarter, Q3 2025 posted revenue of KRW 1.66 billion and an operating loss of KRW 2.12 billion, while Q4 2025 showed revenue of KRW 2.06 billion and an operating loss of KRW 3.95 billion, with losses widening notably in the second half.

Revenue jumped to KRW 5.05 billion in Q1 2026, and operating profit briefly turned positive at roughly KRW 0.13 billion, but revenue slipped back to KRW 2.66 billion in Q2 2026 with an operating loss of KRW 1.10 billion, meaning the turnaround was not sustained.

Operating cash flow was negative every year from 2022 through 2025, with a cash outflow of KRW 7.22 billion in 2025, indicating persistent cash burn roughly tracking the scale of operating losses.

Owners' equity fell from KRW 19.15 billion in 2022 to KRW 1.20 billion in 2025, illustrating how accumulated losses have steadily eroded the capital base.

05

Industry analysis

The LCD optical-film and backlight-unit (BLU) materials segment in which Koiz operates has reportedly faced structural growth stagnation amid the maturing display panel industry.

As large panel makers' production bases have re-centered around China, small and mid-sized Korean materials suppliers are said to be vulnerable to customer concentration and pricing pressure. Koiz itself experienced a revenue gap tied to the divestiture of a Chinese partner's business unit.

Changes in U.S. tariff policy have also been cited as affecting the broader industry, suggesting continued revenue volatility tied to supply-chain realignment.

The company has attempted to address this structural pressure by expanding into a new metal-oxide (cathode material) business, though this area is reported to require large-scale capital investment and customer qualification separate from its existing operations.

Across the broader KOSDAQ market, phased tightening of market-cap-based management-issue and delisting requirements has coincided with a wave of small, financially fragile companies—Koiz among them—entering delisting procedures.

06

Outlook

Koiz's path forward has effectively narrowed to the execution of its delisting procedure. The company was designated a management-issue stock on June 5, 2026 due to a market-cap shortfall, and as of August 27 had failed to clear the threshold on any of the 57 trading days since.

Prior to that, its market cap fell below KRW 15 billion on February 25, and after a management-issue-risk notice on April 1, a Q1 earnings-driven share price surge temporarily helped it escape the immediate risk.

However, after the KOSDAQ market-cap maintenance threshold was raised again from KRW 15 billion to KRW 20 billion in July, the company fell short of the standard once more, leading to a trading halt on September 2 and the start of clearing trading on September 3.

Clearing trading runs for seven sessions through September 11, with final delisting set for September 14. This is reported as the first delisting case since the tightened market-cap standard took effect.

After delisting, shares may still be tradable on over-the-counter venues such as K-OTC, though registration and timing have not been confirmed. Whether the company issues further disclosures on customer-testing results or the direction of its metal-oxide new business also remains to be seen.

Bull factors

Ongoing Customer Testing for New Business

The company has stated that it continued customer testing for its new metal-oxide (cathode material) business even as its existing LCD materials business struggled. It has said it is reviewing potential business models, acquisitions, and collaborations that could generate synergies, backed by continued R&D investment. However, the company itself has acknowledged uncertainty over when this would translate into sales.

Brief Q1 2026 Earnings Improvement

Consolidated Q1 2026 revenue rose sharply to KRW 5.05 billion versus the prior quarter, and operating profit turned slightly positive at about KRW 0.13 billion. This can be read as evidence that a revenue recovery has some potential to offset the fixed-cost structure.

However, revenue fell back to KRW 2.66 billion in Q2 with an operating loss of KRW 1.10 billion, so the profit was not sustained.

Repeated Attempts at Capital Structure Cleanup

In November 2025 the company carried out a 10-for-1 reverse stock split, reducing shares outstanding from about 30.61 million to 3.06 million and cutting paid-in capital from KRW 15.3 billion to roughly KRW 1.53 billion.

This was interpreted as an accounting measure to cover accumulated deficits of nearly KRW 40 billion on the balance sheet. A subsequent rights offering followed as part of continued efforts to restructure its finances.

Bear factors

Delisting Finalized, Clearing Trading Underway

Koiz's delisting was finalized due to a market-cap shortfall, with trading halted on September 2 and clearing trading beginning September 3. Clearing trading runs for seven sessions through September 11, with final delisting effective September 14.

This is recorded as the first delisting case since the tightened KOSDAQ market-cap standard took effect in July.

Long-Running Structural Losses and Revenue Decline

Koiz is reported to have posted losses for nine consecutive years since 2017. Revenue fell sharply to KRW 12.65 billion in 2025 from a year earlier, while the operating loss widened to KRW 8.07 billion.

This deterioration appears to reflect the combined impact of a Chinese partner's business divestiture and U.S. tariff issues.

Repeated Capital Raises and Shareholder Dilution

The company carried out a reverse stock split followed by a rights offering, after which the listing of new shares brought total shares outstanding back up to roughly 5.05 million. In May 2026 it privately issued a convertible bond worth KRW 1 billion, leaving room for further dilution.

Such repeated capital raises are seen as having failed to deliver a fundamental improvement in the balance sheet.

Risk factors

Delisting and Liquidity Risk

During clearing trading, the usual price-limit band does not apply, so sharp swings can recur under the single-price auction method. Once delisting takes effect, exchange trading ends entirely, and any subsequent trading would be limited and contingent on registration with an over-the-counter market. Whether and when such registration occurs has not been confirmed.

Balance Sheet Risk

Owners' equity attributable to controlling shareholders fell from KRW 19.15 billion in 2022 to KRW 1.20 billion in 2025, and concerns about capital impairment have been raised repeatedly. Operating cash flow was negative every year from 2022 through 2025, reflecting a persistent cash-outflow structure. A still-large accumulated deficit is also cited as an obstacle to balance-sheet improvement.

Business and Customer Risk

The 2025 revenue decline appears to have been directly affected by an external factor—the divestiture of a Chinese partner's business unit. Compounding this, U.S. tariff issues added further uncertainty across the industry.

In a business structure reliant on specific partners and customers, similar external factors could affect results again.
