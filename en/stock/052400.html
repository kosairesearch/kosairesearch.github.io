Report overview

Q3 Metal Card Base Effect; Platform and Overseas Base Expand

Kona I's Q3 2026 operating profit fell 32.5% year on year, but cumulative January–September operating profit rose, and metal card order recovery and the U.S. production base are the next items to watch.

02

Business structure

Kona I is a global fintech company that runs a digital identity (DID) business covering card manufacturing, a payment platform business centered on local currency and Kona Business, and the MVNO service MONA.

The DID business supplies metal cards, general cards, chip-on-board (COB) and smart cards to overseas financial institutions on the company's own chip operating system, and the company says it has an export network across more than 90 countries.

Overseas revenue rose from KRW 30.1 billion in 2020 to KRW 148.0 billion in 2025, accounting for 48% of total revenue, according to company figures reported in June 2026.

However, DID overseas segment revenue fell 51.5% year on year to KRW 25.1 billion in Q3 2026, and segment operating profit fell 58.4% to KRW 8.72 billion. The company attributed this mainly to lower shipments of high-margin mirror cards.

Metal card volumes vary widely by quarter because they follow the ordering and product launch schedules of overseas financial institutions.

The payment platform business is centered on local currency, which as of September 2026 was run in 65 local governments nationwide, with 17.1 million cumulative users and KRW 76.5 trillion in cumulative issuance.

Excluding local currency, the combined Q3 transaction value of the Kona Business, Kona Card and Kona Plate platforms rose more than 50% year on year. The MVNO service MONA passed 140,000 cumulative subscribers, and its subscriber count has grown for 14 consecutive quarters since its 2023 launch.

Platform earnings rely mainly on payment fees, and NH Investment & Securities cited 2025 payment volume of KRW 12.4 trillion and payment fee revenue of KRW 55.2 billion.

04

Earnings analysis

On an annual basis, revenue rose from KRW 242.2 billion in 2022 to KRW 280.2 billion in 2023 (up 15.7%), fell to KRW 236.3 billion in 2024 (down 15.7%), and reached KRW 309.2 billion in 2025 (up 30.8%), the largest year of 2022–2025.

Operating profit fell from KRW 48.8 billion in 2022 to KRW 33.7 billion in 2023 (down 31.0%) and KRW 33.4 billion in 2024 (down 0.8%), then jumped to KRW 88.9 billion in 2025 (up 166.1%). The operating margin moved from 20.2% in 2022 to 12.0% in 2023, 14.1% in 2024 and 28.8% in 2025.

Profit attributable to controlling shareholders fell to KRW 29.2 billion in 2023 (down 14.9%), the low point, then rose to KRW 30.3 billion in 2024 (up 3.7%) and KRW 74.6 billion in 2025 (up 146.5%), growing for two consecutive years. Net profit including non-controlling interests was KRW 74.5 billion in 2025.

Operating cash flow moved from -KRW 684.7 billion in 2022 to KRW 20 billion in 2023, a turn to positive, and reached KRW 152.4 billion in 2025. The debt ratio fell from 174.8% in 2022 to 116.8% in 2025, after 108.9% in 2024.

At year-end 2025, total equity was KRW 257.9 billion and total liabilities were KRW 301.2 billion. The quarterly path diverged from the annual rise. Q3 2025 revenue of KRW 93.3 billion and operating profit of KRW 30.2 billion were described by the company at the time as its best quarter since its founding.

Operating profit then declined for four consecutive quarters through Q3 2026: KRW 28.8 billion in Q4 2025, KRW 24.8 billion in Q1 2026, KRW 21.4 billion in Q2 2026 and KRW 20.4 billion in Q3 2026.

Profit attributable to controlling shareholders peaked at KRW 23.4 billion in Q1 2026, then fell to KRW 22.5 billion in Q2 and KRW 11.5 billion in Q3. The Q3 2026 operating margin was 28.6%, up from 25.3% in the prior quarter, according to the company.

The company attributed the Q3 decline to a high base from a large limited-edition metal card shipment in Q3 2025 and to a temporary foreign exchange loss in non-operating items after the won strengthened against the dollar.

05

Industry analysis

Demand from overseas is driven by card orders from overseas financial institutions. According to the company, Q1 is a seasonal low as shipments decline at the start of the year, and orders concentrate in Q2 and Q3.

Despite the spread of mobile payments, the company says demand for high-end metal cards is rising as cards become more premium; this is the company's own market view. Domestic demand depends on local currency budgets and local government execution.

In an October 2026 report, LS Securities said the 2027 government local currency budget proposal of KRW 1.45 trillion would rise about 26% from KRW 1.15 trillion this year, pointing to structural growth room, while noting that National Assembly review and local execution remain.

Payment earnings are sensitive to transaction volume. NH Investment & Securities argued that fixed costs are largely set, so growth in payment volume generally translates into profit growth. Stablecoin and security token offering (STO) frameworks are also cited as business opportunities.

Samsung Securities said in a September 2026 report that the company is pursuing a 'local currency 2.0' combining stablecoins and STOs with local currency. Overseas, reaching small and mid-sized U.S. banks is a stated challenge, and the company said the PSC deal was aimed at securing local customer access.

06

Outlook

In March 2026 the company announced a dividend policy for fiscal 2026–2028 to return at least 25% of consolidated net profit as cash dividends. In July 2026 it decided its first quarterly dividend since its founding, totaling KRW 11.3 billion according to NH Investment & Securities.

The brokerage estimates cited here come from an October 2026 report, prepared before the Q3 results were announced on November 4, 2026. NH Investment & Securities estimated 2026 revenue of KRW 345.2 billion, up 11.7%, and operating profit of KRW 98.2 billion, up 10.4%.

Measured against cumulative January–September revenue of KRW 232.3 billion and operating profit of KRW 66.5 billion (preliminary), reaching that estimate would require Q4 revenue and operating profit to rise well above recent quarters.

The same report projected 2027 revenue of KRW 414.6 billion and operating profit of KRW 115.8 billion.

LS Securities described Q3 2026 as a period showing a high base and a supply gap, and said that visibility on earnings growth could rise if metal card supply normalizes, North American customers diversify and local currency transaction value grows after Q4.

Kiwoom Securities said in September 2026 that metal card growth in the second half would lead to margin improvement. The company said it plans to consolidate PSC results from Q4 2026 and is also bidding for new contracts with large U.S. financial institutions.

The next items to check are the size of the Q4 metal card volume recovery and PSC's contribution to consolidated results.

08

Bull factors

Domestic metal card orders recovering

According to company figures, Q3 2026 metal card orders rose about 2.5 times year on year, and cumulative orders through September rose more than 50%, exceeding full-year 2025 order volume. Q3 shipments measured by revenue also more than doubled.

Because orders are recognized as revenue in step with customers' receiving schedules, how quickly this order flow converts into revenue after Q4 is the item to check.

Platform transaction growth continues

The company said the combined Q3 transaction value of the Kona Business, Kona Card and Kona Plate platforms rose more than 50% year on year, extending growth for 13 consecutive quarters. New corporate welfare clients expanded Kona Business, and 48 NH agricultural cooperatives adopted it for member cards. LS Securities pointed to the proposed expansion of the 2027 local currency budget as room for growth.

U.S. production base and PSC

The company completed the purchase of 100% of U.S. card maker PSC and said it now has local production capacity, with a positive customer response since the deal.

It says local production added to its in-house chip OS and export network in more than 90 countries allows it to supply metal and general cards together to U.S. financial institutions. PSC results are scheduled to be consolidated from Q4 2026.

09

Bear factors

Volatility in overseas metal card volume

DID overseas segment revenue fell 51.5% year on year to KRW 25.1 billion in Q3 2026, and segment operating profit fell 58.4%. The company cited lower shipments of high-margin mirror cards.

Because metal card revenue swings by quarter with overseas issuers' order cycles, a single quarter's high base can weigh on the full-year trend, which is cited as a bearish factor.

Slowing earnings momentum

Operating profit declined for four consecutive quarters from Q4 2025 through Q3 2026, and profit attributable to controlling shareholders fell for two consecutive quarters after peaking at KRW 23.4 billion in Q1 2026, reaching KRW 11.5 billion in Q3.

Non-operating foreign exchange losses widened the drop in net profit relative to operating profit. The company describes this as a temporary factor.

Dependence on local currency policy

Growth in the platform business relies heavily on local currency budgets and local government execution. LS Securities expects the 2027 budget to increase but noted that National Assembly review and local execution remain.

In Q3 the local currency transaction volume stayed roughly flat year on year, and growth came from higher charge amounts and fee income.

10

Risk factors

Exchange rate and foreign currency assets

The company holds dollar assets including export proceeds and funds for the PSC acquisition, so a fall in the won-dollar rate from the start of a quarter can generate foreign exchange losses in non-operating items.

In Q3 2026 the rate fell by about 180 won, and the drop in net profit exceeded the drop in operating profit. The company calls this temporary. Net profit can be volatile depending on the size of exchange rate moves.

Overseas customer concentration and integration

Overseas revenue reached KRW 148.0 billion in 2025, or 48% of the total, so the company depends heavily on orders from overseas financial institutions. When card issuance by major U.S. customers slows, shipments fall.

The PSC acquisition completed in October 2026 is expected to widen local production and sales reach, but integration results and the speed of earnings contribution can be gauged from Q4 results.

Regulatory and policy variables

The stablecoin and STO business depends on obtaining the necessary legal status and on how the framework is institutionalized. Samsung Securities described the local currency 2.0 concept, but the legal status required for participation in issuance is a future task.

The local currency budget will be set through National Assembly review, so delays in policy timing could affect platform growth expectations.

  2. Around January–February 2027 (typical timing of annual preliminary results)

  3. End of March 2027

    Statutory filing deadline for the 2026 annual report. Full-year 2026 figures will enter the filed results, and the annual cash dividend, measured against the 2026–2028 policy of at least 25% of consolidated net profit, will also be confirmed.

  4. Around May 2027

    Q1 2027 results. These will show how quickly the Q3 metal card orders convert to revenue and whether profit attributable to controlling shareholders rebounds.

12

Overall view

Kona I's Q3 2026 revenue of KRW 71.3 billion and operating profit of KRW 20.4 billion declined year on year because of a high base in overseas metal card shipments.

Preliminary cumulative January–September revenue and operating profit nonetheless rose, and domestic metal card orders and platform transaction volumes continue to grow.

On filed results, 2025 revenue of KRW 309.2 billion and operating profit of KRW 88.9 billion were the largest in 2022–2025, and the 28.8% operating margin was also the highest in that period.

By contrast, operating profit has declined for four consecutive quarters since Q4 2025, and profit attributable to controlling shareholders fell to KRW 11.5 billion in Q3.

Bullish factors are the recovery in metal card orders, platform growth and the U.S. production base; bearish factors are quarterly swings in overseas volume, non-operating currency effects and dependence on local currency policy.

Brokerage estimates from October 2026 assume a sharp Q4 recovery, so whether that is achieved is the central item to check. The next checkpoints are the size of PSC's first consolidated contribution and the recovery in metal card volume in the Q4 2026 results.
