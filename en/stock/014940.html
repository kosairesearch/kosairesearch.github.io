02

Business structure

Oriental Precision & Engineering runs two lines as a marine equipment maker: marine cranes and ship structures. Revenue is split between the machinery division, which covers marine cranes, and the structure division, which covers deck houses.

In Q1 2026, as disclosed on May 8, 2026, each division contributed KRW 28.2 billion, or 50.0% of revenue. The same quarter's revenue was 96.4% domestic and 3.6% export.

By customer, Samsung Heavy Industries was the largest at KRW 28.8 billion (51.1%), followed by HD Hyundai group companies at KRW 13.9 billion (24.7%) and Hanwha Ocean at KRW 6.0 billion (10.7%).

Revenue from Hanwha Ocean rose 58.2% year on year and revenue from HD Hyundai group companies grew 26.1%, showing a broadening customer base. A 2024 press report said more than 90% of deck house volume went to Samsung Heavy Industries.

A 2025 press report noted the company has built a record of supplying cranes for LNG carriers and specialized naval cranes. The structure business is reported to operate through its subsidiary. One domestic brokerage report described the company as supplying steadily within an oligopoly shared by three competitors.

The company is developing eco-friendly cranes, auxiliary propulsion parts for wind-assisted ships, and maintenance equipment for floating offshore wind turbines, and had registered 11 patents as of the May 2026 disclosure.

04

Earnings analysis

Annual revenue rose from KRW 131.6 billion in 2022 to KRW 157.5 billion in 2023 (up 19.7%) and KRW 207.3 billion in 2024 (up 31.6%), then was flat at KRW 207.3 billion in 2025. Among 2022 to 2025, 2024 was the largest year and 2022 the smallest.

Operating profit went from KRW 9.18 billion in 2022 to KRW 12.8 billion in 2023 (up 39.0%) and KRW 24.9 billion in 2024 (up 94.8%). In 2025 operating profit was KRW 24.3 billion, down 2.4% year on year.

The operating margin climbed from 7.0% in 2022 to 12.0% in 2024 and eased to 11.7% in 2025, so it stayed in double digits after 2024 while revenue moved sideways.

Net profit attributable to controlling shareholders was KRW 8.37 billion in 2022, KRW 8.91 billion in 2023 (up 6.4%) and KRW 26.1 billion in 2024 (up 192.4%), before falling 26.5% to KRW 19.1 billion in 2025.

Because 2024 net profit growth outpaced operating profit growth, non-operating items or tax effects appear to have contributed, and the footnotes of the annual report should be checked for detail.

Total net profit for the same years is disclosed as KRW 8.37 billion, KRW 8.91 billion, KRW 26.1 billion and KRW 19.1 billion, the same figures as profit attributable to controlling shareholders.

Operating cash flow rose from KRW 11.1 billion in 2022 to KRW 27.9 billion in 2025, and the debt ratio fell from 120.3% in 2022 to 89.8% in 2025.

Quarterly, operating profit was KRW 6.41 billion in Q3 2025 and fell to KRW 3.44 billion in Q4 2025, down 46.4% from the prior quarter, with revenue at KRW 51.9 billion (down 1.8%). Q1 2026 recovered to KRW 56.4 billion in revenue (up 8.9% quarter on quarter) and KRW 7.98 billion in operating profit (up 132.3%).

Q2 2026 revenue of KRW 59.7 billion was the largest of the five disclosed quarters.

Q3 2026 revenue was KRW 56.1 billion (down 6.0% quarter on quarter, up 6.2% year on year), operating profit was KRW 7.67 billion (down 13.0% quarter on quarter, up 19.8% year on year), and net profit attributable to controlling shareholders was KRW 4.97 billion (down 26.8% quarter on quarter, down 15.4% year on year).

Net profit attributable to controlling shareholders over the last four quarters (Q4 2025 to Q3 2026) totaled KRW 20.2 billion.

05

Industry analysis

The marine equipment sector moves with order intake at domestic shipbuilders. A 2024 press report said expanding orders at the three major shipbuilders led to fuller production lines at equipment suppliers. A 2025 press report carried brokerage analysis that rising LNG carrier orders would lift the company's revenue.

One domestic brokerage report cited rising demand for eco-friendly ships and a recovering shipping market as drivers of order growth, and pointed to rising equipment order prices as a margin tailwind. On the cycle, press reports say the shipbuilding boom has pushed production lines close to full utilization.

Because shipyard orders reach equipment suppliers with a lag, the backlog trend is a useful clue to future revenue. On competition, the company is viewed as holding a supply position within an oligopoly of three domestic players.

Samsung Heavy Industries' large share of revenue means its exposure to that shipyard's order flow is high. Revenue from Hanwha Ocean and HD Hyundai group companies grew sharply in Q1 2026, which also shows a broadening customer base. With exports at 3.6% of revenue, domestic shipbuilding orders largely drive sales.

08

Bull factors

Backlog-based visibility

The backlog at the end of Q1 2026 was KRW 307.2 billion, above 2025 revenue of KRW 207.3 billion. A 2024 press report quoted a company official saying order books were full through 2027 to 2028. Orders are also beginning to spread across more customers, which supports visibility.

Room for margin improvement

The operating margin rose from 7.0% in 2022 to 12.0% in 2024 and held at 11.7% in 2025. One brokerage report expected higher line utilization and rising order prices to lift margins further. Margin gains are one route to higher profit even when revenue moves sideways.

High-value ship types and eco products

A 2025 press report pointed to the company's experience supplying cranes for LNG carriers and naval vessels as a beneficiary of growing high-value ship orders. The company is developing eco-friendly cranes, wind-assisted propulsion parts and floating offshore wind maintenance equipment. It had registered 11 patents as of the May 2026 disclosure.

Bear factors

Customer concentration

Samsung Heavy Industries alone accounted for 51.1% of Q1 2026 revenue. A 2024 press report also said more than 90% of deck house volume went to that shipbuilder. Changes in that yard's orders feed directly into reported results.

Earnings volatility

Operating profit in 2025 was KRW 24.3 billion, down 2.4%, and profit attributable to controlling shareholders was KRW 19.1 billion, down 26.5%. Q4 2025 operating profit was KRW 3.44 billion, down 46.4% from the prior quarter. Q3 2026 profit attributable to controlling shareholders was KRW 4.97 billion, down 15.4% year on year.

Shrinking backlog trend

The backlog fell from KRW 327.7 billion at the end of Q3 2024 to KRW 307.2 billion at the end of Q1 2026. Revenue in 2025 stayed at KRW 207.3 billion, flat on 2024, so top-line growth paused. Quarterly revenue recovered in 2026, but a turn in the annual trend has not yet appeared in the disclosures.

Risk factors

Shipbuilding order cycle

If domestic shipbuilders' orders slow or delivery schedules slip, utilization in both the machinery and structure divisions is affected together. With exports at only 3.6% of revenue, exposure to domestic order changes is high. Delivery adjustments could also shift revenue timing between quarters.

Raw material costs and one-off expenses

In the cumulative 2025 Q3 results commentary, raw material price swings and one-off costs were cited as variables for holding operating profit. If material prices rise faster than order prices, margins could come under pressure. The size and timing of one-off costs should be checked in quarterly and annual reports.

Share price volatility and theme-driven trading

A 2025 analysis pointed to political theme-stock debates driving greater share price volatility. Share prices can move apart from reported earnings for stretches. Alongside business risks such as customer concentration and input costs, trading flows also affect the share price.

Overall view

Oriental Precision & Engineering held a KRW 307.2 billion backlog at the end of Q1 2026 and is building 2026 quarterly results on a base of KRW 207.3 billion in 2025 revenue and KRW 24.3 billion in operating profit.

On an annual basis 2024 was the peak, and in 2025 revenue held steady while operating profit and net profit attributable to controlling shareholders declined. In 2026, revenue rose quarter on quarter in Q1 and Q2 and fell in Q3, though Q3 revenue and operating profit were both up year on year.

Positives include order visibility, a double-digit operating margin maintained through 2025, and a lower debt ratio. Negatives include concentration on Samsung Heavy Industries, quarterly earnings volatility and the declining backlog trend.

Both sets of factors deserve equal weight, and the disclosures will show which side gains the upper hand. The next checkpoints are the preliminary Q4 and full-year 2026 results, the 2026 annual report and the Q1 2027 backlog.
