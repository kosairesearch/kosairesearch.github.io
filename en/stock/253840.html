02

Business structure

Sugentech is an in-vitro diagnostics company founded in 2011 that moved to KOSDAQ in 2019 as a technology-growth company. Built on BIO, NANO, IT and AI convergence technologies, it runs multiplex immunoblot, point-of-care testing (POCT), digital healthcare and health and beauty businesses.

Its growth strategy links these three platforms into a prevention, diagnosis and management healthcare ecosystem. Its key products are the SGTi-Allergy Screen PLUS allergy test reagent and the S-Blot 3 PLUS immunoblot reader, both undergoing clinical evaluation to improve accuracy and cost efficiency.

Government and public procurement buyers form a visible customer base: in February 2022 the company disclosed a KRW 22 billion self-test kit supply contract through the Public Procurement Service, and reports described Spain's health ministry concluding a supply deal after its own clinical testing of Sugentech products.

Overseas, the CE-IVDR-certified S-Blot 3 is being used to widen distribution in Europe and India, and the company exhibited at WHX Labs Dubai 2026. Its femtech subsidiary is extending the company into hormone data platforms, and the subsidiary hosted the Korea Femtech Summit 2026 on June 30, 2026.

The in-vitro diagnostics market is crowded, so platform strength and certification scope shape the entry conditions in each market. Product-level and segment revenue shares are best checked against the revenue breakdown in the annual business report.

04

Earnings analysis

On a disclosed annual basis, revenue fell from KRW 101.4 billion in 2022 to KRW 7.13 billion in 2023, down 93.0%, then recovered to KRW 10.1 billion in 2024, up 41.6%, before declining 6.7% to KRW 9.42 billion in 2025. Among 2022 to 2025, 2022 was the largest year and 2023 the smallest.

Operating profit of KRW 25 billion in 2022 (operating margin 24.6%) turned into an operating loss of KRW 22.8 billion in 2023, and the loss then narrowed to KRW 22 billion in 2024 and KRW 18.8 billion in 2025.

Losses have been recorded for three years running from 2023 to 2025, with operating margins of -319.1%, -218.1% and -199.9%.

Net income attributable to controlling shareholders moved from KRW 26.7 billion in 2022 to a loss of KRW 17.2 billion in 2023, a loss of KRW 15.2 billion in 2024, and a wider loss of KRW 23.3 billion in 2025.

Net loss including non-controlling interests, at KRW 23.5 billion in 2025, is a different figure from the controlling-shareholder result.

The 2022 revenue peak appears to have leaned on COVID-19 testing contracts, and a February 2022 disclosure of a KRW 22 billion self-test kit contract was reported as roughly 28% of the prior year's sales.

Operating cash flow went from KRW 30.4 billion in 2022 to KRW -12.2 billion in 2023, KRW -7.52 billion in 2024 and KRW -14.6 billion in 2025.

At the end of 2025, total equity was KRW 76.5 billion (KRW 76.3 billion attributable to controlling shareholders) and total liabilities were KRW 21.7 billion, with the debt ratio rising from 10.6% in 2024 to 28.3% in 2025.

Quarterly revenue went from KRW 1.48 billion in Q3 2025 and KRW 2.87 billion in Q4 2025 to KRW 2.35 billion in Q1 2026, then KRW 3.08 billion in Q2 and KRW 3.45 billion in Q3 2026, rising for two consecutive quarters.

Operating losses narrowed from KRW 4.84 billion in Q3 2025 and KRW 5.12 billion in Q4 2025 to KRW 4.68 billion, KRW 4.23 billion and KRW 3.29 billion across Q1 to Q3 2026.

The controlling-shareholder net loss, however, widened to KRW 4.19 billion in Q3 2026 from KRW 3.79 billion in Q2, and the four quarters from Q4 2025 to Q3 2026 total KRW 23.4 billion. Third-party compilation shows first-half 2026 revenue up 7.3% year on year, operating loss up 0.5% and net loss down 3.7%.

Industry analysis

Company-analysis sources describe the in-vitro diagnostics reagent and kit market as growing since COVID-19, as seasonal infectious disease prevalence has risen and interest in preventive and early diagnosis has grown.

Aging populations and digital technology advances are cited as drivers of demand for point-of-care testing.

Sugentech's revenue, however, has not moved in step with market growth: revenue of KRW 101.4 billion in 2022 fell to KRW 7.13 billion in 2023, which suggests shifts in infectious disease testing demand were reflected.

Whether the two consecutive quarterly revenue gains in 2026 come from recurring demand rather than outbreak-driven orders can be checked against product-level sales in the business report.

Competitively, the company stresses its combination of multiplex immunoblot and POCT platforms, but many companies compete across molecular and immunoassay diagnostics. Overseas entry depends on country-level certification and government clinical testing.

Reports of Spain's health ministry concluding a supply deal after its own clinical testing illustrate how government evaluation can lead to contracts. The CE-IVDR certification of S-Blot 3 meets the conditions for European market entry.

Precise comparisons of revenue scale and share against peers are most accurate when each company's DART periodic reports are read side by side.

Outlook

The China business centers on a local joint venture.

According to the Q1 2026 quarterly report, the arrangement covers an 800 million yuan JV stake, a 1 billion yuan milestone and a royalty of 1.5% of sales, and the JV receives production and quality-control technology for the Surearly product along with exclusive sales rights in China.

Milestone receipts and royalty size depend on Chinese sales, so whether local sales begin is the next checkpoint. Clinical evaluation of the allergy test reagent and the S-Blot 3 PLUS is under way, and how these product improvements feed into the revenue mix is a key point to watch.

In femtech, the subsidiary shared a global vision for a couples' prenatal care platform ahead of a planned US market entry. On the public procurement side, reports say the pregnancy test kit was placed on a UNICEF long-term arrangement, opening a channel to supply UN agencies for up to five years.

Whether the revenue gain in Q3 2026 continues into Q4 is the first checkpoint for the quarterly trend. It is also worth watching whether operating losses keep narrowing and whether the controlling-shareholder net loss moves in the same direction.

The next scheduled periodic filing is the 2026 annual business report, which will set out full-year operating margin and cash flow figures on the DART basis.
