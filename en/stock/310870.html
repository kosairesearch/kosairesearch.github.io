Defense and Orders Expand; Earnings Volatility Is the Key Test

02

Business structure

DYC is a automotive powertrain parts maker founded in October 2000, and its core business is precision parts that transmit power. A securities report from October 2023 cited shafts and pinions as its main products.

Customers are reported to include global Tier-1 suppliers such as GKN and ZF, which supply OEMs including GM, Mitsubishi, Porsche, Mercedes-Benz, BMW and Stellantis. Reports said three EV-dedicated parts had been confirmed, and hybrid-vehicle parts are gaining revenue share.

The company said it was selected as supplier for nine powertrain projects from 2025 through 2026, with total supply value of more than KRW 100 billion. The defense segment runs through the subsidiary DYC Dynamics, which is registered as a parts supplier to a Hanwha facility in Gumi and sells defense components.

The subsidiary was consolidated into first-half 2026 results, broadening the mix across automotive and defense. In May 2026 LIG D&A invested KRW 2 billion through a third-party rights offering, and the full amount was reinvested in DYC Dynamics' capital increase.

In September 2026 the company said it would extend supply into robotics, aerospace and data centers. A 2023 securities report explained that the company manages currency risk by linking sales to material costs and using currency forward contracts.

Competition is project-based sourcing from automakers, and the segment revenue split can be checked in the business-segment disclosures of the periodic reports.

04

Earnings analysis

Annual sales moved from KRW 120.7 billion in 2022 to KRW 122.4 billion in 2023 (up 1.5%), fell to KRW 100.1 billion in 2024 (down 18.2%), and recovered to KRW 111 billion in 2025 (up 10.9%). Among 2022 to 2025, 2023 was the largest sales year and 2024 the smallest.

Operating profit fell from KRW 9.56 billion in 2022 to KRW 9.14 billion in 2023 (down 4.4%) and to KRW 4.9 billion in 2024 (down 46.4%), then edged up to KRW 5.12 billion in 2025 (up 4.5%). The operating margin declined from 7.9% in 2022 to 7.5% in 2023, 4.9% in 2024 and 4.6% in 2025.

In 2025, sales growth of 10.9% outpaced operating profit growth of 4.5%, which pushed the margin lower. Controlling-shareholder net income was KRW 4.29 billion in 2022 and KRW 4.4 billion in 2023, fell to KRW 631 million in 2024 (down 85.7%), the smallest year, and recovered to KRW 3.67 billion in 2025 (up 482.2%).

Operating cash flow rose from KRW 4.88 billion in 2024 to KRW 13.1 billion in 2025. The debt ratio fell from 153.9% in 2022 to 103.6% in 2024 before climbing back to 143.6% in 2025.

On a quarterly basis, sales rose from KRW 28.5 billion in the third quarter of 2025 to KRW 36.5 billion in the second quarter of 2026, while operating profit reached KRW 2.56 billion in the first quarter and KRW 3.73 billion in the second quarter of 2026.

Media reported the second quarter of 2026 as a record result, citing automotive parts sales and the consolidation of the defense subsidiary.

In the third quarter of 2026, sales were KRW 34.7 billion (down 4.9% from the prior quarter, up 22.1% year on year), but operating profit fell to KRW 1.39 billion (down 62.6% from the prior quarter) and the controlling-shareholder result was a net loss of KRW 2.44 billion.

Operating profit stayed positive while the shareholder result turned negative, so non-operating items and taxes are the lines to check in the periodic report. The controlling-shareholder net income for the last four quarters (fourth quarter of 2025 to third quarter of 2026) totals KRW 4.09 billion.

05

Industry analysis

The automotive powertrain parts market that DYC serves is shifting from combustion engines toward hybrids and EVs. A 2025 media report said hybrid demand has grown faster than full EV demand amid the so-called chasm, raising the share of hybrid parts in DYC's sales.

Reports also said confirmed EV-dedicated part orders could raise the electrified share further. Demand from automakers reaches DYC through large Tier-1 suppliers, so production swings at OEMs are passed through those intermediaries.

In the auto parts sector on KOSDAQ, DYC is among the smaller companies, and its small earnings base makes profit swings larger. On the defense side, a commentator quoted in a July 2026 report said the value of the defense business is not fully reflected in the current corporate value.

The same quote argued that a re-rating should consider both eco-friendly auto parts and defense materials, parts and equipment. Inquiries for precision machining in robotics and aerospace have increased, according to the company. Competition in auto parts is decided by price and delivery at the project level.

DYC says it links defense precision machining capability to its auto parts production. The next industry checkpoint is which mix of hybrid and EV volume grows in the company's order book.

06

Outlook

In an August 2026 report, the company said it expects growth to continue in the second half. According to the same report, DYC is in talks with global automakers on new parts supply, and is expanding the vehicle models covered by existing customers and pursuing new vehicle orders.

Nine powertrain projects are reported to total more than KRW 100 billion in supply value, and the company said it is pursuing four additional projects with the aim of winning more orders within the year. The company describes these projects as a basis for stable revenue over the next five years.

In defense, DYC is linking DYC Dynamics' reverse engineering, prototyping and high-precision machining to its auto parts business. The company has also outlined expansion into higher-value future industries such as robotics. Funds reinvested in the subsidiary serve as capital for the defense expansion.

Because third-quarter 2026 operating profit fell to KRW 1.39 billion and the controlling-shareholder result turned to a net loss, the key question is whether order growth translates into margin recovery.

The outlook the company has presented centers on orders and new supply talks, and any quantitative targets should be checked in its disclosures. The next checkpoints are the fourth-quarter 2026 provisional results and the operating margin trend in the 2026 annual report.

It also said four more projects are underway with the aim of additional wins within the year. Reports described the volume as comparable to annual sales in 2025. Earnings recovery in 2025 and Q2 2026 results

Operating profit rose to KRW 2.56 billion in the first quarter and KRW 3.73 billion in the second quarter of 2026. Media reported the second-quarter result as a record. Operating cash flow also rose to KRW 13.1 billion in 2025 from KRW 4.88 billion in 2024.

Expansion into defense and future industries

The defense subsidiary DYC Dynamics was consolidated into first-half 2026 results, widening the business into two areas. LIG D&A invested KRW 2 billion in May 2026, strengthening the funding base of the defense unit. In September 2026 the company said it would extend supply to robotics, aerospace and data centers. Some observers argue the value of the defense business is not fully reflected yet.

Bear factors

Sharp profit drop in Q3 2026

Third-quarter 2026 operating profit was KRW 1.39 billion, down 62.6% from the prior quarter. The controlling-shareholder result swung to a net loss of KRW 2.44 billion, from a profit of KRW 3.03 billion in the second quarter. Sales of KRW 34.7 billion were higher than a year earlier, but profit did not keep pace.

Because operating profit stayed positive while the net result turned negative, non-operating items also need to be checked.

Low operating margin

The operating margin declined every year after 2022 and did not rebound in 2025. In 2025 sales rose 10.9% while operating profit rose only 4.5%, which pushed the margin lower. If margins do not recover as sales grow, earnings volatility remains. In 2024 operating profit fell 46.4%, making it the smallest year among 2022 to 2025.

Renewed debt burden

The debt ratio rose from 103.6% in 2024 to 143.6% in 2025. Total liabilities at the end of 2025 were KRW 77.5 billion, above total equity of KRW 54 billion. In years with small controlling-shareholder profit, fixed costs such as interest weigh more heavily. Capital raised at the subsidiary level is another point to monitor in the balance sheet.

Risk factors

FX and raw materials

A 2023 securities report said exchange-rate moves can have a sensitive effect on pre-tax profit. The company said it links sales to material costs and hedges with currency forward contracts. If the hedge scope or the linkage changes, FX effects could flow through to earnings. Raw material and freight cost swings also feed directly into margins.

Automaker production and customer concentration

Orders are awarded project by project from automakers, and supply reaches them through large Tier-1 suppliers. A 2023 report warned that a prolonged strike at U.S. automakers could hit sales. Production adjustments on specific models or projects translate directly into revenue swings. Project volumes could also shift with the pace of the EV transition.

Execution of new businesses and funding needs

New areas such as defense and robotics require both orders and capital investment. The May 2026 rights offering proceeds were fully reinvested in the subsidiary. If new businesses do not convert into orders as expected, the investment burden could weigh on earnings. How defense results are reflected through consolidation also needs to be tracked.

Overall view

DYC is broadening its business by consolidating a defense subsidiary alongside its automotive powertrain parts manufacturing. Controlling-shareholder net income recovered to KRW 3.67 billion in 2025, and operating profit rose in the first half of 2026, with media reporting the second quarter as a record.

Nine powertrain projects and a target of four additional wins within the year support the order outlook. On the other side, the operating margin declined from 7.9% in 2022 to 4.6% in 2025, and in the third quarter of 2026 operating profit was KRW 1.39 billion with a controlling-shareholder net loss of KRW 2.44 billion.

The debt ratio rose to 143.6% at the end of 2025, and total liabilities exceed total equity. Views on the defense business's value and more cautious views on margins coexist, and with a small earnings base, valuation readings are volatile.

The next checkpoints are the fourth-quarter 2026 provisional results and the operating margin and controlling-shareholder earnings trend in the 2026 annual report.
