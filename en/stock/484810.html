02

Business structure

TXR Robotics, founded in 2017, is a robotics and logistics automation company that listed on KOSDAQ in March 2025. Its largest shareholder is a logistics affiliate of the Yujin Group.

The company's core model is turnkey delivery, covering planning, design, procurement, manufacturing, installation and maintenance with in-house hardware and software. Its logistics lineup includes the FULFILIO Tilt Tray Sorter, PIVOTLE Wheel Sorter, SPING Flap Sorter and CELLUMATION Modular Conveyor.

In August 2026 it said it had completed a loop-type tilt tray sorter capable of up to 8,000 items per hour. It is pairing these systems with AI-based random piece-picking robot technology to extend automation to non-standard tasks.

Its robotics business covers autonomous mobile robots, collaborative robots, factory automation and service robots, with recent applications in fire response and in semiconductor facilities through modular lifting robots.

At a September 2026 investor briefing, the company said robotics automation had reached the 60% range of its orders. Logistics customers named in past coverage include Coupang and Samsung Electronics. At its 2025 listing, Yuilrobotics, Robotis and Hyundai Movex were used as comparison companies. The company describes its direction as a move from logistics automation toward a robotics company.

04

Earnings analysis

On a DART-filed basis, 2024 revenue was KRW 57.2 billion, operating profit KRW 5.27 billion and net profit attributable to controlling shareholders KRW 3.84 billion. Revenue in 2025 was KRW 56.6 billion, down 1.0% year on year, and operating profit swung to an operating loss of KRW 772 million.

Net profit attributable to controlling shareholders turned into a net loss of KRW 15.3 billion, and net profit including non-controlling interests moved from KRW 3.84 billion to a net loss of KRW 15.3 billion.

In a February 2026 disclosure the company attributed the operating loss to higher selling and administrative expenses, including R&D, and the net loss to temporary non-operating charges such as the recognition of a goodwill impairment.

Operating margin fell from 9.2% to -1.4%, and operating cash flow improved from -KRW 9.28 billion to -KRW 2.4 billion. The debt ratio declined from 144.9% to 71.5%, with total equity of KRW 55.3 billion and total liabilities of KRW 39.5 billion at year-end 2025.

By quarter, Q3 2025 revenue was KRW 7.89 billion with an operating loss of KRW 975 million, and Q4 2025 revenue rose to KRW 32.3 billion with an operating profit of KRW 1.84 billion.

Net loss attributable to controlling shareholders in Q4 2025 was KRW 12.9 billion, moving in the opposite direction to the operating profit. Revenue then fell 65.9% quarter on quarter to KRW 11 billion in Q1 2026, followed by KRW 10.1 billion in Q2 and KRW 10.5 billion in Q3.

Operating losses were KRW 1.24 billion in Q1 2026, KRW 1.56 billion in Q2 and KRW 1.31 billion in Q3, three consecutive loss quarters, with the Q3 loss wider than a year earlier.

Net losses attributable to controlling shareholders were KRW 1.3 billion, KRW 1.44 billion and KRW 1.52 billion across those quarters, and the last four quarters total KRW 17.2 billion. Q3 2026 revenue rose 33.7% year on year, but that growth has not yet translated into a better operating result.

05

Industry analysis

The company frames the decline of the working-age population and rising labor costs as global megatrends that sustain demand for automation in logistics and manufacturing over the medium term.

Growth in e-commerce and parcel volumes has lifted demand for sorting equipment, and the company promotes sorters capable of up to 8,000 items per hour. Domestic listed peers cited as comparisons in logistics and robotics automation include Robotis, Yuilrobotics and Hyundai Movex.

In the same machinery and equipment sector, Doosan Robotics and Rainbow Robotics could serve as comparisons for the robotics segment.

In disaster-response robotics, the company signed an exclusive domestic supply agreement for fire-fighting robots with GuoXing Intelligent in December 2024 and received KC registration for its fire-response robot in July 2026. The company has also said it supplied fire-response robots to an oil refinery emergency team.

In semiconductors, a modular lifting robot supply contract with a global semiconductor utility installer is presented as a sign of rising demand. The company positions turnkey delivery and in-house hardware and software as differentiators and has said it intends to reduce reliance on external technology and equipment.

Demand conditions look favorable, but competitive intensity and the volatility of project-based orders are variables to weigh alongside them.

Outlook

When it listed in 2025, the company said it would actively pursue new businesses including AI-based piece-picking robots, and it described commercialization as close at that time. Progress on commercialization and its contribution to revenue will be visible in future regular reports.

In June 2026 the company was named lead organization for the 2026 Ministry of Trade, Industry and Energy program supporting rapid commercialization of AI application products.

In July 2026 it said it had signed a modular lifting robot supply contract worth KRW 12.98 billion with a global semiconductor utility installer, and that cumulative orders for the year had passed KRW 20 billion.

The company said expectations for additional orders in semiconductors and advanced manufacturing are growing. In August 2026 it said it had completed development of a loop-type tilt tray sorter and plans to reduce dependence on external technology and equipment.

Overseas, it discussed cooperation on entering the Middle East with officials of the K-Industrial Hub project in Abu Dhabi in September 2026, and said that if cooperation takes shape it could secure an export base covering the Middle East, Africa and Europe.

At the same September investor briefing it outlined plans for a semi-humanoid robot and an overseas expansion strategy. In July 2026 it obtained KC registration for its fire-response robot.

The next checkpoints are how quickly orders convert into revenue, when new businesses show up in quarterly results, and whether operating losses narrow.

Overall view
