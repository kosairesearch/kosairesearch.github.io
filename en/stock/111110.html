Hojeon: Whether the profit rebound can hold

02

Business structure

Hojeon is a sports and high-performance outdoor apparel manufacturer and exporter founded in 1985, listed on the KOSPI market in 2017. Its core model is OEM, producing garments to order for overseas brands. Production is based in six factories in Indonesia and one in Vietnam.

According to a 2023 Korea IR Council report, Indonesian production accounted for more than 85% of total sales. Exports made up 95.77% of 2025 sales, while domestic sales were only 4.23%.

Its main customers are The North Face, Under Armour, Lululemon and Athleta, and a 2024 results report said these four brands accounted for about 80% of total sales. Sales tied to Arc'teryx rose sharply year on year, and new Carhartt supply added revenue, broadening the customer base.

Its product lines include functional outerwear, sportswear and uniforms, and supply of Swedish military uniforms also contributed to 2025 growth. Youngone, a peer in the same industry, had 2022 sales of KRW 3.91 trillion, far larger than Hojeon's KRW 480 billion. As of September 2026, the company had nine unlisted subsidiaries.

04

Earnings analysis

On reported annual figures, sales fell from KRW 480 billion in 2022 to KRW 441.5 billion in 2023 (down 8.0% year on year), then rose two consecutive years to KRW 464 billion in 2024 (up 5.1%) and KRW 520.9 billion in 2025 (up 12.3%). Sales in 2025 were the largest of the 2022 to 2025 period.

The company attributed 2025 sales growth to higher sales to Arc'teryx and Athleta and to a broader buyer base, and Arc'teryx supply volume rose about 758% year on year. Operating profit in the same year fell to KRW 25 billion (down 15.2%), with tariff-sharing costs linked to US tariff issues cited as a drag.

Net profit attributable to controlling shareholders fell 46.9% to KRW 12.5 billion, the smallest of the 2022 to 2025 years, while net income including non-controlling interests was KRW 11.3 billion. Larger foreign-exchange losses from higher currency volatility also weighed on net profit.

The operating margin declined every year, from 8.5% in 2022 to 8.0%, 6.4% and 4.8% in 2025. Margin compression during a period of sales growth points to cost pressure and tariff and currency costs absorbing the gains.

Operating cash flow fell to KRW 373 million in 2024, a period when inventory rose from KRW 70 billion at end-2023 to KRW 113.4 billion at end-2024.

Quarterly profit fell from KRW 14.4 billion in Q3 2025 to KRW 6.01 billion in Q4 (down 58.2% quarter on quarter), turned to an operating loss of KRW 1.43 billion in Q1 2026, and returned to a profit of KRW 4.35 billion in Q2. Q3 2026 operating profit reached KRW 20.5 billion, up 370.1% quarter on quarter and 42.4% year on year.

05

Industry analysis

Hojeon operates in an OEM market where volumes move with apparel orders from overseas sports and outdoor brands. A 2023 Korea IR Council report noted that recent results and inventory flows at major customers closely tracked the apparel business cycle.

A September 2026 data set recorded that slowing global growth reduced clothing consumption and pushed results lower. The same source showed first-half 2026 sales down 8.8% year on year and operating profit down 37.1%, while net income turned to a profit.

Where a company depends heavily on a few brands, one customer's inventory adjustment passes quickly into volume changes. Among industry peers, Youngone, Hansae and JS are large OEM players.

Hojeon's 2022 sales were smaller than Youngone's, but it has been widening its customer base toward high-value outdoor and workwear brands such as Arc'teryx and Carhartt. The sharp rise in Arc'teryx supply in 2025 shows how demand for premium outdoor products reached the company's sales.

Because production is concentrated in Indonesia, US tariff policy acts directly as a cost variable. With the operating margin at 4.8% in 2025, customer pricing power and production efficiency appear to matter more to profit than the cycle alone.

06

Outlook

The company said in July 2026 it set up a Vietnam subsidiary to raise production efficiency and respond to orders from global buyers. A company official attributed stronger cost competitiveness to the full operation of a new Indonesian factory and productivity gains at existing plants.

The company said it would strengthen its growth base by expanding new buyers and producing higher-value products. On customer diversification, the Q3 2026 results were attributed to higher Arc'teryx-related sales and new Carhartt supply.

A September 2025 securities company visit report projected Arc'teryx-related sales would exceed USD 37 million in 2026. That projection dates from September 2025 and should be read as a broker estimate at that time, not as current company guidance.

Tariffs and currency remain variables, since tariff-sharing costs and foreign-exchange losses weighed on 2025 profit. The extent of the profit recovery after 2026 depends on how much these two cost items ease.

Profitability in Q2 and Q3 2026 following the Q1 operating loss is the basis for checking whether the new-line stabilization effect is holding. Quarterly sales move sharply with customer orders, so whether quarterly recovery carries into annual results requires further quarterly reports.

08

Bull factors

Premium customer expansion and volume growth

Arc'teryx supply volume rose about 758% in 2025, and new Carhartt supply added revenue. The company said it is increasing the share of higher-value apparel production. A broader base across outdoor and workwear could reduce the effect of order swings from any single brand.

Cost competitiveness and quarterly profit recovery

Q3 2026 operating profit reached KRW 20.5 billion, up 42.4% year on year, and Q2 turned to an operating profit of KRW 4.35 billion. The company said stabilization of new factory lines and productivity gains at existing plants strengthened cost competitiveness.

If the quarterly recovery continues after Q2, it would provide grounds for reversing the decline in the annual margin.

Share price below book value and shareholder return plans

As of September 2026, the price-to-book ratio was below 1x. A September 2025 securities report viewed the maintained dividend and planned second-half share buybacks positively. The discount to net assets and the return plans are facts that can be reviewed separately from any investment judgment.

Bear factors

Lower operating margin and three years of decline

The operating margin fell from 8.5% in 2022 to 4.8% in 2025. Operating profit declined three years in a row from 2023 to 2025, and net profit attributable to controlling shareholders fell to KRW 12.5 billion. Lower profit in 2025 despite higher sales points to cost burdens.

Customer concentration and first-half sales decline

A report on 2024 results said four major brands account for about 80% of sales. First-half 2026 sales fell 8.8% year on year and operating profit fell 37.1%. If apparel consumption keeps weakening, lower orders from top customers could translate directly into lower sales.

Financial burden, inventory and borrowings

Total liabilities at end-2025 were KRW 231.8 billion, with a debt ratio of 125.5%. At end-March 2026, inventory stood at KRW 134.6 billion and short-term borrowings at KRW 154.8 billion. Given that operating cash flow fell to KRW 373 million in 2024, inventory and borrowing levels could weigh heavily on cash flow.

Risk factors

Tariffs and trade policy

Tariff-sharing costs linked to US tariff issues reduced 2025 operating profit in part. With production concentrated in Indonesia, changes in tariff policy feed directly into costs. The pace of margin recovery depends on how much of the tariff burden is shared with customers.

Currency volatility

In 2025, higher currency volatility increased foreign-exchange losses and weighed on net profit. Because a higher average exchange rate in the second half had helped sales, a reversal in direction could move both sales and profit. With exports at 95.77% of sales, sensitivity to exchange rates is high.

Customer inventory and demand cycle

A 2023 report noted that major customers' inventory flows tracked the apparel cycle. Q1 2026 sales fell 35.3% quarter on quarter to KRW 89.2 billion and turned into an operating loss.

Because quarterly sales move with customer orders, prolonged inventory adjustments at customers could make sustaining quarterly profits difficult.

Overall view

Hojeon manufactures on an OEM basis for overseas outdoor and athleisure brands. Sales grew to KRW 520.9 billion in 2025, but profitability has declined since 2022. Operating profit fell for three consecutive years from 2023 to 2025, and the operating margin dropped to 4.8%.

Quarterly results in 2026 moved from an operating loss in Q1 to a profit in Q2 and KRW 20.5 billion in Q3. Positives include customer expansion with Arc'teryx and Carhartt and cost gains from the new Indonesian factory and the Vietnam subsidiary.

Weaknesses include concentration in four major brands, tariff and currency costs, and inventory and short-term borrowings of KRW 154.8 billion at end-March 2026. The valuation sits in a low-multiple range, but whether the earnings behind that multiple last is a question for quarterly results.

The next checkpoints are the 2026 full-year preliminary results and the 2026 annual report, which will show whether the quarterly recovery carries into annual profit.
