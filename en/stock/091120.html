Business structure

Founded in 2001 and listed on KOSDAQ in 2007, EM-Tech operates across two business lines: components and products.

Per its 2025 annual business report, the components segment generated roughly KRW 193.2 billion, or 94.2% of total revenue, producing module assemblies for phones, smart watches and TWS devices, Micro Speakers, Vibration Motors, automotive electronic mechanisms and haptic modules.

The products segment contributed roughly KRW 11.9 billion (5.8% of revenue), covering e-cigarette devices, auto-injectors, Smart Home IoT devices, healthcare devices and wireless speakers.

Its primary customer is Samsung Electronics, and the company has received the MX Division's top quality award in 2022 and top-supplier awards in 2022 and 2024, plus an excellent-supplier award in 2025.

04

Earnings analysis

Annual results showed a profit in 2022 with revenue of KRW 415.8 billion, operating profit of KRW 33.9 billion and controlling-interest net profit of KRW 33.4 billion, before revenue fell 36.6% to KRW 263.5 billion in 2023, flipping to an operating loss of KRW 14.6 billion and a controlling-interest net loss of KRW 11.8 billion.

In 2024, revenue dropped further to KRW 167.6 billion (down 36.4% year on year), the smallest figure among the 2022-2025 period, while the operating loss widened to KRW 30.5 billion as losses persisted.

In 2025, revenue rebounded 22.4% to KRW 205.1 billion, and although the operating loss of KRW 19.9 billion continued, it narrowed, with the controlling-interest net loss also shrinking to KRW 11.5 billion.

As a result, the company posted losses for three consecutive years from 2023 through 2025, and the operating margin deteriorated from 8.2% in 2022 to a low of -18.2% in 2024 before partially recovering to -9.7% in 2025.

On a quarterly basis, revenue slowed from KRW 53.2 billion in the second quarter of 2025 to KRW 52.1 billion in the third quarter (down 2.0% quarter on quarter) and KRW 46.4 billion in the fourth quarter (down 11.0%), rebounded to KRW 48.6 billion in the first quarter of 2026 (up 4.7%), then fell again to KRW 40.4 billion in the second quarter of 2026 (down 16.7% quarter on quarter and down 24.0% year on year).

The operating loss widened from KRW 1.46 billion in the second quarter of 2025 to KRW 4.38 billion and KRW 10.1 billion in the following two quarters, narrowed to KRW 4.8 billion in the first quarter of 2026, and widened again to KRW 7.32 billion in the second quarter, with all five quarters recording both operating and controlling-interest net losses.

The sum of controlling-interest net losses over the trailing four quarters (third quarter 2025 through second quarter 2026) reached KRW 15.3 billion.

Operating cash flow generated inflows of KRW 29.7 billion and KRW 30.3 billion in 2022 and 2023, respectively, slowed to KRW 7.31 billion in 2024, and turned to an outflow of KRW 15.8 billion in 2025, while the debt-to-equity ratio climbed steadily from 57.5% in 2022 to 73.1% in 2025.

05

Industry analysis

EM-Tech's core components business supplies micro speakers, dynamic receivers and module assemblies for smartphones and wearables, so its performance is directly tied to demand trends in the handset market.

Etoday reported in September 2026 that the company had continued to post operating losses for 12 consecutive quarters amid weakness in the components business and the burden of new-business development costs.

The smartphone market remains a period of persistent pricing pressure on component makers due to lengthening replacement cycles and a growing share of mid- and low-tier models, a dynamic consistent with the 24.0% year-on-year revenue decline in the second quarter of 2026.

In the auto-injector market the company is pursuing as a new business, patent-holding European leaders have long dominated global supply, while the company states that domestic demand is rising rapidly on the back of Korea's biopharmaceutical industry growth, including GMP plant expansion, biosimilars, K-beauty and long-acting drug development.

The e-cigarette segment is preparing a new device through co-development with a major global player, a structure tied to demand trends in the heated-tobacco device market.

While the broader KOSDAQ electronics and electrical equipment sector has shown a recovery aided by memory and AI-related component demand, EM-Tech's heavier reliance on the smartphone end market places it on a somewhat different demand cycle within the sector.

06

Outlook

In April 2026, the company issued a zero-coupon exchangeable bond backed by its Anterogen shares, raising KRW 16.5 billion earmarked entirely for capital expenditure, with the largest share allocated to localizing and building dedicated global facilities for its auto-injector business.

According to a company official cited in September 2026, the new e-cigarette device has completed development and is nearly ready for mass production, with the customer now at the stage of deciding whether to launch it.

Having completed FDA DMF registration for the auto-injector in July 2025, the company is pursuing a strategy of growing the business domestically through collaboration with Korean biopharmaceutical companies before expanding into global markets.

The components business is broadening its application range beyond phones, smart watches and wireless earbuds into automotive electronic mechanisms and haptic modules.

However, the October 20, 2026 designation of the stock as an investment-caution issue due to trading concentration among a small number of accounts signals near-term supply-demand volatility separate from the new-business outlook.

Third-quarter 2026 results have not yet been disclosed in a regular report, and while a financial data provider estimates a release around mid-November, this is not a confirmed filing date.

The timing and scale of new-business revenue contribution, together with any recovery in components demand in the second half, remain the key variables for the next earnings trajectory.

08

Bull factors

Narrowing Loss Trend

Revenue grew 22.4% year on year in 2025, breaking a two-year decline, while the operating loss narrowed from KRW 30.5 billion in 2024 to KRW 19.9 billion in 2025. The controlling-interest net loss also shrank from KRW 44.5 billion to KRW 11.5 billion over the same period, reflecting a moderation in losses. This appears to reflect both cost efficiencies and a revenue recovery working together.

Two New Businesses Nearing Commercialization

The new e-cigarette device has completed co-development with a major global partner and is nearly ready for mass production, leaving only the customer's launch decision.

The auto-injector completed FDA DMF registration in July 2025, positioning the company's domestic platform to enter a market long dominated by leading European suppliers. Both businesses represent revenue streams separate from the existing components business, offering potential portfolio diversification.

Funding Capacity via Non-Operating Assets

In April 2026, the company issued a zero-coupon exchangeable bond backed by its Anterogen shares, raising KRW 16.5 billion without interest burden. The proceeds are earmarked entirely for capital expenditure, securing new-business investment funding even amid continued operating losses. This flexible use of strategic investment assets could help ease the financial burden.

Bear factors

Five Straight Quarters of Losses and a Sharp Q2 Revenue Drop

From the second quarter of 2025 through the second quarter of 2026, all five quarters recorded both operating and controlling-interest net losses. Second-quarter 2026 revenue fell to KRW 40.4 billion, down 24.0% year on year and 16.7% quarter on quarter. In contrast to the annual improvement trend, losses widened again on a quarterly basis.

Deteriorating Cash Flow and Rising Leverage

Operating cash flow generated inflows of roughly KRW 30 billion annually in 2022 and 2023, slowed to KRW 7.31 billion in 2024, and turned to an outflow of KRW 15.8 billion in 2025. Over the same period, the debt-to-equity ratio climbed steadily from 57.5% to 73.1%. Weakening cash generation just as new-business investment ramps up is a burden for financial flexibility.

Uncertain Commercialization Timing for New Businesses

Although mass-production readiness for the new e-cigarette device is complete, the final launch decision rests with the customer, a variable outside the company's direct control. The auto-injector has completed FDA DMF registration, but additional steps remain before commercial supply agreements can be signed.

Neither business has a specified timeline for revenue recognition, making it difficult to anticipate when they will contribute to earnings.

Risk factors

Customer and End-Market Concentration Risk

The components business is heavily reliant on a small number of major customers, including Samsung Electronics, so changes in a customer's smartphone shipment volumes or model mix directly affect revenue.

Shifts in end-market demand have been cited among the factors behind the 24.0% year-on-year revenue decline in the second quarter of 2026. A revenue structure concentrated on specific customers and product lines heightens quarter-to-quarter earnings volatility.

New-Business Certification and Regulatory Risk

Although the auto-injector has completed FDA DMF registration, additional medical-device approvals and quality-management requirements must be met before commercial supply can proceed.

The e-cigarette business is also subject to shifting regulatory environments across countries that could alter launch timing or product specifications. For both new businesses, the timing of earnings contribution hinges on decisions by regulators and customers.

Trading and Supply-Demand Volatility Risk

On October 20, 2026, the stock was designated an investment-caution issue due to trading concentration among a small number of accounts, with the top 10 accounts accounting for 44.87% of buy-side involvement over three days and a price swing of 15.95%.

With earnings still in loss territory, this concentration of trading in specific accounts could amplify share-price volatility driven by near-term supply and demand. The possibility of further exchange actions, such as an investment-warning designation, also warrants monitoring.

Overall view

EM-Tech posted losses for three consecutive years from 2023 through 2025, though 2025 marked a positive inflection with revenue recovery and narrowing losses; in 2026, however, operating and net losses continued for five straight quarters through the second quarter, reviving quarter-to-quarter volatility.

The components business remains sensitive to shifts in smartphone end-market demand, and the fact that the e-cigarette and auto-injector new businesses are both in the final stage of commercialization stands out as the key variable for the next phase.

The shift to an operating cash outflow in 2025 and the steady rise in the debt-to-equity ratio represent financial burdens, and the April 2026 exchangeable bond issuance backed by Anterogen shares can be read as a funding measure to offset this.

The October 2026 investment-caution designation, separate from earnings, illustrates near-term supply-demand volatility. The upcoming third-quarter disclosure and the customer decisions on launching and supplying the new businesses are the key points to confirm when gauging the earnings trajectory ahead.
