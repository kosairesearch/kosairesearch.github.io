02

Business structure

Daewonsanup designs, manufactures and sells automotive seats, and it reports a single business segment built around seats. At home it supplies seats for several KIA models, including the Carnival, Niro, Stonic, EV3, EV4 and Morning.

A research report from late 2023 described the company as the exclusive seat supplier for most vehicles built at KIA's Gwangju plant, which points to high dependence on one OEM customer. Overseas, it runs subsidiaries in China, Russia and Vietnam that produce seats for local Hyundai Motor and KIA vehicles.

The same late-2023 report put revenue shares at 5.1% for China, 4.6% for Russia and 4.7% for Vietnam, so the three regions together accounted for roughly 14%. The report also attributed a sharp fall in Russian sales to the Russia-Ukraine war, along with weak exports to China.

On competition, the same late-2023 report said mid-sized companies hold a high share of sales in this market. Because revenue tracks the model mix and production plans of its OEM customers, the business is directly tied to vehicle output.

04

Earnings analysis

Annual revenue rose from KRW 737 billion in 2022 to KRW 1.06 trillion in 2025, and grew for three straight years from 2023 through 2025. Revenue in 2025 was up 13.7% from the prior year, yet operating profit of KRW 70.8 billion fell 5.6%, so top-line growth did not translate into higher operating profit.

Operating profit peaked at KRW 75 billion in 2024, up 58.5% from 2023, and the operating margin climbed from 4.3% in 2022 to 8.0% in 2024 before easing to 6.7% in 2025.

Net profit attributable to controlling shareholders bottomed at KRW 35.7 billion in 2023, rose 103.2% to KRW 72.6 billion in 2024 and grew 5.8% to KRW 76.9 billion in 2025, marking two consecutive years of gains.

In 2025, net income of KRW 76.9 billion exceeded operating profit of KRW 70.8 billion, and financial data from Hankyung Korea Market showed non-operating income of roughly KRW 32 billion that year.

Operating cash flow moved from negative KRW 3.07 billion in 2022 to KRW 77.7 billion in 2023, KRW 135 billion in 2024 and KRW 110.3 billion in 2025. Capital expenditure fell from KRW 30 billion in 2024 to KRW 21.8 billion in 2025, according to the stock data portal domino.

On a quarterly basis, the third quarter of 2025 posted revenue of KRW 274.3 billion and operating profit of KRW 26.9 billion, then the fourth quarter swung to an operating loss of KRW 4.29 billion.

The first quarter of 2026 returned to operating profit of KRW 11.1 billion on revenue of KRW 235.8 billion, followed by KRW 13.1 billion in the second quarter on revenue of KRW 258.5 billion, and KRW 17.3 billion in the third quarter on revenue of KRW 241.4 billion.

Compared with a year earlier, the third quarter saw revenue fall 12.0%, operating profit fall 35.6% and net profit attributable to controlling shareholders fall 57.3%.

The specific cause of the fourth-quarter operating loss, and whether the third-quarter revenue decline reflects customer volumes or model mix, are points to verify in the quarterly report narrative.

Industry analysis

The automotive seat market in which Daewonsanup operates is structurally tied to OEM production. A late-2023 research report said the company is strongly affected by the results and cyclical swings of automakers.

The current supply list includes electric models such as the EV3 and EV4, so a shift in model mix from combustion to electric vehicles could change the composition of seat demand. The same report said mid-sized companies hold a large share of sales in this market.

That structure tends to limit pricing leverage and customer diversification for any single supplier. Overseas, the sharp drop in Russian sales and weak exports to China weighed on results in the period reviewed. Local production hubs mean volumes move with local OEM output.

Up-to-date supply and demand data for the cycle position is outside the scope of the disclosed figures, so recent quarterly sales trends and customer production plans need to be read together.

Revenue grew from KRW 737 billion in 2022 to KRW 1.06 trillion in 2025, which reflects both model-mix expansion and overseas operations. The operating margin, however, eased to 6.7% in 2025 from 8.0% in 2024, so the effect of cost pressure and volume mix on margins should be tracked quarter by quarter.

Outlook

The short-term trend is already visible in the 2026 quarterly results. Operating profit rose from KRW 11.1 billion in the first quarter to KRW 13.1 billion in the second and KRW 17.3 billion in the third, a quarter-on-quarter recovery over two quarters.

Yet third-quarter operating profit remained 35.6% below the prior-year level of KRW 26.9 billion, which shows the recovery has not yet reached the 2025 level. For the recovery to continue, revenue needs to return toward the KRW 274.3 billion level of the third quarter of 2025, or gross margins need to hold.

If revenue declines further from the KRW 241.4 billion of the third quarter of 2026, margin recovery could be constrained.

Net profit attributable to controlling shareholders fell to KRW 10.8 billion in the third quarter from KRW 20 billion in the second, so earnings quality and the share of non-operating items should be examined together.

The trailing four-quarter total for net profit attributable to controlling shareholders is KRW 55.3 billion, and the fourth quarter of 2025 was the smallest quarter in that window at KRW 5.48 billion. The most direct variable is how much of KIA's new-model and electric-vehicle volume flows into next-quarter revenue.

Overseas subsidiaries are exposed to geopolitical conditions and local sales trends, so whether the overseas revenue share rises again is another point to watch. Equity of KRW 584.5 billion and a debt ratio of 29.5% give the company a financial base for absorbing capital spending and volume swings.

The 2026 annual report will confirm full-year revenue and operating profit and explain the fourth-quarter operating loss, and the first-quarter 2027 report will allow a direct comparison with the KRW 11.1 billion operating profit of the first quarter of 2026.

Bull factors

Earnings recovery and cash generation

Net profit attributable to controlling shareholders rose from KRW 35.7 billion in 2023 to KRW 72.6 billion in 2024 and KRW 76.9 billion in 2025, two straight years of growth. Operating cash flow in 2025 was KRW 110.3 billion, above that year's net profit.

Operating profit turned positive in the first quarter of 2026 and increased quarter-on-quarter in both the second and third quarters.

Financial capacity

Total equity was KRW 584.5 billion and total liabilities KRW 172.6 billion at the end of 2025, for a debt ratio of 29.5%. That ratio had risen to 38.9% in 2024 before falling in 2025. Capital expenditure eased from KRW 30 billion in 2024 to KRW 21.8 billion in 2025, which lowered the investment burden. This financial capacity gives the company a base for absorbing volume swings.

Customer base and overseas footprint

At home the company supplies seats for several KIA models, including the EV3 and EV4 electric vehicles. It also runs subsidiaries in China, Russia and Vietnam that produce seats for local Hyundai Motor and KIA vehicles. Second-quarter 2026 revenue of KRW 258.5 billion was up 9.7% from the prior quarter.

Bear factors

Third-quarter slowdown year on year

Third-quarter 2026 revenue of KRW 241.4 billion was down 12.0% from a year earlier. Operating profit of KRW 17.3 billion fell 35.6%, and net profit attributable to controlling shareholders of KRW 10.8 billion fell 57.3%. The quarterly recovery has not yet brought profit back to the 2025 level.

Dependence on one OEM's cycle and models

When automaker results weaken or the economy slows, the company's volumes tend to fall with them. Its exclusive supply position for vehicles built at KIA's Gwangju plant means changes in model transitions or production plans flow directly into revenue. Operating profit in 2025 fell 5.6% from the prior year, and the mix of volumes may have contributed.

Reliance on non-operating items and quarterly volatility

Net income of KRW 76.9 billion in 2025 exceeded operating profit of KRW 70.8 billion, and Hankyung Korea Market data show non-operating income of roughly KRW 32 billion that year. Core operating profit alone does not fully explain net income in that year.

Quarterly results have also swung sharply, as the fourth quarter of 2025 showed an operating loss of KRW 4.29 billion.

Risk factors

EV volume ramp-up risk

The ramp-up pace of electric models such as the EV3 and EV4, which are already on the supply list, could shape the revenue trend. If EV production rises more slowly than planned, the company may not be able to offset lower volumes from combustion-engine models.

Whether KIA's new-model production plans flow into next-quarter revenue is the most direct check on this variable. The pace of the shift to electric vehicles could also change the composition of seat demand.

Overseas subsidiary geopolitical risk

A late-2023 report said Russian sales dropped sharply because of the Russia-Ukraine war, and weak exports to China also weighed on results at the time. Subsidiaries in China, Russia and Vietnam are exposed to local regulation and demand shifts, so changes in the overseas revenue share need continued tracking.

Limited pricing leverage

Individual seat suppliers may have limited pricing leverage in dealings with automakers. Because mid-sized companies hold a large share of sales in this market, it may be hard to absorb price-adjustment demands through volume from other customers.

Cost items such as raw materials and labor may not be passed on to unit prices immediately. If the timing of passing cost increases on to selling prices is delayed, the pace of the earnings recovery could also be constrained.
