Curoholdings: Losses Narrowed in 2025, Widened Again in 2026

Curoholdings cut its 2025 operating loss to almost break-even, but it swung back to an operating loss in the second quarter of 2026 and the loss widened in the third quarter. Whether the losses persist under a heavy debt load is the key point to monitor.

02

Business structure

Curoholdings was founded in 1987 and listed on KOSDAQ in 2001, operating as a holding company with three businesses through subsidiaries: coffee distribution, entertainment, and natural resources. It listed on the KOSDAQ market on June 26, 2001.

The core business is coffee, where the company holds exclusive distribution rights in Korea for the Italian premium coffee brand illy and supplies beans, capsules and coffee machines to B2B and B2C markets.

Its B2B customers are centered on premium hotels, fine-dining restaurants, department stores and golf clubs, and it is widening consumer channels through an official online mall and directly operated stores .

Coffee operations are split among subsidiaries, including Curo Traders, which imports and sells coffee and coffee machines, and Curo F&B in coffee . The illy operator highlights that it became the first Italian coffee company to earn B Corp certification .

In entertainment, subsidiary Aitworks produces dramas and has produced the series Shin's Quiz . The company's earnings commentary said expanded participation in OTT platform content production contributed to improved entertainment results .

In natural resources, Curocom Energy LLC, based in Houston, Texas, develops overseas oil and gas fields . As of September 2025, that subsidiary's assets were about KRW 17.1 billion .

The group's affiliates are linked through cross shareholdings, so the ownership structure matters when reading the holding company's numbers . The revenue weight by segment should be checked against segment information in the annual business report.

04

Earnings analysis

Annual revenue rose from KRW 67.5 billion in 2022 to KRW 83.9 billion in 2023, the largest year in the 2022 to 2025 period, and then fell two consecutive years to KRW 61.1 billion in 2024 and KRW 56.7 billion in 2025.

Operating income went from KRW 1.49 billion in 2022 to an operating loss of KRW 2.59 billion in 2023, and then to operating losses of KRW 2.66 billion in 2024 and KRW 9.25 million in 2025, which makes three consecutive loss years from 2023 to 2025.

The 2025 loss narrowed sharply, and the company attributed this to improved coffee profitability, lower non-operating losses from the closure of a subsidiary, and lower losses after an affiliate closed unprofitable stores . The operating margin moved from 2.2% in 2022 to -3.1% in 2023, -4.4% in 2024 and -0.0% in 2025.

The net loss attributable to controlling shareholders widened from KRW 2.64 billion in 2022 to KRW 6.78 billion in 2023 and KRW 16.7 billion in 2024 before narrowing to KRW 9.17 billion in 2025, and it has been a loss for four consecutive years from 2022 to 2025.

The 2023 net loss including non-controlling interests was KRW 9.78 billion, larger than the controlling-shareholder figure, so the gap reflects losses borne by minority interests.

Operating cash flow was KRW -2.92 billion in 2022, KRW -6.44 billion in 2023, positive KRW 976 million in 2024 and KRW -1.02 billion in 2025.

On a quarterly basis, revenue rose from KRW 13.1 billion in the third quarter of 2025 to KRW 15.1 billion in the first quarter of 2026, then declined to KRW 15 billion in the second quarter and KRW 13.8 billion in the third quarter.

Operating results swung from an operating loss of KRW 81.43 million in the third quarter of 2025 to an operating profit of KRW 22.51 million in the fourth quarter, then KRW 174 million in the first quarter of 2026, before turning to an operating loss of KRW 650 million in the second quarter and KRW 1.06 billion in the third quarter.

Third-quarter 2026 revenue was 5.1% higher than a year earlier, yet the operating loss widened, which means cost pressure offset the sales growth. The net loss attributable to controlling shareholders over the last four quarters totaled KRW 8.23 billion.

Industry analysis

The coffee distribution business depends on B2B accounts such as premium hotels, restaurants and department stores, so keeping those accounts and managing costs drive profitability. The illy brand emphasizes B Corp certification to maintain its premium positioning .

The key question is how much cost inflation can be passed on to prices, and one news report cited a coffee price increase in early 2026 as a profitability measure . The entertainment business depends on drama production and OTT platform content, so revenue can swing with title scheduling and platform orders.

The company's earnings commentary named expanded OTT content production as a factor in improved results . The natural resources business depends on oil and gas production, so crude and gas prices shape its earnings.

Compared with large holding companies in the same sector, this company is small and has many subsidiaries, so the results of individual affiliates feed directly into consolidated numbers.

In 2025, the closure of a subsidiary and of an affiliate's unprofitable stores were cited as reasons losses narrowed, which shows that restructuring of affiliates directly affects the bottom line . In coffee, premium brands compete both on brand and for B2B accounts.

The contribution of entertainment and natural resources appears smaller than coffee, and segment figures should be checked against the annual report.

Whether the KRW 174 million operating profit in the first quarter of 2026 came before the price increase also needs to be checked against the quarterly breakdown.

The next periodic report is the 2026 annual business report, and the next checkpoint within it is the direction of the annual operating margin and the debt-to-equity ratio. Whether the expansion of OTT content participation leads to a recovery in quarterly revenue is also a point to follow.

Bull factors

Improved coffee profitability

The 2025 operating loss narrowed to KRW 9.25 million, and the company cited improved coffee profitability as a reason . The fourth quarter of 2025 returned to an operating profit of KRW 22.51 million, and the first quarter of 2026 posted KRW 174 million.

The operating margin improved from 2.2% in 2022 to -0.0% in 2025, which supports the case for a normalization trend.

Affiliate restructuring reduced drag

Closing a subsidiary reduced non-operating losses, and closing unprofitable stores at an affiliate also helped cut losses . The fact that restructuring of non-core units shows up in 2025 results is an early sign of structural change. Whether the effect persists in later quarters still needs to be confirmed.

Expanding content business

The entertainment segment contributed to improved results as participation in OTT platform production grew . Subsidiary Aitworks has a track record in drama production . A second revenue source outside coffee could spread the cost burden across the group.

Bear factors

Losses widened again by quarter

After an operating loss of KRW 650 million in the second quarter of 2026, the third-quarter operating loss grew to KRW 1.06 billion. Third-quarter 2026 revenue rose 5.1% from a year earlier, yet the operating loss widened, so sales growth did not translate into earnings.

Heavy balance sheet burden

The debt-to-equity ratio rose from 120.0% in 2022 to 305.6% in 2024 and 340.6% in 2025. Total liabilities at the end of 2025 were KRW 70.8 billion, far above total equity of KRW 20.8 billion. One news report pointed out that the current ratio is below 100% .

Subsidiary funding and capital impairment concerns

If Curo F&B remains fully capital-impaired, recovery of the roughly KRW 5.6 billion long-term loan becomes an issue. One news report said the company avoided capital impairment through reverse stock splits, rights offerings and debt-to-equity conversion . If the loan is slow to be repaid, the financial burden on the consolidated group could increase.

Risk factors

Financial and liquidity risk

A debt-to-equity ratio of 340.6% and total liabilities of KRW 70.8 billion could translate into interest and repayment pressure. Operating cash flow returned to negative KRW 1.02 billion in 2025, which increases reliance on borrowing.

Because liquidity concerns have been raised in the press, the maturity structure of short-term debt should be checked in the annual report.

Affiliate transactions and funding risk

The profit and loss of subsidiaries and affiliates can move consolidated results sharply. In 2025, the closure of a subsidiary and of affiliate stores was cited as affecting results .

If the recovery of related-party loans and intra-group funding flows are not clear in disclosures, the range of readings on earnings widens.

Concentration and quarterly volatility

The coffee business relies on exclusive distribution rights in Korea for the illy brand. Changes in contract terms or raw coffee prices would affect costs and margins directly.

Quarterly operating results that have swung between profit and loss, such as a profit in the fourth quarter of 2025 and a loss in the second quarter of 2026, show that the stability of the earnings structure is still to be proven.

Overall view

Curoholdings is a holding company that centers on coffee distribution and also runs entertainment and natural resources businesses. The 2025 operating loss narrowed sharply to KRW 9.25 million, and the company attributed this to better coffee profitability and affiliate restructuring.

However, operating losses reappeared in the second and third quarters of 2026, and the loss widened in the third quarter.

Revenue peaked at KRW 83.9 billion in 2023 and fell for two consecutive years, and quarterly revenue has also declined for two straight quarters from its KRW 15.1 billion peak in the first quarter of 2026.

On the financial side, the debt-to-equity ratio of 340.6% and total liabilities of KRW 70.8 billion are a burden, and press coverage has raised concerns about subsidiary funding and capital impairment.

The bullish factors are improved coffee profitability, affiliate restructuring and content expansion, while the bearish factors are renewed quarterly losses and the balance sheet burden.

The next checkpoints are the preliminary 2026 full-year results, the annual operating margin and debt-to-equity ratio in the 2026 annual business report, and the operating loss trend in the first-quarter 2027 report.
