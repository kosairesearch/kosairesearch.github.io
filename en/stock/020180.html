02

Business structure

DaishinInformation was founded in 1987 as a provider of information-system construction and operations, and it had 642 employees as of March 31, 2026.

Its core model is winning information-system projects from government and public institutions, and its listed main businesses include financial software, OKnet, DS-NET and output solutions.

In the fiscal year ended March 2026, system construction accounted for KRW 133 billion, or 51.3% of sales, and other IT services, including maintenance and operations, KRW 80.9 billion, or 31.2%.

In the April to September 2026 half-year report, other IT services came to KRW 46.74 billion (51.2%), system construction KRW 25.03 billion (27.4%) and hardware products KRW 14.78 billion (16.2%).

Its R&D centers on industrial mobile terminals such as Android-based PDAs, and R&D spending for April to September 2026 was KRW 795 million, or 0.87% of sales.

Public-sector agencies are the core customers, and the procuring agency of the business that drew the past sanction still accounts for the largest share of sales. A three-year maintenance contract with Korea Land and Housing Corporation runs from January 1, 2026 to December 31, 2028 and is worth KRW 17.85 billion.

A 2025 equipment supply contract with the Public Procurement Service, worth KRW 34.27 billion to the company, was jointly won by five companies with a 28% stake attributed to DaishinInformation.

Public construction contracts, multi-year maintenance and equipment supply together build sales, so quarterly revenue depends on when deliveries are made.

04

Earnings analysis

Annual sales were KRW 228.5 billion in the fiscal year ended March 2023, KRW 256.8 billion in the year ended March 2024 (up 12.4%), KRW 202.9 billion in the year ended March 2025 (down 21.0%) and KRW 259.5 billion in the year ended March 2026 (up 27.9%), making the year ended March 2026 the largest and the year ended March 2025 the smallest in the period.

Operating profit rose from KRW 4.18 billion in the year ended March 2023 to KRW 8.84 billion (up 111.7%), KRW 9.1 billion (up 2.9%) and KRW 16 billion (up 75.6%) in the year ended March 2026, growing for three straight years from the year ended March 2024.

The company attributed the improvement in the year ended March 2026 mainly to a rise in construction project wins.

Owner-attributable net profit moved from a net loss of KRW 48.36 million in the year ended March 2023 to a profit of KRW 6.57 billion in the year ended March 2024, then KRW 5.74 billion (down 12.6%) and KRW 12.6 billion (up 119.3%) in the year ended March 2026, when net profit including non-controlling interests was also KRW 12.6 billion.

Operating cash flow went from KRW 1.76 billion in the year ended March 2023 to negative figures of KRW 6.33 billion and KRW 8.63 billion in the next two years, then turned to KRW 6.2 billion in the year ended March 2026, while the debt ratio fell from 248.2% to 185.4%.

By quarter, the fourth quarter of 2025 concentrated sales of KRW 132 billion (up 172.2% from the prior quarter) and operating profit of KRW 16.5 billion (up 653.1%).

The first quarter of 2026 turned to a loss, with sales of KRW 40.5 billion (down 69.3%) and an operating loss of KRW 2.51 billion, while the second quarter had sales of KRW 42.1 billion (up 4.0%) and an operating loss of KRW 828 million, a narrower loss.

Third-quarter sales rose to KRW 49.2 billion (up 16.9% from the prior quarter and up 1.5% year on year) and operating profit of KRW 1.53 billion swung to a profit, yet operating profit was down 30.2% year on year.

Owner-attributable net profit for the last four quarters totals KRW 11.9 billion, combining the KRW 13.9 billion profit in the fourth quarter of 2025 with net losses in the first two quarters of 2026 and a KRW 670 million profit in the third quarter.

The semi-annual report put sales for April to September 2026 at KRW 91.34 billion, up 4.9%, helped by higher public-sector maintenance sales and rental income from the Daishin IT Tower.

Quarterly swings reflect a fourth-quarter concentration alongside first-half losses, so annual figures alone do not explain the quarterly path.

05

Industry analysis

Competition in public IT is changing, in the company's view, as large corporations' participation limits on big public software projects have been eased.

The competitive axis is shifting toward technical capability and execution capacity, and the company is responding by deepening relationships with existing clients and expanding cooperation with other operators.

In cyclical terms, DaishinInformation's sales move sharply when large public-sector construction contracts cluster, and in the April to September 2026 half-year other IT services, which include maintenance and operations, made up the largest share.

On the hardware side, higher memory prices have pushed up equipment prices and added cost pressure, and the company cited this as a reason its annual operating profit could decline slightly from the prior year.

Its backlog of KRW 78.9 billion equals roughly 30% of sales in the year ended March 2026, so a gap in large contracts could widen quarterly swings. Listed peers in the same sector include Samsung SDS, LG CNS and Hyundai Autoever, which means competition within the public SI market is broad.

The company is classified as a mid-sized enterprise with 642 employees, so how it reduces reliance on a single large project relative to larger listed SI operators will shape its position. On AI, the company is pursuing learning and internalization of AI and cloud technology alongside stronger public IT consulting.

06

Outlook

In its semi-annual report, the company said it will expand contracts for building and maintaining legacy infrastructure and focus on digital and AI transformation services based on cloud and generative AI.

A confirmed large contract is the Daejeon headquarters storage DR integration project, signed on November 2, 2026 with the Public Procurement Service, worth KRW 42.31 billion, which equals a 50% share of a KRW 84.62 billion joint award among four companies.

The project's delivery deadline is March 31, 2027, with an inspection scheduled for April 14, 2027 and payment receipt scheduled for April 22, 2027. Of the KRW 78.9 billion backlog at the end of March 2026, about KRW 19 billion was reported to be reflected in sales for the fiscal year from April 2026 to March 2027.

On AI work, the company reportedly won a GPU server project for an AI data center in the defense sector and an integrated platform project for the Ministry of Government Legislation during 2026. A maintenance contract with Korea Land and Housing Corporation running from 2026 to 2028 remains a multi-year sales base.

The company expects annual operating profit for the fiscal year ending March 2027 to decline slightly from the prior year. On shareholder returns, it decided in June 2026 to acquire treasury shares worth KRW 2 billion and plans to cancel all acquired shares.

The CEO said accumulated retained earnings above KRW 30 billion would support a larger dividend than in the prior fiscal year.

Bull factors

Record sales on public-sector wins

Sales for the fiscal year ended March 2026 rose 27.9% to KRW 259.5 billion, which press coverage described as the highest on record. The backlog stood at KRW 78.9 billion at the end of March 2026, roughly 30% of latest annual sales.

A KRW 42.31 billion Public Procurement Service contract signed in November 2026 also shows that public construction awards continue.

Three straight years of operating profit growth and lower debt

Operating profit rose for three straight years from the year ended March 2024 through the year ended March 2026, and the operating margin reached 6.2%, up from 1.8% in the year ended March 2023.

The debt ratio fell from 248.2% in the year ended March 2025 to 185.4% in the year ended March 2026, and operating cash flow turned positive at KRW 6.2 billion.

Stronger shareholder returns and management stakes

The company has paid annual cash dividends for 12 consecutive years and decided in June 2026 to buy back KRW 2 billion of shares for cancellation.

In September 2026, CEO Choi Hyun-taek raised his stake from 4.27% to 5.06% through open-market purchases, and co-CEO Lee Jae-won raised his from 9.37% to 9.63% in August 2026.

09

Bear factors

Volatile quarterly operating profit

After an operating loss of KRW 2.51 billion in the first quarter of 2026 and KRW 828 million in the second, the third quarter returned to a KRW 1.53 billion profit, though that was down 30.2% year on year. Operating profit for April to September 2026 was about KRW 700 million, down 65.1% year on year. The company's projection of a slight annual decline in operating profit is also cited as a bearish factor.

Past sanctions and customer concentration

Bidding-eligibility restrictions were imposed following the 2021 and 2022 dispositions, with the 2022 case carrying a nine-month limit that reflected the earlier record. Commentary has argued that this history weighed on investor sentiment.

The procuring agency tied to the sanctioned project still accounts for the largest share of sales, so reliance on one client remains high.

Cost pressure and easing competitive barriers

Higher memory prices, which pushed up equipment prices, were cited as a factor that could lower operating profit. The easing of limits on large corporations joining big public software projects is seen as widening competition.

Hardware product sales were KRW 14.78 billion, or 16.2% of sales, for April to September 2026, a segment where equipment price changes flow through.

10

Risk factors

Bidding and sanction risk

The company describes past bidding restrictions as a latent risk shared by any public-procurement contractor. Related administrative sanctions were all lifted by a special amnesty in August 2023, and the company says no similar disposition has occurred since. The CEO said bids are now reviewed from the preparation stage to block a recurrence.

Client concentration and delivery timing

The procuring agency tied to the sanctioned project is the largest sales contributor, so budget shifts at that agency directly affect results.

The Daejeon DR project has a delivery deadline of March 31, 2027 and a scheduled payment date of April 22, 2027, so delays in inspection or payment would push back sales and cash recognition. The company says that agency's budget and project scale are expanding.

Competition and technology transition

Easing limits on large corporations is shifting competition in big public software projects toward technical capability and execution. The company is building AI and cloud capability, but sales from these newer services have not yet reached the scale of its existing construction and maintenance business. Whether the pace of transition translates into bid competitiveness is the key question.

Overall view

In the fiscal year ended March 2026, DaishinInformation posted sales of KRW 259.5 billion, operating profit of KRW 16 billion and owner-attributable net profit of KRW 12.6 billion, with all three up from the prior year.

The operating margin rose from 1.8% in the year ended March 2023 to 6.2% in the year ended March 2026, and the debt ratio also declined. Quarterly results, however, were uneven, with concentration in the fourth quarter of 2025, losses in the first two quarters of 2026 and a third-quarter return to profit.

Operating profit for April to September 2026 was about KRW 700 million, down 65.1% year on year. On the revenue side, the KRW 78.9 billion backlog and the KRW 42.31 billion contract signed in November 2026 are cited as support for upcoming results.

Shareholder returns and the rise in management stakes are documented by filings and reports, while past bidding restrictions and client concentration remain open questions.

Valuation is limited to factual comparisons, with the PBR below 1x and the PER below the industry PER, and should be read alongside loss-making quarters and client concentration.

The next checkpoints are whether the operating margin holds at 6.2% in the year ending March 2027 and whether inspection and payment on the Daejeon DR project follow the disclosed schedule in April 2027.
