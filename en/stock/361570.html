RBW Returns to Profit, Faces a Revenue Test

RBW swung to an operating profit of KRW 169 million in 2025 and posted KRW 2.4 billion in operating profit with KRW 4.3 billion in profit attributable to owners in the second quarter of 2026, but whether it can actually meet its disclosed 2026 revenue guidance of KRW 80.9 billion remains the next thing to watch.

02

Business structure

RBW is a comprehensive content company best known for producing Mamamoo, and it operates multiple labels including WM Entertainment and DSP Media. Its key artists include Mamamoo, Oh My Girl, KARD, ONEWE, and Kwon Eunbi.

In 2026, subsidiary WM Entertainment announced that it had signed a comprehensive business transfer agreement with 257 Entertainment and begun post-merger integration, gaining the intellectual property of genderless boy group XLOV. 257 Entertainment CEO Park Jae-yong newly took office as co-CEO of WM Entertainment, running the company alongside existing CEO Kim Jin-woo.

The business is divided into music and copyright, concerts, management, production outsourcing, and other segments; according to its 2026 business-year outlook disclosure, projected revenue by segment was presented as KRW 24.5 billion for concerts, KRW 22.6 billion for music and copyright, KRW 13.1 billion for management, KRW 9.3 billion for production outsourcing, and KRW 11.4 billion for other categories.

Over the past two years the company restructured by trimming lower-margin artists and subsidiaries, and filled those gaps with already profitable outside artists such as XLOV and Kwon Eunbi.

Compared with larger agencies such as Hybe, SM Entertainment, JYP Entertainment, and YG Entertainment, RBW's revenue scale is smaller, but it is distinguished by a multi-label roster spanning different genres and artist tenures.

04

Earnings analysis

Consolidated revenue rose from KRW 63.1 billion in 2022 to KRW 91.4 billion in 2023, up 44.9% and a peak at which RBW recorded revenue of KRW 91.4 billion, its highest since founding. Revenue then fell for two straight years, to KRW 62.5 billion in 2024 (down 31.6%) and KRW 46.5 billion in 2025 (down 25.7%).

Operating profit of KRW 2.82 billion in 2022 turned into an operating loss of KRW 2.25 billion in 2023, widened to an operating loss of KRW 12.2 billion in 2024, then swung to an operating profit of KRW 169 million in 2025.

The company attributed the 2025 improvement to the fact that operating profit improved on better music and album profitability and cost cuts, while a smaller goodwill impairment reduced the net loss.

Profit attributable to owners widened from a net loss of KRW 3.56 billion in 2023 to a net loss of KRW 8.64 billion in 2024, then narrowed to a net loss of KRW 4.14 billion in 2025, marking three consecutive years of losses.

By quarter, an operating loss of KRW 1.82 billion in the second quarter of 2025 and KRW 365 million in the third quarter were followed by an operating profit of KRW 2.36 billion in the fourth quarter, before reverting to an operating loss of KRW 450 million in the first quarter of 2026 and then an operating profit of KRW 2.4 billion in the second quarter.

Profit attributable to owners also rose for two straight quarters, from KRW 117 million in the fourth quarter of 2025 to KRW 174 million in the first quarter of 2026 and KRW 4.3 billion in the second quarter, bringing the trailing four-quarter total (Q3 2025–Q2 2026) to KRW 3.94 billion.

Reporting noted that cumulative first-half operating profit was KRW 1.9 billion and net income KRW 4.5 billion, both turning positive year-on-year. Still, the debt ratio climbed from 63.2% in 2022 to 168.9% in 2025, meaning financial leverage has risen even as the top and bottom lines recover.

05

Industry analysis

In 2026, the K-pop industry is seeing physical album exports repeatedly set records while revenue structures shift toward concerts, platforms, and intellectual property.

In the first half of 2026, the K-entertainment industry has been reorganizing around three new pillars: concerts, platforms, and intellectual property. Hybe, SM, JYP, and YG are all expanding income streams beyond physical albums.

According to Korea Customs Service data, South Korea's K-pop album exports reached an all-time record of US$257.48 million in the first half of 2026, up 125 percent from a year earlier, with the United States overtaking Japan as the single largest importer of Korean physical albums for the first time.

At the same time, 2025 K-pop album exports surpassed US$300 million for the first time, even as total unit sales fell for a second straight year amid a weak domestic market. This points to a dual trend of export-value growth alongside domestic sales softness.

RBW is a smaller, multi-label operator relative to the four major agencies, and it is following the broader industry shift toward concerts and IP monetization through its use of music neighboring rights and expanded overseas touring.

Within the KOSDAQ entertainment and culture sector, it belongs to a group of names with relatively high earnings volatility, with quarterly revenue swinging notably around comeback and tour schedules.

06

Outlook

RBW presented consolidated revenue guidance of KRW 80.9 billion for 2026 through a fair disclosure filed in June 2026. Entertainment agency RBW (361570) disclosed that it projects consolidated revenue of KRW 80.9 billion for the 2026 business year.

However, this projection was prepared internally based on business plans and has not been audited by an external auditor, and there is a precedent in which 2025 revenue of KRW 46.4 billion missed the KRW 50 billion guidance by an error rate of -7.2 percent.

According to reporting, given first-half revenue of KRW 24.2 billion, the company is projecting more than KRW 50 billion in revenue in the second half alone.

Drivers of the expected second-half pickup include Mamamoo's world tour across 11 countries and 18 cities that has been running since June, as well as expanded overseas concerts by XLOV.

On the funding side, RBW set up the 'KPOP Music IP Investment LLC' to purchase and source music IP, and plans to raise an additional KRW 5 billion by year-end to expand the project to KRW 30 billion, with a goal of securing a total of 843 songs of music IP (619 existing plus 224 new).

The new songs will be invested over the next three years into new album production for Mamamoo, Kwon Eunbi, Oh My Girl, KARD, and ONEWE, among others.

Whether the WM Entertainment–257 Entertainment integration proceeds as planned, and when XLOV's expanding global activity feeds through to results, are also worth monitoring.

08

Bull factors

Return to Profit and Stronger Cash Position

After swinging to a KRW 169 million operating profit in 2025, RBW posted KRW 2.4 billion in operating profit and KRW 4.3 billion in profit attributable to owners in the second quarter of 2026, an improving quarterly trend.

At the same time, the company sold a portion of its music neighboring rights, an intangible asset, for KRW 18 billion in cash during the second quarter. As a result, cash and cash equivalents at the end of the first half rose 194.1% from a year earlier to KRW 31.2 billion. This greater liquidity is relevant to the company's capacity to fund future album production.

Diversified Artist Portfolio

WM Entertainment signed a comprehensive business transfer agreement with 257 Entertainment and began post-merger integration, gaining the IP of global rookie act XLOV.

XLOV showed strong momentum, with first-week sales of its May-released second mini album reaching about 224,000 copies, up 163.3% from its previous release, while Mamamoo has been on a world tour across 11 countries and 18 cities since June. The lineup now combines an established core IP with a newly added global IP.

Proactive Disclosure and New Funding Channels

The company disclosed its 2026 revenue outlook down to the segment level, and its music-IP investment project, involving a bank, venture capital firms, and a music distributor, has secured KRW 25 billion so far.

The participation of outside financial and strategic capital such as Hana Bank and Kakao Entertainment marks a funding channel distinct from the venture-capital-led investment typical of the industry. The CEO also raised his stake to 18.69% through open-market purchases in February 2026.

09

Bear factors

Earnings Volatility and Guidance Uncertainty

Revenue declined for two straight years after 2023, and in 2025 actual revenue of KRW 46.4 billion missed the KRW 50 billion guidance by an error rate of -7.2 percent.

The 2026 guidance of KRW 80.9 billion is likewise an unaudited internal forecast, and it implies more than KRW 50 billion in revenue expected in the second half alone, concentrating the plan in a narrow window. Any delay in concert or album schedules could disrupt achievement of that target.

Rising Financial Leverage

The debt ratio rose from 63.2% in 2022 to 168.9% in 2025, and total equity fell to KRW 67.5 billion at the end of 2025 compared with prior years.

The special-purpose-vehicle structure built around neighboring rights allows RBW to reclaim those rights only under certain conditions, so future contract terms could affect cash management.

Operating cash flow also fell sharply to KRW 252 million in 2025 from KRW 21.4 billion in 2024, suggesting that core operating cash generation has not yet stabilized.

Artist Departures and Integration Risk

Over the past two years, RBW has parted ways with numerous artists including Bromance, Purple Kiss, B1A4, Youthere, Onf, and Oneus. The termination or transfer of artist contracts carries both the cost of replacing revenue sources and the risk of fandom attrition.

Organizational and cost variables that may arise from the post-merger integration with 257 Entertainment have also not yet been fully reflected in results.

10

Risk factors

Financial Risk

With the debt ratio having risen to 168.9% at the end of 2025, the company's 2026 disclosure record also includes decisions to provide debt guarantees for third parties, warranting attention to guarantee exposure tied to affiliates.

Funds raised through the sale of neighboring rights are structured around a conditional repurchase arrangement that could affect long-term cash management. Given the sharp decline in operating cash flow in 2025, the possibility of needing additional external funding cannot be ruled out.

Execution Risk

The 2026 revenue outlook of KRW 80.9 billion is an unaudited internal forecast, and there is a precedent in which actual results of KRW 46.4 billion missed guidance of KRW 50 billion by -7.2 percent.

If the integration between WM Entertainment and 257 Entertainment does not proceed as planned, the timing of related cost recognition could be delayed. It also remains to be seen whether the additional fundraising for the KRW 30 billion music-IP project and the related new-song production schedule proceed as planned.

Industry and External Environment Risk

While K-pop album exports have been strong, there is a gap between export value and sales volume, as 2025 exports surpassed $300 million even as total unit sales fell for a second straight year amid a weak domestic market.

Expanded overseas touring exposes the company to external variables such as exchange rates, local concert costs, and shifts in local demand. Because larger agencies are also expanding income streams beyond physical albums, competitive intensity in concerts and IP monetization is rising as well.

Overall view

After consecutive losses in 2023 and 2024, RBW swung to an operating profit in 2025 and continued to improve in the second quarter of 2026 with KRW 2.4 billion in operating profit and KRW 4.3 billion in profit attributable to owners.

It bolstered cash liquidity by selling music neighboring rights and launching a KRW 30 billion music-IP investment vehicle, and diversified its artist lineup by adding global rookie act XLOV's IP through WM Entertainment's acquisition of 257 Entertainment.

On the other hand, the company's 2026 revenue guidance of KRW 80.9 billion is an unaudited internal estimate, and given that 2025 guidance missed actual results by -7.2%, whether it is achieved bears watching.

The debt ratio rose from 63.2% in 2022 to 168.9% in 2025, and operating cash flow fell sharply to KRW 252 million in 2025 from the prior year, both points worth monitoring separately from the profitability recovery.

The back-loaded nature of the revenue plan, the timing at which Mamamoo's world tour and XLOV's overseas concerts translate into recognized revenue, and how 257 Entertainment integration costs are reflected remain variables for future quarterly results.

The next quarterly report and whether the annual guidance is met are likely to provide further grounds for assessment.
