02

Business structure

RBW is a content and entertainment company whose revenue spans music, album and video copyrights, concerts, artist management, production services and other businesses such as online retail.

A WiseReport breakdown for September 2025 shows copyright rights at 40.62% of sales, management at 24.20%, concerts at 11.92%, production services at 3.87% and other at 19.39%.

An AlphaSquare breakdown differs, with music and records at 30.13%, concerts at 22.55%, management at 19.18%, production services at 15.57% and other at 12.57%, so segment weights depend on the aggregation method.

The internal 2026 forecast splits expected sales into concerts (KRW 24.5 billion), music, records and copyrights (KRW 22.6 billion), management (KRW 13.1 billion), production services (KRW 9.3 billion) and other (KRW 11.4 billion), and it has not been audited by an external auditor.

In 2024, 81.70% of concert revenue came from exports, while 82.83% of music and copyright revenue was domestic, so overseas exposure differs sharply by segment. Copyright income accumulates from a catalog, whereas concert revenue moves with tour schedules and event timing.

Management revenue depends on the activity of signed artists, including broadcast and advertising appearances. The company promotes its K-POP artist incubation system as a point of difference, and its 2023 investor materials stressed a portfolio not concentrated in any one business.

Among peers, Hybe, SM Entertainment, JYP Ent. and YG Entertainment have larger artist rosters and global distribution, while RBW sits in the mid-sized tier that spreads revenue across several businesses.

04

Earnings analysis

Annual revenue rose from KRW 63.1 billion in 2022 to KRW 91.4 billion in 2023, a 44.9% increase that marked the peak, then fell 31.6% to KRW 62.5 billion in 2024 and 25.7% to KRW 46.5 billion in 2025, two straight years of decline. Among 2022–2025, 2023 was the largest year and 2025 the smallest.

The company attributed the 2025 revenue decline to the exclusion of subsidiaries from consolidation.

Operating profit of KRW 2.82 billion in 2022 turned into an operating loss of KRW 2.25 billion in 2023, a swing into loss, then widened to an operating loss of KRW 12.2 billion in 2024, before returning to an operating profit of KRW 169 million in 2025.

Operating margin moved from 4.5% in 2022 to -2.5% in 2023, -19.5% in 2024 and 0.4% in 2025.

Net income attributable to shareholders was KRW 392 million in 2022, then a net loss of KRW 3.56 billion in 2023, KRW 8.64 billion in 2024 and KRW 4.14 billion in 2025, so losses have run for three straight years from 2023 through 2025, with the 2025 loss narrower than in 2024.

Despite an operating profit in 2025, the shareholder net loss shows that non-operating items outweighed operating results. Net income including non-controlling interests was a net loss of KRW 3.82 billion in 2025, a separate measure from the shareholder figure.

Quarterly revenue rose from KRW 10.1 billion in Q3 2025 to KRW 13.6 billion in Q4 2025, fell 29.9% to KRW 9.56 billion in Q1 2026, then reached KRW 14.7 billion in Q2 and KRW 21.3 billion in Q3, two consecutive quarters of growth.

Operating results moved from an operating loss of KRW 365 million in Q3 2025 to an operating profit of KRW 2.36 billion in Q4 2025, an operating loss of KRW 450 million in Q1 2026, an operating profit of KRW 2.4 billion in Q2 2026 and KRW 1.39 billion in Q3 2026, which was down 42.1% quarter on quarter but a swing to profit year on year.

Net income attributable to shareholders was KRW 4.3 billion in Q2 2026, above that quarter's operating profit, before a net loss of KRW 246 million in Q3 2026. The last four quarters together total KRW 4.34 billion of net income attributable to shareholders.

The debt ratio rose from 63.2% in 2022 to 168.9% in 2025, and operating cash flow swung from KRW 11.2 billion in 2022 to negative KRW 1.8 billion in 2023, KRW 21.4 billion in 2024 and KRW 252 million in 2025.

05

Industry analysis

Entertainment companies tend to see revenue swing sharply with the album and tour cycles of individual artist groups, so annual results can be volatile. In an AlphaSquare comparison of 50 entertainment companies on a 2025 annual basis, RBW's operating margin of 0.36% sat above the industry average of -10.97%.

The same comparison showed a debt ratio of 168.93% against an industry average of 79.38%, placing RBW among the more leveraged companies in the group. Large agencies such as Hybe, SM Entertainment, JYP Ent. and YG Entertainment build scale through global platforms and large artist rosters.

RBW mixes copyrights, concerts, management and production services, so a break in one artist's activity can hit results more than it would at a larger company.

Music distribution income depends on platform contract terms, and a 2021 report said the company renewed a music distribution contract worth KRW 50 billion with Kakao M.

Concert revenue is linked to overseas touring demand for K-POP broadly, so the direction of overseas concert demand is a key driver for RBW, which exports a large share of its concert sales. This peer comparison uses 2025 annual figures, so the company's position within the industry may shift with later results.

Outlook

The company's 2026 consolidated revenue forecast is KRW 80.9 billion, prepared on an internal basis and not yet audited by an external auditor. A 2025 forecast of KRW 50.0 billion, prepared the same way, compared with actual revenue of KRW 46.4 billion, an error rate of -7.2%.

Adding up disclosed Q1–Q3 2026 revenue gives about KRW 45.6 billion, just above half of the forecast.

By segment, the forecast puts concerts first at KRW 24.5 billion, followed by music, records and copyrights at KRW 22.6 billion, management at KRW 13.1 billion, other at KRW 11.4 billion and production services at KRW 9.3 billion. Whether the full-year forecast is reached therefore depends largely on Q4 2026 revenue.

Q4 2025 revenue was KRW 13.6 billion, so Q4 concert scheduling and seasonality are the main variables. Q3 2026 operating profit of KRW 1.39 billion was down 42.1% from Q2 2026's KRW 2.4 billion, so whether profitability holds alongside rising sales is the next point to track.

Whether the two-quarter revenue rise continues into Q4 is the benchmark for progress against the forecast. The full-year outcome will be confirmed in the 2026 preliminary results and the annual business report.

Bull factors

Sharp quarterly revenue rebound

Revenue reached KRW 14.7 billion in Q2 2026, up 53.9% from the prior quarter, and KRW 21.3 billion in Q3, up another 44.5%. Q3 sales rose 110.2% from a year earlier. Against KRW 10.1 billion in Q3 2025, quarterly revenue has more than doubled.

Shift toward operating profitability

Operating margin moved from -19.5% in 2024 to 0.4% in 2025, as an operating loss of KRW 12.2 billion in 2024 gave way to a KRW 169 million profit. Q3 2026 operating profit of KRW 1.39 billion compared with an operating loss of KRW 365 million a year earlier. Quarterly operating profit appeared in Q4 2025, Q2 2026 and Q3 2026.

Diversified portfolio and overseas concert demand

RBW combines concerts, music, management and production services, so weakness in one line can be partly offset by others. In 2024, 81.70% of concert revenue came from exports, leaving room for overseas demand to flow into results. Concerts are also the largest line in the internal 2026 forecast at KRW 24.5 billion.

Bear factors

The company attributed the 2025 drop to the exclusion of subsidiaries from consolidation. Shareholder-level losses and quarterly swings

Net losses attributable to shareholders have run for three straight years from 2023 through 2025, reaching KRW 4.14 billion in 2025. The Q2 2026 profit of KRW 4.3 billion exceeded that quarter's operating profit of KRW 2.4 billion, suggesting non-operating items played a large role. Q3 2026 then swung back to a net loss of KRW 246 million.

Rising financial leverage

Risk factors

Dependence on artist activity

Management, concert and music revenue depend on signed artists' album releases, tours and broadcast appearances. A gap in one group's activity or a contract change can move quarterly results sharply. Even with diversification, the risk is larger in periods when a few core artists carry much of the revenue.

Gap between forecast and actual results

The KRW 80.9 billion 2026 revenue forecast was prepared internally and has not been audited. For 2025, the KRW 50.0 billion forecast compared with KRW 46.4 billion in actual revenue, an error rate of -7.2%. If Q4 2026 revenue falls short, the gap between the full-year forecast and actual results could widen.

Financial capacity and earnings stability

The debt ratio reached 168.9% and operating cash flow fell to KRW 252 million in 2025, narrowing financial room. If profits do not stabilize at a profit level, debt burden could keep weighing on earnings. Shareholders' equity was KRW 58.2 billion of the KRW 67.5 billion total equity at year-end 2025.

Overall view

RBW's revenue has fallen since its 2023 peak, but the 2025 operating profit turn and two consecutive quarters of revenue growth in Q2 and Q3 2026 show a change in the business trend.

Operating margin recovered from -19.5% in 2024 to 0.4% in 2025, and Q3 2026 operating profit of KRW 1.39 billion was a swing to profit year on year.

On a shareholder basis, however, losses have run for three straight years from 2023, and Q3 2026 still showed a KRW 246 million net loss, so quarterly earnings have not stabilized.

The debt ratio rose from 63.2% in 2022 to 168.9% in 2025, and total liabilities of KRW 114.1 billion at year-end 2025 exceeded total equity of KRW 67.5 billion, making financial burden a key variable.

The 2026 revenue forecast of KRW 80.9 billion is an internal projection, and Q1–Q3 totals about half of it, so Q4 results will set the direction.

Positive factors are the quarterly revenue recovery, the shift toward operating profit and diversified segments, while negative factors are the early stage of recovery, continued shareholder-level losses, high leverage and dependence on artist activity.
