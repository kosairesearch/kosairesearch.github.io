Dong IN Entech: Capacity Build-Out Versus Earnings Recovery

Dong IN Entech's sales grew in 2024 and 2025 and reached a record first-quarter level in 2026, but attributable net income fell for three straight years through 2025 and turned into a net loss in the third quarter of 2026, so earnings recovery remains the key open question.

  2. 2

  3. 3

02

Business structure

Dong IN Entech is an ODM manufacturer of outdoor bags and backpacks for global brands.

Of consolidated sales of KRW 133.3 billion in the first half of 2026, bag ODM sales were KRW 96.5 billion, or 72.4%, outdoor goods ODM sales were KRW 25.8 billion, and own-brand goods and others were KRW 10.4 billion, according to a August 2026 press report based on the half-year report.

Sales are concentrated in the premium outdoor brand Arc'teryx and the largest customer Gregory, with brokerage reports from 2025 putting Gregory at 15 to 16 percent and Arc'teryx at 11.9 percent. A brokerage said Gregory orders, which had fallen on excess inventory in 2024, improved from 2025.

A new customer was added, and a March 2025 brokerage report expected its revenue contribution to exceed KRW 10 billion in 2026.

On the production side, a new plant in Batan, the Philippines, dedicated to the US customer Cotopaxi broke ground in November 2025, and the company plans to raise current capacity of roughly KRW 300 billion by more than 50 percent by 2027.

The company set up a tumbler production unit, SPIKE MANUFACTURING CORP., in the Philippines in March 2025, and plans to complete the local plant and start trial production in the second half of 2026 before full sales efforts from 2027. Supply of outdoor special products is scheduled to begin in the second half of 2026.

Competition centers on production-site choices by global brands, and a brokerage assessed that Philippine production is less exposed to US tariffs than China or Vietnam.

04

Earnings analysis

On a reported basis, sales fell 13.8% from KRW 250.6 billion in 2022 to KRW 216.1 billion in 2023, then rose 4.9% to KRW 226.7 billion in 2024 and 7.7% to KRW 244.1 billion in 2025.

Operating profit went from KRW 42.7 billion in 2022 to KRW 28.3 billion in 2023 (down 33.8%) and KRW 21.1 billion in 2024 (down 25.3%), then rebounded to KRW 22.6 billion in 2025 (up 7.0%). The operating margin was 17.0% in 2022, 13.1% in 2023, and 9.3% in both 2024 and 2025, so sales growth has not restored margins.

Net income attributable to controlling shareholders fell from KRW 28.3 billion in 2022 to KRW 20.4 billion in 2023 (down 28.1%), KRW 17.4 billion in 2024 (down 14.7%), and KRW 11.5 billion in 2025 (down 33.7%), a decline for three consecutive years from 2023 through 2025.

Total net income including non-controlling interests was KRW 11 billion in 2025, and the company attributed the change mainly to lower foreign exchange gains and higher foreign exchange losses. The debt-to-equity ratio fell from 284.6% in 2022 to 63.7% in 2023, then rose to 95.9% in 2024 and 97.7% in 2025.

At the end of 2025, total equity was KRW 152.5 billion, of which KRW 152.2 billion belonged to controlling shareholders, and total liabilities were KRW 149 billion.

Operating cash flow was KRW 26.7 billion in 2022 and KRW 10.5 billion in 2023, turned to a net outflow of -KRW 1.06 billion in 2024, and recovered to KRW 20.8 billion in 2025.

Quarterly, sales in the fourth quarter of 2025 reached KRW 69.7 billion (up 27.5% from the prior quarter), operating profit KRW 8.51 billion (up 68.8%), and attributable net income KRW 6.78 billion (up 63.9%).

In the first quarter of 2026, sales were KRW 62 billion, operating profit KRW 6.47 billion (down 24.0% from the prior quarter), and attributable net income KRW 6.1 billion.

The second quarter recovered to sales of KRW 71.3 billion, operating profit of KRW 9.03 billion (up 39.5%), and attributable net income of KRW 8.47 billion.

In the third quarter of 2026, sales were KRW 60.5 billion (up 10.7% year on year) and operating profit KRW 5.66 billion (up 12.2%), but attributable net loss was KRW 3.38 billion, a swing into deficit from a year earlier.

The gap between rising operating profit and a net loss can be traced to non-operating items and income tax in the third-quarter report.

05

Industry analysis

Demand for outdoor bags and backpacks is sensitive to inventory adjustments and seasonality at global brands. In 2024, amid global inflation and high interest rates, some customers adjusted inventory levels and orders fell, and the company said production orders returned to earlier levels from the second half of 2024.

The outdoor bag industry typically sees the first quarter as a slow season, yet a May 2026 press report said the company posted a relatively high margin in the first quarter, helped by the opening of the Chinese outdoor market and wider global orders.

Tariffs were a major variable for 2025 results, and a brokerage said advance orders in the second quarter of 2025 were not large because of tariffs. The same brokerage viewed Philippine production as less exposed to tariffs than China or Vietnam, which could support order competitiveness.

Some observers expect a windfall because global tumbler production is concentrated in China. The company cited an external research estimate that the global backpack market will reach about KRW 40 trillion by 2030.

Among sector peers, Youngone and Hansae compete in global brand contract manufacturing with an apparel-OEM focus, whereas Dong IN Entech is distinguished by its high share of outdoor bags and backpacks.

Whether customer diversification and production-site spread can cushion cyclical swings will become clearer through utilization trends from 2027.

Outlook

The company said on May 15, 2026 that first-quarter 2026 sales of KRW 62 billion were a record for a first quarter. A company official said the company would keep its growth momentum in the second half based on secured customer orders.

Third-quarter 2026 sales were KRW 60.5 billion, up 10.7% year on year, and operating profit rose 12.2% from a year earlier. Supply of outdoor special products is scheduled to begin in the second half of 2026, and the company expects new revenue within the year, according to an August 2026 press report.

The Philippine tumbler plant is set to complete its local build and begin trial production in the second half of 2026, with full sales efforts from 2027. The company also announced plans to add new plants in 2026 and 2027, per a November 2025 press release.

Cumulative capital expenditure through the third quarter of 2025 was KRW 11.3 billion. At the March 2026 shareholders' meeting, the company added tumbler and footwear manufacturing to its business purposes, creating a basis to widen its product range beyond backpacks and outdoor gear.

As of a March 2025 press report, a plan was presented to use KRW 46.8 billion of reserves for tax-free dividends. The next checkpoints are how fourth-quarter 2026 sales compare with KRW 69.7 billion in the fourth quarter of 2025, and the full-year operating margin in the 2026 annual report.

A dedicated plant for Cotopaxi is expanding capacity, and the company set a goal of raising capacity by more than 50% by 2027. The tumbler unit plans trial production in the second half of 2026 and full sales from 2027. Supply of outdoor special products starts in the second half of 2026.

Financial buffer and dividend reserves

Operating cash flow swung from a net outflow of -KRW 1.06 billion in 2024 to KRW 20.8 billion in 2025. Bear factors

Weak margins and falling net income

The operating margin fell from 17.0% in 2022 to 9.3% in 2025 and has stayed at that level since 2024. Earnings have not kept pace with sales growth, which is the main bearish point.

Swing to a net loss in the third quarter of 2026

Attributable net loss in the third quarter of 2026 was KRW 3.38 billion, a swing into deficit both sequentially and from a year earlier. Operating profit rose to KRW 5.66 billion, yet the bottom line was a loss. Currency gains and losses, which the company cited for the 2025 decline, add to earnings volatility.

Customer concentration and tariff exposure

Gregory accounts for 15 to 16 percent and Arc'teryx for 11.9 percent of sales according to 2025 brokerage reports, so dependence on a few customers is high. A brokerage noted that tariffs kept second-quarter advance orders small in 2025. A drop in orders from a major customer would quickly affect utilization and results.

10

Risk factors

Foreign exchange

The company cited lower foreign exchange gains and higher foreign exchange losses as the main reason for the 2025 decline in net income. Given its export-oriented model, non-operating results can swing sharply with the won exchange rate. This adds volatility to attributable net income and should be read alongside quarterly results.

Tariffs and customer concentration

Changes in tariff policy could reshape customers' production-site choices and advance-order sizes. High exposure to Gregory and Arc'teryx means one customer's inventory adjustment quickly becomes a revenue swing. Even with diversification, dependence on top customers is unlikely to fall quickly.

Capacity execution and utilization

The company targets more than 50 percent capacity growth by 2027, but start-up timing for the new plants and the tumbler unit may shift. If new capacity does not fill, depreciation could weigh on margins. With the debt-to-equity ratio at 97.7% in 2025, the pace of investment should be read alongside financial burden.

Overall view

Dong IN Entech shows a visible sales recovery, with revenue rising in both 2024 and 2025 and first-quarter 2026 sales reaching a first-quarter record. However, the operating margin has stayed at 9.3% since 2024, and attributable net income fell for three consecutive years through 2025.

In the third quarter of 2026, operating profit rose yet the company swung to an attributable net loss. The central question is whether capacity expansion in the Philippines and the new tumbler and outdoor special product lines, which start operating from the second half of 2026, translate into margin recovery.

Market capitalization sits below controlling shareholders' equity, and the debt-to-equity ratio, though lower than in 2022, rose again to 97.7% in 2025. The next checkpoints are the operating margin in the fourth quarter of 2026 and the full-year 2026 annual report, along with the drivers of the third-quarter net loss.
