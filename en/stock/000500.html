Gaon Cable: Order Backlog Expansion Meets Profit Recovery

Gaon Cable's revenue and profit rose together after 2025, supported by US AI data center power infrastructure demand and domestic renewable-energy transmission orders, and quarterly operating profit has grown for three straight quarters through the third quarter of 2026.

02

Business structure

Gaon Cable makes power infrastructure products including distribution cable, extra-high-voltage cable and busduct, and its scope is widening from internal distribution networks within generation sites to the high-voltage segments that connect them to external transmission grids.

A 154kV extra-high-voltage cable supply and installation contract for the Saemangeum floating solar project, a 73.2MW installation announced on November 6, 2026, extends the company's role from internal distribution cable it had already won to external transmission.

Outside Korea, the US subsidiary LSCUS is the core growth vehicle, as rising US AI data center investment has lifted demand for busduct and distribution cable.

LSCUS has signed long-term supply agreements with global big tech companies and recently won a distribution cable contract worth roughly KRW 120 billion from a global cloud company.

According to LS Cable & System's announcement on November 11, 2026, the backlog of Gaon Cable's group rose from KRW 194.1 billion at end-2025 to KRW 833.4 billion, and more than 80% of that sits with LSCUS.

The company describes itself as moving beyond cable manufacturing toward a supplier of power infrastructure for AI data centers. Its customers span US data center and cloud operators and domestic renewable-energy project developers.

In extra-high-voltage and subsea segments it competes with cable makers such as Taihan Cable & Solution, and within the sector it is benchmarked against LS ELECTRIC, Hyosung Heavy Industries and HD Hyundai Electric.

04

Earnings analysis

On an annual basis, revenue rose from KRW 1.42 trillion in 2022 to KRW 1.5 trillion in 2023 (up 5.8% year on year), KRW 1.73 trillion in 2024 (up 15.2%) and KRW 2.55 trillion in 2025 (up 47.4%).

Revenue, operating profit and net income attributable to controlling shareholders each increased for three consecutive years from 2023 through 2025. Operating profit moved from KRW 28.5 billion in 2022 to KRW 43.7 billion in 2023 (up 53.6%), KRW 45 billion in 2024 (up 3.0%) and KRW 79.2 billion in 2025 (up 75.9%).

Operating margin was 2.0% in 2022, 2.9% in 2023, 2.6% in 2024 and 3.1% in 2025. In 2024 revenue grew 15.2% while margin slipped from 2.9% to 2.6%, and 2025 marked the highest margin in the period.

Net income attributable to controlling shareholders rose from KRW 10.5 billion in 2022 to KRW 17.9 billion, KRW 25.4 billion and KRW 51.4 billion in 2025, which was up 102.7%. Net income including non-controlling interests is disclosed at the same amounts in each year.

Operating cash flow was KRW 25.5 billion in 2022, KRW 19.4 billion in 2023, KRW 39.3 billion in 2024 and KRW 41.7 billion in 2025. The debt-to-equity ratio was 148.2% in 2022, 142.2% in 2023 and 129.9% in 2024, then climbed to 184.8% in 2025.

Quarterly revenue fell from KRW 649.4 billion in the third quarter of 2025 to KRW 613.7 billion in the fourth quarter (down 5.5% quarter on quarter), then rose to KRW 763.6 billion, KRW 869.4 billion and KRW 986.5 billion in the first three quarters of 2026, marking three consecutive quarterly increases.

Operating profit bottomed at KRW 7.95 billion in the fourth quarter of 2025 (down 69.6% quarter on quarter), then rose to KRW 27.8 billion, KRW 36.1 billion and KRW 52 billion across the first three quarters of 2026, again three consecutive increases.

Third-quarter 2026 operating profit grew 99.1% year on year, faster than the 51.9% revenue growth. Net income attributable to controlling shareholders reached KRW 30 billion in the third quarter of 2026, up 60.6% year on year, and totaled KRW 75.6 billion over the last four quarters.

05

Industry analysis

The main demand driver is the expansion of US AI data center investment, with rising needs for in-facility distribution and power stabilization equipment lifting demand for busduct and distribution cable.

In Korea, transmission networks for renewable-energy generation sites are emerging as a new demand source, and the Saemangeum floating solar project is part of a program planned at 2.1GW in total. Measured by industry cycle, power infrastructure investment is in an expansion phase.

LS Cable & System reported group-level backlog of KRW 9.85 trillion at the end of the third quarter of 2026, up 20.2% from end-2025, and industry observers read this as a restructuring of the business mix rather than simple growth in cable demand.

Daishin Securities described AI data center distribution equipment demand as being in an early stage and pointed to high entry barriers and track records in high-power industries as advantages.

Relative to peers, Gaon Cable combines power cable manufacturing with US AI data center distribution and busduct, so its growth drivers are split across product lines. Separating Gaon Cable's standalone competitiveness from the LS group's consolidated figures is difficult with the information available.

06

Outlook

The company said it will actively pursue order opportunities in renewable-energy grid projects in Korea and abroad, including follow-on phases of the Saemangeum project. Based on this contract, it plans to expand its participation in grid work for solar and offshore wind generation sites.

The scale and timing of follow-on contracts have not yet been set. Overseas, LSCUS accounts for most of the group's backlog, so its supply contract flow is a key variable for the group outlook.

In a report dated September 30, 2026, Daishin Securities projected that LSCUS busduct revenue would rise 5.8 times to about KRW 450 billion in 2026 and reach KRW 2.2 trillion by 2030 while holding operating margins near 10%. These are brokerage estimates and should be read separately from company guidance.

From the disclosed figures, the near-term checkpoints are whether year-to-date operating profit growth through the first three quarters of 2026 carries into the full year and whether fourth-quarter profit keeps recovering from the 2025 low.

Over the medium term, the pace at which new LSCUS contracts convert into revenue, and the expansion of domestic extra-high-voltage supply results, are the items to watch.

08

Bull factors

Rapid expansion of the order backlog

The backlog of Gaon Cable and its subsidiaries rose from KRW 194.1 billion at end-2025 to KRW 833.4 billion, accounting for about 39% of the increase in LS Cable & System's consolidated backlog. If US AI data center investment continues, this backlog has room to convert into future revenue. LSCUS has long-term supply agreements with global big tech companies.

Continued quarterly profit recovery

Operating profit bottomed at KRW 7.95 billion in the fourth quarter of 2025 and then grew for three consecutive quarters through the third quarter of 2026. Third-quarter operating profit rose 99.1% year on year, faster than revenue growth. The press described the quarter as a record for quarterly results.

Expansion into renewable-energy transmission

The 154kV extra-high-voltage contract for the Saemangeum floating solar project, announced on November 6, 2026, extended the company's scope from distribution to external transmission. The company intends to pursue follow-on Saemangeum work and renewable-energy grid orders in Korea and abroad. A growing track record in high-voltage work could widen the pool of eligible projects.

09

Bear factors

Burden from a high multiple

The share price trades well above the average multiple of same-industry peers. Market capitalization is far above end-2025 total equity, so the premium to book value is large. Press coverage in late September 2026 reported debate over whether the share price was high relative to earnings.

Thin margins and higher financial leverage

Operating margin improved from 2.0% in 2022 to 3.1% in 2025 but remains thin. In 2024 margin fell to 2.6% even as revenue grew 15.2%. The debt-to-equity ratio was 184.8% at end-2025, with total liabilities of KRW 894.1 billion exceeding total equity of KRW 483.8 billion.

Dependence on the US investment cycle

More than 80% of the KRW 833.4 billion backlog belongs to LSCUS. If US AI data center investment slows, demand for busduct and distribution cable could weaken together. Quarterly profit has also swung sharply before, as the fourth quarter of 2025 showed, so earnings volatility is high.

10

Risk factors

Customer and regional concentration

Overseas revenue and backlog are concentrated in the US subsidiary LSCUS. Because contracts with global big tech and cloud companies form the core, changes in a single customer's investment schedule could affect results directly. Domestic renewable projects depend on developers' timelines, which can shift supply dates.

Profitability and costs

Annual operating margin ranged from 2.0% to 3.1% over 2022 to 2025. Cable makers' costs are exposed to raw material prices such as copper, so price increases could pressure margins. Whether margins rise along with revenue needs continued confirmation on a quarterly basis.

Balance sheet and contract execution

Total liabilities at end-2025 of KRW 894.1 billion exceeded total equity of KRW 483.8 billion. Backlog is measured at contract value, so revenue recognition can lag depending on delivery and installation schedules. Follow-on projects remain at the planning stage, with amounts and timing not yet set.

12

Overall view

Gaon Cable is a power cable maker whose revenue and operating profit rose together after 2025, with quarterly results continuing to recover through the third quarter of 2026. Its growth rests on US AI data center distribution demand and domestic renewable-energy transmission orders.

The backlog rose from KRW 194.1 billion at end-2025 to KRW 833.4 billion, which improves revenue visibility. However, more than 80% of the backlog sits with the US subsidiary, making the company sensitive to one region's investment cycle.

Annual operating margin has ranged from 2.0% to 3.1% and remains thin, and the debt-to-equity ratio rose to 184.8% at end-2025.

The share price trades well above the average multiple of same-industry peers, and there are competing readings: one holds that growth expectations are already priced in, while the other holds that the pace of earnings recovery can absorb the valuation burden.

Broker target prices are KRW 360,000 from Daishin Securities (September 30, 2026) and KRW 290,000 from KB Securities (August 31, 2026), as presented by each firm. The next checkpoints are the preliminary fourth-quarter 2026 results, the 2026 annual report, and new contracts from the Saemangeum follow-on work and LSCUS.
