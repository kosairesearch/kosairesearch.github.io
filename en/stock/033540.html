02

Business structure

Paratech Company is a fire protection equipment specialist founded in 1973 and listed on KOSDAQ in December 1997. The business is split between fire protection equipment manufacturing and fire protection system installation, and the company has said installation accounted for about 70% of total revenue.

Installation revenue reached a record KRW 195.1 billion in 2024, according to a report by Edaily on March 27, 2026. In 2025 installation sales fell about 46% to KRW 106.1 billion, and about KRW 13.1 billion of the total KRW 13.7 billion operating loss came from that division.

The manufacturing side follows a premium strategy built on high value-added products certified by UL and FM.

Its flagship products are the K160 high-flow sprinkler, the first in Asia to receive FM certification, and a domestically localized early suppression fast response (ESFR) sprinkler, supplied mainly to large logistics warehouses and data centers.

Overseas sales rose 12.86% to about KRW 7.9 billion in 2025 from KRW 7 billion, according to a company statement dated March 31, 2026. The company attributed the consolidated manufacturing operating loss of KRW 600 million to the consolidation of non-core subsidiaries.

The company also said weaker domestic construction activity reduced supply contracts and orders in 2025, so order flow remains the largest variable for revenue.

04

Earnings analysis

On a disclosed annual basis, revenue rose from KRW 214.1 billion in 2022 to KRW 218.4 billion in 2023, up 2.0%, and then to KRW 278 billion in 2024, up 27.3%, before falling to KRW 173.9 billion in 2025, down 37.4%. Among the 2022 to 2025 years, 2024 was the largest and 2025 the smallest.

Operating results moved from an operating loss of KRW 1.81 billion in 2022 and a larger operating loss of KRW 16 billion in 2023 to an operating profit of KRW 3.72 billion in 2024, then back to an operating loss of KRW 13.7 billion in 2025.

Operating margin was -0.8% in 2022, -7.3% in 2023, 1.3% in 2024 and -7.9% in 2025. Attributable net income turned to a profit of KRW 1.67 billion in 2024 and then to an attributable net loss of KRW 22 billion in 2025.

Net loss including non-controlling interests was also KRW 22 billion in 2025, and the company said impairments on investment assets and a review of recoverability of loans increased the loss.

Operating cash flow was KRW 5.72 billion in 2022, negative KRW 15.8 billion in 2023 and negative KRW 2.71 billion in 2024, then turned to KRW 17 billion in 2025. The debt-to-equity ratio moved from 116.6% in 2022 and 108.2% in 2023 to 109.3% in 2024 and 123.9% in 2025.

At end-2025, total equity was KRW 80.2 billion, of which KRW 80.1 billion was attributable to shareholders, and total liabilities were KRW 99.3 billion.

Quarterly revenue went from KRW 43.3 billion in Q3 2025 to KRW 38.9 billion, KRW 27.2 billion, KRW 26 billion and KRW 22.8 billion in Q3 2026, a decline in four consecutive quarters from Q4 2025 through Q3 2026.

Operating loss widened from KRW 5 billion in Q3 2025 to KRW 7.13 billion in Q4 2025, narrowed to KRW 6.16 billion in Q1 2026 and KRW 1.13 billion in Q2 2026, and turned to an operating profit of KRW 1.77 billion in Q3 2026.

Attributable net loss fell from KRW 13.5 billion in Q4 2025 to KRW 5.49 billion in Q1 2026 and KRW 1.48 billion in Q2 2026, and Q3 2026 showed attributable net income of KRW 580 million. The profit appeared in one quarter only, so the trend is still short.

05

Industry analysis

Paratech's end-market demand is closely tied to the domestic construction cycle. The company cited the weakening of domestic construction activity and the resulting fall in orders as the main reason for its 2025 decline.

Its installation business depends on building and project awards, so revenue fell sharply when the cycle turned down. The company says its manufacturing side, which centers on certified sprinklers supplied to large logistics warehouses and data centers, held up better than installation.

Intensifying competition within the industry was also cited in the disclosure as a factor behind the 2025 operating loss, with price pressure and higher costs appearing alongside weaker contract volume.

Fire safety certifications such as UL and FM can act as entry barriers in export and large facility projects, which is the basis for the company's differentiation claim. Whether the domestic construction cycle has bottomed is not yet clear from the public indicators cited here.

06

Outlook

On March 27, 2026, the company said it would fully restructure its business around manufacturing in 2026. It plans to adjust the share of installation, which relies heavily on the domestic market, and to strengthen overseas exports and its premium manufacturing lineup.

A company statement dated March 31, 2026 said 2026 would be the year overseas export-led growth becomes full-scale. On orders, one analysis cited official 2026 contracts for SAFEX, Cheongna and Lotte Bio totaling KRW 90 billion, equal to 51.8% of recent revenue, in a report dated September 5, 2026.

The same analysis listed net growth in the order backlog alongside operating profit and cash collection as conditions for improvement, and listed contract shrinkage, continued losses, convertible bond conversion and further share issuance as warning signs. The company has not made a numerical revenue target public.

The next confirmation point is full-year 2026 results, which will be fixed in the 2026 annual business report. Within that report, the Q4 2026 figures, progress on installation projects and overseas export sales will become part of the disclosed record.

08

Bull factors

Profitability in manufacturing

The company said its manufacturing division posted standalone operating profit of about KRW 1.8 billion in 2025. K160 and ESFR sprinklers certified by UL and FM are supplied to large logistics warehouses and data centers, and the company credits its high value-added strategy for the profit.

Overseas sales rose 12.86% to about KRW 7.9 billion in 2025, which the company presents as an early sign of export growth.

Quarterly profit direction turning

Operating profit in Q3 2026 was KRW 1.77 billion, a swing from loss. Operating loss narrowed from KRW 6.16 billion in Q1 2026 to KRW 1.13 billion in Q2 2026. Attributable net loss also shrank from KRW 13.5 billion in Q4 2025 to KRW 1.48 billion in Q2 2026 before Q3 showed attributable net income of KRW 580 million. The steady narrowing of losses can be presented as evidence of recovery.

Large contracts and cash flow

One analysis dated September 5, 2026 put the combined official 2026 contracts for SAFEX, Cheongna and Lotte Bio at KRW 90 billion, equal to 51.8% of recent revenue. Operating cash flow moved from negative KRW 2.71 billion in 2024 to KRW 17 billion in 2025. If these contracts convert into cash as projects progress, that would support the recovery case.

09

Bear factors

Dependence on installation and construction cycle

Installation revenue fell about 46% to KRW 106.1 billion in 2025. About KRW 13.1 billion of the KRW 13.7 billion operating loss came from this division. The company attributed the revenue decline to a weaker domestic construction market and fewer orders.

Because installation carries a large share of the business, a slower construction recovery could delay the earnings recovery as well.

Revenue falling for consecutive quarters

Revenue has declined in four consecutive quarters from Q4 2025 through Q3 2026. Q3 2026 revenue of KRW 22.8 billion was down 47.4% from the same quarter a year earlier. The operating profit appeared in one quarter, so whether it continues will depend on results in later quarters while the revenue base is shrinking.

Financial structure and accumulated losses

Attributable net losses over the last four quarters total KRW 19.9 billion. The debt-to-equity ratio rose to 123.9% at end-2025 from 109.3% in 2024, and total liabilities stood at KRW 99.3 billion. The company said impairment recognition on investment assets and loans increased the 2025 net loss.

One analysis lists continued losses, convertible bond conversion and further share issuance among the signals that would weaken the investment case.

10

Risk factors

Construction cycle and order risk

If the domestic construction downturn lasts longer than expected, installation orders could fall further. The 2025 decline began with fewer supply contracts and orders. If large 2026 contracts face schedule or cost delays during execution, the pace of revenue recovery could also slow.

Price competition and cost pressure

In its disclosure, the company cited order declines, price pressure from intensified competition and higher costs as factors behind the 2025 operating loss. If price competition continues, profitability in manufacturing could also be affected. If higher costs cannot be passed on to customers, recovery in operating margin could be limited.

Financial and capital structure risk

Total liabilities stood at KRW 99.3 billion at end-2025, and the debt-to-equity ratio rose to 123.9%. Shares outstanding more than doubled after 2020, which points to a history of dilution. One analysis lists convertible bond conversion and further share issuance as warning signs. If capital raising becomes necessary, existing shareholders' ownership stakes could be affected.

Overall view

The 2025 deterioration at Paratech came from a sharp fall in installation revenue and cost pressure, and the company said it will restructure its business around manufacturing in 2026 on the strength of manufacturing profit.

On a disclosed basis, revenue fell from KRW 278 billion in 2024 to KRW 173.9 billion in 2025, and the operating loss was KRW 13.7 billion. In 2026, the operating loss narrowed through Q2 before turning to an operating profit of KRW 1.77 billion in Q3.

Over the same period, however, revenue declined for four consecutive quarters, and attributable net losses over the last four quarters total KRW 19.9 billion. The rise in the debt-to-equity ratio to 123.9% at end-2025 is also a financial item to watch.

Positive factors include the profitability of certified products, the large 2026 contracts and the swing to positive operating cash flow in 2025. Negative factors include dependence on the construction cycle, price competition, impairments and the capital structure.

Because the profit has appeared in only one quarter, the trend data is still short, and the next checkpoints are project progress on installation contracts and full-year 2026 results.
