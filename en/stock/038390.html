02

Business structure

RedcapTour operates a long-term rental car business for corporate and government or public-sector clients and a business-travel and event business.

The company's 2025 annual disclosure put rental revenue at KRW 325.9 billion with operating profit of KRW 40.1 billion, and travel revenue at KRW 39.7 billion with operating profit of KRW 6.6 billion. Press reports describe rental as about 90% of total revenue and travel as about 10%.

The rental unit has used its RMS corporate mobility service and B-Lifecare EV battery management solution to add corporate clients and long-term rental contracts.

The electric vehicle share of the fleet reached 19.1% at the end of the second quarter of 2026, up from 16.3% a year earlier, as public agencies expanded adoption of eco-friendly cars.

The travel unit depends on overseas business trips by semiconductor, secondary battery and automotive electronics companies, along with overseas events from K-culture and K-beauty firms.

In the third quarter of 2026 the travel unit posted revenue of KRW 10.1 billion and operating profit of KRW 2.3 billion, a 22.8% operating margin. The 2025 integration of the Korean partnership with American Express Global Business Travel is expected to lift revenue from transferred client accounts.

Listed peers in the travel services classification include Hanatour Service, MODE TOUR NETWORK INC and Yellow Balloon Tour, but the combination of corporate rental and trip management sets RedcapTour apart from a typical travel agency.

A Newspim report said LG Group-affiliated clients account for roughly 30%-plus of the business and framed broadening the client base as a challenge.

04

Earnings analysis

On a fiscal-year basis, revenue rose from KRW 262.1 billion in 2022 to KRW 338.2 billion in 2023 (up 29.0% year on year), KRW 358.9 billion in 2024 (up 6.1%) and KRW 365.6 billion in 2025 (up 1.9%). Revenue has increased for three consecutive years from 2023, and 2025 is the largest year of the 2022 to 2025 span.

Operating profit went from KRW 36.3 billion in 2022 to KRW 38.8 billion in 2023 (up 6.7%), KRW 43.6 billion in 2024 (up 12.4%) and KRW 46.8 billion in 2025 (up 7.4%), a run of three consecutive annual gains.

The operating margin fell from 13.9% in 2022 to 11.5% in 2023 before recovering to 12.1% in 2024 and 12.8% in 2025.

Net income attributable to controlling shareholders dropped from KRW 21.2 billion in 2022 to KRW 16.6 billion in 2023 (down 21.7%), then rose to KRW 20.2 billion in 2024 (up 21.7%) and KRW 23.7 billion in 2025 (up 17.0%).

Net income including non-controlling interests is disclosed at the same amounts as controlling-shareholder net income in every year from 2022 to 2025.

The debt ratio was 222.7% in 2022, 263.7% in 2023, 223.3% in 2024 and 252.9% in 2025, when total equity stood at KRW 201.3 billion against total liabilities of KRW 509 billion.

Operating cash flow was -KRW 52.8 billion in 2022 and -KRW 71.4 billion in 2023, turned positive at KRW 76.5 billion in 2024, and was KRW 24.9 billion in 2025.

Quarterly operating profit fell from KRW 10.3 billion in Q3 2025 to KRW 5.1 billion in Q4 2025, then rose to KRW 17.9 billion in Q1 2026, which the company described as its highest quarterly operating profit, and the company attributed part of that gain to strategic sales of vehicles at the end of their contracts.

Operating profit then fell to KRW 15.7 billion in Q2 2026 and KRW 10.5 billion in Q3 2026, two consecutive quarterly declines, and the company said Q3 revenue dropped because used-car sale volumes shifted into Q4.

Net income attributable to controlling shareholders went from KRW 665 million in Q4 2025 to KRW 10.9 billion in Q1 2026, then KRW 9.13 billion in Q2 and KRW 4.63 billion in Q3, and the four quarters from Q4 2025 to Q3 2026 total KRW 25.3 billion.

05

Industry analysis

Demand in the travel unit comes from overseas expansion by Korean corporations.

The company said key clients opened overseas production bases and widened global operations, which pushed air ticket sales sharply higher, and it cited expansion beyond semiconductors and secondary batteries into K-culture, beauty, entertainment and food.

By contrast, in 2025 business-trip demand from secondary battery and petrochemical clients declined, and the detention of Korean workers in the United States compounded the drop in revenue, according to the company. Press reports said the Middle East conflict has kept the travel industry as a whole in a downturn.

The company said some group events were cancelled because of the Middle East conflict, but demand for business trips from major clients offset much of that effect.

The rental unit benefits from public policy to expand eco-friendly vehicles among government and public agencies, and the company said it has held top-grade status in the Korean public procurement service rental category for nine consecutive years from 2018 to 2026.

RedcapTour's client base is centered on corporations and government or public institutions. The company points to customized business-travel management and its global network as its differentiators.

Still, the concentration of clients in particular industries and a particular business group remains a source of exposure to changes in the business environment.

06

Outlook

In its second-quarter 2026 results announcement, the company said revenue gains from integrating the Korean partnership with American Express Global Business Travel were expected to gather pace in the second half.

The same release indicated that group events cancelled in the first half were likely to resume in the second half, and the company expects travel-unit results to keep improving. Its first-quarter 2026 announcement cited growing scale at global events such as CES and MWC as a factor behind higher trip demand.

The rental unit will keep focusing on eco-friendly vehicles, as public-sector electric vehicle demand is driving results. The company plans to strengthen operating services such as RMS and B-Lifecare and to upgrade its AI chatbot and business travel management system.

In June 2026 it became the first Korean travel company to have its greenhouse gas reduction target approved under the Science Based Targets initiative, aiming to cut Scope 1 and 2 emissions by 54.6% by 2033 from 2023 levels.

However, the timing of rental-fleet replacement demand and used-car sales that shifted into Q4 remains a source of quarterly volatility. In its third-quarter release the company said it would step up sales activity for the rest of the year.

08

Bull factors

Corporate travel demand and overseas expansion

Operating profit reached KRW 17.9 billion in Q1 2026 and KRW 15.7 billion in Q2 2026, which points to a durable earnings base. The company attributes rising trip demand to overseas production line expansion by semiconductor, secondary battery and automotive electronics firms, along with the spread of K-culture.

The travel unit's operating margin rose to 22.8% in the third quarter of 2026, up 7.7 percentage points from a year earlier.

Public-sector EV contracts

As government and public agencies expand adoption of eco-friendly vehicles, the electric share of the rental fleet rose to 19.1% at the end of the second quarter of 2026 from 16.3% a year earlier. The company said it is bundling RMS and B-Lifecare operating services to lengthen contracts.

Its nine consecutive years of top-grade status in the public procurement rental category is cited as evidence of its public-sector competitiveness.

Cash flow recovery and shareholder returns

Operating cash flow turned positive at KRW 76.5 billion in 2024 and remained positive at KRW 24.9 billion in 2025. Net income attributable to controlling shareholders rose 17.0% to KRW 23.7 billion in 2025. The company continues to pay both quarterly and year-end dividends.

09

Bear factors

Slowdown in third-quarter earnings

Third-quarter 2026 operating profit of KRW 10.5 billion fell 33.3% from the prior quarter, and revenue of KRW 81.4 billion dropped 8.5% from a year earlier. Net income attributable to controlling shareholders of KRW 4.63 billion declined 6.9% year on year. The decline follows the first-quarter 2026 peak and continues a two-quarter slide.

High debt ratio and shrinking equity

At the end of 2025 total liabilities were KRW 509 billion against total equity of KRW 201.3 billion, a debt ratio of 252.9%. The ratio rose from 223.3% in 2024, and equity declined from the end of 2024.

Given the larger debt load relative to equity, the financial burden from interest rate and borrowing conditions also needs to be watched.

Client concentration and industry exposure

Press reports put LG Group-affiliated clients at roughly 30%-plus of the business. In 2025, business-trip demand from secondary battery and petrochemical clients declined, and revenue fell as a result.

High dependence on particular industries and a business group makes earnings more sensitive to changes in those sectors.

10

Risk factors

Client and industry concentration

Reports say the client base is concentrated in LG Group-affiliated companies and a few industries. In 2025, business-trip demand from secondary battery and petrochemical clients declined, compounded by the detention of Korean workers in the United States.

If investment cycles weaken for particular client groups, trip and vehicle demand could fall together, raising revenue volatility.

Macro conditions and geopolitics

The Middle East conflict led to some group event cancellations, and the company itself cited high interest rates and high oil prices as difficult operating conditions. Press reports described the travel industry as broadly in a downturn.

If oil prices and interest rates reduce corporate travel budgets and vehicle rental demand, both the travel and rental units could be affected at the same time.

Financial leverage and quarterly earnings swings

The debt ratio stood at 252.9% at the end of 2025, higher than at the end of 2024. Quarterly operating profit swung from KRW 5.1 billion in Q4 2025 to KRW 17.9 billion in Q1 2026, and results depend on the timing of used-car sales.

If the timing of sales shifts from quarter to quarter, as the company has described, quarterly earnings become harder to forecast.

12

Overall view

Built on rental and business-travel units serving corporate and public-sector clients, RedcapTour recorded three consecutive years of higher annual revenue and operating profit through 2025. Net income attributable to controlling shareholders also rose for two straight years after its 2023 low.

Operating profit hit a quarterly high of KRW 17.9 billion in Q1 2026 but then declined for two consecutive quarters to KRW 15.7 billion and KRW 10.5 billion.

The third-quarter revenue decline was attributed to used-car sales and vehicle replacement demand shifting into Q4, while the travel unit's operating margin rose to 22.8%.

On the financial side, the debt ratio at the end of 2025 was 252.9%, higher than a year earlier, and total equity of KRW 201.3 billion was lower than at the end of 2024.

Supporting factors are corporate travel demand, public-sector EV contracts and the shareholder return structure, while offsetting factors are client concentration, the Middle East conflict and the high debt ratio.

The next checkpoints are the operating margin and debt ratio in the FY2026 annual report and first-quarter 2027 results.
