Wits: Earnings Recovery After Losses Returned Is the Key Test

Wits' revenue rose to KRW 168.7 billion in 2025, but its operating margin fell to 1.5%, and it posted operating losses in the second and third quarters of 2026. Whether it can restore earnings is the central question.

02

Business structure

Wits develops and manufactures wireless power transmission solutions. The company was established as a new corporate entity in April 2019 and was registered as a first-tier supplier to Samsung Electronics in July 2019.

The business is split into electronics and automotive segments; at end-2025, sales were 73.36% electronics, 21.66% automotive and 4.98% other (FnGuide data). The core of electronics is mobile wireless charging receiver modules, which accounted for roughly 49% of the product sales mix in a company-profile breakdown.

Display products for home appliances represented about 22.5%, power and communication control modules about 13.4%, and automotive camera modules about 5.4%. In 2025, export shares for mobile receiver modules were about 85%, which points to heavy reliance on overseas sales.

The automotive segment expanded into in-vehicle wireless charging and power and communication control modules, and in 2024 the company secured a power communication control business through a business transfer from an automotive company.

In December 2025 it began supplying 7KW slow chargers for electric vehicles and consolidated a subsidiary acquired in the same period. Production is handled largely by its Vietnamese unit, WITS VINA Co., Ltd, which is used to build cost competitiveness. Samsung Electronics is the core customer.

In wireless charging components, the company competes with large parts makers such as Samsung Electro-Mechanics and LG Innotek, but its scale is much smaller. R&D spending in 2025 was about KRW 8.3 billion, equal to 4.92% of revenue.

04

Earnings analysis

On the basis of disclosed results, revenue was KRW 99.5 billion in 2023, KRW 90.8 billion in 2024 (down 8.8% year on year), and KRW 168.7 billion in 2025 (up 85.9%), making 2025 the largest year of the 2023 to 2025 period.

Operating profit fell from KRW 10.3 billion in 2023 to KRW 2.18 billion in 2024 (down 78.8%), then recovered to KRW 2.59 billion in 2025 (up 19.1%). 2023 was the largest year for operating profit and 2024 the smallest. The operating margin was 10.3% in 2023, 2.4% in 2024 and 1.5% in 2025, falling even as revenue grew.

FnGuide's data shows the cost of sales ratio rose to 79.52% in 2025 from 75.92% in 2024, so higher volume came with heavier cost burdens that squeezed margins.

Net profit attributable to controlling shareholders was KRW 7.61 billion in 2023, KRW 1.55 billion in 2024 (down 79.7%), and KRW 1.93 billion in 2025 (up 24.9%). Total net income for 2025 was KRW 2.85 billion, higher than the parent-attributable figure, with the gap reflecting non-controlling interests.

Operating cash flow went from KRW 20.3 billion in 2023 to -KRW 4.02 billion in 2024 and -KRW 28.6 billion in 2025, a widening deficit. At end-2025, total equity was KRW 45.9 billion, of which KRW 45.1 billion was attributable to controlling shareholders, and total liabilities were KRW 112.4 billion.

Quarterly swings were larger. After operating profit of KRW 1.58 billion on revenue of KRW 55.7 billion in Q3 2025, Q4 2025 revenue fell 19.9% sequentially to KRW 44.6 billion and the company posted an operating loss of KRW 1.98 billion.

Q1 2026 returned to operating profit of KRW 1.5 billion on revenue of KRW 54.9 billion. Q2 2026 revenue was KRW 42.7 billion with an operating loss of KRW 1.24 billion and a net loss attributable to controlling shareholders of KRW 3.37 billion.

Q3 2026 revenue was KRW 45.5 billion with an operating loss of KRW 103 million, narrower than the prior quarter but a swing to loss from a year earlier. Given these large swings, whether any one-off items were involved should be checked in the quarterly report footnotes.

05

Industry analysis

Wits' revenue moves sharply from quarter to quarter depending on whether set makers such as Samsung Electronics adopt its modules. When it reported Q1 2025 results, the company said it had won orders for all Galaxy S25 models except the standard version.

In a later analysis of the nine-month 2025 results, strength in the Galaxy lineup and expanded wireless charging module sales from enhanced AI features were cited as drivers. The position of the mobile wireless charging cycle is therefore a key variable for earnings.

In 2026, however, quarterly revenue has moved between KRW 54.9 billion, KRW 42.7 billion and KRW 45.5 billion, and profit has alternated between profit and loss. The automotive segment is widening through in-vehicle wireless charging and 7KW EV slow chargers, but it still accounts for roughly a quarter of sales.

Auto parts depend on long platform adoption cycles, so they should be read as medium-term order trends rather than near-term earnings. Peers Samsung Electro-Mechanics and LG Innotek are larger in scale and more diversified.

Wits holds a first-tier supplier position with Samsung Electronics, but its high customer concentration makes earnings sensitive to changes in contract terms.

The Vietnam production base is cited as a cost advantage, yet the specific causes of the higher 2025 cost of sales ratio cannot be separated from the disclosures alone.

06

Outlook

When it reported Q3 2025 results, the company said it intended to secure higher profitability in 2026. The return to operating profit in Q1 2026 fit that direction, but operating losses in Q2 and Q3 mean the durability of the recovery is not yet confirmed.

On the product side, the company began supplying wireless charging components for robots in June 2026, and in the same month a 2.2KW high-output product received KI international certification. It also signed a comprehensive business cooperation MOU with a Turkish company in June 2026.

In December 2025 it began supplying 7KW EV slow chargers and signed a supply agreement with a domestic EV charging operator. Whether these businesses translate into revenue can be checked in the segment breakdown of upcoming regular reports.

Because the consolidation scope widened with the subsidiary acquired in December 2025, the split between consolidated results and the subsidiary's contribution is another point to verify in the 2026 annual report.

Q4 2026 results have not yet been disclosed, and annual preliminary results and the 2026 annual report are due after the end of 2026. The first benchmark for earnings recovery is whether the operating margin exceeds the 1.5% recorded in 2025.

08

Bull factors

Supplier base with Samsung Electronics and export modules

Wits has supplied mobile wireless charging modules since it was registered as a first-tier supplier to Samsung Electronics. In 2025, export shares for mobile receiver modules were about 85%, indicating an established overseas customer base.

Strength in the Galaxy lineup was cited as a driver of sales growth in the nine-month 2025 analysis. If the supplier relationship holds, adoption in new set models could support a revenue rebound.

Product expansion into automotive and EV charging

In December 2025 the company began supplying 7KW EV slow chargers, and in June 2026 a 2.2KW high-output product received KI international certification. Supply of wireless charging components for robots also began that month. With automotive at 21.66% of sales at end-2025, the business mix is diversifying. Whether these certifications and launches convert into revenue will show up in future segment breakdowns.

2025 operating profit recovery and Q1 2026 profit

Operating profit rose from KRW 2.18 billion in 2024 to KRW 2.59 billion in 2025, up 19.1%. In Q1 2026 the company returned to operating profit of KRW 1.5 billion after a loss in the prior quarter. Net profit attributable to controlling shareholders was KRW 2.97 billion in that quarter. The swing back to profit is a reference point for judging earnings strength.

09

Bear factors

Operating losses in Q2 and Q3 2026

The company posted an operating loss of KRW 1.24 billion in Q2 2026 and KRW 103 million in Q3 2026, two consecutive quarters of operating losses. Q3 revenue rose from the prior quarter, but operating profit did not return to positive territory.

The current earnings trend sits some distance from the higher profitability the company described in late 2025. Whether quarterly results return to profit is the point that decides this bearish case.

Cost pressure and thin margins

The cost of sales ratio rose from 75.92% in 2024 to 79.52% in 2025. The operating margin fell from 10.3% in 2023 to 2.4% in 2024 and 1.5% in 2025. Despite 85.9% revenue growth in 2025, margins declined, suggesting that expansion came with higher costs. Without margin recovery, larger sales may not translate into earnings improvement.

Financial burden and weaker cash flow

Cash outflow from operating activities widened over the period, so the core business did not fund itself. The debt-to-equity ratio rose to 245.0% at end-2025 from 139.6% in 2024, with total liabilities of KRW 112.4 billion.

Against total equity of KRW 45.9 billion, that debt load could limit how quickly earnings recover. With weaker cash generation, external financing needs are a key item to monitor.

Risk factors

Customer and model concentration

Revenue depends heavily on set makers such as Samsung Electronics. As the company's Q1 2025 disclosure showed, which models it wins can determine quarterly sales. Customer diversification into automotive and robotics is underway but remains small in share. A cut in key-customer orders or a timing gap in model transitions could amplify quarterly earnings swings.

Financing and related-party transactions

In August 2024 Wits issued a first private convertible bond worth KRW 24 billion. Days earlier, on August 11, it disclosed a KRW 10 billion loan to a subsidiary at a 4% annual rate. Large mezzanine financing and loans to affiliates shortly after listing merit attention on cash flows. Conversion or repayment progress on the convertible bonds can be checked in regular reports.

Cost structure and delayed earnings recovery

With the cost of sales ratio at 79.52% in 2025, the operating margin remained at 1.5%. Whether the cost increase came from materials, labor or product mix cannot be separated from the disclosures alone. Operating losses in Q2 and Q3 2026 suggest the cost structure has not yet improved. Integration costs from the subsidiary and new business expansion could also delay the earnings recovery.

Overall view

Wits' revenue rose to KRW 168.7 billion in 2025, but its operating margin fell to 1.5%, and it posted operating losses in Q2 and Q3 2026. The return to profit in Q1 2026 and the narrower Q3 loss show how volatile quarterly results have been.

The net loss attributable to controlling shareholders over the last four quarters totaled KRW 383 million. Strengths include its first-tier supplier position with Samsung Electronics, a high export share for mobile wireless charging modules, and product expansion into automotive and EV charging.

Weaknesses include customer and model concentration, a higher cost of sales ratio, a 245.0% debt-to-equity ratio and a wider operating cash flow deficit. The KRW 24 billion private convertible bond issued in August 2024 and the loan to a subsidiary are reasons to monitor cash flows.

With both strengths and weaknesses present, the next checkpoints are the 2026 annual results and the cash flow and operating margin in the 2026 annual report.
