02

Business structure

Samsung E&A operates three segments: Chemicals, Advanced Industries and New Energy. It designs, procures and builds petrochemical and fertilizer plants, advanced-industry facilities such as semiconductor fabs, and energy-transition facilities.

In the third quarter of 2026, Advanced Industries segment revenue reached KRW 1.07 trillion, up 166.0% year on year, with a segment gross margin of 17.0%. New Energy segment revenue was KRW 791 billion, up 141.8%, but its gross margin fell from 25.9% a year earlier to 21.4%.

Third-quarter new orders comprised KRW 4.9 trillion in Chemicals, KRW 3.9 trillion in Advanced Industries and KRW 600 billion in New Energy. The largest single award in September 2026 was a fertilizer plant of about KRW 4.7 trillion, won solely by Samsung E&A from SABIC Agri-Nutrients.

Backlog stood at KRW 27.2 trillion at the end of the third quarter of 2026, roughly three years of work against 2025 revenue of KRW 9 trillion. By region, the Middle East and North Africa accounted for 56% of backlog at the end of the first quarter of 2026.

Because clients are mainly energy investors in petrochemicals, fertilizers and gas, order flow is sensitive to oil prices and geopolitics. Group affiliates are another pillar; the group's advanced-industry order target was raised from KRW 3 trillion to as much as KRW 6 trillion.

Compared with listed peers in the same sector, including Hyundai Engineering & Construction, Daewoo Engineering & Construction, DL E&C and GS Engineering & Construction, the plant EPC share is high, so the earnings drivers differ and comparisons need to account for business mix.

04

Earnings analysis

On a disclosed basis, annual revenue rose from KRW 10.1 trillion in 2022 to KRW 10.6 trillion in 2023, up 5.7%, which was the peak year, then fell 6.2% to KRW 9.97 trillion in 2024 and 9.4% to KRW 9.03 trillion in 2025, a decline in both 2024 and 2025.

Among 2022 to 2025, 2023 was the largest year and 2025 the smallest. Operating profit rose from KRW 702.9 billion in 2022 to KRW 993.1 billion in 2023, up 41.3%, then fell 2.2% to KRW 971.6 billion in 2024 and 18.5% to KRW 792.1 billion in 2025, a decline in both 2024 and 2025.

The operating margin was 7.0% in 2022, 9.3% in 2023, 9.7% in 2024 and 8.8% in 2025, with 2024 the highest. Net profit attributable to controlling shareholders was highest in 2024 at KRW 756.9 billion and fell 18.4% to KRW 617.5 billion in 2025.

Net profit including non-controlling interests was KRW 648.3 billion in 2025, a different figure from the controlling-shareholder measure. Operating cash flow was KRW -459.8 billion in 2023, recovered to KRW 1.64 trillion in 2024, and fell to KRW 254.4 billion in 2025.

The debt ratio fell from 204.6% in 2022 to 125.8% in 2025.

Quarterly operating profit rose from KRW 176.5 billion in the third quarter of 2025 to KRW 277.4 billion in the fourth quarter, dipped to KRW 188.2 billion in the first quarter of 2026, and then rose for two consecutive quarters to KRW 273.1 billion and KRW 500.6 billion.

The company attributed the third-quarter improvement to revenue recognition from large chemicals and advanced-industry projects, cost control, and productivity gains from AI, digital transformation and robotics.

Hana Securities, however, estimated that about KRW 200 billion of one-off gains were included in third-quarter operating profit, and analysts noted that roughly KRW 100 billion of provisions were released on final settlement of the Yanbu power plant project.

Press calculations indicate that reaching the KRW 1.4 trillion full-year operating profit target would require KRW 438.1 billion more in the fourth quarter.

05

Industry analysis

Among Korean construction and engineering companies, Samsung E&A has a high share of overseas plant orders, so Middle East petrochemical and fertilizer project flow largely determines its results.

Market analysts attribute the third-quarter 2026 improvement to two drivers: revenue recognition from large Middle East chemical projects and the semiconductor upcycle.

On the chip side, iM Securities said in a July 2026 report that backlog for the Pyeongtaek P4 and P5 works stood at about KRW 2.2 trillion and KRW 700 billion at the end of the first quarter of 2026, and that the completion of P4 alongside the start of P5 phase two could lead to a sizable rise in new orders.

In an October 2026 report, Hana Securities cited semiconductor investment expansion in Yongin and Honam after Pyeongtaek, Middle East chemical orders, and possible energy investment growth in Latin America and the CIS as growth drivers.

The raised advanced-industry order target for group affiliates was also cited as supporting domestic volume stability. On the other side, the Middle East and North Africa, which hold 56% of backlog, face geopolitical and oil-price risks that could shift order timing.

Meritz Securities said in an October 2026 report that it expects orders of about KRW 18 trillion in 2027 as well.

In terms of cycle position, the earnings recovery reflects large projects entering revenue recognition, but analysts are separating one-off gains from the third quarter, so the fourth quarter and beyond need to be checked.

Revenue and profit structures also differ from other companies in the same sector, so comparisons should account for business mix.

06

Outlook

The company raised its 2026 full-year order target from KRW 12 trillion to KRW 19 trillion, its operating profit target from KRW 800 billion to KRW 1.4 trillion, and its revenue target from KRW 10 trillion to KRW 11 trillion.

Cumulative new orders of KRW 17.1 trillion through the third quarter already exceed the original order target but remain short of the raised KRW 19 trillion.

Pipeline projects include a methanol project in Mexico worth up to USD 2.5 billion, a urea fertilizer project in Qatar, the Kafji gas project in Saudi Arabia worth USD 2 billion, a urea plant in Qatar, and an LNG project with INPEX in Indonesia.

In an October 2026 report, Meritz Securities said the KRW 19 trillion order target is a record and expects about KRW 18 trillion of orders again in 2027. Press calculations show that reaching the KRW 1.4 trillion operating profit target would require about KRW 438.1 billion more in the fourth quarter.

For shareholder returns, the company decided to buy back about KRW 780 billion of shares from October 23, 2026 to January 22, 2027, cancelling KRW 690 billion and using KRW 90 billion for employee bonuses.

The company presents balanced growth across the Chemicals, Advanced Industries and New Energy segments as its medium-term direction. The next checkpoints are whether the raised full-year operating profit target is met, the scale of fourth-quarter new orders, and the timing of the share cancellation.

The 2027 full-year targets are likely to be disclosed in early 2027, consistent with the company's practice of setting targets at the start of the year.

08

Bull factors

About Three Years of Work in Backlog

Backlog at the end of the third quarter of 2026 stood at KRW 27.2 trillion, roughly three years of work against 2025 revenue of KRW 9 trillion.

Cumulative new orders of KRW 17.1 trillion through the third quarter exceeded the original KRW 12 trillion target, and the company raised its full-year order target to KRW 19 trillion. Large Middle East chemical projects and advanced-industry awards together are cited as evidence of growth.

Quarterly Operating Profit Up for Two Straight Quarters

Operating profit bottomed at KRW 188.2 billion in the first quarter of 2026, then rose to KRW 273.1 billion in the second quarter and KRW 500.6 billion in the third. Third-quarter operating profit was up 183.6% from KRW 176.5 billion a year earlier. The company cites cost control and productivity gains from AI, digital transformation and robotics.

Share Buyback and Cancellation Decision

On October 22, 2026, the board approved buying back about KRW 780 billion of shares, of which KRW 690 billion will be cancelled and KRW 90 billion used for employee bonuses. The buyback period runs from October 23, 2026 to January 22, 2027. The company described it as a special shareholder return following the overachievement of its annual targets.

09

Bear factors

One-Off Gains in Third-Quarter Profit

Hana Securities estimated that about KRW 200 billion of one-off gains were included in third-quarter 2026 operating profit. In the chemicals segment, about KRW 100 billion of existing provisions were reportedly released on final settlement of the Yanbu power plant project. Whether operating profit holds up without these items in the fourth quarter remains to be confirmed.

Concentration in the Middle East and North Africa

The Middle East and North Africa accounted for 56% of backlog at the end of the first quarter of 2026. Orders are sensitive to oil prices and geopolitical developments, which analysts cite as a risk. A delay in a single regional project could push back the timing of revenue recognition.

Weaker 2025 Results and Cash Flow

Revenue fell 9.4% to KRW 9.03 trillion in 2025, and operating profit fell 18.5% to KRW 792.1 billion. Net profit attributable to controlling shareholders fell 18.4% to KRW 617.5 billion. Operating cash flow dropped from KRW 1.64 trillion in 2024 to KRW 254.4 billion in 2025.

10

Risk factors

Project Cost and Settlement

Large plant projects move quarterly profit sharply with final settlements and provision releases, as the Yanbu settlement in the third quarter showed. Cost overruns or schedule delays could make the raised operating profit target harder to reach.

Regional and Oil Price Exposure

With the Middle East and North Africa above half of backlog, geopolitical tension, falling oil prices or local tender adjustments could shift order timing and revenue recognition. Future order disclosures will show whether regional diversification progresses.

Cycle Concentration and Buyback Execution

Advanced-industry revenue rose 166.0% in the third quarter of 2026 and depends heavily on semiconductor investment schedules. A slowdown in chip investment could reduce volume. Whether the buyback and the KRW 690 billion cancellation are executed as planned is the execution risk for shareholder returns.

Overall view

Samsung E&A's disclosed results peaked in 2023, with revenue and operating profit declining in 2024 and 2025. Since the first-quarter 2026 low, operating profit has risen for two consecutive quarters, placing the company in a recovery phase.

Cumulative new orders of KRW 17.1 trillion through the third quarter and backlog of KRW 27.2 trillion at quarter-end, about three years of work, add visibility to revenue.

The raised full-year targets of KRW 19 trillion in orders, KRW 1.4 trillion in operating profit and KRW 11 trillion in revenue are the company's stated benchmarks, and their achievement will be tested in fourth-quarter results.

Positive factors are order growth, rising advanced-industry revenue, and the KRW 780 billion buyback with KRW 690 billion to be cancelled.

Negative factors are the roughly KRW 200 billion of one-off gains in third-quarter operating profit, geopolitical and oil-price exposure in the Middle East and North Africa, which hold 56% of backlog, and lower operating cash flow in 2025 than in 2024.

The debt ratio fell from 204.6% in 2022 to 125.8% in 2025, but total liabilities remained KRW 5.59 trillion at the end of 2025. The next checkpoints are preliminary fourth-quarter 2026 results, completion and cancellation of the buyback, and confirmed figures in the 2026 annual report.
