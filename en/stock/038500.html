02

Business structure

SAMPYO Cement was founded in December 1990 and listed on KOSDAQ in February 2001, with its headquarters in Samcheok, Gangwon Province. The company was renamed from Dongyang Cement in March 2017, and since July 2023 its largest shareholder side has held a 54.68% stake.

As of the third quarter of 2025, cement accounted for 94.07% of consolidated revenue and ready-mix concrete for 5.93%, so the business is effectively a single cement franchise. Main cement products include Portland cement, clinker and slag cement.

The ready-mix business started in September 2021 with the acquisition of ready-mix units from Sampyo Industrial and S Nature, and a Namgwangju plant was added in January 2026. Its domestic cement market share rose gradually from 15.8% in 2022 and 16.0% in 2023 to 16.2% in 2024.

A 2025 brokerage note ranked it as a fourth-place player with a share of about 16%. Past press reports indicated that most of its shipments move by coastal sea transport from the Samcheok base.

Both cement and ready-mix sales are more than 99% domestic, so exposure to the local construction cycle is high while currency sensitivity is limited.

On sustainability, the company developed the low-carbon BLUE MENT brand and is expanding KS certification, and it received an integrated A grade in the KCGS 2026 ESG assessment announced on November 16, 2026.

04

Earnings analysis

Annual revenue rose from KRW 721.1 billion in 2022 to KRW 823.7 billion in 2023, up 14.2%, which was the largest year of 2022 to 2025. Revenue then fell 4.0% to KRW 790.8 billion in 2024 and 14.4% to KRW 676.9 billion in 2025, a two-year decline from 2024 through 2025.

Operating profit climbed from KRW 71.1 billion in 2022 to KRW 84.7 billion in 2023 (up 19.1%) and KRW 103.9 billion in 2024 (up 22.7%), with 2024 the largest year of the period.

Operating profit then dropped 26.3% to KRW 76.6 billion in 2025, and the company attributed this to prolonged weakness in construction activity reducing profitability. The operating margin was 9.9% in 2022, 10.3% in 2023, 13.1% in 2024 and 11.3% in 2025.

Net income attributable to controlling shareholders peaked at KRW 66.1 billion in 2024, up 95.5%, and fell 38.3% to KRW 40.8 billion in 2025, while total net income including non-controlling interests was also reported at KRW 40.8 billion for that year.

Quarterly operating profit moved from KRW 12.9 billion in the third quarter of 2025 to KRW 33.2 billion in the fourth quarter, up 158.2% from the prior quarter.

Press reports tied the KRW 10 billion operating profit in the first quarter of 2026 largely to kiln maintenance being split across the first and second quarters, which reduced the first-quarter cost burden. Maintenance costs and higher petroleum coke prices began to weigh from the second quarter.

In the third quarter of 2026, revenue was KRW 154.4 billion, down 1.2% from a year earlier, operating profit was KRW 7.85 billion, down 39.0%, and net income attributable to controlling shareholders was KRW 510 million, down 88.9%.

Net income attributable to controlling shareholders over the last four quarters totaled KRW 31.5 billion. On the cost side, the cement capacity utilization rate fell from 61.6% in 2024 to 49.7% in 2025, which raised the fixed-cost burden.

Debt-to-equity improved from 111.3% in 2022 to 84.0% in 2025, with total equity of KRW 786.3 billion and total liabilities of KRW 660.5 billion at the end of 2025. Operating cash flow was KRW 80.9 billion in 2022, KRW 120.2 billion in 2023, KRW 126.1 billion in 2024 and KRW 93.9 billion in 2025.

05

Industry analysis

Domestic cement demand is directly exposed to the construction downturn. Citing Korea Cement Association data, a June 2026 press report said 2025 domestic cement shipments were 38.1 million tons, down 12.8% from a year earlier and the lowest level since 1991.

The same report expected shipments to remain around 36 million tons in 2026. Because cement plants require heavy capital investment, capacity cannot be cut immediately when shipments fall, so fixed-cost pressure persists. SAMPYO Cement's cement capacity utilization fell to 49.7% in 2025 from 61.6% in 2024.

The ready-mix industry is also under strain: a 2025 press report said industry utilization dropped to 14%, the lowest since the 1998 currency crisis, and several regional producers entered court-led rehabilitation. Ready-mix accounted for roughly 6% of SAMPYO Cement's revenue in 2025.

In the first quarter of 2026, the ready-mix unit reported revenue of KRW 14.4 billion and an operating loss of KRW 700 million according to press reports.

In competition, the company ranks around fourth in domestic share at about 16%, competing on cost and price with cement makers such as HANIL CEMENTCO and Asia Cement.

Industry commentary has focused on cost control and plant efficiency rather than price increases as the main defense of earnings while construction starts remain weak. Whether demand has passed its trough is still debated, and the view depends on when construction activity recovers.

06

Outlook

The company has framed its near-term direction around cost reduction and profitability. After the February 2026 disclosure, a company representative said it would keep pursuing cost savings, including greater use of alternative fuels.

At the same time, the company said it plans to introduce technologies such as physical AI to improve production efficiency.

When announcing first-quarter results, the company said cost increases from the construction slump and Middle East geopolitical risk would be reflected from the second quarter, so further earnings improvement would be limited.

The former ready-mix site in Seongsu-dong has its district unit plan and special planning zone development plan approved by the Seoul Metropolitan Government at the end of 2025.

A February 2026 Seoul Economic Daily report said soil remediation was underway and construction could begin as early as late 2026 after permits. The target completion year is 2032, with a mixed office, residential and commercial complex of up to 79 floors planned.

That construction timeline was a forecast made in February 2026 and needs to be confirmed against current permit progress. Real estate proceeds would take years to materialize, so core profitability still depends on cement shipments and cost structure.

The company kept an A+ grade in the social category for four consecutive years in the 2026 ESG assessment, which reflects its non-financial management system. No formal company guidance figures were confirmed, so this outlook is limited to disclosed plans and reported facts.

08

Bull factors

First-quarter rebound and cost management

Operating profit in the first quarter of 2026 was KRW 10 billion, sharply up from KRW 2.1 billion a year earlier. Press reports attributed the gain largely to splitting kiln maintenance across the first and second quarters. The company said it will continue cost reduction, including greater use of alternative fuels.

Gradual gain in market share

Domestic cement market share rose from 15.8% in 2022 to 16.0% in 2023 and 16.2% in 2024, a steady gain over three years. Cement makes up most of consolidated revenue, so share changes flow directly into results. The gain shows the competitive position held up even while industry demand weakened.

Separate asset value from the Seongsu-dong site

The Seoul Metropolitan Government approved the district unit plan and special planning zone development plan for the site at the end of 2025. A February 2026 press report said construction could begin as early as late 2026, with completion targeted for 2032. If the project advances, asset value could become visible separately from core cement earnings.

Bear factors

Weak shipments and lower utilization

Domestic cement shipments in 2025 were 38.1 million tons, the lowest since 1991, and about 36 million tons was projected for 2026. The company's cement utilization fell from 61.6% in 2024 to 49.7% in 2025. With fixed costs staying in place, operating profit fell sharply.

Renewed earnings weakness in the third quarter

Third-quarter 2026 revenue was KRW 154.4 billion, down 1.2% from a year earlier. Operating profit was KRW 7.85 billion, down 39.0%, and net income attributable to controlling shareholders was KRW 510 million, down 88.9%. The failure of the first-quarter rebound to carry forward is a bearish point.

Energy cost increases and external variables

In the first quarter, petroleum coke was priced at KRW 375,599 per ton, up 121% from a year earlier. Bituminous coal accounted for 61% of raw material purchases and petroleum coke for 34%, leaving costs exposed to energy price swings. Middle East geopolitical risk was flagged as an added cost burden in the second half.

10

Risk factors

Demand and industry risk

If construction starts do not recover, cement shipments could fall further from the roughly 36 million tons projected for 2026. Domestic sales exceed 99% of the total, so the company bears the full impact of the local construction cycle. Utilization may stay limited until demand clearly bottoms out.

Cost and energy risk

Higher petroleum coke and bituminous coal prices have begun to reach cost of sales from the second quarter. Kiln maintenance costs that were spread out are returning, so the cost burden can persist. If the company cannot pass costs on, its operating margin could fall further.

Development timing risk

The Seongsu-dong site must go through soil remediation, building review and permitting, and a late-2026 construction start was a February 2026 forecast. The completion target of 2032 means development gains are many years away. A delay could reset the development expectations that have been reflected in the share price.

Overall view

SAMPYO Cement's disclosed results peaked in revenue in 2023 and declined through 2025, while operating profit peaked at KRW 103.9 billion in 2024 and fell to KRW 76.6 billion in 2025.

The KRW 10 billion operating profit in the first quarter of 2026 was reported to have been largely aided by spreading kiln maintenance across two quarters. However, third-quarter 2026 operating profit of KRW 7.85 billion was down 39.0% from a year earlier, so the rebound did not carry forward.

Net income attributable to controlling shareholders over the last four quarters totals KRW 31.5 billion. The industry backdrop remains weak, with 2025 shipments at their lowest since 1991 and few clear recovery signals for 2026.

Strengths include a gradual gain in market share, a lower debt-to-equity ratio and the separate asset value of the Seongsu-dong site. Weaknesses include lower utilization, higher energy costs and uncertainty over development timing.

The next checkpoints are the Seongsu-dong construction decision, preliminary full-year 2026 results and the confirmed figures in the 2026 annual report.
