Report overview

Defense Growth Meets Core Business Losses

Welcron swung to a consolidated operating loss of KRW 11.1 billion on revenue of KRW 269 billion in 2025, but turned quarterly profitable in the second quarter of 2026 with revenue of KRW 72 billion, operating profit of KRW 2.18 billion and controlling net income of KRW 5.87 billion, raising the question of whether gains in defense and plant businesses can offset weakness in its core operations.

02

Business structure

Welcron Company was founded in 1992 and listed on KOSDAQ in 2003 as a functional textile materials specialist.

The company operates across microfiber cleaners, nanofiber products, super-fibers and eco-friendly fibers, while expanding into construction, plant engineering, renewable energy and waste recycling through its subsidiary Welcron Hantec.

Its living goods segment centers on functional bedding under the 'SESA' brand and microfiber cleaners, while nanofiber technology has extended beyond masks and filters into bulletproof vests, armor plates and add-on armor for defense applications.

As of 2024, revenue was split roughly 37.53% textiles, 22.63% construction, 26.60% plant engineering and 10.69% renewable energy, reflecting a shift from a single-industry textile firm to a diversified business structure.

In the defense segment, the company has supplied add-on armor for Hyundai Rotem's K808 wheeled armored vehicle and Hanwha Aerospace's Tigon armored vehicle. Welcron's domestic market share in armor plates is estimated at around 70%, with production capacity also assessed as among the largest domestically.

Welcron Hantec, which handles the plant and construction business, covers energy-saving industrial equipment, food and pharmaceutical facilities, environmental equipment, desalination and power-generation equipment, and general construction, and also supplies crystallization equipment for secondary-battery precursor and spent-battery recycling as well as water treatment systems for LNG power plants.

Hygiene affiliate Welcron Healthcare is not separately listed but is classified as a related party of Welcron subject to ownership disclosure.

The textile and living-goods market is mature with numerous domestic and overseas competitors, while defense materials involve high entry barriers and a smaller set of competitors.

04

Earnings analysis

Annual revenue declined from KRW 445.7 billion in 2022 to KRW 425.9 billion in 2023 (down 4.4%), KRW 329.1 billion in 2024 (down 22.7%), and KRW 269 billion in 2025 (down 18.3%), marking three consecutive years of decline from 2023 through 2025.

Operating profit also fell each year from KRW 6.3 billion in 2022 to KRW 4.85 billion in 2023 and KRW 2.17 billion in 2024, before swinging to an operating loss of KRW 11.1 billion in 2025.

Controlling net income likewise turned from a profit of KRW 644 million in 2022 to a net loss of KRW 4.61 billion in 2023, followed by losses of KRW 4.71 billion in 2024 and KRW 18.1 billion in 2025, extending losses for three straight years with widening magnitude.

On a quarterly basis, operating profit of KRW 846 million in the second quarter of 2025 turned into an operating loss of KRW 9.18 billion in the third quarter, a period in which, according to company performance commentary, one-off subsidiary-related provisions and bad-debt write-offs weighed on results.

The operating loss narrowed to KRW 1.82 billion in the fourth quarter, yet the controlling net loss widened to KRW 14.3 billion in the same period.

In the first quarter of 2026, despite a 13.0% quarter-on-quarter revenue decline driven by reduced orders and delayed construction in the plant and construction segment, operating profit turned positive at KRW 569 million on cost reductions and efficiency measures.

The second quarter of 2026 showed marked improvement, with revenue of KRW 72 billion, operating profit of KRW 2.18 billion (up 282.7% quarter on quarter and 157.8% year on year), and controlling net income of KRW 5.87 billion (up 2,007.6% year on year).

Still, the cumulative controlling net loss over the trailing four quarters (third quarter 2025 through second quarter 2026) stood at KRW 12.8 billion, making it premature to call a single profitable quarter a definitive trend reversal.

Net income including non-controlling interests widened from a loss of KRW 841 million in 2022 to a loss of KRW 49.2 billion in 2025, a far larger loss than the controlling net loss figure, suggesting a growing share of losses attributable to non-controlling interests at consolidated subsidiaries.

05

Industry analysis

The textile and living-goods segment is a mature market with numerous domestic and overseas competitors, exposing commodity products such as microfiber cleaners and bedding to pricing pressure.

By contrast, the defense materials segment has emerged as a growth driver amid expanding defense budgets and replacement demand for next-generation personal protective equipment, with reported replacement needs covering roughly 300,000 military personnel.

Welcron has broadened its product range from personal body armor and armor plates to add-on armor for tanks and armored vehicles, light tactical vehicles, military trucks and naval protective materials.

A supply-chain linkage is cited in which Welcron's add-on armor and armor plates could be bundled when major domestic defense firms such as Hanwha Aerospace and Hyundai Rotem export armored vehicles and weapons systems overseas.

However, the scale and timing of any resulting new orders for Welcron from expanded overseas defense exports still require confirmation.

In the plant and construction segment, growth opportunities are cited from recovering secondary-battery demand for precursor and spent-battery recycling equipment, as well as water-treatment equipment orders tied to the expansion of LNG combined-cycle power plants.

This segment serves precursor manufacturers, materials suppliers and battery-recycling firms, and has a track record supplying pure-water production, cooling-tower discharge reuse and wastewater treatment systems to power plants.

Overall, Welcron straddles two distinct cycles — a mature textile market and a higher-growth defense and energy-transition market — a structure that amplifies consolidated earnings volatility given differing profitability and growth paces across segments.

06

Outlook

Stunning Value Research stated in an October 19, 2026 report that it expects a profit turnaround in 2026 driven by synergy between maximized defense-segment utilization and expanded sales of ice bedding products.

The company has set a target to expand defense segment revenue to KRW 50 billion by 2029, roughly 4.5 times the 2024 level of KRW 11.1 billion.

On the production side, the company is described as having completed capacity expansion at its Gimhae defense plant and a second autoclave investment to support mass-production yields.

Stunning Value Research reported that, following a landmine protection kit export to Saudi Arabia, export contracts for tactical vehicle add-on armor to Poland, Romania, Indonesia and the Philippines are pending, though whether and at what scale these contracts are actually signed will need to be confirmed through future disclosures.

Subsidiary Welcron Hantec stated that its operating profit and net income turned positive in the first half of 2026 after financial burdens tied to the Sihwa Multi-Techno Valley project financing were resolved, and it reportedly plans to pursue selective orders centered on public-sector contracted construction and quality industrial facilities, along with expansion of its senior housing business.

Should the secondary-battery industry recovery and LNG power expansion materialize, opportunities for Welcron Hantec's crystallization and water-treatment equipment orders are cited as likely to increase, though this is based on a theoretical estimate of the overall market opportunity rather than confirmed order flow.

Overall, full-year 2026 results appear likely to hinge on the pace of improvement in the defense and plant segments versus stabilization in the textile and living-goods business.

08

Bull factors

High Growth and High Margins in Defense Materials

Independent research firm Stunning Value Research found that Welcron's defense segment revenue grew 132% from KRW 11.1 billion in 2024 to KRW 25.8 billion in 2025. Protective materials are characterized as high-margin consumables with limited seasonality, which some assessments say supports a floor for earnings.

Replacement demand for military protective equipment and supply-chain linkages with overseas defense firms are cited as additional growth opportunities. However, how much of this growth will translate into consolidated earnings improvement still requires further confirmation.

Return to Quarterly Profit in Q2 2026

Second-quarter 2026 revenue rose 21.8% quarter on quarter and 12.5% year on year to KRW 72 billion. Operating profit increased 282.7% quarter on quarter to KRW 2.18 billion, and controlling net income turned positive quarter on quarter at KRW 5.87 billion.

Operating cash flow also turned positive at KRW 21.7 billion in 2025, emerging from the cash outflow phase of 2023-2024. Whether this trajectory continues into subsequent quarters remains a key point to confirm.

Increased Shareholding by Controlling Investors

Controlling shareholder Lee Young-gyu and related parties disclosed an increase in their combined holding ratio to 22.83% as of September 28, 2026, up 0.81 percentage points from the prior report. Affiliate Welcron Healthcare also raised its stake from 2.84% to 3.22% through open-market purchases in September 2026.

Open-market purchases by executives including Shin Jeong-jae and Jeong Seong-sik were also reported. These are factual ownership changes, however, and should not be interpreted as a guarantee of future share price direction.

Bear factors

Structural Decline in Core Business Revenue

Consolidated revenue fell for three consecutive years from 2023 through 2025, declining from KRW 445.7 billion in 2022 to KRW 269 billion in 2025. Without a recovery in the core textile and living-goods business, growth in defense and plant segments alone may not be sufficient to reverse the overall revenue trend.

Revenue also fell 13.0% quarter on quarter in the first quarter of 2026 due to reduced orders and delayed construction in the plant and construction segment. This indicates that demand softness in non-defense segments remains ongoing.

Elevated Debt Ratio and Financial Structure Burden

The debt-to-equity ratio rose sharply from 163.1% in 2022 to 243.8% in 2024 and 444.9% in 2025. Total equity fell to KRW 60.4 billion at the end of 2025, of which controlling interest equity was only KRW 40.5 billion, indicating a weakened capital buffer.

Multiple convertible bond issuances in 2025 also contributed to the rise in liabilities. If financial structure improvement is delayed, pressure for additional fundraising could intensify.

Uncertain Durability of the Cumulative Loss Trend

The cumulative controlling net loss over the trailing four quarters (third quarter 2025 through second quarter 2026) was KRW 12.8 billion, making it premature to call a single profitable quarter in the second quarter of 2026 a definitive trend reversal.

Net income including non-controlling interests showed a much larger loss of KRW 49.2 billion in 2025 than the controlling net loss, indicating a sizable share of losses at consolidated subsidiaries.

In the third and fourth quarters of 2025, one-off subsidiary-related provisions and bad-debt write-offs weighed on results. The possibility of similar one-off factors recurring cannot be ruled out.

10

Risk factors

Convertible Bond and Financial Structure Risk

Welcron issued convertible bonds in six tranches between February and October 2025, with conversion prices set between KRW 5,758 and KRW 9,501. The bonds include refixing clauses that can periodically lower conversion prices, meaning potential dilution could change depending on share price movements.

The sequential arrival of bondholders' early redemption request windows is also a variable affecting cash needs. Given the already elevated debt ratio, continued monitoring of additional fundraising pressure is warranted.

Consolidated Subsidiary Risk

Independent research firm Finlit assessed that subsidiary Welcron Hantec could have borne up to KRW 42.45 billion in potential debt assumption risk related to the Sihwa Multi-Techno Valley project financing.

Reports in 2026 indicated this risk was being resolved, but a recurrence of similar contingent liabilities could again burden the consolidated financial statements.

The fact that net income including non-controlling interests has shown much larger losses than the controlling net loss also demonstrates how volatility at subsidiaries significantly affects consolidated results.

Continued monitoring of order intake and construction progress in the plant and construction segment is warranted.

Order and Export Uncertainty

According to reports on defense exports, tactical vehicle add-on armor export contracts with Poland, Romania, Indonesia and the Philippines are said to be pending, but whether these contracts are actually signed, their scale, and timing have not yet been confirmed through official disclosures.

The pace at which expanded overseas defense exports by Korean firms translate into new orders for Welcron also remains uncertain. The plant and construction segment is affected by shifts in public and private sector order volumes, raising the possibility of recurring order declines or construction delays. Investors should continue to monitor future order disclosures and confirmation of export contract signings.

12

Overall view

Welcron appears to be at an inflection point, having moved from years of declining revenue and profit since 2022 and a sizable loss in 2025 to a quarterly profit in the second quarter of 2026.

Growth and high margins in the defense segment, along with the resolution of project-financing risk at subsidiary Welcron Hantec, are cited as positive factors, but the structural decline in core textile and living-goods revenue and a debt ratio reaching 444.9% remain burdens.

On a trailing four-quarter basis, the controlling net loss persists, so whether the second-quarter 2026 improvement is one-off or trend-based will need to be confirmed with subsequent quarterly results.

Conversion prices on the 2025 convertible bonds are well above the current share price, limiting near-term dilution concerns, but refixing clauses and the sequential arrival of early redemption windows remain variables to monitor.

Whether defense export contracts are actually signed, the path toward the 2029 revenue target, and upcoming quarterly disclosures from the third quarter of 2026 onward are likely to be the key inputs for future assessment.
