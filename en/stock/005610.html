Back in Quarterly Profit; Overseas Sales and Hoppang in Focus

02

Business structure

Samlip operates a bakery business making bread, sandwiches and cakes, a food business handling flour, eggs, processed meat and fresh goods, a distribution business supplying food ingredients, and other businesses.

On a provisional January-to-September 2026 basis (announced November 16, 2026), distribution was the largest segment at KRW 1.3234 trillion, followed by bakery at KRW 636.2 billion and food at KRW 498.5 billion.

Distribution is centered on the ingredient distribution subsidiary SPC GFS; its sales rose 3.0% over the period, but operating profit fell 80.3% to KRW 1.1 billion. Bakery sales dipped 1.0% while operating profit fell 80.8% to KRW 4.5 billion. Food sales declined 9.2% and its operating loss widened to KRW 11.5 billion.

Weaker profitability in the manufacturing-based bakery and food units is the core backdrop to the 2026 earnings slump. The company cut bakery SKUs by 20.9% and food SKUs by 15.6% as of end-September 2026 by pruning low-margin products.

Food sales were also pressured by the exit from consumer-facing processed meat and a reduced gas station business. Overseas, expanded sales at Costco in the United States and rising cheesecake export volumes lifted global business sales to KRW 55.8 billion.

Samlip Hoppang, a key product, drives winter peak-season sales, and according to the company, sales through convenience store channels rose about 44% year on year in the first three weeks after launch.

04

Earnings analysis

Annual sales rose from KRW 3.31 trillion in 2022 to KRW 3.43 trillion in 2023, a 3.6% increase, making 2023 the largest year in the 2022 to 2025 span. Sales then fell 0.2% to KRW 3.43 trillion in 2024 and 1.7% to KRW 3.37 trillion in 2025, a two-year decline from 2024 through 2025.

Operating profit rose from KRW 89.5 billion in 2022 to KRW 91.7 billion in 2023 and KRW 95 billion in 2024, the largest year, before falling 59.2% to KRW 38.7 billion in 2025. The operating margin held at 2.7% to 2.8% in 2022 through 2024 and dropped to 1.1% in 2025.

Net profit attributable to controlling shareholders peaked at KRW 86.5 billion in 2024, up 72.2%, then fell 83.8% to KRW 14 billion in 2025. Net profit including non-controlling interests is disclosed at the same amounts as the controlling-shareholder figure in each year.

The debt ratio improved from 232.9% in 2022 to 158.5% in 2025, but total liabilities at the end of 2025 were KRW 763.3 billion against total equity of KRW 481.6 billion. Operating cash flow peaked at KRW 257.9 billion in 2024 and fell to KRW 88.2 billion in 2025. The quarterly pattern is sharper.

Operating profit fell from KRW 9.15 billion in the third quarter of 2025 to KRW 4.74 billion in the fourth quarter, down 48.1% quarter on quarter, and turned to an operating loss of KRW 4.3 billion in the first quarter of 2026.

It returned to profit at KRW 110 million in the second quarter and rose to KRW 1.93 billion in the third quarter, still down 78.9% year on year.

Net results attributable to controlling shareholders moved from net profit of KRW 655 million in the third quarter of 2025 to a net loss of KRW 125 million in the fourth quarter, KRW 6.81 billion in the first quarter of 2026, KRW 3.05 billion in the second quarter and KRW 2.27 billion in the third quarter.

Cost of sales in the first quarter of 2026 reached KRW 707.2 billion, up from KRW 690.1 billion a year earlier, adding pressure on margins.

05

Industry analysis

Samlip's demand spans convenience stores, large supermarkets and online channels, and in winter Samlip Hoppang drives seasonal peak demand. According to the company, convenience store sales rose year on year in the first three weeks after the Hoppang launch, and total sales across all channels rose about 20%.

On raw materials, higher wheat prices reduced results in the flour business, and broader input cost increases added pressure across the group, so grain price trends weigh heavily on margins by segment.

Competitively, Samlip is extending its share with new sandwich, chilled and dessert products built on its bakery base, while cutting SKUs to remove low-margin items, which narrows the product portfolio.

Overseas, sales through Costco in the United States are expanding, and the company is adding dedicated export production lines to build capacity. Overseas sales remain small relative to total revenue but are the fastest-growing part of the business.

An analyst at Shinhan Investment Corp. said the key variables for future earnings are the expansion of the higher-margin overseas share and the efficiency of lower-margin operations.

The domestic bakery business is in a phase where seasonal demand and cost inflation act at the same time, so the pace of recovery depends on input cost trends and growth in overseas sales.

06

Outlook

The company has set a 2030 standalone target of KRW 2.1107 trillion in sales, KRW 170 billion in operating profit and an 8% operating margin, as reported at the end of September 2026. It plans to lift standalone operating profit from KRW 5.4 billion in 2026 to KRW 50 billion in 2027.

Its four growth axes are overseas, D-commerce, frozen dough and product innovation, with more R&D and marketing spending. Global sales are targeted to rise from provisional January-to-September 2026 sales of KRW 55.8 billion to KRW 140 billion to KRW 150 billion in 2027 and KRW 500 billion in 2030.

The company plans to expand dedicated export production lines to 16 by 2027. Costco store count was reported in November 2026 to be expanding from 300 to 600. The company's full-year 2026 consolidated outlook, disclosed on November 16, 2026, shows sales of KRW 3.3898 trillion and operating profit of KRW 13.1 billion.

Given provisional January-to-September operating loss of KRW 2.26 billion, reaching that full-year operating profit implies a fourth quarter of around KRW 15 billion, and one newspaper reported an expected fourth-quarter operating profit of KRW 15.4 billion.

Whether the winter Hoppang sales strength supports this outlook is the near-term check. The next confirmation points are whether full-year 2026 results match the outlook and whether the 2027 standalone operating profit plan of KRW 50 billion shows up in quarterly results.

08

Bull factors

Fast overseas growth and export capacity build-out

Global business sales reached KRW 55.8 billion in provisional January-to-September 2026, up 52% year on year. The company targets KRW 140 billion to KRW 150 billion in 2027 and plans to expand dedicated export lines to 16 by 2027.

Monthly export volumes of key items such as cheesecake have doubled, according to the company. Because the overseas share is still small, there is room to change the business mix, which is the core bull argument.

Quarterly operating profit recovery

After a first-quarter 2026 operating loss of KRW 4.3 billion, the company returned to profit at KRW 110 million in the second quarter, and third-quarter operating profit rose to KRW 1.93 billion. The company expects Samlip Hoppang sales to lift fourth-quarter results.

Convenience store sales rose about 44% year on year in the first three weeks after launch. The sequential improvement in quarterly operating profit is the core bull point.

Profit-focused portfolio restructuring

As of end-September 2026, bakery SKUs were cut 20.9% and food SKUs 15.6%, with food already past its 15% reduction goal and bakery targeting 30%. The company has set a 2030 standalone operating margin target of 8% and operating profit of KRW 170 billion. The bull case rests on whether pruning low-margin lines translates into better profitability.

09

Bear factors

Weakened earnings base and continued losses

Operating profit for 2025 fell 59.2% to KRW 38.7 billion, and the operating margin dropped to 1.1%. Net losses attributable to controlling shareholders have continued for four quarters, so the timing of a return to profit is the next point to check.

Provisional January-to-September 2026 operating results show a loss of KRW 2.26 billion, compared with operating profit of KRW 33.99 billion a year earlier. A thinner earnings base is the core bear point.

Cost pressure and safety investment

Cost of sales in the first quarter of 2026 rose to KRW 707.2 billion from KRW 690.1 billion a year earlier. Higher wheat prices reduced the flour business's results, and in the third quarter higher safety-related investment and raw material costs weighed on operating profit.

First-quarter net results also reflected losses tied to the Siheung center fire. If cost increases are not recovered through pricing, margin pressure persists.

Weaker domestic bakery and food profitability

On a provisional January-to-September 2026 basis, bakery operating profit fell 80.8% to KRW 4.5 billion, and the food operating loss widened to KRW 11.5 billion. Distribution operating profit also fell 80.3% to KRW 1.1 billion. Third-quarter sales of KRW 823.4 billion were 4.6% lower than a year earlier. Weaker volume and profit in the core domestic businesses together form the bear argument.

10

Risk factors

Top-line shrinkage from restructuring

Product cuts, the exit from consumer processed meat and a smaller gas station business keep pressure on sales. Food sales fell 9.2% on a provisional January-to-September 2026 basis. Items cut to improve profitability could slow any sales recovery. Whether restructuring gains offset the loss of revenue is the key question.

Growth investment cost burden

The plan to raise R&D and marketing spending across the four growth axes of overseas, D-commerce, frozen dough and product innovation is moving into execution.

Because this spending is tied to the 2027 standalone operating profit target of KRW 50 billion, a sales ramp that lags the cost base could delay margin improvement. Early costs of new businesses may hit quarterly operating profit before revenue builds. Spending by growth axis and each axis's contribution to sales need to be tracked together.

Leverage and concentration in overseas growth

Total liabilities at the end of 2025 were KRW 763.3 billion against equity of KRW 481.6 billion. The debt ratio fell to 158.5% in 2025 but remains high. Growth is concentrated in overseas business and Costco sales expansion, so delays against targets could push back the timing of earnings recovery. Export facility spending and the pace of progress toward overseas sales targets are the next checkpoints.

Overall view

Samlip's net profit attributable to controlling shareholders peaked at KRW 86.5 billion in 2024, after which operating profit and net profit fell sharply in 2025, leaving a 1.1% operating margin for that year.

In 2026, a first-quarter operating loss of KRW 4.3 billion was followed by a return to profit in the second quarter and higher operating profit in the third, yet net losses attributable to controlling shareholders have run for four consecutive quarters, totaling KRW 12.3 billion over the last four.

Positive factors are 52% growth in global sales, expanding export capacity, strong Hoppang season sales and profit-focused restructuring through SKU cuts.

Negative factors are weaker profit in the bakery and food businesses, cost pressure from raw materials and safety investment, and a balance sheet where total liabilities exceed equity.

The company's full-year 2026 operating profit outlook of KRW 13.1 billion assumes a sharp fourth-quarter improvement given the provisional year-to-date operating loss. The next checkpoints are whether the quarterly profit trend carries into full-year recovery and whether overseas sales reach their target path. Valuation can only be compared against book value, since no earnings-based multiple can be calculated.
