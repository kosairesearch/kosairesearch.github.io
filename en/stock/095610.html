02

Business structure

Tesco, founded in September 2002, makes wafer front-end equipment for semiconductor fabs. Its core products are PECVD deposition tools and dry etch and clean equipment, and it has been widening its lineup to include the ACL thin-film tool, the Tetra tool and the BSD tool for DRAM.

According to SK Securities' July 2026 report, 2025 sales by segment were KRW 25.2 billion for semiconductor equipment, KRW 9.7 billion for parts and maintenance, and about KRW 0.2 billion for other items.

Its main customers are Samsung Electronics and SK hynix; a supply contract with SK hynix disclosed on January 26, 2026 was KRW 19.7 billion, equal to 8.20% of recent sales, and a KRW 48.5 billion contract was disclosed on March 12 of the same year.

On the display side, Tesco has developed thin-film encapsulation and MOCVD tools for UV LED and supplies Samsung Display and Chinese panel makers.

According to brokerage analysis reported in February 2026, Tesco is preparing to enter the hybrid bonding tool market for HBM packaging through joint development with HANMI Semiconductor.

Competitively, it shares the memory capex cycle with other front-end equipment makers, and its focus on specific process tools such as PECVD and ACL is a defining trait. The simultaneous mention of NAND reinvestment and DRAM expansion is also central to reading its business mix.

04

Earnings analysis

Full-year results started in 2022 with sales of KRW 358 billion, operating profit of KRW 55.9 billion and net profit attributable to shareholders of KRW 46.8 billion.

In 2023 sales fell 59.0% to KRW 146.9 billion and the company swung to an operating loss of KRW 5.86 billion, while net profit attributable to shareholders dropped 96.7% to KRW 1.57 billion.

In 2024 sales rose 63.4% to KRW 240.1 billion and operating profit turned positive at KRW 38.5 billion, with net profit attributable to shareholders reaching KRW 42.7 billion, up 2,623.8%.

In 2025 sales were KRW 351.1 billion (up 46.3%), operating profit KRW 57.8 billion (up 50.3%) and net profit attributable to shareholders KRW 56.9 billion (up 33.4%). Sales and net profit attributable to shareholders each rose for two consecutive years from 2024 through 2025, and 2022 was the largest year for sales.

The operating margin moved from 15.6% in 2022 to -4.0% in 2023 and recovered to 16.0% in 2024 and 16.5% in 2025. At the end of 2025, total equity was KRW 391.8 billion and total liabilities were KRW 95.7 billion, with the debt ratio rising from 9.7% in 2022 to 24.4% in 2025.

Operating cash flow grew from KRW 15.4 billion in 2022 to KRW 82.4 billion in 2025. On a quarterly basis, sales rose 73.1% to KRW 117 billion in the fourth quarter of 2025, fell 16.9% to KRW 97.2 billion in the first quarter of 2026, then rose to KRW 123.7 billion and KRW 124.1 billion in the second and third quarters.

Operating profit was KRW 22.2 billion, KRW 26 billion and KRW 24.6 billion across the first three quarters of 2026. Net profit attributable to shareholders went from KRW 24.6 billion to KRW 34 billion and then down to KRW 12 billion in the third quarter, and the last four quarters total KRW 80.6 billion.

The filings alone do not identify what drove the gap between third-quarter operating profit and net profit attributable to shareholders, so non-operating items and taxes are the next things to check in the annual report.

05

Industry analysis

The wafer front-end equipment market depends heavily on memory capex direction. In its July 2026 report, SK Securities noted that tool move-ins for Samsung Electronics' P4 PH4 and PH2 and SK hynix's M15X were picking up pace in the second half of 2026.

The same report said large investments in P5 and Y1 were slated for 2027, and that capacity build-outs could be up to twice the 2026 level. An April 2026 SK Securities report expected 2027 DRAM capacity additions to grow sharply versus 2026 as new fabs come online.

Signs of NAND investment resuming have also been reported. Brokers have repeatedly framed the memory expansion cycle as likely to extend. Tesco's position rests on PECVD tools, which one broker said carry higher order value per unit of NAND investment than for DRAM.

Its BSD tool is moving from NAND into DRAM applications, and analysts view it as a high-priced deposition tool with margin upside. Reports also indicate that China-bound tool supply has restarted.

The sector as a whole, however, is exposed to capex timing: when investment slips, orders and sales can move together, as Tesco's 59.0% sales decline in 2023 illustrates. Shares of peer front-end equipment makers often move with the same industry flow, which is worth weighing when judging where the cycle stands.

A May 2026 report said 2027 estimates could be raised again if BSD contributions to DRAM sales are confirmed.

A February 2026 brokerage analysis said Tesco is preparing for the hybrid bonding tool market for HBM4E and HBM5 generations through joint development with HANMI Semiconductor, and that this business could add about 20% of total sales from 2028 onward.

The same analysis projected 2026 sales of KRW 500 billion; that is an analyst estimate, not a company target. The next checkpoint is whether tool move-ins at Samsung Electronics' P4 and SK hynix's M15X proceed as planned in the second half of 2026.

Whether the planned P5 and Y1 investments turn into actual purchase orders can be tracked through the frequency of order disclosures. The timing of customer volume production for hybrid bonding tools has not been fixed, so commercialization speed is a key variable.

Because fourth-quarter and full-year 2026 results have not yet been filed, year-end order backlog and fourth-quarter operating margin are the next items to track.

Bull factors

Memory capacity plans extend

In its July 2026 report, SK Securities said large P5 and Y1 investments slated for 2027 could expand capacity up to twice the 2026 level. Tool move-ins for Samsung's P4 and SK hynix's M15X are also gathering pace in the second half of 2026. If these schedules turn into purchase orders, equipment sales visibility would rise.

Order backlog at a record level

According to an SK Securities estimate report, order backlog at the end of the first quarter of 2026 stood at KRW 145.5 billion, a record level. A KRW 48.5 billion contract with SK hynix was disclosed on March 12, 2026. With domestic lead times of two to three months, orders can convert into near-term sales relatively quickly.

New tools and hybrid bonding expansion

The BSD tool is described as moving from NAND into DRAM applications. A February 2026 brokerage analysis estimated the hybrid bonding business developed with HANMI Semiconductor could add about 20% of total sales from 2028 onward.

Bear factors

Sharp swings in quarterly net profit

Net profit attributable to shareholders in the third quarter of 2026 was KRW 12 billion, down 64.9% from KRW 34 billion in the second quarter. Operating profit in the same quarter was KRW 24.6 billion, down only 5.4% from the prior quarter, so the gap between operating profit and net profit widened sharply.

The last four quarters total KRW 80.6 billion, and quarterly swings of this size weigh on the stability of annual earnings.

Memory cycle and customer concentration

Sales fell 59.0% to KRW 146.9 billion in 2023, showing direct exposure to a memory downturn. Recent orders are heavily weighted toward SK hynix, and if Samsung's capacity schedule slips, tool move-ins and revenue recognition could be delayed together.

The shift from NAND-centered to DRAM-centered sales also means the timing of benefits depends on customer investment plans.

Rising debt and elevated expectations

The debt ratio rose from 9.7% in 2022 to 24.4% in 2025. Total liabilities at the end of 2025 were KRW 95.7 billion, still below total equity of KRW 391.8 billion, but the pace of increase is fast.

With the market value well above book equity, a slowdown in orders or earnings expectations could heighten share price sensitivity.

Risk factors

Capex cycle

When memory makers delay capex, orders and sales move down together, as the 59.0% sales decline in 2023 showed. Large P5 and Y1 investments are flagged for 2027, but their execution timing could shift with customer plans.

Customer concentration and tool move-in timing

Disclosed orders are concentrated with SK hynix. If tool move-ins at Samsung's P4 and SK hynix's M15X are delayed, quarterly revenue recognition could slip. The resumption of China-bound tool supply could be affected by external factors such as trade controls.

Commercialization timing of new businesses

Hybrid bonding tools are in joint development with HANMI Semiconductor, and the timing of customer volume production has not been fixed. The post-2028 expansion potential is a brokerage estimate. If commercialization slips, the expected revenue contribution would move later.

Overall view

Tesco's sales and operating profit both rose in 2025 against a backdrop of DRAM build-outs and wider tool offerings, and quarterly sales have held around KRW 100 billion through 2026.

Operating profit stayed at a high level of KRW 24.6 billion in the third quarter of 2026, but net profit attributable to shareholders fell to KRW 12 billion, so earnings quality and stability remain items to confirm.

Strengths include the record order backlog, the 2027 capacity plans and the hybrid bonding expansion, while weaknesses include the memory capex cycle, customer concentration and quarterly profit volatility.

Market value is well above total equity at the end of 2025, making the earnings outlook central to reading the valuation. The most recent broker target price cited here is KRW 230,000 from SK Securities' July 13, 2026 report, which reflects that broker's view.

The next checkpoints are the 2026 fourth-quarter preliminary results, the 2026 annual report and actual purchase orders for the 2027 P5 and Y1 investments.
