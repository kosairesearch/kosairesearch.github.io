Report overview

Consolidation Lifts Profit as Gold Trading Swings Revenue

Hancom WITH's consolidation of Hancom lifted its operating profit to KRW 9.43 billion in Q3 2026, but a KRW 28.6 billion net loss attributable to controlling shareholders and lower gold-trading revenue mark a mixed quarter.

  1. 1

    Q3 2026 revenue was KRW 128.9 billion (down 14.4% year on year) and operating profit was KRW 9.43 billion (up 493.3% year on year), with the consolidation effect showing first at the operating line.

  2. 2

    The same quarter posted a net loss of KRW 28.6 billion attributable to controlling shareholders, which the company attributes to a one-time, non-cash accounting loss from remeasuring its existing Hancom stake.

  3. 3

    Full-year 2025 consolidated revenue was KRW 771.2 billion (up 72.1%) and operating profit was KRW 6.09 billion (up 376.1%), the largest year in the 2022 to 2025 span.

  4. 4

    Over the last four quarters (Q4 2025 to Q3 2026), the net loss attributable to controlling shareholders totaled KRW 23.1 billion, so quarterly earnings stability is still to be confirmed.

  5. 5

    Management points to AI authentication and post-quantum cryptography (PQC) supply in security, and to defense and fire-safety deliveries by Hancom Lifecare as growth drivers.

02

Business structure

Hancom WITH is the holding company of the Hancom group. It began consolidating Hancom as a subsidiary in Q3 2026, after raising its sole stake in Hancom to 31.2%, while the controlling shareholder side including related parties holds 40.15%.

Hancom Lifecare, a Hancom subsidiary, has been included in consolidated results from the same point. The gold-trading business, run through a gold-trading subsidiary, was the main contributor to the 2025 revenue gain.

The security segment supplies AI authentication that detects deepfake faces and synthetic voices to financial and public institutions, extends continuous authentication after login, and applies post-quantum cryptography to data and communications encryption products sold to public, financial and defense customers.

The defense and fire-safety segment, centered on Hancom Lifecare, completed most deliveries of the K5 next-generation gas mask for the Korean army, and new fire-safety products such as lightweight air breathers and the PG-1 helmet are gaining traction.

In August 2026 the company signed a five-year exclusive distribution deal with a French robotics firm to enter unmanned firefighting robots. In 2026 it also sold a security subsidiary and removed it from consolidation.

04

Earnings analysis

On a consolidated basis, revenue rose from KRW 28.7 billion in 2022 to KRW 410.1 billion in 2023 (up 1,328.8%), KRW 448.2 billion in 2024 (up 9.3%) and KRW 771.2 billion in 2025 (up 72.1%), which is three consecutive years of growth from 2023 through 2025, with 2025 the largest year in the 2022 to 2025 span.

Operating loss of KRW 689 million in 2022 and KRW 2.46 billion in 2023 persisted, before operating profit of KRW 1.28 billion in 2024 turned the result positive and KRW 6.09 billion in 2025 rose 376.1%. The operating margin moved from -2.4% in 2022 to -0.6% in 2023, 0.3% in 2024 and 0.8% in 2025.

The company said 2025 was a record year, and it attributed growth to the competitiveness of its gold-trading subsidiary combined with a rise in global gold prices.

Profit attributable to controlling shareholders fell from KRW 6.81 billion in 2022 to KRW 900 million in 2023 (down 86.8%), turned into a net loss of KRW 3.82 billion in 2024, and returned to a KRW 7.84 billion profit in 2025.

Net profit including non-controlling interests, at KRW 9.37 billion in 2025, is a different figure. Operating cash flow improved from KRW -4.8 billion in 2023 to KRW 1.9 billion in 2024 and KRW 6.5 billion in 2025.

The debt ratio rose from 56.3% in 2022 and 48.0% in 2023 to 54.2% in 2024 and 67.3% in 2025, the highest of the period.

Quarterly revenue peaked at KRW 274 billion in Q4 2025 (up 82.0% quarter on quarter), fell to KRW 166 billion in Q1 2026 and KRW 99.8 billion in Q2 2026, then recovered to KRW 128.9 billion in Q3 2026.

Q3 2026 operating profit of KRW 9.43 billion rose 493.3% year on year, while the net loss attributable to controlling shareholders of KRW 28.6 billion reflected a one-time, non-cash remeasurement loss on the existing Hancom stake, as the company explained. Over the last four quarters, the net loss attributable to controlling shareholders totaled KRW 23.1 billion.

05

Industry analysis

In security, demand for zero-trust is widening, and continuous authentication that re-checks users after login is finding more applications. Rising deepfake threats are also lifting demand for authentication that detects AI-generated faces and synthetic voices.

Post-quantum cryptography targets high-trust sectors such as public agencies, finance and defense.

In domestic security software the company competes with players such as Ahnlab, and it has set out a plan to become a specialist AI security platform company by extending PQC cryptographic modules across industries and building global competitiveness in deepfake detection.

Gold trading is sensitive to international gold prices: revenue fell in Q3 2026 as adjustments in gold prices reduced physical gold distribution volumes at its gold-trading subsidiary. The 2025 gain was analyzed as the product of higher global gold prices and stronger safe-haven demand.

The RWA business, which tokenizes physical gold, splits roles with a partner that handles sourcing and storage of the gold. The company has secured a 40% stake in a joint venture and is pursuing approval from the Dubai virtual asset regulator.

Defense deliveries form a revenue stream that does not depend on gold prices, so the business mix is moving away from gold trading alone.

06

Outlook

Management said it intends to make the results of its digital finance and security businesses more concrete in 2026. In security, the company plans to commercialize a 'no-awareness authentication' technology that identifies users without extra actions, and to widen the range of industries using AI biometric solutions.

Expanding the use of continuous authentication is part of the same plan. The CEO said the group's earnings base has thickened and that the company intends to produce results in its own businesses, including AI authentication and post-quantum cryptography.

In the physical-gold-backed digital asset business, a partner handles sourcing and custody of physical gold while the company builds a token issuance and redemption system backed by that gold.

The outcome of the company's pursuit of approval from the Dubai virtual asset regulator will shape how quickly the business can launch. The digital gold asset trading platform that the company planned to unveil in Q1 2026 should be tracked through later filings and reports for whether and when it opened.

In zero-trust, the company is taking part in a government pilot project with a travel company as the demand provider, so whether the pilot moves to full deployment will shape the expansion of security revenue.

On the consolidated side, the key checkpoint is whether the consolidation effect holds quarter by quarter from Q4 2026. The next periodic report is the 2026 annual report, which will fix full-year operating margin and profit attributable to controlling shareholders.

08

Bull factors

Wider consolidated earnings base

After consolidation, Q3 2026 operating profit reached KRW 9.43 billion, up 493.3% year on year. Hancom Lifecare, newly included in the consolidated results, posted Q3 revenue of KRW 30.9 billion and operating profit of KRW 3.4 billion, with revenue up 31% and an operating margin of 11.0%. Subsidiary earnings are now being added to the consolidated statements.

Expanding security technology supply

The company supplies AI authentication that detects deepfake faces and synthetic voices to financial and public institutions, and plans to widen continuous authentication after login. It has completed registration of core post-quantum cryptography patents and applied them to communications and data encryption products. Patents together with product applications form the base of the security business.

Defense and fire-safety deliveries

Most deliveries of the K5 next-generation gas mask for the Korean army's ninth production lot have been completed, and laser target designator deliveries were also booked. As deliveries that do not move with gold prices grow, the earnings dependence on gold prices should decline.

09

Bear factors

Volatility in gold-trading revenue

Q3 2026 revenue fell 14.4% year on year, as gold price adjustments reduced physical gold distribution at its gold-trading subsidiary. The gold-trading business was the main driver of 2025 earnings improvement. Because revenue can swing sharply if gold prices change direction, this is a bearish factor.

Accumulated losses for controlling shareholders

The net loss attributable to controlling shareholders in Q3 2026 was KRW 28.6 billion, which the company described as a one-time, non-cash valuation loss. Even so, the four-quarter total from Q4 2025 to Q3 2026 remains a net loss of KRW 23.1 billion.

Whether profit attributable to controlling shareholders builds steadily each quarter is a point for the next periodic report.

Rising financial leverage

The debt ratio rose from 56.3% in 2022 and 48.0% in 2023 to 54.2% in 2024 and 67.3% in 2025. Operating cash flow improved to KRW 6.5 billion in 2025, but that is small against KRW 771.2 billion of revenue. Because the wider consolidation adds both assets and liabilities, financial structure volatility could also rise.

10

Risk factors

Gold prices and distribution

Revenue depends on physical gold distribution volumes and gold prices, which makes quarterly results volatile. The company attributed the Q3 2026 revenue decline to gold price adjustments reducing distribution volumes. Adding gold-backed digital asset business could increase exposure to both price and regulation.

Accounting and consolidation scope

Remeasurement of the existing stake flowed into the net loss, moving quarterly net profit sharply. Consolidation scope changes with ownership levels and disposals, and a security subsidiary was removed from consolidation in 2026.

The gap between operating profit and profit attributable to controlling shareholders needs to be read separately.

Commercialization and regulation

The digital asset and RWA business is still in the stage of pursuing approval from the Dubai virtual asset regulator, so approval and timing will set the pace. The zero-trust project is still a pilot, so the timing of any move to full deployment is uncertain. Expansion of security technology also depends on how quickly financial and public institutions adopt it.

  2. Late March 2027 (statutory filing deadline for the annual report)

  3. Timing not set (in progress)

  4. Timing not set (pilot under way)

    The results of the zero-trust pilot run with a government ministry and a national cybersecurity agency, and whether it moves to full deployment. This is the marker for the potential expansion of security revenue.

12

Overall view

Hancom WITH posted Q3 2026 operating profit of KRW 9.43 billion, turning back to a quarterly profit on the consolidation of Hancom.

Revenue for the same quarter fell 14.4% year on year as gold distribution volumes dropped, and the KRW 28.6 billion net loss attributable to controlling shareholders reflected a one-time accounting loss tied to the consolidation.

The 2025 filed results showed revenue of KRW 771.2 billion and operating profit of KRW 6.09 billion, both larger than in 2024, and that growth was closely tied to the gold-trading business and rising gold prices.

Bullish factors include a wider consolidated earnings base, defense and fire-safety deliveries, and expanding AI authentication and PQC supply in security.

Bearish factors include revenue volatility tied to gold prices, a KRW 23.1 billion cumulative net loss attributable to controlling shareholders over the last four quarters, and a rising debt ratio.

Valuation can be read only through the price-to-book relationship and the direction of earnings recovery, since the recent losses prevent a PER calculation.

The next checkpoints are the operating margin and profit attributable to controlling shareholders in the 2026 annual report, and the outcome of the Dubai approval for the RWA joint venture.
