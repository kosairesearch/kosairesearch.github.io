02

Business structure

Hwashin Precision is an auto parts maker headquartered in Yeongcheon, Gyeongbuk, operating automotive chassis parts, precision machining, slitting products, aftermarket parts and raw material processing.

The stock is classified under the aerospace and defense sector, but the business that generates its earnings is automotive parts. By product group, chassis parts and slitting products appear as the leading categories (Jobkorea business analysis, as of March 2026).

The core chassis line consists of lightweight aluminum parts such as lower arms, supplied to Hyundai Motor and KIA dedicated EV platform (E-GMP) models.

Supplied models began with the GV70 EV and IONIQ 6 in 2022, expanded to EV3, IONIQ 9 and LX3 in 2024 and to EV4, EV5, Tasman and PV5 in 2025, and the Staria EV (US4 EV) joined the lineup in April 2026.

As of the first quarter of 2026, affiliate Hwashin contributed KRW 65.4 billion of revenue and Hyundai Mobis KRW 20.4 billion, with the top two customers accounting for more than 70% of total sales. Volume is tied to the production plans of the automaker group, so customer concentration is high.

In 2026 the company outlined plans to extend its aluminum lightweighting technology into humanoid robot parts and industrial AI, and in April 2026 it was selected for a regional innovation R&D program. Competition centers on cost and quality among first-tier suppliers to automakers.

04

Earnings analysis

Annual revenue rose from KRW 317.9 billion in 2022 to KRW 359.9 billion in 2023 (up 13.2%), fell to KRW 322 billion in 2024 (down 10.5%), and rose to KRW 378.9 billion in 2025 (up 17.7%), the largest year in the 2022-2025 span.

Operating profit fell from KRW 9.3 billion in 2022 to KRW 8.71 billion in 2023 (down 6.3%) and KRW 1.34 billion in 2024 (down 84.6%), the smallest year in the span, before recovering to KRW 11.8 billion in 2025 (up 778.7%).

In 2024 the operating margin dropped to 0.4% as revenue fell 10.5%, and in 2025 it reached 3.1%, above the 2.9% of 2022.

Profit attributable to controlling shareholders was KRW 7.08 billion in 2022, KRW 9.03 billion in 2023 (up 27.6%), KRW 2.64 billion in 2024 (down 70.8%) and KRW 10.3 billion in 2025 (up 291.2%), and net income was reported at the same amounts in each year.

On a quarterly basis, revenue was KRW 104.5 billion in Q4 2025 (up 19.3% quarter on quarter) with operating profit of KRW 4.18 billion, then peaked in Q1 2026 at KRW 121.3 billion (up 16.1% quarter on quarter) with operating profit of KRW 12.2 billion (up 190.9%).

The first-quarter operating profit alone exceeded the full-year 2025 figure of KRW 11.8 billion. Press coverage cited the expansion of EV parts supply as the driver of that rebound.

Q2 2026 revenue then fell to KRW 101.5 billion (down 16.3% quarter on quarter) with operating profit of KRW 10.3 billion (down 15.5%), and Q3 2026 revenue was KRW 83.9 billion (down 4.2% year on year) with operating profit of KRW 2.4 billion (up 634.8% year on year), marking two consecutive quarterly declines after Q1.

Operating profit fell faster than revenue, which suggests cost and fixed-expense pressure, though the detailed causes are to be checked in the quarterly report footnotes. Operating cash flow was KRW 16.9 billion in 2022, KRW 18.7 billion in 2023, KRW 412 million in 2024 and KRW 29.2 billion in 2025.

At the end of 2025, total liabilities were KRW 79.4 billion and total equity KRW 118.5 billion, with a debt ratio of 67.0%.

05

Industry analysis

The key demand driver is the expansion of EV lineups at automakers. Since 2022 Hwashin Precision has widened its chassis supply alongside a rising number of Hyundai Motor and KIA dedicated EV platform models, and the Staria EV joined the lineup in April 2026.

This places the company in a segment where lightweight aluminum parts demand is growing relative to combustion-engine parts. Supply volumes, however, are tied to the production plans of specific models, so a production change in one model feeds directly into quarterly revenue.

As a first-tier supplier supplying EV platform parts within an automaker group, its high customer concentration makes negotiating position and volume allocation major variables for earnings volatility. Robot parts were the focus of expectations in June 2026.

Press reports said Boston Dynamics engineers visited the Yeongcheon headquarters to examine possible parts supply, but no official supply contract or project participation has been confirmed.

Although classified under aerospace and defense, the business is automotive parts, so it is difficult to read it through the same demand cycle as defense companies.

All treasury shares were cancelled in November 2025.

On the business side, the Staria EV joined the lineup in April 2026, and expansion into humanoid robot parts and industrial AI using aluminum lightweighting technology remains at the planning stage. Participation in an industrial AI solution demonstration program began in May 2026. Three points are worth tracking.

First, whether Q4 2026 operating profit recovers from the KRW 2.4 billion of Q3 2026, which will be visible in the 2026 annual report. Second, whether a robot parts supply contract is confirmed through a disclosure. Third, whether the payout target carries beyond the 2026 fiscal year.

Quarterly results also depend on the EV production schedules of key customers, so those schedules should be tracked alongside company filings.

Bull factors

Expanding EV platform supply

Chassis supply expanded from the GV70 EV and IONIQ 6 in 2022 to the Staria EV in April 2026, all on dedicated EV platforms. As automakers add electrified models, the number of lightweight aluminum part programs has grown alongside, and this accumulation of supplied models widens the earnings base.

Profit recovery trend

Operating profit recovered from KRW 1.34 billion in 2024 to KRW 11.8 billion in 2025, up 778.7%, and the first quarter of 2026 alone reached KRW 12.2 billion, above the full-year 2025 figure. The operating margin moved from 0.4% in 2024 to 3.1% in 2025, reversing the earlier decline. Q3 2026 operating profit of KRW 2.4 billion was still up 634.8% year on year.

Financial stability and shareholder returns

At the end of 2025, total equity was KRW 118.5 billion and total liabilities KRW 79.4 billion, a debt ratio of 67.0%. Press coverage reports that the NICE credit rating of A- has held for three consecutive years from 2024. The 2026 value-up plan targets a payout of around 25%, and all treasury shares were cancelled in November 2025.

Bear factors

Two straight quarterly profit declines

Operating profit fell from KRW 12.2 billion at the Q1 2026 peak to KRW 10.3 billion in Q2 and KRW 2.4 billion in Q3, two consecutive declines. Q3 revenue of KRW 83.9 billion was down 4.2% year on year, so both sales and profit weakened together.

Whether the first-quarter level was sustainable will be clearer from the quarterly trend in upcoming periodic reports.

Customer concentration

As of the first quarter of 2026, affiliate Hwashin and Hyundai Mobis together accounted for more than 70% of revenue. High dependence on group volume means that customer production plans or platform changes can move earnings directly. Because stable group demand is also a bull point, concentration is a factor that cuts both ways.

Unverified robot expectations

In June 2026 the share price rose sharply in a short period on robot parts expectations, and the Korea Exchange designated the stock as an investment-warning issue citing overheating. No official supply contract or project participation has been confirmed. Coverage noted that if expectations are not realized, volatility could increase.

Risk factors

Customer and platform concentration

The top two customers account for more than 70% of revenue, so production adjustments or model discontinuations at a key customer flow straight into sales. If EV platform models are replaced or volumes decline, supply volumes can fall quickly. As supplied models have grown, dependence on individual models also needs monitoring.

Quarterly earnings volatility

Quarterly operating profit has swung widely, from KRW 327 million in Q3 2025 to KRW 4.18 billion in Q4 2025, KRW 12.2 billion in Q1 2026 and KRW 2.4 billion in Q3 2026. The operating margin also fell to 0.4% in 2024. Given such swings, a single quarter is not enough to judge the trend.

Share price volatility and expectation testing

The share price rose sharply in June 2026, leading to an investment-warning designation, and the exchange stated that further steps such as trading halts could follow if the stock rises beyond a certain level. If results from robot parts business do not materialize, price expectations could be reset. The share price and reported earnings can move along different paths.

Overall view

Hwashin Precision recovered from its 2024 low, with operating profit reaching KRW 11.8 billion in 2025, up 778.7%. After peaking at KRW 12.2 billion in Q1 2026, operating profit declined to KRW 10.3 billion in Q2 and KRW 2.4 billion in Q3, two consecutive quarterly declines.

Q3 2026 revenue of KRW 83.9 billion was down 4.2% year on year, and profit attributable to controlling shareholders over the last four quarters totals KRW 23.5 billion. Bull factors are expanding supply to dedicated EV platform models, the earnings recovery since 2025, the stated payout target and the A- credit rating.

Bear factors are two straight quarters of profit decline, customer concentration with the top two accounting for more than 70% of revenue, and the lack of official confirmation for robot parts expectations.

Both sides carry equal weight, and reading share price moves tied to robot expectations separately from reported earnings gives the more accurate picture. The sector label is aerospace and defense, but the earnings base is automotive parts.

The next checkpoints are the fourth-quarter operating profit and dividend decision in the 2026 annual report, and the quarterly profit level in the Q1 2027 quarterly report.
