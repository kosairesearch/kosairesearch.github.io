Pivoting to AI Data Centers, but Losses and Financial Strain Remain

OptiCore is shifting its revenue base from 5G telecom optical transceivers to 400G and 800G transceivers for AI data centers. Net income attributable to controlling shareholders turned positive in the third quarter of 2026, but operating losses and capital impairment remain.

02

Business structure

OptiCore was founded in 2016 on optical communication laser technology and listed on KOSDAQ in 2022 through a merger with a SPAC. Its core products are optical transceivers used in access networks, supplying tunable-wavelength 10Gbps and 25Gbps modules for the fronthaul and midhaul segments of 5G wireless networks.

The tunable-wavelength design lets carriers manage inventory with a single module type instead of stocking different wavelengths for each base station. The company also makes optical multiplexers and related parts, and has been a supplier to SK Telecom's 5G smart concentrator program.

As capital spending by telecom customers fell, revenue from the legacy business declined, and the company has positioned 400G and 800G transceivers for data centers as its new growth driver.

Data center supply contracts disclosed in September 2025 (about KRW 6.7 billion), December 2025 (KRW 6 billion) and December 2025 (KRW 4 billion) were reported to bring cumulative orders above KRW 10 billion.

Eugene Investment & Securities estimated in a report dated March 16, 2026 that the data-com share of revenue would rise from 43% in 2025 to 70% in 2026 and 82% in 2027; these are estimates. R&D spending exceeded KRW 2 billion each year from 2022 to 2024, and recurring R&D expense was about KRW 2.9 billion in 2024.

A company information site lists 13 patents and 14 government projects. Because the company does not disclose customer information, customer concentration and market share are not presented in figures.

04

Earnings analysis

Annual revenue went from KRW 24.3 billion in 2022 to KRW 26.3 billion in 2023, an 8.3% increase that marked the peak, before falling to KRW 23.3 billion in 2024 (down 11.5%) and KRW 21.5 billion in 2025 (down 7.7%).

Revenue declined for two consecutive years after 2023, which was the largest year in the 2022 to 2025 period, while 2025 was the smallest. Operating losses were KRW 2.68 billion in 2022, KRW 4.06 billion in 2023, KRW 6.52 billion in 2024 and KRW 5.93 billion in 2025, a four-year run of losses.

Operating margins were -11.0%, -15.4%, -28.0% and -27.6% over the same years, so the loss ratio widened as revenue shrank. In 2025 the operating loss was smaller than in 2024, but the margin was still -27.6%.

Net loss attributable to controlling shareholders was KRW 10.8 billion in 2022, KRW 4.37 billion in 2023, KRW 6.54 billion in 2024 and KRW 22.5 billion in 2025.

The 2025 consolidated net loss, including non-controlling interests, was KRW 22.6 billion, and the disclosure attributed the increase mainly to higher non-operating expenses, including financing costs and fair-value losses linked to convertible bonds and financial assets.

The debt ratio rose from 70.3% in 2022 to 76.7%, 107.6% and 201.8% in 2025. At the end of 2025, total equity was KRW 22.9 billion, of which controlling shareholders' equity was KRW 23 billion, and total liabilities were KRW 46.2 billion.

Operating cash flow moved from a KRW 1.53 billion inflow in 2022 to outflows of -KRW 705 million in 2023, -KRW 1.3 billion in 2024 and -KRW 14.8 billion in 2025. On a quarterly basis, revenue was KRW 3.13 billion in the third quarter of 2025 with an operating loss of KRW 1.42 billion.

It rose to KRW 13.2 billion in the fourth quarter (up 321.5% from the prior quarter) with an operating loss of KRW 828 million, which coincides with the timing of data center orders starting to be recognized.

Revenue was KRW 11.9 billion in the first quarter of 2026 (down 9.8%) with an operating loss of KRW 661 million, then fell to KRW 3.39 billion in the second quarter (down 71.5%) with an operating loss of KRW 1.87 billion.

In the third quarter of 2026, revenue was KRW 4.82 billion (up 42.2% from the prior quarter and up 53.9% year on year) and the operating loss narrowed to KRW 669 million.

Net income attributable to controlling shareholders turned positive at KRW 3.41 billion in the same quarter, although an operating loss remained, so the contribution of non-operating items should be checked in the detailed line items of the periodic report.

On the disclosed figures, the company has posted operating losses for five consecutive quarters from the third quarter of 2025 to the third quarter of 2026. Net loss attributable to controlling shareholders over the last four quarters (fourth quarter of 2025 to third quarter of 2026) totaled KRW 15.6 billion.

05

Industry analysis

Demand for optical transceivers is moving in different directions across telecom and data centers. In telecom, the company says slower 5G subscriber growth has reduced new network build-out capex, shifting spending toward maintaining and upgrading existing equipment and shrinking the domestic market.

Industry observers point to slower 5G growth as a key reason that R&D results have been converted into revenue later than expected.

On the data center side, Eugene Investment & Securities views the active build-out of domestic data centers and rising demand for localized transceivers as creating room for OptiCore, which holds reference customers, to keep winning orders.

The shift depends on supplying 800G and 1.6T products to hyperscalers, which would raise the data-com share of revenue. A company information site describes the technical completeness and commercialization capability of its tunable-wavelength solution as strengths relative to major competitors.

Those technology advantages still need to be tested through repeated orders from large data center customers, which quarterly revenue will show. Domestic data center investment and localization policy are further variables that will determine how much demand actually rises. Whether telecom customers resume equipment investment also remains a factor that could change the revenue mix.

06

Outlook

The company says it is pursuing its existing 5G telecom equipment business and its data center equipment business in parallel. Cumulative data center orders reached above KRW 10 billion as of the end of 2025, with the contract period reported to run through the end of March 2026.

At the time of reporting, revenue recognition for these contracts was expected to be concentrated in the first quarter of 2026. Fourth-quarter 2025 revenue of KRW 13.2 billion and first-quarter 2026 revenue of KRW 11.9 billion are consistent with that timing.

However, second-quarter 2026 revenue fell to KRW 3.39 billion and the operating loss widened to KRW 1.87 billion. This suggests that whether follow-on orders can sustain quarterly revenue after a concentrated delivery period is the central question.

Eugene Investment & Securities estimated in its March 16, 2026 report that the operating margin would improve from -12% in 2025 to 10% in 2026 and 23% in 2027. That estimate reflects assumptions at the time of publication and still needs to be tested against reported results.

The same report flagged potential dilution from convertible bonds and overhang risk as variables. In December 2025, a fund linked to a non-bank lender was reported to have subscribed to KRW 5 billion of the company's convertible bonds.

The next checkpoint is the 2026 annual business report, where full-year revenue recovery and operating margin change can be compared with 2025.

Revenue volatility and order continuity

Risk factors

Audit opinion and listing status

A March 2026 report said the 2025 financial statements received an audit disclaimer, with the auditor stating it could not obtain sufficient audit evidence. The report said this raised the possibility of delisting.

Whether the audit opinion is resolved and the outcome of exchange review should be checked in subsequent disclosures.

Governance and fund transactions

After the founder sold most of his stake in July 2025, Black Mountain Holdings became the largest shareholder. Reports cited a KRW 13 billion property purchase and KRW 10.5 billion in loans to subsidiaries and related entities.

A shareholder filed a complaint with the Financial Supervisory Service in September 2025, and the regulator was reported to be reviewing the facts.

Dilution and failure of the business shift

Potential dilution and overhang from convertible bonds were flagged as variables in the Eugene report. Industry observers note that if data center orders prove one-off, resolving capital impairment could take longer. If 800G and 1.6T share gains are slower than expected, the projected data-com revenue share could fall short.

On the disclosed figures, revenue peaked at KRW 26.3 billion in 2023 and then declined for two consecutive years, while operating losses have continued for four consecutive years from 2022 through 2025.
