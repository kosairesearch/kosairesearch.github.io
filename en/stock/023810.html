Infac: Sales Growth Versus Narrowing Profits

Infac's revenue rose for three straight years from 2023 through 2025, but operating profit fell in 2024 and 2025, and the company swung to a net loss attributable to controlling shareholders in the third quarter of 2026.

02

Business structure

Infac is an automotive parts company founded in 1969 that mainly produces automotive actuators, solenoid valves and mechanical cables. The company has 11 affiliates in Korea and overseas, and INFAC POLAND serves as its overseas production base.

Its product lines include antenna and nozzle actuators, ignition cables, valves and injection-molded parts, control cables and horns, and it is widening its range into power electronics such as PCB assemblies as demand for eco-friendly vehicles grows.

Export accounted for 58.6% of sales at the end of 2025 according to fnguide's breakdown, and some products such as electronic parking brake (EPB) cables are shipped entirely overseas.

Its customers include domestic automakers Hyundai Motor, KIA and Hyundai Mobis, along with overseas OEMs such as GM, Volkswagen and Brembo. During the electrification shift, the company has been expanding supply of battery modules, EPB and ECS parts. R&D spending was KRW 24.6 billion in 2025, equal to 4.2% of sales.

Price competition among electrified-parts suppliers has intensified, so sales growth has not translated into better profitability.

04

Earnings analysis

Annual revenue went from KRW 512.2 billion in 2022 to KRW 542.9 billion in 2023 (up 6.0% year on year), KRW 563.9 billion in 2024 (up 3.9%) and KRW 586.4 billion in 2025 (up 4.0%), rising three years in a row from 2023 through 2025, with 2025 the largest year in the 2022 to 2025 span.

Operating profit was KRW 20.8 billion in 2022, peaked at KRW 23.5 billion in 2023 (up 13.0%), then fell 14.9% to KRW 20 billion in 2024 and 1.2% to KRW 19.8 billion in 2025, a two-year decline.

Sales grew while operating profit shrank, and operating margin moved from 4.1% in 2022 and 4.3% in 2023 down to 3.6% in 2024 and 3.4% in 2025.

Net profit attributable to controlling shareholders fell from KRW 13.2 billion in 2022 to KRW 11.1 billion in 2023 (down 15.8%) and KRW 2.33 billion in 2024 (down 79.1%), before recovering to KRW 12 billion in 2025 (up 414.1%).

Total net profit including non-controlling interests was KRW 16.9 billion in 2022, KRW 15.4 billion in 2023, KRW 7.27 billion in 2024 and KRW 18.1 billion in 2025, so in 2025 the controlling-shareholder profit rose sharply while operating profit edged lower.

In the third quarter of 2025, revenue was KRW 148.6 billion, operating profit KRW 6.44 billion and controlling-shareholder net profit KRW 940 million; in the fourth quarter, revenue fell 3.6% to KRW 143.3 billion and an operating loss of KRW 4.64 billion turned the quarter into a loss.

In the first quarter of 2026, revenue rose 10.4% to KRW 158.1 billion and operating profit of KRW 2.01 billion returned to profit, and in the second quarter revenue rose 21.5% to KRW 192.1 billion with operating profit of KRW 8.55 billion (up 324.7% from the prior quarter).

In the third quarter of 2026, revenue was KRW 173.4 billion (down 9.7% from the prior quarter, up 16.7% year on year), operating profit fell to KRW 1.06 billion (down 83.6% year on year) and a controlling-shareholder net loss of KRW 2.47 billion marked a swing to loss from a year earlier.

Controlling-shareholder net profit for the last four quarters totaled KRW 2.19 billion. Operating cash flow was KRW 48 billion in 2022, KRW 40.6 billion in 2023 and KRW 18.3 billion in 2024, then rose to KRW 99.3 billion in 2025.

The debt ratio rose from 198.7% in 2022 to 281.3% in 2025, with total liabilities of KRW 493.7 billion and total equity of KRW 175.5 billion at the end of 2025.

05

Industry analysis

Demand from electric vehicles, the main downstream market, has slowed. According to fnguide's company overview summary, EV growth has cooled, but battery safety technology development and new model launches continue, and the shift toward electrification is expected to continue.

In cycle terms, demand for internal-combustion parts is declining while demand for electrified parts is rising, so the industry is in a transition period.

On pricing, competition among electrified-parts suppliers has intensified, adding pressure on unit prices, according to the commentary accompanying Wisereport's first-quarter 2026 results.

On customers, domestic demand is centered on Hyundai Motor, KIA and Hyundai Mobis, so production at domestic automakers feeds directly into revenue. With exports at 58.6% of sales at the end of 2025, exchange rates and overseas OEM production are also key variables.

Listed peers in the same sector include Hanon Systems, Hyundai WIA, Sungwoo Hitech and Myoung Shin Industry, all of which compete for electrified-parts orders. The pace at which electrified products replace combustion-related sales will largely determine the path to profit recovery.

Because margin structures for electrified products may differ from those of existing products, the same revenue gain can contribute differently to operating profit.

06

Outlook

Based on confirmed facts, Infac has been expanding supply of battery modules, EPB and ECS parts from the first quarter through the third quarter of 2026, according to Wisereport's company results commentary.

The company has said it plans to develop actuator controllers and charging converters ahead of demand to broaden its supply chain. Revenue rising to KRW 192.1 billion in the second quarter of 2026 suggests the supply expansion showed up in sales.

However, the operating margin was strong only in the second quarter and weakened again in the third quarter. In the third quarter of 2026, revenue fell 9.7% from the prior quarter while operating profit dropped to KRW 1.06 billion, so lower sales coincided with weaker profitability.

Results for the fourth quarter of 2026 have not yet been filed in a periodic report, so the comparison against the fourth quarter of 2025 operating loss of KRW 4.64 billion is the next quarterly reference point.

On a full-year basis, whether operating profit holds near the 2025 level of KRW 198 billion is the key check. The 2026 annual business report will be the first periodic filing that fixes full-year 2026 earnings and the balance sheet.

With the debt ratio at 281.3%, whether electrified products improve profitability will shape the medium-term trajectory.

Bear factors

Operating profit peaked at KRW 23.5 billion in 2023, then fell to KRW 20 billion in 2024 and KRW 19.8 billion in 2025, a two-year decline. The operating margin dropped from 4.1% in 2022 to 3.4% in 2025. Sales growth has not carried through to profit.

Intensifying price competition among electrified-parts suppliers is cited as the factor weighing on profitability.

Higher financial leverage

The debt ratio rose from 198.7% in 2022 to 281.3% in 2025. Total liabilities of KRW 493.7 billion far exceed total equity of KRW 175.5 billion. Non-controlling interests have grown, widening the gap between total profit and the share attributable to controlling shareholders.

Risk factors

EV demand and price competition

EV market growth has slowed while price competition among electrified-parts suppliers intensifies. This is a factor that can keep revenue gains from reaching operating profit. Earnings volatility will persist until the order volume and unit pricing of new electrified products become clear.

Balance sheet and cash flow

The debt ratio of 281.3% means liabilities are large relative to equity, with total liabilities of KRW 493.7 billion. With operating profit declining, the debt burden could reduce financial cushioning.

Operating cash flow rose to KRW 99.3 billion in 2025 from KRW 18.3 billion in 2024, but the annual pattern since 2022 has been uneven.

Customer concentration and currency

Domestic customers are centered on Hyundai Motor, KIA and Hyundai Mobis, and exports were 58.6% of sales at the end of 2025. Swings in automaker production and exchange rates can affect both sales and margins. Customer concentration supports order stability and is also a source of volatility.

Overall view

Infac's 2025 revenue of KRW 586.4 billion is larger than in 2022, but operating profit has declined for two years since peaking in 2023, and the operating margin stands at 3.4%.

Quarterly results in 2026 improved in the second quarter, with revenue of KRW 192.1 billion and operating profit of KRW 8.55 billion, then reversed in the third quarter to operating profit of KRW 1.06 billion and a net loss attributable to controlling shareholders of KRW 2.47 billion.

The recovery of controlling-shareholder net profit to KRW 12 billion in 2025 is a positive factor, yet the last four quarters total only KRW 2.19 billion. A debt ratio of 281.3% and total liabilities of KRW 493.7 billion remain a burden on the balance sheet.

Price competition among electrified-parts suppliers and slower EV demand stand in the way of turning sales growth into profit.

Market capitalization below the book value of equity attributable to controlling shareholders is cited as an asset-based comparison, but whether earnings capacity recovers is the central question.

The next checkpoints are preliminary fourth-quarter and full-year 2026 results and the operating margin in the 2026 annual business report.
