02

Business structure

VenueG was established in 1979 to operate wedding halls and wedding consulting services and listed on KOSDAQ in 1993.

Its current business is organized around three pillars: wedding halls, a golf course, and a hotel, while its former core department store business (Grand Department Store and Grand Mart) was shut down and reclassified as a discontinued operation after prolonged sales declines and operating losses.

The wedding hall segment is centered on 'The VenueG Seoul' near Balsan Station on Seoul Subway Line 5, featuring a 1,200-space parking facility and food-and-beverage service led by hotel-trained chefs, and accounted for about 68.74% of total revenue in the second quarter of 2026.

The golf course segment is run by subsidiary VenueG CC (48.67% equity stake) on a 27-hole course and is estimated to represent about 29% of revenue. The hotel segment is operated by subsidiary Hotel Grand Distribution.

Competition comes from mid-sized operators in the integrated leisure services market spanning wedding halls, golf courses and hotels; when the company still ran department stores it competed directly with large retailers such as Shinsegae, Lotte Shopping, Hyundai Department Store and GS Retail, but that competitive landscape changed after the exit.

The controlling shareholder group led by Kim Man-jin and related parties held a 52.82% stake as of September 23, 2026, while Jeongdo Jinheung Co., a holder of more than 10%, has continued to expand its stake through on-market purchases.

The company holds treasury shares equal to 16.6% of total shares outstanding, which keeps raising questions about potential future changes to its treasury stock policy.

04

Earnings analysis

Annual revenue rose from KRW 58.9 billion in 2022 to KRW 65 billion in 2023 (up 10.4%) and KRW 70.1 billion in 2024 (up 7.8%), before falling to KRW 48.2 billion in 2025 (down 31.2%); among 2022-2025, 2024 was the largest and 2025 the smallest.

Operating profit moved from KRW 11.3 billion in 2022 to KRW 9.22 billion in 2023 (down 18.4%), KRW 17.8 billion in 2024 (up 93.3%), and KRW 15 billion in 2025 (down 15.6%), with 2024 the largest and 2023 the smallest.

Net income attributable to controlling shareholders swung from a loss of KRW 19.9 billion in 2022 to a profit of KRW 13.8 billion in 2023, back to a loss of KRW 12.6 billion in 2024, then to a profit of KRW 93.7 billion in 2025, flipping sign nearly every year.

Net income including non-controlling interests followed the same pattern: a loss of KRW 17.5 billion in 2022, a profit of KRW 12.3 billion in 2023, a loss of KRW 11.3 billion in 2024, and a profit of KRW 101.1 billion in 2025.

The operating margin climbed from 19.2% to 14.2%, 25.4% and 31.2%, peaking in 2025, while the debt ratio eased from 76.4%, 77.5% and 78.3% down to 63.1% in 2025; year-end 2025 equity stood at KRW 445.4 billion (of which KRW 390.6 billion attributable to controlling shareholders), liabilities at KRW 280.9 billion, and operating cash flow rose steadily from KRW 903 million to KRW 11.7 billion, KRW 17.9 billion and KRW 18 billion.

On a quarterly basis, revenue moved from KRW 14.6 billion in the second quarter of 2025 to KRW 10.7 billion in the third quarter (down 26.5%), KRW 13.8 billion in the fourth quarter (up 28.6%), KRW 10.4 billion in the first quarter of 2026 (down 25.0%), and KRW 12.6 billion in the second quarter of 2026 (up 22.0% quarter on quarter, down 13.5% year on year), while operating profit swung from KRW 6.8 billion to KRW 1.37 billion (down 79.9%), KRW 4.74 billion (up 246.9%), KRW 1.92 billion (down 59.5%) and KRW 5.36 billion (up 179.4% quarter on quarter, down 21.1% year on year).

In contrast, controlling-shareholder net income rose for four straight quarters, from KRW 9.57 billion to KRW 31.3 billion (up 227.4%), KRW 43.9 billion (up 40.0%), KRW 55 billion (up 25.3%), and KRW 208 billion (up 278.6% quarter on quarter, up 2,074.9% year on year), bringing the trailing four-quarter total (third quarter 2025 through second quarter 2026) to KRW 338.2 billion.

This surge in net income is attributable mainly to expanded fair-value gains on listed equity holdings that lifted financial income.

05

Industry analysis

Korea's marriage count appears to have passed a trough and is rebounding, with analysis pointing to rising average spending per wedding driven by expanding demand for 'small luxury' weddings.

This suggests both the number of ceremonies and the average ticket size—the two key drivers of wedding hall revenue—are improving at the same time.

The golf course industry is in a mature stage marked by supply of small and mid-sized courses near the capital region and persistent price competition between membership and public-access formats.

VenueG CC experienced roughly a month and a half of operational disruption from a natural disaster in the summer of 2025, underscoring its exposure to weather and other non-recurring variables.

Having already exited retail, the company no longer competes directly with traditional department store operators such as Shinsegae, Lotte Shopping and Hyundai Department Store, but as an integrated leisure operator spanning wedding halls, hotels and golf, it is seen as facing limits to high growth.

On the financial-asset side, roughly 90% of its listed equity portfolio is concentrated in Samsung Electronics and SK hynix, giving it a profit structure tied closely to the domestic stock market and the semiconductor cycle.

This structure differentiates it from typical retail and leisure peers and is a key reason market attention has shifted toward its financial asset value rather than its core operations.

Outlook

The company plans to convert the site of its discontinued Grand Department Store Ilsan branch into a wedding hall, which is cited as a potential driver for further expansion of the wedding hall segment.

A substantial portion of the proceeds from the KRW 37.6 billion second-tranche exchangeable bond issued in July 2026 is earmarked for construction, fire-safety and interior work on the 'VenueG 1995 Wedding' facility.

Independent research house Aris projected in an August 18, 2026 report that 2026 full-year revenue would reach KRW 51.6 billion with operating profit of KRW 16.5 billion, though this is an external estimate rather than official company guidance.

On the financial income side, the amplitude of quarterly valuation gains and losses tied to Samsung Electronics and SK hynix share prices is likely to persist, and since the Samsung Electronics share price has reportedly traded below the second-tranche EB's exchange price of KRW 376,155, the risk of a cash redemption burden at maturity remains a variable.

On shareholder returns, a shareholder proposal to raise the dividend and retire treasury shares was voted down at the March 2026 annual general meeting, but the company's existing treasury shares, equal to 16.6% of shares outstanding, could resurface as a retirement or disposal issue going forward.

The core wedding hall business is expected to see some expansion on the back of a marriage count rebound and 'small luxury' demand, while the golf course and hotel segments remain exposed to weather and other non-recurring factors that could keep quarterly results volatile.

08

Bull factors

Expanding Gains on Equity Holdings

Fair-value gains on listed equity holdings such as Samsung Electronics and SK hynix drove the surge in net income in 2025 and 2026. Independent research house Valuefinder assessed in a May 28, 2026 report that these financial assets were worth roughly three times the market capitalization at the time.

Because the size of valuation gains and losses fluctuates with the domestic stock market and the semiconductor cycle, financial income has room to improve further in periods when the market rises.

It should be noted, however, that this stems from changes in the market value of held assets rather than from operating performance.

Marriage Rebound and Wedding Hall Expansion

Analysis points to a rebound in Korea's marriage count and rising average wedding spending driven by expanding demand for 'small luxury' weddings. In addition, a plan to convert the Grand Department Store Ilsan site into a wedding hall is under way, which is cited as room for expansion in the wedding hall segment.

As wedding halls accounted for about 68.74% of total revenue in the second quarter of 2026, improvements in underlying demand could flow through relatively directly.

Treasury Shares and Ownership Structure

Treasury shares equal to 16.6% of total shares outstanding leave room for shareholder-return policy to change depending on future retirement or disposal decisions.

Both the controlling shareholder group and 10%-plus holder Jeongdo Jinheung Co. expanded their stakes through on-market purchases in the second half of 2026. This is a relevant fact to consider regarding ownership stability.

09

Bear factors

Core Business Growth Stagnation

The core business centered on wedding halls, a hotel and a golf course is viewed as a mature operation with limited high-growth potential, with first-quarter 2026 revenue declining year on year.

Second-quarter 2026 operating profit also fell 21.1% year on year to KRW 5.36 billion, indicating a slow pace of operational improvement. Unlike the surge in net income, core profitability has not shown a clear rebound signal.

Quality and Volatility of Net Income

Because the surge in controlling-shareholder net income stems from fair-value gains on listed equity holdings rather than operations, a correction in the domestic stock market could cause net income to reverse sharply.

The company's business structure itself has also changed frequently, as seen in the 31.2% revenue decline in 2025 following the department store discontinuation. Overall, earnings continuity and predictability remain relatively volatile.

Gap Versus Exchangeable Bond Strike Price

The exchange price of the second-tranche EB issued in July 2026 was set at KRW 376,155 per share, but the Samsung Electronics share price has reportedly since fallen below that level.

With no refixing clause to adjust the exchange price, a cash redemption burden at maturity is possible if the share price fails to recover. This illustrates a limitation of funding structures backed by held shares as the exchange asset.

10

Risk factors

Financial Asset Volatility Risk

Because a substantial portion of net income derives from fair-value gains and losses linked to Samsung Electronics and SK hynix share prices, a downturn in the semiconductor cycle or the domestic stock market could cause net income to decline quickly.

The held shares are classified as financial assets at fair value through profit or loss (FVPL), so valuation gains and losses flow directly into the income statement every quarter.

Funding Structure and Redemption Risk

The KRW 37.6 billion EB issued in July 2026 must be redeemed in cash at maturity if the Samsung Electronics share price remains below the exchange price, which could create an additional funding burden.

The first-tranche EB was issued under a similar structure, so further mezzanine issuances could become a financial burden variable going forward.

Non-recurring Factors in Core Operations

The golf course segment experienced roughly a month and a half of operational disruption from a natural disaster in the summer of 2025, showing its exposure to weather and other uncontrollable variables.

The wedding hall segment is also driven by the demographic variable of marriage counts, so the possibility of a longer-term demand slowdown cannot be ruled out.

12

Overall view

VenueG's core business—wedding halls, a golf course and a hotel—remains stable, while fair-value gains and losses on its Samsung Electronics and SK hynix equity holdings have come to effectively determine the direction of quarterly results.

After controlling-shareholder net income turned positive at KRW 93.7 billion in 2025, it rose further to KRW 208 billion in the second quarter of 2026, marking four consecutive quarters of growth since the second quarter of 2025, even as operating profit declined year on year over the same period, showing a divergence between the core business and net income.

The company has raised funds twice through exchangeable bonds backed by Samsung Electronics shares, and the tranche issued in July 2026 could lead to a cash redemption burden at maturity if the Samsung Electronics share price stays below the exchange price.

The wedding hall segment is cited as having room for expansion on the back of a marriage count rebound, 'small luxury' demand, and the planned conversion of the Grand Department Store Ilsan site into a wedding hall, but the core business as a whole—including the golf course and hotel—is also seen as facing the limits of a mature industry.

For investors, separating operating performance from financial-asset valuation gains and losses appears useful for assessing the substance of reported earnings.

The next quarterly report and the trajectory of Samsung Electronics and SK hynix share prices are likely to be the key variables shaping future earnings direction.
