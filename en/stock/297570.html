02

Business structure

Atlas Link (Aloys) is an OTT set-top box company founded in September 2015 that changed its name from Aloys, with the new listing name applied from July 3, 2026. Its core business is Android-based OTT multimedia devices sold under the FORMULER brand.

The company promotes a next-generation OTT solution that combines a cloud LLM with Edge AI, offering content recommendation, natural-language search and real-time subtitle translation.

In the second half of 2023 it acquired 4 million new shares of its subsidiary Korea Pile for KRW 7 billion, and Korea Pile was consolidated in 2025. Korea Pile's PHC pile business makes prestressed concrete piles used in foundation work, so demand tends to follow construction activity.

Reported first-half 2026 segment figures show KRW 15.9 billion of revenue (operating profit KRW 3.3 billion) for multimedia devices and KRW 18.3 billion (operating profit KRW 2.8 billion) for PHC piles.

The PHC pile segment accounted for more than half of consolidated revenue, shifting the revenue mix toward the subsidiary. Reported stakes in Korea Pile differ: Korea Pile's own website lists a 63.06% holding by the parent, while a 2026 report headline cites 95%, so the filing should be checked.

In July 2026 a Roa Holdings group company became the largest shareholder, ending a management dispute. Compared with large sector peers such as Samsung Electro-Mechanics and LG Innotek, the business mix and scale differ widely.

04

Earnings analysis

Annual revenue went from KRW 28.7 billion in 2022 to KRW 26.6 billion in 2023 (down 7.5%), then rose to KRW 30.9 billion in 2024 (up 16.4%) and KRW 42.9 billion in 2025 (up 38.7%). 2023 was the low point, followed by two consecutive years of growth from 2024 through 2025, and 2025 was the largest year of the 2022–2025 period.

Operating profit was KRW 4.3 billion in 2022, KRW 4.25 billion in 2023 (down 1.1%), KRW 4.81 billion in 2024 (up 13.1%) and KRW 6.54 billion in 2025 (up 36.0%). The operating margin stayed in a 15–16% band (15.0%, 16.0%, 15.5% and 15.2% from 2022 to 2025), so higher revenue did not widen the margin.

Net income attributable to controlling shareholders was KRW 6.08 billion in 2022 and KRW 4.9 billion in 2023 (down 19.5%), turned into a net loss of KRW 403 million in 2024, and returned to KRW 4.97 billion in 2025.

Total net income in 2025 was KRW 4.47 billion, below the controlling-shareholder figure, which indicates a loss attributed to non-controlling interests. Reports say subsidiary Korea Pile posted operating losses of KRW 4.4 billion in 2024 and KRW 2.5 billion in 2025, and returned to profit in the first half of 2026.

On a quarterly basis, revenue rose from KRW 12.4 billion in Q3 2025 to KRW 19.6 billion in Q3 2026 (up 58.5% year on year), and operating profit rose from KRW 1.23 billion to KRW 4.5 billion (up 266.3%).

Revenue has grown for four consecutive quarters from Q4 2025 through Q3 2026, and operating profit growth far outpaced revenue growth.

Net income attributable to controlling shareholders fell 23.0% quarter on quarter to KRW 1.68 billion in Q2 2026 before rising 89.5% to KRW 3.18 billion in Q3, so quarterly swings are large.

The first-half 2026 figures announced on August 14, 2026 (revenue KRW 34.15 billion, operating profit KRW 6.1 billion) roughly match the sum of the disclosed Q1 and Q2 results.

Operating cash flow fell from KRW 12.7 billion in 2023 to KRW 7.41 billion in 2024 and KRW 5.63 billion in 2025, and the debt ratio rose from 33.1% in 2022 to 105.5% in 2025.

05

Industry analysis

Atlas Link's revenue comes from two different businesses. One is Android-based OTT set-top boxes, and the other is PHC piles, which follow demand for foundation work at construction sites.

Because the supply and demand dynamics of the two markets differ, results are exposed to both OTT device demand and construction activity. The company positions its cloud LLM and Edge AI solution as a differentiator for its set-top boxes.

Sector peers such as Samsung Electro-Mechanics and LG Innotek run large component and module production systems, so their business mix differs clearly from a company that sells finished devices and construction materials.

Reported first-half 2026 figures show the multimedia device segment with revenue of KRW 15.9 billion and operating profit of KRW 3.3 billion, indicating that the core business kept its profitability. The company has said it will extend its OTT base into AI, optical communications and edge computing.

However, the reported segment breakdown does not separate these new businesses, so their revenue contribution is hard to gauge from available figures.

06

Outlook

In July 2026, alongside the name change, the company said it would expand AI and optical communication businesses.

CEO Shin Jeong-kwan said the company would pursue promising new businesses that create synergy with existing operations and contribute to shareholder value, with the aim of growing revenue and profitability.

On the asset side, reports said the company bought land and a building in Dongsung-dong, Jongno-gu for KRW 17.4 billion and also decided to acquire land in Gimpo for KRW 21.6 billion and additional Korea Pile shares for KRW 13.1 billion.

Together these three items total KRW 52.1 billion, well above first-half cash and equivalents of KRW 12.5 billion. Another report put first-half cash at KRW 15 billion, so the reported liquidity figure varies by source.

How the asset purchases are funded, whether from cash or borrowing, will shape the future debt ratio and interest burden. Because Korea Pile moved from a 2025 loss to a first-half 2026 profit, how the subsidiary's results appear in the 2026 annual report is a key item to check.

Given steady set-top box sales and the large PHC pile revenue share, revenue remains heavily dependent on the subsidiary for now. The stated direction is to grow both scale and profitability.

08

Bull factors

Scale gains from consolidation

Q3 2026 revenue of KRW 19.6 billion rose 58.5% year on year, extending a run of four straight quarterly gains. After Korea Pile was consolidated, the PHC pile segment generated KRW 18.3 billion in first-half 2026 revenue.

Reports also put the set-top box segment at KRW 15.9 billion of revenue and KRW 3.3 billion of operating profit over the same half.

Profit growth outpacing sales

Q3 2026 operating profit of KRW 4.5 billion rose 266.3% year on year, far above the 58.5% revenue growth. Full-year 2025 operating profit rose 36.0% to KRW 6.54 billion, and net income attributable to controlling shareholders swung from the 2024 loss to KRW 4.97 billion. Korea Pile's move from a 2025 loss to a first-half 2026 profit also added to consolidated profitability.

Governance settled, new businesses outlined

In July 2026 the largest shareholder changed to a Roa Holdings group company, ending the management dispute, and the company was renamed Atlas Link. The company has said it will extend its technology into AI, optical communications and edge computing. Its cloud LLM and Edge AI OTT solution is presented as a differentiator for the existing set-top box business.

Bear factors

Asset purchases and funding strain

Reports describe KRW 17.4 billion for the Dongsung-dong property, KRW 21.6 billion for Gimpo land and KRW 13.1 billion for Korea Pile shares, totaling KRW 52.1 billion. First-half cash was reported at KRW 12.5 billion (another report says KRW 15 billion), so the investments far exceed liquidity.

If borrowing funds the gap, the debt ratio and interest costs would rise, making the funding method a key variable.

Weaker financial structure

The debt ratio climbed from 33.1% in 2022 to 43.6% in 2023, 48.3% in 2024 and 105.5% in 2025. Total liabilities at end-2025 were KRW 57.7 billion, more than total equity of KRW 54.7 billion.

Operating cash flow fell from KRW 12.7 billion in 2023 to KRW 7.41 billion in 2024 and KRW 5.63 billion in 2025, leaving a gap between profit growth and cash generation.

Subsidiary dependence and volatile profit

The PHC pile segment accounts for more than half of first-half consolidated revenue, so results depend heavily on Korea Pile. Reports say the subsidiary posted operating losses of KRW 4.4 billion in 2024 and KRW 2.5 billion in 2025.

Net income attributable to controlling shareholders fell 23.0% quarter on quarter to KRW 1.68 billion in Q2 2026, and reports say the share price fell to less than half its early-year high.

10

Risk factors

Financing and funding risk

Total property and equity investments of KRW 52.1 billion exceed first-half cash, so borrowing to fill the gap could push the debt ratio higher from 105.5% at end-2025. Additional debt would weigh on interest costs and financial flexibility. The timing and funding method of these payments must be confirmed through disclosures.

Subsidiary and construction-cycle risk

The PHC pile segment ties to foundation work demand, so a construction slowdown could reduce subsidiary sales. Korea Pile posted losses in 2024 and 2025, and its first-half 2026 return to profit is a single half-year result, too short to judge sustainability.

Because more than half of consolidated revenue comes from this segment, its volatility passes directly into consolidated profit.

Governance transition and new-business execution risk

The largest shareholder change in July 2026 ended the management dispute, but it came alongside organizational restructuring and a renaming, so the management structure is still settling.

AI and optical communication businesses are not separated in the reported segment breakdown, making their contribution hard to measure. Results from these new businesses may take time to appear in reported earnings.

12

Overall view

In full-year 2025 Atlas Link reported revenue of KRW 42.9 billion, operating profit of KRW 6.54 billion and net income attributable to controlling shareholders of KRW 4.97 billion, showing both expansion and a return to profit.

In 2026 quarterly revenue and operating profit have grown for four consecutive quarters, and the consolidation of subsidiary Korea Pile has been the main driver of scale.

On the other hand, reported asset purchases of KRW 52.1 billion far exceed first-half cash and equivalents, and the debt ratio stood at 105.5% at end-2025. The July 2026 change in largest shareholder, which ended the management dispute, and the stated push into AI and optical communications are cited as positives.

Because the set-top box business and the PHC pile segment operate together, subsidiary results largely determine the direction of consolidated profit. Reports also say the share price has fallen despite improved earnings, leaving a clear gap between the two.

The next items to confirm are the operating margin, debt ratio and operating cash flow in the 2026 annual report, and how the asset purchase payments are funded.
