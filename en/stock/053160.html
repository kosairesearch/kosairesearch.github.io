Business structure

FreeMs was founded in 1989 and listed on KOSDAQ in 2001 as a maker of electrical components for heavy equipment. Its core product is wire harnesses used in construction machinery and industrial vehicles, backed by control technology such as hydraulic proportional valves and equipment controllers.

Jobkorea's breakdown as of March 2026 shows construction-equipment harnesses at about 80%, industrial-vehicle harnesses at about 17%, products at about 2%, rental at about 1%, and other items at about 1%.

Butler's company data for Q2 2026 sales shows HD Hyundai Construction Equipment at about 79%, Doosan Bobcat Korea at about 16%, other products at about 4%, rental at about 1%, and raw-material sales at about 1%.

Most revenue therefore rests on supply to two construction-equipment makers, so their production plans and sourcing decisions drive results.

Earlier company profiles listed OEM production of financial terminals such as sports-betting and kiosk units, but that line does not appear as a separate item in the recent sales mix.

Competition runs through a supplier-panel system in which vehicle makers select partners, and development capability and delivery and quality records act as entry barriers. Headcount stood at 38 as of June 30, 2026, down from 89 in 2022, which may constrain development staffing and new-customer expansion.

Earnings analysis

Annual revenue fell from KRW 29.6 billion in 2022 to KRW 22.9 billion in 2023 (down 22.6% year on year) and KRW 17.7 billion in 2024 (down 22.9%), then rebounded to KRW 23.1 billion in 2025 (up 30.7%). 2022 was the largest year in the 2022-2025 span and 2024 the smallest.

Operating profit of KRW 659 million in 2022 turned into an operating loss of KRW 12.33 million in 2023, and the loss widened to KRW 2.35 billion in 2024 before returning to operating profit of KRW 388 million in 2025.

Operating margin ran 2.2% in 2022, -0.1% in 2023, -13.3% in 2024 and 1.7% in 2025, so margin erosion was steepest in 2024, the year sales were lowest.

Controlling-shareholder net income fell from KRW 1.34 billion in 2022 to KRW 734 million in 2023 (down 45.1%), became a net loss of KRW 1.45 billion in 2024, and returned to KRW 899 million in 2025. Net income including non-controlling interests was also KRW 899 million in 2025.

Operating cash flow was -KRW 1.2 billion in 2022, KRW 1.3 billion in 2023, -KRW 382 million in 2024 and KRW 1.52 billion in 2025. On a quarterly basis, Q3 2025 showed revenue of KRW 5.44 billion, operating profit of KRW 20.92 million and controlling-shareholder net income of KRW 405 million.

Q4 2025 revenue was KRW 5 billion (down 8.1% from the prior quarter), with operating profit of KRW 112 million and a controlling-shareholder net loss of KRW 263 million. Q1 2026 revenue was KRW 4.97 billion, with an operating loss of KRW 32.62 million and a controlling-shareholder net loss of KRW 981 million.

Q2 2026 revenue rose to KRW 5.54 billion, yet the company still posted an operating loss of KRW 232 million, while controlling-shareholder net income of KRW 645 million suggests non-operating items supported the bottom line.

Q3 2026 revenue was KRW 5.43 billion with an operating loss of KRW 86.13 million, a smaller loss than the prior quarter but a swing to loss from Q3 2025. The controlling-shareholder net loss over the last four quarters totals KRW 586 million.

Industry analysis

FreeMs demand tracks production volumes in construction machinery and industrial vehicles. With construction-equipment harnesses as the core of the mix, capital spending and utilization in the construction-equipment market largely determine order volume.

Revenue falling from KRW 29.6 billion in 2022 to KRW 17.7 billion in 2024 and recovering to KRW 23.1 billion in 2025 illustrates that demand volatility. Public figures alone, however, do not allow the causes of the decline and recovery to be split by customer.

Listed peers in the same electronic components sector, such as Samsung Electro-Mechanics, LG Innotek and Isupetasys, generally have broader customer and industry bases, so FreeMs differs markedly in customer concentration and revenue scale.

FreeMs occupies a narrow niche as a supplier specializing in electrical harnesses for construction equipment. Its position is protected by the quality standards and joint-development history that vehicle makers require, which limits new entrants.

The flip side is that a reduced order from one customer translates quickly into lower revenue. Industrial-vehicle harnesses at about 17% as of March 2026 suggest a second demand axis beyond construction equipment.

Bear factors

Three consecutive quarterly losses in 2026

Operating losses ran from Q1 through Q3 2026, and the controlling-shareholder net loss over the last four quarters totals KRW 586 million. The Q3 2026 operating loss of KRW 86.13 million compares with an operating profit of KRW 20.92 million in Q3 2025, a swing to loss year on year.

The Q2 2026 controlling-shareholder profit appears to reflect non-operating items, so a recovery in the core operating base is not yet visible.

Concentrated customer base

Construction-equipment harnesses make up about 80% of the mix, and for Q2 2026 sales HD Hyundai Construction Equipment accounted for about 79% and Doosan Bobcat Korea for about 16%. A drop in production or orders from either customer feeds straight into total revenue. Without a broader customer base, there is limited buffer against order swings.

Flat sales and a smaller scale

Quarterly revenue was KRW 4.97 billion in Q1 2026, KRW 5.54 billion in Q2 and KRW 5.43 billion in Q3, holding in the mid-KRW 5 billion range. Q3 2026 revenue fell 0.3% year on year. Against KRW 29.6 billion in 2022, the KRW 23.1 billion recorded in 2025 still leaves a wide gap.

Risk factors

Development staffing and new customers

Headcount stood at 38 as of June 30, 2026, down from 89 in 2022, leaving a small team for electrical design and control development. If one or two development engineers leave, schedules for new harness and control products could slip.

Because vehicle makers judge suppliers on development capability and delivery records, a thin engineering base could also slow the company's push to win business beyond its current construction-equipment customers.

Expansion of the industrial-vehicle axis

Industrial-vehicle harnesses accounted for about 17% of the mix as of March 2026, reading as a second demand axis beyond construction equipment. If this line does not expand, the revenue base stays tied to the two construction-equipment customers.

The timing at which new supply to industrial-vehicle makers would show up in results cannot be confirmed from current disclosures alone. Whether this second axis grows fast enough to absorb swings in construction-equipment orders is the key question.

Dividend and treasury-share management

The 77.5% payout ratio for 2025 rests on 2025 profit. If losses continue through 2026, sustaining the same dividend level could become harder. The treatment of the 15.08% treasury stake held as of March 2026 could change the free-float and shareholder mix.
