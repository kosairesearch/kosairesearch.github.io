02

Business structure

Maniker F&G is a meat processing and home meal replacement (HMR) company that develops and produces frozen, chilled and retort chicken products, including fried chicken, samgyetang and chicken breast.

The company supplies products to domestic restaurant, institutional, distribution and online channels as well as overseas markets. According to an Alphasquare company profile, main sales come from product sales such as Hot Crispy Chicken Patty and Insam Hanppuri Samgyetang, and most revenue is generated domestically.

Overseas, the company exported retort samgyetang and frozen poultry to eight countries and regions, including the United States, Canada, Japan, Germany, the Netherlands, Italy, the UK and Hong Kong, according to a November 2026 company announcement.

Its export footprint began with US samgyetang shipments in 2014, expanded to Canada in 2020, and reached the European Union and the UK in 2024.

In June 2026 the company first shipped five export items to the UK, including three flavors of Korean-style sweet and spicy chicken and two chicken breast steak products, extending a range that had centered on samgyetang. Production capacity includes a second plant completed in November 2024 for premium HMR products.

The company has said it intends to expand HMR lines in line with rising single-person household and convenience food demand and to push into the B2C market. The overseas sales base built around samgyetang is the starting point for the company's broader export push into convenience foods.

04

Earnings analysis

According to DART periodic reports, annual sales rose from KRW 89.4 billion in 2022 to KRW 101.2 billion in 2023, up 13.3% year on year, and to KRW 107.7 billion in 2024, up 6.4%, before easing to KRW 106.8 billion in 2025, down 0.9%. Among 2022 to 2025, 2024 was the largest year and 2022 the smallest.

Operating profit swung from an operating loss of KRW 1.72 billion in 2022 to operating profit of KRW 2.1 billion in 2023, a turnaround to profit, then rose to KRW 3.84 billion in 2024, up 82.7%, and fell to KRW 2.49 billion in 2025, down 35.3%.

The operating margin moved from -1.9% in 2022 to 2.1% in 2023, 3.6% in 2024 and 2.3% in 2025, staying in the low single digits throughout the period.

Net profit attributable to shareholders went from a net loss of KRW 262 million in 2022 to KRW 2.07 billion in 2023, a turnaround to profit, then KRW 2.99 billion in 2024, up 44.5%, and KRW 2.17 billion in 2025, down 27.6%.

A FnGuide summary notes that in the first half of 2025 sales grew while profitability weakened on cost pressure. Operating cash flow was -KRW 13.5 billion in 2022, KRW 3.06 billion in 2023, KRW 13.8 billion in 2024 and -KRW 4.94 billion in 2025, showing wide swings.

Quarterly sales bottomed at KRW 23.7 billion in the fourth quarter of 2025, down 19.4% from the prior quarter, and then rose to KRW 27.7 billion, KRW 30.8 billion and KRW 32.9 billion in the first through third quarters of 2026.

Quarterly operating profit was uneven, at KRW 239 million in the first quarter of 2026, KRW 1 billion in the second quarter and KRW 651 million in the third quarter, down 31.0% year on year.

Third-quarter 2026 net profit attributable to shareholders of KRW 26 billion is far larger than the operating profit of KRW 651 million, and the Yongin land sale closed in the same quarter on September 30, 2026, at a transfer price of KRW 55.6 billion.

Vegan News reported that cumulative operating profit for January to September 2026, based on the third-quarter report, rose 16.7% to KRW 1.9 billion.

Because most of the KRW 27.9 billion total for the last four quarters comes from the third quarter of 2026, profit figures for that window appear to depend heavily on non-operating, one-time items.

05

Industry analysis

Domestic demand for home meal replacement products is expanding alongside rising single-person households and demand for shorter cooking times, and a FnGuide summary cites HMR market growth as a driver of the company's sales.

Because the products are chicken-based, raw material prices and cost pressure remain key variables for margins. Sales grew in the first half of 2025 while profitability weakened, which reflects this cost structure.

Competition spans poultry processors and frozen convenience food makers, and comparison points include raw material sourcing and production capacity. The company expanded premium HMR output with a second plant completed in November 2024.

Overseas, the company is building on its samgyetang export base to broaden convenience food exports, a path that can be compared with export-led growth at Samyang Foods. Domestically, growth depends on demand that substitutes for restaurant meals and on online channel expansion.

Compared with large food groups, the company has a much smaller scale and less bargaining power in distribution and marketing. The company itself notes that preferred flavors, packaging formats and sales channels differ by export market, which requires locally tailored product development.

06

Outlook

The company displayed its UK export lineup, including three flavors of sweet and spicy chicken and chicken breast steak, at a Seoul food industry exhibition in November 2026. Expanding distribution of existing export products and finding new import and distribution partners were presented as immediate tasks.

The company said it will reflect local feedback in product development, adjusting flavor, portion size, packaging and cooking methods to each market's needs.

The UK and EU markets were entered in 2024, but new products such as sweet and spicy chicken only began shipping in June 2026, so their contribution to sales remains at an early stage.

In Korea, the second plant completed in 2024 handles premium HMR production, and expanding HMR lines and entering the B2C market are the stated directions.

On the Yongin land sale, the compensation payment was received on September 28, 2026, and the Korea Land and Housing Corporation acquired ownership on the expropriation start date of September 30, 2026. The company received the compensation while maintaining an objection to the transfer amount.

Registration dates for the transfer were listed as undetermined in the filing. The filings do not yet show how this transaction will affect results after the fourth quarter of 2026.

The 2026 annual report is the next periodic report, where the size of the land-sale gain and the sales contribution of export products can be checked together.

Bull factors

Broadening export channels

Retort samgyetang and frozen poultry now reach eight countries and regions, and after entering the EU and UK in 2024, the company first shipped new products such as sweet and spicy chicken and chicken breast steak in June 2026.

The export lineup is broadening from samgyetang into convenience foods, which can be read as a structural shift. The company said it will reflect local market feedback in product development.

A recovery in quarterly sales

Quarterly sales recovered from KRW 23.7 billion in the fourth quarter of 2025 to KRW 32.9 billion in the third quarter of 2026, up 11.8% year on year. Vegan News reported that cumulative sales for January to September 2026 reached KRW 91.4 billion, up 10.1%.

Whether the sales recovery has translated into operating profit gains is mixed from quarter to quarter, so the link between top-line growth and profitability needs to be checked.

Balance sheet and cash inflow

The debt-to-equity ratio declined from 59.6% in 2022 to 54.2% in 2025, with total equity of KRW 79.5 billion and total liabilities of KRW 43.1 billion at the end of 2025. The Yongin land compensation was deposited on September 28, 2026, with a transfer price of KRW 55.6 billion.

The disclosed pro forma summary shows retained earnings rising by KRW 41.4 billion, which strengthens the equity base.

Bear factors

Thin operating margins

The operating margin improved from -1.9% in 2022 to 2.3% in 2025 but has remained in the low single digits. Operating profit fell 35.3% to KRW 2.49 billion in 2025, and third-quarter 2026 operating profit of KRW 651 million was down 31.0% year on year. Even as sales grow, the share converted into profit remains small.

Earnings quality and one-off items

Third-quarter 2026 net profit attributable to shareholders of KRW 26 billion is far above operating profit of KRW 651 million, reflecting the effect of the land sale closed in the same quarter.

Core operating profit is on the order of a few hundred million won per quarter, so net profit alone does not show the underlying business strength. Profitability weakened in the first half of 2025 under cost pressure as well.

Volatile operating cash flow

Operating cash flow swung from -KRW 13.5 billion in 2022 to KRW 3.06 billion in 2023, KRW 13.8 billion in 2024 and -KRW 4.94 billion in 2025. First-quarter 2026 operating profit of KRW 239 million fell 72.3% from the prior quarter, which also points to large quarterly swings. The cash flow pattern appears to depend on working capital and the timing of raw material purchases.

Risk factors

Earnings volatility

Annual operating profit turned to profit in 2023, then reached KRW 3.84 billion in 2024 and KRW 2.49 billion in 2025, while quarterly operating profit moved from KRW 239 million in the first quarter of 2026 to KRW 1 billion in the second. If cost pressure rises, sales growth may not reach the bottom line. Raw material sourcing and the ability to pass on prices are the key variables for earnings.

Land-sale uncertainty

The company received the compensation while maintaining an objection to the transfer amount. Registration dates for the ownership transfer were listed as undetermined in the filing. Once the one-time land-sale effect is no longer in the numbers, net profit in later quarters may move closer to operating profit levels.

Major shareholder stake and trading flows

The de facto controlling shareholder bought 1,145,206 shares on the open market between November 10 and 17, 2026, and its stake stood at 36.62% at the report date. A higher controlling stake can reduce the float available for trading and may increase price volatility.

Short-term flows such as foreign net buying can also influence the share price, so changes in trading flows deserve attention.

Overall view

Maniker F&G is centered on chicken-based processed foods and home meal replacements, and sales have risen for three consecutive quarters since the fourth quarter of 2025. Exports are broadening from samgyetang into convenience foods such as sweet and spicy chicken and chicken breast steak.

On the other hand, the operating margin was a thin 2.3% in 2025, and large swings in annual operating profit and operating cash flow mean earnings persistence has not yet been tested.

Third-quarter 2026 net profit attributable to shareholders of KRW 26 billion reflects the Yongin land sale closed in the same quarter and should be read apart from operating results.

The on-market purchases by the controlling shareholder group and the decline in the debt-to-equity ratio are recorded as positive factors on the financial and trading side.

The next point to check is how much of the land-sale gain appears in the 2026 annual report and whether the operating margin exceeds the 2025 level. This report is for information only and does not include a buy or sell opinion or a target price.
