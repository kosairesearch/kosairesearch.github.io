02

Business structure

ICH, founded in August 2012, is a specialty advanced electronic materials company. Its main products are adhesive tapes, electromagnetic interference (EMI) shielding gaskets, film-type antennas (MFA) and high-performance foam.

Its core products are foam materials that protect internal electronic components from impact and vibration, and RF gaskets for electromagnetic shielding, with polyurethane (PU) and polyethylene (PE) foams as the core product families.

The business still centers on smartphone components, but the company says it is widening applications to foldable phones and robots. It currently supplies high-performance foam materials to two of the world's leading foldable-phone makers.

On October 26, 2026 it announced it had obtained a primary vendor code from Samsung Electronics, allowing direct supply of materials, semi-finished parts and finished products.

A smartphone wireless-charging module component order announced on October 29, 2026 is the first case of finished-product supply for a next-generation flagship model, after earlier supply in material form only.

In robotics, foam materials for a global electric-vehicle maker's humanoid robot passed quality testing and entered mass supply from August 2026. The company cites market-share (MS) decline and customer cost-reduction (CR) pressure from intensifying supply-chain competition as drivers of 2025 earnings deterioration.

In the second quarter of 2026, Middle East geopolitical risk pushed up oil and petrochemical prices, and the key raw material for PU foam rose by as much as 50% in a short period, temporarily weakening profitability.

04

Earnings analysis

On a disclosed annual basis, revenue rose from KRW 22.8 billion in 2022 to KRW 43.2 billion in 2023 (up 89.3% year on year) and KRW 64.6 billion in 2024 (up 49.5%), then fell to KRW 46.9 billion in 2025 (down 27.4%). Among 2022 to 2025, 2024 was the largest year and 2022 the smallest.

Operating losses over the same period were KRW 7.64 billion in 2022, KRW 10.1 billion in 2023, KRW 3.23 billion in 2024 and KRW 14.5 billion in 2025, a continuous loss record from 2022 to 2025. In 2023 the loss widened even as revenue grew, and cost increases were cited as the driver of weaker profitability.

The loss narrowed in 2024, but in 2025 the operating margin fell to -30.9% and the operating loss widened again to KRW 14.5 billion.

The company attributed the 2025 deterioration to market-share (MS) decline and cost-reduction (CR) pressure amid intensifying supply-chain competition, and to delays in customers' new production plans.

The net loss attributable to controlling shareholders was KRW 12.8 billion in 2024 and KRW 24.7 billion in 2025, two consecutive years of losses, and the 2025 net loss including non-controlling interests was also KRW 24.7 billion.

Operating cash flow was -KRW 10.7 billion in 2022 and -KRW 11.7 billion in 2023, turned positive at KRW 3.05 billion in 2024, and returned to -KRW 6.75 billion in 2025. The debt-to-equity ratio rose from 28.4% in 2022 to 255.5% in 2025, increasing financial pressure. The quarterly path shows a clear turning point.

After an operating loss of KRW 3.78 billion in the third quarter of 2025, the fourth-quarter operating loss narrowed to KRW 2.99 billion; the first quarter of 2026 swung to operating profit of KRW 726 million, then the second quarter returned to an operating loss of KRW 2.02 billion.

The third-quarter 2026 operating loss was KRW 339 million, narrower than a year earlier.

However, the net loss attributable to controlling shareholders in that quarter was KRW 3.46 billion, which widened from the prior quarter, and the net loss attributable to controlling shareholders over the last four quarters totals KRW 14.5 billion.

05

Industry analysis

ICH operates in the internal component-materials market for mobile devices such as smartphones, where volumes for cushioning foam and EMI shielding gaskets are set by customers' new-model launch schedules and production plans. The factors the company cites for its 2025 earnings decline fit this structure.

In 2025, market share fell amid intensifying supply-chain competition, customers pressed harder for cost reductions, and customers' new production plans were delayed.

On a cumulative basis through the third quarter of 2025, falling demand for tape, film and MFA products, intensifying market competition, weak IT-device markets and customer inventory adjustments were identified as drivers of lower sales.

In 2026, foldable-phone material shipments are rising, and new applications such as robots, energy storage systems (ESS) and automobiles are being added.

Listed peers in the electronic components sector include Samsung Electro-Mechanics, LG Innotek and Isupetasys, but ICH is a materials company centered on gaskets, foam and film antennas, so its product mix differs from theirs.

Where the sector sits in its cycle will depend on the shipment trend of end-customers, which will be a variable for upcoming results.

Outlook

The company said it expects to reach an operating profit in the fourth quarter of 2026. Its stated basis is higher supply volumes for foldable-phone materials and expanded supply of new products used in ESS and automobiles.

RF gaskets and functional foam materials newly supplied in the second quarter of 2026 for premium global smartphones were also cited as supporting second-half sales. Stabilization of raw-material prices was presented as a precondition for second-half improvement.

The break-even sales level the company gave fell from about KRW 65 billion to KRW 49 billion, while cumulative sales for January to September 2026 were KRW 35.2 billion; on a simple comparison, the gap between the two figures is the revenue scale that would need to come in the fourth quarter.

A wireless-charging module finished product is slated for a new flagship model from a global smartphone maker set to launch in 2027.

In robotics, the August 2026 extraordinary shareholder meeting approved adding robot material-component manufacturing and sales to the business purpose, and mass supply of PU foam for humanoid robots is the starting point of this business.

The company also said that expanding the third-party allotment limit to 50% of total issued shares creates room to attract strategic investors. These outlooks are company expectations, and fourth-quarter results will be confirmed in the 2026 annual report.

Bull factors

Cost restructuring lowers break-even

The company says relocating production and streamlining headcount cut its break-even sales level from about KRW 65 billion to KRW 49 billion. The third-quarter 2026 operating loss narrowed to KRW 339 million from a year earlier, and the operating margin improved to -2.8%. The first quarter of 2026 had already swung to operating profit of KRW 726 million.

Higher-value new applications

ICH supplies foam materials to two leading foldable-phone makers and has been in mass supply of humanoid-robot materials for a global electric-vehicle maker since August 2026. Its primary vendor code from Samsung Electronics in October 2026 opens the way to direct finished-product supply.

The wireless-charging module component is also set to ship as a finished product for a next flagship model. The company says new products for ESS and automobiles will expand too.

Wider business scope and funding room

At the August 2026 extraordinary shareholder meeting, robot material-component manufacturing and sales was added to the business purpose. The third-party allotment limit rose from 20% to 50% of total issued shares. A 5-for-1 share consolidation cut issued shares from about 25.79 million to about 5.16 million. The company says this gives it flexibility in share-price stabilization and fundraising.

Bear factors

Cumulative losses and debt load

Operating losses have continued for four consecutive years from 2022 to 2025, and the 2025 net loss attributable to controlling shareholders was KRW 24.7 billion. The debt-to-equity ratio was 255.5% at the end of 2025, with total liabilities of KRW 68.3 billion well above total equity of KRW 26.7 billion. After the first-quarter 2026 profit, the second quarter returned to an operating loss of KRW 2.02 billion.

Falling sales and slow growth

Sales fell to KRW 46.9 billion in 2025, down 27.4% year on year. Third-quarter 2026 sales of KRW 12 billion were down 4.2% from a year earlier, and cumulative sales for January to September rose only 0.8%.

On a cumulative basis through the third quarter of 2025, weaker demand for tape, film and MFA products and a sluggish IT-device market pulled sales down.

Raw-material costs and Q4 uncertainty

Second-quarter 2026 profitability weakened temporarily as oil and petrochemical prices rose on Middle East risk. The company's second-half improvement case assumes raw-material prices stabilize, but input costs can move again with external factors. The fourth-quarter turnaround is the company's expectation and is not yet a disclosed result.

Risk factors

Raw materials and external shocks

The key raw material for PU foam once rose by as much as 50% in a short period and caused the second-quarter 2026 profitability dip. If raw-material prices rise again, the KRW 49 billion break-even framework could come under pressure. Middle East geopolitical risk, which can shift oil and petrochemical price paths, is another variable.

Customer concentration and launch timing

Because revenue leans on foldable and smartphone customers, delays in a major customer's launch schedule or production plan translate directly into volume gaps. In 2025 the company cited delays in customers' new production plans as a factor in its weaker results. Revenue scale from new applications in robots, ESS and automobiles has not yet appeared in disclosures.

Capital structure and governance changes

Because the third-party allotment limit now stands at 50%, the number of shares could increase in future fundraising. Operating cash flow was -KRW 6.75 billion in 2025 and the debt-to-equity ratio is 255.5%.

A disclosure dated November 16, 2026 reported that Vice President Hong Ki-kwang left the company effective November 13, 2026.

Overall view

ICH recorded operating losses for four consecutive years, including a KRW 14.5 billion operating loss and a KRW 24.7 billion net loss attributable to controlling shareholders in 2025, and in 2026 the losses have been narrowing on cost-structure gains.

The cumulative operating loss for January to September 2026 was KRW 1.6 billion, far below KRW 11.5 billion a year earlier, and the third-quarter operating loss narrowed to KRW 339 million.

The company's statement that its break-even sales level fell to KRW 49 billion, and the expansion into foldable-phone, robot and wireless-charging components, are cited as strengths.

On the other side, the second quarter of 2026 returned to an operating loss, ending the first-quarter profit within one quarter, and raw-material price swings remain a risk.

A debt-to-equity ratio of 255.5% and total equity of KRW 26.7 billion remain a financial burden, and the fourth-quarter turnaround is the company's expectation rather than a disclosed result.

The share price trades below net assets, but because the last four quarters were net losses, no earnings-based multiple can be calculated. The next checkpoints are whether fourth-quarter 2026 results show an operating profit and how much revenue from new applications appears in the 2026 annual report.
