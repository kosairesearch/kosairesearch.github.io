02

Business structure

Wiable is a telecom infrastructure company listed on KOSDAQ since 2002, and its core business is building, operating and maintaining shared wireless base station relay networks.

The company leases towers and relay networks so that several carriers can use the same infrastructure, and its customers include the three major mobile operators, SK Telecom, KT and LG Uplus, along with public agencies such as the Ministry of Oceans and Fisheries and the Korea Expressway Corporation.

A May 2024 news report cited base station usage fees of KRW 34.6 billion received in 2023 and gave the company a market share of about 70% in shared base stations, with a rival holding about 30%.

The company describes its relay networks in special zones such as national parks and subway lines as a way to reduce duplicate investment and environmental damage.

Beyond base station leasing, the business covers network construction and maintenance in special areas such as railways, subways and tunnels, defense work including system support for the Defense Acquisition Program Administration, and renewable energy generation such as solar power.

The defense push is framed as a response to rising smart defense demand under the Defense Innovation 4.0 plan, applying existing network operating skills to military modernization.

Amendments approved at the March 2026 annual general meeting added solar generation facility installation and operation, energy diagnosis and consulting, national R&D projects, and subway and tunnel telecom facility maintenance to the stated business purposes.

The media subsidiary BK&Media, which runs a concert venue and food and beverage operations, was reported to have 2023 revenue of KRW 300 million and a net loss of KRW 300 million.

Base station leasing and maintenance remain the center of the company's earnings, while the revenue contribution from new businesses is not yet clearly visible in the disclosures.

04

Earnings analysis

Annual sales moved from KRW 64.3 billion in 2022 to KRW 72.6 billion in 2023 (up 12.9% year on year) and KRW 79.4 billion in 2024 (up 9.5%), before falling 4.9% to KRW 75.5 billion in 2025. The largest sales year in 2022–2025 was 2024, and the smallest was 2022.

Operating profit fell from KRW 3.4 billion in 2022 to KRW 2.88 billion in 2023 (down 15.3%), then rose 32.6% to KRW 3.83 billion in 2024 and 43.1% to KRW 5.48 billion in 2025, increasing for two consecutive years.

The operating margin ran at 5.3% in 2022, 4.0% in 2023, 4.8% in 2024 and 7.2% in 2025, the highest of the four years. The company attributed the 2025 improvement mainly to lower costs, so profit rose even as sales declined.

Net income attributable to controlling shareholders went from KRW 2.44 billion in 2022 to KRW 2.34 billion in 2023 (down 3.9%), then KRW 2.99 billion in 2024 (up 27.8%) and KRW 4.55 billion in 2025 (up 52.0%), again rising for two years after the 2023 low.

Net income including non-controlling interests was also reported at KRW 4.55 billion for 2025. Operating cash flow was KRW 14.8 billion in 2022, KRW 10.6 billion in 2023, KRW 16 billion in 2024 and KRW 15.7 billion in 2025, recovering after the 2023 dip. Quarterly results were volatile.

Sales in Q3 2025 were KRW 15.6 billion with operating profit of KRW 885 million, and Q4 2025 sales jumped to KRW 30.8 billion (up 98.1% from the prior quarter) with operating profit of KRW 3.77 billion (up 325.9%).

Q1 2026 sales fell to KRW 12 billion (down 61.2%) and operating profit to KRW 1.33 billion (down 64.7%), and sales then rose for two consecutive quarters to KRW 16.4 billion in Q2 and Q3 2026.

Operating profit in Q3 2026 was KRW 1.45 billion (up 16.9% from Q2), and net income attributable to controlling shareholders over the last four quarters totaled KRW 6.48 billion.

05

Industry analysis

The shared base station market that Wiable serves moves with mobile carriers' network build-outs and capital spending. Pinpoint News reported that heavier investment in 5G upgrades and early positioning for 6G had been a driver of the sector's rally.

The same report said expectations for more base station construction were reflected in Wiable's share price. Reports also noted that a resumption of capital spending by overseas carriers had influenced buying in domestic telecom equipment stocks.

In defense, the company is positioning its expansion around rising demand tied to the Defense Innovation 4.0 plan. A May 2024 report put Wiable's share of the shared base station market at about 70%, with a rival holding about 30%, but that figure is from 2024 and may not reflect today's structure.

A 2024 report also pointed to limits on the profitability of the base station business, noting that revenue fell from KRW 86.3 billion in 2019 to KRW 72.6 billion in 2023.

Sales then rebounded to KRW 79.4 billion in 2024 before falling again to KRW 75.5 billion in 2025, so the top line has been broadly flat over several years.

The industry position will therefore depend on whether base station leasing demand holds and on whether defense, solar and ESS businesses grow to a larger share of revenue.

06

Outlook

In a voluntary corporate value plan released in March 2026, the company said it would keep capital expenditure (CAPEX) at its current level for the next three years while looking for new businesses.

The same plan included building an integrated power trading management platform and expanding investment in solar generation.

On dividends, the company said it would maintain the dividend level to the extent that this does not harm shareholder value, and the payout ratio for 2025, the latest completed fiscal year, was disclosed at 52.6%.

The treasury share cancellation disclosed in March 2026 covered 5,021,241 shares, equal to 9.5% of issued shares, which reduces the number of shares in circulation.

The articles of incorporation were amended to add solar generation facility installation and operation and energy diagnosis to the stated business purposes, which prepares the ground for renewable energy as a formal business area.

A report dated August 24, 2026 said Wiable would invest in the LFP battery maker Lumpriel to expand its ESS business. These new businesses have not yet made a large contribution to results, which is consistent with cumulative sales for January–September 2026 rising only 0.1%.

The next checkpoint is how revenue from businesses beyond base station leasing shows up in the annual disclosures. The full-year 2026 operating margin, and whether it holds near the 7.2% recorded in 2025, is also a key data point.

Bull factors

Recovery in operating profit and margins

Operating profit rose for two consecutive years after the 2023 low, reaching KRW 3.83 billion in 2024 and KRW 5.48 billion in 2025, and the operating margin of 7.2% in 2025 was the highest in the disclosed period. The company credited lower costs as the main reason for the improvement.

Operating profit for Q3 2026 was KRW 1.45 billion, up 63.5% year on year, showing that the recovery also appears at the quarterly level.

Expanded shareholder return measures

In its March 2026 corporate value plan, the company said it would maintain its dividend level, and the 2025 payout ratio was disclosed at 52.6%. In the same month it decided to cancel about KRW 9.5 billion worth of repurchased treasury shares, 5,021,241 shares equal to 9.5% of issued shares. Cancellation reduces the number of shares in circulation, which raises per-share measures.

Diversification into new businesses

Amendments approved at the March 2026 shareholder meeting added solar facility installation, energy diagnosis and national R&D projects to the company's business purposes. A report dated August 24, 2026 said Wiable would invest in the LFP battery maker Lumpriel to expand its ESS business.

Defense work, linked to demand under the Defense Innovation 4.0 plan, connects to the company's existing network operating skills.

Bear factors

Flat top line and the 2025 sales decline

Sales fell 4.9% to KRW 75.5 billion in 2025 after peaking at KRW 79.4 billion in 2024. Cumulative sales for January–September 2026 rose only 0.1% year on year. A 2024 report had also pointed to a long decline, with revenue falling from KRW 86.3 billion in 2019 to KRW 72.6 billion in 2023.

Limited results from new businesses

A 2024 report said the company judged a chemical products business unlikely to meet its expected returns and shelved it. Renewable energy was added to the business purposes in 2008, but the report said it had not yet produced clear results. The media subsidiary BK&Media reported 2023 revenue of KRW 300 million and a net loss of KRW 300 million.

Governance history and trading volatility

A 2024 report cited the chief executive's 2014 indictment as a governance risk. After a sharp share price rise in April 2026, the stock was designated an investment warning issue from April 14, and the designation was lifted on April 27 before a one-day investment caution designation on April 28. The largest shareholder held 42.23% as of April 2025.

Risk factors

Customer concentration

Base station leasing income depends on contracts with the three major carriers and public agencies, so changes in their capital spending or lease terms feed directly into results. A 2024 report also described tower leasing to the three carriers as the main revenue source. How carrier capex changes affect leasing demand is the core variable for this business.

Share price volatility and trading rules

On March 24, 2026, the share price jumped 23.45% in a single session, and short-term swings have been large. The stock was then designated an investment warning issue in April 2026, and the exchange said it could be re-designated after the lifting if conditions were met. Trading conditions can shift quickly for a stock with frequent sharp moves.

New business investment and financial load

New businesses such as solar and ESS require sizable investment, and their contribution to reported results is not yet clear. Total liabilities were KRW 61.6 billion at the end of 2025, with a debt-to-equity ratio of 78.9%, so the balance sheet still carries meaningful debt. Holding CAPEX flat while expanding new business investment makes the allocation of cash the key issue.

Overall view

Wiable's operating profit and net income attributable to controlling shareholders rose for two consecutive years after the 2023 low, and its 2025 operating margin of 7.2% was the highest in the disclosed period.

Sales, however, peaked at KRW 79.4 billion in 2024 and fell to KRW 75.5 billion in 2025, and cumulative sales for January–September 2026 rose only 0.1% year on year.

Quarterly results have been volatile, with a sharp Q4 2025 jump followed by a steep Q1 2026 decline and two consecutive quarters of higher sales in Q2 and Q3 2026. On shareholder returns, the company has kept its dividend level, disclosed a 2025 payout ratio of 52.6%, and decided to cancel treasury shares.

The core business remains shared base station leasing, and the revenue contribution from defense, solar and ESS businesses is not yet clear in the reported figures.

The reported share of about 70% in shared base stations is a strength, but it comes from a 2024 report and should be read separately from the current structure. The share price has been volatile, as the April 2026 investment warning designation shows.
