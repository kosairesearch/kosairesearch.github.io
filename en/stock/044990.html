ACF Sales Recover, Earnings Volatility Remains

H&SHigh Tech's third-quarter 2026 revenue of KRW 27 billion marked a third consecutive quarterly increase, yet operating profit fell 6.0% year on year and net income attributable to controlling shareholders was only KRW 597 million, so sales and profit are moving in different directions.

02

Business structure

H&SHigh Tech was founded in 1995 and listed on KOSDAQ in 2024. It runs a materials division centered on ACF (anisotropic conductive film) and an electronics division producing crystal units and oscillators.

ACF is an ultra-fine bonding material that electrically connects display panels and circuit boards, and it is used in smartphones, TVs, monitors and camera modules. The materials division generated KRW 57.3 billion of revenue in 2025, about 70% of the total.

Camera-module ACF revenue was KRW 18.6 billion in the first half of 2026, about 41% of the total and the largest single product line. In the same period, ACF for printed circuit boards (PWB) brought in KRW 7.4 billion and touch-panel ACF (TOUCH) KRW 1.2 billion.

The key camera-module ACF customer is a global big-tech company referred to as Company A. The company has supplied it for over ten years, and once Company A specifies the material in its bill of materials, first-tier camera-module vendors source it.

In the electronics division, crystal revenue reached KRW 8.3 billion in the first half of 2026, more than four times the KRW 1.9 billion a year earlier, helped by price increases linked to higher raw-material costs. The company says about 80% of total revenue is settled in US dollars.

In a July 2025 report, Daishin Securities described the global ACF market as dominated by a few suppliers, including Japan's Dexerials and Resonac. H&SHigh Tech holds the number one position in Korea and the number three position globally.

04

Earnings analysis

On a reported basis, annual revenue fell from KRW 97.2 billion in 2022 to KRW 80.6 billion in 2023 (down 17.1%) and KRW 76.2 billion in 2024 (down 5.5%), then rose to KRW 81.9 billion in 2025 (up 7.5%).

Operating profit was KRW 21.6 billion in 2022, KRW 14 billion in 2023 (down 35.4%), KRW 13.4 billion in 2024 (down 4.2%) and KRW 9.66 billion in 2025 (down 27.7%), a decline for three consecutive years from 2023 to 2025. The operating margin moved from 22.2% to 17.3%, 17.6% and 11.8%.

The CEO attributed the 2025 profit decline to hiring and facility investment, and the July 2025 purchase of a second Ansan site was part of that spending.

Net income attributable to controlling shareholders peaked at KRW 30.1 billion in 2023 (up 55.9%) before falling to KRW 14.7 billion in 2024 and KRW 8.89 billion in 2025, two straight years of decline.

The reason 2023 net income far exceeded operating profit of KRW 14 billion cannot be determined from the available material. Operating cash flow also declined from KRW 35.3 billion in 2022 to KRW 10.7 billion in 2025.

Quarterly revenue bottomed at KRW 21.6 billion in the fourth quarter of 2025, then rose to KRW 22.2 billion, KRW 22.8 billion and KRW 27 billion in the first to third quarters of 2026, three consecutive quarterly increases.

Quarterly operating profit swung from KRW 3.07 billion in the first quarter to KRW 1.97 billion in the second and KRW 3.97 billion in the third, and the third quarter was down 6.0% year on year.

The first-quarter dip reflected higher R&D and talent-acquisition costs, with ordinary R&D expense within SG&A at KRW 2.0 billion, roughly double the KRW 1.0 billion of a year earlier. First-half consolidated revenue of KRW 45.0 billion was reported as the highest ever for a half-year period.

Net income attributable to controlling shareholders fell from KRW 3.64 billion in the first quarter of 2026 to KRW 1.53 billion in the second and KRW 597 million in the third, two straight quarterly declines and 86.3% lower year on year.

Why third-quarter net income was so far below operating profit of KRW 3.97 billion should be checked in the non-operating items of the quarterly report.

05

Industry analysis

The ACF market is an oligopoly of a few top suppliers; in a July 2025 report Daishin Securities put Dexerials' share at 60% to 70% and Resonac's at about 15%.

The same report said H&SHigh Tech entered the OLED market on a unit price about one-third that of competitors and the benefit of domestic production, and had secured a 5% share.

The company points to seasonality: premium-model sales at the top global mobile makers concentrate from September through year-end, lifting second-half ACF supply. In the first half of 2026, sales of that maker's mid-to-low-end models rose far more than expected, which lifted shipments of the company's main product.

A weaker won also raised won-denominated revenue, given the high share of dollar-settled sales. In the electronics division, higher raw-material costs were passed through as price increases that lifted crystal revenue. Part of the growth therefore reflects pricing and currency, not volume alone.

The company is trying to shift its weight from display materials toward semiconductor packaging materials, including domestic substitutes for build-up film, a field long dominated by Japanese suppliers.

Camera-module ACF volumes swing by quarter with a single customer's product schedule, so the business is sensitive to smartphone launch timing and exchange rates.

06

Outlook

The company said that with new smartphone launches and wider premium-model sales in the second half of 2026, ACF supply should grow and annual revenue could exceed KRW 100 billion for the first time since its founding.

That is a company expectation and needs to be checked against the quarterly revenue trend through the third quarter and the fourth-quarter result.

A company official said second-half growth would be supported by mass production of OLED products for a major Chinese customer, new E-Paper approval and expanded camera-module ACF supply to a global smartphone customer.

The industry expects adoption to extend beyond rear cameras to front cameras in the follow-up model due in the first half of 2027. High-density and array ACF have completed development, and the company plans sequential mass production from the second half, based on evaluation results.

For semiconductor packaging materials, the goal is to finish customer line evaluations by the end of 2026, and the business has not yet entered full-scale mass production.

In a February 2026 interview the CEO said the large investment phase had ended and that operating leverage, where higher sales flow directly into profit, would take hold. However, third-quarter 2026 operating profit was below the prior-year level, so later quarters are needed to confirm a margin recovery.

08

Bull factors

Wider adoption of camera-module ACF

Camera-module ACF is the largest product line, with first-half 2026 revenue of KRW 18.6 billion, about 41% of the total. If adoption extends to front cameras from the model due in the first half of 2027, ACF content per device would rise, and its share within Company A's supply is said to be able to exceed half. That could translate into room for higher revenue and operating margin through a larger customer share.

Light leverage and insider buying

The debt-to-equity ratio was 12.5% at the end of 2025, with total liabilities of KRW 12.9 billion and total equity of KRW 102.9 billion. The CEO bought 4,000 shares on the open market on October 27, 2026, lifting his stake to 21.30%.

Vice Chairman Eui-ho Bae also made 11 open-market purchases between July 13 and October 22, 2026. These are noted as facts only and are not read as a signal on the share price.

Expansion options in high-value ACF and semiconductor materials

High-density COG and array COG/COP ACF have completed development and entered commercialization, with product approvals and test evaluations under way at multiple customers, including global ones. For semiconductor packaging materials, the target is to finish customer line evaluations by the end of 2026. Passing those evaluations could add a new pillar to a revenue base concentrated in displays and cameras.

Bear factors

Revenue is growing while margins have compressed

The annual operating margin fell from 22.2% in 2022 to 11.8% in 2025, and in the third quarter of 2026 revenue rose 14.7% year on year while operating profit fell 6.0%. The company said salaries and benefits for new talent and depreciation from facility investment had increased.

First-half 2026 R&D expense of KRW 4.4 billion already exceeded the full-year 2024 figure of KRW 4.2 billion. The question is whether the fixed-cost burden offsets revenue growth.

Large quarterly swings in net income and lower cash flow

Net income attributable to controlling shareholders fell from KRW 3.64 billion in the first quarter of 2026 to KRW 1.53 billion in the second and KRW 597 million in the third, two straight quarterly declines.

The gap versus third-quarter operating profit of KRW 3.97 billion hints at non-operating factors, but the cause cannot be confirmed from the available material. Operating cash flow also declined from KRW 35.3 billion in 2022 to KRW 10.7 billion in 2025, so earnings quality and cash generation both need checking.

Dependence on one customer and dollar settlement

Camera-module ACF accounted for about 41% of first-half 2026 revenue, a large share for a single product line. Its sales rose as mass-production volumes were concentrated in the first half ahead of Company A's second-half flagship launch.

About 80% of revenue is settled in dollars, so won-denominated results shift with exchange rates. If customer schedules or exchange rates move unfavorably, quarterly results could swing more widely.

Risk factors

Customer concentration and product cycles

Camera-module ACF volumes depend on the launch schedule and adoption scope of Company A's new smartphone models. First-half 2026 revenue was influenced by production volumes front-loaded ahead of the second-half flagship launch.

If adoption is not extended in the follow-up model, or the launch slips, quarterly revenue and utilization could be disrupted. The contract terms and share of that business are not available in public materials.

Currency and raw materials

About 80% of revenue is settled in dollars, and the won-dollar rate rose to the mid-1,500 range in the first half of 2026, boosting won-denominated sales. If the rate falls, won revenue could shrink even at the same overseas volume.

Crystal revenue grew as higher raw-material costs were reflected in prices, so a decline in raw-material prices could work in the opposite direction.

New-business uncertainty and shareholding structure

Semiconductor packaging materials have not reached mass production and must still pass customer line evaluations. If evaluations are delayed or failed, only the R&D cost burden might remain.

In addition, the expiry of a joint-holding agreement removed several shareholders from the group of related parties, and the holding ratio of the largest shareholder's group fell by 6.76 percentage points.

Whether those shareholders will later sell shares has not been confirmed, so future ownership filings need to be followed.

Overall view

H&SHigh Tech's third-quarter 2026 revenue of KRW 27 billion rose for a third consecutive quarter, but operating profit fell 6.0% year on year and net income attributable to controlling shareholders fell 86.3% to KRW 597 million.

Annual operating profit declined for three straight years from 2023 to 2025, and the operating margin fell from 22.2% to 11.8%. On the bullish side, the cited points are wider adoption of camera-module ACF, a low 12.5% debt-to-equity ratio, and expansion options in high-value ACF and semiconductor packaging materials.

On the bearish side, rising costs keep revenue growth from translating into profit, quarterly net income swings widely, and the business depends on a specific customer and dollar settlement. Annual revenue above KRW 100 billion is a company expectation, not an established fact.

The next points to check are the customer evaluation results for semiconductor materials, the fourth-quarter margin in the 2026 annual report, and the scope of ACF adoption in the follow-up model in the first half of 2027.
