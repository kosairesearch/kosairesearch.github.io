02

Business structure

Billions was founded in 1973 as a maker of hygienic and medical rubber products and listed on KOSDAQ in 2001. In 2023 it acquired two entertainment companies, after which it ran condom and medical rubber sales alongside entertainment.

The company describes itself as the number one condom maker in the domestic market, and its main products are condoms, a finger-thimble product, and gloves. Gloves are divided into medical, industrial, protective, and examination types.

The manufacturing side produces condoms to international standards, with regular and specialty types and a range of colors and fragrances. The entertainment side manages artist activities and plans and produces content, including dramas, and pairs new-talent marketing with OTT content production.

An outside data set puts the Q2 2026 sales mix at 55% management and 45% medical device manufacturing and sales. Once the annual report is filed, segment sales can be checked on the same basis. The largest shareholder changed on May 21, 2024.

At the extraordinary shareholders meeting on November 6, 2026, an amendment to the articles and the election of Kim Tae-sung as an inside director both passed. The amendment also reworded the business purpose covering e-commerce.

04

Earnings analysis

Annual revenue rose from KRW 78.1 billion in 2022 to KRW 97 billion in 2023, the largest year in the 2022 to 2025 period, with 2023 up 24.3% year on year. Revenue then fell for two straight years, to KRW 41.7 billion in 2024 (down 57.0%) and KRW 25.3 billion in 2025 (down 39.3%).

The operating loss was KRW 8.88 billion in 2022, KRW 7.49 billion in 2023, KRW 1.13 billion in 2024, and KRW 834 million in 2025, a loss in each of four consecutive years that narrowed every year. The operating margin read -11.4%, -7.7%, -2.7%, and -3.3%, so 2025 was slightly weaker than 2024 as revenue declined.

The net loss attributable to controlling shareholders was KRW 18.4 billion in 2022, KRW 13.8 billion in 2023, KRW 1.96 billion in 2024, and KRW 7.13 billion in 2025, which was a loss in each of four consecutive years and widened again in 2025.

Net income including non-controlling interests was KRW 8.61 billion in 2024, positive, while the loss attributable to controlling shareholders that year was KRW 1.96 billion, and the gap between the two reflects non-controlling interests.

Operating cash flow went from negative KRW 1.64 billion in 2022 and negative KRW 3.36 billion in 2023 to positive KRW 677 million in 2024 and KRW 722 million in 2025. The debt ratio rose from 61.6% in 2022 to 115.9% in 2023 and 121.8% in 2024, then fell to 98.6% in 2025.

Total equity at end-2025 was KRW 24.5 billion, all of it attributable to controlling shareholders, and total liabilities were KRW 24.2 billion.

Quarterly revenue went from KRW 7.58 billion in Q3 2025 and KRW 5.11 billion in Q4 2025 (down 32.6% quarter on quarter) to KRW 7.89 billion in Q1 2026 (up 54.4%), then fell to KRW 6.76 billion in Q2 2026 (down 14.3%) and KRW 4.84 billion in Q3 2026 (down 28.4%), a decline over two consecutive quarters.

The operating loss was KRW 12.81 million in Q3 2025 and KRW 63.54 million in Q4 2025, then turned to operating profit of KRW 149 million in Q1 2026. Q2 2026 returned to an operating loss of KRW 580 million, and Q3 2026 widened to KRW 1.04 billion, so the loss has run for two consecutive quarters.

Net loss attributable to controlling shareholders was KRW 1.35 billion in Q3 2025 and KRW 4.49 billion in Q4 2025, and no quarterly figures are available for Q1 through Q3 2026 because those reports have not yet been filed.

The combined net loss attributable to controlling shareholders over the last four quarters (Q4 2025 to Q3 2026) is KRW 22.7 billion.

05

Industry analysis

Billions is classified under rubber products manufacturing, while its trading theme is listed as entertainment. As a result, the company runs two businesses on different cycles, manufacturing and content, under one corporate entity.

Condoms and gloves are manufacturing products whose economics depend on raw material prices and plant utilization. The description of the company as the top condom maker in the domestic market comes from the company's own profile.

Competition in entertainment sits between large listed talent agencies such as HYBE, SM Entertainment, JYP Entertainment, and YG Entertainment, and smaller production firms. Billions is a small-cap company that is hard to compare directly with the large listed agencies.

Drama and OTT production carries large earnings swings tied to hit rates, and other listed companies in the sector share that trait. Even within media and entertainment, Cheil Worldwide, CJ ENM, and Studio Dragon run different business models, so their results are hard to compare directly.

Whether the two consecutive annual revenue declines came from entertainment or from manufacturing can only be separated with segment data. Medical rubber products depend on healthcare institution demand and procurement conditions.

Overall view

Billions is a KOSDAQ company that combines condom and medical rubber manufacturing with entertainment. On a DART filing basis, the annual operating loss narrowed from KRW 8.88 billion in 2022 to KRW 834 million in 2025.

Revenue, however, peaked at KRW 97 billion in 2023 and then fell in both 2024 and 2025, and Q3 2026 revenue of KRW 4.84 billion was down 36.2% year on year. Q1 2026 showed an operating profit of KRW 149 million, but operating losses continued in Q2 and Q3 2026.

On the financial side, the debt ratio fell from 121.8% in 2024 to 98.6% in 2025, and operating cash flow was positive in both 2024 and 2025.

On the burden side are the combined KRW 22.7 billion net loss over the last four quarters, its relation to year-end 2025 equity of KRW 24.5 billion, and the changes in the largest shareholder and board since 2024.

On valuation, the price-to-book ratio sits below 1x and the price-to-earnings ratio cannot be calculated, so the reading depends heavily on whether earnings recover. The next point to confirm is the full-year result and segment sales in the 2026 annual report, due by late March 2027.

In short, the current filings show a tug-of-war between a narrowing annual loss and weakening quarterly results.
