Consumer MCU Maker: Operating Gains Diverge from Net Profit Slowdown

ABOV Semiconductor turned operating profit positive in 2025 on the back of its consumer MCU franchise, but its third-quarter 2026 net profit attributable to controlling shareholders fell sharply from a year earlier, making earnings durability the next item to verify.

02

Business structure

ABOV Semiconductor is a fabless chipmaker whose core product is the microcontroller unit (MCU) used in home appliances and industrial electronics.

The company supplies more than 1,000 types of 8-bit and 32-bit MCUs and has widened its lineup to touch sensors, ambient light sensors and power semiconductors using high-voltage devices.

In the first three quarters of 2025, consumer MCUs generated KRW 95.8 billion of cumulative revenue from KRW 180.9 billion in total, or 53%, making them the largest revenue source. The second pillar is the packaging (PKG) business of subsidiary Winpac, which recorded KRW 36.7 billion, or 20%, over the same period.

Reports said Winpac's revenue reached KRW 39.7 billion in the first half of 2026, equal to 30% of consolidated revenue, indicating a growing weight for packaging. The CEO said the company serves more than 70 customers, including three major domestic appliance makers.

Overseas, the company is expanding into China, Japan, Europe and India, and its company profile notes recent development of the Turkish market. On competition, the company says its in-house analog IP lets it shrink die size and offer competitive pricing.

In the automotive MCU market, global players such as NXP, Renesas and Infineon lead. The AI MCU developed with Samsung Electronics is at the stage of supply promotion.

04

Earnings analysis

Annual revenue went from KRW 242.6 billion in 2022 to KRW 232.4 billion in 2023 (down 4.2% year on year) and KRW 232.1 billion in 2024 (down 0.1%), before rising to KRW 244.1 billion in 2025 (up 5.2%). Among 2022 to 2025, 2025 was the largest year for revenue and 2024 the smallest.

Operating income moved from KRW 25.8 billion in 2022 to an operating loss of KRW 14.6 billion in 2023, a swing into loss, and then an operating loss of KRW 5.1 billion in 2024, a continued loss with a narrower gap.

In 2025 operating income returned to KRW 10.1 billion, and the operating margin progressed from 10.6% in 2022 to -6.3% in 2023, -2.2% in 2024 and 4.2% in 2025.

Net profit attributable to controlling shareholders swung from a loss of KRW 12.5 billion in 2023 to KRW 2.65 billion in 2024 and KRW 10.1 billion in 2025, up 281.4% year on year.

Net income including non-controlling interests is a different figure, and the net losses of KRW 33.4 billion in 2023 and KRW 18.1 billion in 2024 show that losses at non-controlling interests were large in those years. Net income in 2025 was KRW 1.96 billion.

Quarterly figures went from revenue of KRW 58.3 billion and controlling-shareholder net profit of KRW 3.4 billion in the third quarter of 2025 to revenue of KRW 63.2 billion (up 8.3% quarter on quarter) and a net loss attributable to controlling shareholders of KRW 2.12 billion in the fourth quarter, a swing into loss.

In the first quarter of 2026, revenue was KRW 62.5 billion (down 1.0% quarter on quarter), operating income KRW 3.16 billion (up 128.1%) and controlling-shareholder net profit KRW 5.16 billion, the peak of the recent quarters.

In the second quarter revenue rose to KRW 68.9 billion (up 10.1%) and operating income to KRW 3.52 billion (up 11.3%), while controlling-shareholder net profit fell 39.1% to KRW 3.14 billion.

In the third quarter revenue was KRW 66.7 billion (up 14.4% year on year), operating income KRW 2.56 billion (up 71.6% year on year), and controlling-shareholder net profit KRW 442 million (down 87.0% year on year).

Operating income rose while net profit attributable to controlling shareholders fell sharply, so the two measures diverged. Net profit attributable to controlling shareholders totaled KRW 6.62 billion over the last four quarters.

Whether non-operating items or non-controlling interests drive this gap should be checked in the notes and income statement of the 2026 annual report.

At the end of 2025, total equity was KRW 154.4 billion, of which equity attributable to controlling shareholders was KRW 118.9 billion, and total liabilities were KRW 131.6 billion.

Operating cash flow was KRW -2.41 billion in 2022, KRW 32.4 billion in 2023, KRW 27 billion in 2024 and KRW 14.7 billion in 2025, remaining positive after 2023 but lower in 2025 than in 2024.

05

Industry analysis

The company has described the domestic MCU market as KRW 500 billion and the global market as more than KRW 13 trillion, according to a late-2025 report. As the appliance market shifts toward premium models, MCUs are becoming more capable.

Reports in early 2024 pointed to demand for MCUs that support on-sensor AI, in which AI is embedded in image sensors. The same report noted that MCUs in global appliances and in industrial pumps, fans and fire detection devices are moving to 32-bit.

The company is pursuing expansion from appliances into industrial electronics. Reports also highlighted an expansion of power management chips and sensor supply. In terms of position, media have introduced the company as the domestic No. 1 MCU maker.

Global competitors in automotive MCUs include NXP, Renesas and Infineon, and the automotive market may require time for new entrants to win customers. The company's in-house IP design and low-power MCU lineup are presented as tools for meeting cost demands from appliance customers.

At the industry level, appliance replacement cycles and the adoption of AI functions are the main variables for MCU demand.

06

Outlook

The company said in a late-2025 interview that it plans to begin full-scale mass production of its AI MCU in 2026. According to the company, the AI MCU achieves recognition rates above 90%, which it says helps it operate more stably in real-world environments.

The AI MCU developed in collaboration with Samsung Electronics is at the supply-promotion stage, and the company is also widening applications to healthcare and automotive semiconductors. In automotive MCUs, the company works with Hyundai Mobis on a national R&D project, but no related revenue has yet been recorded.

The company announced on its homepage that it held the launch ceremony of 'A-1', a company-wide task force to strengthen its automotive business, in November 2026.

Winpac's packaging business grew to KRW 39.7 billion in revenue in the first half of 2026, and reports said mass production and utilization rates will be the key factors going forward. On shareholder returns, the March 2026 shareholders' meeting approved the financial statements and a cash dividend.

The next periodic report, the 2026 annual report, will show whether AI MCU and automotive businesses are reflected in reported results, and it will also include fourth-quarter 2026 results.

The speed at which new products convert into revenue, on top of a stable consumer MCU base, is the key variable for the next phase.

08

Bull factors

Consumer MCU base and product breadth

The company offers more than 1,000 MCU products along with touch and ambient light sensors and power semiconductors. The CEO said it has more than 70 customers, including three major domestic appliance makers, and consumer MCUs made up 53% of cumulative revenue in the first three quarters of 2025.

Its in-house analog IP is said to reduce die size and support price competitiveness, keeping the consumer MCU base central to results.

Recovery in operating profitability

Operating income moved from an operating loss of KRW 14.6 billion in 2023 and a loss of KRW 5.1 billion in 2024 to KRW 10.1 billion in 2025. The operating margin rose to 4.2% in 2025, out of the loss zone.

Third-quarter 2026 operating income of KRW 2.56 billion was up 71.6% year on year, so the operating-level improvement has continued on a quarterly basis.

AI MCU and business diversification

The company is pursuing supply of an AI MCU, described as achieving recognition rates above 90%, in collaboration with Samsung Electronics. In November 2026 it launched a company-wide task force, 'A-1', to strengthen its automotive business.

Packaging subsidiary Winpac's revenue of KRW 39.7 billion in the first half of 2026 equaled 30% of consolidated revenue, broadening the business base.

09

Bear factors

Consecutive declines in controlling-shareholder profit

Net profit attributable to controlling shareholders fell from KRW 5.16 billion in the first quarter of 2026 to KRW 3.14 billion in the second and KRW 442 million in the third. The third-quarter figure was down 87.0% year on year, diverging from the rise in operating income.

With a four-quarter total of KRW 6.62 billion, the profit base is small and can be significantly affected by non-operating items.

Cash flow and working capital pressure

Operating cash flow fell to KRW 14.7 billion in 2025 from KRW 27 billion in 2024. Reports said trade receivables reached KRW 29.6 billion in the first half of 2026, up KRW 5 billion from year-end 2025, adding to working capital needs.

Operating cash flow was a net inflow of KRW 8.2 billion in the same half, but the debt-to-equity ratio stood at 105.0% in 2023, so financial headroom depends on earnings.

Early automotive entry and cost pressure

Reports say the company's automotive MCU work with Hyundai Mobis under a national R&D project has yet to generate related revenue. Global players including NXP, Renesas and Infineon lead the market, so winning new customers may take time. In the first half of 2026 revenue rose 7.1%, but cost of sales rose 8.7%, increasing cost pressure.

10

Risk factors

Appliance demand and customer concentration

Because consumer MCUs account for more than half of revenue, changes in set demand from customers, including three major domestic appliance makers, directly affect sales.

After operating losses in 2023 and 2024, a demand slowdown could bring losses back, and the pace of customer diversification determines the size of this risk.

Packaging volume and utilization

Winpac's packaging business accounted for 30% of consolidated revenue in the first half of 2026, a larger share. Reports pointed to mass-production expansion and utilization rates as the key factors going forward. If utilization does not rise as expected, the pace of packaging revenue growth could slow. The operating trajectory of this business is the next variable for consolidated results.

AI MCU supply timing

The AI MCU, which the company says achieves recognition rates above 90%, is at the supply-promotion stage in collaboration with Samsung Electronics. Supply volumes and timing have not been set, so its revenue contribution is not yet visible in reported results.

If supply is delayed or customer adoption stays narrow, the conversion of new products into revenue could slow. The next check point is whether supply materializes in the first half of 2027.

12

Overall view

ABOV Semiconductor's business is anchored in consumer MCUs, and 2025 saw both a swing to operating profit and an increase in net profit attributable to controlling shareholders.

In 2026, operating income rose year on year each quarter, but net profit attributable to controlling shareholders has declined for two consecutive quarters since the first-quarter peak, so the two measures diverge.

The expanding share of the Winpac packaging business and the lower debt-to-equity ratio, from 105.0% in 2023 to 85.2% in 2025, are cited as strengths. On the other hand, the four-quarter total of KRW 6.62 billion is small, so results can be easily swayed by non-operating factors.

The AI MCU and automotive MCU efforts remain at the planning or early stage and are not yet reflected in reported results. The next checkpoint is the 2026 annual report, where the source of the gap between operating income and net profit attributable to controlling shareholders is the central question.
