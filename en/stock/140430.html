Order Backlog Grows as Operating Losses Persist for Three Quarters

Catis, a KOSDAQ-listed company specializing in spatial-awareness security platforms, has posted operating losses for three straight quarters from the first quarter through the third quarter of 2026, while order backlog growth and large contracts remain the key variables for an earnings recovery.

02

Business structure

Catis is a spatial-awareness security platform company founded in 2006 that develops technology to process, in real time, status information on moving objects such as people and robots inside secured areas.

Its business is split into three segments: industrial infrastructure security platforms, PIDS (Perimeter Intrusion Detection System), and technology services.

The industrial infrastructure segment supplies software and hardware that process and control data generated at facilities such as airports, while PIDS detects and controls infrastructure perimeters. Technology services cover security technology and customized consulting.

Its flagship offering is the quantum-cryptography-based next-generation security platform AxiQuant 1.5, which the company says it is using to expand orders.

The company is also advancing upgrades of its post-quantum cryptography security platform, the introduction of quantum key distribution communication equipment, and an Edge AI-based perimeter defense system.

Its customers appear to be large facility operators such as airports and data center operators, and in May 2026 it signed a data center physical security contract with JAICNA's counterpart company. Major shareholders and related parties hold 53.37% of common shares.

Catis listed on KOSDAQ on May 3, 2024 through a merger with KB 22nd SPAC. Unlike Ahnlab, which centers on cybersecurity software, Catis focuses on building physical space perimeter and access infrastructure projects, so the two differ in business character.

04

Earnings analysis

Annual sales were KRW 30 billion in 2023, KRW 31.7 billion in 2024 (up 5.5% year on year), and KRW 27.9 billion in 2025 (down 11.9% year on year). Among 2023 to 2025, 2024 was the largest year and 2025 the smallest.

Operating profit fell from KRW 1.89 billion in 2023 to KRW 1.59 billion in 2024 (down 15.7% year on year), and in 2025 the company recorded an operating loss of KRW 537 million, turning from profit to loss year on year. The operating margin was 6.3% in 2023, 5.0% in 2024, and -1.9% in 2025.

The company attributed the main changes to lower sales from an extended aviation security personal screening project and higher imported raw material costs from the sharp rise in the exchange rate.

Net profit attributable to controlling shareholders moved from KRW 2.11 billion in 2023 to a net loss of KRW 5.09 billion in 2024, a swing into the red, and then to a net loss of KRW 352 million in 2025, a narrower loss.

Net profit including non-controlling interests was a net loss of KRW 5.09 billion in 2024 and KRW 352 million in 2025, the same amounts as shown for the controlling shareholders in the reported figures.

Operating cash flow went from KRW 5.75 billion in 2023 to -KRW 3.06 billion in 2024, and then turned positive at KRW 52.28 million in 2025. Total equity at the end of 2025 was KRW 39 billion, all attributable to controlling shareholders, and total liabilities were KRW 24.5 billion.

The debt ratio declined from 95.0% in 2023 to 70.2% in 2024 and 62.7% in 2025.

Quarterly sales rose from KRW 4.46 billion in the third quarter of 2025 to KRW 9.37 billion in the fourth quarter (up 109.9% quarter on quarter), then fell to KRW 2.43 billion in the first quarter of 2026 (down 74.1%), rose to KRW 6.92 billion in the second quarter (up 185.3%), and fell to KRW 2.94 billion in the third quarter of 2026 (down 57.5% quarter on quarter and down 34.1% year on year).

At the operating level, the company swung from an operating loss of KRW 587 million in the third quarter of 2025 to an operating profit of KRW 1.57 billion in the fourth quarter, then back to an operating loss of KRW 1.42 billion in the first quarter of 2026.

The loss narrowed to KRW 61.39 million in the second quarter of 2026 and widened to KRW 815 million in the third quarter. The company has recorded operating losses for three consecutive quarters from the first quarter through the third quarter of 2026.

Net profit attributable to controlling shareholders was KRW 689 million in the second quarter of 2026, but losses of KRW 1.2 billion in the first quarter and KRW 853 million in the third quarter show that the operating and bottom-line directions diverged in some quarters.

The sum of controlling-shareholder net profit over the last four quarters, from the fourth quarter of 2025 through the third quarter of 2026, was KRW 578 million. The question is how much of the backlog reported at KRW 31.7 billion as of the third quarter of 2025 converts into quarterly sales in 2026.

05

Industry analysis

Catis operates in a market where physical security for critical facilities such as airports and data centers intersects with spatial-awareness technology. Security projects carry a time lag between contract signing and revenue recognition, so order inflows reach sales in sequence.

The company said its order backlog as of the third quarter of 2025 was KRW 31.7 billion across 42 orders, up 19.2% and 27.3% respectively from the prior quarter. The data center physical security contract signed in May 2026 can be read as an example of demand linked to data center investment.

On the policy side, the company was selected for the Ministry of SMEs and Startups' Jump-Up program second cohort, with the stated aim of upgrading its PIDS platform.

In competition, the domestic IT services market mixes large system integrators such as Samsung SDS, LG CNS and Hyundai Autoever with specialized security firms, and Catis is far smaller than the large integrators.

Its business differs from Ahnlab, which centers on cybersecurity software, and because Catis's installation-based projects are tied to physical spaces, order changes can translate quickly into quarterly sales swings, as the fourth-quarter 2025 spike and the declines after the second quarter of 2026 show.

Quantum cryptography and Edge AI represent next-generation security directions, but the company has disclosed few figures on how large their contribution to earnings is, so the scale of the business shift remains hard to gauge.

Because the sector's results move with public and infrastructure procurement timing, a single large contract's recognition date can drive quarterly results.

Outlook

The company said its order backlog as of the third quarter of 2025 had reached roughly the level of annual sales and that major project orders were expected to keep rising in the second half, supporting annual growth. However, full-year 2025 sales fell 11.9% from the prior year, so expectations and results diverged.

With third-quarter 2026 sales down to KRW 2.94 billion, how much of the backlog converts into quarterly sales is the next checkpoint. The May 2026 contract runs from May 27, 2026 to December 30, 2027, so revenue recognition could be spread from the fourth quarter of 2026 through 2027.

The pace at which this contract is recognized will shape sales in the fourth quarter of 2026 and the first quarter of 2027. The Jump-Up program targets an upgrade of the PIDS platform, so whether that upgrade translates into order competitiveness is the item to watch.

At the March 2026 annual general meeting, the articles of incorporation added robot hardware and software development, manufacturing and sales, robot system integration and automation facility construction, and robot-related platform development to the business purpose.

The same meeting approved a share consolidation and a plan for disposing of treasury shares. The robot business has no reported figures yet, so the scale of the shift should be tracked through the business overview in the periodic report.

The effect of the share consolidation and treasury share disposal on the shareholder structure is also a point to follow.

Quarterly sales swung from KRW 9.37 billion in the fourth quarter of 2025 to KRW 2.43 billion in the first quarter of 2026, down 74.1%, before rebounding to KRW 6.92 billion in the second quarter. The swings suggest sales depend heavily on a small number of projects.

Earnings capacity and cost pressure

The company previously cited higher imported raw material costs as a factor, and if exchange-rate and cost pressure persist, margin recovery could be slow.

Risk factors

Conversion lag of orders

Because there is a lag between order backlog and recognized revenue, quarterly sales can move independently of order growth, as the 34.1% year-on-year decline in third-quarter 2026 sales shows. If the recognition timing of large contracts falls at quarter boundaries, quarterly results can skew in one direction.

The data center contract runs through December 30, 2027, so results could keep fluctuating depending on the recognition pace.

Costs and exchange rates

The company cited higher imported raw material costs from the sharp rise in the exchange rate as a main cause of earnings changes. The higher the share of imported security and communications equipment parts, the more directly exchange-rate moves affect margins.

On fixed-price contracts, passing cost increases to customers is difficult, which could add pressure on the operating margin.

Small market capitalization and ownership structure

Major shareholders and related parties hold 53.37% of common shares, which limits the free float. The small market capitalization can produce large share-price swings. The share consolidation approved at the March 2026 general meeting could change the share count and shareholder structure.

Overall view

Catis specializes in spatial-awareness security platforms. After swinging to an operating loss for full-year 2025, it has posted operating losses for three straight quarters from the first quarter through the third quarter of 2026.

A backlog of KRW 31.7 billion as of the third quarter of 2025 and the May 2026 data center contract worth KRW 3,158,630,000 support revenue visibility. On the other hand, quarterly sales have swung sharply, with third-quarter 2026 sales down 34.1% year on year.

On the financial side, the debt ratio fell to 62.7% and total equity at the end of 2025 was KRW 39 billion, but the earnings base is thin at KRW 578 million of controlling-shareholder net profit over the last four quarters.

The bullish factors are order accumulation and quarterly returns to profit, while the bearish factors are persistent operating losses and sales volatility.

The next checkpoints are the full-year operating margin in the 2026 annual report and the sales trend in the first quarter of 2027, and the conversion of the order backlog into quarterly sales is central to interpreting results.
