Report overview

Testing Whether Toebox Korea's Profit Recovery Holds

Toebox Korea's operating profit fell to KRW 4.06 million in 2025, returned to profit in the first two quarters of 2026, then swung to an operating loss in the third quarter, so whether the recovery holds is the key question.

  1. 1

    Operating profit for 2025 was KRW 4.06 million, down 99.7% from the prior year. The company attributed the drop to an inventory valuation allowance at a clothing subsidiary.

  2. 2

    Operating profit turned positive at KRW 834 million in Q1 2026 and rose to KRW 1.17 billion in Q2. The third quarter then posted an operating loss of KRW 593 million.

  3. 3

    Its largest shareholder changed to a children's furniture company in July 2026. The value-up plan approved by the board on September 30, 2026 targets KRW 50 billion in revenue and an operating margin of 10% or more by 2029.

  4. 4

    The debt ratio was 18.0% at the end of 2025, and total equity of KRW 31.8 billion is entirely attributable to controlling shareholders. Even so, net income attributable to controlling shareholders for the last four quarters totals only KRW 290 million.

02

Business structure

Toebox Korea plans, sources and distributes children's shoes and apparel. The company says it holds the No. 1 share of Korea's children's footwear market and its core model is importing proven premium brands for domestic distribution (Newspim, April 22, 2026).

Its sales base is roughly 80 department store and outlet locations nationwide, and online sales made up only about 16% in 2025 because offline distribution fees and store management costs are high (Edaily, August 7, 2026). Its product strategy centers on a 'Core 5 / Flex 5' structure built around its top 10 brands.

Core 5 includes in-house brands alongside Mini Melissa, while Flex 5 is described as a cash-cow group of global children's fashion footwear from brands such as adidas, Skechers, Hunter and Crocs (Seoul Economic Daily).

In the first half of 2026 the company said unit sales of the Melissa and Mini Melissa jelly shoe brands rose 38% year on year (Newspim, August 6, 2026). By segment, a year-on-year summary for the nine months to September 2024 showed footwear revenue up 9.4% while apparel revenue fell 35.3% (FnGuide summary).

In response, 2025 saw the merger of an apparel subsidiary into the parent and the closure of an overseas subsidiary (Newspim, March 13, 2026).

In July 2026 the largest shareholder changed to a children's furniture company, and the two sides are working to expand online sales through that group's e-commerce platform (Edaily, August 7, 2026).

The company also plans to extend its product range from infancy through childhood and integrate back-office functions such as purchasing, logistics and management to cut costs.

On competition, exclusive distribution rights for overseas brands and an expanding in-house brand lineup are cited as differentiators (Seoul Economic Daily).

04

Earnings analysis

Annual revenue fell from KRW 51.2 billion in 2022 to KRW 48.6 billion in 2023 (down 5.0%), KRW 44.7 billion in 2024 (down 8.2%) and KRW 39.7 billion in 2025 (down 11.1%). 2022 was the largest year and 2025 the smallest among 2022 to 2025.

Operating profit fell from KRW 4.99 billion in 2022 to KRW 2.95 billion in 2023 (down 40.9%), KRW 1.17 billion in 2024 (down 60.3%) and KRW 4.06 million in 2025 (down 99.7%), and operating margin declined from 9.7% to 6.1%, 2.6% and 0.0%.

For 2024, the company pointed to lower apparel sales and investment spending on new brands (Digital Today). In 2025, an inventory valuation allowance at an apparel subsidiary largely wiped out operating profit.

The company described it as an advance recognition of potential losses at overseas subsidiaries, a one-off accounting treatment that does not involve actual cash outflow (Newspim, March 13, 2026).

Net income attributable to controlling shareholders went from KRW 4.15 billion in 2022 and KRW 2.48 billion in 2023 (down 40.3%) to a net loss of KRW 576 million in 2024, and a net loss of KRW 1.25 billion in 2025, which widened the losses. Net losses in 2024 and 2025 make two consecutive loss years.

Operating cash flow turned from KRW -183 million in 2024 to KRW 590 million in 2025.

Quarterly results went from an operating loss of KRW 544 million in Q3 2025 and KRW 79.78 million in Q4 2025 to operating profit of KRW 834 million in Q1 2026, which the company described as its best result in three years (Newspim, April 22, 2026).

Q2 2026 operating profit was KRW 1.17 billion, up 40.1% from the prior quarter, with net income attributable to controlling shareholders of KRW 962 million. The company said first-half 2026 operating profit reached KRW 2 billion, up 100% year on year (Newspim, August 6, 2026).

Yet Q3 2026 revenue of KRW 8.61 billion rose 14.7% year on year while the quarter posted an operating loss of KRW 593 million, and that loss widened from the prior year.

The factors behind the Q3 loss are hard to separate from the disclosed figures, so cost of sales and selling and administrative expenses in the next periodic report become the point to check.

05

Industry analysis

On reported results, revenue has declined for three consecutive years from 2023 through 2025, so any industry recovery is closely tied to the company's own restructuring of its distribution.

Seasonality is pronounced: spring sales rise, and family events after the April and May school term drive gift demand, while the first quarter is usually slow. Even so, the company said its Q1 2026 operating profit was its highest in three years (Newspim, April 22, 2026).

It also said it recorded its highest-ever revenue during the May 2026 Golden Week and Children's Day holidays (Newspim, August 6, 2026). On the external side, rising visits by foreign tourists to Korea have lifted department store and other fashion retail sales, which was cited as a positive (Newspim, August 6, 2026).

Online channels are presented as a growth axis through IP-based e-commerce partnerships, but online's share remains low. On listing rules, regulators have tightened KOSDAQ delisting criteria, and the company is pursuing capital raising, buybacks and a business restructuring at the same time (Edaily, August 7, 2026).

Against competitors, the company stresses its high share of the domestic children's footwear market and exclusive overseas brand distribution. The share figure rests on company statements, so it should be read alongside outside estimates.

06

Outlook

In the value-up plan approved by the board on September 30, 2026, the company targets KRW 50 billion in revenue and an operating margin of 10% or more by 2029.

The plan also covers raising inventory turnover from 1.3 times to 1.5 times, lifting online's share of sales from 16.5% to 25%, and cutting financial assets to 20% or less of total assets by 2029 (Digital Today and Nate News).

On shareholder returns, the company set a target of returning 15% or more of average net income over three years and said it is considering introducing cash dividends after earnings stabilize (Bloter). A recovery of PBR to 1.0x or higher is listed as a market-valuation goal (Digital Today).

One news report judged the 10% margin target as somewhat conservative, since first-half 2026 margin had already reached the high 8% range (Nate News, October 2026). The company expects growth to continue in the second half of 2026 and cites synergies from the new largest shareholder's group (Newspim, August 6, 2026).

It also plans to expand overseas sales through a listing on Amazon (Newspim, April 22, 2026). Under a KRW 2.4 billion share buyback trust signed with Samsung Securities, shares will be repurchased through April 2027, and the company has yet to decide how to dispose of them (Bloter).

The stake held by the largest shareholder carries both call and put options, so transfers of the founder's remaining stake could follow once results are fixed under the contract (Nate News). The next checks are the 2026 fourth-quarter results and progress on the value-up plan.

08

Bull factors

First-half profit recovery

The company said first-half 2026 operating profit reached KRW 2 billion, up 100% year on year. Q1 operating profit turned positive at KRW 834 million and rose to KRW 1.17 billion in Q2. Press reports put first-half operating margin at 8.7%, up from 4.6% a year earlier. Compared with the KRW 4.06 million operating profit of 2025, the direction has clearly turned.

Change of largest shareholder and channel overhaul

In July 2026 the largest shareholder became a children's furniture company with a 27.74% stake. Using that group's e-commerce capabilities, the company aims to lift online's share of sales from 16.5% to 25% and raise inventory turnover from 1.3 times to 1.5 times. The move reads as an attempt to change the cost structure of an offline-heavy distribution model.

Financial capacity and shareholder return measures

At end-2025 the debt ratio was 18.0%, total liabilities were KRW 5.73 billion and total equity was KRW 31.8 billion. In August 2026 the company moved KRW 10 billion from capital reserves to retained earnings to create room for dividends (Bloter). A KRW 2.4 billion buyback trust runs through April 2027. Operating cash flow also returned to positive at KRW 590 million in 2025.

09

Bear factors

Return to losses in the third quarter

Q3 2026 posted an operating loss of KRW 593 million, widening from the prior year. Revenue for the quarter rose 14.7% year on year to KRW 8.61 billion, but the growth did not carry through to profit. Net loss attributable to controlling shareholders was KRW 442 million.

Because the second-quarter profit trend reversed within a single quarter, the durability of the recovery is still unproven.

Long-term margin decline and a thin earnings base

Operating margin fell from 9.7% in 2022 to 6.1% in 2023, 2.6% in 2024 and 0.0% in 2025. Net income attributable to controlling shareholders over the last four quarters totals only KRW 290 million. The 2025 net loss attributable to controlling shareholders widened to KRW 1.25 billion. With a small earnings base, profit can swing sharply on a single quarter's cost changes.

Offline dependence and burden from apparel

The company depends on about 80 department store and outlet locations, and online accounted for roughly 16% of sales in 2025. High distribution fees and store costs have been cited as drags on profitability (Edaily).

In the nine months to September 2024, apparel revenue fell 35.3%, and in 2025 an inventory allowance at an apparel subsidiary largely erased operating profit.

10

Risk factors

Inventory and provisioning risk

Apparel is produced ahead of each season and booked as inventory, so any drop in value flows through cost of sales as a valuation loss. In 2025 an inventory allowance at an apparel subsidiary cut operating profit to KRW 4.06 million.

The company targets inventory turnover of 1.5 times, but whether long-dated stock is reduced as planned will need to be checked in the next periodic report.

Governance and change of largest shareholder

After the largest shareholder changed in July 2026, the chief executive was replaced in August. The largest shareholder's stake carries both call and put options. The founder, Lee Seon-geun, still holds about 5.59%, and a full exit remains possible (Nate News). Changes in control could affect continuity in business strategy and shareholder return policy.

Listing-maintenance regulation

KOSDAQ designates a company as administrative-issue when its market capitalization stays below KRW 20 billion for 30 consecutive trading days, and delisting can follow if recovery requirements are not met (Nate News).

With delisting standards tightened, the company is pursuing capital raising and buybacks at the same time (Edaily). Market capitalization sits near that threshold, so both regulatory changes and share price moves bear on listing status.

12

Overall view

Toebox Korea's operating profit fell to KRW 4.06 million in 2025 and then returned to profit in the first half of 2026. Q1 operating profit of KRW 834 million and Q2 operating profit of KRW 1.17 billion underpin this, and the company said first-half operating profit rose 100% year on year.

However, Q3 2026 swung back to an operating loss of KRW 593 million, so the durability of the recovery has not yet been tested. Revenue fell for three consecutive years from KRW 51.2 billion in 2022 to KRW 39.7 billion in 2025, and operating margin declined to 0.0%.

Positives include the change of largest shareholder in July 2026 and plans to integrate online channels and back-office functions, a debt ratio of 18.0%, and shareholder return measures such as buybacks and a capital reserve transfer.

Negatives include a thin earnings base with only KRW 290 million of net income attributable to controlling shareholders over the last four quarters, dependence on offline stores, and inventory valuation risk.

The 2029 targets of KRW 50 billion in revenue and an operating margin of 10% or more remain some distance from current results. The next checkpoints are the 2026 preliminary full-year results and the 2026 annual business report.
