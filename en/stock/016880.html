Holding Company's Earnings Shift After Funeral Unit Deal; Debt Remains a Test

Operating profit rose 151.3% to KRW 78.1 billion in 2025 and attributable net income turned to KRW 120.8 billion, but acquisition debt, a 1,491.1% debt-to-equity ratio and the education unit's profitability remain open questions.

02

Business structure

Woongjin was founded in 1983 to manufacture and sell books and educational publications and moved to a holding company structure in 2007 after a spin-off of its investment and education-culture business.

In 2025 it acquired the pre-need funeral business and renamed the unit, bringing a life-care business into the group. The Korea Fair Trade Commission confirmed that Woongjin met the holding company requirements as of December 31, 2025, with the conversion effective January 1, 2026.

Direct subsidiaries include Woongjin Thinkbig (59.73%), Woongjin Play City (80.26%), a golf club operator (66.67%), Woongjin Hucom (50.82%), Woongjin Everskyy (75.63%) and WJ Life Holdings (100%). The funeral business sits as a third-tier subsidiary under WJ Life Holdings and WJ Life.

As of the first three quarters of 2025, Woongjin Thinkbig accounted for KRW 434.8 billion, or 51.9%, of group revenue. In 2025 the funeral unit's revenue rose 12% to KRW 309.5 billion, while funeral-service revenue rose only 9.7% to KRW 170.4 billion.

The remainder came from financial investment, travel and education products tied to pre-need contracts. In the pre-need industry, rankings are set by the size of customer prepayments, and the unit held KRW 2.622 trillion at the third quarter of 2025. The group therefore combines a shrinking education franchise with a prepayment-funded funeral business.

04

Earnings analysis

Revenue on a disclosed basis fell from KRW 1.05 trillion in 2022 to KRW 1.02 trillion in 2023 (down 3.0%) and KRW 1.01 trillion in 2024 (down 1.0%), before rebounding to KRW 1.15 trillion in 2025 (up 14.1%).

Operating profit bottomed at KRW 42.3 billion in 2022 and KRW 22.4 billion in 2023 (down 47.2%) before rising to KRW 31.1 billion in 2024 and KRW 78.1 billion in 2025 (up 151.3%). The company attributed the 2025 gains to newly consolidated subsidiaries and recognition of fair-value gains in non-operating income.

The operating margin moved from 4.0% in 2022 to 2.2% in 2023, 3.1% in 2024 and 6.8% in 2025. Attributable net income swung from a KRW 8.22 billion profit in 2022 to a KRW 24.8 billion loss in 2023, narrowed to a KRW 5.18 billion loss in 2024 and turned to a KRW 120.8 billion profit in 2025.

Net income including non-controlling interests was KRW 114.4 billion in 2025, a different figure. In the third quarter of 2025 revenue reached KRW 325.2 billion and operating profit KRW 38.2 billion, which Yonhap-style coverage described as the highest of the last three years.

Operating profit then fell to KRW 23.2 billion in the fourth quarter. In the first quarter of 2026 operating profit was KRW 31.7 billion, a turn to profit, with the funeral unit's operating profit of roughly KRW 33.4 billion reported as the main driver while most other affiliates posted losses.

Operating profit rose to KRW 53.6 billion in the second quarter of 2026 and fell to KRW 33 billion in the third quarter, a 13.6% decline year on year. Cumulative operating profit through the third quarter of 2026 was KRW 118.3 billion.

Attributable net income was KRW 19.5 billion in the fourth quarter of 2025, KRW 7.41 billion in the first quarter of 2026 and KRW 11.8 billion in the second quarter, before a KRW 2.58 billion attributable net loss in the third quarter.

Total equity at end-2025 was KRW 336.1 billion, of which KRW 177.9 billion was attributable to shareholders, against total liabilities of KRW 5.01 trillion. Operating cash flow rose to KRW 261.6 billion in 2025 from a range of KRW 89.9 billion to KRW 112.4 billion in 2022 to 2024.

05

Industry analysis

Woongjin's earnings span two end markets: pre-need funerals and education. In funerals, shifting ceremony customs and a shrinking population are cited as operating variables, and industry estimates put no-wake funerals at around 15% of the market.

In education, falling school-age population and weak consumer spending were cited in 2025 coverage as pressure on efficiency.

Woongjin Thinkbig recorded an operating loss of KRW 10.5 billion in 2025, and in the third quarter of 2026 it returned to operating profit of KRW 4.1 billion on a preliminary basis, although cumulative revenue through September 2026 fell 8.4%.

In the pre-need market, rankings are set by customer prepayment balances, which is the basis on which Woongjin's funeral unit is compared with peers. The group returned to the Korea Fair Trade Commission's designated conglomerate list in 2026 for the first time since selling Coway in 2013.

Compared with holding peers such as SK Square and Meritz Financial Group, Woongjin's portfolio leans more toward unlisted lifestyle-service assets.

06

Outlook

Management said the holding structure will focus on how to create group synergies and differentiate its pre-need products. Day-to-day fund management sits with in-house professional executives. Market coverage expects a focus on expanding new businesses using the funeral unit's prepayment balances.

The funeral unit's prepayments passed KRW 3 trillion and it acquired a professional funeral hall and a premium wedding hall. Management has also indicated it will speed up efforts to upgrade the business structure after returning to the conglomerate list.

On governance, the company disclosed that it will resolve violations within two years of the January 1, 2026 conversion date, and the governance core-indicator compliance rate was reported at 33.3%. The largest shareholder increased its stake to 17.02% in March 2026.

Items to confirm in the 2026 annual report are whether holding-level operating profit holds at third-quarter levels, whether acquisition financing and interest costs decline, whether prepayments keep growing, and whether education affiliates sustain their profit recovery.

Woongjin Thinkbig's third-quarter 2026 turn to profit is a single quarter and should be checked against full-year results.

08

Bull factors

Operating profit base has shifted

Operating profit rose from KRW 22.4 billion in 2023 to KRW 78.1 billion in 2025, with a 6.8% margin in 2025, the highest of 2022 to 2025. The consolidation of the funeral unit broadened the earnings base behind this shift.

Prepayment-funded funeral business

Pre-need rankings are set by prepayment balances, and the funeral unit held KRW 2.622 trillion at the third quarter of 2025. Coverage reports that cumulative prepayments later passed KRW 3 trillion. Expansion into new businesses funded by these balances is cited as a growth axis for the group.

Holding conversion and return to conglomerate status

The holding structure took effect January 1, 2026, with six direct subsidiaries and lower-tier affiliates under one governance framework. Assets above KRW 5 trillion returned the group to the disclosure-designated conglomerate list. The group now has a structure to adjust funding and business mix at the holding level.

Bear factors

Acquisition financing and interest burden

Acquisition debt stood at KRW 834.5 billion at the end of the third quarter of 2025, and cumulative interest expense of KRW 36.4 billion was roughly double the prior-year level. The interest coverage ratio was 1.5 times against 1.7 times a year earlier. The debt-to-equity ratio rose to 1,491.1% at end-2025 from 414.0% in 2024.

Declining core education revenue

Woongjin Thinkbig posted a 2025 operating loss of KRW 10.5 billion, and cumulative revenue through September 2026 fell 8.4%. Net sales in the publishing and distribution segment also declined in the third quarter of 2025. The 2026 third-quarter profit is a single-quarter result and does not yet confirm a core business recovery.

Earnings quality and quarterly volatility

Net income gains in 2025 included fair-value gains in non-operating income tied to consolidation. Operating profit fell 38.4% quarter on quarter in the third quarter of 2026 and attributable results turned to a loss.

Attributable net income over the last four quarters totaled KRW 36.1 billion, well below the full-year 2025 figure.

10

Risk factors

Financing and balance-sheet risk

Acquisition debt and interest costs are large relative to earnings. Persistently high rates or lower returns on prepayment balances would weigh on holding-level profit. With the debt-to-equity ratio at 1,491.1% in 2025, maturity and rate terms of the borrowings need monitoring.

Governance and regulatory risk

The company disclosed it will resolve Fair Trade Act violations within two years of conversion. The governance core-indicator compliance rate was reported at 33.3%. Expanded disclosure duties and internal-transaction rules could add near-term costs.

Demand-structure risk

Smaller ceremonies and a shrinking population could limit long-term demand for pre-need funerals. Education faces a structural decline in school-age population. In both businesses, demand declines could reach reported earnings with a lag.

Overall view

Woongjin's operating profit rebounded from its 2023 low to KRW 78.1 billion in 2025 after the funeral unit was consolidated, and the operating margin rose to 6.8%. Cumulative operating profit through the third quarter of 2026 widened to KRW 118.3 billion.

However, attributable net income was partly shaped by fair-value gains in 2025, and the last four quarters total KRW 36.1 billion. The third quarter of 2026 swung to an attributable net loss, showing quarterly volatility.

On the balance sheet, total liabilities of KRW 5.01 trillion and a debt-to-equity ratio of 1,491.1% remain the largest burden. Cumulative interest expense of KRW 36.4 billion through the third quarter of 2025 illustrates that cost.

The education affiliate Woongjin Thinkbig returned to a quarterly profit in the third quarter of 2026, although cumulative revenue is declining. On governance, violations must be resolved within two years of conversion.

The next checkpoints are whether the 2026 annual report keeps full-year profitability and how the debt structure changes.
