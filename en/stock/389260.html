02

Business structure

Dae Myoung Energy was founded in 2000 as a renewable energy specialist, listed on KOSDAQ in 2022, and has 16 unlisted subsidiaries. The business has three segments: EPC (design, procurement and construction), power generation, and O&M (operations and maintenance).

Based on 2024 revenue, EPC accounted for 53.55%, power generation 36.21%, O&M 5.74%, and other 4.50%. Power generation and O&M together were 42% of 2024 revenue, which a domestic brokerage analyst viewed as more than double the share of domestic peers.

The same analysis estimated that construction on the company's own projects earns a margin more than double that of typical externally commissioned EPC work.

Its generation assets are mainly onshore wind, with eight sites totaling 278 MW in operation, and the company says it holds the top share of Korea's private wind power market. In offshore wind, it is pursuing about 1.7 GW of development, and it joined CS Wind and others in the Anma offshore wind consortium.

In energy storage, it completed the Jeju Bukchon long-duration BESS and in April 2026 won two Honam BESS projects totaling KRW 67 billion. One press report put 2025 EPC revenue in the annual business report at about KRW 99.1 billion.

In 2022, its share of domestic wind installed capacity was 8.4%, ranking third among domestic operators and first among private power generators. As of August 21, 2026, the largest shareholder and related parties held 71.76%, leaving a free float ratio of 28.21%.

04

Earnings analysis

Annual revenue fell from KRW 88 billion in 2022 to KRW 61.6 billion in 2023, down 29.9%, then rose to KRW 67.8 billion in 2024, up 9.9%, and to KRW 131 billion in 2025, up 93.3%. Revenue increased for two consecutive years after the 2023 low, from 2024 through 2025, and 2025 was the largest year of 2022 to 2025.

One press analysis credited EPC work with most of the 2025 growth. Operating profit went from KRW 25.6 billion in 2022 to KRW 16.7 billion in 2023, down 34.6%, and KRW 9.71 billion in 2024, down 42.0%, bottoming in 2024 before recovering to KRW 16.8 billion in 2025, up 73.1%.

Profit attributable to owners of the parent went from KRW 26.1 billion in 2022 to KRW 6.14 billion in 2023, down 76.5%, then rose to KRW 7.79 billion in 2024, up 26.8%, and to KRW 14.1 billion in 2025, up 81.6%.

Net profit including non-controlling interests was KRW 26.8 billion in 2022 and KRW 14.1 billion in 2025, a different measure from profit attributable to owners of the parent. The operating margin fell from 29.1% in 2022 to 27.2% in 2023, 14.3% in 2024 and 12.8% in 2025.

The debt ratio improved from 187.3% in 2024 to 131.9% in 2025, with total equity of KRW 164.7 billion and total liabilities of KRW 217.1 billion at the end of 2025. Operating cash flow was KRW 11.6 billion in 2022, KRW 2.57 billion in 2023, KRW 28.1 billion in 2024 and KRW 1.4 billion in 2025.

Quarterly results started in the third quarter of 2025 with revenue of KRW 37 billion, operating profit of KRW 240 million and a net loss attributable to owners of the parent of KRW 1.92 billion.

Revenue then reached KRW 52.8 billion in the fourth quarter, with operating profit of KRW 7.89 billion and profit attributable to owners of the parent of KRW 10.9 billion, a swing to profit.

First-quarter 2026 revenue was KRW 30.8 billion with operating profit of KRW 17.9 billion, and securities data sites attributed the year-on-year improvement to a larger construction contract base and higher power and REC sales.

Second-quarter 2026 revenue was KRW 25.3 billion with operating profit of KRW 3.4 billion, and third-quarter revenue was KRW 54 billion with operating profit of KRW 5.12 billion. Revenue timing for development assets depends on permitting and financing schedules, which helps explain the quarter-to-quarter swings.

Profit attributable to owners of the parent in the second quarter of 2026 was KRW 16.5 billion, well above operating profit, and the reason should be checked against the notes to the periodic report. Profit attributable to owners of the parent over the last four quarters totaled KRW 34.2 billion.

05

Industry analysis

The government plans to add about 2.22 GW of energy storage by 2029 under the 11th Basic Plan for Electricity Supply and Demand. Industry analysis notes that the central ESS contract market weighs track record heavily, which favors companies with early reference projects.

The company has been described as a leading EPC contractor in this market after winning two Honam projects on the back of its Jeju Bukchon BESS work. The company presents AI-driven growth in electricity demand as a reason to expand energy storage.

In onshore wind, the company said the national average utilization rate is about 14.5%, while the regional averages of sites it developed reach 20% to 30%.

The domestic wind competitive ranking cited in press reports is based on 2022 installed capacity, so the latest standings should be checked against current industry statistics.

In offshore wind, the 532 MW Anma project was awarded in a competitive bid in December 2024, and additional projects of 400 MW and 800 MW are in development. A company official said it is preparing to enter offshore wind O&M and virtual power plant businesses, building on onshore experience.

Recurring power generation and O&M revenue tends to favor developers that build a larger share of their own projects.

06

Outlook

The Gokseong Green Wind (42 MW) construction EPC contract was signed in April 2026 for KRW 136 billion, with a contract period running to March 1, 2029.

Its EPC revenue is scheduled to be recognized in stages from 2026 through 2028, and after commercial operation starts in 2028, power generation and O&M revenue is expected to accumulate over 20 years.

Because this is a self-developed project, its operating margin is estimated at more than double that of externally commissioned EPC work. The two Honam BESS projects, Goheung Naro and Gwangyang Hwangeum, are being built with BS Hanyang in a consortium, with both sites targeting completion in March 2027.

Additional ESS bids were expected to proceed in sequence from the second half of 2026, and the government's plan for about 2.22 GW of storage is cited as a source of further orders. The 26 MW Gimcheon wind project was planned for completion and operation by 2026.

The Jeju Bukchon BESS has completed construction and commercial operation. For offshore wind, a 2025 report said the Anma project has a five-year construction period with commercial operation targeted for the first half of 2029.

In the next periodic report, the scale of Gokseong EPC revenue recognized and the trend in the operating margin will be the points to check. If the Honam BESS sites are completed at the end of March 2027, the pace of revenue recognition after 2027 could change.

Because quarterly revenue moves with permitting and financing schedules, whether order size translates directly into quarterly profit should be checked in the quarterly reports.

Bull factors

Large Order Backlog

The KRW 136 billion Gokseong Green Wind EPC contract is set to be recognized in stages from 2026 through 2028. Adding the KRW 67 billion Honam BESS projects builds a base for revenue growth in 2026 and 2027, and third-quarter 2026 revenue rose 46.1% year on year to KRW 54 billion.

Own-Project Revenue Model

Power generation and O&M made up 42% of 2024 revenue, and construction on the company's own projects is estimated to earn a higher margin than externally commissioned EPC work. Once plants are built, generation and O&M revenue accumulate over 20 years, so income continues after EPC revenue.

Profit attributable to owners of the parent has been positive in every quarter from the fourth quarter of 2025 through the third quarter of 2026.

Stronger Balance Sheet

The debt ratio fell from 187.3% in 2024 to 131.9% in 2025, with total equity of KRW 164.7 billion at the end of 2025. Operating profit rose 73.1% to KRW 16.8 billion in 2025, and profit attributable to owners of the parent rose 81.6% to KRW 14.1 billion. Whether quarterly net profit continues is the next point for gauging any further decline in the debt ratio.

09

Bear factors

Margin Erosion

The operating margin fell from 29.1% in 2022 to 12.8% in 2025. One press analysis noted that margins declined every year and that lower-margin EPC construction carried most of the growth. Operating profit bottomed at KRW 9.71 billion in 2024, which reflects the same trend.

Quarterly Volatility

Operating profit fell from KRW 17.9 billion in the first quarter of 2026 to KRW 3.4 billion in the second quarter, and was KRW 5.12 billion in the third quarter. Revenue timing for development assets depends on permitting and financing schedules.

In the second quarter, profit attributable to owners of the parent of KRW 16.5 billion far exceeded operating profit, so the earnings structure needs to be checked in the periodic report notes.

Weaker Operating Cash Flow

Operating cash flow fell from KRW 28.1 billion in 2024 to KRW 1.4 billion in 2025. Cash generation did not follow the rise in operating profit that year.

Total liabilities of KRW 217.1 billion at the end of 2025 exceeded total equity of KRW 164.7 billion, so working capital and borrowing costs remain variables in reading earnings.

Risk factors

Bidding and Policy Risk

The central ESS contract market weighs track record heavily, so the outcome of additional bids can shape the order flow. If government storage plans or bid schedules change, the timing of the order pipeline could slip. Wind permitting timelines are also tied to the policy environment, which can affect project pace.

Construction and Permitting Timing Risk

Revenue timing for development assets depends on permitting and financing schedules. If construction on the Gokseong project runs late, revenue planned for 2026 through 2028 could shift. Reports give different stakes in the Anma offshore wind project, 11.7% and 12.7%, so the periodic report should be checked.

Shareholding and Liquidity

As of August 21, 2026, the largest shareholder and related parties held 71.76%, and the free float ratio was 28.21%. Foreign ownership was 1.13%, so the number of shares available for trading is limited. In this structure, changes in supply and demand can widen the swings in the share price.

12

Overall view

Dae Myoung Energy's 2025 revenue of KRW 131 billion and operating profit of KRW 16.8 billion both rose, and profit attributable to owners of the parent has been positive in every quarter from the fourth quarter of 2025 through the third quarter of 2026.

However, the operating margin fell from 29.1% in 2022 to 12.8% in 2025, and quarterly operating profit swung from KRW 17.9 billion in the first quarter of 2026 to KRW 3.4 billion in the second and KRW 5.12 billion in the third.

On orders, the KRW 136 billion Gokseong EPC contract and the KRW 67 billion Honam BESS projects are described as the base for revenue from 2026 through 2028.

Strengths include a large order backlog, a recurring generation and O&M model built on own projects, and a lower debt ratio, while weaknesses include the margin decline, quarterly volatility, and operating cash flow of KRW 1.4 billion in 2025.

On valuation, quarterly earnings swings widen the range of multiple interpretation and dividends offer little basis for comparison, so the key reference is whether the operating margin recovers.

The next checkpoints are the 2026 annual business report around March 2027, completion of the Honam BESS projects at the end of March 2027, and commercial operation of Gokseong in 2028. This report is for information only and does not include a buy or sell opinion.
