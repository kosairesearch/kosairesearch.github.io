Power-grid demand lifts LS's operating profit

02

Business structure

LS is a holding company that owns stakes in group affiliates and manages the group. Its consolidated revenue and operating profit are built from the results of its power-infrastructure subsidiaries.

The core subsidiaries are LS's cable unit, which supplies submarine and underground cable and busway, LS Electric, which makes switchgear, transformers and high-voltage GIS, and LS MnM, which runs copper smelting.

LS MnM was reported to produce about 680,000 tons of electrolytic copper a year at its Ulsan Onsan smelter (E-Daily, first half of 2026).

The group's ability to supply everything from copper production to transmission, substation and distribution equipment within one group is cited as a basis for large order wins (Nate News, August 2026).

Subsidiary revenue for January to September 2026 was reported at KRW 7.19 trillion for the cable unit, KRW 4.83 trillion for LS Electric and KRW 15.12 trillion for LS MnM (Maeil Business, November 16, 2026).

LS Electric's own third-quarter 2026 materials show sales of KRW 1.88 trillion and switchgear sales of KRW 541.6 billion, up 120% year on year.

As of the end of the second quarter of 2026, its backlog was largest in extra-high-voltage transformers, followed by switchgear, and the backlog reached KRW 7.04 trillion at the end of the third quarter of 2026.

Its North American production sites are the Bastrop campus in Texas and the LS Electric Utah plant (Nate News, August 2026). Because LS is a holding company, its consolidated results depend on subsidiary earnings, ownership stakes and the split of profit with non-controlling interests.

04

Earnings analysis

Annual revenue rose from KRW 17.5 trillion in 2022 to KRW 31.9 trillion in 2025. Revenue has increased for three consecutive years from 2023 through 2025, and 2025 revenue rose 15.7% from the prior year. Operating profit was largest in 2024 at KRW 1.07 trillion and fell 1.9% to KRW 1.05 trillion in 2025.

Over the same period the operating margin declined from 3.9% in 2024 to 3.3% in 2025, so 2025 was a year of higher revenue but thinner margins. Profit attributable to controlling shareholders fell from KRW 451.6 billion in 2022 to KRW 237.3 billion in 2024, then rose 14.1% to KRW 270.8 billion in 2025.

In 2024 operating profit rose 19.3% while controlling-shareholder profit fell 45.6%, so the two measures did not move in the same direction. Allocation of profit to non-controlling interests and non-operating items in the consolidated structure may explain part of this gap.

Net income including non-controlling interests was KRW 485.1 billion in 2025, above the controlling-shareholder figure. The debt ratio was 202.7% in 2022, 170.3% in 2023, 198.3% in 2024 and 225.5% in 2025, and at the end of 2025 total liabilities were KRW 17.3 trillion against total equity of KRW 7.68 trillion.

Operating cash flow fell from KRW 858.1 billion in 2024 to KRW 206.3 billion in 2025.

Quarterly operating profit was KRW 257.1 billion in the third quarter of 2025 and KRW 255.3 billion in the fourth quarter, then rose to KRW 476.1 billion in the first quarter of 2026, up 86.5% from the previous quarter, and KRW 595.6 billion in the second quarter, up 25.1%.

Second-quarter revenue of KRW 11 trillion was reported as the highest quarterly figure on record (Nate News, August 2026). Third-quarter 2026 operating profit of KRW 488.3 billion fell 18.0% from the previous quarter but rose 89.9% from a year earlier.

Profit attributable to controlling shareholders was KRW 185.5 billion in the same quarter, keeping the recovery trend going. Over the last four quarters, from the fourth quarter of 2025 through the third quarter of 2026, controlling-shareholder profit totaled KRW 547.9 billion.

05

Industry analysis

Demand for power equipment is growing as AI data-center investment expands and aging grids in North America and Europe are replaced at the same time (Nate News, August 2026).

In an April 2026 report, LS Securities said that the shortage of extra-high-voltage transformers in the United States is continuing and that medium- and low-voltage distribution has also entered an upswing. This is a brokerage view, and reports differ on how long the cycle will last.

On copper, LS MnM's results improved on higher copper and sulfuric acid prices despite weaker smelting fees (Hankookilbo, November 16, 2026). Exports are also gaining weight: LS Electric's export share was 53% in the second quarter of 2026, up from 51% in the prior quarter (Digital Daily, July 23, 2026).

Competitively, LS's strength lies in combining cable, transformers, distribution equipment and copper smelting in one value chain. The flip side is that demand for power equipment leans heavily on capital spending by a small number of large customers, which can make earnings sensitive if that spending slows. Expanding local production in the United States also bears on how LS handles tariffs and logistics.

06

Outlook

The company has said it will keep growing on the back of its order backlog and will continue expanding subsea cable, busway and distribution capacity at domestic and overseas sites (E-Daily, November 16, 2026).

LS Electric set a 2026 new-order guidance of KRW 4.09 trillion, and first-quarter new orders of KRW 1.09 trillion were reported to equal about 26.7% of that guidance (Daily Invest, reported in the first half of 2026).

New orders were about KRW 2.1 trillion in the second quarter and KRW 1.59 trillion in the third quarter of 2026, according to company releases and its third-quarter results material. Adding the quarterly figures puts the total above the annual guidance level.

Backlog figures differ by source, however: a November 16, 2026 report put LS's cable unit at about KRW 10 trillion and LS Electric at over KRW 9 trillion, while LS Electric's third-quarter material shows a backlog of KRW 7.04 trillion.

On capacity, LS Electric's Busan plant was reported to have raised annual extra-high-voltage transformer capacity from KRW 200 billion to KRW 600 billion (E-Daily, first half of 2026). North American local production is also being expanded.

Next checkpoints are how the annual order and backlog figures are defined in the 2026 annual report, and how the expansion spending shows up in the debt ratio and operating cash flow.

08

Bull factors

Revenue visibility from the order backlog

The combined order backlog of LS's cable and electrical-equipment subsidiaries was reported at KRW 19.6 trillion at the end of the first half of 2026 (Nate News, August 2026). It is attributed to AI data-center investment and grid replacement in North America and Europe. The bullish case is that a larger backlog improves visibility on revenue in later quarters.

Recovery in operating profit

Operating profit rose 86.5% from the previous quarter to KRW 476.1 billion in the first quarter of 2026, and 25.1% to KRW 595.6 billion in the second quarter. Second-quarter revenue and operating profit were both reported as the highest quarterly figures on record (Nate News, August 2026). Third-quarter operating profit of KRW 488.3 billion was up 89.9% year on year, so the recovery continued.

Expanded North American capacity

LS Electric was reported to have raised annual extra-high-voltage transformer capacity at its Busan plant from KRW 200 billion to KRW 600 billion (E-Daily, first half of 2026). Its U.S. production centers in Texas and Utah make medium- and low-voltage equipment and distribution systems. Bulls point to closer local production as a way to shorten equipment delivery periods.

09

Bear factors

A small share reaching controlling shareholders

Operating profit in 2025 was KRW 1.05 trillion, while profit attributable to controlling shareholders was KRW 270.8 billion. In the third quarter of 2026, controlling-shareholder profit of KRW 185.5 billion compared with operating profit of KRW 488.3 billion. How much of the operating recovery reaches controlling shareholders is the main bearish question.

Balance-sheet burden and slower cash flow

The debt ratio at the end of 2025 was 225.5%, the highest of the four years, and total liabilities were KRW 17.3 trillion. With operating cash flow lower, borrowing needs could rise as expansion and overseas investment grow, which is the bearish argument.

Thinner margins and quarterly volatility

The operating margin fell from 3.9% in 2024 to 3.3% in 2025. Third-quarter 2026 operating profit fell 18.0% from the previous quarter, and controlling-shareholder profit in the fourth quarter of 2025 was KRW 18.2 billion, down 72.3% from the prior quarter.

Differences in subsidiary profitability and copper-linked swings in smelting results also move quarterly earnings.

10

Risk factors

Demand cycle and customer concentration

Power-equipment demand relies heavily on AI data-center spending and grid replacement. If the pace of capital spending by large technology customers changes, order flow and the timing of revenue recognition could both shift. Even a large backlog can see earnings timing vary with delivery schedules and customer plans.

Raw-material prices and currency

LS MnM's results are affected by copper and sulfuric acid prices. Metal price moves can drive results even when smelting fees are weak. External variables such as a weaker won against the dollar can also affect profit as the export share grows.

Expansion spending and financial structure

Expansion is proceeding at domestic sites, including a Cheongju plant and supplier capacity, alongside investment in U.S. plants. Expansion spending can strain short-term cash flow and borrowing levels.

With the debt ratio at 225.5%, the speed at which the expansion turns into converted orders and revenue is a key point to track.

  4. Around May 2027 (expected)

12

Overall view

LS's operating profit rose sharply through the third quarter of 2026 on order growth at its cable and electrical-equipment subsidiaries. Operating profit in the third quarter was KRW 488.3 billion, up 89.9% year on year.

However, profit attributable to controlling shareholders was KRW 270.8 billion for 2025 against annual operating profit of KRW 1.05 trillion, so how much of the operating recovery reaches the holding company is the central question.

The debt ratio at the end of 2025 was 225.5%, the highest of the four years, and operating cash flow declined in 2025. Reported combined subsidiary backlog of KRW 19.6 trillion at mid-2026 supports revenue visibility. Results could still swing with copper prices and the pace of capital spending by large customers.

The next checkpoints are the financial structure in the 2026 annual report and the speed of backlog conversion in first-quarter 2027 results.
