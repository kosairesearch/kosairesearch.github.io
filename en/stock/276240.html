02

Business structure

Elivision is a digital signage specialist founded in January 2004 that changed its corporate name to Elivision Co., Ltd. in 2008 and began manufacturing and selling in earnest. The company listed on KONEX in July 2018 and focuses on kiosks and digital information displays.

According to a Hi Investment & Securities report published in September 2022, the 2021 sales of 4.08 billion KRW were made up of interaction kiosks at 2.41 billion KRW (about 59%), intelligent digital signage at 1.28 billion KRW (about 31%), and maintenance at 390 million KRW (about 10%).

Because kiosks are the largest segment, the revenue structure leans heavily on terminal sales. Elivision holds a patent for automatic height adjustment that adapts the device to the user's physical conditions, and it presents this as a core differentiator of its barrier-free kiosks.

In 2019 it widened its portfolio to include a salon smart mirror and an unmanned convenience store system, and its company press list shows that it began supplying dedicated kiosks for people with disabilities in 2024.

On competition, analysis cited in the press points to intensifying rivalry in digital signage that has limited growth in the core business. In August 2026 the company agreed to a share exchange with robotics and drone firm Robowalks to bring it in as a 100% subsidiary.

Robowalks is described as supplying AMRs, collaborative robots, exploration robots and drones, and as having a nationwide after-sales service network. On that basis, Elivision plans to expand into smart factories and public, safety and defense markets (B2G).

03

Recent trends

At the annual general meeting held on March 31, 2026, the approval of the 22nd fiscal year financial statements was passed. Based on the standalone statements disclosed at that meeting, sales were 2,252 million KRW, the operating loss was 313 million KRW and the net loss was 345 million KRW.

On the same basis, total equity was negative 1,949 million KRW, so capital impairment continued. Looking further back, 2022 sales fell 18.6% year on year to 3.32 billion KRW and the operating loss was 477.7 million KRW.

Total equity at the end of 2023 was negative 2.939 billion KRW, a larger capital impairment than in the prior year. Elivision raised funds through two large rights offerings in 2022 and 2023, and the largest shareholder's stake fell from 35.12% to 34.18% over the same period.

A disclosure dated March 10, 2026 showed that major shareholder Lee Jong-su's holding rose to 29.83%. The share price was 452 KRW on March 31, 2026, down 10.14% from the prior day. As of October 1, 2026, the three-month return was 31.45% and the six-month return was 41.74%.

The current price is 720 KRW and the market capitalization is 8.9 billion KRW. Separately, Simply Wall St counted the latest financial reports as more than a year old and recorded no analyst coverage.

04

Outlook

The company has set a target of reaching breakeven in 2026 and said it would push its full growth phase from 2027. Plans to pursue a transfer listing to KOSDAQ in 2028 have been reported.

Media reports say that full enforcement of kiosk accessibility obligations under the Disability Discrimination Act is expected to lift demand for barrier-free kiosks, mainly from public institutions.

A securities industry source said the kiosk market may expand on regulatory changes, but that a terminal-only business may face limits on growth and valuation.

The same source said that if robotics translates into actual orders and earnings, the company could receive a different assessment from conventional kiosk makers during a KOSDAQ transfer. That view is conditional on the robotics business converting into results.

The first checkpoint for the business pivot is how much new order flow the smart-factory and public-safety markets generate after the Robowalks combination. On the financial side, operating losses continue while equity remains negative, so whether breakeven is reached and how funding is raised remain the key variables.

The large rights offerings in 2022 and 2023 did not lead to a sustained earnings recovery, which underlines this point.

05

Bull factors

Demand expected from mandatory accessibility rules

Accessibility obligations for kiosks under the Disability Discrimination Act have applied in full since 2026. Media reports expect the barrier-free kiosk market to expand, mainly among public institutions. Elivision's press list shows it began dedicated kiosk supply contracts for people with disabilities in 2024.

Robowalks deal widens the business scope

In August 2026 Elivision agreed to bring Robowalks in as a 100% subsidiary. Robowalks is described as supplying AMRs and collaborative robots, with defense and safety manufacturing capability and a nationwide after-sales network.

Elivision said it aims to build new orders in smart factories and B2G markets into a new growth axis for revenue.

Barrier-free patent and product track record

Elivision holds a patent for automatic height adjustment that adapts kiosks to users' physical conditions. Its press list shows a October 2024 win in a patent invalidation lawsuit, which reportedly gives the company a defensible position on the technology.

The company also obtained a US patent for its 'Mirabo' smart mirror in 2019, adding to its record of technology-based products.

06

Bear factors

Cumulative losses and capital impairment

On a standalone basis for the 22nd fiscal year, the operating loss was 313 million KRW and the net loss was 345 million KRW, so losses continued. Total equity on the same basis was negative 1,949 million KRW. At the end of 2023, total equity was already negative 2.939 billion KRW, with capital impairment widening.

Growth limits of a terminal-centric model

2022 sales fell 18.6% from 2021 to 3.32 billion KRW. Media reports cited intensifying competition in digital signage and the impact of COVID-19 as the main causes. A securities industry source said a terminal-only business may face limits on growth and valuation.

Reliance on rights offerings and dilution

Elivision raised funds through two large rights offerings in 2022 and 2023. The largest shareholder's stake fell from 35.12% in 2022 to 34.18% in 2023. Simply Wall St calculates an average weekly price movement of 22.1%, compared with an electronics sector average of 10.8%.

Risk factors

Policy and regulation

Kiosk market growth depends on how broadly accessibility rules are applied and enforced. If the regulation does not translate into demand at the expected pace, growth in the terminal business could slow. Securities industry commentary has also flagged the limits of a regulation-driven terminal business.

Business transition and competition

With the addition of robotics and automation through Robowalks, integrating it with the kiosk business and securing orders become simultaneous tasks. Reports describe rising competition in digital signage. If robotics orders are delayed, the cost burden of the acquisition could weigh on financials first.

Financial and liquidity

Operating losses under capital impairment keep dependence on external funding high. Repeated share issuance could dilute per-share value and shift shareholding ratios. Simply Wall St counted Elivision's latest financial reports as more than a year old.

08

Overall view

Elivision is a KONEX company that has centered on digital signage and kiosks, and in 2026 it faces two changes at once: a robotics subsidiary combination and the start of full kiosk accessibility enforcement.

The strengths are kiosk accessibility obligations under the Disability Discrimination Act, its barrier-free patent and supply record, and a wider reach into smart-factory and B2G markets through Robowalks.

The weaknesses are a financial structure with continuing operating losses and negative equity, a decline in revenue scale since 2022, and a securities industry view that a terminal-centric business has growth limits.

The company has set targets of breakeven in 2026, growth from 2027, and a KOSDAQ transfer listing in 2028. Whether those targets are met depends on converting robotics activity into actual orders and revenue, which is the common view across recent reports.

The share price moves widely and has no analyst coverage, so external benchmarks for judging price direction are limited. Financial improvement will be confirmed through results for the 2026 fiscal year and the way funding is raised. Resolving capital impairment and reaching breakeven are linked variables.
