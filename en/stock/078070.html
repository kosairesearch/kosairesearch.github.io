Report overview

Operating profit rebounds as Q3 attributable profit slumps

Ubiquoss Holdings' operating profit recovered to KRW 30.6 billion in 2025 and rose in 2Q and 3Q 2026, yet 3Q 2026 attributable net income fell to KRW 83.66 million, so the earnings trend is diverging.

02

Business structure

Ubiquoss Holdings became a listed holding company on KOSDAQ after converting to a holding structure in March 2017, and its main activities are managing, leasing and making new investments in subsidiaries.

Consolidated subsidiaries include the network-equipment maker Ubiquoss, an industrial-board manufacturer, an investment arm for new-technology finance, and a medical-device developer. In the segment breakdown for 1Q 2026, the network business generated KRW 22.9 billion, or 69.76% of total revenue.

Within subsidiary Ubiquoss, the same quarter's sales were split into switch and other network equipment at KRW 13.64 billion (59.5%), FTTH subscriber-network equipment at KRW 7.40 billion (32.3%), and maintenance and other at KRW 1.88 billion (8.2%).

Domestic sales accounted for 98.9% of subsidiary Ubiquoss's revenue in that quarter, while exports were only KRW 262 million. Subsidiary Ubiquoss is described as the domestic leader in the subscriber-network market of domestic carriers, with Ethernet switches and FTTH solutions as its core products.

The industrial-board subsidiary supplies memory-module test equipment for DDR5 server memory to SK hynix, and the company said its sales rose 122.7% in 2025 after entering that market.

In early 2026 the company announced a shift to an investment-type holding model that divides assets into Core (40%), Growth (30%), financial assets (20%) and Seed (10%).

The medical-device subsidiary is developing a non-invasive monitoring device for breathing and blood flow based on electrical impedance tomography, without radiation exposure. The largest shareholder is Chairman Lee Sang-geun with a 50.10% stake at end-2025, and minority shareholders hold 26.12%.

04

Earnings analysis

Annual revenue rose from KRW 151.6 billion in 2022 to KRW 171.6 billion in 2023 (up 13.2% year on year), fell to KRW 125.6 billion in 2024 (down 26.8%), and recovered to KRW 155.7 billion in 2025 (up 23.9%). Among 2022 to 2025, 2023 was the largest year for revenue and 2024 the smallest.

Operating profit went from KRW 14.3 billion in 2024 (down 56.0%) to KRW 30.6 billion in 2025 (up 113.3%), and operating margin moved from 11.4% to 19.7% over the same period. Attributable net income was smallest in 2024 at KRW 8.91 billion and largest in 2025 at KRW 20.4 billion (up 128.7%).

Net income in 2025 was KRW 33.6 billion, higher than attributable net income because the minority-interest share is included. Press coverage attributed the 2025 rebound mainly to growth at the industrial-board subsidiary.

Operating cash flow went from KRW 7.1 billion in 2022 to KRW 42.9 billion in 2024 before falling to KRW 32.1 billion in 2025, and the debt ratio declined from 12.8% in 2022 to 8.7% in 2025.

Quarterly revenue rose from KRW 39.6 billion in 3Q 2025 to KRW 43.5 billion in 4Q 2025, dropped to KRW 32.9 billion in 1Q 2026 (down 24.5% quarter on quarter), and then rose for two consecutive quarters to KRW 43.3 billion in 2Q and KRW 52.3 billion in 3Q.

Operating profit bottomed at KRW 5.75 billion in 1Q 2026 (down 42.8% quarter on quarter), then increased for two consecutive quarters to KRW 10.6 billion in 2Q and KRW 11.8 billion in 3Q, with 3Q operating profit up 43.8% year on year, faster than the 32.0% revenue growth.

By contrast, attributable net income fell from KRW 10 billion in 1Q 2026 and KRW 11.5 billion in 2Q to KRW 83.66 million in 3Q (down 98.7% year on year), and the sum for the last four quarters, from 4Q 2025 to 3Q 2026, is KRW 27 billion.

The breakdown of non-operating items, income tax and minority-interest allocation that separates operating profit from attributable net income is an item to check in the quarterly report notes.

05

Industry analysis

The wired access-network equipment market where subsidiary Ubiquoss operates is a structure in which earnings depend heavily on carriers' capital spending. A 2024 report said that as 5G infrastructure buildout approached completion, major domestic carriers reduced their equipment investment.

The company explained that subsidiary Ubiquoss has entered a mature phase after 5G commercialization, slowing its growth, and that a growth gap could emerge before 6G investment ramps up.

Government plans reported in press coverage call for raising optical-cable penetration from about 91% to 98% by 2030 and expanding backbone capacity roughly fourfold from current levels.

Based on this policy plan, an analysis by Hana Securities projected a recovery in FTTH revenue and higher demand for switch replacement as backbone traffic grows.

Domestic sales account for 98.9% of subsidiary Ubiquoss's revenue, which means its exposure to domestic telecom investment cycles is high relative to overseas demand.

The memory-module test equipment market entered by the industrial-board subsidiary is sensitive to the investment decisions of a few customers and follows a cycle different from wired telecom equipment. The different cycles of the two businesses diversify group earnings but also add volatility.

In medical devices, the subsidiary's non-invasive monitoring technology is drawing attention, but it is still described as being in development. Subsidiary Ubiquoss is presented as the domestic leader in subscriber-network equipment, yet its low overseas revenue share keeps its reliance on domestic operators high.

06

Outlook

The company has stated that, under its investment-type holding model, the Growth bucket (30% of assets) is meant to drive mid- to long-term growth in net asset value (NAV). On the financial side, it said it will maintain a debt ratio below 10% and keep announcing shareholder-return policies on a three-year cycle.

On new businesses, it said it will pursue bolt-on acquisitions of similar companies, and press coverage reported that part of its cash could be used for such deals. The next checkpoint is whether the recovery in FTTH revenue at subsidiary Ubiquoss and demand for switch replacement translate into actual sales.

Because the memory-module test equipment sales of the industrial-board subsidiary rose 122.7% in 2025, the key question is how much of that growth holds in the 2026 quarterly results.

The medical-device subsidiary's non-invasive monitoring device is still in development, so the commercialization timeline should be confirmed through future disclosures or investor relations materials.

The timing of the move from 5G to 6G investment is a variable that will shape demand for subsidiary equipment and is tied to how quickly new businesses can fill the growth gap the company described.

Government plans to expand optical-cable coverage to 98% by 2030 and to enlarge backbone capacity are medium-term variables that could support subsidiary FTTH and switch demand.

Whether the sharp drop in 3Q attributable net income reflects a one-off item or a recurring pattern should be checked against the annual profit structure in the 2026 annual report.

At end-2025, total equity was KRW 383.9 billion, of which the equity attributable to parent shareholders was KRW 265.4 billion, so the first comparison point is how market capitalization sits relative to that book value.

The price-to-book ratio is well below 1x, meaning the shares trade at a price under reported net assets. Book net assets do not fully reflect the market value of subsidiary stakes, so the reading of the discount or premium depends on how the subsidiary values are assessed.

08

Bull factors

Operating profit recovery and margin improvement

Operating profit rose 113.3% in 2025 to KRW 30.6 billion, and operating margin climbed from 11.4% in 2024 to 19.7%. Operating profit rose for two consecutive quarters in 2Q and 3Q 2026 after bottoming in 1Q.

Because 3Q operating profit growth outpaced revenue growth, the improvement in profitability is seen as arriving alongside the sales recovery.

Growth at the industrial-board subsidiary and new-business options

The industrial-board subsidiary entered the DDR5 server memory-module test equipment market for SK hynix, and the company said its sales rose 122.7% in 2025. The company positions this business as a growth asset that drives mid- to long-term NAV growth.

The medical-device subsidiary is developing a non-invasive monitoring device and is cited as a longer-term option.

Financial stability and cash holdings

The debt ratio was 8.7% at end-2025, with total liabilities of KRW 33.3 billion against total equity of KRW 383.9 billion. Operating cash flow stayed positive, at KRW 37.5 billion in 2023, KRW 42.9 billion in 2024 and KRW 32.1 billion in 2025. Press coverage reported cash and cash equivalents of KRW 144 billion at end-2025.

Bear factors

Sharp drop in 3Q attributable profit

3Q 2026 operating profit rose 43.8% year on year to KRW 11.8 billion, but attributable net income fell 98.7% to KRW 83.66 million. Operating gains were not carried through to shareholders in that quarter, so the bridge between the two lines needs to be checked.

The four-quarter total of KRW 27 billion also reflects large quarter-to-quarter swings, which weakens earnings stability.

Earnings volatility and the 2024 experience

In 2024, revenue fell 26.8%, operating profit fell 56.0%, and attributable net income fell 36.3%.

Whether the 2025 rebound is a one-off recovery or a sustainable trend cannot yet be judged from a single year. 2024 was the smallest year among 2022 to 2025 for both operating profit and attributable net income, showing how sensitive results can be to external conditions.

Slowing growth at the core subsidiary and reliance on telecom capex

The company said subsidiary Ubiquoss has entered a mature phase after 5G commercialization, slowing its growth. With domestic sales at 98.9% of that subsidiary's revenue, the business depends heavily on domestic carriers' capital spending. Coverage in 2024 cited reduced equipment investment by major customers as a factor in weaker results.

Risk factors

Telecom capex cycle

Subsidiary Ubiquoss's revenue is sensitive to domestic carriers' equipment spending. As 5G buildout nears completion, investment could decline, and a gap may appear before 6G investment ramps up, according to the company.

Whether government plans to expand optical-cable coverage translate into actual demand is the variable that could reduce this risk.

Concentration in subsidiaries and new-business uncertainty

Press coverage named the industrial-board subsidiary's growth as the main driver of the 2025 rebound. Results could depend heavily on a few customers at one subsidiary. The medical-device subsidiary is still in development, and bolt-on acquisitions could produce different outcomes depending on price and integration.

Quarterly earnings volatility and ownership concentration

The sharp drop in 3Q attributable net income shows how quickly quarterly results can swing. Chairman Lee Sang-geun held 50.10% at end-2025, a high concentration, while minority shareholders hold 26.12%.

A high largest-shareholder stake means capital allocation and shareholder-return decisions can weigh heavily on one person's judgment.

12

Overall view

Ubiquoss Holdings recovered from the 2024 slowdown, with operating profit of KRW 30.6 billion and an operating margin of 19.7% in 2025, and operating profit rose for two consecutive quarters in 2Q and 3Q 2026.

Growth in memory-module test equipment sales at the industrial-board subsidiary and FTTH and switch sales at subsidiary Ubiquoss are the two pillars supporting results.

On the other hand, 3Q 2026 attributable net income was KRW 83.66 million, down 98.7% year on year, so operating gains did not carry through to shareholders in that quarter.

The balance sheet looks stable, with a debt ratio of 8.7% and total liabilities of KRW 33.3 billion at end-2025, and the company is reshaping asset allocation as it moves to an investment-type holding model.

However, the telecom capex cycle, the results of new businesses, and the 50.10% stake held by Chairman Lee Sang-geun at end-2025 remain open variables. The next items to check are the preliminary full-year 2026 results in early 2027 and the 2026 annual report due by the end of March 2027.

How much the gap between operating profit and attributable net income narrows in those two filings is the key to reading the earnings structure.
