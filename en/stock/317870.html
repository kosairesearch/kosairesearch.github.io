02

Business structure

Envioneer is an advanced composite materials company founded in 2001 and listed on KOSDAQ in 2019, producing its products with advanced nonwoven technology based on a wet-laid manufacturing process. Most revenue comes from positively charged water purification filters.

Of consolidated revenue of KRW 9.7 billion in the first half of 2026, positively charged filters contributed KRW 5.4 billion, or 55.4%, and other products including ceramic paper contributed KRW 2.3 billion. On a standalone basis, first-half revenue was KRW 8.4 billion, up 43.2% from a year earlier.

The new growth segments are ceramic paper and basalt paper, used as flame-retardant materials that slow the spread of heat runaway in ESS and EV batteries, along with meta-aramid paper, an insulation material. Analysts note that the actual revenue contribution will take time.

In a July 2025 report, the company said it had begun full-scale supply of ceramic flame-retardant material for ESS to a major domestic company.

Meta-aramid paper is a segment where DuPont's Nomex brand has long held a near monopoly in global markets, and the company said it signed an exclusive yarn supply contract with a Toray Group partner in 2022 running through 2035.

Its subsidiary makes degassing membranes for industrial ultrapure water and began supplying an IPA off-gas removal module for an S-brand semiconductor process in 2025, with mass deliveries planned from 2026.

Planned production facility investment for meta-aramid and related lines totals KRW 65.4 billion, of which KRW 41.2 billion had been spent by the end of June 2026.

04

Earnings analysis

On an annual basis, revenue rose from KRW 11 billion in 2022 to KRW 14.7 billion in 2023, up 32.9%, making 2023 the largest year of 2022 to 2025, before slipping to KRW 14.2 billion in 2024 (down 2.9%) and KRW 14.3 billion in 2025 (up 0.4%).

Operating losses were KRW 701 million in 2022, KRW 205 million in 2023, KRW 2.11 billion in 2024 and KRW 6.22 billion in 2025, a string of four consecutive years of operating losses from 2022 through 2025.

The operating margin improved from -6.4% in 2022 to -1.4% in 2023, then deteriorated to -14.8% in 2024 and -43.6% in 2025. The company attributed the widening 2025 operating loss to higher cost of sales ratio and selling, general and administrative expenses.

Net loss attributable to controlling shareholders was KRW 163 million in 2022, KRW 4.67 billion in 2023, KRW 1.71 billion in 2024 and KRW 9.32 billion in 2025, while the total net loss including non-controlling interests was KRW 11.3 billion in 2025, larger than the controlling-shareholder figure.

The debt ratio fell from 90.6% in 2022 to 42.5% in 2024 before jumping to 131.2% in 2025, when total liabilities of KRW 46.7 billion exceeded total equity of KRW 35.6 billion. Operating cash flow went from KRW 1.74 billion in 2022 and KRW 1.85 billion in 2023 to -KRW 20.92 million in 2024 and -KRW 6.07 billion in 2025.

Quarterly revenue reached KRW 4.5 billion in the fourth quarter of 2025, up 24.3% from the prior quarter, peaked at KRW 5.08 billion in the first quarter of 2026, and then declined for two consecutive quarters to KRW 4.61 billion in the second quarter and KRW 4.14 billion in the third.

Operating losses have run for five consecutive quarters from the third quarter of 2025 through the third quarter of 2026; the KRW 1.13 billion loss in the third quarter of 2026 was smaller than a year earlier but larger than the prior quarter.

Net income attributable to controlling shareholders turned to a KRW 966 million profit in the second quarter of 2026 before returning to a KRW 1.05 billion loss in the third quarter, and the sum of the last four quarters' controlling-shareholder net losses is KRW 7.63 billion.

The company said about half of the 2025 operating profit decline came from its subsidiary's development costs and called the charges temporary costs of the transition to mass production.

Press coverage reported that ceramic paper production costs fell below selling prices after yields stabilized in the first half of 2026.

05

Industry analysis

The flame-retardant and insulation materials market that Envioneer has entered is linked to tighter safety standards for ESS and EV batteries, and it is viewed as a segment where demand could expand.

A Korea IR Association report cited tighter battery thermal-runaway safety standards as a positive and said ceramic paper was becoming likely to be standardized as a flame-retardant material, centered on major domestic cell makers.

Beyond ESS and EV, ceramic paper is used as insulation in high-temperature industries such as steelmaking, power plants and chemicals, and globally Morgan of the UK and 3M supply comparable products for aircraft, automotive and high-temperature gaskets.

Meta-aramid paper has long been dominated by DuPont's Nomex brand because of technical difficulty and heavy capital investment, which makes entry hard. Envioneer pairs its meta-aramid plans with an exclusive yarn supply arrangement with a Toray Group partner, which gives it a raw-material link.

Press coverage reported that a domestic battery maker had set ceramic paper as its customer specification, though the eventual supply volume depends on company disclosures. In semiconductor equipment, its subsidiary is seeking to expand supply of exhaust-gas removal units for large domestic chipmakers.

Against global materials companies, Envioneer is a domestic materials maker with limited financial capacity, and that gap is a variable in competition. With most revenue still coming from filters, how fast new materials gain revenue share will be the main measure of its position in the sector.

06

Outlook

The company said that if subsidiary equipment sales expand and new materials reach mass production, the earnings structure would clearly improve from 2026 to 2027. For the second half of 2026, the company official said price increases and higher volumes from a major filter customer were expected.

Ceramic paper is being shifted toward higher-margin products, and with production costs now below selling prices, the second half depends on turning that margin gain into operating profit. Its subsidiary is expected to make mass deliveries of an IPA off-gas removal module for an S-brand semiconductor process from 2026.

Expansion of newer product lines, including basalt paper and FRBP, is cited as another factor behind an improved earnings trend. A new meta-aramid plant of about KRW 70 billion is under construction and is set to begin full operation in 2027.

In April 2026 the company raised funds by selling part of its subsidiary stake for KRW 5 billion, and the subsidiary has a plan to pursue an IPO once earnings become visible, although that is expected to take time.

Because bank borrowings rose in the first half of 2026, the execution of capital spending and refinancing plans will need to be confirmed. The next checkpoints are the operating margin and debt movements in the 2026 annual report and the start-up timing of the meta-aramid plant.

Bull factors

Ceramic paper cost improvement

The company said ceramic paper production costs fell below selling prices after yields stabilized in the first half of 2026. Consolidated operating loss for the period narrowed from KRW 2.9 billion to KRW 1.4 billion. The company also plans to shift its main ceramic paper product toward higher-margin items.

Meta-aramid supply position

A new meta-aramid plant of about KRW 70 billion is set to begin full operation in 2027. Yarn is secured under an exclusive supply contract with a Toray Group partner running through 2035. Global supply of these insulation and flame-retardant materials is viewed as limited, which is cited as room for growth.

Semiconductor equipment subsidiary

Its subsidiary supplies an IPA off-gas removal module for an S-brand semiconductor process, with mass deliveries scheduled from 2026. The company said expanded equipment sales at the subsidiary and new materials mass production could together improve the earnings structure from 2026 to 2027. The partial stake sale in April 2026 provides a reference point for the subsidiary's valuation.

Bear factors

Debt and cash flow pressure

Bank borrowings rose from KRW 15.3 billion at the end of 2025 to KRW 21.1 billion at the end of the first half of 2026. Operating cash flow in the same half was a net outflow of KRW 1.2 billion and has not turned positive. With KRW 41.2 billion of a KRW 65.4 billion facility plan already spent, further funding needs remain.

Losses widening again by quarter

Operating losses have run for five consecutive quarters from the third quarter of 2025 through the third quarter of 2026. The KRW 1.13 billion loss in the third quarter of 2026 was larger than the KRW 784 million in the second quarter.

Quarterly revenue has also fallen for two straight quarters since its first-quarter 2026 peak, so top-line recovery has paused.

Convertible bond put option risk

The KRW 20 billion ninth-series convertible bond enters its put option claim period on March 3, 2027, with the first early redemption date on May 2, 2027. Press analysis said that because the conversion price was about 2.5 times the share price at the time, a put exercise looked more likely than conversion.

The KOSDAQ market cap requirement for continued listing is also scheduled to rise to KRW 30 billion in January 2027.

Risk factors

Funding and refinancing

Put option claims and early redemption dates on convertible bonds cluster in the first half of 2027, making refinancing the largest variable. The company said it is considering refinancing depending on conditions. It chose a subsidiary stake sale rather than a rights offering, citing debt-ratio concerns.

Process yield and cost

Early mass production of ceramic paper saw low yields and process losses push product costs above selling prices, and yields later improved after process upgrades. The company described the 2025 cost increase at the parent as temporary, with yield gains and process efficiency measures being rolled out in stages. If cost improvements do not hold, the recovery in profitability could be delayed.

Dilution and listing requirements

On March 27, 2026, stock options for 111,000 common shares were granted to 16 employees and directors, and unexercised options outstanding were given as 252,000 shares. The KOSDAQ market cap requirement for continued listing is scheduled to rise to KRW 30 billion in January 2027. Share count growth combined with a higher listing threshold could add pressure on shareholders.

Overall view

Envioneer's disclosed results show operating losses running from 2022 through 2025, with the 2025 operating loss widening to KRW 6.22 billion. In 2026, stabilized ceramic paper yields lifted first-half revenue to KRW 9.7 billion and narrowed the operating loss.

However, the third-quarter 2026 operating loss of KRW 1.13 billion was larger than the prior quarter, and revenue has declined for two consecutive quarters. Controlling-shareholder net losses over the last four quarters total KRW 7.63 billion.

On the financial side, the debt ratio jumped to 131.2% at the end of 2025, and bank borrowings rose to KRW 21.1 billion at the end of the first half of 2026.

The company says meta-aramid plant operation and semiconductor equipment deliveries could reshape the earnings structure from 2027, but this is management's outlook.

The next checkpoints are the January 2027 listing requirement increase and the convertible bond put option claim period from March 3, 2027, while the operating margin and debt movements will be disclosed in the 2026 annual report.
