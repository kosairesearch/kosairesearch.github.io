02

Business structure

SeA Mechanics was founded in 1999 and listed on KOSDAQ in March 2022 as a precision parts maker built on aluminum die casting. At its August 19, 2026 extraordinary shareholder meeting, the company adopted a new name that signals a robot-centered identity.

Its core process is high-vacuum precision die casting, which minimizes porosity and impurities and can also be applied to robot joint housings and actuator cases. According to one report's tally, parts accounted for KRW 24.2 billion (72.0%) and the IT business for KRW 8.7 billion (25.9%) of first-quarter 2026 revenue.

Major customers span large-cap value chains including LG Electronics, LG Energy Solution and the Hyundai Motor Group.

In ESS parts, supply to LG Energy Solution's North American projects has been the earnings driver, and the company said that program could generate cumulative revenue of about KRW 190 billion over 2025 to 2028.

In robotics, the company supplies arm mechanisms for AI home robots to a global consumer electronics maker and signed a joint development MOU with a collaborative robot company in June 2026. Robot-related parts reportedly make up a single-digit share of total revenue.

In AIDC, the company was registered as a supplier to a large Korean power equipment maker and is preparing first shipments of aluminum die-cast parts in the fourth quarter of 2026.

The company was also selected as a 2026 World Class Plus firm, which could bring up to KRW 5 billion in national R&D funding over up to four years. Competition is intensifying as several listed KOSDAQ companies make aluminum die-cast parts for EV, ESS and robot applications.

04

Earnings analysis

On a disclosed annual basis, revenue moved from KRW 87.6 billion in 2022 to KRW 86.8 billion in 2023 (down 1.0% year on year), then rose to KRW 92.3 billion in 2024 (up 6.4%), the largest year of 2022 to 2025, before falling to KRW 85.8 billion in 2025 (down 7.1%), the smallest year.

Operating profit fell from KRW 2.25 billion in 2022 (operating margin 2.6%) to KRW 1.99 billion in 2023 (down 11.5%), then turned into an operating loss of KRW 2.66 billion in 2024, a swing from profit to loss.

The 2025 operating loss of KRW 1.85 billion was smaller than the year before, and 2024 and 2025 were two consecutive loss years, with operating margins of -2.9% and -2.2%.

Attributable net income rose from KRW 3.83 billion in 2022 to KRW 6.35 billion in 2023 (up 66.0%), the peak year, then fell to KRW 2.54 billion in 2024 (down 59.9%) and KRW 1.42 billion in 2025 (down 44.1%), a two-year decline.

Net income including non-controlling interests was disclosed at the same levels for each year, so it followed the same path. In 2024 and 2025, operating losses coincided with positive net income, so the sources of profit need to be traced to non-operating items.

The debt-to-equity ratio climbed each year from 23.9% in 2022 to 45.1%, 84.1% and 101.0% in 2025. At the end of 2025, total equity and attributable equity were both KRW 74.2 billion, and total liabilities were KRW 75 billion.

Operating cash flow was KRW 4.79 billion in 2022, KRW 7.61 billion in 2023 and KRW 3.88 billion in 2024, then turned to minus KRW 11.7 billion in 2025, far below that year's net income of KRW 1.42 billion.

Quarterly results moved from an operating loss of KRW 344 million in the third quarter of 2025 to operating profit of KRW 403 million in the fourth quarter, a turn to profit. First-quarter 2026 revenue was KRW 33.6 billion, up 36.7% quarter on quarter, with operating profit of KRW 1.65 billion, up 310.3%.

Second-quarter revenue reached KRW 37.4 billion (up 11.4%) and operating profit KRW 2.04 billion (up 23.4%). Third-quarter revenue rose to KRW 46.9 billion (up 25.2% quarter on quarter and 101.0% year on year), but operating profit of KRW 1.48 billion fell 27.3% from the prior quarter.

Press reports put the third-quarter operating margin at about 3.16%, down from about 5.45% in the second quarter. The company attributed the drop to foreign-exchange gains and losses from a weaker won and a reverse base effect from the prior quarter.

Attributable net income was KRW 2.1 billion in the fourth quarter of 2025, KRW 2.21 billion in the first quarter of 2026, KRW 3.19 billion in the second quarter, and KRW 490 million in the third quarter.

Attributable net income for the last four quarters (fourth quarter of 2025 through third quarter of 2026) totals KRW 7.99 billion.

05

Industry analysis

Demand from end markets comes from three directions. First, ESS parts depend on large North American projects of battery customers.

Major customers are adding or expanding hyperscale AI data centers across North America, and they are diversifying parts sourcing, with the company selected as a new supplier in that process. Second, robot parts are at an early stage, with demand opening in AI home robots and collaborative robots.

Third, AIDC cooling and power infrastructure parts reuse thermal-management technology built in automotive electronics. In cycle terms, legacy businesses that slid into losses in 2024 on weak EV demand and softer IT structure demand have not fully recovered, and new businesses contribute little to sales.

Quarterly operating profit has been positive since the fourth quarter of 2025, which the company links to ESS backlog starting to flow into results. On competition, the company's Gumi plant, which combines integrated casting and precision machining with local direct delivery, is viewed as building a barrier to entry.

Still, several listed KOSDAQ companies make aluminum die-cast parts, so price and volume competition remains possible.

Large component makers in the electronics and parts sector, including LG Innotek and Samsung Electro-Mechanics, are expanding into automotive electronics and AI server parts, so the company's expansion overlaps with the same demand pools.

Profitability in this industry is strongly influenced by raw material costs, exchange rates and customer capacity schedules.

Outlook

The company's target for 2026 annual revenue is about KRW 150 billion. Press reports say securities analysts view reaching this target as manageable.

Adding up disclosed quarterly revenue, the first three quarters of 2026 total KRW 117.9 billion, so the fourth quarter would need to reach about KRW 32.1 billion to hit the target.

That is well below the third-quarter level of KRW 46.9 billion, so the target would still be reachable even if fourth-quarter revenue falls from the third quarter. ESS parts backlog was reported above KRW 600 billion in an August 2026 report and later reported at about KRW 700 billion.

In AIDC, the company completed supplier registration and plans first supply of power distribution system parts and assemblies in the fourth quarter of 2026, extending its growth into data center infrastructure.

In robotics, the next checkpoint is whether the joint development MOU with a collaborative robot company moves into mass production. The company said it is reviewing one or two strategic M&A deals to internalize core technology.

It also said it will keep investing in robotics and AIDC cooling using cash generated by its core businesses. On profitability, the exchange-rate swings cited for the third-quarter operating profit decline need to be watched for their effect in the fourth quarter. A company official said the company plans to ship the backlog in sequence while defending profitability.

Bull factors

Revenue expansion on the ESS backlog

The ESS parts backlog has reportedly passed KRW 700 billion as supply to a battery customer's European plants and North American projects expands. Third-quarter 2026 revenue reached KRW 46.9 billion, up 101.0% year on year. The company said it will ship the backlog in sequence to expand sales.

Consecutive quarterly profits and operating profit recovery

Quarterly operating profit has been positive from the fourth quarter of 2025 through the third quarter of 2026. It rose from an operating loss of KRW 344 million in the third quarter of 2025 to KRW 2.04 billion in the second quarter of 2026. First-quarter 2026 operating profit of KRW 1.65 billion was up 310.3% from the prior quarter.

New channels in robotics and AIDC

The company supplies arm mechanisms for AI home robots to a global consumer electronics maker and signed a joint development MOU with a collaborative robot company.

It completed supplier registration with a large power equipment maker and begins first supply of power distribution parts and assemblies in the fourth quarter of 2026, extending its growth into data centers.

It also plans to apply thermal-management technology from automotive electronics to AIDC liquid cooling solutions.

Bear factors

Slower profitability in the third quarter

Third-quarter 2026 operating profit of KRW 1.48 billion fell 27.3% from the second quarter, and press reports put the operating margin down from about 5.45% to about 3.16%. The company attributed this to foreign-exchange gains and losses and a reverse base effect. Attributable net income of KRW 490 million fell 84.6% from the prior quarter.

Cash flow and financial burden

Operating cash flow in 2025 was minus KRW 11.7 billion, moving opposite to net income of KRW 1.42 billion. The debt-to-equity ratio rose from 23.9% in 2022 to 101.0% in 2025. Total liabilities at the end of 2025 were KRW 75 billion, close to total equity of KRW 74.2 billion.

New businesses contribute little to sales yet

Robot-related parts make up a single-digit share of total revenue. New businesses such as collaborative robots and precision robot parts remain at the R&D and prototype stage. Recovery in legacy businesses, which turned to losses in 2024 on weak EV demand and IT structure demand, is not yet complete.

10

Risk factors

Customer and project concentration

The ESS earnings rebound depends heavily on supply to LG Energy Solution's North American projects. Changes in the customer's investment schedule or project timing could widen quarterly revenue swings. Given the size of the backlog, shipment timing largely determines quarterly results.

Exchange rates and FX gains or losses

The company cited exchange-rate movements as a cause of the third-quarter operating profit decline. FX gains and losses can swing sharply from quarter to quarter and disrupt earnings continuity. The third quarter, in which profit fell from the prior quarter, is an example.

Execution of the business transition

AIDC and robot parts still need mass-production stabilization and quality verification. Technology internalization through M&A is still at the target-screening stage, so timing and results are uncertain. Growing investment in new businesses could require external funding and add to the financial burden.

Overall view

SeA Mechanics moved past operating losses in 2024 and 2025 and has posted quarterly operating profit since the fourth quarter of 2025. Third-quarter 2026 revenue of KRW 46.9 billion rose 101.0% year on year, and the ESS parts backlog has been reported at about KRW 700 billion.

However, third-quarter operating profit fell 27.3% from the prior quarter, and the operating margin declined. Operating cash flow was minus KRW 11.7 billion in 2025, and the debt-to-equity ratio rose to 101.0%.

Robot parts remain a single-digit share of revenue, and new-business contribution will become clearer only after AIDC first supply in the fourth quarter of 2026.

The next checkpoints are whether fourth-quarter revenue holds at a level consistent with the annual target and whether the collaborative robot MOU moves into mass production.
