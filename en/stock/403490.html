02

Business structure

WooDeumGee Farm grows, sells and distributes tomatoes as a smart farm operator, running about 11.1 ha of glasshouses according to a corporate information service. Its flagship stevia tomato brand Tomango began mass production in 2019 and has been the main earnings source.

The crop mix is also widening to European-type vegetables such as romaine and butterhead lettuce. The company says its self-designed Korean semi-closed glasshouse, combined with ICT-based climate control and accumulated growing data, allows year-round output despite heat waves and cold snaps.

The business has three strands. The first is farm production and sales through distribution channels. The second is smart farm construction, where a subsidiary bundles greenhouse design, construction, materials supply and operating consulting.

The third is overseas agricultural development, based on a land-use agreement with Bolikhamxay Province in Laos. Etoday reported that the company won external construction work in 2025 and is pursuing more in 2026.

Its largest project is the Seosan smart farm complex on about 152,135 square meters of land, where the company acts as project manager through a special purpose company. Newspim noted that individual farms in Korea have adopted smart farming at only about 3%.

04

Earnings analysis

Based on reported results, annual revenue rose from KRW 44.9 billion in 2022 to KRW 56.6 billion in 2023 (up 25.9% year on year) and KRW 63.9 billion in 2024 (up 13.0% year on year), then came to KRW 63.7 billion in 2025 (down 0.4% year on year). Over 2022-2025, the highest year was 2024 and the lowest was 2022.

Operating profit fell from KRW 3.81 billion in 2022 to KRW 3.35 billion in 2023 (down 12.1% year on year), recovered to KRW 3.55 billion in 2024 (up 6.0% year on year), and swung to an operating loss of KRW 0.88 billion in 2025.

The operating margin went from 8.5% in 2022 to 5.9% in 2023 and 5.6% in 2024, then to -1.4% in 2025.

Net income attributable to controlling shareholders was KRW 4.02 billion in 2022, KRW 1.45 billion in 2023 and KRW 2.94 billion in 2024, and in 2025 it was a net loss attributable to controlling shareholders of KRW 4.1 billion.

Net income including non-controlling interests was a net loss of KRW 4.39 billion in 2025, which should be viewed separately from the figure attributable to controlling shareholders.

The company cited the cost burden from higher external purchase prices as the cause of the 2025 loss and explained that selling prices are hard to adjust in the short term.

A securities information service summarized that the operating loss resulted from lower gross profit and higher selling, general and administrative expenses, owing to a heavier cost burden on its main products, Tomango and tomatoes.

On a quarterly basis, the loss widened from revenue of KRW 13.5 billion and an operating loss of KRW 1.11 billion in Q3 2025, through revenue of KRW 17.0 billion and an operating loss of KRW 1.07 billion in Q4, to revenue of KRW 12.4 billion and an operating loss of KRW 2.22 billion in Q1 2026.

Revenue of KRW 18.4 billion in Q2 2026 (up 47.8% quarter on quarter) led to a return to profit, with operating profit of KRW 0.75 billion.

Revenue in Q3 2026 was KRW 13.4 billion (down 0.3% year on year), and operating profit of KRW 0.61 billion marked a return to profit from an operating loss in the same period a year earlier.

Net income attributable to controlling shareholders returned to profit at KRW 0.14 billion in Q3 2026, but the cumulative net loss attributable to controlling shareholders over the latest four quarters is KRW 5.92 billion.

The profit recovery appeared first at the operating level, and net income attributable to controlling shareholders turned positive only in Q3 2026.

05

Industry analysis

WooDeumGee Farm is classified under food and beverages, but its actual business is closer to greenhouse agriculture, farm produce distribution and smart farm construction. Newspim reported that individual farms in Korea have adopted smart farming at only about 3%.

The same report said the government expects the related market to grow under its first smart agriculture development plan covering 2025 to 2029. On the climate side, concerns over a possible strong El Niño have raised the profile of food security.

In the same report, experts said high-tech smart farms could become a tool against food security risks and raw material price swings as climate uncertainty grows.

On competition, the company emphasizes its semi-closed glasshouse design and construction together with operating data, and says productivity per area is more than double that of open fields, a claim it makes itself. Within tomatoes, the Tomango brand is the core of its competitiveness.

However, the 2025 results showed that when external purchase prices rise, passing on the cost is difficult, which exposes a vulnerable cost structure. Reports also say that the limits of open-field farming are drawing distributors and investors toward smart farms.

06

Outlook

The company presents the Seosan smart farm complex, agricultural development in Laos and its engineering and construction business as its medium-term growth pillars.

A December 2024 Hankyung report described Seosan as a project with a total cost of more than KRW 330 billion across about 150,000 pyeong, and the company said that roughly KRW 90 billion allocated to the smart farm build-out would produce more than 6,000 tons of fruit and vegetables a year and more than KRW 30 billion in new annual revenue.

A November 2026 Money Today report put the total project cost at KRW 305.5 billion and repeated the company's expectation of about KRW 30 billion in additional annual revenue from purchasing and distributing output after completion.

The difference between the two cost figures may reflect different project scope or aggregation, so it should be checked against disclosures.

For Laos, a December 2025 report said the company secured use rights to about 450,000 pyeong under a memorandum with Bolikhamxay Province, could expand development to about 18 million pyeong, and expects roughly KRW 700 billion in revenue over the long term.

The same report set a target of starting construction in the first quarter of 2026, with first harvest one year after groundbreaking. Newspim reported in August 2026 that a feasibility study covering a 23,400-hectare K-smart farm project across Bolikhamxay and Saravan provinces was under way.

For the second half of 2026, the company listed higher production efficiency, product diversification, wider distribution channels and domestic and overseas smart farm projects as its plan.

In September 2026 it held an investor relations session for securities company wealth managers and presented an expansion strategy for overseas markets.

The Q1 2026 construction target has already passed, so the next check is whether actual groundbreaking dates and first harvest timing match what is disclosed in the annual report and ad hoc filings.

Bull factors

Operating-level profits

Bear factors

2025 losses and cost pressure

The company recorded a full-year 2025 operating loss of KRW 884 million and a net loss attributable to controlling shareholders of KRW 4.1 billion. Higher external purchase prices lifted costs, and the company said selling prices are hard to change quickly.

Whether the combination of lower gross profit and higher selling and administrative expenses has eased in 2026 remains the question.

Rising financial burden

The debt-to-equity ratio climbed from 22.3% in 2022 to 100.2% in 2025, and total liabilities at the end of 2025 were KRW 38.2 billion, close to total equity of KRW 38.1 billion. The loan balance to the subsidiary Nine Farm is KRW 16.23 billion, with maturity extended to October 30, 2027.

In April 2026 a funding support agreement capped at KRW 12.6 billion was set for the subsidiary's loan repayment.

Earnings volatility and dilution

Q1 2026 showed an operating loss of KRW 2.22 billion, a quarter in which losses widened. The KRW 7.0 billion convertible bond issued in April 2026 would add shares equal to 10.24% of existing total shares on conversion.

In August 2026 the company also received a notice on possible designation as a management-issue stock and disclosed a trading halt related to a share consolidation.

10

Risk factors

Subsidiary finances and funding support

The subsidiary Nine Farm had negative total equity and a net loss of about KRW 1.4 billion in its 2025 fiscal year, according to an April 2026 filing. Because the parent provides loans and a funding support agreement, the subsidiary's burden can pass through to the parent's finances. The support cap is KRW 12.6 billion, or 20% of the principal and interest on a KRW 63.0 billion financing.

Climate and production risk

The company says its semi-closed glasshouses block sudden temperature swings and pest intrusion, but output still depends on facility operation costs and weather. With concerns over a possible strong El Niño reported in 2026, supply and price volatility for farm goods could increase. If external purchase prices rise again, cost pressure could return as it did in 2025.

Share dilution and market signals

The convertible bond can be converted from April 10, 2027 to March 10, 2029, with shares equal to 10.24% of existing total shares. The terms allow the conversion price to be adjusted if the share price falls, which could add dilution. The August 2026 notice on possible management-issue designation may also weigh on market sentiment.

Overall view

WooDeumGee Farm is built on two pillars: tomato production in semi-closed glasshouses and smart farm construction. Annual revenue in 2025 was KRW 63.7 billion, slightly lower, and the company swung to an operating loss of KRW 884 million and a net loss attributable to controlling shareholders of KRW 4.1 billion.

Operating profit turned positive in Q2 and Q3 2026, and net profit attributable to controlling shareholders turned positive at KRW 143 million in Q3. However, the net loss attributable to controlling shareholders over the last four quarters totals KRW 5.92 billion, so no earnings-based multiple can be calculated.

Supporting factors include continued quarterly operating profit, revenue plans from the Seosan complex and Laos agriculture, and external construction orders at the subsidiary.

Opposing factors include cost pressure in 2025, a debt-to-equity ratio that rose to 100.2%, a funding structure built on subsidiary loans and a convertible bond, and a past notice on possible management-issue designation.

Plans differ in scale and timing across reports, so how construction and operation feed into results is the next point to check. This report is provided for information only and does not contain an investment opinion or target price.
