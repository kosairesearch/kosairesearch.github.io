Xplus: Expanding Scope Amid Continued Losses

Xplus, a mobile accessory maker, posted an operating loss in each year from 2022 through 2025. Its third-quarter 2026 operating loss of KRW 1.71 billion narrowed from the prior quarter but widened from a year earlier, and the company is moving to add energy and port transport to its business scope.

02

Business structure

Xplus makes and sells mobile IT accessories, mainly smartphone cases, wireless chargers and earphone cases.

A company overview summarized as of September 2024 says the company uses intellectual property to develop IT products with differentiated form factors, customization and new materials, and supplies them to Samsung Electronics and online markets.

It has also been expanding offline distribution of goods built around character intellectual property partnerships.

According to a news report dated November 17, 2026, accessories made up about 95.8% of cumulative sales of KRW 9.79 billion through the third quarter of 2026, with the segment's cumulative sales at KRW 9.37 billion. The remaining share, about 4%, comes from other segments.

In 2025, the company said its consolidated results reflected a newly consolidated subsidiary.

Its expansion beyond accessories began with articles of incorporation: a shareholder meeting on March 26, 2026 approved a business purpose amendment, and on November 17, 2026 the company said it would table an energy and port transport proposal at an extraordinary meeting on December 24, 2026.

The proposal covers microgrid and power-supply facility design, construction, maintenance and operation, plus vessel leasing using tugboats.

Accessory sales follow the launch cycles and order volumes of finished-goods brands, so quarterly revenue swings are large, as seen in the 72.6% decline from the prior quarter in the fourth quarter of 2025 and the 185.5% increase in the first quarter of 2026.

Earnings analysis

Annual revenue was KRW 16.5 billion in 2022, the largest year in the 2022 to 2025 period, before falling 40.7% to KRW 9.78 billion in 2023, the smallest year. It then reached KRW 10.4 billion in 2024, up 6.7% year on year, and KRW 10.1 billion in 2025, down 3.4% year on year.

The operating loss grew from KRW 77.05 million in 2022 to KRW 3.7 billion in 2023, KRW 3.89 billion in 2024 and KRW 5.16 billion in 2025, and the operating margin moved from -0.5% in 2022 to -37.9% in 2023, -37.3% in 2024 and -51.2% in 2025.

The company has posted losses for four straight years from 2022 to 2025, and the 2025 loss was the largest of those four years.

The loss attributable to controlling shareholders was KRW 4.52 billion in 2022 and KRW 8.55 billion in 2023, narrowed to KRW 2.11 billion in 2024, and widened again to KRW 2.29 billion in 2025.

Net loss including non-controlling interests was KRW 4.52 billion, KRW 8.55 billion, KRW 2.11 billion and KRW 2.29 billion in the same years. For the 2025 operating loss, the company attributed the change mainly to a newly consolidated subsidiary whose results were reflected in consolidated profit and loss.

The debt-to-equity ratio rose from 101.5% in 2022 and 555.8% in 2023 to 139.6% in 2024 and 176.7% in 2025, when total liabilities of KRW 36.6 billion exceeded total equity of KRW 20.7 billion.

Operating cash flow was -KRW 1.1 billion in 2022, -KRW 4.48 billion in 2023 and -KRW 2.07 billion in 2024, then reached -KRW 15.1 billion in 2025, the largest outflow in the period.

Quarterly revenue was KRW 5.02 billion in the third quarter of 2025, KRW 1.38 billion in the fourth quarter (down 72.6% from the prior quarter), KRW 3.93 billion in the first quarter of 2026 (up 185.5%), KRW 2.66 billion in the second quarter (down 32.3%), and KRW 3.2 billion in the third quarter (up 20.3% from the prior quarter and down 36.3% year on year).

Quarterly operating losses were KRW 753 million, KRW 2.22 billion, KRW 1.08 billion, KRW 2.29 billion and KRW 1.71 billion across the third quarter of 2025 through the third quarter of 2026, which makes five straight loss-making quarters.

The loss attributable to controlling shareholders turned to a KRW 4.88 billion profit in the fourth quarter of 2025 from a KRW 5.48 billion loss in the prior quarter, even though that quarter's operating loss was KRW 2.22 billion, so non-operating items changed the direction of quarterly net income and their detail should be checked in the next regular report.

Losses then returned, at KRW 3.39 billion in the first quarter of 2026, KRW 3.87 billion in the second and KRW 4.93 billion in the third, which makes three straight loss-making quarters. The loss attributable to controlling shareholders over the last four quarters totals KRW 7.32 billion.

05

Industry analysis

The mobile accessory market that Xplus serves is tied to smartphone shipments and replacement cycles. The company is classified under Machinery and Equipment on the exchange, but the data provider compares it with mobile phone parts companies.

Accessory orders cluster around the launch schedules of finished-goods brands, so a gap in orders shows up in quarterly revenue quickly. Because the company supplies large customers such as Samsung Electronics and online channels, customer orders carry heavy weight in its results.

Its intellectual property collaboration goods and differentiated products are the strategy the company presents to set itself apart. Still, with revenue staying around KRW 10 billion in 2024 and 2025, a clear expansion in scale has not yet appeared.

Cumulative revenue through the third quarter of 2026 rose 12.5% from the same period of 2025, but third-quarter revenue alone fell 36.3% year on year, so the quarterly picture is mixed.

Energy and port transport, the new fields presented, have demand structures far removed from accessories, so they should be assessed separately from the existing competitive landscape, including entry barriers and payback periods.
