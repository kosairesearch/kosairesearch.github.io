Nano: Catalyst Orders Expand, Recovery Test Set for 2027

Nano swung to an operating loss of KRW 4.93 billion in the third quarter of 2026, and it is targeting a profit recovery from 2027 on long-term supply contracts in North America and Europe and a new plant.

02

Business structure

Nano was founded in April 1999 and listed on KOSDAQ in May 2014. Its core business is SCR (selective catalytic reduction) denitration catalysts, which reduce nitrogen oxides generated when waste and fuel are burned.

The catalysts come in honeycomb, layered and plate forms, and the company's business summary highlights mass production of all three commercial types as a strength.

Revenue mix differs by source: one source tallies SCR denitration catalysts at 65.70%, TiO2 at 27.69%, SCR denitration equipment at 5.28% and other at 1.33%, while another tallies SCR catalysts at 41.14%, automotive parts at 37.78% and TiO2 at 21.08%.

By subsidiary, the Korean unit accounted for 82.23% of denitration catalyst sales and the Chinese unit for 17.73% as of December 2025. Main customers are industrial facilities such as power plants and steel mills.

In June 2026 the company signed a KRW 2.1 billion contract with POSCO to supply plate-type catalysts, its second supply to the same facility. It has also secured long-term supply contracts of over one year with North American global customers and a Swiss engineering firm, aimed at AI data center power equipment.

It is expanding supply of high-density catalysts for power generation engines and gas turbines. In July 2026 it announced a catalyst supply project from a leading domestic petrochemical company.

04

Earnings analysis

On a disclosed basis, annual revenue rose from KRW 66.8 billion in 2022 to KRW 88.1 billion in 2023 (up 31.8% year on year) and KRW 88.6 billion in 2024 (up 0.6%), then fell to KRW 85.7 billion in 2025 (down 3.3%). Among 2022 to 2025, 2024 was the largest year and 2022 the smallest.

Operating profit moved from an operating loss of KRW 3.55 billion in 2022 to operating profit of KRW 1.62 billion in 2023, a turn to profit, then KRW 1.66 billion in 2024 (up 2.1%) and KRW 5.53 billion in 2025 (up 233.4%), so it has risen for two consecutive years from 2024 through 2025.

The operating margin went from -5.3% in 2022 to 1.8% in 2023, 1.9% in 2024 and 6.5% in 2025. Net profit attributable to controlling shareholders was a loss of KRW 13 billion in 2022 and a loss of KRW 5.3 billion in 2023, then turned to profit of KRW 30.4 billion in 2024.

Because that 2024 figure is far above operating profit, non-operating items likely played a large role. In 2025 it fell 76.9% to KRW 7.03 billion, while net profit including non-controlling interests was KRW 4.85 billion.

The debt ratio fell from 5,421.6% in 2022 to 181.1% in 2024 and 136.3% in 2025, when total equity was KRW 39.9 billion, of which controlling shareholders held KRW 42.7 billion, and total liabilities were KRW 54.4 billion.

Operating cash flow was KRW 5.89 billion in 2022, KRW 217 million in 2023, KRW 8.11 billion in 2024 and KRW 134 million in 2025.

Quarterly, revenue of KRW 34.5 billion and operating profit of KRW 5.52 billion in the third quarter of 2025 gave way to revenue of KRW 21.8 billion (down 36.8% quarter on quarter) and operating profit of KRW 258 million (down 95.3%) in the fourth quarter.

The first quarter of 2026 recorded revenue of KRW 11 billion and an operating loss of KRW 889 million, a period in which the company said a large share of shipments failed to meet revenue recognition criteria such as performance evaluations at power plant customers and moved into the second quarter.

The second quarter rebounded to revenue of KRW 26 billion and operating profit of KRW 583 million.

The third quarter of 2026 recorded revenue of KRW 16.5 billion (down 36.6% quarter on quarter and 52.2% year on year), an operating loss of KRW 4.93 billion and a net loss attributable to controlling shareholders of KRW 8.72 billion.

Cumulative third-quarter revenue of KRW 53.4 billion, announced by the company on November 16, 2026, fell 16.4% year on year, with operating results swinging to a loss over the same period. Net loss attributable to controlling shareholders over the last four quarters totaled KRW 12.5 billion.

05

Industry analysis

Demand for denitration catalysts is described as expanding from tighter nitrogen oxide emission rules, power demand from AI data centers, and rising need for catalysts in eco-friendly power plants. This trend is cited as the backdrop for improved gross profit and operating profit.

On the supply side, the company says Korea's petrochemical catalyst market has a high share of imported products and high barriers to entry. Based on its domestic petrochemical references, the company plans to broaden into general industrial catalysts and Middle East petrochemical markets.

Compared with large listed chemical companies, Nano is a small firm specialized in denitration catalysts, so its product portfolio and revenue scale differ widely. On the positive side, regulation and data center power demand form a medium-term demand base.

On the negative side, higher raw material prices added cost pressure and Middle East geopolitical risk delayed overseas supply. Power plant catalysts are recognized as revenue only after performance evaluation, so quarterly results can swing widely.

Even when orders rise, a shift in the revenue recognition timing across a quarter boundary creates a lag in reported earnings. The pace at which industry improvement shows up in results therefore depends on customer evaluation schedules and cost trends.

06

Outlook

The company expects that from 2027, volumes from overseas orders including the Middle East will be fully recognized as revenue, and that combined with the start of the new plant, production capacity expansion will support revenue growth and margin improvement.

Growth drivers it cites are the expansion of long-term supply contracts in North America and Europe, rising demand for catalysts for power generation engines and gas turbines, and added capacity at the new plant.

For the third-quarter 2026 operating loss, the company said overseas project supply delays and temporary cost increases weighed on results. In August 2026, reports said the new plant's effects were starting to show, with catalyst orders from Middle East and domestic power plants continuing.

The June 2026 POSCO contract and the July 2026 domestic petrochemical project show the order base is broadening.

Points to confirm are first, how much 2027 overseas order volume reaches revenue, second, the timing and utilization of the new plant, third, full-year figures in the 2026 annual report, which will include fourth-quarter 2026 results, and fourth, changes in raw material prices and Middle East supply schedules.

Full-year figures can be compared with the disclosed 2025 revenue of KRW 85.7 billion and operating profit of KRW 5.53 billion. The company's profit recovery scenario depends on executing volume from 2027, so that execution is the checkpoint.

Bull factors

Long-term overseas contracts and new plant

The company says it has secured supply contracts of over one year with North American global customers and a Swiss engineering firm. It expects Middle East order volumes to be recognized as revenue from 2027. Combined with the new plant, the expanded capacity is the basis for the bull case on revenue growth.

Direction of earnings capacity has turned

The operating margin improved from -5.3% in 2022 to 6.5% in 2025. Operating profit grew in both 2024 and 2025 after the 2023 turn to profit, reaching KRW 5.53 billion in 2025, up 233.4%.

However, the third quarter of 2026 returned to an operating loss of KRW 4.93 billion, so continuity of the recovery still needs confirmation.

Broadening domestic and overseas references

In June 2026 Nano signed a contract with POSCO to supply plate-type catalysts, its second supply to the same facility. In July 2026 it announced a supply project from a leading domestic petrochemical company.

Because the domestic petrochemical catalyst market has high import shares and high entry barriers, the company says accumulated references support its push into Middle East petrochemicals.

Bear factors

Whether the 2026 loss swing persists

The third quarter of 2026 posted an operating loss of KRW 4.93 billion and a net loss attributable to controlling shareholders of KRW 8.72 billion. The first quarter of 2026 also recorded an operating loss.

Net loss attributable to controlling shareholders over the last four quarters totals KRW 12.5 billion, and cumulative revenue fell 16.4% year on year, which supports the bear case that recovery could slip.

Revenue decline and supply delays

Revenue in 2025 was KRW 85.7 billion, down 3.3% year on year. Third-quarter 2026 revenue of KRW 16.5 billion was down 52.2% from a year earlier.

Higher raw material prices and Middle East geopolitical risk delayed overseas project supply, and if those volumes start only in 2027, the revenue gap before then could be long.

Share price volatility and trading concentration

On November 18, 2026, the Korea Exchange designated Nano as an investment caution issue due to concentrated trading in a small number of accounts. On a sell basis, the share price fell 34.60% over the prior three days, and the top ten accounts accounted for 44.66% of sell-side involvement. A caution designation was also made on May 8, 2026 after a sharp price rise.

Risk factors

Order execution and revenue recognition timing

Because revenue is recognized only after performance evaluations at power plant customers, shipments that cross a quarter boundary delay reported results. The first quarter of 2026, with revenue of KRW 11 billion and an operating loss of KRW 889 million, is an example of such a carry-over. If overseas volumes cluster after 2027, quarter-to-quarter swings could widen.

Raw material costs and geopolitical risk

The company described higher raw material prices as a temporary cost factor in the third quarter. Whether that persists must be judged from cost ratios in later quarters. Geopolitical instability in the Middle East has already delayed overseas supply, so the delivery schedule could slip again.

Financial burden and short-term borrowing

Total liabilities at the end of 2025 were KRW 54.4 billion, exceeding total equity of KRW 39.9 billion, and the debt ratio was 136.3%. A disclosure of a short-term borrowing increase was filed on May 27, 2026.

If the net loss attributable to controlling shareholders of KRW 8.72 billion recorded in the third quarter of 2026 continues, reducing debt through earnings becomes harder.

Overall view

Nano is a small chemical company centered on SCR denitration catalysts. Operating profit rose from the 2023 turn to profit through 2025, and the debt ratio fell sharply.

Operating losses in the first and third quarters of 2026 mean earnings have not yet stabilized, and cumulative third-quarter revenue fell 16.4% year on year.

Positive factors include long-term supply contracts in North America and Europe, overseas volumes and new plant operations from 2027, and a broader reference list including POSCO and a domestic petrochemical company.
