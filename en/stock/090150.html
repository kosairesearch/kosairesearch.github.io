02

Business structure

iWIN is an automotive parts maker listed on KOSDAQ since October 20, 2006, and its core product is automotive seat heaters. According to the company profile, it supplies more than 90% of domestic seat heater volume and delivers to major automakers.

Overseas, it runs sales subsidiaries in the United States, Slovakia and China and operates a production plant in Vietnam. Product sales at the end of 2025 split 57.30% domestic and 42.70% export. R&D spending in 2025 was KRW 2.66 billion, or 2.29% of revenue (per a Wisereport aggregation).

At its first-half 2026 results briefing, the company said it is developing an HOD sensor that detects steering wheel hand-off, an SBR sensor for seat belt reminders, and an ECU. Its former affiliate iWIN Plus, now renamed, saw iWIN sell its entire 28.32% stake over the counter in January 2026.

The land and buildings in Gijang, Busan were sold for KRW 22.77 billion, with the final payment received in May 2026. Subsidiary iWIN Auto is scheduled to be absorbed through a merger effective December 31, 2026, and its latest fiscal-year revenue was disclosed at KRW 34.0 billion.

The company's earnings commentary cites intensifying domestic and external competition. Labor-wage talks delays at automakers are also cited as a source of revenue volatility.

04

Earnings analysis

On a disclosed annual basis, revenue rose from KRW 94.4 billion in 2022 to KRW 108.3 billion in 2023 (up 14.7% year on year) and KRW 119.3 billion in 2024 (up 10.2%), then fell to KRW 115.9 billion in 2025 (down 2.9%). Across 2022 to 2025, 2024 was the largest year and 2022 the smallest.

Operating profit went from KRW 2.26 billion in 2022 to KRW 6.35 billion in 2023 (up 181.3%) and KRW 7.9 billion in 2024 (up 24.4%), then came in at KRW 6.88 billion in 2025 (down 13.0%).

Operating margin was 2.4% in 2022, 5.9% in 2023, 6.6% in 2024 and 5.9% in 2025, so it held in the mid-single digits even as revenue declined in 2025.

Net profit attributable to controlling shareholders was a loss of KRW 3.62 billion in 2022, a loss of KRW 35.3 billion in 2023 (losses continued and widened), and a loss of KRW 3.9 billion in 2024 (losses continued and narrowed), before turning to a profit of KRW 8.64 billion in 2025.

Net profit including non-controlling interests was KRW 8.95 billion in 2025, which differs from the controlling-shareholder figure. In 2023 operating profit improved while the controlling-shareholder net loss widened to KRW 35.3 billion, so the direction at the operating and net levels diverged.

At year-end 2025, total equity was KRW 50.3 billion, of which KRW 46.2 billion belonged to controlling shareholders, and total liabilities were KRW 81.8 billion. The debt ratio moved from 218.0% in 2024 to 162.6% in 2025.

Operating cash flow was KRW 2.51 billion in 2022, KRW 6.61 billion in 2023, KRW 3.98 billion in 2024 and KRW 9.54 billion in 2025.

Quarterly revenue was KRW 28.7 billion in the third quarter of 2025, KRW 27.1 billion in the fourth quarter (down 5.7% quarter on quarter), KRW 27.5 billion in the first quarter of 2026, KRW 29.1 billion in the second quarter (up 6.0%), and KRW 27.8 billion in the third quarter (down 4.6% quarter on quarter and down 3.3% year on year), staying in a range of roughly KRW 27 billion to 29 billion.

Operating profit fell from KRW 2.14 billion in the third quarter of 2025 to KRW 485 million in the fourth quarter (down 77.4%), then recovered to KRW 1.58 billion in the first quarter of 2026, KRW 3.96 billion in the second quarter (up 150.9%) and KRW 2.93 billion in the third quarter (down 26.1% quarter on quarter, up 36.8% year on year).

Net profit attributable to controlling shareholders was KRW 2.13 billion, KRW 5.66 billion, KRW 2.29 billion, KRW 14.3 billion and KRW 205 million across the same quarters.

Of the KRW 22.4 billion total for the last four quarters, KRW 14.3 billion came in the second quarter of 2026, so most of the total sits in one quarter. The company attributed first-half profitability gains to cost reduction and the sale of tangible assets.

With operating profit of KRW 2.93 billion and controlling-shareholder net profit of KRW 205 million in the third quarter, the gap between the two lines widened, and non-operating and tax items are worth examining in the next periodic report.

05

Industry analysis

The end market depends on domestic automaker production volumes and labor-wage negotiation timing. The company's earnings commentary attributes the revenue decline to seat heaters becoming standard options, which eased seasonal swings.

When the option becomes standard fitment, seasonal order surges shrink and volume tracks vehicle output more closely. The 6% year-on-year revenue decline in the first half of 2026 can be read in the same context.

The company's direction is to widen its sensor and electronic control lineup in line with growth in autonomous driving and advanced driver-assistance systems (ADAS). On competition, the earnings commentary cites intensifying domestic and external rivalry.

Overseas sales subsidiaries and the Vietnam plant support an export share of 42.70% as of year-end 2025. In a comparison with the automotive parts industry (137 companies, annual basis for 2025) compiled by AlphaSquare, iWIN's operating margin is above the industry average.

However, its market capitalization and revenue scale sit well below the top of the industry, so margin comparisons alone do not capture business size. A product portfolio centered on seat heating is narrower than larger parts makers that have extended into electrification and thermal systems.

Outlook

The company has publicly outlined three directions. First, a focus on cost competitiveness and profitability; it announced that first-half 2026 operating profit rose 30% and cited cost reduction and the sale of tangible assets as drivers.

Second, new product development and certification; it is advancing an HOD sensor, an SBR sensor and an ECU, and its CEO said the company has completed development and certification of new products to secure future growth drivers.

Third, shareholder returns; the company has not paid dividends because of insufficient distributable reserves, but says it will review dividend and other policies based on its improved financial structure.

On structure, subsidiary iWIN Auto will be absorbed on December 31, 2026, with iWIN surviving under its existing name and taking over the business as is. Objection notices closed on November 24, 2026, and the creditor objection period runs from November 26, 2026 to December 30, 2026.

The new shares are scheduled to list on January 17, 2027. The next periodic report is the 2026 annual report, where the fourth-quarter 2026 earnings structure and any dividend decision will be checkable.

Whether new product certification translates into revenue growth remains a company statement without disclosed quantification.

Bull factors

Recovery in operating margin

Operating margin rose from 2.4% in 2022 to 5.9% in 2023 and 6.6% in 2024, and held at 5.9% in 2025. Operating profit in the third quarter of 2026 was KRW 2.93 billion, up 36.8% year on year. The company cited cost reduction as a profitability driver in the first half of 2026. The recovery at the operating level occurred even as revenue declined.

Improving financial structure

The debt ratio fell from 218.0% in 2024 to 162.6% in 2025. Total equity at year-end 2025 was KRW 50.3 billion, up from KRW 44.8 billion in 2023. Operating cash flow rose from KRW 2.51 billion in 2022 to KRW 9.54 billion in 2025. Stronger operating cash generation alongside lower leverage is the basis for the improved financial capacity.

New product pipeline

The company said it is developing an HOD sensor, an SBR sensor and an ECU. Its CEO said the company has completed development and certification of new products to secure growth drivers. The lineup targets autonomous driving and ADAS growth, which offers room to expand beyond seat heaters. The company has not quantified when certified products will contribute to revenue.

Bear factors

Declining revenue trend

Revenue peaked at KRW 119.3 billion in 2024 and declined to KRW 115.9 billion in 2025 (down 2.9%). First-half 2026 revenue fell 6% year on year, and third-quarter 2026 revenue of KRW 27.8 billion was down 3.3% year on year.

The company attributes this to seat heaters becoming standard options, which eased seasonal swings. Whether the decline is structural or temporary should be assessed from revenue trends in the next periodic report.

Earnings concentration and reliance on asset sales

Net profit attributable to controlling shareholders in the second quarter of 2026 was KRW 14.3 billion, well above that quarter's operating profit of KRW 3.96 billion. The company cited the sale of tangible assets as one factor behind first-half profitability gains.

If the effect of asset sales does not recur, the quarterly earnings base could be lower. This is why operating-level recovery and net-level results need to be viewed separately.

Cumulative losses and a short profit record

Net losses attributable to controlling shareholders ran from 2022 to 2024, with a KRW 35.3 billion loss in 2023. After the 2025 return to profit, net profit attributable to controlling shareholders fell to KRW 205 million in the third quarter of 2026. The track record of sustained earnings is still short. The lingering effect of past losses on the capital structure can be seen in the debt ratio trend.

Risk factors

New product adoption delay

The HOD sensor, SBR sensor and ECU are products the company says it is developing. The CEO said development and certification of new products are complete, but the timing at which certification leads to automaker production awards and revenue growth has not been quantified.

If adoption schedules slip, the revenue contribution needed to offset lower seat heater volume could also be delayed.

Convertible bonds and dilution

Six convertible bond issues are outstanding, and conversion price adjustment disclosures followed on May 11, 2026 and July 24, 2026. A conversion price adjustment can increase the number of shares issued on conversion, which is a dilution factor to watch.

The 10-to-1 share consolidation in 2026 reduced total common shares outstanding to about 4.19 million, which should be read alongside the conversion terms.

Dividend delay and shareholder returns

No dividend was paid because distributable reserves were insufficient, and the company said it will review future dividends based on improved financial structure. The current dividend yield is below the industry average.

The largest shareholder and related parties held 29.69% as of March 26, 2026, and the free float ratio is 63.35%. Until a review result is disclosed, shareholder returns remain limited relative to earnings.

Overall view

iWIN, which supplies most of Korea's seat heater volume, posted a return to annual net profit attributable to controlling shareholders in 2025 and higher operating profit in the first half of 2026.

Revenue, however, has declined from the KRW 119.3 billion peak in 2024 to KRW 115.9 billion in 2025 and KRW 27.8 billion in the third quarter of 2026. The company explains this as the result of seat heaters becoming standard options, which leveled seasonal demand.

On the strength side, operating margin rose from 2.4% in 2022 to 5.9% in 2025, and the debt ratio fell from 218.0% in 2024 to 162.6% in 2025.

On the weakness side, earnings are clearly concentrated by quarter: of the KRW 22.4 billion net profit attributable to controlling shareholders over the last four quarters, KRW 14.3 billion came in the second quarter of 2026, and the third quarter fell to KRW 205 million.

Because first-half profitability included the effect of asset sales, whether that effect recurs is the next point to check. The merger of subsidiary iWIN Auto, with a December 31, 2026 effective date and new shares scheduled to list on January 17, 2027, and the 2026 annual report are the next checkpoints.
