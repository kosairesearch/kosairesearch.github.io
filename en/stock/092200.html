02

Business structure

Dae-Il manufactures and sells automotive parts, heavy equipment parts and motorcycle parts.

Its exchange sector classification is transportation equipment and parts, and the operating business is automotive and heavy equipment parts manufacturing rather than the shipbuilding label used in the input, so the description below follows that structure.

On a cumulative basis through Q3 2024, automotive parts accounted for about KRW 469.6 billion, or roughly 84.1% of sales, with heavy equipment parts at about KRW 64.7 billion (12.1%) and motorcycle parts at about KRW 1.7 billion.

Its core products are electrified components such as reducers and e-Axles, and a corporate profile based on cumulative data through Q3 2025 described their production share as rising quickly. Production and supply through its U.S. subsidiary in Kentucky were cited as a driver of earnings improvement.

In a May 2023 report, the company said its North American unit would absorb KRW 60 billion of capex through 2024 and could generate about KRW 150 billion of sales in 2025. Brokerage commentary pointed to orders for Hyundai Mobis and Hyundai Transys as the base for North American sales.

As a new business, its subsidiary Dae-Il Advanced Materials, in which it holds 40%, was set up in April 2024 to pursue sodium-based battery materials and ESS materials.

At the March 2026 annual general meeting, shareholders approved articles changes adding physical AI R&D and manufacturing and sales of robots and robot parts to the business purpose. Customer timing for electrified vehicle investment and U.S. production schedules are the main variables for sales.

04

Earnings analysis

On a reported basis, sales moved from KRW 695.9 billion in 2022 to KRW 728.7 billion in 2023 (up 4.7% year on year), KRW 719.1 billion in 2024 (down 1.3%), and KRW 757.3 billion in 2025 (up 5.3%). Among 2022 to 2025, 2025 was the largest year for sales and 2022 the smallest.

Operating profit rose from KRW 31.3 billion in 2022 to KRW 36.1 billion in 2023 (up 15.4%), the peak of the period, and then fell to KRW 21.8 billion in 2024 (down 39.5%) and KRW 18.6 billion in 2025 (down 15.0%), declining in two consecutive years.

The operating margin moved from 4.5% in 2022 to 5.0% in 2023, 3.0% in 2024 and 2.4% in 2025, so margin compression continued even in 2025, when sales grew. Sales expansion alongside a thinner margin suggests rising cost pressure, but the reported figures alone do not pin down the specific causes.

Parent net income went from KRW 3.04 billion in 2022 to KRW 36.7 billion in 2023 (up 1,107.6%), KRW 14.2 billion in 2024 (down 61.3%), and turned into a KRW 10.5 billion net loss in 2025.

Net income including non-controlling interests followed a similar path, at KRW 4.74 billion in 2022, KRW 21 billion in 2023, KRW 16.6 billion in 2024 and a KRW 4.77 billion net loss in 2025, and the two measures are defined differently and should be read separately.

By quarter, Q3 2025 showed sales of KRW 188.8 billion, operating profit of KRW 4.71 billion and parent net income of KRW 1.68 billion. Q4 2025 sales fell to KRW 168.8 billion (down 10.6% quarter on quarter), with an operating loss of KRW 2.73 billion and a parent net loss of KRW 12.9 billion.

Q1 2026 sales were KRW 165.2 billion with operating profit of KRW 2.82 billion, a return to profit. Q2 2026 sales rose to KRW 196.6 billion (up 19.0% quarter on quarter), operating profit reached KRW 8.73 billion (up 209.4%), and parent net income was KRW 3.3 billion.

Q3 2026 sales were KRW 172 billion (down 12.5% quarter on quarter and down 8.9% year on year), operating profit was KRW 4.9 billion (down 43.9% quarter on quarter, up 4.1% year on year), and the parent recorded a KRW 6.21 billion net loss, a swing from the prior-year profit.

Operating cash flow was KRW 13.8 billion in 2022, -KRW 7.78 billion in 2023, KRW 9.4 billion in 2024 and KRW 9.74 billion in 2025, positive since 2024. In 2022, a foreign exchange translation loss of about KRW 14 billion on foreign-currency borrowings was recorded in non-operating items.

05

Industry analysis

Dae-Il's core market is electrified components for automakers. Automotive parts made up about 84.1% of cumulative sales through Q3 2024, so automakers' EV production plans feed directly into its results.

On a cumulative basis through Q3 2025, the company said the production share of electrified components such as reducers and e-Axles was rising quickly. Growth in U.S. EV demand and local production expanded the role of its Kentucky operation.

If EV demand slows or automakers cut output, order volumes and utilization could both be affected.

Cycloidal reducers for robot joints and drive units share technical foundations with electrified components, and a 2025 report said talks with a humanoid robot maker on reducer supply were under way, a stage described as early.

Among electrified component suppliers, customer access and production footprint shape competitiveness. The cycle position of end demand depends on EV demand trends, and the company's pattern of sales growth alongside a thinner margin in 2024 and 2025 illustrates that pressure.

08

Bull factors

Electrified parts mix and U.S. local production

Automotive parts made up about 84.1% of cumulative sales through Q3 2024, and cumulative data through Q3 2025 described a rising share of electrified components such as reducers and e-Axles. Kentucky local production was cited as an earnings driver.

If the electrification shift continues, this structure could broaden the sales base, which is the core of the bullish case.

Operating profit stayed positive through 2026

Operating profit turned positive at KRW 2.82 billion in Q1 2026, reached KRW 8.73 billion in Q2 (up 209.4% quarter on quarter), and was KRW 4.9 billion in Q3, so all three quarters of 2026 were profitable at the operating level.

Compared with the KRW 2.73 billion operating loss in Q4 2025, this marks a shift at the operating line. Recovery at that level is the starting point for earnings repair.

Robotics and new business foundation

At the March 2026 annual general meeting, physical AI R&D and manufacturing and sales of robots and robot parts were added to the business purpose. A 2025 report said talks with a humanoid robot maker on cycloidal reducer supply were under way, though at an early stage. The widening business scope is a bullish factor.

Bear factors

Continuing parent net losses

The parent recorded a KRW 10.5 billion net loss in 2025, and the last four quarters total a KRW 16.5 billion parent net loss. In Q3 2026 the parent posted a KRW 6.21 billion net loss, a swing from prior-year profit.

The fact that operating-level profit is not carrying through to parent earnings is the core of the bearish case.

Margin erosion and sales decline

The operating margin fell from 4.5% in 2022 to 2.4% in 2025. Q3 2026 sales were KRW 172 billion, down 8.9% from a year earlier. Q3 operating profit fell 43.9% from Q2, which suggests quarterly profitability has not yet stabilized.

Balance sheet burden and FX exposure

Total liabilities of KRW 539 billion at end-2025 are about three times total equity of KRW 179.4 billion. The debt ratio eased to 300.4% but remains high. As the 2022 foreign exchange translation loss on foreign-currency borrowings showed, currency exposure is also a source of earnings volatility.

10

Risk factors

Financial leverage

Total liabilities are roughly three times total equity, so interest costs and refinancing needs weigh on earnings. A drop in operating profit or weaker operating cash flow could narrow financial room quickly. Parent shareholders' equity of KRW 142.2 billion gives a limited capital cushion.

Exchange rates and foreign-currency borrowing

In 2022, a foreign exchange translation loss of about KRW 14 billion on foreign-currency borrowings was booked in non-operating items and moved reported results. As overseas facilities and borrowings grow, currency swings have a larger effect on net income. Reported earnings can diverge from operating performance depending on the direction of exchange rates.

Execution risk in new businesses

Talks on robot reducer supply were described as early-stage, and the recycled feedstock business had a 2026 commercial production target in a 2025 report. Investment costs may arrive before new-business revenue. Recovery in the core business could be delayed if it coincides with new-business spending.
