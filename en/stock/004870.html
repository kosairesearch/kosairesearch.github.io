02

Business structure

Tway Holdings Incorporation is a holding company that, according to its website, was established in 1945 and listed in 1977. According to the Wisereport company profile, PHC piles account for 100% of its sales mix, so the core business reads as concrete pile manufacturing and sales.

The 2025 annual revenue of KRW 5.03 billion is consistent with the scale of this business. The same profile notes that pile sales fell on a slowdown in construction, but profitability improved as supply shrank through a business conversion and a plant closure, according to its first-quarter 2026 comment.

The company's business purpose still includes aviation-related agency services, in-flight meal production and sales, and travel product sales, so its link to aviation remains in its articles of incorporation. The more material asset is its airline subsidiary.

Shareholders approved at the March 31, 2026 general meeting a change of the airline's name to Trinity Airways Co., Ltd., and the carrier is described as the second-largest low-cost carrier in Korea.

The airline's 2025 annual revenue was KRW 1,798.2 billion, up about 17% year on year, while its operating loss widened to KRW 265.4 billion. Reports said its debt ratio at year-end 2025 exceeded 3,400%, more than double those of rival low-cost carriers such as Jeju Air, Jin Air and Air Busan.

The largest shareholder changed to Sono International in June 2025 and held 46.26% as of June 2026. Sources differ on the stake relationship: Wikipedia describes the airline as a major subsidiary, while Wisereport lists it as an affiliate with a 17.96% stake as of December 2025.

04

Earnings analysis

On a disclosed annual basis, revenue went from KRW 11.5 billion in 2022 to KRW 7.77 billion in 2023 (down 32.2% year on year), KRW 5.24 billion in 2024 (down 32.6%), and KRW 5.03 billion in 2025 (down 4.0%).

Revenue has declined for three years in a row from 2023 to 2025, and 2022 is the largest year in the 2022 to 2025 period.

Operating losses were KRW 1.86 billion in 2022, KRW 3.01 billion in 2023, KRW 5.22 billion in 2024 and KRW 3.61 billion in 2025, and the operating margin moved from -16.2% in 2022 to -99.6% in 2024 before improving to -71.6% in 2025.

In 2025 the operating loss shrank even as revenue fell 4.0%, which suggests some cost adjustment contributed.

Net income attributable to controlling shareholders fell from KRW 53.1 billion in 2022 to KRW 4.56 billion in 2023 (down 91.4%), turned into a net loss of KRW 20.8 billion in 2024, and widened to a net loss of KRW 55.7 billion in 2025.

The KRW 53.1 billion profit in 2022 is far larger than that year's operating loss of KRW 1.86 billion, which points to a large contribution from non-operating items. Net income including non-controlling interests is disclosed at the same amount as attributable net income in each year.

Operating cash flow was negative every year: KRW -1.73 billion in 2022, KRW -1.38 billion in 2023, KRW -3.52 billion in 2024 and KRW -4.02 billion in 2025, the largest outflow in the period.

At the end of 2025, total equity was KRW 94.2 billion, all attributable to controlling shareholders, against total liabilities of KRW 25 billion, a debt ratio of 26.5% (26.0% in 2024).

Quarterly revenue fell from KRW 1.05 billion in the third quarter of 2025 to KRW 560 million in the fourth quarter (down 46.7% quarter on quarter), then rose to KRW 1.09 billion in the first quarter of 2026, KRW 1.28 billion in the second and KRW 2.49 billion in the third, which was up 93.9% quarter on quarter and 136.8% year on year.

Operating losses narrowed from KRW 624 million in the third quarter of 2025 to KRW 31.38 million in the third quarter of 2026, yet the loss has continued for five consecutive quarters.

Attributable net income swung from a loss of KRW 28.4 billion in Q3 2025 to a profit of KRW 2.42 billion in Q4 2025, a loss of KRW 36.4 billion in Q1 2026, a profit of KRW 2.74 billion in Q2 2026 and a loss of KRW 13.5 billion in Q3 2026.

The last four quarters total a net loss attributable to controlling shareholders of KRW 44.7 billion.

Operating-level results are in the hundreds of millions of won while attributable results run to tens or hundreds of billions, so non-operating items and the way subsidiary results are reflected appear to drive most of the volatility.

05

Industry analysis

The airline subsidiary that drives the holding's results faced rising cost pressure in 2026. Geopolitical tension in the Middle East pushed oil prices sharply higher, and a March 2026 report said brokerage analysts widely expected no earnings improvement in 2026 because of jet fuel price volatility.

A weaker won also raises aircraft lease and maintenance costs. In 2025 the subsidiary expanded medium- and long-haul routes to Europe and the Americas by deploying large aircraft, which raised fixed costs, according to the company.

Among low-cost carriers, its debt ratio is reported at more than twice those of rivals, which is treated as a weakness in financial capacity. On the demand side, cargo is emerging as a new growth engine: cargo volume rose 92% from 18,000 tons in 2024 to 34,000 tons in 2025 after the introduction of wide-body aircraft.

In piles, construction slowdown is weighing on demand, while the business conversion and plant closure have cut supply and improved profitability, according to Wisereport. Because pile demand is tied to construction activity, the pace of recovery remains uncertain.

06

Outlook

No full-year guidance from the company was found in the reviewed sources, so the outlook rests on the subsidiary's fleet, route and funding plans.

A March 2026 report said the subsidiary holds a total of KRW 373.3 billion in funding including two 2025 rights offerings of KRW 210 billion and KRW 90 billion in perpetual bonds, and planned a KRW 73.3 billion rights subscription on March 11 and 12.

The funds were said to go to operating costs such as fuel, handling fees and aircraft leases. On the fleet side, the A330-900 is the core plan, with about 37% more seats and 14% lower fuel burn per seat, to support a profit-focused route network.

The airline plans to run its China routes and seasonal charter routes flexibly and cut capacity on off-season Southeast Asia and Oceania routes. Its cargo strategy involves more direct contracts to cut agency fees and a higher share of premium special cargo.

The company is building a hangar at Incheon International Airport with about KRW 150 billion of investment, described as the first in-house maintenance facility among Korean low-cost carriers, with capacity for about 70 aircraft a year.

At the holding level, the March 2026 general meeting approved raising the authorized share count and the convertible and warrant bond limits substantially. A free capital reduction listed on July 24, 2026, reducing the share count, and that change will flow into per-share metrics going forward.

The 2026 annual report, due by late March 2027, will confirm fourth-quarter results and how subsidiary results were reflected.

Bull factors

Quarterly revenue recovery

Revenue rose for three consecutive quarters from the first quarter through the third quarter of 2026, and the third-quarter figure of KRW 2.49 billion was up 93.9% quarter on quarter and 136.8% year on year. Over the same span the operating loss narrowed to KRW 31.38 million in the third quarter of 2026. Improvement in quarterly operating results is cited as the core bullish argument.

Fleet and route restructuring at the subsidiary

The subsidiary plans to add the A330-900, which offers about 37% more seats and 14% lower fuel use per seat, to build scale. It is running China and seasonal charter routes flexibly and expanding routes from regional airports.

The hangar project at Incheon, costing about KRW 150 billion, is aimed at improving on-time performance and safety credibility.

Cargo growth and capital reinforcement

Cargo volume rose 92% from 18,000 tons in 2024 to 34,000 tons in 2025. Combined with KRW 210 billion in two 2025 rights offerings and KRW 90 billion in perpetual bonds, the March 2026 rights subscription brings total funding to KRW 373.3 billion. The holding's debt ratio at the end of 2025 was 26.5%, low compared with the subsidiary's reported level.

Bear factors

Accumulated net losses attributable to shareholders

Net losses attributable to controlling shareholders were KRW 20.8 billion in 2024 and KRW 55.7 billion in 2025, two straight years of losses. Over the last four quarters the total was a KRW 44.7 billion loss, so no earnings-based price multiple can be calculated.

Bears point out that even as operating losses shrink, the swings in attributable results make earnings capacity hard to gauge.

Operating losses and cash outflow

Operating losses were recorded in each year from 2022 to 2025, with KRW 3.61 billion in 2025. Operating cash flow was KRW -4.02 billion in 2025, the largest outflow of the four years. Revenue fell from KRW 11.5 billion in 2022 to KRW 5.03 billion in 2025, which bears read as a sign of weak core business strength.

Subsidiary financial strain and dilution

The subsidiary recorded an operating loss of KRW 265.4 billion in 2025 and a debt ratio above 3,400% at year-end. At the March 2026 general meeting, the authorized share count was raised from 500 million to 1 billion and the convertible and warrant bond limits were each raised from KRW 50 billion to KRW 500 billion. Jet fuel price spikes and brokerage views that earnings will not improve in 2026 add to the pressure.

Risk factors

Subsidiary funding and share dilution

The subsidiary has built funding through rights offerings and perpetual bonds, but further raises may be needed if operating losses persist. The much larger limits for convertible and warrant bonds open the possibility of a higher share count.

Because holding-level results depend on the stake relationship with the subsidiary, this process becomes a source of volatility.

Fuel price, currency and demand swings

Jet fuel prices and the won-dollar exchange rate directly affect lease and maintenance costs. In 2025, a weaker won was cited as a factor that raised aircraft lease and maintenance expenses. Instability in the Middle East could also weigh on passenger demand and route operations.

Ownership structure and tradable shares

The largest shareholder holds 46.26%, which limits the share of freely tradable stock. The free capital reduction listed on July 24, 2026 reduced the share count and changes the base for per-share comparisons. The free float ratio was a little above half as of June 2026.

Overall view

Tway Holdings Incorporation is a holding company with an airline subsidiary, and although holding-level revenue is small, much of its earnings volatility comes from subsidiary results and non-operating items.

Operating losses continued on a disclosed basis from 2022 through 2025, and attributable net losses accumulated in 2024 and 2025. Holding-level revenue rose for three straight quarters in 2026 and the operating loss narrowed, but attributable net income alternated between profit and loss.

Bullish factors include the quarterly revenue recovery, the subsidiary's fleet replacement and route restructuring, cargo expansion and capital reinforcement.

Bearish factors include the subsidiary's high debt ratio and large operating loss, higher jet fuel prices and currency pressure, and possible dilution from expanded convertible and warrant bond limits.

The share price trading below book value per share and the absence of a dividend are also facts to weigh alongside the data. The next checkpoint is the 2026 annual report, followed by the first-quarter 2027 quarterly report.
