Report overview

Revenue Recovers, Losses Persist

MediaZen's 2025 revenue rose 89.6%, but operating losses have continued for three straight quarters through Q3 2026, so whether profitability recovers is the next checkpoint.

  1. 1

    Revenue reached KRW 22.8 billion in 2025, up 89.6% from the prior year and the largest year in 2022–2025. Operating loss narrowed to KRW 2.23 billion from KRW 4.67 billion in 2024.

  2. 2

    Q3 2026 revenue was KRW 3.72 billion, up 78.2% year on year, yet the company posted an operating loss of KRW 1.07 billion, its third straight loss-making quarter.

  3. 3

    Attributable net losses over the last four quarters total KRW 5.2 billion, and the debt ratio stood at 153.5% at end-2025, up from 85.7% in 2022.

  4. 4

    Automotive voice systems account for about half of first-half 2026 revenue, while public-sector AI projects and kiosk exports to Japan are being pursued as new growth lines.

  5. 5

    The 2024 trading halt and control dispute keep governance and trading liquidity on the list of items to track.

02

Business structure

MediaZen was founded in June 2000 and listed on KOSDAQ in November 2019 as a voice recognition and AI solutions company. JobKorea's breakdown as of March 2026 puts automotive voice system development at 51%, in-car voice solution sales at 22%, other at 16%, and AI solution sales and development at 11%.

Another JobKorea page, on the same reference date, shows a different split of 52%, 38%, 9% and 1%, so segment weights vary by source. WiseReport's 2025 data shows voice recognition services at 81.66% domestic and 18.34% export, while other revenue was 87.02% export.

The company says its automotive voice business earns steady revenue on the basis of trust built with customers. New business lines include a large language model summary solution for AICC, a newly launched AI English education service, and an unmanned storage kiosk.

At the March 2026 shareholder meeting, AI hardware manufacturing was added to the articles of incorporation, and the company is pursuing kiosk exports to Japan after obtaining Japanese PSE certification.

In the public sector, it is developing an AI platform for the Korea Fair Trade Commission's unfair terms review and an AI system supporting subcontracting contracts, with support from MSIT and NIPA.

Competitively, the same IT and software sector includes large platform companies such as NAVER and Kakao and large SI firms such as Samsung SDS and LG CNS, so MediaZen's scale and customer structure differ markedly.

04

Earnings analysis

On an annual basis, revenue was KRW 17.4 billion in 2022, KRW 12.8 billion in 2023 (down 26.5% year on year), KRW 12 billion in 2024 (down 5.8%), and KRW 22.8 billion in 2025 (up 89.6%).

For the 2025 increase, the company cited improved profitability from higher service and product sales in a March 2026 filing on a standalone basis.

Operating loss widened from KRW 2.04 billion in 2022 to KRW 5.27 billion in 2023, then narrowed to KRW 4.67 billion in 2024 and KRW 2.23 billion in 2025, and the company has reported losses in each of the four years from 2022 to 2025.

The operating margin was -11.7% in 2022, -41.3% in 2023, -38.9% in 2024 and -9.8% in 2025, with 2025 the smallest loss rate of the period.

Net losses attributable to controlling shareholders were KRW 1.86 billion in 2022, KRW 5.46 billion in 2023, KRW 4.18 billion in 2024 and KRW 2.72 billion in 2025, and the net loss including non-controlling interests shows the same levels for those years.

On a standalone basis compiled by WiseReport, R&D spending was KRW 2.09 billion in 2023 (16.42% of revenue), KRW 1.98 billion in 2024 (16.44%) and KRW 1.67 billion in 2025 (7.32%).

Quarterly, revenue went from KRW 2.08 billion with an operating loss of KRW 735 million in Q3 2025 to KRW 7.75 billion in Q4 2025 (up 271.8% from the prior quarter), with an operating profit of KRW 369 million and attributable net income of KRW 48.22 million.

Q1 2026 revenue was KRW 3.36 billion with an operating loss of KRW 1.95 billion, and Q2 2026 revenue was KRW 2.79 billion with an operating loss of KRW 2.06 billion.

Q3 2026 revenue was KRW 3.72 billion (up 33.0% from the prior quarter and up 78.2% year on year), and the operating loss of KRW 1.07 billion was smaller than in Q2 but larger than a year earlier. Attributable net losses over the last four quarters total KRW 5.2 billion.

On cash flow, operating cash outflow was -KRW 4.71 billion in 2025, the largest outflow since 2022, and at end-2025 total liabilities were KRW 24.2 billion against total equity of KRW 15.8 billion. A Korean business daily identified receivables and bad-debt allowances as a burden on cash flow.

05

Industry analysis

The voice AI market is moving along two tracks. One is automotive voice recognition, where orders are placed around automakers' development schedules, which makes quarterly revenue prone to swings.

According to a Korean newspaper report, automotive voice system development accounted for roughly half of MediaZen's first-half 2026 revenue of KRW 6.16 billion. The other is the adoption of generative AI in the public and corporate sectors.

The company held a workshop with ETRI and the Korea Fair Trade Commission on AI adoption in public administration and has framed civil service response and voice-based services as research directions.

In August 2025 it was selected as part of the NC AI consortium in the government's K-AI national LLM program, taking on LLM development and demonstration for the public sector.

Among listed peers in the same sector, large platform and SI companies such as NAVER, Kakao, Samsung SDS, LG CNS and Hyundai Autoever are far ahead in capital and headcount.

MediaZen is a small company specialized in voice technology, and its own profile highlights development of 21 language packs, technology verification through the IQS index and mass-production experience. A speech pronunciation evaluation patent was registered in May 2025.

Compared with large platform companies, however, its scale and financial capacity are limited, which bounds its position within the sector. Quarterly revenue moving from KRW 2.08 billion in Q3 2025 to KRW 7.75 billion in Q4 2025 and KRW 3.36 billion in Q1 2026 also shows the volatility of its business structure.

06

Outlook

According to a Korean newspaper report, backlog stood at about KRW 8.21 billion in the 2026 half-year report. Because revenue recognition depends on delivery, inspection and customer schedules, the key question is how much of this backlog converts into quarterly revenue.

The Ghana university equipment supply contract was amended in August, reducing the contract amount to about KRW 4.46 billion, and some payment collection issues remain. For the kiosk business, Japanese PSE certification has been obtained, but sales contracts and repeat orders still require separate confirmation.

In public-sector AI, the company is developing an AI platform for the Korea Fair Trade Commission's unfair terms review and an AI system supporting subcontracting contracts, and is focusing on upgrading public and industrial AI services that combine voice recognition with generative AI, as reported in July 2026.

In March 2026, Yoo Young-geun joined Song Min-gyu as a co-CEO, and the company explained that the appointment was made for management continuity and financial stability.

The items to check next are the change in backlog during Q4 2026, the progress of Ghana contract payment collection, and whether a Japan kiosk sales contract is signed. After that, the 2026 annual report will show how the operating margin moved from -9.8% in 2025.

Bull factors

Top-line recovery

Revenue reached KRW 22.8 billion in 2025, up 89.6%, and Q3 2026 revenue of KRW 3.72 billion rose 78.2% year on year. Q4 2025 recorded revenue of KRW 7.75 billion and an operating profit of KRW 369 million, a swing to profit. Whether revenue growth carries across quarters is the next point to confirm.

Narrowing operating losses

Operating loss narrowed from KRW 5.27 billion in 2023 to KRW 2.23 billion in 2025. The Q3 2026 operating loss of KRW 1.07 billion was smaller than the KRW 2.06 billion posted in Q2 2026. The operating margin improved from -41.3% in 2023 to -9.8% in 2025.

Public-sector AI expansion

In August 2025 the company was selected as part of the NC AI consortium in the government's K-AI national LLM program, handling LLM development and demonstration for the public sector. An AI platform for the Korea Fair Trade Commission's unfair terms review is being developed with NIPA support. These efforts aim to diversify revenue sources beyond automotive voice systems.

Bear factors

Persistent losses

The company has reported losses in each year from 2022 to 2025, and net losses attributable to controlling shareholders fell from KRW 5.46 billion in 2023 to KRW 2.72 billion in 2025. Profit appeared only in Q4 2025 in the disclosed period.

Losses have continued for three quarters into 2026, and attributable net losses over the last four quarters total KRW 5.2 billion.

Thinner financial cushion

The debt ratio rose from 85.7% in 2022 to 153.5% in 2025, and total liabilities of KRW 24.2 billion at end-2025 exceed total equity of KRW 15.8 billion. Operating cash outflow of -KRW 4.71 billion in 2025 was the largest in the period. The company faces continued losses with a thinner financial cushion.

Revenue volatility and contract risk

Quarterly revenue fell 56.6% from KRW 7.75 billion in Q4 2025 to KRW 3.36 billion in Q1 2026. The Ghana contract amount was reduced to about KRW 4.46 billion in an August amendment, and some payment collection issues remain. Receivables are cited as a burden on cash flow.

10

Risk factors

Governance and trading risk

Trading was fully suspended after the exchange designated the company for a listing-qualification review in October 2024, and trading resumed in June 2025. A control dispute between the founder side and the largest shareholder led to embezzlement and breach-of-trust complaints and litigation, as reported in 2025. The March 2026 shareholder meeting moved the company to a co-CEO structure.

Financial and cash flow risk

At end-2025, total liabilities were KRW 24.2 billion against total equity of KRW 15.8 billion, and the debt ratio was 153.5%. Operating cash outflow of -KRW 4.71 billion in 2025 means that continued losses could raise the need for additional funding. The balance sheet and cash flow statement in the 2026 annual report are where this will be checked.

Business and contract risk

The kiosk export to Japan has PSE certification, but sales contracts and repeat orders still need separate confirmation. The reduced Ghana contract amount and collection problems are sources of volatility in overseas revenue.

Automotive voice systems account for about half of first-half revenue, which makes the company sensitive to automaker order flow.

12

Overall view

MediaZen's 2025 revenue rose 89.6% to KRW 22.8 billion, confirming a recovery in scale, and Q3 2026 revenue rose 78.2% year on year. Operating losses, however, have been reported in every year from 2022 to 2025 and in each of the three quarters from Q1 to Q3 2026.

The 2025 operating loss of KRW 2.23 billion was smaller than the KRW 5.27 billion of 2023, but the only profitable quarter in the disclosed period is Q4 2025. On the financial side, the debt ratio of 153.5% is above the 2022 level, and operating cash outflow was -KRW 4.71 billion in 2025.

The business is anchored by automotive voice revenue, with public-sector AI and kiosk exports as added lines. The next checkpoints are how quickly the KRW 8.21 billion backlog converts, whether the Ghana contract payments are collected, and where the operating margin lands in the 2026 annual report.
