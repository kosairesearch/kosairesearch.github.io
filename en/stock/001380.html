Seat-parts recovery meets a Q3 2026 loss

SG Global's seat-parts-centered business grew revenue and net income in the first half of 2026, but returned to an operating loss of KRW 257 million and a net loss attributable to controlling shareholders of KRW 715 million in Q3 2026.

02

Business structure

SG Global is a KOSPI-listed automotive parts maker founded in 1954, engaged in the manufacture and sale of automotive seat trim covers and car seats. Wisereport's company profile tallies the main product revenue mix as 75.21% seat parts, 13.35% seats, 6.71% display, and 4.73% other, without showing the base year.

Seat-parts revenue is directly linked to automaker production volumes, and in 2025 lower output and lower unit supply prices reduced sales. In the first half of 2026, higher automaker production and stable order intake supported seat-parts revenue.

The seat business improved on higher sales of premium vehicles and SUVs. The display business makes display components on a processing basis and recorded continued operating losses in 2025, which weighed on overall results.

Production is handled by two unlisted consolidated subsidiaries: Choongnam Vietnam Textile, which provides seat-cover processing, and SG ELECTRONICS VINA, which provides display processing. Both names include Vietnam or VINA.

Real estate leasing is also included among the business segments; the domestic and export split for the other category, which includes leasing income, was 65.98% domestic and 34.02% export as of December 2025.

Competition centers on price and delivery terms among automaker suppliers, and pressure on unit supply prices contributed to the 2025 revenue decline. Listed auto-parts peers include Hyundai WIA, Sungwoo Hitech, and Seoyon E-Hwa.

04

Earnings analysis

On an annual basis, SG Global moved from an operating loss of KRW 1.44 billion in 2022 to an operating profit of KRW 6.61 billion in 2023, a swing to profitability, while revenue rose 42.7% to KRW 96.6 billion that year.

Net income attributable to controlling shareholders turned positive at KRW 20.2 billion in 2023, well above that year's operating profit. Revenue reached KRW 99.4 billion in 2024, up 3.0%, making it the largest year of 2022 to 2025, but operating profit fell 6.8% to KRW 6.15 billion.

In 2025, revenue declined 6.0% to KRW 93.5 billion, operating profit fell 19.3% to KRW 4.96 billion, and net income attributable to controlling shareholders fell 55.6% to KRW 2.81 billion. The operating margin moved from -2.1% in 2022 to 6.8% in 2023, then to 6.2% in 2024 and 5.3% in 2025.

According to FnGuide's 2025 annual summary, weak economic conditions hit all segments, with seat parts hurt by lower automaker output and unit price cuts and the display business weighed down by continued operating losses.

Quarterly, Q3 2025 revenue of KRW 15.9 billion came with an operating loss of KRW 503 million, followed by Q4 2025 revenue of KRW 27 billion and operating profit of KRW 924 million, a swing back to profit. Q1 2026 revenue was KRW 27.8 billion with operating profit of KRW 2.71 billion, up 192.7% from the prior quarter.

Q2 2026 revenue was KRW 29.1 billion with operating profit of KRW 2.08 billion, down 23.0%. Q3 2026 revenue was KRW 22 billion, down 24.4% from the prior quarter and up 38.3% year on year, with an operating loss of KRW 257 million, returning to losses.

The net loss attributable to controlling shareholders was KRW 715 million, with the loss widening from the year-earlier period. According to Wisereport's earnings commentary, the first-half 2026 improvement came from higher automaker production, stable order intake, and a mix shift toward premium vehicles and SUVs. First-half revenue rose 12.4% year on year and net income rose 28.7%.

Industry analysis

Domestic automaker production is the most direct variable setting the direction of SG Global's seat-parts revenue. In 2025, lower automaker output and lower unit prices combined to reduce seat-parts sales. This shows how automaker production volumes flow through delivery volumes into seat-parts revenue.

Growth in premium vehicles and SUVs was a favorable demand factor for the seat business. On the cycle, annual revenue passed its 2024 peak and declined in 2025, and the first half of 2026 showed a recovery.

However, Q3 2026 revenue fell 24.4% from the prior quarter, so the recovery has been uneven from quarter to quarter. Display processing relies on demand separate from the auto production cycle, so its results do not move in step with the seat business.

Seat parts accounted for 75.21% of the revenue mix, a high degree of business concentration. Market capitalization is small compared with large automakers and major parts makers.

Compared with listed auto-parts peers such as Hyundai WIA, Sungwoo Hitech, and Seoyon E-Hwa, the seat-focused structure is the distinguishing feature.

In the first half of 2026, operating profit rose 5.4% while revenue rose 12.4%, which suggests that the pace at which industry recovery converts into margins has been limited.

Outlook

The starting point for the outlook is two diverging trends: the first-half 2026 improvement and the Q3 2026 return to losses. First-half 2026 revenue rose 12.4% year on year and net income rose 28.7%.

In Q3, revenue fell 24.4% from the prior quarter to KRW 22 billion and the company posted an operating loss of KRW 257 million, so the two trends point in different directions. Seat-parts revenue depends on automaker production, so Q4 2026 production data is the first checkpoint.

The display business recorded continued operating losses in 2025, so whether those losses narrow is a segment-level checkpoint. Filings show a share consolidation and a change in the largest shareholder.

The consolidation ratio, effective date, and business direction after the shareholder change should be checked against the original DART filings. The next periodic report is the 2026 annual report, when full-year 2026 revenue and operating profit will be fixed as reported results.

Q1 2027 results can be read against the KRW 2.71 billion operating profit of Q1 2026. Whether quarterly operating profit returns to the levels of Q1 and Q2 2026 will indicate how durable the profit recovery is.

Bull factors

First-half 2026 revenue and profit growth

According to Wisereport's earnings commentary, first-half 2026 consolidated revenue rose 12.4% year on year and net income rose 28.7%. Higher automaker production, stable orders, and a shift toward premium vehicles and SUVs were cited as drivers of the seat business. Q1 2026 operating profit of KRW 2.71 billion was the largest of the last five quarters on a reported basis.

Lower leverage and steady operating cash flow

The debt ratio fell from 53.5% in 2022 to 30.6% in 2025. Total liabilities at the end of 2025 were KRW 46.5 billion against total equity of KRW 152 billion.

Operating cash flow was positive in every year of the disclosed period, at KRW 7.82 billion in 2022, KRW 2.99 billion in 2023, KRW 9.32 billion in 2024, and KRW 8.95 billion in 2025.

Share price well below book value

On a price-to-book basis, market capitalization sits well below the book value attributable to controlling shareholders. On an asset-value view, this gap can be cited as a bullish argument.

The interpretation is that if asset value and earnings recovery progress together, market valuation may move closer to asset value.

Overall view

SG Global's seat-parts-centered business swung from an operating loss in 2022 to profit in 2023, then saw earnings decline in 2024 and 2025. Revenue peaked at KRW 99.4 billion in 2024 and fell to KRW 93.5 billion in 2025. In the first half of 2026, revenue and net income rose year on year, signaling a recovery.

However, Q3 2026 returned to an operating loss of KRW 257 million and a net loss attributable to controlling shareholders of KRW 715 million, so the durability of the recovery remains to be confirmed.

On the balance sheet, the debt ratio of 30.6% at the end of 2025 and positive operating cash flow each year since 2022 are stabilizing factors.

The share price sits well below book value and the company has no cash dividend history, so asset-value comparisons and the pace of earnings recovery point in different directions.

The next checkpoints are the Q4 2026 preliminary results, the 2026 annual report, the Q1 2027 quarterly report, and the share consolidation and largest-shareholder change disclosures.
