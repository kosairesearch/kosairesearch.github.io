Report overview

Sales Slow as Profits Recover: What to Track

Gaeasoft's sales have declined for two straight quarters since the first quarter of 2026, yet third-quarter 2026 operating profit of KRW 7.6 billion was up 86.3% from a year earlier.

02

Business structure

Gaeasoft was founded in 1998 and listed on KOSDAQ in 2002, and it now controls eight subsidiaries including Oasis and a fulfillment unit. The group runs three segments: IT services, advertising, and distribution and e-commerce.

The IT services unit builds and operates wired and wireless systems and has been widening into telecom and public-sector application work and logistics system projects. The advertising unit provides integrated marketing services.

Distribution and e-commerce is the center of earnings, run mainly through Oasis, which operates a dawn-delivery platform for fresh food and accounted for 89.1% of consolidated sales by entity in the third quarter of 2023. A 2024 SK Securities report put that share at roughly 95%.

Gaeasoft held 55.2% of Oasis as of the third quarter of 2023. The fulfillment subsidiary Route was established in February 2021 and operates a fulfillment center in Uiwang.

Press reports said Oasis membership rose from 1.68 million at the end of 2023 to 1.96 million at the end of 2024 and passed 2 million in February 2025. Because IT services and advertising are small by entity revenue, group profit moves mainly with Oasis's marketing spending and margins.

04

Earnings analysis

Annual results moved in different directions for sales and profit.

Sales rose from KRW 454.6 billion in 2022 to KRW 508.9 billion in 2023 (up 11.9%), KRW 547.2 billion in 2024 (up 7.5%) and KRW 587.1 billion in 2025 (up 7.3%), so sales increased for three consecutive years from 2023 through 2025, and 2025 was the largest year of 2022 through 2025.

Operating profit swung from an operating loss of KRW 3.08 billion in 2022 to KRW 15.2 billion in 2023, a turn to profit, then rose to KRW 29.1 billion in 2024 (up 91.3%) before falling to KRW 25.1 billion in 2025 (down 13.6%).

Operating margin went from -0.7% in 2022 to 3.0% in 2023 and 5.3% in 2024, then eased to 4.3% in 2025. The 2025 filing attributed the profit decline to higher advertising and marketing spending at Oasis, so revenue grew but costs grew faster.

Net profit attributable to controlling shareholders went from a loss of KRW 2.57 billion in 2022 to KRW 10.6 billion in 2023, then KRW 17.9 billion in 2024 (up 68.9%) and KRW 12.6 billion in 2025 (down 29.2%).

Net profit including non-controlling interests was KRW 19.2 billion in 2025, a different figure from the controlling-shareholder number, and it was below KRW 28 billion in 2024. Operating cash flow was KRW 15.1 billion in 2022, KRW 42.7 billion in 2024 and KRW 31.5 billion in 2025.

On a quarterly basis, the third quarter of 2025 had the lowest operating profit in the disclosed series at KRW 4.08 billion. Profit rebounded to KRW 7.91 billion in the fourth quarter of 2025 (up 94.0% from the prior quarter) and peaked at KRW 10.3 billion in the first quarter of 2026.

It then fell to KRW 9.9 billion in the second quarter (down 4.3%) and KRW 7.6 billion in the third (down 23.2%). Sales recovered to KRW 145.8 billion in the first quarter of 2026, then declined to KRW 145.1 billion and KRW 137.5 billion, with third-quarter sales down 8.8% from a year earlier.

Controlling-shareholder net profit peaked at KRW 5.72 billion in the first quarter of 2026 and then held near KRW 4.9 billion in each of the next two quarters. Profit recovery and softer sales are occurring at the same time.

05

Industry analysis

Demand from the downstream market is driven by growth in online grocery spending. A company-level summary cited growth in the online food market and mobile shopping trends as factors that could lift the growth prospects of the dawn-delivery platform.

On competition, a 2024 brokerage view held that exits by rival operators had made the e-commerce environment more favorable for Oasis. In March 2025, KB Securities said Gaeasoft was expected to expand its scale through mergers and acquisitions and more aggressive advertising.

The same report gave a KB Securities estimate that Oasis's 2024 sales were about KRW 512.2 billion, up 7.7%, with operating profit of about KRW 22.2 billion, up 74.8%. These are brokerage estimates, not audited results.

In the third quarter of 2025, operating margin of 2.7% was described as among the lowest among peers. Compared with large IT services groups such as Samsung SDS and LG CNS, Gaeasoft has a smaller IT services share, so it is more exposed to online retail conditions.

In cycle terms, sales growth that ran from 2023 has slowed in 2026 while quarterly margins have swung, and whether advertising efficiency improves is the main variable for its position against the industry.

Outlook

Among the company's plans, several are documented. First, it plans to extend AI-based unmanned automated store technology to its offline stores. The timing has not been tied to a specific date in the available material, so progress is best tracked through the business section of the 2026 annual report.

Second, logistics expansion: a January 2024 brokerage report described a plan to build a Eonyang logistics center in 2024 and 2025 to extend dawn delivery to the Gyeongsang and Chungcheong regions. Operation of that center and any regional expansion can be checked in the annual and quarterly filings.

Third, IT services are widening into telecom, public-sector application development and logistics system projects. Fourth, a first-half 2026 aggregate showed operating profit up 54.1% year on year on sales down 1.7%.

Fifth, on capital policy, treasury shares were retired in a profit-funded cancellation, with the listing-date record showing 1,991,782 shares removed on September 28, 2026.

Whether sales stop declining after two quarters of decline is the first test in the next reports, and whether the store and logistics expansion translate into sales recovery can be judged from filings after the 2026 annual report.

08

Bull factors

Quarterly profit recovery

Operating profit rose from KRW 7.91 billion in the fourth quarter of 2025 to KRW 10.3 billion in the first quarter of 2026. Third-quarter 2026 operating profit of KRW 7.6 billion was up 86.3% from a year earlier, and a first-half 2026 aggregate showed operating profit up 54.1%.

Profit grew while sales softened, which points to improved profitability as the core of the story. Controlling-shareholder net profit also peaked at KRW 5.72 billion in the first quarter of 2026.

Growth base at subsidiary Oasis

According to a KB Securities estimate, Oasis's 2024 sales were about KRW 512.2 billion, up 7.7%, and its operating profit rose 74.8%. Membership grew from 1.96 million at the end of 2024 to more than 2 million in February 2025.

The bull argument is that a larger member base can improve marketing efficiency and repeat purchases. The estimate comes from a March 2025 report, and audited figures are confirmed in periodic filings.

Treasury share cancellation and financial room

On September 28, 2026, 1,991,782 treasury shares were retired through a profit-funded cancellation. With no cash dividend paid, share cancellation has served as the shareholder return tool.

The debt-to-equity ratio fell from 59.2% in 2022 to 40.3% in 2025, and operating cash flow stayed positive at KRW 31.5 billion in 2025. Bulls point to this financial room as supporting evidence.

Bear factors

Sales turning lower

Sales fell in the second and third quarters of 2026, and third-quarter sales of KRW 137.5 billion were down 8.8% from a year earlier. A first-half 2026 aggregate showed sales down 1.7% year on year. The 7.3% sales growth in 2025 has stalled in 2026.

Advertising costs and thin margins

Operating profit fell 13.6% in 2025 because advertising and marketing spending increased. Operating margin of 2.7% in the third quarter of 2025 was among the lower margins in the peer group. Full-year 2025 operating margin was 4.3%, below 5.3% in 2024.

Quarterly earnings volatility

Third-quarter 2026 operating profit fell 23.2% from the prior quarter, and controlling-shareholder profit has held near KRW 4.9 billion since the second quarter. Two consecutive quarterly declines after the first-quarter peak leave open whether the profit recovery can continue. The group's reliance on one subsidiary adds to the volatility.

10

Risk factors

Concentration in one subsidiary

Most consolidated revenue comes from Oasis. Oasis accounted for 89.1% of consolidated sales by entity in the third quarter of 2023, and a 2024 SK Securities report put the share at roughly 95%. Demand and marketing efficiency at this one subsidiary directly drive group profit. Whether IT services grow enough to diversify group results will determine how far this concentration eases.

Marketing costs and competition

Per the 2025 filing, higher advertising and marketing spending drove the decline in operating profit. If competition in online shopping and dawn delivery persists, cost pressure could build again.

Sustaining quarterly profit recovery in 2026 depends on controlling marketing spend, so this risk feeds directly into earnings.

Wide quarterly swings

Quarterly operating profit swung from KRW 4.08 billion in the third quarter of 2025 to KRW 10.3 billion in the first quarter of 2026. Such wide swings make it difficult to judge the trend from any single quarter.

The first-half 2026 aggregate was compiled by a third-party data provider, so it may differ from figures in periodic filings.

Overall view

Gaeasoft's sales rose for three consecutive years from 2023, but operating profit fell to KRW 25.1 billion in 2025 as advertising and marketing spending increased.

Sales peaked at KRW 145.8 billion in the first quarter of 2026 and then declined for two quarters, while operating profit dropped from KRW 10.3 billion in the first quarter to KRW 7.6 billion in the third.

Even so, third-quarter operating profit was up 86.3% from a year earlier, and controlling-shareholder profit over the last four quarters totaled KRW 18.1 billion, above the full-year 2025 figure. Bulls cite the quarterly profit recovery, the Oasis member base, treasury share cancellation and a lower debt ratio.

Bears point to slowing sales, advertising costs, dependence on Oasis and large quarterly swings. The two sides read the same results differently. The next checkpoints are the 2026 annual report and the first-quarter 2027 report, where whether the sales decline stops and whether operating margin holds are the key tests.
