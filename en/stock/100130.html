02

Business structure

Dongkuk Structures & Construction Company was founded in 2001 as a manufacturer of wind towers and construction contractor for wind farms, and it listed on KOSDAQ in 2009. Its largest shareholder is Dongkuk Industries Company, which held 51.26% as of June 30, 2026.

The business consists of a renewable energy segment centered on wind towers and a construction segment covering wind-farm construction. A Wisereport breakdown shows the renewable energy segment at 77.30% of sales and construction at 22.70%.

An AlphaSquare breakdown shows wind towers at 53.54%, construction and energy development at 45.84%, and other at 0.62%, so the two sources differ because they use different classification standards. In 2022, the steel segment accounted for 48.2% of consolidated sales, but the consolidated group has changed since then.

Major wind-tower customers include Vestas, Siemens Gamesa and GE Renewable Energy, and Nordex and Enercon are also named as customers. Wind-tower exports were reported at about 73.5% of 2024 wind-tower sales, with the United States as the key export market.

Production facilities in Pohang, Korea are seen as an advantage for serving domestic demand, while some competitors reportedly produce entirely overseas.

04

Earnings analysis

Sales declined from KRW 477.1 billion in 2022 to KRW 398.3 billion in 2023 (down 16.5% year on year), KRW 165.8 billion in 2024 (down 58.4%), and KRW 119.7 billion in 2025 (down 27.8%).

Sales have fallen for three consecutive years from 2023 through 2025, with 2022 the largest year and 2025 the smallest in the 2022 to 2025 period. The company attributed the 2025 decline to a construction downturn and lower demand for wind towers bound for the United States after high tariffs.

Operating results went from an operating loss of KRW 11.4 billion in 2022 to an operating loss of KRW 31.8 billion in 2023 (loss widened), then an operating loss of KRW 5.53 billion in 2024 (loss narrowed), before an operating profit of KRW 6.94 billion in 2025, which marked a turn to profit.

The operating margin moved from -2.4% in 2022 and -8.0% in 2023 to -3.3% in 2024 and 5.8% in 2025.

The net loss attributable to controlling shareholders was KRW 7.84 billion in 2022, KRW 30.2 billion in 2023, KRW 40.2 billion in 2024 and KRW 1.67 billion in 2025, a loss in each of four consecutive years from 2022 through 2025.

The net loss including non-controlling interests narrowed from KRW 39.8 billion in 2024 to KRW 1.67 billion in 2025, and the company said lower non-operating expenses, driven by a reduction in provisions tied to construction work, improved profitability.

On a quarterly basis, sales were KRW 32.4 billion in Q2 2025 and KRW 32.6 billion in Q3 2025, then fell to KRW 9.52 billion in Q4 2025 (down 70.9% from the prior quarter).

Sales recovered to KRW 15.8 billion in Q1 2026 (up 66.0% from the prior quarter) before dropping to KRW 5.03 billion in Q2 2026 (down 68.1% quarter on quarter and down 84.5% year on year).

Operating profit of KRW 4.03 billion in Q2 2025 was followed by an operating loss in each of the four quarters from Q3 2025 through Q2 2026.

Q3 2025 showed a net profit attributable to controlling shareholders of KRW 1.04 billion, but three consecutive quarters of net losses followed, and the Q2 2026 loss widened to KRW 12.8 billion.

The net loss attributable to controlling shareholders totaled KRW 17.8 billion over the last four quarters (Q3 2025 to Q2 2026).

05

Industry analysis

Demand for wind towers depends heavily on the US onshore wind market and domestic wind policy. C-journal, citing a report from the Global Wind Energy Council, reported that US new onshore wind capacity is projected to grow from 5.3 GW in 2024 to 13.5 GW in 2030, an annual growth rate of 16.9%.

Over the same period, global new onshore wind capacity growth was projected at 6.6% per year. The company, however, said high tariffs and a construction downturn reduced demand for wind towers bound for the United States, so opposing demand factors are operating at the same time.

On October 28, 2025, the company disclosed a temporary halt to wind-tower production starting November 1, 2025, with a planned restart date of December 15. In Korea, reports said the Three Energy Laws, which include an offshore wind act, passed the National Assembly.

Some competitors reportedly produce entirely overseas, which is seen as giving Dongkuk Structures & Construction Company a relative advantage in serving domestic demand. Because wind-tower exports make up a large share of sales, the business is directly exposed to changes in US policy.

Outlook

The nearest checkpoint is Q3 2026 results. Investing.com lists the next earnings release as November 18, 2026. Q2 2026 sales of KRW 5.03 billion and an operating loss of KRW 3.06 billion show that recent quarterly momentum is weak.

The next items to confirm are whether Q3 sales recover from the Q2 level and whether the operating loss narrows. Projected US onshore wind growth of 16.9% per year keeps the demand base in place. Whether the high tariffs and construction downturn cited by the company ease, however, remains an open variable.

Total equity at the end of 2025 was KRW 185.6 billion and total liabilities were KRW 70.7 billion, a balance sheet that has improved since 2022. Operating cash flow was positive at KRW 66.1 billion in 2025, in contrast to negative operating cash flow from 2022 through 2024.

Full-year 2026 results will be confirmed in the annual report for fiscal 2026, expected around March 2027. The key items will be whether annual sales decline for a fourth straight year and whether operating profit stays positive.

08

Bull factors

Return to operating profit and a stronger balance sheet

Operating profit reached KRW 6.94 billion in 2025 after an operating loss in 2024. The debt ratio fell from 79.6% in 2022 to 38.1% in 2025. Operating cash flow was KRW 66.1 billion in 2025, ending a run of negative figures from 2022 through 2024.

Projected growth in US onshore wind

C-journal, citing a GWEC report, said US new onshore wind capacity could reach 13.5 GW by 2030, a 16.9% annual growth rate. That is about 2.5 times the 6.6% global average. Because the United States is the key export market, this growth forms the base for wind-tower demand.

Domestic production base and domestic policy

Production facilities in Pohang are seen as giving an advantage in serving domestic wind demand. Reports said the Three Energy Laws passed the National Assembly, improving the domestic wind policy environment, including offshore wind. If construction and domestic demand grow together, the sales mix could change.

Bear factors

Sales decline and a sharp drop in quarterly revenue

Sales have declined for three consecutive years to KRW 119.7 billion in 2025. Q2 2026 sales fell 84.5% year on year to KRW 5.03 billion. The shrinking revenue base has made it harder to absorb fixed costs.

Widening quarterly losses and cumulative net losses

Operating losses have continued for four straight quarters from Q3 2025 to Q2 2026. The net loss attributable to controlling shareholders widened to KRW 12.8 billion in Q2 2026. The total for the last four quarters reached KRW 17.8 billion.

US tariffs and concentration in exports

Exports were reported at about 73.5% of 2024 wind-tower sales, with the United States as the key market. The company said high US tariffs reduced demand for wind towers bound for the country. The structure leaves sales exposed to swings in US policy.

10

Risk factors

Trade and tariff risk

Wind-tower exports to the United States make up a large share of sales, leaving the company directly exposed to tariff policy. The company cited high tariffs as a cause of the 2025 sales decline. If tariff levels persist, orders and shipment timing could be pushed back.

Customer and demand concentration risk

Major customers are concentrated among global turbine makers such as Vestas, Siemens Gamesa and GE Renewable Energy. Quarterly sales can move sharply depending on the order timing of individual customers. The temporary production halt disclosed in October 2025 illustrates that volatility.

Financial and cash flow risk

Operating cash flow was negative from 2022 through 2024, and the positive figure in 2025 covers only one year. Quarterly net losses through Q2 2026 could reduce equity over time. Total equity was KRW 185.6 billion at the end of 2025, and the pace of earnings recovery will shape financial capacity.

Overall view

Dongkuk Structures & Construction Company's 2025 consolidated results show sales of KRW 119.7 billion (down 27.8% year on year) and operating profit of KRW 6.94 billion, a turn to profit.

Sales have fallen for three consecutive years from 2023, and the company cited high US tariffs and a construction downturn as the main causes. The debt ratio improved to 38.1% in 2025 from 79.6% in 2022, and total equity at the end of 2025 was KRW 185.6 billion.

In contrast, Q2 2026 showed sales of KRW 5.03 billion, an operating loss of KRW 3.06 billion and a net loss attributable to controlling shareholders of KRW 12.8 billion. The net loss attributable to controlling shareholders totaled KRW 17.8 billion over the last four quarters.

Bullish factors are the return to profit, operating cash flow of KRW 66.1 billion in 2025 and projected US onshore wind growth. Bearish factors are export concentration and widening quarterly losses.

On valuation, the price-to-book ratio is below 1x, while the price-to-earnings ratio cannot be calculated because of recent net losses. The next checkpoints are the Q3 2026 earnings release scheduled for November 18, 2026 and the fiscal 2026 annual report expected around March 2027.
