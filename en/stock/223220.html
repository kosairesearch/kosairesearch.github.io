02

Business structure

Logis Mon was founded in August 2013 in Seoul and listed on the KONEX market in July 2015. The company runs B2C small-parcel delivery and B2B corporate freight transportation, serving online sellers and mid-sized companies.

Mobile game content publishing was once its core business, but one half-year disclosure analysis reported that most revenue came from parcel logistics rather than games. The game segment's contribution fell sharply after a title launched in 2017 underperformed.

Company profile materials also mention a media commerce platform and small-parcel collection and delivery. Reports said that in 2024 the company handled close to 3 million B2C shipments for about 200 online commerce clients.

Its competitors include large parcel and logistics operators such as Logen, CJ Logistics, and HANJIN Logistics, and the scale gap is wide enough that a direct market-share comparison is difficult.

Revenue in 2025 was KRW 5,489 million against KRW 10,164 million in 2024, so disclosures alone cannot separate a volume decline from pricing changes.

03

Recent trends

Revenue for 2025 was KRW 5,489 million, against KRW 10,175 million in 2023 and KRW 10,164 million in 2024. Over the same period the operating loss narrowed each year, from KRW 1,140 million in 2022 to KRW 490 million in 2023, KRW 366 million in 2024, and KRW 148 million in 2025.

The net loss was KRW 281 million in 2024 and KRW 166 million in 2025. Total equity at end-2024 was negative KRW 390 million, with paid-in capital fully eroded. The separate financial statements for the 13th fiscal year reported paid-in capital of KRW 108 million and total equity of KRW 315 million at end-2025.

The December 2025 third-party allotment, which planned to raise KRW 300 million for operating funds, falls within this period. On January 20, 2026, a raise of 540,206 common shares at KRW 584 was disclosed, followed by a third-party allotment at KRW 559 on February 20.

Media reports on the February 20 disclosure gave different raise sizes, roughly KRW 1 billion and KRW 1.7 billion, so the number of new shares also differs across reports. The largest shareholder changed on March 24, 2026, from Dplus No. 2 Investment Partnership to IK Partnership Fund, which reported a 30.71% stake.

The share price fell from KRW 3,490 at 4:10 p.m. on March 30 to KRW 2,090 on April 14, and the current price is KRW 131.

04

Outlook

On earnings, the key variables are whether revenue recovers and whether the operating loss keeps narrowing. According to the February 20, 2026 disclosure, a total of KRW 3,999,999,965 in operating funds is planned for deployment from 2026 through 2028 and beyond.

The funds are slated for essential operating needs, adoption of new technology, and balance-sheet improvement. At the February 2026 issue price of KRW 559, the discount to the reference price of KRW 620 was 9.98%, and continued raises would add cumulative dilution as the share count grows.

The outcome of the largest-shareholder change and the control dispute lawsuit will shape governance and future funding methods. The next checkpoint is the revenue and operating profit trend in the third-quarter 2026 report, due by November 14, 2026.

The lawsuit's progress and the actual use of raised funds are also items to track. As a KONEX stock with small daily trading value, the share price continues to show wide fluctuations.

05

Bull factors

Narrowing operating losses

The operating loss shrank from KRW 1,140 million in 2022 to KRW 148 million in 2025, a sharp reduction over four years. One half-year disclosure analysis cited lower selling and administrative expenses as the driver of the improvement. The narrower loss despite lower revenue points to cost restructuring as one factor.

Shift out of capital impairment

At end-2024 total equity was negative and paid-in capital was fully eroded, which was the starting point for the later turnaround. Separate financial statements for the 13th fiscal year show total equity of KRW 315 million, turning positive.

Capital raised through third-party allotments appears to have contributed to this improvement in financial stability.

Online commerce volume base

Reports indicated that the B2C volume comes from online commerce clients. It also runs B2B freight transport on consignment, giving it two customer bases. Disclosures do not show how this volume relates to the 2025 revenue decline.

06

Bear factors

Revenue decline and continued losses

Revenue for 2025 was KRW 5,489 million, roughly half of the 2024 level. The operating loss was KRW 148 million and the net loss was KRW 166 million, so the deficit persists. Whether the company can absorb fixed costs on a smaller revenue base remains a weakness.

Frequent third-party allotments

Capital-raise disclosures followed on December 19, 2025, January 20, 2026, and February 20, 2026. Issue prices fell from KRW 4,520 to KRW 584 and then KRW 559, with the February issue at a 9.98% discount to the reference price. Media reports on raise sizes differ, which makes the extent of dilution hard to pin down.

Governance instability and share price collapse

The largest shareholder changed on March 24, 2026, and in September disclosures covered another change in the largest shareholder and a control dispute lawsuit. On September 17, a 10% holder reported a 33.33% stake based on 12,831,567 total shares outstanding. The share price has declined from KRW 3,490 to KRW 2,090 and now stands at KRW 131.

07

Risk factors

Macro

B2C volume depends on online consumer spending and the shipping volumes of e-commerce sellers. Because the company competes with large parcel operators, it is exposed to pricing pressure. A prolonged slowdown in consumption could again pressure revenue and operating profit.

Market and listing

As a KONEX-listed company, its daily trading value was only KRW 367,162. Low liquidity means the share price can move sharply even on small trades. If capital impairment and losses persist, regulatory and market-management risks could also grow.

Governance and dilution

The largest shareholder has changed more than once in a short period, and a control dispute lawsuit is ongoing. Depending on the outcome, management direction and funding plans could shift. Repeated capital raises accumulate dilution for existing shareholders.

08

Overall view

Logis Mon's operating loss has narrowed every year since 2022 even as revenue shrank, and equity turning positive at end-2025 is a financial improvement signal.

Still, the 2025 revenue of KRW 5,489 million and net loss of KRW 166 million show the deficit persists, and disclosures alone cannot separate the causes of the revenue decline versus 2024.

The series of raises from December 2025 to February 2026, the falling issue prices, the 2026 change in the largest shareholder, and the control dispute lawsuit add uncertainty around governance and dilution.

The share price has fallen sharply since March 2026 to KRW 131, and low trading value on KONEX adds to volatility. This report is a qualitative summary for information purposes and does not offer a view on the direction of the share price.

The use of raised funds, the lawsuit's progress, and the profit trend in the third-quarter 2026 report will be key data points for assessing both finances and governance.
