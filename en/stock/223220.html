02

Business structure

Logis Mon is a provider of other transport-related services that was listed on the KONEX market on July 9, 2015. It was founded on August 16, 2013. In November 2022 it completed a merger with Domyeong, a parcel collection service firm, and in December of that year it changed its name from IP Monster to Logis Mon.

The company describes itself as a logistics operator handling e-commerce goods from ordering and storage through distribution and customer delivery, covering B2C small parcels and B2B corporate freight.

It has been expanding in last-mile delivery, using Seoul-metropolitan-area hub infrastructure for same-day or next-day service with domestic platforms and retailers.

The company website lists 8 directly operated vehicles, 12 contracted vehicles and about 1.5 million consigned parcels per month, although that introduction only covers activity through 2022.

On the platform side, it presents OEM ready-meal production and distribution under the 'eaTalk' brand, along with management of last-mile delivery partners.

Its contract history includes a 2023 agreement with Goodsflow (SK Energy) for the 'Better Kind Parcel' service, a July 2023 B2B freight contract with Korea Houghton, and a May 2024 renewal of its parcel transport contract with HANJIN Logistics.

No confirmed disclosed figures exist for revenue by segment, so this is described qualitatively only. With 14 employees, the company is better seen as a small subcontracted carrier than as a peer of listed logistics firms such as CJ Logistics, HANJIN Logistics, Logen or Hansol Logistics.

Recent trends

On a standalone basis for the 13th fiscal year (2025), revenue was KRW 5,489 million, the operating loss was KRW 148 million and the net loss was KRW 166 million. Total assets were KRW 1,442 million, total liabilities KRW 1,127 million and total equity KRW 315 million.

Data based on NICE credit information put 2024 revenue at KRW 10.16 billion, which suggests revenue fell sharply in 2025, although the specific cause is not confirmed. The operating loss narrowed from KRW 1.14 billion in 2022 to about KRW 149 million in 2025.

Capital raising began with a December 19, 2025 third-party allotment of 66,371 common shares raising KRW 299,996,920, and on February 20, 2026 a third-party allotment plan with total operating-fund use of KRW 3,999,999,965 was disclosed.

On March 6, 2026 the largest shareholder became The Plus No. 2 Investment Partnership (46.64%), and on April 14 it changed again to IK Partnership. On September 16, 2026 Lim Yunjong became the largest shareholder, and on September 11 a petition for an injunction against new share issuance had been filed.

Lim Yunjong's filing shows 4,277,189 new common shares acquired as of September 17, 2026 for a 33.33% stake, at a stated unit price of KRW 100. The share price was KRW 3,490 on March 30, 2026, versus KRW 117 as of October 27, 2026. We found no disclosure or press report explaining the size of this decline.

Outlook

First, the outcome of the injunction suit filed on October 13, 2026 seeking to bar the convening of board meetings and the tabling of agenda items (Case No. 2026Kahap21869) may affect how the board operates.

Second, the injunction suit to suspend an internal director's duties (2026Kahap21867) could change the composition of management depending on how it is decided. Third, at the October 23 extraordinary meeting, 15 business purposes, including blockchain R&D and e-money and foreign-exchange related work, were deleted.

Because no additions or changes to business purposes were recorded, the future business direction cannot be judged from filings alone.

Fourth, on the industry side, FnGuide describes a growing B2C delivery market driven by online consumption, intensifying competition over delivery differentiation, and expanding cooperation between large retailers or e-commerce firms and small carriers.

That backdrop may support demand for small carriers such as Logis Mon, but it also brings pressure from parcel rates and labor costs.

Fifth, whether existing contracts such as the HANJIN Logistics parcel agreement continue, and whether proceeds from capital raisings actually narrow the operating loss, are items that will show up in reported results. Room for operating-margin improvement depends on how far revenue scale recovers and fixed costs are reduced.

Bull factors

Narrowing operating losses

In NICE-based data, the operating loss narrowed from KRW 1.14 billion in 2022 to about KRW 149 million in 2025. The 2025 standalone operating loss of KRW 148 million is modest relative to revenue of KRW 5,489 million. If the cost structure stabilizes, there is room for the business to move closer to break-even. Revenue fell over the same period, however, so the quality of the improvement needs separate verification.

Last-mile demand and existing clients

FnGuide notes that retailers' and e-commerce firms' race to shorten delivery times is widening cooperation with promising small carriers. The company renewed its HANJIN Logistics parcel transport contract in May 2024 and signed contracts with Goodsflow and Korea Houghton in 2023.

As a small carrier with Seoul-area collection hubs, it is positioned to receive such cooperative demand. Recent volumes and rates under these contracts could not be confirmed from filings.

Capital injections and cleaned-up business purposes

As of September 17, 2026, Lim Yunjong acquired 4,277,189 new shares at KRW 100 per share. A third-party allotment for operating funds was also decided in February 2026. On October 23, 15 crypto-asset-related business purposes were deleted, narrowing the business scope in the articles.

If new funds go into operating cash flow, the capital buffer could grow. The actual use of proceeds needs confirmation in later filings.

Bear factors

Falling revenue and a thin equity base

In NICE-based data, revenue fell from KRW 10.16 billion in 2024 to KRW 5.49 billion in 2025. On a 2025 standalone basis, total equity of KRW 315 million compared with total liabilities of KRW 1,127 million. Continued operating and net losses together with a shrinking top line are a burden. The post-capital-raising balance sheet must be checked in subsequent quarterly reports.

Control dispute and frequent shareholder changes

DART filings on October 14, 2026 disclosed injunction suits seeking to bar board meetings and suspend an internal director's duties. One event-history tracker counts 17 litigation or dispute disclosures and 11 rights offerings.

The largest shareholder changed on March 4, March 6, April 14 and September 16, weakening continuity of decision-making. A prolonged dispute could delay execution of operating strategy.

Dilution and very low liquidity

Before the February 2026 capital raising, shares outstanding were 1,803,100, and total issued shares stood at 5,702,919 as of March 24, 2026. In September, new shares were acquired at KRW 100 per share. Repeated share issuance dilutes existing holders. Trading value on October 27, 2026 was only KRW 766,188, so executing trades at a desired time may be difficult.

Risk factors

Macro

Parcel and small-freight transport is sensitive to online consumption trends and parcel-rate competition. FnGuide says competition over delivery-service differentiation is intensifying as the B2C market grows. Small carriers have limited pricing power versus large parcel companies. Rising fuel and labor costs could weigh on margins.

Governance and legal

After the application for permission to convene a shareholders' meeting disclosed on September 17, 2026 in the control dispute, October brought injunction suits challenging board meetings and a director's duties. Court rulings could change board composition and the handling of agenda items.

The outcome and timing of the litigation are not known. The result of the injunction against new share issuance also needs to be confirmed in later filings.

Market and liquidity

The KONEX market has limited participants and thin trading. With a market capitalization of KRW 1.5 billion and daily trading value of KRW 766,188, even small trades can move the price substantially. The share price, which was KRW 3,490 on March 30, 2026, stands at KRW 117, showing very high volatility. New share listings and changes in major holdings can also shift supply and demand.

08

Overall view

Logis Mon is a small logistics operator handling small-parcel collection and last-mile delivery in the Seoul metropolitan area, and on a 2025 standalone basis it recorded revenue of KRW 5,489 million and an operating loss of KRW 148 million.

The narrowing of operating losses since 2022 and industry commentary pointing to growing last-mile cooperation demand are positives on the business side. Falling revenue, a thin equity base and dilution from repeated share issuance weigh on the other side.

Since September 2026, control-dispute litigation and injunctions have continued, so non-operating variables loom large. Low liquidity on KONEX also amplifies price volatility.
