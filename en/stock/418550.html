02

Business structure

Jeio was founded in 1994 and mainly operates plant engineering and carbon nanotube (CNT) manufacturing.

According to press reports, the company began CNT research for secondary battery conductive agents in 2003, started mass production of multi-wall CNT (MWCNT) in 2006, and began producing thin-wall CNT (TWCNT) in 2014.

Reports describe TWCNT as a product the company is the only one to produce globally, with better physical properties than multi-wall grades and lower prices than single-wall grades. A January 2023 report noted that Jeio and LG Chem were the only two domestic producers of CNT for secondary battery conductive agents.

Customers named in press reports include SK On, Northvolt and Contemporary Amperex Technology (CATL), and a report at the end of 2024 said supply to Samsung SDI had begun. Single-wall CNT for anode use had finished development and was in customer sample testing as of January 2023.

Plant engineering has been expanding in scale as anode and cathode capex at battery makers and recycling facility investment rise, according to a company profile note from WiseReport dated April 2026.

In June 2025 the company signed its first supply contract for dry electrode process plants with a domestic secondary battery customer and later said supply had been completed.

In December 2025 it won a contract to supply vertical vacuum dryers to a domestic defense and mobility customer, extending its advanced materials equipment business beyond batteries. The revenue split by business segment should be checked in the segment note of the annual business report.

04

Earnings analysis

On a reported annual basis, sales went from KRW 67.7 billion in 2022 to KRW 114.5 billion in 2023, a 69.2% increase and the largest year in the 2022–2025 period.

Sales then fell for two consecutive years, to KRW 82.9 billion in 2024 (down 27.6%) and KRW 55.2 billion in 2025 (down 33.4%), with 2025 being the smallest year in the period. Operating loss of KRW 2.26 billion in 2022 turned into an operating profit of KRW 12 billion in 2023, with an operating margin of 10.5%.

The company then returned to an operating loss of KRW 5.46 billion in 2024 (operating margin of -6.6%), and in 2025 the operating loss of KRW 4.23 billion (operating margin of -7.7%) continued but narrowed.

Net profit attributable to controlling shareholders reached KRW 17.4 billion in 2023 before turning to a loss of KRW 8.87 billion in 2024 and KRW 7.44 billion in 2025, which marks two consecutive years of losses.

Net income including non-controlling interests shows the same losses of KRW 8.87 billion in 2024 and KRW 7.44 billion in 2025. A press report from the second quarter of 2025 attributed the operating profit decline to fixed-cost pressure from expanding battery materials capacity.

Operating cash flow went from KRW 27.6 billion in 2023 to negative KRW 4.91 billion in 2024 and recovered to KRW 872 million in 2025.

Quarterly sales fell from KRW 15.9 billion in the third quarter of 2025 to KRW 9.37 billion in the fourth quarter (down 40.9% quarter on quarter), then bottomed at KRW 7.17 billion in the first quarter of 2026 (down 23.5%) before rising to KRW 8.93 billion in the second quarter (up 24.6%) and KRW 11.5 billion in the third quarter (up 28.3%).

Third-quarter 2026 sales were still down 27.7% from a year earlier.

Operating loss widened from KRW 661 million in the third quarter of 2025 to KRW 3.79 billion in the fourth quarter, narrowed to KRW 1.96 billion and KRW 1.55 billion in the first and second quarters of 2026, and widened again to KRW 1.97 billion in the third quarter.

Net loss attributable to controlling shareholders was KRW 3.55 billion in the fourth quarter of 2025 and KRW 2.83 billion in the third quarter of 2026, and the four-quarter total is KRW 9.25 billion.

Equity at the end of 2025 was KRW 170.2 billion, all attributable to controlling shareholders, against total liabilities of KRW 37.3 billion. The amounts behind the widening fourth-quarter 2025 loss should be checked in the notes to the annual business report.

05

Industry analysis

In the secondary battery conductive agent market, the shift from carbon black to CNT is the most important structural variable. Reports in 2023 said CNT's higher conductivity could cut conductive agent input by up to 80% and that CNT could compensate for weaknesses of silicon anodes.

Only Jeio and LG Chem have been identified as domestic producers of CNT powder, which is why the two are described as a duopoly. A January 2023 report said the company targeted annual capacity of 5,000 tonnes by 2025, while the same report projected LG Chem at about 6,100 tonnes.

On capacity targets alone, Jeio's plan is smaller than LG Chem's, which reads as a competitive weakness. End demand depends on the pace of the EV slowdown debate and cell makers' capacity expansion, and a 2025 report cited high EV production costs as a central cause of that slowdown.

Dry electrode processing, which removes the drying step of the wet process to lower production cost and environmental burden, is presented as a next-generation process, and Jeio said it had completed development of dry dispersion and dry coating and fiberization equipment.

If this process spreads, the way conductive agents are supplied could change, so the company faces both an opportunity and a structural-change risk. The company has said that Chinese CNT producers exist but that Korean material quality is higher; this is the company's own assertion.

Outlook

The medium-term plan the company has stated is to expand new businesses in secondary battery materials by 2028, based on dry process equipment and plant technology. Following the first dry electrode process plant contract in June 2025, the company signaled it would expand this equipment business.

The advanced materials equipment business aims to extend beyond batteries into defense, aerospace and chemicals, with a December 2025 defense customer contract cited as the first example.

A June 2023 report described a plan to invest about KRW 70 billion in a second Ansan plant to add CNT capacity, with targets of 3,000 tonnes by the end of 2024 and 5,000 tonnes in 2025.

The current operating scale and whether this investment was executed should be checked in the facilities section of the 2026 annual business report. On customers, SK On, Northvolt, CATL and Samsung SDI have been named, and commentary described ongoing customer diversification.

A June 2023 securities-industry view held that earnings growth at CNT materials companies could approach that of cathode makers.

Since 2024, however, sales and profits have declined, and third-quarter 2026 sales were still down from a year earlier, so whether that view is reflected in results should be checked against quarterly figures.

The next checkpoint is the operating margin in the 2026 annual business report, which is due by late March 2027.

Bull factors

Expansion into dry electrode equipment

In June 2025 the company announced its first supply contract for dry electrode process plants with a domestic secondary battery customer, and later said supply had been completed.

The company has stated plans to expand new secondary battery materials businesses by 2028 based on dry process equipment and plant technology. In December 2025 it also announced a contract to supply vertical vacuum dryers to a defense and mobility customer, widening where its equipment can be applied.

Shift to CNT conductive agents and TWCNT position

According to press reports, CNT is being adopted more widely as a conductive agent replacing carbon black, with reports citing input reductions of up to 80%.

The company's TWCNT is described in reports as the only product of its kind produced globally, with better physical properties than multi-wall grades and lower prices than single-wall grades. The narrowing of domestic CNT powder producers to two is also cited as an advantage in the competitive landscape.

Two straight quarters of sales growth in 2026

Sales bottomed at KRW 7.17 billion in the first quarter of 2026, then rose to KRW 8.93 billion in the second quarter and KRW 11.5 billion in the third, marking two consecutive quarters of growth. Third-quarter sales were up 28.3% from the prior quarter.

Customers named in press reports include SK On, Northvolt, CATL and Samsung SDI, and commentary describes a broadening customer base.

Bear factors

The cumulative net loss attributable to controlling shareholders over the last four quarters is KRW 9.25 billion, with the fourth quarter of 2025 the largest at KRW 3.55 billion.

Annual sales down two consecutive years

Sales peaked at KRW 114.5 billion in 2023 and then fell to KRW 82.9 billion in 2024 and KRW 55.2 billion in 2025. Third-quarter 2026 sales were still 27.7% below the same quarter a year earlier.

Capacity competition and fixed-cost pressure

In the domestic CNT duopoly, a January 2023 report put LG Chem's capacity target above Jeio's. Operating cash flow was negative KRW 4.91 billion in 2024, which is relevant alongside the capital investment burden.

Risk factors

End demand and customer concentration

CNT revenue is directly tied to cell makers' capacity additions and EV demand. If the EV slowdown debate persists, customers' pace of adopting CNT conductive agents could slow. Heavy reliance on a few customers would pass any volume adjustments directly into revenue swings.

Fixed costs and capital investment burden

A press report from the second quarter of 2025 attributed lower operating profit to fixed-cost pressure from expanded capacity. When sales decline while fixed costs remain, the return to breakeven slows. Operating cash flow was negative in 2024 and recovered to KRW 872 million in 2025, a small amount.

Process transition and technology substitution

If dry electrode processing spreads, the way conductive agents are supplied and the product mix could change. Single-wall CNT for anodes was in customer sample testing as of January 2023, and commercialization timing has not been reported. Alternative conductive materials could change the pace at which CNT is adopted.

Overall view

Jeio posted profits in 2023, then recorded net losses in 2024 and 2025 and has had operating losses in five straight quarters through the third quarter of 2026.

Sales rose for two consecutive quarters in 2026, from a first-quarter low of KRW 7.17 billion to KRW 11.5 billion in the third quarter, which marks a sign of recovery.

Operating losses over the same stretch stayed around KRW 1.97 billion in the third quarter, so the sales recovery has not yet translated into better profitability. On the business side, the domestic CNT duopoly position and the dry electrode and advanced materials equipment contracts are the growth pillars.

On the other side, annual sales have fallen for two years since the 2023 peak of KRW 114.5 billion, and the four-quarter net loss attributable to controlling shareholders totals KRW 9.25 billion. The debt-to-equity ratio at the end of 2025 was a low 21.9%, but operating cash flow was negative in 2024.

Because strengths and weaknesses coexist, the next checkpoints are the operating margin trend in the 2026 annual business report and whether losses continue in the first quarter of 2027.
