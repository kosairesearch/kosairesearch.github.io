Report overview

Backlog Tops KRW 10 Trillion as Cost Ratio Improves

KUMHO Engineering & Construction built up KRW 2.85 trillion in cumulative new orders and a KRW 10.20 trillion backlog through the third quarter of 2026, and operating profit has risen for three consecutive quarters. The debt ratio of 337.2% at the end of the third quarter and the slump in the housing market remain variables to watch.

  1. 1

    Cumulative new orders through the third quarter of 2026 reached KRW 2.85 trillion, up 91.5% from a year earlier and already above the full-year target of KRW 2.7 trillion (company announcement, November 12, 2026).

  2. 2

    Operating profit in the third quarter of 2026 was KRW 22.2 billion, up 44.1% from a year earlier. The company attributed this to a cost of sales ratio that fell from 93.7% to 91.9%.

  3. 3

    On an annual basis, the company moved from an operating loss of KRW 181.8 billion and a net loss attributable to controlling shareholders of KRW 225.7 billion in 2024 to an operating profit of KRW 47.1 billion and net profit attributable to controlling shareholders of KRW 63.9 billion in 2025.

  4. 4

    The debt ratio fell from 520.2% at the end of 2025 to 337.2% at the end of the third quarter of 2026 (company announcement). Perpetual convertible bonds issued in October are scheduled to be reflected in equity in the fourth quarter.

  5. 5

    Housing accounts for the largest share of the backlog at KRW 4.65 trillion. Because the company relies on LH private-participation public housing orders amid a housing market slowdown, this is the key variable.

02

Business structure

KUMHO Engineering & Construction is a general contractor centered on building, housing and civil engineering, with a comparatively small overseas backlog.

Cumulative new orders through the third quarter of 2026 of KRW 2.85 trillion were split into building at KRW 1.28 trillion, housing at KRW 928 billion and civil engineering at KRW 640 billion, and each division grew from a year earlier (company announcement, November 12, 2026).

The backlog stood at KRW 10.20 trillion at the same date, made up of housing at KRW 4.65 trillion, building at KRW 3.04 trillion, civil engineering at KRW 2.39 trillion and overseas at KRW 120 billion.

Because housing is the largest part of the backlog, the flow of housing orders shapes the weight of the business as a whole.

The housing division is building its order base under the ARTERA apartment brand, while securing public works that offer relatively stable project economics as local unsold housing keeps the market weak (ETtoday-style economic press coverage cited by iToday, October 2026).

The core project type is private-participation public housing ordered by the Korea Land & Housing Corporation (LH), where cumulative orders through the third quarter reached KRW 1.41 trillion.

On the non-housing side, the Jangji vehicle depot three-dimensional conversion project (KRW 175.3 billion, won in July) and the Samsung Electronics Flakt Korea factory construction contract signed in August are representative, and the company is extending into complex public facility development and advanced industrial facilities.

Newly disclosed contracts in November 2026 include the Songdo Severance Hospital construction project with Yonsei University Health System (KRW 372.0 billion) and the relocation project for the Livestock Resources Development Division of the National Institute of Animal Science (KRW 327.9 billion).

Segment revenue shares are not included in the disclosed results, so the order mix and backlog mix are the practical way to gauge the weight of each business.

For comparison with listed peers in the same sector, such as Daewoo Engineering & Construction, GS Engineering & Construction and DL E&C, the starting point is to place order mix, cost ratio and debt ratio side by side.

04

Earnings analysis

Based on the disclosed annual results, revenue was KRW 2.05 trillion in 2022, KRW 2.22 trillion in 2023 (up 8.3% year on year), KRW 1.91 trillion in 2024 (down 13.7% year on year) and KRW 2.02 trillion in 2025 (up 5.4% year on year). 2023 was the largest year and 2024 the smallest year in the 2022 to 2025 period.

Operating profit fell from KRW 55.9 billion in 2022 to KRW 21.8 billion in 2023 (down 61.0% year on year), then turned into an operating loss of KRW 181.8 billion in 2024 and returned to an operating profit of KRW 47.1 billion in 2025. Operating margin was 2.7% in 2022, 1.0% in 2023, -9.5% in 2024 and 2.3% in 2025.

A 2024 research report from Kyobo Securities pointed to large loss-clearing in the third quarter of 2024 as the reason for the 2024 loss, which shows that cost burden was the central variable for profitability.

Net profit attributable to controlling shareholders was KRW 21.2 billion in 2022, KRW 1.11 billion in 2023 (down 94.8% year on year), a net loss of KRW 225.7 billion in 2024 and KRW 63.9 billion in 2025.

Net profit including non-controlling interests was a net loss of KRW 228.5 billion in 2024 and KRW 62.4 billion in 2025, which differs from the controlling-shareholder figure.

Quarterly, operating profit went from KRW 15.4 billion in the third quarter of 2025 to KRW 9.79 billion in the fourth quarter (down 36.3% quarter on quarter), then rose to KRW 12.1 billion in the first quarter of 2026, KRW 16.5 billion in the second quarter and KRW 22.2 billion in the third quarter, an increase in each of three consecutive quarters.

Third-quarter 2026 revenue was KRW 494.4 billion, down 5.5% year on year, while operating profit rose 44.1%.

Net profit attributable to controlling shareholders jumped to KRW 4.43 billion... more precisely KRW 44.3 billion in the fourth quarter of 2025 before falling to KRW 10.9 billion in the first quarter of 2026 (down 75.3% quarter on quarter), then KRW 14.1 billion in the second quarter and KRW 9.94 billion in the third quarter, a sizable swing between quarters.

The sum over the latest four quarters was KRW 79.3 billion. Operating cash flow was KRW 57.1 billion in 2022, -KRW 154.5 billion in 2023, KRW 80.3 billion in 2024 and KRW 51 billion in 2025, with large swings from year to year.

05

Industry analysis

The construction industry is affected both by the housing sales market and by the flow of public orders.

In an October 2026 article, iToday analyzed that KUMHO Engineering & Construction is leaning into public housing orders because public works offer relatively stable order flow while the housing market remains weak, with local unsold homes among the factors.

The company said that its housing division will expand orders mainly through LH private-participation public housing projects in line with government housing supply policy, and that it will selectively win private housing projects in prime sites.

On the non-housing side, demand for facilities and production plants tied to the expansion of the semiconductor industry has been cited.

The company said it will widen its business into advanced industrial facilities such as the Honam semiconductor cluster and into renewable energy infrastructure including the energy expressway. In terms of cycle position, the housing division is in a downturn while public and industrial facility orders fill the gap.

In 2024, Kyobo Securities described the construction sector's share prices as weak because of concerns over project financing risk and profit erosion from rising construction costs.

In the same report, the company was expected to keep relatively stable earnings thanks to lower project financing exposure and a high share of public works.

Listed peers in the same sector include Daewoo Engineering & Construction, GS Engineering & Construction, DL E&C and Hyundai Engineering & Construction, so order mix, cost ratio and debt ratio are the natural points for side-by-side comparison.

The company's strengths are its public works share and its housing backlog, and its weaknesses are its high debt ratio and its exposure to the housing downturn.

06

Outlook

Among the company's own targets, the full-year 2026 new order goal of KRW 2.7 trillion has already been exceeded on a cumulative basis through the third quarter. The company said it will keep its profitability improvement on track based on its ample backlog.

The backlog of KRW 10.20 trillion at the end of the third quarter equals about five times 2025 annual revenue, so how quickly this backlog converts into revenue is the next point to check.

Among the major contracts, the Songdo Severance Hospital construction project is worth KRW 372.0 billion, runs from November 12, 2026 to December 31, 2028, carries no down payment or advance payment, and is billed as work progresses.

The disclosure states that the contract amount and period may change with design modifications.

The relocation project for the Livestock Resources Development Division (KRW 327.9 billion) runs from September 1, 2027 to April 12, 2031, so its revenue contribution is expected to begin in and after the second half of 2027.

The Samsung Electronics Flakt Korea factory construction project, with a total floor area of 23,588 square meters, will house a main HVAC factory and laboratories.

On the financial side, the company said the perpetual convertible bonds issued in October will be reflected in fourth-quarter 2026 equity and will push the debt ratio lower. The next point to confirm is whether operating margin and debt ratio in the 2026 annual business report continue the third-quarter trend.

In housing, execution of LH private-participation public housing projects and selective wins in private housing will determine the order trajectory.

08

Bull factors

Early achievement of order target and a larger backlog

Cumulative new orders through the third quarter of 2026 of KRW 2.85 trillion already exceed the full-year target of KRW 2.7 trillion and rose 91.5% from a year earlier. The backlog reached KRW 10.20 trillion, up 9.8% from the end of 2024, and equals about five times annual revenue.

Orders rose in every division, and LH private-participation public housing alone accounted for KRW 1.41 trillion.

Operating profit up for three consecutive quarters on cost improvement

Operating profit bottomed at KRW 9.79 billion in the fourth quarter of 2025 and then rose to KRW 12.1 billion in the first quarter of 2026, KRW 16.5 billion in the second quarter and KRW 22.2 billion in the third quarter, an increase in three consecutive quarters.

According to the company, the cost of sales ratio fell from 93.7% to 91.9% in the third quarter, lifting operating margin to 4.5%. Cost improvement lifted profit even as revenue declined.

Visible path to a stronger balance sheet

The company said the perpetual convertible bonds issued in October will be reflected in fourth-quarter equity and lower the ratio further. Read alongside the decline from 588.8% at the end of 2024 to 520.2% at the end of 2025, the balance sheet is moving in a lighter direction.

09

Bear factors

Revenue declining and a thin earnings base

Third-quarter 2026 revenue was KRW 494.4 billion, down 5.5% year on year. Operating margin was 2.3% in 2025 and -9.5% in 2024. The 4.5% margin in the third quarter of 2026 is a sign of recovery, but for a company with annual revenue of about KRW 2 trillion, the earnings base remains thin.

Housing downturn and dependence on public orders

Housing is the largest part of the backlog, so housing market conditions can affect how the backlog converts into revenue. With the housing market weak amid local unsold homes, the company is leaning on public orders such as LH private-participation public housing. Any slowdown or delay in public orders could affect the order flow and the pace of revenue conversion.

Large quarterly swings and past heavy losses

Net profit attributable to controlling shareholders fell 75.3% quarter on quarter, from KRW 44.3 billion in the fourth quarter of 2025 to KRW 10.9 billion in the first quarter of 2026.

In 2024 the company recorded an operating loss of KRW 181.8 billion and a net loss attributable to controlling shareholders of KRW 225.7 billion, following large loss-clearing in the third quarter of 2024. Profit recovery is under way, but quarterly swings should be read alongside that history.

10

Risk factors

Market risk · Housing downturn

If the housing market slump persists amid local unsold homes, private housing orders could stall and dependence on public orders would grow. Because the KRW 4.65 trillion housing backlog is the center of the business, a weaker sales market directly affects construction starts and revenue conversion.

The company has said it will only selectively win private housing projects, which may limit the speed of order intake.

Financial risk · Debt burden

Total liabilities at the end of 2025 were KRW 1.26 trillion and total equity was KRW 242.6 billion. The debt ratio of 337.2% at the end of the third quarter is lower but still high. Whether the perpetual convertible bonds are reflected in fourth-quarter equity as planned is the check on the balance sheet improvement.

Earnings risk · Cost and contract terms

The third-quarter cost of sales ratio of 91.9% improved but remains above 90%. The Songdo Severance Hospital disclosure states that the contract amount and period may change with design modifications, and payment is tied to construction progress without a down payment or advance payment. Cost increases or schedule delays could change the pace of the earnings recovery.

12

Overall view

KUMHO Engineering & Construction's cumulative new orders through the third quarter of 2026 reached KRW 2.85 trillion, above its full-year target, and its backlog of KRW 10.20 trillion equals about five times annual revenue.

Operating profit has risen for three consecutive quarters since bottoming in the fourth quarter of 2025, and the cost of sales ratio disclosed by the company has fallen. The company returned to operating profit of KRW 47.1 billion and net profit attributable to controlling shareholders of KRW 63.9 billion in 2025.

Yet the 2024 operating loss of KRW 181.8 billion and net loss of KRW 225.7 billion remain in the record, and quarterly swings in net profit attributable to controlling shareholders are large. The debt ratio fell from 520.2% at the end of 2025 to 337.2% at the end of the third quarter but remains high.

Because housing is the largest part of the backlog, the housing downturn and the flow of public orders remain the variables that shape how the business develops.

The next points to check are the reflection of the perpetual convertible bonds in fourth-quarter 2026 equity, the fourth-quarter and full-year 2026 results, and the operating margin and debt ratio in the 2026 annual business report.
