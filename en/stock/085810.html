Business structure

Alticast is a digital broadcasting software company founded in 1999. Its core business is supplying middleware and cloud-based broadcast solutions that run on set-top boxes and other broadcast devices to broadcasters and device makers.

In the first half of 2020, the media solutions line accounted for about 71% of revenue (calculated from reported figures), and the OVP/OTT line for 21.5%. About 37% of media solutions revenue in that period came from exports (calculated).

On a consolidated basis for the first half of 2023, the digital broadcast quality assurance (QA) business made up 62.25% of revenue and the cloud-based solution business 30.44%. In April 2025 the company acquired Daehyun Gigi, an HVAC installation contractor, adding a construction segment to consolidated revenue.

A June 2026 company profile from WiseReport shows construction revenue at 93.65% of sales, which means the broadcast software share of revenue has shrunk. Breakdowns differ by source and date, and another compilation shows QA at 59.38% and MW solutions at 31.44%, so the mix should be read with its source and date.

Subsidiary AltiPlatform is pursuing a blockchain-based enterprise payment and promotion solution business. Competition comes from overseas set-top box middleware suppliers and from broadcasters' in-house development capabilities.

04

Earnings analysis

Based on the regular reports, revenue is confirmed only for 2025, at KRW 53.2 billion, while no revenue data are available for 2022 through 2024.

Operating results were a loss of KRW 8.89 billion in 2022, a loss of KRW 6.28 billion in 2023, and a loss of KRW 4.02 billion in 2024, before turning to an operating profit of KRW 3.18 billion in 2025. The 2025 operating margin was 6.0%.

Press coverage linked the 2025 turn to profit to the results of Daehyun Gigi, acquired in April 2025.

Attributable net income was a loss of KRW 17.8 billion in 2022, a loss of KRW 26 billion in 2023, a loss of KRW 13 billion in 2024, and a loss of KRW 813 million in 2025, which the filings describe as four consecutive years of losses from 2022 to 2025.

Net income including non-controlling interests was KRW 12.8 million in 2025, a profit, while the attributable share was a loss, so the two figures had opposite signs. The debt-to-equity ratio moved from 48.6% in 2022 to 143.7% in 2023, then fell to 21.6% in 2024 and rose to 38.6% in 2025.

At the end of 2025, total equity was KRW 54 billion, equity attributable to controlling shareholders was KRW 50.8 billion, and total liabilities were KRW 20.8 billion.

Operating cash flow was -KRW 8.83 billion in 2022, -KRW 3.77 billion in 2023, -KRW 3.84 billion in 2024, and -KRW 2.16 billion in 2025, negative in all four years.

On a quarterly basis, operating profit of KRW 2.18 billion and attributable net income of KRW 1.8 billion in the third quarter of 2025 gave way to operating profit of KRW 338 million in the fourth quarter (down 84.5% from the prior quarter) and an attributable net loss of KRW 3.85 billion.

Revenue of KRW 24.3 billion in the second quarter of 2026 rose 39.2% from the prior quarter, but the company still posted an operating loss of KRW 268 million. Third-quarter 2026 revenue fell 8.4% to KRW 22.3 billion while the operating loss widened to KRW 1.75 billion.

The third-quarter figures show revenue growth not carrying through to profit, and the cost-line drivers need to be confirmed in the next annual report.

05

Industry analysis

Alticast's demand base is investment in broadcast platforms such as set-top boxes, IPTV and OTT services. Middleware orders tend to rise when devices are replaced or services expand, so the business follows broadcasters' capex cycles.

In the first half of 2023, the QA business was the largest line at 62.25% of revenue, and the cloud-based solution business accounted for 30.44%. A 2013 press report cited a 91% domestic broadcast market share, with 62% in IPTV, but that figure is more than a decade old and cannot be read as a current share.

The same report said royalty revenue was 70% of sales in the year it covered, which points to a license-based revenue model. Current competition is generally seen as depending on overseas middleware suppliers and broadcasters' in-house platform strategies.

With construction at 93.65% of revenue as of June 2026, the company's industry position is shifting from a broadcast software company toward a group that includes an equipment construction segment.

Because the business mix now differs from that of other listed IT and software companies, simple multiple comparisons carry less meaning. Quarterly results are sensitive to the timing of individual orders in a platform industry that is still in transition.

Whether the third-quarter loss came from the software or construction segment can be determined only from segment disclosures.

The company also expects its subsidiary AltiPlatform's B2B business to produce visible results soon.

At the annual general meeting on March 24, 2026, shareholders approved the financial statements, a capital reduction, and the offset of accumulated deficits from capital reserves, and the reduction was completed on April 22, 2026.

The company explained that once the accumulated deficits are fully offset, dividends or share cancellations become possible when profits arise. As of July 13, 2026, cancellation of 157,438 common shares reduced the listed share count to 14,136,953.

The 1,231,404 new shares issued on November 4, 2026 through a third-party allotment were listed on November 17, bringing the total to 15,368,357, and all of them are subject to a lock-up until November 16, 2027.

The Korea Exchange said the company could be designated an administrative issue if its market capitalization stays below KRW 20 billion for five further trading days from November 12. The 2026 annual business report is typically filed by March 31, 2027.

The next checkpoints are whether full-year 2026 operating profit stays positive, how fourth-quarter results compare with the third-quarter loss, and whether the administrative issue designation takes place.

Risk factors

Business mix and customer concentration

Broadcast solutions depend on the investment cycles of broadcasters and device makers, and in the first half of 2023 the QA business accounted for 62.25% of revenue. Some compilations show the broadcast software share shrinking after the construction segment was added, which complicates the reading of results. Segment profitability has to be confirmed from segment information in the regular reports.

New business and overseas execution

Subsidiary AltiPlatform's blockchain-based enterprise payment and promotion B2B business is expected by the company to show visible results, but these have not yet appeared in reported earnings. Broader North American operations and lower fixed-cost structures may also fall short of expectations.

If new-business revenue is delayed and fixed costs stay high, the durability of the return to profit could be limited, making execution a key variable.

Competitive landscape shifts

In set-top box middleware, competition from overseas suppliers continues, and if broadcast platform operators build up in-house development capabilities, outside middleware orders could decline. Because the company's revenue model is license-based, such a shift would reach royalty revenue directly.

If price competition with overseas suppliers intensifies, a recovery in profitability in the broadcast software business could also be delayed.

Overall view

Alticast swung from an operating loss to an operating profit of KRW 3.18 billion in 2025, and its debt-to-equity ratio of 38.6% in 2025 was lower than the 143.7% recorded in 2023.

However, operating losses have run for three straight quarters from the first quarter through the third quarter of 2026, and attributable net losses over the last four quarters total KRW 9.14 billion.

The 2026 capital reduction and deficit offset reshaped the equity base, and in November the 1,231,404 new shares were listed, increasing the share count. Strengths include the return to profit, improved financial structure, the construction segment added through the Daehyun Gigi acquisition, and new business efforts.

Weaknesses include continued quarterly operating losses, negative operating cash flow, a higher share count, and the administrative issue designation risk. The reason the third-quarter revenue increase failed to reach profit should be confirmed from the regular reports.

The next checkpoints are full-year 2026 operating profit and fourth-quarter results in the 2026 annual report, and whether the administrative issue designation is made. Dividends and share cancellations are now possible only after profits arise, so shareholder returns are tied to a recovery in earnings.
