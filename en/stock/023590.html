02

Business structure

Daou Technology is an IT services company founded in 1986, with its core anchored in financial IT services that run infrastructure and systems for Kiwoom Securities and other Kiwoom group financial firms.

A May 2024 Daishin Securities report divided the company into communications, commerce, business infrastructure and financial IT segments, and identified financial IT and business infrastructure, which carries the Daou Office groupware and data center revenue, as the main segments.

Financial IT revenue was described as rising when group IT demand grows rather than in direct proportion to brokerage revenue at Kiwoom Securities.

The communications segment includes the Ppurio bulk messaging service and the Enfax internet fax service, while the commerce segment includes the Sabangnet shopping mall management solution. Enterprise software such as the Daou Office groupware and the Terasmail mail solution sits within business infrastructure.

The same report said KRW 379 billion was invested in a Daou cloud data center in Yongin and that operating racks were planned to expand from 650 to 3,000 to 4,000. Data center revenue is split between retail and wholesale customers with different margins, so rack growth does not translate directly into revenue growth.

The customer base spans group affiliates, small merchants and large enterprises. In the domestic large-scale SI market, group-affiliated IT service providers of large conglomerates have long held a large share.

04

Earnings analysis

Annual results show revenue expansion alongside uneven profit. Revenue rose from KRW 9.21 trillion in 2022 to KRW 9.86 trillion in 2023 (up 7.0% year on year), then to KRW 11.6 trillion in 2024 (up 18.0%) and KRW 17.5 trillion in 2025 (up 50.5%).

Revenue has increased for three consecutive years from 2023 through 2025, and 2025 is the largest year of the 2022 to 2025 period.

Operating profit fell from KRW 718.7 billion in 2022 to KRW 639.9 billion in 2023 (down 11.0%), the low point of the period, then rose two straight years to KRW 1.17 trillion in 2024 (up 83.6%) and KRW 1.56 trillion in 2025 (up 32.7%). Operating margin was 7.8% in 2022, 6.5% in 2023, 10.1% in 2024 and 8.9% in 2025.

In 2025 revenue grew sharply, yet operating margin was lower than in 2024, so profitability did not keep pace with expansion.

Net income attributable to controlling shareholders was KRW 372.6 billion in 2022, KRW 226 billion in 2023 (down 39.3%), KRW 355.8 billion in 2024 (up 57.5%) and KRW 505.2 billion in 2025 (up 42.0%).

Net income including non-controlling interests was KRW 680.5 billion, KRW 490 billion, KRW 852.8 billion and KRW 1.16 trillion in the same years, and the gap between the two measures reflects non-controlling interests.

Quarterly revenue moved from KRW 3.47 trillion in Q3 2025 to KRW 5.69 trillion in Q4 2025, KRW 9.48 trillion in Q1 2026 and KRW 16.2 trillion in Q2 2026, before falling 56.5% quarter on quarter to KRW 7.04 trillion in Q3 2026, up 103.0% year on year.

Operating profit was KRW 641.3 billion in Q1 2026, KRW 817.8 billion in Q2 2026 and KRW 434.1 billion in Q3 2026, with the Q3 figure up 1.7% year on year. Net income attributable to controlling shareholders was KRW 90.6 billion in Q3 2026, down 34.5% year on year.

Because operating profit did not keep pace with the revenue surge in the 2026 quarters, the share of revenue converted to operating profit is lower than in Q3 2025. Operating cash flow moved from -KRW 2.02 trillion in 2022 to -KRW 6.53 trillion in 2025, a widening outflow.

The sum of net income attributable to controlling shareholders over the latest four quarters is KRW 701.2 billion.

05

Industry analysis

Domestic IT services move with information technology spending from demand sources such as finance, the public sector and retail, and a structure in which group-affiliated SI providers handle conglomerate IT systems has persisted for a long time.

A 2012 securities analysis said that group-affiliated large IT service providers held about 30% of the domestic system integration market, and it classified Daou Technology as a mid-sized company.

Financial IT grows when IT demand from group financial affiliates such as Kiwoom Securities expands, so the company's growth is partly linked to investment flows at those financial firms.

Large SI competitors such as Samsung SDS, LG CNS and Hyundai Autoever are generally seen as having scale advantages built on group-affiliated contracts.

Daou Technology is trying to differentiate in infrastructure by combining its own data center, colocation and cloud services, and Daishin Securities projected in 2024 that data center revenue would gradually grow.

The messaging and commerce solution businesses overlap with platform companies such as NHN and Kakao in customer touchpoints. Cloud migration and data center expansion are industry-wide investment trends, and profitability can shift with rental rates and utilization even when demand rises.

Whether the sharp swings in 2026 quarterly revenue stem from industry conditions or from business-specific factors can be separated using segment disclosures.

06

Outlook

The data center business is cited as the company's mid-term growth pillar. According to a May 2024 Daishin Securities report, KRW 379 billion was invested in the Daou cloud data center in Yongin, and operating racks were planned to rise from 650 to between 3,000 and 4,000.

The same report placed the timing of reaching that scale around the fifth to sixth year of operation. Retail customers are filled gradually after completion, while wholesale capacity can start operating immediately under pre-contracts.

The revenue contribution from added racks therefore depends on the pace of customer acquisition. Financial IT is linked to IT investment by group affiliates such as Kiwoom Securities, so group IT demand is the basic axis of the earnings outlook.

Revenue rose to KRW 17.5 trillion in 2025, but operating margin of 8.9% was below 10.1% in 2024, so whether growth translates into margin recovery can be gauged from the operating margin trend in the next annual report.

Quarterly results in 2026 swung from the KRW 16.2 trillion range in Q2 to KRW 7.04 trillion in Q3, indicating high quarter-to-quarter volatility. The year-on-year decline in Q3 2026 net income attributable to controlling shareholders is another factor that will shape the direction of profit.

Specific business plans such as the timing of a shift to subscription-based products are not described with figures in the material reviewed here.

08

Bull factors

Scale expansion and earnings recovery

Revenue grew from KRW 9.21 trillion in 2022 to KRW 17.5 trillion in 2025. Operating profit rose two straight years from the 2023 low of KRW 639.9 billion to KRW 1.56 trillion in 2025. Net income attributable to controlling shareholders also rose for two years to KRW 505.2 billion in 2025.

Data center expansion plan

According to a May 2024 Daishin Securities report, KRW 379 billion was invested in the Yongin data center. A plan was presented to expand operating racks from 650 to between 3,000 and 4,000. The report projected that data center revenue would gradually grow.

Captive demand from group IT

The financial IT segment provides IT infrastructure services to group financial affiliates such as Kiwoom Securities. Its revenue has been described as rising when group IT demand increases. That group demand base can act as a stabilizing factor for revenue.

Bear factors

Widening negative operating cash flow

Operating cash flow moved from -KRW 2.02 trillion in 2022 to -KRW 6.53 trillion in 2025, with the outflow widening every year. The outflow did not shrink even in years when profit increased. This trend is the clearest weakness in reading the financial structure.

High debt ratio

Total liabilities at year-end 2025 were KRW 75.5 trillion, far above total equity of KRW 7.49 trillion. The debt-to-equity ratio rose from 849.4% in 2022 to 1,006.9% in 2025. A heavier debt burden relative to equity can increase sensitivity to changes in interest rates and borrowing conditions.

Volatility in quarterly profit

Q3 2026 revenue was KRW 7.04 trillion, down 56.5% from the prior quarter. Net income attributable to controlling shareholders was KRW 90.6 billion in the same quarter, down 34.5% year on year. Operating profit rose 1.7% year on year yet bottom-line profit fell, showing a wide gap across profit levels.

10

Risk factors

Financial structure risk

With total liabilities far exceeding total equity and operating cash outflows continuing, changes in borrowing terms could affect both interest burden and funding conditions. Whether capital is strengthened or the asset structure is adjusted will be a key variable for future financial stability.

Dependence on group demand

The financial IT segment is linked to IT demand from group affiliates such as Kiwoom Securities. If IT investment at group financial firms slows or the group structure changes, revenue in that segment would be affected. Whether the share of external customers grows will be a measure of this dependence.

Data center payback risk

KRW 379 billion was invested in the Yongin data center, and retail customers fill capacity gradually after completion. If rack utilization does not grow as expected, the payback period could lengthen.

Data center revenue is split between retail and wholesale with different margins, so profitability can vary considerably.

Overall view

Daou Technology's revenue grew substantially from 2022 through 2025, and net income attributable to controlling shareholders has risen for two straight years since the 2023 low. However, operating margin in 2025 was 8.9%, below 10.1% in 2024, so profitability did not keep pace with expansion.

Quarterly revenue in 2026 rose into the KRW 16 trillion range before falling to KRW 7.04 trillion in Q3, while operating profit of KRW 434.1 billion stayed near the prior-year level.

Net income attributable to controlling shareholders fell 34.5% year on year to KRW 90.6 billion in Q3, leaving a clear gap between revenue scale and bottom-line profit. Bullish factors include the earnings recovery, the data center expansion plan and the captive demand base from group IT.

Bearish factors include the widening negative operating cash flow, the debt-to-equity ratio of 1,006.9% and quarterly profit volatility. The relative weight of these factors will shift with the figures in upcoming periodic reports.

The next confirmation points are operating cash flow, the debt structure and data center-related revenue trends in the 2026 annual report and the Q1 2027 quarterly report.
