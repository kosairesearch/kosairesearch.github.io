Report overview

High-priced orders lift operating profit, but Q3 net income falls

Operating profit rose 86.1% in 2025 and 24.3% year on year in Q3 2026, yet net income attributable to controlling shareholders fell 43.9% in the same quarter.

02

Business structure

Daehan Shipbuilding builds ships and boats and was listed on the KOSPI market on August 1, 2025. Its core products are Suezmax and Aframax crude oil tankers, and the company highlights repeat construction of the same vessel types as a source of production efficiency.

All four major orders disclosed in 2026 were for crude oil tankers, which points to a high concentration in tankers. In January, Nordic American Tankers, a New York-listed shipping company, ordered two Suezmax-class crude oil tankers worth KRW 252 billion.

On May 29, an Oceania shipowner signed a KRW 282.8 billion contract, and on July 24, a European shipowner signed a KRW 271.3 billion contract. The November 11 contract with an Oceania shipowner was worth KRW 249.8 billion, equal to 20.34% of 2025 revenue.

Customers are global shipping companies and shipowners, with sales regions spanning Oceania and Europe. Compared with large listed shippers such as HD Korea Shipbuilding & Offshore Engineering, Hanwha Ocean and Samsung Heavy Industries, its production scale is smaller and its vessel concentration is relatively high. Segment revenue shares are not covered here because no verified figures were available.

04

Earnings analysis

On a filed basis, 2025 revenue was KRW 1.23 trillion, up 14.2% from the prior year, and operating profit was KRW 294.1 billion, up 86.1%. The operating margin rose from 14.7% in 2024 to 23.9% in 2025, so profit grew much faster than revenue.

Net income attributable to controlling shareholders rose 44.1%, from KRW 172.7 billion to KRW 248.8 billion, and net income including non-controlling interests was reported at the same levels over the same period. The debt-to-equity ratio fell from 197.6% in 2024 to 40.8% in 2025.

At the end of 2025, total liabilities were KRW 445.7 billion and total equity was KRW 1.09 trillion, all of which was attributable to controlling shareholders. Operating cash flow rose from KRW 158.3 billion in 2024 to KRW 162 billion in 2025.

Quarterly, revenue climbed from KRW 274 billion in Q3 2025 with operating profit of KRW 66.6 billion to KRW 350.4 billion with KRW 95.3 billion in Q4. Q1 2026 revenue was KRW 308.3 billion, down 12.0% quarter on quarter, with operating profit of KRW 82.6 billion, down 13.3%.

Q2 2026 recovered to KRW 354.4 billion in revenue and KRW 95.2 billion in operating profit. Q3 2026 revenue was KRW 327.7 billion, down 7.5% quarter on quarter and up 19.6% year on year, and operating profit was KRW 82.7 billion, down 13.1% quarter on quarter and up 24.3% year on year.

Net income attributable to controlling shareholders went from KRW 65.8 billion in Q3 2025 to KRW 78.7 billion in Q4, KRW 77.4 billion in Q1 2026 and KRW 78.7 billion in Q2, then down to KRW 36.9 billion in Q3.

According to a Digital Today report, Q3 pre-tax income was KRW 48.6 billion, down from KRW 103.9 billion in Q2 on a preliminary basis. Because the drop in net income (53.1%) far exceeds the drop in operating profit (13.1%), non-operating items and taxes are the key question.

Net income attributable to controlling shareholders over the last four quarters (Q4 2025 to Q3 2026) totaled KRW 271.7 billion.

05

Industry analysis

Tanker orders continued to flow in the shipbuilding market, and mid-sized shipbuilders also posted higher cumulative profits. A DailyIan report dated November 16, 2026 said Daehan Shipbuilding, HJ Heavy Industries and K-Shipbuilding all increased profits in the first three quarters.

The same report cited an industry official who said demand for replacing aging oil tankers and tighter environmental rules continues to support orders. The cycle position is shaped by the time lag between when orders are placed and when revenue is recognized.

Seoul Finance reported that the start of construction on high-priced vessels ordered in 2024 drove the profit gains. DailyIan noted that work secured at high prices is reflected in earnings after passing through the construction process.

Press reports said the shipowner on the November 2026 contract is a New York-listed shipping company that resumed newbuild tanker orders for the first time in about 20 years. Competitively, Daehan Shipbuilding is a mid-sized yard with strength in Suezmax tankers.

Large shipbuilders are the main beneficiaries of the upcycle, while mid-sized yards are responding by concentrating on specific vessel types and repeat construction to raise margins.

06

Outlook

The company has not presented formal 2026 full-year guidance in its filings. Instead, the order flow and delivery schedule frame future revenue. Including the November 11, 2026 contract, the 2026 cumulative orders reached 19 vessels, and press reports put the order backlog at KRW 4.66 trillion.

The vessels contracted in January 2026 are scheduled for delivery in October and November 2028. The May 29 contract runs to December 31, 2028, and the July 24 contract runs to December 31, 2029. The November 11 contract runs to June 28, 2030, with deliveries scheduled for May and June 2030.

Contract end dates are tied to delivery and may shift with construction progress. On shareholder returns, the company resolved a policy to return 30% of net income over three years. It also continues to combine dividends with share buybacks and cancellations.

Industry commentary notes that expanding into new vessel types could bring added costs. The 2026 annual business report, the next periodic filing, will confirm full-year actual results and fourth-quarter figures for the first time, and the first-quarter 2027 preliminary results are the next checkpoint after that.

08

Bull factors

High-priced backlog flowing into revenue

Seoul Finance reported that profits rose as high-priced vessels ordered in 2024 entered full construction. The same report said the company posted operating margins in the 20% range for eight consecutive quarters.

DailyIan reported that the operating margin for January to September 2026 was 26.3%, above the prior-year period. The sustained margin level is cited as evidence of cost competitiveness and the benefit of repeat construction.

Order accumulation and long delivery schedule

Orders in 2026 have built up to 19 vessels once the November 11, 2026 contract is added, and the reported order backlog is KRW 4.66 trillion. Delivery schedules run from 2028 through 2030, so construction volume is secured over several years.

Vessels from the November 2026 contract are set for delivery in May and June 2030. The long delivery schedule is cited as a factor that raises the visibility of future revenue.

Shareholder return plan

Seoul Finance reported that the company resolved a shareholder-friendly plan to return 30% of net income over three years. Law Issue reported that the company keeps a policy combining dividends with share buybacks and cancellations.

Net income attributable to controlling shareholders rose 44.1% in 2025, which shows the earnings base behind the return policy. Because the plan is tied to net income, its size will move with earnings.

09

Bear factors

Sharp drop in Q3 net income

Net income attributable to controlling shareholders of KRW 36.9 billion in Q3 2026 was 43.9% lower than a year earlier and 53.1% lower than the prior quarter. Operating profit in the same quarter rose to KRW 82.7 billion, but the decline in net income was far larger, leaving a clear gap between profit levels.

Pre-tax income also fell to KRW 48.6 billion from the prior quarter. Explanations for non-operating factors are central at this stage.

Currency and working-day headwinds

Seoul Finance reported that working days fell by four from the prior quarter and that the average won-dollar rate dropped from 1,502 to 1,423 in Q3. The company said it held the single-quarter operating margin at 25.3% despite these unfavorable conditions.

Even so, worsening conditions could weigh on revenue recognition and margins. Exchange rates and operating days remain external drivers of quarterly swings.

Vessel concentration and slower revenue growth

Digital Today reported that Q1 2026 revenue rose only 0.2% year on year. All major orders disclosed in 2026 were for crude oil tankers, so earnings are tied to the tanker ordering cycle. If tanker orders slow, a gap in new contracts could affect later revenue. Concentration in one vessel type raises margins during an upcycle but also increases exposure in a downturn.

Risk factors

Market cycle and vessel concentration

Because disclosed orders are centered on crude oil tankers, earnings are linked to tanker ordering and freight trends. Replacement of aging tankers and tighter environmental rules support demand, but a slowdown could affect order pace and newbuild prices. Industry commentary warns that expanding into other vessel types may add costs.

Currency and cost pressure

The average won-dollar rate fell to 1,423 in Q3 2026 from 1,502 in the prior quarter. Depending on contract currency structure, the won-denominated value of revenue may be reduced. Fewer working days weighed on output efficiency. The company said it maintained production flow through dock utilization and process efficiency.

Contract terms and delivery timing

Contracts are paid in line with construction progress, and the contract end date is set by delivery, so it may change as work proceeds. A delay in delivery could push back revenue recognition. Down payments and advance payments bring some cash forward, but final collections remain tied to construction progress.

Overall view

Daehan Shipbuilding's 2025 results showed a sharp rise in earnings capacity, with revenue up 14.2%, operating profit up 86.1% and net income attributable to controlling shareholders up 44.1%. The operating margin rose to 23.9%, and the debt-to-equity ratio fell to 40.8%.

Quarterly revenue in 2026 moved around the KRW 300 billion level, and operating profit ranged between KRW 82.6 billion and KRW 95.2 billion. However, Q3 2026 net income attributable to controlling shareholders fell 53.1% quarter on quarter to KRW 36.9 billion, so operating-level and net-income-level trends diverged.

On orders, cumulative vessels reached 19 with a reported backlog of KRW 4.66 trillion, and deliveries extend through 2030. The shareholder return plan commits to returning 30% of net income over three years.

Bullish factors are the revenue recognition of high-priced orders, order accumulation and shareholder returns, while bearish factors are the sharp Q3 net income drop, currency and working-day headwinds, and concentration in tankers.

The next checkpoints are the non-operating and tax structure in the 2026 annual business report, first-quarter 2027 preliminary results, and cumulative orders at the end of 2026.
