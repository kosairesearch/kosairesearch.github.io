Revenue Keeps Growing, but Operating Losses Run Three Quarters

Revenue rose for three straight years through 2025 to a record level, but operating profit peaked in 2023, fell for two consecutive years, and has been in operating loss from the first through the third quarter of 2026.

02

Business structure

UbiVelox runs a mixed portfolio spanning smart cards, black boxes, home appliance distribution and mobile services. According to JobKorea company data as of March 2026, the revenue mix was roughly 52% home and environmental appliances, about 23% black boxes and about 17% smart cards.

The home appliance unit distributes a robot vacuum brand in Korea and is the largest contributor to total revenue.

Smart cards are built on the company's own chip operating system and cover credit and check cards, USIM and eSIM for telecom use, electronic resident cards and mobile driver's licenses, with major customers among Korean banks, card issuers and the three telecom operators.

SK Securities analyzed on March 20, 2026 that this unit is growing on the back of its exclusive market position. High-value cards such as metal cards are the growth driver, and the company said on May 14, 2026 that metal card supply in the first quarter rose by more than 80% from a year earlier.

The black box business holds the top domestic share and relies heavily on exports, with roughly 59% of black box revenue exported as of year-end 2025, expanding into North America and Europe.

In automotive electronics the company is broadening its range from black boxes into camera products, smart audio and switch controllers, and it entered the vehicle aftermarket on the back of a global supply agreement with BMW and expanded supply to Mercedes-Benz Korea. Headcount stood at 302 as of June 30, 2026.

04

Earnings analysis

On a reported basis, annual revenue rose from KRW 449.3 billion in 2022 to KRW 543.6 billion in 2023 (up 21.0%), KRW 607.1 billion in 2024 (up 11.7%) and KRW 667.9 billion in 2025 (up 10.0%), marking three consecutive years of growth through 2025. 2025 was the largest year of the 2022 to 2025 span, and SK Securities and local media described that year's revenue as a record.

Operating profit went from KRW 14.8 billion in 2022 to KRW 49.3 billion in 2023 (up 233.8%), the peak, then fell to KRW 34.1 billion in 2024 (down 30.9%) and KRW 21.5 billion in 2025 (down 37.1%), a two-year decline.

The operating margin was 3.3% in 2022, 9.1% in 2023, 5.6% in 2024 and 3.2% in 2025, so margins fell after 2023 even as sales grew.

According to the company, 2025 sales were lifted by black box exports, strong environmental appliance sales and the entry into K-culture accessories, while profit was hit by higher chip-related cost ratios, higher selling and administrative expenses, an impairment on operating assets and a weaker won.

Net income attributable to controlling shareholders fell from KRW 18.8 billion in 2023 (up 608.4%) to KRW 14.2 billion in 2024 (down 24.0%) and then turned into a KRW 7.23 billion net loss in 2025. Net income including non-controlling interests was a KRW 19.4 billion loss in 2025.

Quarterly operating profit slipped from KRW 5.02 billion in the third quarter of 2025 to KRW 4.38 billion in the fourth quarter (down 12.8%), then turned into operating losses of KRW 2.43 billion, KRW 2.02 billion and KRW 1.96 billion across the first three quarters of 2026, the third straight loss quarter.

Revenue rose to KRW 172.6 billion in the second quarter of 2026 (up 12.5% quarter on quarter) before falling to KRW 138.4 billion in the third quarter (down 19.8% quarter on quarter and 15.2% year on year).

Net income attributable to controlling shareholders was KRW 305 million in the first quarter and KRW 1.89 billion in the second quarter, then became a KRW 3.77 billion loss in the third quarter. Operating cash flow went from KRW 24.4 billion in 2024 to an outflow of KRW 27.4 billion in 2025.

05

Industry analysis

For the Korean smart card market, Kiwoom Securities said in an earlier report that around 120 million cards are issued annually and that UbiVelox holds a share in the 30% range and ranks first. That figure is from an older report and may not reflect the current share.

High-value cards carry higher unit prices than plain plastic cards and are cited as a factor reshaping the revenue mix. The company said that high-value card supply in 2026 has already exceeded full-year 2025 supply, which indicates the shift is accelerating.

In black boxes, tighter North American rules on Chinese-made products are a variable that the company sees as an opportunity for products whose technology has been recognized. In home appliances, an SK Securities analysis noted steady growth in domestic distribution of the Roborock robot vacuum.

On the other hand, a summary from a company data provider said that in the first half of 2026 revenue fell in home appliances and black boxes on weaker demand and intensifying competition, as of August 31, 2026.

On the input side, higher memory and other component prices are weighing on costs, and the company said it is offsetting this with black box price adjustments and higher shipment volumes.

06

Outlook

On May 14, 2026, the company said it aims to make 2026 the first year of diversifying its automotive electronics portfolio. In its February 26, 2026 results announcement, it said it expects a good performance in 2026, citing steady growth in overseas black boxes and home appliances and the expansion of new businesses.

In smart cards, the company plans to grow financial services based on the domestic payment standard following the full introduction of KLSC, and to extend into mobile and contactless payments. In security, it is upgrading post-quantum cryptography based technology.

In mobility, it is studying ways to extend vision AI, built on driving video data from its black boxes, into other industries such as robotics, and it said on August 18, 2026 that it has commercialized an AI black box built on an AI chip used in autonomous driving platforms.

Its digital twin business and overseas push for its map platform are also under way. The company is also pursuing commercialization of its intellectual property with global big tech firms, automakers' OEM suppliers and mobility companies.

How much these new businesses will contribute to revenue is hard to gauge from the segment information disclosed so far. The next checkpoint is when the 2026 annual report discloses full-year operating margin together with segment revenue for these new businesses.

08

Bull factors

Expanding high-value card supply

The company said metal card supply in the first quarter of 2026 rose by more than 80% from a year earlier. It also said on August 18, 2026 that high-value card supply in 2026 had already surpassed full-year 2025 supply.

Post-quantum cryptography security and the KLSC rollout are presented as platforms for widening its payments business.

Black box demand opportunity from North American rules

Tighter rules on Chinese-made products are taking hold in North America. The company expects demand for its technology-recognized products could grow. With roughly 59% of black box revenue exported, the business is also highly exposed to this market shift.

Broadening automotive customer base

Media reported that a supply agreement with BMW's global headquarters demonstrated the company's technology competitiveness. Mercedes-Benz Korea has been added as a new customer, giving it entry into the vehicle aftermarket.

The company is widening its product range beyond black boxes into camera products, smart audio and switch controllers.

09

Bear factors

Three straight quarters of operating losses

Operating losses ran from the first through the third quarter of 2026, narrowing from KRW 2.43 billion to KRW 1.96 billion but not yet ending. The last four quarters total a KRW 11.9 billion loss attributable to controlling shareholders. Operating cash flow also turned into a KRW 27.4 billion outflow in 2025.

Cost and selling expense pressure

Higher chip-related cost ratios and rising selling and administrative expenses were cited for the 2025 drop in operating profit. Memory and other component prices are still weighing on costs in 2026.

The company offsets this with black box price adjustments, but whether that offset is sufficient will show up in upcoming quarterly figures.

Revenue decline and softer demand

Third-quarter 2026 revenue was KRW 138.4 billion, down 19.8% quarter on quarter and 15.2% year on year. A company data summary said first-half 2026 revenue fell in home appliances and black boxes on weaker demand and more intense competition. Because home appliance distribution is the largest revenue unit, the impact of this shift is large.

10

Risk factors

Costs and currency

Rising memory and other component prices feed directly into black box and smart card costs. A weaker won was also cited as a factor reducing 2025 profit. The black box business, with its high export share, is exposed to currency swings.

Regulation and external conditions

Tighter North American rules on Chinese-made products are an opportunity for black boxes, but any change in their scope or direction would change the size of that opportunity. Overseas black box sales could become more dependent on rules in specific regions. The pace at which new payment standards and security technologies move into the market also remains uncertain.

Balance sheet and ownership

The debt-to-equity ratio was 110.4% in 2025, with total liabilities of KRW 289.7 billion. Of the KRW 262.3 billion total equity, KRW 132.2 billion belongs to controlling shareholders. The largest shareholder group holds 30.33% and NHN holds 8.50%, as of September 11, 2026.

Overall view

UbiVelox combines smart cards, black boxes and home appliance distribution, and its revenue rose for three consecutive years through 2025.

Operating profit, however, peaked at KRW 49.3 billion in 2023 and fell for two consecutive years, and net income attributable to controlling shareholders swung to a KRW 7.23 billion loss in 2025.

Operating losses ran from the first through the third quarter of 2026, though the size of the loss narrowed from KRW 2.43 billion to KRW 1.96 billion.

On the positive side, the main factors cited are expanding high-value card supply, black box demand opportunities from North American rule changes and a broader automotive customer base.

On the negative side, cost and selling expense pressure, softer demand and rising competition in home appliances and black boxes, and the third-quarter revenue decline are visible. Financially, the debt-to-equity ratio has stayed above 100%, and operating cash flow turned into an outflow in 2025.

Because these signals point in different directions, the next checkpoints are the operating margin and cash flow trend in the 2026 annual report, due in late March 2027, and whether operating losses persist in the first-quarter 2027 report, due in mid-May 2027.
