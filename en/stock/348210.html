Nextin: Testing HBM and New-Tool Growth After Five Loss-Making Quarters

02

Business structure

Nextin is a Korean maker of front-end wafer inspection equipment founded in 2010, and its tools detect micro-pattern defects that arise during circuit fabrication.

According to company materials, its core lineup includes the AEGIS series for front-end inspection, the IRIS series for 3D NAND inspection, and the KROKY series for high-bandwidth memory (HBM) processes.

IRIS-II, a 3D wafer inspection tool using near-infrared light, was supplied to customers in 2024, and KROKY and ASPER were launched in 2025. The company's investor materials position ASPER as a next-generation tool for advanced packaging and say development of hybrid bonding inspection has started.

On customers, the company's IR material records SK hynix as having completed mass-production performance verification in December 2024, and it has run joint development with Intel since 2018. On geography, a Hanwha Investment & Securities report from April 2025 noted that 81% of 2024 revenue came from Greater China.

A News1 report from August 2026 described a growing share of services in revenue relative to product sales. The company operates a China subsidiary set up in 2024 and a Japan subsidiary set up in 2025, and its company profile notes an R&D lab in Israel. Headcount was 152 as of September 2026.

04

Earnings analysis

On a disclosed basis, revenue fell from KRW 114.9 billion in 2022 to KRW 87.9 billion in 2023 (down 23.5% year on year), recovered to KRW 113.7 billion in 2024 (up 29.3%), and then dropped to KRW 66.9 billion in 2025 (down 41.2%). 2022 was the largest year and 2025 the smallest among 2022 to 2025.

The operating margin stayed in the 40% range at 49.2% in 2022, 41.1% in 2023 and 41.3% in 2024, then fell to -1.7% in 2025.

In 2025 the company recorded an operating loss of KRW 1.16 billion, a swing to loss from the prior year, along with a net loss attributable to controlling shareholders of KRW 2.46 billion and a net loss of KRW 2.65 billion including non-controlling interests.

The company cited a contraction in capital spending by front-end customers as the main reason for the 2025 swing.

By quarter, 3Q 2025 revenue was KRW 10.7 billion with an operating loss of KRW 3.67 billion. 4Q 2025 revenue rose 48.3% quarter on quarter to KRW 15.9 billion, and the operating loss narrowed to KRW 3.48 billion. 1Q 2026 revenue was KRW 7.93 billion, down 50.2% quarter on quarter, and the operating loss widened to KRW 5.54 billion. 2Q 2026 revenue rebounded 162.7% to KRW 20.8 billion, the operating loss narrowed to KRW 786 million, and net income attributable to controlling shareholders of KRW 1.39 billion marked a return to profit. 3Q 2026 revenue was KRW 10.2 billion, down 51.2% quarter on quarter and 5.4% year on year, with an operating loss of KRW 2.86 billion and a net loss attributable to controlling shareholders of KRW 6.47 billion, a wider loss than a year earlier.

For the first half of 2026, News1 reported in August 2026 that weak China equipment sales drove the decline in product revenue, and that R&D spending of about KRW 6.2 billion rose roughly 30% from about KRW 4.8 billion a year earlier, equal to 21.7% of revenue. First-half gross margin was about 48%.

Operating cash flow went from KRW 51.1 billion in 2022 and KRW 42.8 billion in 2024 to negative KRW 1.11 billion in 2025, and the debt ratio rose from 20.4% in 2024 to 48.4% in 2025. The reasons cited for earnings swings are the capital spending cycle and the trend in China equipment sales.

05

Industry analysis

Nextin's end-market demand is tied to memory makers' capital spending. The company itself cited a contraction in front-end capital spending as the main reason for the 2025 decline.

After the 3Q 2025 results, brokers described 2025 as a trough period and placed the focus on a turnaround in 2026, according to Hanwha Investment & Securities and Samsung Securities reports.

On demand, the prevailing view in an April 2025 Hanwha Investment & Securities report is that AI chips and HBM expansion are raising demand for precision inspection in stacked processes.

The company's own investor materials put the pattern inspection equipment market at more than KRW 7 trillion, a figure the company estimates itself. China remains a large variable.

In the same period, Samsung Securities said equipment import restrictions were slowing process transitions, but China-bound sales were not fully blocked.

Global inspection peers such as Onto Innovation and Camtek serve as comparison points, and Hanwha Investment & Securities noted in April 2025 that they maintain profitability around 24%.

Nextin points to its joint development history with Intel and its completed SK hynix mass-production verification as evidence of technical credibility.

In April 2026, Daishin Securities projected 2026 revenue of KRW 129.0 billion and operating profit of KRW 37.0 billion, and 2027 revenue of KRW 172.0 billion and operating profit of KRW 60.0 billion, with China-bound revenue expected at KRW 36.4 billion in 2026 and KRW 58.8 billion in 2027.

Reported quarterly revenue for 1Q to 3Q 2026 was KRW 7.93 billion, KRW 20.8 billion and KRW 10.2 billion, and all three quarters carried operating losses, so the gap between full-year estimates and disclosed results is large.

As of October 23, 2026, the average broker estimate compiled by WiseReport put the 4Q 2026 operating loss at KRW 500 million and 2027 revenue at KRW 119.6 billion with operating profit of KRW 26.0 billion.

A April 2026 report said the new ASPER tool was scheduled for launch in the second half of 2026, and brokers have said its customer qualification timing could add to 2026 results.

A March 31, 2026 report said the shares fell after a voluntarily disclosed SK hynix supply contract, with the market reading the contract size as below expectations or already priced in. A supply contract disclosure was filed on October 6, 2026, according to the DART disclosure list.

A April 2026 Daishin Securities report described the China subsidiary as scheduled to begin operations in the second half of 2026. The next checkpoints are the full-year revenue and operating profit confirmed in the 2026 annual report, and the timing at which ASPER revenue is recognized.

Bull factors

Demand from HBM inspection tools

Precision inspection demand is rising in HBM stacking, and the KROKY series is cited as a growth line (Hanwha Investment & Securities, April 2025).

The company's IR material says the KROKY family has expanded to W, R and M variants and records SK hynix mass-production verification completed in December 2024. 2Q 2026 revenue of KRW 20.8 billion is the largest of the last five quarters.

Possible resumption of China orders

Samsung Securities said right after the 3Q 2025 results that China-bound sales are not fully blocked. The company set up a China subsidiary in 2024 and listed completion of a China factory for October 2025 (company IR).

Daishin Securities projected China-bound revenue of KRW 36.4 billion in 2026 and KRW 58.8 billion in 2027 in an April 2026 report.

A profitable quarter and recovery estimates

2Q 2026 was the only quarter of the last five with net income attributable to controlling shareholders, at KRW 1.39 billion. The average broker estimate compiled by WiseReport as of October 23, 2026 puts 2027 operating profit at KRW 26.0 billion, implying a recovery from the 2026 operating loss path. Reports have also said a launch of ASPER in the second half of 2026 would add a new revenue source.

09

Bear factors

Weak China sales and equipment import limits

81% of 2024 revenue came from Greater China (Hanwha Investment & Securities, April 2025). First-half 2026 product sales fell 41% on weak China equipment sales (News1, August 2026). Samsung Securities also said equipment import restrictions were slowing process conversion after the 3Q 2025 results.

R&D costs and margin pressure

First-half 2026 R&D spending was about KRW 6.2 billion, up roughly 30%, equal to 21.7% of revenue (News1, August 2026). Gross margin was about 48% in the first half, yet operating losses persisted.

The 3Q 2026 operating loss was KRW 2.86 billion and the net loss attributable to controlling shareholders was KRW 6.47 billion, both wider than a year earlier.

Revenue volatility and contract reception

Revenue fell 41.2% in 2025, and quarterly revenue in 2026 swung between KRW 7.93 billion, KRW 20.8 billion and KRW 10.2 billion. After a voluntarily disclosed SK hynix supply contract in March 2026, the shares fell because the market read the contract size as below expectations.

Backlog at year-end 2024 stood at KRW 11.7 billion, lower than a year earlier (Hanwha Investment & Securities, April 2025).

Risk factors

China regulation and geopolitical risk

A high share of China-bound revenue means tighter equipment import rules pass directly into sales. The weak China equipment sales in the first half of 2026 already showed up as lower product revenue.

How far the China factory and subsidiary expansion are exposed to this variable needs to be tracked through DART disclosures.

New-tool ramp and revenue timing

If the launch and customer qualification of ASPER slip, growth assumptions for 2026 and beyond are pushed back. A April 2026 report had the tool scheduled for the second half, so whether that timing holds is central.

Quarterly revenue moved between KRW 7.93 billion, KRW 20.8 billion and KRW 10.2 billion, so the timing of new-tool revenue recognition will shape quarterly results.

Financial capacity and cost structure

Operating cash flow turned negative at KRW 11.1 billion in 2025, and the debt ratio rose to 48.4%. Total equity at year-end 2025 was KRW 150.1 billion, all of it attributable to controlling shareholders, against total liabilities of KRW 72.7 billion.

If revenue recovery lags while R&D and headcount costs keep rising, continuing losses would erode that equity base.

Overall view

Nextin's revenue fell from KRW 114.9 billion in 2022 to KRW 66.9 billion in 2025, and the operating margin swung to -1.7% in 2025. Operating losses continued through 3Q 2026, yet 2Q 2026 revenue of KRW 20.8 billion was the largest of the last five quarters.

Strengths include demand for the KROKY series for HBM, the possibility of China orders resuming, and new revenue from ASPER, which is scheduled to launch in the second half of 2026.

Weaknesses include weak China equipment sales and import restrictions, higher R&D costs in the first half, and a wider net loss attributable to controlling shareholders in 3Q 2026.

Total equity of KRW 150.1 billion at year-end 2025 exceeded total liabilities of KRW 72.7 billion, but operating cash flow was negative at KRW 11.1 billion in 2025.

With the last four quarters in net loss, earnings-based multiples cannot be calculated, so asset-based comparisons and whether 2027 profit forecasts materialize are the focus.
