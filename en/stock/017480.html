02

Business structure

Samhyun Steel was founded in 1978, listed on KOSDAQ in 2001, and operates as a primary steel company serving as a hot-rolled coil sales and processing center for the steelmaker that supplies its coil, and as a designated section-steel distributor for Hyundai Steel Company.

It runs a single business segment covering steel processing and sales, buying hot-rolled coil and heavy plate and selling them after coil cutting, plate processing, and section-steel processing.

Based on the 2025 business report, manufactured (processed) products accounted for 50.60% of revenue and merchandise (wholesale) for 49.40%.

Processed products include PIPE PILE and GUARDRAIL structures, containers, and stair and parking-floor materials based on heavy plate, along with automotive interior parts and general machinery components.

Merchandise sales go to shipbuilding, bridges, construction equipment, offshore structures, and general steel structures. Its main customers are in construction, shipbuilding, and heavy equipment, and the largest single customer accounted for 8.90% of 2025 revenue.

The company disclosed a 13.86% market share on 2024 revenue. Hot-rolled supply is tied to the steelmaker that supplies its coil, and section steel to its Hyundai Steel designated-distributor arrangement, so customer relationships, processing capability, and cost control are the main competitive factors.

In 2025 it added online retail and solar power generation to its business purpose, but no specific investment plan has been disclosed. At the end of 2025 it had no borrowings and KRW 106.7 billion in cash and cash equivalents.

04

Earnings analysis

Annual revenue went from KRW 340.6 billion in 2022 to KRW 262.4 billion in 2023 (down 22.9% year on year), KRW 223.7 billion in 2024 (down 14.8%), and KRW 218 billion in 2025 (down 2.5%), a decline of three consecutive years from 2023 through 2025.

Among 2022 to 2025, 2022 was the largest revenue year and 2025 the smallest. Operating profit went from KRW 19.1 billion in 2022 to KRW 9.73 billion in 2023 (down 49.1%) and KRW 3.56 billion in 2024 (down 63.4%), then rose to KRW 5.1 billion in 2025 (up 43.2%).

Among 2022 to 2025, 2022 was the largest operating profit year and 2024 the smallest. The company attributed the 2024 drop to weak domestic steel market conditions, where falling steel prices compressed margin spreads.

In 2025 revenue fell on lower sales volume and price changes, but gross profit rose and cost reduction and efficient sales strategy lifted operating profit, although selling and administrative expenses increased on higher retirement benefit costs.

The operating margin was 5.6% in 2022, 3.7% in 2023, 1.6% in 2024, and 2.3% in 2025, recovering modestly from the 2024 low.

Net income attributable to controlling shareholders was KRW 16 billion in 2022, KRW 10.4 billion in 2023 (down 35.0%), KRW 5.51 billion in 2024 (down 47.1%), and KRW 6.35 billion in 2025 (up 15.2%). Net income including non-controlling interests was reported at the same amounts for each year.

Operating cash flow fell from KRW 38.8 billion in 2022 to KRW 15.9 billion in 2023, KRW 9.24 billion in 2024, and KRW 6.79 billion in 2025.

By quarter, revenue was KRW 53.1 billion in the third quarter of 2025, KRW 58.2 billion in the fourth quarter (up 9.5% from the prior quarter), KRW 57.4 billion in the first quarter of 2026 (down 1.4%), KRW 61.4 billion in the second quarter (up 7.1%), and KRW 53.9 billion in the third quarter of 2026 (down 12.3% sequentially, up 1.5% year on year).

Operating profit was KRW 5.4 billion in the second quarter of 2026, the highest of the period (up 250.1% sequentially), and KRW 1.31 billion in the third quarter (down 75.8% sequentially, up 30.3% year on year).

Net income attributable to controlling shareholders over the last four quarters (fourth quarter 2025 to third quarter 2026) totaled KRW 11 billion.

A corporate performance commentary tied the first-quarter 2026 improvement to supply adjustments, facility maintenance, anti-dumping duties, and a rebound in heavy plate prices.

05

Industry analysis

The hot-rolled and heavy plate processing and distribution market in which Samhyun Steel operates is structured so that earnings swing sharply with demand from downstream construction, shipbuilding, and heavy equipment industries and with steel selling prices.

In 2024, weak domestic steel market conditions pushed steel prices lower and compressed margin spreads. Press coverage of the 2025 business report said weak downstream demand and low-priced imports made sales conditions difficult.

The same coverage noted that demand weakness persisted outside some industries even after anti-dumping duties were imposed.

In the first quarter of 2026, supply adjustments, facility maintenance, and anti-dumping duties combined to lift hot-rolled coil prices, and heavy plate prices rebounded on higher supply prices and lower distribution volumes, according to one analysis.

The pattern reads as a phase in which prices are rebounding while demand has not yet recovered.

Competitively, Samhyun Steel holds a distribution position as a hot-rolled processing center and a section-steel designated distributor, so its profit depends mainly on margins at the processing and distribution stage rather than on manufacturing.

In this structure, the gap between selling prices and raw material costs passes directly into operating margin swings. The disclosed market share on 2024 revenue was 13.86%.

The payout ratio for 2025, the prior fiscal year, was 73.0%, and the company disclosed that it qualifies as a high-dividend company under Korean tax rules. Press coverage from March 2026 said demand weakness was expected to persist and make 2026 difficult.

Quarterly operating profit in 2026 was KRW 1.54 billion in the first quarter, KRW 5.4 billion in the second, and KRW 1.31 billion in the third, showing wide quarter-to-quarter swings. Third-quarter 2026 revenue of KRW 53.9 billion rose only 1.5% year on year.

The item to confirm in the 2026 business report is whether the full-year operating margin exceeds the 2025 level of 2.3% and whether the decline in revenue has stopped.

The debt-free balance sheet and KRW 106.7 billion in cash and cash equivalents at the end of 2025 form the financial base for dividends and investment capacity.

No specific investment plan has been disclosed for the newly added online retail and solar power businesses, so there is not yet data on when they might contribute to results.

Bear factors

Continued revenue decline

Revenue declined for three consecutive years from 2023 through 2025, reaching KRW 218 billion in 2025. Third-quarter 2026 revenue of KRW 53.9 billion rose only 1.5% year on year. Coverage tied to the 2026 outlook said demand weakness was expected to persist.

Thin margins and lower operating cash flow

The operating margin was 2.3% in 2025, well below 5.6% in 2022. Operating cash flow fell from KRW 38.8 billion in 2022 to KRW 6.79 billion in 2025. Because margins narrow quickly when selling prices fall, profit can drop sharply as it did in 2024.

Quarterly earnings volatility

Operating profit fell 75.8% sequentially from KRW 5.4 billion in the second quarter of 2026 to KRW 1.31 billion in the third quarter. Wide quarterly swings make the full-year trend difficult to read from any single quarter. If the price rebound proves temporary while demand stays weak, earnings volatility could widen again.

Risk factors

Downstream demand and imports

Coverage reports continued weak demand from major customers in construction, shipbuilding, and heavy equipment. Low-priced imports were cited as a factor that worsened sales conditions. Demand weakness persisted outside some industries even after anti-dumping duties were imposed, which is a continuing risk.

Price and policy variables

The first-quarter 2026 improvement relied heavily on higher hot-rolled coil prices and anti-dumping duties. If the policy effect fades or prices fail to keep pace with cost, margins could narrow again. The gap between raw material procurement cost and selling price directly determines the operating margin.

Customer concentration and business structure

Hot-rolled supply depends on the steelmaker that supplies its coil, and section steel depends on the Hyundai Steel designated-distributor arrangement, so any change in these relationships would have a large effect. The largest customer accounted for 8.90% of 2025 revenue.

The company added online retail and solar power to its business purpose, but no specific investment plan has been disclosed, so their contribution remains uncertain.

Overall view

Samhyun Steel is a primary steel company that processes and sells hot-rolled coil from its coil supplier and also serves as a designated section-steel distributor for Hyundai Steel Company. Revenue declined for three consecutive years from 2023 through 2025, falling 2.5% to KRW 218 billion in 2025.

Operating profit, however, rose 43.2% to KRW 5.1 billion in 2025, and the operating margin recovered from 1.6% in 2024 to 2.3%. In 2026, quarterly profit has swung widely, with operating profit of KRW 5.4 billion in the second quarter and KRW 1.31 billion in the third after the first-quarter improvement.

The balance sheet is stable, with no borrowings and a debt ratio of 10.4%, and a plan to maintain a payout ratio of at least 40% has been disclosed.

On the other side, operating cash flow fell from KRW 38.8 billion in 2022 to KRW 6.79 billion in 2025, and reports continue to cite weak downstream demand and low-priced imports.

The next points to confirm are the full-year 2026 operating margin in the business report, the 2026 year-end dividend, and first-quarter 2027 results.
