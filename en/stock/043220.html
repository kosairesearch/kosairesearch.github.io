02

Business structure

Founded in 1995 and listed on KOSDAQ in 2001, TS Nexgen is a specialty machinery maker whose core products have long been industrial dampers for power plants and marine vessels, plus busway electrical distribution systems.

Dampers are precision components that regulate gas and air flow in thermal and nuclear power plants, petrochemical facilities, and marine SOx scrubber systems, requiring custom design and high durability.

The company has been described as having earned recognition for its competitiveness in the marine SOx damper segment, including related industry awards.

Since 2022 the company has pursued diversification into bio-healthcare, acquiring a 73.43% stake and management control of Neurosona, an ultrasound brain-disease treatment device specialist, from SU Holdings (now DHX Company) in May 2024.

Neurosona is described as holding low-intensity focused ultrasound neuromodulation technology and developing hospital-use brain treatment systems and home brain-care products. The company's website also lists a stem cell and immune cell therapeutics subsidiary under its bio division.

However, exact revenue contribution by segment has not been confirmed from public disclosures, and the overall pattern of recent results suggests the core industrial equipment business remains the primary revenue driver.

The controlling shareholder has changed multiple times since the 2024 management change, most recently to EN Plus in March 2026, whose stake stood at only 3.71%, underscoring an unsettled ownership structure.

04

Earnings analysis

Annual revenue rose 4.1% from KRW 25.5 billion in 2022 to a peak of KRW 26.5 billion in 2023, then declined for two straight years to KRW 21.5 billion in 2024 (down 19.0% year-on-year) and KRW 14.5 billion in 2025 (down 32.4%).

Operating losses narrowed from KRW 2.62 billion in 2022 to KRW 1.72 billion in 2023, but then widened again to KRW 5.73 billion in 2024 and KRW 5.87 billion in 2025, marking four consecutive years of operating losses.

The operating margin deteriorated sharply alongside the revenue decline, from -10.3% in 2022 to -26.6% in 2024 and -40.4% in 2025.

Net income attributable to controlling shareholders turned positive at KRW 39.15 million in 2023 but swung to a net loss of KRW 19.1 billion in 2024, then widened further to a net loss of KRW 38.6 billion in 2025, extending losses for two straight years.

Net income including non-controlling interests also showed large losses, at KRW 20 billion in 2024 and KRW 37.7 billion in 2025.

On a quarterly basis, operating losses widened from KRW 1.3 billion in Q2 2025 to KRW 1.88 billion in Q4 2025, then narrowed to KRW 256 million in Q1 2026 before turning to a small operating profit of KRW 4.18 million in Q2 2026.

However, Q2 2026 revenue of KRW 2.02 billion fell 46.6% quarter-on-quarter and 46.9% year-on-year, suggesting the breakeven result came mainly from cost cuts amid a shrinking top line.

Net losses attributable to controlling shareholders persisted for five consecutive quarters from Q2 2025 through Q2 2026, with the trailing four-quarter total (Q3 2025-Q2 2026) reaching KRW 23.8 billion.

The debt-to-equity ratio rose sharply to 99.1% at the end of 2025 from 56.1% in 2022, and operating cash flow reversed to -KRW 5.18 billion in 2025 after a positive KRW 6.09 billion in 2023.

05

Industry analysis

The end markets for the damper and busway business are power plant construction/maintenance demand and shipping-sector compliance with environmental regulations.

Tightening sulfur oxide emission rules under the International Maritime Organization have historically supported demand for marine desulfurization equipment, though a slowdown in the capex cycle for power generation and shipbuilding could constrain new orders.

Among KOSDAQ and KOSPI-listed peers classified in the same machinery sector, many are larger companies in higher-growth areas such as robotics, construction equipment, and battery equipment, placing TS Nexgen well below them in scale.

In bio-healthcare, the company has cited an estimated $30 billion global market for depression and dementia treatment using low-intensity focused ultrasound technology targeted by Neurosona, though this figure is the company's own estimate and has not been independently verified.

That field requires competing against established treatments such as transcranial magnetic stimulation and numerous global medical device makers, leaving Neurosona, as a new entrant, at an early stage of market positioning.

While exposure to two different cycles across the industrial equipment and bio-healthcare segments could offer some diversification, disclosed results make it difficult to confirm a clear revenue contribution from either segment independently.

06

Outlook

The company's near-term outlook is shaped far more by legal and administrative proceedings over its listing status than by its underlying business.

Consecutive qualified audit opinions for fiscal years 2024 and 2025, both due to scope limitations, triggered delisting grounds under KOSDAQ listing rules; after objections and submission of improvement plans, the Korea Exchange's corporate review committee decided on delisting in June 2026.

The company subsequently filed an injunction with the Seoul Southern District Court to stay the effect of the delisting decision, and trading has remained halted for investor protection pending the court's ruling.

A separate rehabilitation proceeding petition filed by the company was disclosed as rejected on September 14, 2026.

Regarding the Neurosona investment, the book value of the stake was fully impaired to zero as of the end of 2025, and an acceleration clause was triggered on the convertible bonds issued to fund the acquisition, prompting bondholder DHX Company to file a loan claim lawsuit worth KRW 6.05 billion.

A corrected semiannual report (as of June 2026) was filed on September 30, 2026, remaining the most recent official disclosure. No new official guidance on order intake for the core industrial equipment business or on Neurosona's clinical and regulatory approval timeline has been confirmed.

08

Bull factors

Q2 2026 Operating Profit Turned Positive

Q2 2026 operating profit came in at KRW 4.18 million, turning positive both quarter-on-quarter and year-on-year. Reaching breakeven despite a 46.9% year-on-year revenue decline suggests some fixed-cost reduction took hold.

The magnitude of the profit is very small, however, making it premature to call this a trend of profitability recovery.

Decades-Long Industrial Equipment Customer Base

The damper and busway business has accumulated a track record with power generation and shipbuilding customers dating back to the 1990s. Its recognized competitiveness in the marine SOx damper segment could provide an order base if demand tied to environmental compliance resumes.

However, given the revenue decline over the past two years, whether this base translates into actual new orders needs to be confirmed through future disclosures.

Bio-Healthcare Diversification Optionality

Neurosona's low-intensity focused ultrasound brain-disease treatment technology carries long-term growth potential if clinical trials and regulatory approvals proceed. The company has described this as offering cycle diversification away from its legacy power equipment business.

However, with the stake's book value fully impaired at the end of 2025 and related litigation ongoing, realizing this option would require resolving governance and litigation risks first.

Bear factors

Two Straight Years of Revenue Decline

Revenue declined for two straight years after peaking at KRW 26.5 billion in 2023, falling to KRW 14.5 billion in 2025. The top-line contraction continued into Q2 2026, with revenue down 46.9% year-on-year.

In a business with a heavy fixed-cost base, shrinking revenue carries the risk of further profitability deterioration.

Mounting Net Losses and Capital Erosion Concerns

Net losses attributable to controlling shareholders widened from KRW 19.1 billion in 2024 to KRW 38.6 billion in 2025, marking two consecutive loss years, while the trailing four-quarter total also reached KRW 23.8 billion.

The debt-to-equity ratio climbed to 99.1% at the end of 2025 from 56.1% in 2022, reflecting a weaker financial structure. Continued loss accumulation could not be ruled out from escalating into further listing-maintenance issues such as capital impairment.

Prolonged Delisting Process and Trading Halt

Qualified audit opinions for both fiscal 2024 and fiscal 2025 triggered delisting grounds, and the exchange's corporate review committee decided on delisting in June 2026. With trading halted pending a court ruling on the company's injunction request, investors have no clear visibility on when trading might resume.

A separately filed rehabilitation petition was rejected in September 2026, leaving procedural uncertainty unresolved.

10

Risk factors

Listing Maintenance Risk

With delisting grounds already in place, the court's ruling on the company's injunction request remains the key variable. If the injunction is denied, the process could proceed to cleanup trading and delisting, while even if granted, the outcome could change again depending on the underlying lawsuit. During a prolonged trading halt, investors are left unable to transact at all.

Governance and Litigation Risk

Since the 2024 management change, the controlling shareholder has changed multiple times, and an investor group has alleged that roughly KRW 12 billion in losses arose from opaque capital increases and convertible bond transactions during this period.

Regarding the Neurosona investment, an acceleration clause was triggered on convertible bonds issued for the acquisition, and bondholder DHX Company has filed a lawsuit worth KRW 6.05 billion. The outcome of these disputes could further affect financial burden or the stability of corporate control.

Financial Soundness Risk

The debt-to-equity ratio rose to 99.1% at the end of 2025, and operating cash flow turned negative again at -KRW 5.18 billion in 2025. If further asset impairments occur, as happened with the fully written-down Neurosona stake, total equity could shrink further.

With revenue decline and operating losses occurring simultaneously, external financing conditions could also become more difficult.

  2. Around mid-November 2026 (statutory Q3 report filing deadline)

  3. Upon the first-instance ruling in the DHX Company convertible bond lawsuit

    The outcome of the KRW 6.05 billion loan claim lawsuit is a variable that could determine further cash outflows and the scale of financial burden.

  4. Upon disclosure of trading resumption or further listing-eligibility review

    When the KOSDAQ Market Committee or the exchange discloses further review and resolution results, some of the uncertainty over continued listing status could be resolved.

12

Overall view

TS Nexgen has attempted to diversify its traditional industrial equipment business of power-plant and shipbuilding dampers and busway systems by adding a bio-healthcare venture, but it has passed through a phase of two straight years of revenue decline since 2023 alongside simultaneously widening operating losses and net losses attributable to controlling shareholders.

Q2 2026 saw operating profit turn positive at KRW 4.18 million, but revenue in the same quarter fell 46.9% year-on-year, leaving considerable room to interpret this as a limited breakeven result amid a shrinking top line.

The more critical variable is the delisting grounds arising from consecutive qualified audit opinions for fiscal 2024 and 2025, and the current trading halt tied to the company's injunction request against that decision.

The rejection of its rehabilitation proceeding petition in September 2026 and ongoing litigation related to the Neurosona investment add further uncertainty to governance and financial soundness overall.

On the business side, positive elements exist in the industrial equipment customer base and the long-term potential of the bio-healthcare segment, but legal and administrative variables around continued listing status overshadow this structure.

Investors would need to prioritize tracking events directly tied to listing status—court rulings and exchange disclosures—over earnings figures alone.
