02

Business structure

Sunny Electronics makes precision industrial components centered on quartz crystal resonators and related products, and it reports a single business segment.

A quartz crystal resonator generates frequency using the piezoelectric effect of a single quartz crystal, and the company also produces oscillators and voltage-controlled oscillators built on that base. The company exited its telecom business segment in 2022, which narrowed its structure toward precision components.

Its major customers include Samsung Electronics and LG Electronics as fixed set-maker accounts. Based on Veeline's estimates, exports account for roughly 60% of sales and domestic sales for about 40%.

Its main raw materials depend heavily on Japanese imports, at an estimated 70% to 90%, and production takes place at its Chungju plant. R&D spending in 2025 totaled KRW 155 million, about 1.25% of revenue. Competition comes from Japanese technology and Chinese price competitiveness at the same time.

The company was founded in 1966 and listed on the main board in 1987. Because it reports one segment, the product groups are described by their roles rather than by segment revenue share.

04

Earnings analysis

Annual revenue fell from KRW 17.4 billion in 2022 to KRW 12.8 billion in 2023, a decline of 26.8%, held flat at KRW 12.8 billion in 2024 with a 0.4% increase, and then dropped to KRW 12.4 billion in 2025, a 3.5% decline. Revenue was largest in 2022 and smallest in 2025 across the disclosed period.

Operating profit was KRW 1.69 billion in 2022, KRW 902 million in 2023 (down 46.5%), KRW 2.58 billion in 2024 (up 185.7%), and KRW 2.2 billion in 2025 (down 14.6%).

Operating profit fell more sharply than revenue in 2023 and rose sharply in 2024 on roughly flat sales, so margin changes drove operating profit more than revenue size did. Operating margin was 9.7% in 2022, 7.1% in 2023, 20.1% in 2024 and 17.8% in 2025.

Profit attributable to owners of the parent was KRW 5.04 billion in 2022, KRW 3.86 billion in 2023 (down 23.3%), KRW 4.4 billion in 2024 (up 13.8%) and KRW 3.63 billion in 2025 (down 17.5%). Net income including non-controlling interests matched the parent figure in each year.

Operating cash flow fell from KRW 5.07 billion in 2022 to KRW 4.2 billion in 2025. At the end of 2025, total equity was KRW 84.8 billion, all attributable to owners of the parent, and total liabilities were KRW 1.23 billion, a debt ratio of 1.5%.

On a quarterly basis, Q3 2025 revenue was KRW 3.02 billion, operating profit KRW 515 million, and profit attributable to owners of the parent KRW 1.11 billion. Q4 2025 revenue slipped 1.4% quarter on quarter to KRW 2.98 billion, and parent profit fell 47.1% to KRW 585 million.

Q1 2026 revenue was KRW 3.07 billion and parent profit recovered to KRW 1.16 billion. Q2 2026 revenue of KRW 3.55 billion was the highest of the last five quarters, but operating profit fell 45.2% to KRW 245 million and parent profit fell 71.3% to KRW 334 million.

Q3 2026 revenue was KRW 3.39 billion, up 12.4% year on year, and operating profit of KRW 557 million rose 8.3%, while parent profit of KRW 986 million fell 10.9%. Parent profit over the last four quarters totaled KRW 3.07 billion.

Repeated quarters in which revenue rose but profit did not follow suggest that price competition is limiting margins even in quarterly results.

Industry analysis

Demand from end markets is supported by growing electronics and equipment demand, which one securities information service cited for the modest revenue rise in the first half of 2026. Analysts expect continued demand for oscillators.

At the same time, pressure from set makers to cut unit prices means that volumes can hold up while shipment values decline. Lower-priced products from China, Southeast Asia and Japan are intensifying competition. The competitive picture pits Japanese technology against Chinese price competitiveness.

In terms of cycle position, volumes look resilient while unit prices weaken, which makes large revenue gains difficult and leaves margins dependent on technology and cost control.

Larger components makers in the same electronics and components sector, such as Samsung Electro-Mechanics and LG Innotek, have a far broader product range, so direct comparison is limited. The heavy reliance on Japanese imports means supply chain shifts can affect cost.

Low-debt balance sheet

The debt ratio was 1.5% at the end of 2025, with total liabilities of KRW 1.23 billion against total equity of KRW 84.8 billion, all attributable to owners of the parent. The small borrowing burden leaves financial room even as revenue shrinks. Operating cash flow remained at KRW 4.2 billion in 2025.

Anchor customers and shareholder returns

Securing Samsung Electronics and LG Electronics as fixed customers supports the stability of sales channels. Electronics and equipment demand lifted first-half 2026 revenue modestly. A profit-based share cancellation reduced the share count in September 2025.

Bear factors

Shrinking revenue

Revenue fell from KRW 17.4 billion in 2022 to KRW 12.4 billion in 2025, with 2025 down 3.5%. Operating profit fell 14.6% in the same year to KRW 2.2 billion. The bear case is that the shrinking revenue base is gradually weighing on earnings scale.

Price competition and unit-price pressure

Japanese technology and Chinese pricing compete directly for orders, limiting profitability. Low-priced products from China, Southeast Asia and Japan are gradually intensifying competition. Pressure from set makers to lower unit prices is also reducing shipment values, which is a further weakness.

Volatile quarterly profit

Q2 2026 operating profit fell 45.2% quarter on quarter to KRW 245 million, and parent profit fell 71.3% to KRW 334 million. Q3 2026 parent profit of KRW 986 million was down 10.9% year on year. Parent profit over the last four quarters totaled KRW 3.07 billion, with no stable quarterly pattern.

Risk factors

Imported raw materials

Imported raw materials depend heavily on Japan, at an estimated 70% to 90%. Supply disruption or currency swings could pass through to costs quickly. Higher costs in a low unit-price environment could squeeze margins further. Whether sourcing is diversified should be checked in the periodic reports.

Customer concentration and set demand

Heavy reliance on domestic set makers ties the company to their demand and pricing decisions. If set makers keep pushing unit prices down, revenue could decline even if volumes hold. A change in orders from a major customer could move quarterly results substantially.

Share price volatility and ownership

Major shareholders and related parties hold 20.09%, and the free float ratio is 79.91%. On September 9, 2026, the share price rose more than 7% without a clear positive disclosure, which was attributed to supply-demand and technical factors. With a small market capitalization, price moves can be amplified by flows.
