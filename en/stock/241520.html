02

Business structure

DSC Investment, founded in 2012 and listed on KOSDAQ in 2016, mainly forms and manages venture investment funds that invest in early-stage companies. It operates 17 funds with about KRW 1.59 trillion in assets under management as of 2026.

Its income comes from management fees tied to committed fund size, performance fees earned after funds clear a hurdle return, and equity-method gains from its funds. Management fees rose from KRW 13 billion in 2022 to KRW 14.3 billion in 2023 and KRW 16.4 billion in 2024, supported by large fund formations.

Its flagship funds are DSC Home Run Fund No.1, formed in August 2022 with KRW 248 billion in commitments, and DSC Home Run Fund No.2, formed in September 2025 with KRW 284 billion. Performance fees are recognized around exit events, so they vary widely by year.

Subsidiaries include an accelerator and an ERP software business serving LPs and VCs. Its largest holding is an AI semiconductor startup, where it invested KRW 28.5 billion across six rounds from seed to Series D. The largest shareholder, Yoon Geon-su and two related parties, held 24.12% as of October 7, 2026.

04

Earnings analysis

On a disclosed basis, annual revenue fell from KRW 33.8 billion in 2022 to KRW 33.6 billion in 2023, a 0.6% decline, then rose 15.1% to KRW 38.6 billion in 2024 and 16.9% to KRW 45.1 billion in 2025. The 2025 revenue was the largest of the 2022 to 2025 period, while 2023 was the smallest.

Operating profit fell from KRW 18.8 billion in 2022 to KRW 17.5 billion in 2023 and KRW 15 billion in 2024, then rebounded 49.4% to KRW 22.3 billion in 2025. The operating margin dropped from 55.8% in 2022 and 52.3% in 2023 to 38.7% in 2024 before recovering to 49.5% in 2025.

Net profit attributable to controlling shareholders was KRW 15.8 billion in 2022, KRW 14.6 billion in 2023, KRW 10.7 billion in 2024 and KRW 17.3 billion in 2025, up 60.6% in the latest year.

Net income including non-controlling interests was KRW 17.8 billion in 2025, a different figure from the controlling-shareholder amount.

Press reports attributed the 2025 improvement to investment recoveries from portfolio listings and higher equity-method gains from funds, with management fees from large fund formations also contributing. For 2024, the equity-method loss widened from KRW 3.8 billion to KRW 5.5 billion and operating costs rose sharply.

Quarterly revenue jumped 179.1% to KRW 20.5 billion in the fourth quarter of 2025, when operating profit reached KRW 16.2 billion. Operating profit was KRW 4.59 billion in the first quarter of 2026 and KRW 12.2 billion in the second quarter.

In the third quarter of 2026, revenue was KRW 3.07 billion, down 58.3% year on year, and the company posted an operating loss of KRW 1.47 billion. Over the last four quarters, from the fourth quarter of 2025 to the third quarter of 2026, net profit attributable to controlling shareholders totaled KRW 24.1 billion.

Operating cash flow went from KRW 12.1 billion in 2022 and KRW 365 million in 2023 to -KRW 4.6 billion in 2024 and -KRW 11.7 billion in 2025.

05

Industry analysis

The venture investment market is changing on both the fundraising and deal-supply sides. A 2024 press report described a continuing venture winter in the first half.

The company itself said in 2026 that investment demand had concentrated on specific industries and proven companies, narrowing the search for new opportunities.

On the exit side, a Financial Services Commission meeting reported on November 13, 2026 discussed revitalizing M&A and secondary markets, but the company was not mentioned in the meeting materials and the proposals are not confirmed policy.

On the policy capital side, the National Growth Fund approved an KRW 800 billion investment in its AI semiconductor portfolio company on May 28, 2026, according to press reports. Similar companies often cited for comparison include T.S.Investment, Capstone Partners, Company K Partners and Stonebridge Ventures.

Differences between venture firms mainly come from exit results of operating funds and how concentrated their key portfolio is. Because one large portfolio holding strongly influences both earnings and the share price here, the company is sensitive to sector conditions. The structure benefits most when fund formation and a healthier exit market move forward together.

06

Outlook

The company set 2026 as a transition year for rebuilding its investment standards and turning exit results into operating strength. Exit candidates cited include Nexeye, Ono Pharmaceutical, AppClon and Moloco.

Nexeye has pursued a KOSDAQ listing under the technology special listing track and had completed its technology evaluation application as of April 2026.

Its ERP subsidiary plans to upgrade the service and widen its base of LP and VC clients, while its accelerator subsidiary plans to strengthen early-stage deal sourcing and proactive investment.

On the fund side, DSC Home Run Fund No.2, formed in September 2025 with KRW 284 billion, is central to maintaining assets under management and management-fee inflows. The company said that including unrealized assets of four funds in liquidation, it expects an average net multiple of 2x and a net IRR above 17%.

This is the company's own estimate, not realized income. Press reports said the AI semiconductor portfolio company's valuation has exceeded KRW 3 trillion, but no confirmed listing date has been announced. The next checkpoint is when and how much exit value is recognized as performance fees.

Whether management-fee growth and performance-fee recognition move in the same direction will be central to the next quarterly results.

08

Bull factors

Exit cycle reaching the payoff phase

Operating profit reached KRW 22.3 billion in 2025, the largest year of 2022 to 2025. Press reports attributed the gain to exits following portfolio listings and higher equity-method gains.

The company said including unrealized assets of four funds in liquidation, it expects an average net multiple of 2x and a net IRR above 17%.

Expanding management-fee base

Management fees rose from KRW 13 billion in 2022 to KRW 16.4 billion in 2024, and assets under management total about KRW 1.59 trillion across 17 funds. DSC Home Run Fund No.2, formed in September 2025 with KRW 284 billion, is a large fund that expanded the base. Because management fees follow committed capital rather than exit timing, they provide a floor under earnings.

Shareholder returns and subsidiary expansion

Press reports said the company has continued dividends and share cancellations for three consecutive years. Its ERP subsidiary is upgrading a service aimed at expanding its LP and VC client base. Its accelerator subsidiary plans to strengthen early-stage sourcing and proactive investment.

09

Bear factors

Quarterly swing to operating loss

Third-quarter 2026 revenue was KRW 3.07 billion, down 58.3% year on year, and the company posted an operating loss of KRW 1.47 billion and a net loss attributable to controlling shareholders of KRW 1.19 billion.

Compared with KRW 12.2 billion of operating profit in the second quarter of 2026, the quarterly swing is very large. Because performance fees cluster around exit timing, quarterly profit can change sharply.

Persistent negative operating cash flow

Operating cash flow went from KRW 365 million in 2023 to -KRW 4.6 billion in 2024 and -KRW 11.7 billion in 2025. Operating profit rose to KRW 22.3 billion in 2025 even as the gap between profit and cash generation widened. How much valuation and equity-method gains convert into actual cash is the key point to watch.

Possible decline in management fees

Commentary has pointed out that clustered fund maturities could shrink assets under management and reduce management fees. Management fees of KRW 16.4 billion in 2024 leaned heavily on the effect of large fund formations. If new fundraising does not fill the gap from matured funds, the fee base could weaken.

10

Risk factors

Exit timing and performance-fee volatility

Performance fees are recognized at the time of exit, so they vary widely by year and quarter. Press reports described the KRW 10.5 billion in 2024 performance fees as the largest in company history, and keeping that level may be hard if exits slow. If exit-market policies do not take concrete form, the recovery in performance fees could be slower.

Concentration in a large portfolio holding

The company invested KRW 28.5 billion across six rounds in an AI semiconductor startup, from seed to Series D. The sharp share price rise in February 2026 was tied to the recognition of this investment.

Because the listing timing and valuation of this holding weigh heavily on company value, concentration itself is a risk.

Share price volatility and trading risk

The shares were designated an investment warning stock on August 5, 2026, with a 26.83% price change over three days and a 7.04% trading share for a single account that day. The Korea Exchange said the designation resulted from a single account meeting the criteria. For a small-cap stock, concentrated short-term trading can move the price significantly.

12

Overall view

DSC Investment has widened its management-fee base on about KRW 1.59 trillion of assets under management across 17 funds.

In 2025 it posted revenue of KRW 45.1 billion and operating profit of KRW 22.3 billion, the largest year of 2022 to 2025, and net profit attributable to controlling shareholders rose to KRW 17.3 billion.

However, the third quarter of 2026 turned to an operating loss of KRW 1.47 billion, and operating cash flow has been negative since 2024. A large share of earnings depends on exit timing, which makes quarterly results volatile.

The listing and valuation changes of one large portfolio holding also weigh heavily on both earnings and the share price. The 2026 investment warning designation reflects the high volatility of the shares.

The next checkpoints are the size of realized exits such as a Nexeye listing, the cash flow and operating margin in the 2026 annual report, and first-quarter 2027 results.
