02

Business structure

Canvas N is a drama production company founded in 2003. When it moved to KOSDAQ in 2023, the company said it had produced more than 30 dramas, including SBS's Something in Bali and Money Game, as well as MBC's Empress Ki.

The company described a model that earns domestic broadcast rights revenue, overseas OTT licensing revenue and OST royalties from its own intellectual property, and it held 23 titles at that time.

Earnings vary widely with production volume and broadcaster scheduling, a volatility the company itself flagged in its registration statement.

In March 2022, the fashion company F&F acquired a 50.77% stake and became the largest shareholder, after which the company moved to a private equity and investment fund structure, changed its name to Canvas N and later reverted to its Korean name Victory Contents, according to press reports.

Critics also pointed to more than 30 new business purposes unrelated to the core drama business added during that period. Press reports said no drama production revenue was booked in Q1 2026, and the company depends on terrestrial, cable and OTT platform scheduling and orders.

Commentary has also argued that inflows were channeled into real estate purchases rather than drama IP or production capacity.

04

Earnings analysis

Annual revenue went from KRW 32.2 billion in 2022 to KRW 17.5 billion in 2023 (down 45.6% year on year), KRW 9.62 billion in 2024 (down 45.1%) and KRW 4 billion in 2025 (down 58.4%). 2022 was the largest year in the 2022 to 2025 period and 2025 the smallest.

Operating income of KRW 1.73 billion in 2022 turned into an operating loss of KRW 545 million in 2023, widened to an operating loss of KRW 5.12 billion in 2024, and narrowed to an operating loss of KRW 1.48 billion in 2025.

The operating margin was 5.4% in 2022, -3.1% in 2023, -53.2% in 2024 and -36.9% in 2025, so margins fell sharply as revenue shrank.

Net income attributable to controlling shareholders swung from KRW 1.33 billion in 2022 to a net loss of KRW 249 million in 2023, then KRW 7.02 billion in 2024 and KRW 8.66 billion in 2025. Net income including non-controlling interests was disclosed at the same amounts in each year.

At the end of 2025, total equity was KRW 8.37 billion, all of it attributable to controlling shareholders, against total liabilities of KRW 25.8 billion. The debt ratio was 107.1% in 2022, 24.9% in 2023, 133.2% in 2024 and 307.9% in 2025.

Operating cash flow was negative in all four years: KRW -5.6 billion, KRW -3.78 billion, KRW -1.39 billion and KRW -3.15 billion. Quarterly revenue rose from KRW 371 million in Q3 2025 to KRW 2.77 billion in Q4 2025 (up 646.0% from the prior quarter), then fell to KRW 534 million in Q1 2026 (down 80.7%).

It recovered to KRW 659 million in Q2 2026 and KRW 939 million in Q3 2026, rising for two straight quarters, with Q3 up 153.3% year on year.

Quarterly operating results moved from an operating loss of KRW 385 million in Q3 2025 to operating income of KRW 222 million in Q4 2025, then back to losses of KRW 80.41 million in Q1 2026, KRW 826 million in Q2 2026 and KRW 2.08 billion in Q3 2026, three consecutive quarters of operating losses.

Losses attributable to controlling shareholders were KRW 5.73 billion in Q4 2025, KRW 1.06 billion in Q1 2026, KRW 1.31 billion in Q2 2026 and KRW 2.74 billion in Q3 2026, totaling KRW 10.8 billion over the last four quarters.

In Q3 2026, revenue rose but the operating loss widened to KRW 2.08 billion, so higher sales did not reduce losses. In Q4 2025, operating income was positive while the loss attributable to controlling shareholders reached KRW 5.73 billion, which points to a large effect from non-operating items.

05

Industry analysis

Drama production is a business where revenue and profit swing widely with the number of series commissioned and platform orders. In its 2023 registration statement, the company said the uncertainty of the media industry gives its profitability high volatility.

At the time of its listing, the company cited an average revenue per drama of KRW 12.8 billion and a per-title margin of about 7%, though these were its 2023 figures. Press coverage at the same time described intensifying competition among OTT operators to secure content.

That competition helps IP-holding producers through licensing demand, but a smaller production slate also creates a larger revenue gap. The fall to KRW 4 billion in 2025 and the absence of drama production revenue in Q1 2026 show how fewer productions translate directly into weaker results.

Peers such as Studio Dragon and CJ ENM also operate in broadcast and OTT production, so Canvas N's position depends on owning its IP and securing enough productions.

06

Outlook

When it listed in 2023, the company said it planned to produce 12 additional titles by 2025 to expand its IP library. It also outlined expanding overseas OTT partnerships toward China, Europe and Southeast Asia.

Progress on that plan does not show up as a production count in the reported results, so it can only be read indirectly through revenue trends. The 2026 disclosures have centered on fundraising.

Press reports said that of a KRW 34 billion fundraising plan decided on January 22, 2026, the amount whose payment was confirmed was KRW 4 billion. A managing director appointed under a managing-director structure resigned on October 12, 2026, leaving that post vacant.

On October 20, 2026, the board resolved to sell its entire stake in Hanil Onix for KRW 1.5 billion, citing liquidity and asset efficiency, and the disclosure put the amount at 14.99% of equity.

The next regular report is the 2026 annual business report, and until then, whether the fundraising plans are executed remains the main variable for the outlook.

08

Bull factors

Quarterly revenue up two straight quarters in 2026

Q2 2026 revenue of KRW 659 million rose 23.3% from Q1, and Q3 revenue of KRW 939 million rose 42.6%. Q3 revenue was up 153.3% year on year, which marks a rebound in top-line activity. Even so, the scale remains far below the KRW 32.2 billion recorded in 2022.

Revenue model built on owned IP

At its 2023 listing, the company said it held domestic and overseas rights to 23 titles. It described combining broadcast rights, OTT licensing and OST royalties so one title can generate revenue several times. These are the company's 2023 descriptions, and the revenue still depends on how often its titles are scheduled.

Non-core asset sale as a liquidity step

On October 20, 2026, the board resolved to sell 18,182 shares of Hanil Onix for KRW 1.5 billion. The stated purpose is securing liquidity and improving asset efficiency, and the disclosure put the amount at 14.99% of equity.

The sale adds a funding source, but it is small relative to total liabilities of KRW 25.8 billion at the end of 2025.

Bear factors

Three straight quarters of operating losses and five of net losses

Operating losses were KRW 80.41 million in Q1 2026, KRW 826 million in Q2 and KRW 2.08 billion in Q3, three consecutive quarters of operating losses. Losses attributable to controlling shareholders have run for five straight quarters since Q3 2025, totaling KRW 10.8 billion over the last four quarters. Q3 2026 revenue rose, yet the operating loss widened to KRW 2.08 billion.

A chain of withdrawn financing plans

On October 15, 2026, the company withdrew the issuance of its 6th convertible bond (KRW 10 billion) and 8th convertible bond (KRW 3 billion) after the buyers failed to pay.

On November 10, 2026, it also withdrew a KRW 14 billion third-party share issuance allotted to Quantum Bridge New Technology Fund, citing unpaid funds and changed financing conditions. Press reports said that only KRW 4 billion of the KRW 34 billion raised in January was confirmed as paid in.

Disclosure credibility and governance strain

A managing director appointed under a disclosure published on October 7, 2026 resigned on October 12, 2026. On October 8, 2026, trading was suspended and a listing eligibility review trigger was disclosed.

Repeated changes to disclosures and payment dates led to a designation for unfaithful disclosure on October 12, with 10 penalty points and a KRW 40 million fine.

Risk factors

Funding and liquidity

Total liabilities at the end of 2025 were KRW 25.8 billion, far above total equity of KRW 8.37 billion. Operating cash flow was negative in every year from 2022 to 2025. The withdrawal of KRW 13 billion in convertible bonds and a KRW 14 billion share issuance in 2026 narrowed external funding options.

Listing eligibility and disclosure credibility

The October 8, 2026 trading suspension followed a listing eligibility review trigger, and the exchange said it would decide whether the company is subject to a formal review by October 30, 2026.

The unfaithful-disclosure designation stemmed from payment-date changes of six months or more for a share issuance and convertible bonds. Repeated revisions to disclosures weigh on market confidence.

Drama scheduling and earnings volatility

The company itself noted in its registration statement that drama revenue varies with genre, scheduling and the number of broadcasts. Quarterly revenue rose to KRW 2.77 billion in Q4 2025 and then fell to KRW 534 million in Q1 2026, showing how production timing swings quarterly results. Press reports of no drama production revenue in Q1 2026 point to the same pattern.

12

Overall view

Canvas N moved from a profit in 2022 to losses from 2023, and losses continued through 2025. Revenue fell to KRW 4 billion in 2025, and while quarterly revenue has risen for two straight quarters in 2026, operating losses have now run for three consecutive quarters and widened after Q2.

Losses attributable to controlling shareholders totaled KRW 10.8 billion over the last four quarters, and total liabilities of KRW 25.8 billion at the end of 2025 far exceeded total equity.

In October 2026 a KRW 13 billion convertible bond issuance was withdrawn, and in November a KRW 14 billion third-party share issuance was withdrawn, narrowing funding routes.

A listing eligibility review trigger, a designation for unfaithful disclosure and the resignation of a managing director add pressure on market confidence. On the positive side, the quarterly revenue recovery, an IP-based revenue model and the sale of a non-core stake to secure liquidity stand out.

On the negative side, continued operating and net losses, funding setbacks and disclosure credibility issues weigh heavily. The next points to confirm are the full-year profit and debt trends in the 2026 annual business report, and changes in management structure and financing plans.
