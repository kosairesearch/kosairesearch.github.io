02

Business structure

Hanon Systems is a specialist in automotive thermal management systems and is a member of the Hankook & Company Group.

Its core products are thermal components that manage the batteries, motors and cabin climate of a vehicle, with heat pump systems and electric compressors for electric vehicles as its main product lines.

The company says its technology portfolio covers every powertrain, from internal combustion engines through hybrids, battery electric vehicles and fuel cell vehicles. The xEV share of sales rose to 29% in Q1 2026, 31% in Q2 and 33% in Q3.

Its customers are global automakers, and in Q3 rising European electric vehicle demand lifted supply of electrified products to key customers, while production disruptions at major domestic customers weighed on sales.

The company is described as the number one thermal management supplier in Korea and the number two globally. Vehicle makers' parts affiliates such as Hyundai WIA are also entering thermal management, which is widening competition.

Diversification is under way: the company began its aftermarket business in 2026, selling products through domestic T-stations, and won an initial order from the European parts distributor Inter Cars.

It is also applying its thermal technology to ESS, power conversion systems, solid-state transformers and AI data center server cooling. In autonomous driving, it has agreed to supply thermal management systems to Zoox, the robotaxi unit of Amazon.

04

Earnings analysis

Annual revenue rose from KRW 8.63 trillion in 2022 to KRW 10.9 trillion in 2025, growing for three straight years from 2023 through 2025, and 2025 was the largest year of the 2022 to 2025 period. Revenue was KRW 9.56 trillion in 2023 (up 10.8%), KRW 10 trillion in 2024 (up 4.6%) and KRW 10.9 trillion in 2025 (up 8.9%).

Operating profit was largest in 2023 at KRW 277.3 billion and smallest in 2024 at KRW 95.5 billion (down 65.5%). Operating profit in 2025 was KRW 270.4 billion, up 183.0% and recovering from the 2024 low, but still below the 2023 level. Operating margin was 3.0% in 2022, 2.9% in 2023, 1.0% in 2024 and 2.5% in 2025.

Net income attributable to controlling shareholders moved from KRW 51 billion in 2023 to a KRW 363.3 billion loss in 2024, and the 2025 loss of KRW 198.5 billion marked a second straight year of losses with a smaller loss amount.

Net income including non-controlling interests was a loss of KRW 358.6 billion in 2024 and KRW 197.3 billion in 2025, a separate measure from the controlling-shareholder figure.

Operating cash flow declined from KRW 569.3 billion in 2024 to KRW 112.3 billion in 2025, and the debt ratio fell from 283.9% in 2022 to 168.1% in 2025.

On a quarterly basis, operating profit bottomed at KRW 89.7 billion in Q4 2025 and then rose to KRW 97.2 billion, KRW 103.7 billion and KRW 120.6 billion over the first three quarters of 2026. Q3 2026 revenue of KRW 2.66 trillion was down 1.8% from a year earlier.

The company attributed the margin gains to cost competitiveness and operational efficiency. Net income attributable to controlling shareholders was a KRW 212.8 billion loss in Q4 2025, a profit of KRW 66.6 billion in Q1 2026 and KRW 86.5 billion in Q2 2026, and a KRW 143 billion loss in Q3 2026.

The four quarters from Q4 2025 through Q3 2026 total a KRW 202.8 billion loss attributable to controlling shareholders.

05

Industry analysis

The shift to electric vehicles is changing the parts count of cars. According to the Korea Automotive Technology Institute, an internal combustion vehicle has about 30,000 parts while an electric vehicle has about 18,900.

That change is a burden for general auto parts makers, but it is seen as a profit opportunity for thermal management suppliers because the engineering becomes harder. The company's own examples put compressor prices at KRW 120,000 for combustion vehicles and KRW 300,000 for electric vehicles, a gap close to threefold.

Reports cite 882 patent applications for electric vehicle thermal management over the past decade, the highest worldwide. The pace of electrification varies by region and model. In Q1 2026 the company said it responded to slower electrification in global markets with demand for hybrids and new electric models.

In Q3 European sales rose, but production disruptions at major domestic customers reduced total revenue. On the competition side, vehicle makers' parts affiliates such as Hyundai WIA are entering thermal management, and Hyundai WIA says the segment is still small in its revenue.

Industry coverage points to rising demand for thermal management in ESS as battery fire prevention and longer battery life become priorities.

Outlook

A medium-term target reported in February 2026 centered on KRW 11 trillion in revenue and a 5% operating margin. At its Q2 results briefing the company expressed hope that the full-year operating margin could reach around 4%.

Eugene Investment & Securities analyzed in an early November 2026 report that the fourth-quarter operating margin would need to rise to the mid-4% range for the full year to reach 4%. The aftermarket business started in 2026 with sales through domestic T-stations and an initial order from Inter Cars.

In ESS thermal management, reports from August 2026 said prototype valve deliveries to key customers were complete and the company had entered the request-for-quotation stage. The company says it is also phasing in entry into server cooling for AI data centers.

In autonomous driving, it has agreed to supply thermal management systems to Zoox. Eugene Investment & Securities said that converting new orders into actual sales would ease concerns about medium-term growth.

The next checkpoints are whether the Q4 2026 operating margin reaches the mid-4% range and whether the new-business orders convert into revenue.

08

Bull factors

Operating profit up three straight quarters

Operating profit moved from KRW 89.7 billion in Q4 2025 to KRW 97.2 billion, KRW 103.7 billion and KRW 120.6 billion over the next three quarters. Q3 operating profit was up 26.6% from a year earlier. The company cites cost competitiveness and operational efficiency as the drivers. Profit grew even as revenue declined, which the company presents as evidence of a stronger cost structure.

Electrified product mix keeps expanding

The xEV share of sales rose from 29% in Q1 2026 to 31% in Q2 and 33% in Q3. The company said the Q3 share was the highest quarterly level on record. Rising European electric vehicle demand drove more supply of electrified products to key customers.

Reports on 882 electric vehicle thermal management patent applications, ranked first globally, point to the technology base behind the business.

Expansion into non-automotive customers

Reports said prototype valve deliveries to key ESS and data center customers are complete and the company has entered the request-for-quotation stage. In autonomous driving, it agreed to supply thermal systems to Zoox.

The aim is to broaden the customer base beyond automakers to battery cell makers and power infrastructure companies. Aftermarket channels are also widening, including the initial order from Inter Cars.

09

Bear factors

Persistent net losses and a return to the red

Net losses attributable to controlling shareholders ran for two straight years, KRW 363.3 billion in 2024 and KRW 198.5 billion in 2025. Q3 2026 brought a KRW 143 billion loss, a return to the red after a profit a year earlier. Rising operating profit has not yet carried through to the bottom line.

Revenue decline and customer production disruptions

Q3 2026 revenue was KRW 2.66 trillion, down 1.8% from a year earlier. The company cited production disruptions at major domestic customers and currency effects as drags. Annual revenue grew for three straight years through 2025, but quarterly revenue fell again in Q3 2026. Top-line results remain exposed to automaker production adjustments.

Shifting parts structure and rising competition

Analysts note that electric vehicles carry fewer parts than combustion vehicles, a burden for the parts industry as a whole. Parts affiliates of automakers, such as Hyundai WIA, are entering thermal management and widening competition.

Higher engineering difficulty is a strength, but in-house development by automakers or price competition could pressure margins. The timing for new businesses to convert into revenue has not been set out.

10

Risk factors

Customer concentration and production variables

The company itself cited production disruptions at key domestic customers as a cause of the Q3 2026 revenue decline. European electric vehicle demand and currency movements also move quarterly sales. A change in a major customer's production plan could affect the operating profit recovery. Customer diversification is under way, but the business remains tied to automaker output.

Balance sheet burden and cash flow

Total liabilities at year-end 2025 were KRW 6.58 trillion against total equity of KRW 3.91 trillion. Operating cash flow fell from KRW 569.3 billion in 2024 to KRW 112.3 billion in 2025. Reports said the CAPEX-to-EBITDA ratio dropped from 98% in 2024 to 26% in the first half of 2026. The question is whether cash generation can support both new-business investment and financial stability.

Timing of new-business realization

ESS, data center and robotaxi thermal management has mostly been reported at the prototype or request-for-quotation stage. Orders must be confirmed and move into mass production before they show up in results. No disclosed figures yet show the revenue scale of the new businesses.

Until profitability is proven, earnings improvement continues to depend on the existing automotive thermal business.

Overall view

Hanon Systems' Q3 2026 results combined an operating profit recovery with a return to net loss. Operating profit of KRW 120.6 billion rose for three straight quarters, and the xEV share reached 33%, so the direction of the business mix is clear.

On the other side, net income attributable to controlling shareholders swung to a KRW 143 billion loss, and the four quarters through Q3 2026 total a KRW 202.8 billion loss. Revenue fell 1.8% from a year earlier, and total liabilities of KRW 6.58 trillion still exceed total equity of KRW 3.91 trillion.

New businesses in ESS and data center cooling and robotaxis are widening, but most are reported at the bidding or supply-trial stage. Securities firms presented target prices in September and early November 2026, which reflect their assessments at the time.

Strengths and weaknesses carry similar weight here, and the next checkpoints are the Q4 2026 and full-year results, the 2026 annual report, and whether new-business orders convert into revenue.
