02

Business structure

The Lamy was listed on KOSDAQ on July 4, 1997, and began in scrap steel distribution as a raw-material recycling business before widening its scope.

In 2021 it acquired a income-producing property in Gapyeong-gun, Gyeonggi-do and added a resort business, and in 2023 it absorbed a cosmetics company through a merger, making cosmetics the core business.

The cosmetics division develops and sells basic skincare as well as hair and scalp care and body care products, distributed through domestic brand shops, online malls, home shopping and social commerce. Overseas, the company is pursuing wider OEM and ODM opportunities.

Scrap steel distribution provides raw materials, and an outlook published by WiseReport holds that demand will grow as electric arc furnace steelmaking expands and carbon emission limits tighten. The resort unit provides leisure facilities.

Some corporate information sites state that scrap steel distribution was discontinued because of falling profitability, which conflicts with 2026 half-year materials that still discuss the business. The company is classified under the miscellaneous sector, but its structure is strongly that of a cosmetics maker.

According to the company, the 2025 swing into loss was mainly caused by lower sales in the cosmetics division and the impairment of goodwill, an intangible asset. Competition in cosmetics is intensifying as distribution shifts to digital channels and small and mid-sized brands compete more fiercely.

04

Earnings analysis

Annual sales fell from KRW 62.6 billion in 2022 to KRW 51 billion in 2023 (down 18.5% year on year), then rose to KRW 69.8 billion in 2024 (up 36.9%), which was the largest year in 2022–2025. Sales of KRW 50.9 billion in 2025 (down 27.0%) made it the smallest year in the period.

Operating losses of KRW 3.18 billion in 2022 and KRW 3.54 billion in 2023 were followed by an operating profit of KRW 1.62 billion in 2024, which turned into an operating loss of KRW 1.72 billion in 2025. Operating margins were -5.1% in 2022, -6.9% in 2023, 2.3% in 2024 and -3.4% in 2025.

Net loss attributable to controlling shareholders was KRW 5.61 billion in 2022 and KRW 3.29 billion in 2023, turned into a profit of KRW 2.48 billion in 2024, and returned to a loss of KRW 4.65 billion in 2025.

Net income including non-controlling interests moved in the same direction, with a net loss of KRW 4.65 billion in 2025. The company cited lower cosmetics sales and goodwill impairment for the 2025 swing, and the impairment can be read as a one-time charge.

Operating cash flow was -KRW 2.13 billion in 2025, after KRW 1.47 billion in 2024. On a quarterly basis, an operating loss of KRW 495 million in Q3 2025 was followed by KRW 81.37 million in Q4 2025, KRW 668 million in Q1 2026, KRW 805 million in Q2 2026 and KRW 461 million in Q3 2026.

The Q3 2026 operating loss was smaller than both the prior quarter and the same quarter a year earlier. The controlling-shareholder net loss was largest in Q4 2025 at KRW 4.34 billion, while the KRW 1.04 billion loss in Q3 2026 widened from a year earlier.

According to WiseReport data as of September 7, 2026, half-year 2026 standalone sales fell 11.2% year on year, the operating loss rose 28.8%, and net income swung to a loss.

Quarterly sales bottomed at KRW 10.1 billion in Q1 2026 and recovered in Q2 and Q3, but the operating loss did not shrink much alongside the sales recovery.

05

Industry analysis

The cosmetics division operates against a backdrop of steady growth in the domestic cosmetics market and the overseas spread of K-beauty. WiseReport notes that K-beauty is spreading globally and that new market development is active.

Domestic distribution, however, is being reorganized around digital channels, so operating capability in online and home-shopping channels increasingly determines competitiveness.

Intensifying competition among small and mid-sized brands was cited alongside weakness in scrap trading and resort revenue as reasons for lower sales.

A FnGuide company summary reported a view that the global cosmetics market will grow about 6% a year through 2027, led by North America, with emerging markets in the Middle East, Africa and India gaining importance.

WiseReport also explains that demand for scrap steel is expected to rise as electric arc furnace steelmaking advances and carbon emission limits tighten. Scrap steel distribution supplies raw material for electric arc furnaces and is therefore tied to steel industry conditions.

On the product side, The Lamy is pursuing basic, hair and body care products tied to wider overseas sales through OEM and ODM work. Compared with companies centered on their own brands, whether OEM and ODM growth turns into actual revenue is the key test of its competitive position.

06

Outlook

Through Q3 2026, sales have recovered and the operating loss has narrowed, but the controlling-shareholder net loss of KRW 1.04 billion in Q3 2026 widened from a year earlier, so the signals are mixed.

The next test is whether the Q3 sales rebound continues in the same direction as the 11.2% year-on-year decline in half-year 2026 standalone sales. Expansion of overseas OEM and ODM work is a stated direction, but order size and contract values are items to confirm in periodic reports.

The scrap steel unit is cited as benefiting from demand growth tied to electric arc furnace expansion and tighter carbon emission limits.

For cosmetics, responding to the shift toward digital distribution channels is the main task, and brand competition and channel operations will determine whether the sales recovery holds.

The resort unit was weak in H1 2026, and whether its assets can be used more productively and its profitability improve is a variable for the business mix.

On governance, the March 30, 2026 annual general meeting approved charter amendments and director appointments, and the board now operates with one outside director among three directors, an outside-director ratio of 33.3%.

The 2026 annual report will fix annual sales, operating and net results, the goodwill balance and operating cash flow as disclosed full-year figures.

08

Bull factors

Two quarters of sales growth

Sales rose from a Q1 2026 low of KRW 10.1 billion to KRW 12.8 billion in Q2 and KRW 14 billion in Q3. Q3 2026 revenue grew 18.4% year on year. Whether the response to the shift toward digital channels sustains the recovery is the next point to confirm.

Narrowing operating loss

The Q3 2026 operating loss of KRW 461 million was smaller than KRW 495 million in Q3 2025 and than KRW 805 million in Q2 2026. The narrowing coincided with the sales increase. Whether the operating margin returns to positive territory is the indicator for judging structural improvement.

Low debt burden

At end-2025, total equity was KRW 52.4 billion and total liabilities KRW 3.23 billion, a debt ratio of 6.2%. Debt ratios moved from 11.9% in 2022 to 27.0%, 9.2% and 6.2%, and the 2025 level is low. The small borrowing burden can be read as a financial buffer while losses continue.

Bear factors

Accumulated net losses

Controlling-shareholder net losses over the last four quarters total KRW 6.92 billion. The KRW 4.34 billion loss in Q4 2025 was the largest, and Q3 2026 still recorded a KRW 1.04 billion loss.

Against total equity of KRW 52.4 billion, the accumulated losses remain a limited burden on equity, but continued losses would reduce equity over time.

Continuing operating losses

On a disclosed basis, operating losses have run for five consecutive quarters from Q3 2025 through Q3 2026. The 2025 operating loss of KRW 1.72 billion reversed the KRW 1.62 billion operating profit of 2024.

According to WiseReport data as of September 7, 2026, half-year 2026 standalone operating loss rose 28.8% year on year.

Weak business mix and cash flow

In H1 2026, reorganization of the cosmetics market and competition among small and mid-sized brands were cited as reasons for lower sales. WiseReport also attributes the sales decline partly to weaker scrap steel trading and resort operations. Operating cash flow fell to -KRW 2.13 billion in 2025 from KRW 1.47 billion in 2024.

Risk factors

Accounting risk (goodwill)

The company cited the impairment of goodwill, an intangible asset, as a main reason for the 2025 swing into loss. Additional impairment could again destabilize earnings. The 2026 annual report's financial statement notes are where this item can be checked.

Cash flow volatility

Operating cash flow swung from KRW 472 million in 2022 to -KRW 3.19 billion in 2023, KRW 1.47 billion in 2024 and -KRW 2.13 billion in 2025. If cash generation does not support quarters with losses, pressure on equity grows. The next periodic report's cash flow statement will show this.

Governance and business focus

Under the new management, the priority among cosmetics, scrap steel trading and resort operations is not yet clear. This could affect the pace of restructuring the business mix.

Overall view

The Lamy runs scrap steel raw-material recycling, cosmetics and a Gapyeong resort, and swung to an annual loss in 2025 on lower cosmetics sales and a goodwill impairment.

In 2026, sales recovered in Q2 and Q3 after a Q1 low, and the Q3 operating loss was smaller than both the prior quarter and the same quarter a year earlier. However, the controlling-shareholder net loss widened to KRW 1.04 billion in Q3 2026 from a year earlier, and the four-quarter total reached KRW 6.92 billion.

Half-year 2026 standalone figures also show lower sales and a wider operating loss. On the balance sheet, the debt ratio of 6.2% at end-2025 indicates a small borrowing burden, but operating cash flow was -KRW 2.13 billion in 2025.

Valuation sits in a range where PER cannot be calculated and the price is below book value, and whether earnings settle into sustained profit is the key to interpreting it. Strengths are the sales recovery and low debt; weaknesses are continuing losses and a weaker business mix.

The next checkpoints are the December 31, 2026 fiscal year-end and the 2026 annual report due by end of March 2027, where goodwill impairment and cash flow are the key items to confirm.
