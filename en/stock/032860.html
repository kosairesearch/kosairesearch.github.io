Report overview

Diversified Business, Persistent Quarterly Losses

02

Business structure

The Lamy was established in 1985 and listed on KOSDAQ in 1997, originally operating a steel scrap distribution business, before adding resort operations in 2021 through the acquisition of an income-generating property in Gapyeong County, Gyeonggi Province (Over The Mountain Resort), and adding cosmetics in 2023 by merging with Lamy Cosmetics.

According to the most recent disclosed breakdown, revenue is split roughly 67% from the metal and non-metal material recycling (steel scrap distribution) segment, about 31% from cosmetics, and around 1% from the resort business.

The cosmetics segment develops and sells basic skincare products along with hair, scalp, and body care items, distributed through domestic brand shops, online malls, home shopping, and social commerce channels.

Stated competitive factors include turn-key OEM/ODM services that engage with clients from the planning stage, brand recognition built on long-standing experience in cosmetics, and diversified distribution covering large discount marts, convenience stores, Daiso, and home shopping.

The steel scrap distribution business is a traditional B2B operation tied to raw material prices and global steel supply-demand cycles, while the resort segment remains a small family-hotel-style property operation.

The largest shareholder is Biosmart, which exercises group-level control together with special-relationship party Omnisystem and others. Although the company has positioned cosmetics as its core business, steel scrap distribution still accounts for a larger share of actual revenue.

04

Earnings analysis

Annual revenue fell 18.5% from KRW 62.6 billion in 2022 to KRW 51 billion in 2023, rose 36.9% to KRW 69.8 billion in 2024, then fell again 27.0% to KRW 50.9 billion in 2025.

Operating profit showed an operating loss of KRW 3.18 billion in 2022 and a widened operating loss of KRW 3.54 billion in 2023, before turning to an operating profit of KRW 1.62 billion in 2024, only to swing back to an operating loss of KRW 1.72 billion in 2025.

The operating margin was -5.1% in 2022, -6.9% in 2023, 2.3% in 2024, and -3.4% in 2025, showing negative profitability in every year except 2024.

Net income attributable to controlling shareholders followed a similar pattern: a net loss of KRW 5.61 billion in 2022 that narrowed to KRW 3.29 billion in 2023, a swing to net profit of KRW 2.48 billion in 2024, and a reversal to a net loss of KRW 4.65 billion in 2025.

The company attributed the 2025 swing to loss to a decline in cosmetics segment revenue combined with a goodwill impairment charge.

On a quarterly basis, the operating loss started at KRW 369 million in Q2 2025, widened to KRW 495 million in Q3 2025, narrowed to KRW 81.37 million in Q4 2025, widened to KRW 668 million in Q1 2026, and widened further to KRW 805 million in Q2 2026 versus both the prior quarter and the year-earlier period, marking five consecutive quarters of operating losses.

Net losses attributable to controlling shareholders over the same five quarters moved from KRW 329 million to KRW 391 million to KRW 4.34 billion to KRW 752 million to KRW 791 million, notably widening sharply in Q4 2025.

The cumulative net loss attributable to controlling shareholders over the trailing four quarters (Q3 2025 through Q2 2026) reached KRW 6.28 billion, while operating cash flow swung between periods, from -KRW 3.19 billion in 2023 to KRW 1.47 billion in 2024 and back to -KRW 2.13 billion in 2025.

05

Industry analysis

Korea's cosmetics industry continued to be led by export growth into 2026, with cosmetics exports in the first half of 2026 reaching a record USD 7.0 billion according to the Ministry of Food and Drug Safety, with the United States accounting for the largest share at 20.7%.

Brokerage research projected that domestic revenue for cosmetics ODM companies would grow 10-15% year over year in 2026, driven by client companies' expanding global presence.

However, this export strength tends to concentrate among large ODM and brand companies with high overseas channel exposure, making it difficult to gauge the degree to which The Lamy's cosmetics business, which is weighted toward domestic channels such as large discount marts, convenience stores, Daiso, and home shopping, directly benefits from the export momentum.

The steel scrap distribution business is a classic cyclical industry tied to raw material prices and global steel supply-demand cycles, with different volatility drivers than the cosmetics or resort businesses.

The resort business is confined to a small family-hotel-style income property in the Gapyeong area of Gyeonggi Province, subject to leisure demand and seasonality.

Because the company simultaneously operates three unrelated business lines, a boom or downturn in any single end market does not flow through directly to consolidated results, which can offset or amplify outcomes in ways that differ from single-business cosmetics or ODM peers.

Outlook

The company completed a 5-for-1 share consolidation in August 2026, adjusting the total shares outstanding and par value per share; while this action itself does not change shareholder value, it altered the trading unit and float structure.

Regarding cosmetics, The Lamy has been confirmed to be recruiting for an overseas sales team for cosmetics, suggesting continued efforts to expand overseas OEM/ODM sales channels.

However, no quantified guidance such as order backlogs, capacity expansion plans, or new product launch schedules that would determine the direction of annual or quarterly revenue has been separately disclosed to date.

Whether the effects of the 2025 goodwill impairment and the sharp widening of the Q4 2025 net loss attributable to controlling shareholders (KRW 4.34 billion) recur in subsequent quarters remains a key variable for future results.

Amid continued strength in industry-wide K-beauty exports, how much of that momentum transfers to The Lamy's domestically weighted cosmetics revenue will be a key point in assessing upcoming quarterly results.

Given that the controlling shareholder group of Biosmart and Omnisystem raised its combined stake to 54.75% as of September 2026 through the share consolidation and open-market purchases, it is also worth monitoring whether further ownership disclosures or governance-related actions follow.

Bull factors

Low Debt Ratio and Stable Capital Structure

The debt ratio fell to 6.2% by the end of 2025, indicating a limited financial leverage burden. Total equity at the end of 2025 stood at KRW 52.4 billion, entirely attributable to controlling shareholders, reflecting low reliance on external borrowing.

Even amid continued quarterly losses, this financial cushion does not translate into capital impairment concerns.

Industry Tailwind from the K-Beauty Export Boom

Industry-wide export momentum has continued, with domestic cosmetics exports reaching a record USD 7.0 billion in the first half of 2026. The Lamy has also been confirmed to be recruiting for an overseas sales team for cosmetics, pursuing export channel expansion.

Still, given its high domestic distribution weighting, how much of this industry strength translates into actual revenue will need to be confirmed in future quarterly results.

Stable Controlling Shareholder Structure

The combined stake of controlling shareholder Biosmart and its special-relationship parties exceeded a majority at 54.75% as of September 2026, reducing the likelihood of a management control dispute. No major shift in the ownership structure itself has been confirmed since the share consolidation. The stable maintenance of group-level control enhances governance predictability.

Bear factors

Operating losses and net losses attributable to controlling shareholders continued for five consecutive quarters from Q2 2025 through Q2 2026. The Q2 2026 operating loss of KRW 805 million widened versus both the prior quarter and the year-earlier period. A clear turning point toward profitability recovery has not yet been confirmed in disclosed results.

Large Year-to-Year Earnings Swings

Annual revenue swung sharply from -18.5% in 2023 to +36.9% in 2024 to -27.0% in 2025. Operating profit also alternated between losses, losses, profit, and loss again without showing a clear trend. The structure of operating three unrelated business lines simultaneously makes earnings less predictable.

Trading Instability from Concentrated Account Activity

On May 29, 2026, the stock was designated as subject to investor caution due to concentrated selling by a small number of accounts, with the price falling 21.65% over three days and the top ten accounts accounting for a 44.01% share of sell-side involvement.

Given the company's small market capitalization, trading by a limited number of accounts can have a relatively large price impact. A recurrence of such supply-demand events could raise concerns about trading stability.

10

Risk factors

Earnings Volatility Risk

Because the company simultaneously operates three unrelated businesses—steel scrap distribution, cosmetics, and resort operations—weakness in any single segment risks flowing directly through to consolidated results.

The impact of one-off items such as the 2025 goodwill impairment on the financial statements is also difficult to predict under this diversified structure. The repeated reversals in annual revenue direction over recent years underscore this volatility.

Governance Risk

With the combined stake of the controlling shareholder group—Biosmart and Omnisystem—exceeding a majority, the effects of share consolidations or group-level capital policies on minority shareholders warrant ongoing scrutiny.

The company was also designated as an investor-caution issue in March 2024 following five consecutive fiscal years of operating losses, underscoring the need to continuously monitor governance and disclosure-related risks. The possibility of further intercompany transactions or ownership disclosures should also be kept in mind.

Liquidity and Trading Risk

With a small-cap market capitalization, trading by a limited number of accounts can significantly affect the share price, as evidenced by the investor-caution designation in May 2026. In periods of low trading value, order execution may not always proceed smoothly. Such supply-demand factors can amplify share price volatility independent of underlying earnings.

Overall view

The Lamy is a diversified company built on a steel scrap distribution base with added cosmetics and resort operations, having posted operating losses in most of the past four years except 2024, before swinging back to a loss in 2025 amid a revenue decline and goodwill impairment.

From Q2 2025 through Q2 2026, both operating losses and net losses attributable to controlling shareholders continued for five consecutive quarters, with no clear sign of profitability improvement yet visible at the quarterly level.

On the balance sheet side, leverage is not heavy, with the debt ratio falling to 6.2%, but operating cash flow has swung in sign from year to year.

On the industry side, Korea's K-beauty exports have continued to set records into 2026, though The Lamy's cosmetics business is weighted toward domestic distribution, so the degree of linkage to this export momentum will need to be confirmed in future results.

Governance remains stable, with the Biosmart group holding a majority stake, and the share consolidation completed in August 2026 changed the trading unit structure.

Overall, the company is in a phase where managed financial leverage coexists with earnings volatility and continued quarterly losses, making the upcoming quarterly report and annual results key points to watch for signs of profitability recovery.
