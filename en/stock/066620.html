02

Business structure

Kukbo Design is an interior construction company founded in 1988, carrying out interior fit-out work for residential, commercial, office, hotel and leisure buildings.

A 2023 press report described the company as having been ranked first in the interior construction comprehensive evaluation for ten consecutive years starting in 2012.

Based on Q2 2026 revenue in the semi-annual report, the interior business accounted for 98% and the distribution business for 2%, so contract award results effectively determine the revenue structure.

A Q1 2026 press report said the company had eight major sites under way on a parent-only basis, spanning offices, accommodation, casinos and resorts.

Overseas operations are run by a Saudi Arabian subsidiary set up in 2024 and an Indian subsidiary, which reported Q1 2026 sales of KRW 3.8 billion and KRW 2.7 billion respectively.

Parent-level overseas sales fell from KRW 23.4 billion, or 29.2% of total, in Q1 2025 to zero in Q1 2026, and overseas contract wins at the parent were reported as effectively halted. Competition centers on domestic contract bidding, where award volume directly drives revenue and operating profit.

A first-half 2026 earnings commentary attributed the revenue and operating profit decline to fewer domestic contract project awards.

04

Earnings analysis

Annual revenue grew from KRW 378.4 billion in 2022 to KRW 438.9 billion in 2025, and both revenue and operating profit rose for three consecutive years from 2023 to 2025. Revenue growth slowed, at 6.0% in 2023, 5.1% in 2024, and 4.1% in 2025.

Operating profit increased from KRW 28.8 billion in 2022 to KRW 45.8 billion in 2025, and its growth rate was highest in 2024, at 28.7% year on year. The operating margin rose every year: 7.6% in 2022, 8.1% in 2023, 9.9% in 2024, and 10.4% in 2025.

Net profit attributable to controlling shareholders grew from KRW 20.9 billion in 2022 to KRW 40.7 billion in 2024, the highest in the period, and then fell 2.5% to KRW 39.7 billion in 2025.

The financial data show that one reason net profit in 2024 grew by much more than the operating profit growth rate (28.7%) is that dividend income on a standalone basis jumped from KRW 1.9 billion in 2023 to KRW 8.0 billion in 2024.

Net profit including non-controlling interests is reported at the same value as net profit attributable to controlling shareholders from 2022 to 2025.

The debt-to-equity ratio fell from 87.7% in 2022 to 46.1% in 2025, and total equity at the end of 2025, KRW 323.9 billion, all belongs to controlling shareholders, while total liabilities are KRW 149.2 billion.

Cash flow from operating activities came to KRW 23.6 billion in 2022, KRW 42.2 billion in 2023, KRW 29.0 billion in 2024, and KRW 39.6 billion in 2025.

On a quarterly basis, revenue moved at around KRW 100 billion, from KRW 115.9 billion in the third quarter of 2025, through KRW 101.8 billion in the fourth quarter (down 12.2% from the previous quarter), to KRW 100.5 billion in the third quarter of 2026.

Operating profit was highest in the first quarter of 2026 at KRW 15.5 billion, then fell to KRW 7.08 billion in the second quarter (down 54.2% from the previous quarter) and recovered to KRW 9.64 billion in the third quarter.

Net profit attributable to controlling shareholders was KRW 74.7 billion in the second quarter of 2026, far exceeding operating profit for the same quarter, and then swung to a net loss of KRW 30.1 billion in the third quarter.

Because operating profit increased over the same period, the cause of the third-quarter loss needs to be checked in non-operating items. A news report on the first quarter of 2026 attributed the decline in operating profit to a heavier burden from construction input costs.

05

Industry analysis

A slowdown in domestic construction is the largest external pressure on earnings. A first-half 2026 earnings commentary summarized continued weakness in domestic contract awards, a slowing construction market and profitability pressure. Remodeling demand growth was described as a tailwind in a Q1 2026 press report.

On the other side, higher construction input costs pushed the Q1 2026 parent-level operating margin to 15.2%, down from 15.9% a year earlier. Overseas contract wins at the parent were reported as effectively halted in Q1 2026.

In terms of cycle position, the company has built a strong reputation among top domestic interior contractors over many years, but its order flow has weakened in 2026.

The sector label is transport and logistics, yet the business is interior construction, so direct comparison with listed names such as HMM and Hyundai Glovis produces misleading read-throughs; the KOSDAQ construction sector is the more relevant benchmark.

Overseas subsidiaries reported Q1 2026 sales of KRW 3.8 billion and KRW 2.7 billion, a starting base for overseas business, but their scale to date is still limited relative to the parent's lost overseas revenue.

Bull factors

Multi-year improvement in profitability

Operating profit rose from KRW 28.8 billion in 2022 to KRW 45.8 billion in 2025, and the operating margin climbed from 7.6% to 10.4%. Revenue and operating profit both grew for three consecutive years from 2023 through 2025, reinforcing the same trend. The multi-year gain in profitability is a point to examine alongside changes in project mix and cost ratios.

A stronger balance sheet

The debt ratio fell from 87.7% in 2022 to 46.1% in 2025. Equity at year-end 2025 was KRW 323.9 billion, all attributable to controlling shareholders. Operating cash flow also rose to KRW 39.6 billion in 2025 from KRW 23.6 billion in 2022.

Financial asset base and dividend

A year-end cash dividend was approved at the March 2026 general meeting. Parent-level long-term financial assets stood at KRW 196.3 billion at year-end 2025 and form a major part of the asset base. Parent-level dividend income expanded sharply in 2024 and added to earnings.

Bear factors

Slowing top-line growth

Revenue growth slowed from 6.0% in 2023 to 5.1% in 2024 and 4.1% in 2025. Consolidated revenue fell 6.1% year on year in the first half of 2026. A decline in domestic contract awards was cited as the main reason.

Q3 net loss and cost pressure

Profit attributable to controlling shareholders turned into a net loss of KRW 30.1 billion in Q3 2026. A Q1 2026 press report said higher construction input costs pushed down the operating margin. Parent-level overseas sales also dropped to zero in Q1 2026.

Volatile quarterly earnings

Profit attributable to controlling shareholders swung from KRW 74.7 billion in Q2 2026 to a net loss in Q3. Q2 2026 operating profit of KRW 7.08 billion fell 54.2% from the prior quarter. Quarterly results that swing with non-operating items are the largest structural risk.

Risk factors

Overseas subsidiary expansion

The Saudi Arabian and Indian subsidiaries are presented as a way to widen the revenue base in the Middle East and South Asia. Their Q1 2026 sales of KRW 3.8 billion and KRW 2.7 billion remain small in scale.

If contract wins and project execution at these subsidiaries do not grow as planned, their effect in filling the gap left by parent-level overseas sales may be limited. Local tender pace and cost control at the subsidiaries will determine whether the expansion succeeds.

Revenue ramp at major new sites

The resort project (G site), contracted in April 2025, had reached 13.1% completion as of Q1 2026, meaning work had only just started. New projects at two sites are seen as the main drivers of second-half revenue.

If revenue recognition stays slow at low completion rates, second-half revenue could fall short of expectations. The pace at which these two sites convert into revenue will shape quarterly sales in the second half.

Remodeling demand conversion

Remodeling demand growth was described as a tailwind for the interior business in a Q1 2026 press report. However, it has not yet been confirmed whether this demand will convert into actual contract awards. If remodeling orders do not grow as expected, they may struggle to offset a weaker domestic order flow. The share of remodeling work in new contract disclosures will show how much this risk is materializing.

Overall view

Kukbo Design is an interior construction company whose full-year results from 2022 through 2025 show rising revenue and operating profit alongside a falling debt ratio. Quarterly results in 2026 have moved in mixed directions.

Operating profit fell from KRW 15.5 billion in Q1 2026 to KRW 7.08 billion in Q2 and recovered to KRW 9.64 billion in Q3, yet profit attributable to controlling shareholders turned into a net loss of KRW 30.1 billion in Q3. Q3 2026 revenue of KRW 100.5 billion was down 13.3% from a year earlier.

The first-half decline in consolidated revenue is attributed to fewer domestic contract awards and a slowing construction market.

Positive factors include multi-year profit improvement, a low debt ratio, and a capital and financial asset base, while negative factors include weaker awards, cost pressure, the loss of overseas revenue at the parent, and quarterly earnings volatility.

These factors carry equal weight, and interpreting the multiples depends on confirming the direction of earnings. The next checkpoints are the operating margin in the 2026 annual report, Q1 2027 results, and new contract disclosures.
