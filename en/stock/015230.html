02

Business structure

Daechang Forging, founded in 1955 in Gimhae, South Gyeongsang Province, forges and assembles track components such as links, rollers and shoes for construction machinery undercarriages. It supplies OEM parts and aftermarket (A/S) parts to makers of excavators and bulldozers.

As of the Q1 2026 quarterly report, the group had three consolidated subsidiaries, two of them major. The company obtained IATF 16949 certification in 2017, and quality credentials plus long supply histories act as an entry barrier in the forging parts market.

Its overseas base started with the 2021 Track Design India joint venture, whose plant began production on April 1, 2024. In 2023 it acquired a 50% stake in Sunway Daechang Forging (Anhui). Overseas exports made up 71% of sales in the first half of 2023, so overseas revenue weighs more than domestic sales.

A 2023 brokerage view said the Indian joint venture's sales would start from September 2023 for a construction-equipment customer. The company's 2024 IR material cited aftermarket expansion as a driver of second-half sales.

Revenue splits into OEM parts, A/S parts and merchandise sales, and the company said in its 2025 third-quarter cumulative review that merchandise sales growth lifted revenue. Competition centers on expanding overseas OEM production and sales networks.

04

Earnings analysis

Annual results peaked in 2022, fell through 2024 and partly recovered in 2025. Revenue went from KRW 421.5 billion in 2022 to KRW 372.1 billion in 2023 (down 11.7% year on year) and KRW 322.2 billion in 2024 (down 13.4%), before rising to KRW 346.3 billion in 2025 (up 7.5%).

Among 2022 to 2025, 2022 was the largest year for revenue and 2024 the smallest. Operating profit was KRW 49.9 billion in 2022, KRW 46.4 billion in 2023 (down 6.8%), KRW 30.1 billion in 2024 (down 35.2%) and KRW 38 billion in 2025 (up 26.5%).

The operating margin was 11.8% in 2022, 12.5% in 2023, 9.3% in 2024 and 11.0% in 2025, recovering from the 2024 low.

Net profit attributable to controlling shareholders fell from KRW 36.7 billion in 2022 to KRW 33.9 billion in 2023 (down 7.5%), KRW 33.6 billion in 2024 (down 1.1%) and KRW 28.8 billion in 2025 (down 14.3%), a decline in each year from 2023 through 2025.

Net profit including non-controlling interests was KRW 27.5 billion in 2025, a different figure from the controlling-shareholder number, and the gap reflects non-controlling interests.

Operating cash flow rose from KRW 29.3 billion in 2022 to KRW 73.4 billion in 2023, then fell to KRW 40.9 billion in 2024 and KRW 17.7 billion in 2025. On a quarterly basis, Q3 2025 revenue was KRW 86.5 billion, operating profit KRW 10.6 billion and controlling-shareholder net profit KRW 8.96 billion.

Q4 2025 revenue rose 1.3% quarter on quarter to KRW 87.6 billion, while operating profit fell 29.4% to KRW 7.46 billion. Q1 2026 revenue reached KRW 98.4 billion, up 12.3%, with operating profit of KRW 11.5 billion, up 54.5%, and controlling-shareholder net profit of KRW 11.3 billion, up 76.1%.

Q2 2026 revenue rose 3.8% to KRW 102.1 billion and operating profit rose 36.5% to KRW 15.7 billion.

Q3 2026 revenue fell 18.3% quarter on quarter to KRW 83.4 billion, down 3.6% year on year, operating profit fell to KRW 5.73 billion, down 45.7% year on year, and the company posted a controlling-shareholder net loss of KRW 1.61 billion, a swing into loss. Over the last four quarters, controlling-shareholder net profit totaled KRW 28.7 billion.

05

Industry analysis

Daechang Forging's end market is the production of excavators, bulldozers and other construction machinery. Demand for the undercarriage parts it supplies moves with construction equipment output, so the equipment demand cycle is an external driver of results.

In 2023, brokerage analysts expected excavator demand to continue for several years on expanding global infrastructure investment, and Hyundai Motor Securities expected North American and Indian infrastructure spending to keep benefiting the company.

Those views date from 2023 and do not directly show where the demand cycle stands in 2026. A 2023 brokerage view also said stabilizing raw material and freight costs would support mid-term earnings momentum.

With exports at 71% of sales in the first half of 2023, exchange rates and overseas equipment demand are shared variables. The company is classified under automotive, but its results track construction machinery output and overseas infrastructure orders more closely than vehicle sales figures.

In forging parts, quality certification and long supplier relationships create a barrier to new entrants, which tends to keep established suppliers' positions relatively stable. A comparison of market shares and capacity utilization across rival companies is outside the scope of the company disclosures reviewed.

Exposure to Overseas Demand and FX

Overseas exports were 71% of sales in the first half of 2023, so overseas equipment demand and exchange-rate moves feed directly into revenue. Operating cash flow fell from KRW 29.3 billion in 2022 to KRW 17.7 billion in 2025.

10

Risk factors

Customer Concentration and OEM Dependence

Under an OEM supply model, the production plans of a few construction-machinery makers drive revenue. If major customers cut output, plant utilization and sales can move together. The 18.3% quarter-on-quarter revenue decline in Q3 2026 can be read as an example of this demand sensitivity.

FX and Raw Material and Freight Costs

High export exposure means won-dollar and other exchange-rate moves affect revenue and profit directly. A 2023 brokerage view said cost stabilization in raw materials and freight was a mid-term earnings driver. If raw material and freight costs rise again, the operating margin could fall toward the 9.3% seen in 2024.

Concentrated Ownership and Share Count Changes

Park Kwon-il, a major shareholder and CEO, held 21.81% as of November 10, 2026. Shares held by the largest shareholder and related parties totaled 48.23% as of June 1, 2026. Because ownership is concentrated, the number of shares available for trading is limited, which can affect trading volume and price volatility.

Overall view

Daechang Forging forges undercarriage parts for construction machinery and supplies them as OEM and aftermarket parts, with a high share of overseas sales.

Annual results peaked in 2022 and fell through 2024, and operating profit recovered in 2025, but net profit attributable to controlling shareholders declined in each year from 2023 through 2025.

Operating profit and controlling-shareholder net profit recovered through Q2 2026, then revenue and profit fell together in Q3 and the company posted a net loss attributable to controlling shareholders. Over the last four quarters, that net profit totaled KRW 28.7 billion.

On the balance sheet, the debt ratio of 24.3% and total equity of KRW 323.3 billion at the end of 2025 indicate a modest debt burden, and controlling-shareholder open-market purchases and the March 2026 share cancellation were also disclosed.
