Shifting weight to semiconductor logistics as losses persist

02

Business structure

MX Robotics began in 1972 as Sinheung Machinery and grew mainly on automation equipment for traditional manufacturers such as tire, energy, chemical and food producers. After joining the SK Group in 2017, it entered the semiconductor field in earnest, centered on logistics automation for SK hynix fabs.

The company has developed and supplied logistics robots such as rail guided vehicles (RGV), automated guided vehicles (AGV) and gantry robots since 2006, added autonomous mobile robots (AMR) for semiconductors from 2026, and plans to expand the lineup to overhead hoist transport (OHT) systems over the medium to long term.

The business has two main parts. One is traditional logistics automation equipment, including conveyor systems for automotive and EV production lines, battery process automation, warehouse management systems for distribution centers, AGVs and high-speed sorting systems.

The other is process logistics equipment for semiconductor fabs. CEO Jo Jae-kwan said the company expanded its semiconductor process logistics product plant in Osan, Gyeonggi, in 2025 with a cleanroom covering about 1,600 pyeong. Production bases are located in Chungju, Jeonju and Pyeongtaek.

Overseas exposure is high, and Financial News reported that at least 70% of revenue comes from markets including India, the United States and China. The company says it holds more than 180 patents related to smart logistics and automated high-rise warehouses.

On competition, the company describes the Korean logistics automation equipment and robot system market as highly competitive, while entry barriers for local players are high in China. Among listed peers in logistics automation, Hyundai Movex (Hyundai Movex) is one of the names in the same market.

04

Earnings analysis

On a disclosed basis, annual revenue rose from KRW 115 billion in 2022 to a peak of KRW 173.8 billion in 2023, a 51.1% increase, and then fell for two consecutive years to KRW 163.2 billion in 2024, down 6.1%, and KRW 149.1 billion in 2025, down 8.7%.

Among the years from 2022 to 2025, 2023 was the largest and 2022 was the smallest. Profit swings have been larger than revenue swings.

Operating profit went from KRW 2.01 billion in 2022 to an operating loss of KRW 18.8 billion in 2023, turned to operating profit of KRW 7.06 billion in 2024, and then returned to an operating loss of KRW 7.95 billion in 2025. The operating margin was 1.7% in 2022, -10.8% in 2023, 4.3% in 2024 and -5.3% in 2025.

Net profit attributable to controlling shareholders followed the same direction, with a loss of KRW 18.7 billion in 2023, a profit of KRW 11.4 billion in 2024 and a loss of KRW 8.17 billion in 2025.

Net income including non-controlling interests is disclosed at the same amounts as net profit attributable to controlling shareholders in each year.

The company attributed the 2025 loss to one-off costs, including an order drought in the first half, provisions and acquisition-related expenses, and said overseas orders resumed from the second half.

On the balance sheet, the debt-to-equity ratio rose from 56.4% in 2022 to 68.1% in 2023, 83.1% in 2024 and 120.2% in 2025. At the end of 2025, total equity was KRW 53 billion, all of it attributable to controlling shareholders, and total liabilities were KRW 63.7 billion.

Operating cash flow was KRW 5.52 billion in 2022 and KRW 7.81 billion in 2023, then turned negative at -KRW 2.11 billion in 2024 and -KRW 16 billion in 2025.

Quarterly revenue was KRW 33.4 billion in the third quarter of 2025, KRW 29.7 billion in the fourth quarter (down 11.1% from the prior quarter), KRW 25.8 billion in the first quarter of 2026 (down 13.1%), KRW 28.7 billion in the second quarter (up 11.3%) and KRW 28.3 billion in the third quarter of 2026 (down 15.5% from a year earlier).

Operating losses were KRW 3.21 billion, KRW 6.99 billion, KRW 1.14 billion, KRW 1.47 billion and KRW 3.62 billion over the same five quarters, a losing streak of five consecutive quarters from the third quarter of 2025.

In the first quarter of 2026, net income attributable to controlling shareholders turned to a profit of KRW 194 million despite the operating loss, which suggests non-operating items weighed heavily on that quarter.

Over the last four quarters, the combined net loss attributable to controlling shareholders totals KRW 14.9 billion.

05

Industry analysis

MX Robotics’ order flow relies heavily on the semiconductor capital expenditure cycle.

NH Investment & Securities, in a report on parent MX On, said the related business is turning around on a recovery in semiconductor capex, and it expected MX Robotics to see AMR launches and semiconductor-related revenue growth this year, with OHT revenue expansion in 2027.

The company has said it will step up investment in semiconductor logistics automation to respond to larger investment by key customers such as SK hynix.

However, a recovery in semiconductor demand and a rebound in earnings should be viewed separately, and some assessments note that orders have increased in 2026 while an earnings rebound has not yet become visible. In Korea, competition in logistics automation equipment and robot systems is intense.

In China, high entry barriers make local partnerships important. In October 2026 the company signed an MOU with the Chinese logistics automation company OMH Science Group to consider cross-supplying each other’s products in projects, and it also signed a separate MOU with the Chinese cobot maker AUBO Robotics.

Because overseas revenue is a large share, project flows in India, the United States and China, along with exchange rates, feed directly into results.

On the cycle, the company and its parent view the market as one where semiconductor investment is resuming, but translating that into reported earnings is still at an early stage.

Outlook

The company’s stated plans fall into three areas. First, in semiconductors it plans to supply semiconductor AMRs to customers from the second half of 2026, and in the fourth quarter of 2026 it plans to set up an OHT test line at its Osan semiconductor center for customer technical verification and commercialization.

Second, in overseas orders, it disclosed on July 2, 2026 a supply contract worth KRW 19.5 billion with Reliance Industries Limited of India for process logistics automation equipment in solar panel manufacturing, with a contract period from March 25, 2026 to April 7, 2027.

The contract amount equals 13.08% of recent revenue. Third, the order backlog stood at KRW 182.8 billion at the end of March 2026, including KRW 27.6 billion domestically and KRW 155.2 billion overseas.

On funding, parent MX On took part in a KRW 10 billion third-party rights issue in August 2026 at KRW 3,040 per share, with proceeds planned for new business investment, project operating funds and balance sheet improvement, and KRW 5 billion to be spent in each of 2026 and 2027.

The company expects earnings to improve from next year, based on domestic semiconductor investment and overseas project wins. Since operating losses continued through the third quarter of 2026, the key test is whether that expectation shows up in reported quarterly results.

At an extraordinary shareholders’ meeting on September 28, 2026, Kang Dong-jun was appointed as an executive director and Kim Seok-won as a non-executive director. A news report said operating cash flow turned to a net inflow of KRW 2.3 billion in the first quarter of 2026. Profitability of the new businesses remains an open task.

Risk factors

Financial and liquidity

The debt-to-equity ratio reached 120.2% and short-term borrowings rose to KRW 29.3 billion. Operating cash flow was -KRW 16 billion in 2025. Cash flow turned to a net inflow in the first quarter of 2026, but a single quarter is too early to judge a trend.

How fast the KRW 10 billion from the largest shareholder is spent will determine how much it eases the financial burden.

Customer and cycle concentration

The semiconductor business depends heavily on investment plans of major customers such as SK hynix. Even as orders grow, revenue recognition can slip if equipment investment timing is pushed back. The company said an order drought in 2025 weighed on results. Overseas projects can also shift revenue timing if schedules change.

Governance and parent dependence

After the rights issue, parent MX On’s stake rose to 32.2%. Whether the parent keeps supplying funds will affect business continuity. New executive and non-executive directors were appointed at an extraordinary shareholders’ meeting in September 2026.

As technology and business cooperation with the parent deepens, how decision-making is run independently is also worth watching.

Overall view

MX Robotics is shifting from traditional logistics automation equipment toward semiconductor process logistics. Annual revenue has declined for two consecutive years since the 2023 peak, and in 2025 the company swung to an operating loss and a net loss attributable to controlling shareholders.

Operating losses have continued for five consecutive quarters through the third quarter of 2026, and the combined net loss attributable to controlling shareholders over the last four quarters is KRW 14.9 billion.

On the other side, the SK hynix-linked orders, the large Indian contract, the overseas cooperation MOUs and the KRW 182.8 billion order backlog show a broader business base.

On finances, the 120.2% debt-to-equity ratio and rising short-term borrowings are burdens, and how far the KRW 10 billion rights issue from the largest shareholder eases them is the key question. A price-to-book ratio near 1x makes the pace of equity erosion the central variable in valuation.

The 2026 annual report, the next regular filing, will confirm full-year operating results and fourth-quarter trends.
