02

Business structure

Interflex is an electronic components maker focused on flexible printed circuit boards (FPCBs), with FPCBs used in smartphone displays and touch input modules at the core of the business.

The company was launched in 1998 as the FPC production unit of its parent Korea Circuit and became a standalone FPCB specialist after a spin-off in 2000. Around 2017 it supplied both display and touch screen panel (TSP) FPCBs for Apple's OLED iPhone, and its Apple sales share rose sharply.

However, after Apple began sourcing TSP FPCBs from other suppliers in 2018, the Apple share fell steeply by around 2019, and the company pivoted toward Chinese camera module and display customers. A 2020 report named Sunny Optical and BOE as new customers in China.

In the domestic FPCB market it competes with BH and SI Flex, while Samsung Electro-Mechanics and LG Innotek are cited as major rivals in camera modules.

A Hankyung report in November 2025 pointed to expectations of expanded Apple-related FPCB orders and a possible shift toward FPCBs for automotive electronics and industrial IoT equipment.

The customer mix has moved from a Samsung-centric base toward a mix that includes Apple and Chinese manufacturers, and how this shift is reflected in 2026 revenue should be checked against the business section of the annual report.

04

Earnings analysis

On a reported annual basis, sales fell from KRW 442.7 billion in 2022 to KRW 438.2 billion in 2023 (down 1.0% year on year), then rose to KRW 497.5 billion in 2024 (up 13.5%), the largest year in the 2022–2025 window. Sales fell to KRW 468 billion in 2025 (down 5.9%).

Operating profit peaked at KRW 34.4 billion in 2024 (up 58.8%) and was smallest at KRW 21.6 billion in 2023 (down 17.0%); the KRW 28.6 billion posted in 2025 (down 16.7%) was lower than 2024 but above 2023. The operating margin was 5.9% in 2022, 4.9% in 2023, 6.9% in 2024 and 6.1% in 2025.

Parent-level net income rose from KRW 15.3 billion in 2022 to KRW 27.3 billion in 2023 (up 78.5%) and KRW 55.1 billion in 2024 (up 101.8%), then fell to KRW 32.9 billion in 2025 (down 40.3%). Net income including non-controlling interests was KRW 32.8 billion in 2025, a separate figure.

Operating cash flow was KRW 43.6 billion in 2022, KRW 13.7 billion in 2023, KRW 64.4 billion in 2024 and KRW 25.1 billion in 2025, positive in all four years.

On a quarterly basis, sales rose from KRW 102 billion with operating profit of KRW 5.85 billion in Q3 2025 to KRW 148.4 billion with KRW 12.5 billion in Q4 2025 (up 45.5% and 112.8% from the prior quarter), the highest of the five reported quarters on both measures.

Q1 2026 sales were KRW 129 billion (down 13.1% quarter on quarter) with operating profit of KRW 5.29 billion (down 57.6%). In Q2 2026, sales fell to KRW 90.9 billion (down 29.5%) and the company swung to an operating loss of KRW 5.17 billion.

Q3 2026 sales recovered to KRW 109.3 billion (up 20.2% from the prior quarter and up 7.1% from a year earlier), but the company still posted an operating loss of KRW 3.31 billion, a narrower loss than in Q2.

The parent-level net loss in the same quarter was KRW 17.1 billion, much larger than the operating loss, so the composition of non-operating items and taxes is the next item to examine. Parent-level net income for the last four reported quarters, from Q4 2025 through Q3 2026, totaled KRW 5.68 billion.

05

Industry analysis

The FPCB industry depends heavily on smartphone shipments and the new-model cycles of major customers. Interflex posted three consecutive years of large losses from 2014 to 2016 amid domestic FPCB oversupply, and returned to profit in 2017 on Apple OLED iPhone orders.

It swung back to losses in 2018 after Apple began sourcing TSP FPCBs from other suppliers, a case that shows how dependence on a single customer amplifies cyclical swings.

A November 2025 Hankyung report noted that the structural shift has drawn attention while concerns about a slowdown in mobile-centered demand persist.

On the automotive side, analysts cite rising demand for circuit components from the growth of electric and autonomous vehicles, and the same report flagged a lag before automotive and industrial demand translates into sales.

In competition, large component makers such as Samsung Electro-Mechanics and LG Innotek are widening their reach into camera modules and substrates, so Interflex's pace of customer diversification as a focused FPCB supplier is the key variable.

Whether the Q3 2026 sales increase reflected new-model demand or a change in customer mix should be checked against the company's business explanations.

Outlook

The biggest variable for future earnings is Apple-related FPCB volume. A November 2025 Hankyung report conveyed market expectations that deliveries to Apple would begin in earnest, but this was a press-stage expectation and not a volume or timing confirmed by the company.

For FPCBs used in automotive and industrial IoT equipment, the same report pointed to whether supply lines are running as the item to watch.

Q3 2026 sales recovered to KRW 109.3 billion, yet an operating loss of KRW 3.31 billion remained, so whether incremental sales outpace the fixed-cost burden is the next checkpoint. Whether losses in Q2 and Q3 carry into Q4 will likely determine whether full-year 2026 returns to operating profit.

Because the parent-level net loss was far larger than the operating loss, how non-operating items and income tax expense appear in Q4 results also deserves attention. The timing of revenue from automotive FPCBs can be checked in the business description of the 2026 annual report.

The point at which Apple volume gains show up in reported sales can be read through the first-half 2027 quarterly results. Whether the operating margin climbs back above the 6.1% recorded in 2025 is the most direct gauge of profit recovery.

Whether the business shift reduces mobile dependence will become visible over time through changes in the sales mix by customer and by product.

Bull factors

Expected expansion of Apple-related orders

A November 2025 Hankyung report named expanded Apple-related FPCB orders as a key driver. Interflex had previously rebounded on Apple OLED iPhone volume in 2017, so renewed customer volume could lift sales. Full-year 2025 operating profit of KRW 28.6 billion was above the KRW 21.6 billion of 2023.

The expectation, however, is at the press stage, and the size and timing of volume have not been announced by the company.

Expansion into automotive and industrial IoT

A November 2025 report pointed to a possible move into FPCBs for automotive electronics and industrial IoT equipment. Analysts cited rising demand for circuit components as electric and autonomous vehicles expand. Spreading the business beyond smartphones could reduce earnings volatility over time. The same report also noted that there is a lag between starting supply lines and recognizing revenue.

Lower debt burden

At the end of 2025, total liabilities were KRW 83.9 billion and total equity was KRW 317.9 billion, giving a debt ratio of 26.4%. The ratio declined from 45.0% in 2022 to 36.7% in 2023 and 29.5% in 2024.

Operating cash flow was positive in all four years from 2022 to 2025, at KRW 43.6 billion, KRW 13.7 billion, KRW 64.4 billion and KRW 25.1 billion. Financial capacity during loss-making quarters provides room to absorb the time needed for the business shift.

Bear factors

Two consecutive loss-making quarters

Interflex posted an operating loss of KRW 5.17 billion in Q2 2026 and KRW 3.31 billion in Q3, two consecutive loss-making quarters. Parent-level net losses were KRW 4 billion in Q2 and KRW 17.1 billion in Q3, a wider loss in the latter. Parent-level net income for the last four quarters totaled only KRW 5.68 billion.

The core of the bearish case is that profits have not followed the recovery in Q3 2026 sales to KRW 109.3 billion.

Profit decline in 2025

Operating profit fell 16.7% to KRW 28.6 billion in 2025, and parent-level net income dropped 40.3% to KRW 32.9 billion. Parent-level net income had reached KRW 55.1 billion in 2024, so it fell sharply within a single year. The operating margin slipped from 6.9% in 2024 to 6.1% in 2025. The bearish reading is that earnings power has weakened since the 2024 peak.

Apple dependence and customer concentration

Interflex returned to losses around 2018 after Apple volume declined. A 2020 report noted that Apple's share of sales fell from 73.4% in Q3 2017 to 14.1% in Q3 2019. High customer concentration means volume changes flow quickly into earnings swings.

The sales weight of automotive and industrial products still needs to be confirmed in the annual report, so the speed at which structural change shows up in earnings is uncertain.

Risk factors

Customer concentration and volume swings

Heavy reliance on a few smartphone customers, including Apple and Samsung, means decisions on new models or shifts of volume to other suppliers can quickly affect sales. Apple's 2018 move to dual-source TSP FPCBs is an example. Customer product performance and design changes are also outside the company's control.

Customer diversification is underway, but whether it is large enough to be reflected in results should be checked in the annual report.

Mobile demand cycle and oversupply

FPCBs move with smartphone shipments, so a slowdown in mobile demand lowers factory utilization. Interflex posted three consecutive years of large losses from 2014 to 2016 due to domestic FPCB oversupply. A November 2025 report noted that concerns about a slowdown in mobile-centered demand continue.

In a production structure with heavy fixed costs, lower utilization can leave little profit even when sales rise.

Timing and execution risk in new businesses

Automotive and industrial IoT applications take time for qualification and mass-production readiness, so even with rising demand, a lag occurs before sales are recognized. The November 2025 report also flagged this lag as a risk.

If the transition is delayed, the time needed to offset lower mobile volume becomes longer. With operating losses continuing in Q3 2026, rising investment needs for new businesses could reduce financial headroom.

Overall view

Interflex's reported results peaked in 2024, and in 2025 sales, operating profit and parent-level net income all declined. In 2026, quarterly sales have moved up and down, and two consecutive quarters of losses have followed.

Q3 2026 sales of KRW 109.3 billion rose 7.1% from a year earlier, but an operating loss of KRW 3.31 billion and a parent-level net loss of KRW 17.1 billion continued.

On the bullish side, the points cited are expectations for expanded Apple-related FPCB orders, a possible shift toward automotive and industrial IoT, and a financial position strengthened by a debt ratio of 26.4%.

On the bearish side, the points are two consecutive loss quarters, a thinner earnings base with parent-level net income of KRW 5.68 billion over the last four quarters, and a history of sharp earnings swings tied to Apple dependence.

The two views describe the same facts from different angles, and which one prevails will depend on how fast profits recover. Valuation readings also shift with earnings, so the next checkpoints are the Q4 2026 preliminary results and the operating margin in the 2026 annual report.

Whether the timing and size of automotive and industrial revenue become visible in disclosures is another point to follow. This report is provided for information only and contains no buy or sell opinion.
