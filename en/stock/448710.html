Defense Computing Supplier: Order Flow and Quarterly Margins

COTS Technology supplies embedded computers for defense weapon systems. Its 2025 revenue of KRW 68.1 billion was the largest of 2022 to 2025, but operating profit fell to KRW 8.32 billion, and 3Q26 operating profit of KRW 1.58 billion was down 64.4% year on year.

02

Business structure

COTS Technology is an embedded systems specialist for the defense industry that builds single board computers (SBC) and computers mounted on weapon systems. According to a Korea Economic Daily report, it is the only company in Korea able to manufacture SBCs for defense use.

Research Aleum's October 2026 report said the company supplies 19 product types for the K2 tank and five for the Cheongung-II.

Based on a April 2024 brokerage report, the 2023 revenue mix was military display units 26.9%, SBCs 24.9%, weapon-mounted computer units 21.0%, service revenue 12.6%, weapon system systems 5.4%, civilian 2.8%, and other 6.3%. The same report put LIG D&A at 51.6% and Hanwha Systems at 17.8% of 2023 revenue.

For the K2 tank, Hyundai Rotem receives integrated vehicle control computers, military displays and single board computers, and LIG D&A receives an integrated mission computer plus four other products for the Cheongung-II, according to iM Securities in March 2026.

On competition, the company says its SBCs cost about one third less than overseas rivals' products with equal quality, per a CEO interview in November 2025. Research Aleum notes that high prices and long lead times for overseas products support demand for local alternatives.

In civilian business, the company developed an HVDC control system with Hyosung Heavy Industries that was delivered to KEPCO, and has built power plant controllers with Doosan Enerbility since 2023. The company has set a medium-term target of raising the civilian share of total revenue to 50%.

04

Earnings analysis

Annual revenue rose from KRW 41.8 billion in 2022 to KRW 51.3 billion in 2023 (up 22.6%), KRW 65 billion in 2024 (up 26.7%), and KRW 68.1 billion in 2025 (up 4.8%). Revenue has grown three years in a row from 2023 through 2025, with 2025 the largest year and 2022 the smallest among 2022 to 2025.

Growth slowed sharply in 2025 compared with the prior two years. Operating profit rose from KRW 2.79 billion in 2022 to KRW 8.8 billion in 2023 (up 216.0%), then reached KRW 9.48 billion in 2024 (up 7.7%), the largest year of 2022 to 2025. Operating profit in 2025 was KRW 8.32 billion, down 12.3% from the prior year.

The operating margin was 6.7% in 2022, 17.2% in 2023, 14.6% in 2024 and 12.2% in 2025, so it has eased since the 2023 improvement.

Profit attributable to controlling shareholders was KRW 2.3 billion in 2022, KRW 7.01 billion in 2023 (up 205.4%), KRW 8.17 billion in 2024 (up 16.5%), and KRW 7.37 billion in 2025 (down 9.8%).

Net income including non-controlling interests was reported at the same amounts as controlling-shareholder profit in every year from 2022 to 2025.

Quarterly revenue fell from KRW 21.3 billion in 3Q25 with operating profit of KRW 4.43 billion to KRW 19.4 billion in 4Q25 (down 9.2% quarter on quarter), with operating profit of KRW 498 million (down 88.8%).

Revenue was KRW 16.9 billion in 1Q26 (down 12.6%) with operating profit of KRW 1.37 billion (up 174.2%), then recovered to KRW 20.7 billion in 2Q26 (up 22.6%) with operating profit of KRW 2.48 billion (up 81.6%).

In 3Q26, revenue was KRW 19.4 billion (down 8.8% year on year), operating profit KRW 1.58 billion (down 64.4%), and profit attributable to controlling shareholders KRW 1.13 billion (down 69.3%).

Research Aleum attributed the 8.1% operating margin in 1Q26 to raw material cost pressure, depreciation on the new headquarters and R&D headcount growth, and said the 12.0% in 2Q26 was helped in part by revenue recognition on export products.

Profit attributable to controlling shareholders for the last four quarters from 4Q25 to 3Q26 totals KRW 5.27 billion.

05

Industry analysis

Although classified under semiconductors, the business is embedded computing for defense. As AI and unmanned systems spread across weapon systems, the performance and importance of onboard computers are rising, and Research Aleum expects computing device demand to grow.

Research Aleum forecasts the global defense embedded systems market to grow from USD 2.2 billion in 2026 to USD 4.5 billion in 2035, an average annual rate of 8.6%.

On the cycle, export growth for the K2 tank and Cheongung-II has moved into the revenue recognition stage. iM Securities said in March 2026 that domestic fourth-phase K2 mass production orders would start to show up in revenue from 2026.

The export pipeline includes preparations for an additional K2 contract with Poland, talks with Romania and Iraq, and LIG D&A's discussions on Cheongung-II exports to Qatar and Kuwait, according to Research Aleum in October 2026.

Because orders to component suppliers lag system integrators' contracts, order effects tend to come later. Research Aleum said the company has worked with system companies from the development stage and can offer custom designs and meet delivery schedules.

The company is developing next-generation SBCs with NPUs, and Research Aleum sees a shift to higher-performance products as a possible route to higher unit prices and direct overseas sales. Order cycles in defense depend on the overseas contract timing of system integrators. The company says it has doubled production equipment to meet rising demand for K-defense products.

06

Outlook

The company has set a target of KRW 100 billion in annual revenue by 2030, according to a CEO interview in November 2025. In the same interview, the CEO said orders from the second Poland K2 supply contract and the fourth domestic mass production run would be reflected in revenue from 2026.

Revenue in 3Q26 fell 8.8% year on year, so the link between order recognition and the quarterly earnings trend is not yet clear in the quarterly figures.

In January 2026 the company held its New Year ceremony at the new Gwacheon headquarters and unveiled the slogan We Empower Core and a vision to become a global embedded systems company. Research Aleum said the move to the new headquarters raised annual production capacity to as much as KRW 120 billion.

In July 2026 the company announced successful localization of a Cheonma fire control unit component, and in September it attended MSPO in Poland to unveil an AI solution. On October 12, 2026 it disclosed a single-sale supply contract worth KRW 14.77 billion with HD Hyundai Heavy Industries.

Research Aleum expects new orders to increase from the second half of 2027 and noted the company is developing next-generation SBCs with NPUs. If Cheongung-II exports to Qatar and Kuwait and an additional K2 contract with Poland are finalized, the order flow could widen further.

Bear factors

Quarterly earnings volatility

Operating profit in 3Q26 was KRW 1.58 billion, down 64.4% from a year earlier. Operating profit fell to KRW 498 million in 4Q25, recovered to KRW 2.48 billion in 2Q26, and then declined again. Profit attributable to controlling shareholders for the last four quarters totals KRW 5.27 billion. The size of quarter-to-quarter swings is large.

Margin decline and cost burden

Research Aleum attributed the 8.1% margin in 1Q26 to raw material costs, depreciation on the new headquarters and R&D headcount growth. The same analysis said passing higher input costs into selling prices is a key variable for second-half margins.

Shift in balance sheet

The debt-to-equity ratio rose to 124.8% at the end of 2025 from 45.5% in 2024, with total liabilities of KRW 67.2 billion. Operating cash flow was -KRW 7.17 billion in 2024 and KRW 6.26 billion in 2025. How this balance sheet change affects capex and working capital needs will appear in the next annual report.

10

Risk factors

Customer and export concentration

In 2023, LIG D&A accounted for 51.6% of revenue and Hanwha Systems for 17.8%, according to an April 2024 brokerage report. Export volumes depend on the overseas contract timing of system makers such as Hyundai Rotem and LIG D&A. Component orders typically trail system contracts by six months to a year, so order recognition can be delayed.

Cost structure

Raw material costs, depreciation on the new headquarters and R&D headcount growth were cited as the reasons the 1Q26 operating margin fell to 8.1%, according to Research Aleum. The firm expects export volume effects and pass-through of raw material costs to selling prices to continue in the second half. If price pass-through lags, margins could come under pressure again.

Balance sheet

Total equity and the portion attributable to controlling shareholders are both KRW 53.8 billion, against total liabilities of KRW 67.2 billion. Operating cash flow was -KRW 7.17 billion in 2024. If borrowing or working capital needs keep rising, financial burden could grow.

Overall view

COTS Technology supplies embedded computers for defense weapon systems, and its 2025 revenue of KRW 68.1 billion was the largest of 2022 to 2025. The operating margin declined from 17.2% in 2023 to 12.2% in 2025, and 2025 operating profit fell 12.3% to KRW 8.32 billion.

Quarterly results moved from KRW 498 million in operating profit in 4Q25 to KRW 2.48 billion in 2Q26, then fell to KRW 1.58 billion in 3Q26. Positive factors include the KRW 119 billion backlog at the end of 2025 reported by iM Securities, the expansion of K2 and Cheongung-II exports, and added production capacity.

Negative factors include raw material costs, new headquarters depreciation and R&D headcount growth weighing on quarterly margins, and a debt-to-equity ratio that rose to 124.8% at the end of 2025. Results also depend on the timing of overseas contracts with a limited set of system makers.

The next checkpoints are whether margins recover in the 2026 annual report, expected around mid-March 2027, and whether new orders rise in the second half of 2027.
