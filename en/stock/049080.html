Telecom System Pivot Faces a Loss-Making Structure

02

Business structure

Gigalane is an RF communication components company founded in 2000, which expanded its RF business through a 2012 merger and also runs a semiconductor equipment business. Its operations are split into the RF communications business and the semiconductor process equipment and nano-patterning solutions business.

In 2024, the RF communications unit accounted for 74.78% of revenue and semiconductor equipment for 25.15%, according to a news report citing Financial Supervisory Service data.

On a consolidated basis in the first quarter of 2026, semiconductor equipment sales were KRW 360 million, or 4.61% of total revenue, per a quarterly report news item.

Subsidiaries include C3 Technology in Korea for RF coaxial cables and connectors, GIGALANE CO., LTD. in Vietnam for RF communication equipment and parts, and MGM TECHNOLOGY CO., LTD. in China for semiconductor equipment manufacturing and sales.

The company says it has completed O-RU development and is promoting a total solution line-up spanning RF parts to systems. The April 2026 contract with Parallel Wireless was presented as an example of widening the business from RF parts toward system EMS production.

On production efficiency, a 2024 analysis cited cable utilization of 30.05% and antenna module utilization of 25.8%, pointing to low use of installed capacity. Customers are concentrated in telecom equipment supply, and the size of a single contract weighs heavily on quarterly revenue.

04

Earnings analysis

On a disclosed annual basis, revenue fell from KRW 54.7 billion in 2022 to KRW 44.3 billion in 2023 (down 19.1% year on year), KRW 41.7 billion in 2024 (down 5.9%), and KRW 40.3 billion in 2025 (down 3.3%).

Revenue declined for three consecutive years from 2023 to 2025, with 2022 the largest year and 2025 the smallest in the 2022 to 2025 period.

Operating losses were KRW 7.1 billion in 2022, KRW 11.8 billion in 2023, KRW 7.35 billion in 2024, and KRW 12.5 billion in 2025, a loss-making run of four consecutive years from 2022 to 2025.

Operating margin was -13.0% in 2022, -26.6% in 2023, -17.6% in 2024, and -30.9% in 2025, narrowing in 2024 before widening again in 2025.

The disclosed reasons for the wider 2025 loss were operating costs at the new Chinese subsidiary MGM Technology after its full-scale investment, along with higher investment and R&D costs.

Cost pressure was also heavy: a news analysis citing Financial Supervisory Service data reported cost of sales at 92.4% of revenue in 2022 and 94.8% in 2024, and noted that in 2023 cost of sales alone exceeded revenue.

Net loss attributable to controlling shareholders was KRW 8.32 billion in 2022, KRW 11.2 billion in 2023, KRW 6.01 billion in 2024, and KRW 10.6 billion in 2025, while the 2025 net loss including non-controlling interests was KRW 11.6 billion.

The debt-to-equity ratio rose from 50.5% in 2022 to 119.5% in 2025, with total equity at KRW 43.7 billion at year-end 2025, of which KRW 41.3 billion belonged to controlling shareholders, and total liabilities of KRW 52.2 billion.

Operating cash flow was KRW 1.16 billion in 2022, -KRW 1.79 billion in 2023, KRW 4.18 billion in 2024, and -KRW 8.87 billion in 2025, turning negative again in 2025.

Quarterly results show the third quarter of 2025 at KRW 6.27 billion in revenue with an operating loss of KRW 4.51 billion, followed by fourth-quarter revenue of KRW 14 billion (up 123.5% quarter on quarter) and an operating loss of KRW 3.28 billion.

In the first quarter of 2026, revenue fell 44.2% quarter on quarter to KRW 7.81 billion and the operating loss widened to KRW 4.72 billion.

The second quarter of 2026 brought revenue of KRW 13.7 billion (up 75.1%) and a narrower operating loss of KRW 2.38 billion, while the third quarter of 2026 recorded revenue of KRW 12.2 billion (down 11.0% quarter on quarter, up 94.1% year on year) and an operating loss of KRW 2.48 billion. Net loss attributable to controlling shareholders over the latest four quarters totaled KRW 9.39 billion.

05

Industry analysis

Telecom equipment demand is tied to the pace of 5G infrastructure build-out. According to a 2025 earnings comment, the RF communications business saw revenue decline on delayed 5G infrastructure projects and a higher share of carrier maintenance work, while cost cuts improved gross profit.

Open RAN adoption is cited as a new demand driver, and the company says it has completed O-RU development, with commentary also pointing to steady revenue from the private 5G market.

Open RAN equipment under the supply deal is scheduled for installation by the first quarter of 2027 in markets including the United Kingdom, Mexico, Colombia, and Turkey.

In semiconductor equipment, exposure to China is the key variable: the company set up a Wuxi unit in 2024, but analysts flag significant uncertainty from U.S.-China technology regulation and export controls. The company also says it is collaborating with global firms on nano-imprint technology.

Its positioning rests on three intersecting pillars: RF components, system EMS, and semiconductor equipment. With revenue in the KRW 40 billion range, the size of a single contract can move quarterly revenue materially.

News headlines have repeatedly tied the share price to swings in the telecom equipment and LED equipment themes.

06

Outlook

The Parallel Wireless contract is reported with different amounts across outlets. Ei Today reported a first purchase order of about KRW 10 billion, with about KRW 6.2 billion disclosed that day, while Thinkpool summarized a KRW 2.5 billion supply disclosure.

Ei Today also reported that this order is the first batch under a mass supply contract of KRW 15 billion signed in March 2026. The disclosure, as relayed by Thinkpool, showed a contract period from April 17, 2026 to October 31, 2026.

CEO Kim Hyun-je said the investment in Vietnam production capacity and the system EMS build-out had been proven as results, and that the company would seek additional order momentum in global markets based on verified system production capability.

Equipment installation is set to run through the first quarter of 2027, so the timing of revenue recognition and the quarterly scale are the points to check.

In semiconductor equipment, the key questions are the results of nano-imprint collaboration and whether the Chinese unit's sales recover, starting from a 4.61% share of consolidated revenue in the first quarter of 2026.

The next regular report is the 2026 annual report, where the three-year revenue decline can be checked for a halt and the operating margin can be checked against -30.9% in 2025.

Whether the quarterly revenue levels of KRW 13.7 billion and KRW 12.2 billion in the second and third quarters of 2026 carry into the annual figures is another point to watch.

08

Bull factors

Shift to System EMS Mass Production

The Parallel Wireless supply contract is the first batch of a KRW 15 billion mass supply agreement signed in March 2026. The company is reported to be moving its portfolio from RF parts toward system-level products. Whether the Vietnam production base and system capacity convert into revenue is the key point for this pillar.

Recovery in Quarterly Revenue

Fourth-quarter 2025 revenue reached KRW 14 billion, up 123.5% from the prior quarter, and third-quarter 2026 revenue of KRW 12.2 billion was up 94.1% year on year. The third-quarter 2026 operating loss of KRW 2.48 billion was smaller than the KRW 4.51 billion loss in the third quarter of 2025. The higher quarterly revenue level is a factual early sign of a changed earnings base.

Open RAN and Private 5G Technology Base

The company says it has completed O-RU development and is building a total solution line-up. The 2025 earnings commentary includes expectations for steady revenue from the private 5G market.

Open RAN equipment is scheduled for installation in several overseas markets by the first quarter of 2027, so the technology base has room to convert into revenue.

09

Bear factors

Five Straight Quarters of Operating Losses

Operating losses have run for five consecutive quarters from the third quarter of 2025 through the third quarter of 2026. The 2025 operating margin of -30.9% was the lowest in the 2022 to 2025 period.

Net loss attributable to controlling shareholders over the latest four quarters totaled KRW 9.39 billion, and the operating loss persisted even as quarterly revenue moved.

Cost Structure and R&D Burden

Cost of sales was reported at 92.4% of revenue in 2022 and 94.8% in 2024, a pattern of thin margins. A news analysis reported that the R&D cost ratio widened to 14.31% in the first half of 2025.

Revenue has declined for three straight years while cost burdens persisted, and analysts have warned that this could entrench the loss structure.

Financial Strain and Utilization

The debt-to-equity ratio rose from 50.5% in 2022 to 119.5% in 2025, and operating cash flow was negative KRW 8.87 billion in 2025. A 2024 analysis reported cable utilization of 30.05% and antenna module utilization of 25.8%. With low use of installed capacity and rising leverage, funding flexibility could be constrained.

10

Risk factors

Trade and Regulatory Risk

The semiconductor equipment business, run through the Wuxi unit in China, is exposed to U.S.-China technology regulation and export controls. Semiconductor equipment accounted for 25.15% of revenue in 2024 but only 4.61% on a consolidated basis in the first quarter of 2026. Regulatory changes could slow any recovery in Chinese sales.

Order Concentration and Contract Variability

With revenue in the KRW 40 billion range, a single contract carries a large weight. A KRW 2.5 billion order disclosed in April 2026 represented about 6.19% of recent revenue.

Because outlets report the Parallel Wireless amounts differently, the contract value and recognition timing need to be checked against the original DART filings.

Balance Sheet and Cash Flow

At year-end 2025, total liabilities were KRW 52.2 billion against total equity of KRW 43.7 billion, of which KRW 41.3 billion belonged to controlling shareholders. Operating cash flow swung from KRW 4.18 billion in 2024 to negative KRW 8.87 billion in 2025. If losses continue, reliance on borrowing or new equity could rise.

Overall view

Gigalane runs RF communication components and semiconductor equipment, and is moving into system EMS work with a mass supply contract with Parallel Wireless signed in April 2026.

On a disclosed basis, revenue fell for three consecutive years from 2023 to 2025, and operating losses ran for four consecutive years from 2022 to 2025.

Quarterly revenue rose to KRW 13.7 billion and KRW 12.2 billion in the second and third quarters of 2026, and operating losses narrowed to KRW 2.38 billion and KRW 2.48 billion, but the five-quarter run of operating losses from the third quarter of 2025 remains.

Points cited on the bull side include the shift to system orders, the recovery in quarterly revenue levels, and the Open RAN technology base. On the bear side are cost pressure, a weaker balance sheet, and regulatory uncertainty around the Chinese semiconductor business.

On valuation, the P/E cannot be calculated and the share price sits modestly above book value. The next points to confirm are revenue trends and the operating margin in the 2026 annual report, and the installation progress and quarterly operating loss in the first quarter of 2027.
