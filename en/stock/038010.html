02

Business structure

Jeil Technos is a construction materials and shipbuilding steel processing company centered on deck plates, founded in 1971. The business is split into a construction segment and a shipbuilding segment.

The construction segment produces and supplies deck plate products such as NT-DECK and MULTI-DECK, used as a formwork substitute on steel-frame floors to shorten construction periods. The company also promotes the J·F Deck, a technology-designated product, and the 2Way Deck Slab System, a patented method.

The shipbuilding segment relies on 9% nickel steel and high-manganese steel processing know-how for LNG carrier components, mainly cutting, forming, sub-assembly and surface treatment.

As of Q2 2026, construction products accounted for 47% of sales, shipbuilding processing in Korea for 42%, and processing in Vietnam for 11%. Vietnamese operations run through the subsidiaries JEIL VINA and JEIL VINA NT. Construction demand comes from building sites and semiconductor plant expansions.

Shipbuilding work is built on long-term supply arrangements with large shipbuilders. Competition in construction products has intensified alongside a slowdown in the construction market. Segment sales amounts can be cross-checked against the segment disclosures in the annual business report.

Earnings analysis

Annual results turned down after peaking in 2023. Revenue rose from KRW 215.6 billion in 2022 to KRW 267.6 billion in 2023 (up 24.1% year on year), then fell to KRW 183.9 billion in 2024 (down 31.3%) and KRW 133.6 billion in 2025 (down 27.4%), a two-year decline.

Operating profit peaked at KRW 36.2 billion in 2023 (up 86.6%), then fell to KRW 20.7 billion in 2024 (down 42.9%) and KRW 7.14 billion in 2025 (down 65.5%), also a two-year decline. 2023 was the largest year and 2025 the smallest among 2022 to 2025.

The operating margin moved from 9.0% in 2022 to 13.5% in 2023, 11.2% in 2024 and 5.3% in 2025.

Net profit attributable to controlling shareholders fell from KRW 25.9 billion in 2023 to KRW 9.93 billion in 2025, while net profit including non-controlling interests fell from KRW 26 billion to KRW 10.2 billion over the same period. The company attributed the 2025 decline to a slowdown in the construction business.

Quarterly sales rose from KRW 30.8 billion in the third quarter of 2025 to KRW 45.2 billion in the third quarter of 2026, marking four consecutive quarterly increases.

Operating profit, by contrast, swung to a KRW 270 million loss in the fourth quarter of 2025, recovered to KRW 2.13 billion in the first quarter of 2026 and KRW 2.67 billion in the second, then fell to KRW 1.44 billion in the third quarter.

Net profit attributable to controlling shareholders was a KRW 2.1 billion loss in the third quarter of 2026. Over the last four quarters, that figure totals KRW 7.57 billion. Sales grew while profit shrank, so the causes of the third-quarter margin drop need to be traced in the filings.
