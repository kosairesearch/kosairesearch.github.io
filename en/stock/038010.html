02

Business structure

Jeil Technos, listed on KOSDAQ in 2000, manufactures structural metal products, centered on deck plates for building materials and contract processing of steel for shipbuilding.

In its construction segment, the company produces and supplies deck plate products such as NT-DECK and MULTI-DECK used in building safety and construction efficiency.

In shipbuilding, it handles cutting, forming and sub-assembly, and processes steel for LNG carriers using 9% nickel steel and high-manganese steel technology.

By sales mix as of Q2 2026, the building segment (domestic products) accounted for 47%, shipbuilding (domestic contract processing) for 42%, and contract processing in Vietnam for 11%, according to Butler.

In the first half of 2023 the mix was 70% building and 26% shipbuilding, so the weight of shipbuilding and overseas processing has grown.

On the construction side, the company disclosed a Pyeongtaek Campus deck works contract with Samsung C&T and has delivered similar contracts with that customer over the past three years. Deck plates are also supplied for semiconductor plant expansions, per a June 2026 company report.

KB Securities noted in 2023 that the HI-deck and KEM-deck nuclear-use products supplied about 90% of domestic nuclear plant construction. The company has two consolidated subsidiaries, including one in Vietnam.

04

Earnings analysis

Annual sales rose from KRW 215.6 billion in 2022 to KRW 267.6 billion in 2023 (up 24.1% year on year), then fell 31.3% to KRW 183.9 billion in 2024 and 27.4% to KRW 133.6 billion in 2025, marking two straight years of decline.

Operating profit moved from KRW 19.4 billion in 2022 to KRW 36.2 billion in 2023 (up 86.6%), then to KRW 20.7 billion in 2024 (down 42.9%) and KRW 7.14 billion in 2025 (down 65.5%). 2023 was the largest year and 2025 the smallest across 2022 to 2025.

Profit attributable to controlling shareholders fell from KRW 25.9 billion in 2023 to KRW 20.7 billion in 2024 and KRW 9.93 billion in 2025, while net income including non-controlling interests was KRW 10.2 billion in 2025. The operating margin went from 9.0% in 2022 to 13.5% in 2023, 11.2% in 2024 and 5.3% in 2025.

Because profit fell faster than sales, margin compression amplified the earnings decline. The company attributed the 2025 decline to weak construction activity. The debt ratio fell from 118.7% in 2022 to 25.9% in 2025, with total liabilities of KRW 37 billion and total equity of KRW 142.5 billion at end-2025.

Operating cash flow fell from KRW 45.6 billion in 2023 to KRW 29.5 billion in 2024 and KRW 13.1 billion in 2025. Quarterly, sales rose from KRW 30.8 billion in Q3 2025 to KRW 32.8 billion in Q4 2025 (up 6.8% quarter on quarter), but Q4 2025 posted an operating loss of KRW 270 million.

Q1 2026 returned to operating profit of KRW 2.13 billion, and Q2 2026 reached sales of KRW 40.6 billion, operating profit of KRW 2.67 billion (up 25.5%) and net profit attributable to controlling shareholders of KRW 7.48 billion.

The Q2 bottom line far exceeding operating profit points to a meaningful contribution from non-operating items.

In Q3 2026 sales reached KRW 45.2 billion (up 47.1% year on year), but operating profit was KRW 1.44 billion (down 45.9% quarter on quarter), and the company recorded a net loss attributable to controlling shareholders of KRW 2.1 billion, a swing to a loss from a year earlier.

Net profit attributable to controlling shareholders over the last four quarters totals KRW 757 million, or KRW 7.57 billion.

05

Industry analysis

Demand for deck plates depends heavily on the building market and the construction cycle, which matches the company's explanation for the 2024 and 2025 sales declines.

Despite the construction slowdown, deck plate deliveries for semiconductor plant expansions continue, which is cited as support for the building segment (company report, June 2026).

In 2023, KB Securities attributed the earnings upturn to niche plant expansions such as apartment-type factories, semiconductors and secondary batteries, along with product price increases.

Issues surrounding flat-slab structures also led to views in 2023 that deck plate use could expand if column-based structures spread. KB Securities also mentioned in 2023 that its nuclear-use deck track record could support possible use in SMR deck plates.

In shipbuilding, growth is expected on rising demand for eco-friendly vessels (company report, June 2026). In construction, expanding demand for smart and eco-friendly buildings is expected.

The cycle position can be summarized as a phase in which new demand from semiconductor facilities and eco-friendly vessels partly offsets weaker construction orders. Whether new demand can fully replace the construction decline is a point to verify in quarterly results.

Outlook

The most recent disclosed order is a deck works contract (Lot 2) for the P5 FAB2 construction project at Samsung C&T's Pyeongtaek Campus, signed on November 20, 2026. The contract value is KRW 11.66 billion, equal to 8.7% of 2025 consolidated sales.

The contract period runs from November 23, 2026 to May 31, 2028, with an advance payment of KRW 641 million. Interim and final payments are billed as construction progresses, and the disclosure states that the contract value may change through future negotiations.

In 2023, KB Securities projected record annual results for that year, but sales and profits then declined in 2024 and 2025, so market forecasts and actual results diverged.

The next checkpoints are whether quarterly sales keep their four-quarter growth streak after Q4 2026 and whether the Q3 2026 operating profit decline and net loss prove temporary. Billing progress on the Pyeongtaek interim and final payments will show how quickly the order converts into revenue.

Changes in the Vietnam contract processing share (11% as of Q2 2026) will also indicate the direction of overseas operations.

Bull factors

Quarterly sales up four straight quarters

Quarterly sales rose from KRW 30.8 billion in Q3 2025 to KRW 45.2 billion in Q3 2026. Q3 2026 sales were up 47.1% year on year, and the reported figures show four consecutive quarters of growth from Q4 2025 through Q3 2026.

In the building segment, deck plate deliveries for semiconductor plant expansions are cited as a driver of the recovery. Sales growth despite the construction slowdown points to a partly sustained demand base.

New Pyeongtaek deck works contract

The deck works contract with Samsung C&T for Lot 2 at the Pyeongtaek Campus, disclosed on November 20, 2026, has a value of KRW 11.66 billion, equal to 8.7% of recent sales. The contract period runs from November 23, 2026 to May 31, 2028, a project of more than two years.

The disclosure states the company has performed similar contracts with Samsung C&T in the past three years. The contract is reported as a firm amount rather than a conditional one.

Lower debt burden

The debt-to-equity ratio fell from 118.7% in 2022 to 25.9% in 2025. Total liabilities were KRW 37 billion and total equity KRW 142.5 billion at end-2025, reducing debt relative to equity.

Q2 2026 net profit attributable to controlling shareholders of KRW 7.48 billion exceeded that quarter's operating profit of KRW 2.67 billion. With a lighter balance sheet, the burden of financing for expansion or order execution is lower than before.

Bear factors

Earnings down sharply since the 2023 peak

Operating profit fell from KRW 36.2 billion in 2023 to KRW 7.14 billion in 2025, and the operating margin dropped from 13.5% to 5.3%. The company attributed the sales and profit declines to a weak construction sector. Operating profit and profit attributable to controlling shareholders both declined in 2024 and 2025. Profit fell faster than sales, indicating margin pressure alongside volume weakness.

Unstable quarterly profits

After an operating loss of KRW 270 million in Q4 2025, the company returned to operating profit in Q1 and Q2 2026, but Q3 2026 operating profit fell 48.7% year on year to KRW 1.44 billion. In the same quarter, net loss attributable to controlling shareholders was KRW 2.1 billion, a swing from profit a year earlier.

Profit fell even as sales rose, which points to cost structure or sales mix effects. Wide quarterly swings make the direction of full-year profit harder to read.

Weaker operating cash generation

Operating cash flow kept declining in 2024 and 2025 after the 2023 peak. The 2025 figure is below one-third of the 2023 peak. Cash generation weakened along with profits. Whether cash collection keeps pace with the sales recovery in quarters ahead remains an open question.

Risk factors

Raw material and product prices

Steel price swings and their pass-through to product prices are a key variable for earnings. In 2023, KB Securities pointed to raw material price volatility and end-market trends as the largest risks to results.

Because product price increases supported 2023 results, a blocked pass-through would increase margin pressure. Whether price increases keep pace with rising raw material costs is the point to watch.

Order execution and customer concentration

The disclosure states that the Pyeongtaek deck works contract value may change through future negotiations. Interim and final payments are billed as construction progresses, so schedule delays or changes by the client affect the timing of revenue recognition.

A larger share of orders from a single major client raises dependence on that customer. The advance payment of KRW 641 million indicates the size of early-stage cash inflow.

Governance and float structure

The largest shareholder group, comprising a principal shareholder and six others, holds 44.92%, and treasury shares account for 7.60%. The free float ratio is 47.48%, so roughly half the shares are tradable by general holders.

A temporary shareholders' meeting result was disclosed in July 2026, and any governance change could affect the float and financing approach. A limited free float can influence share price volatility and trading liquidity.

Overall view

Quarterly sales rose for four straight quarters into 2026, but in Q3 2026 operating profit fell to KRW 1.44 billion and the company recorded a net loss attributable to controlling shareholders of KRW 2.1 billion.

The debt ratio fell to 25.9%, lowering borrowing pressure, but operating cash flow dropped from KRW 45.6 billion in 2023 to KRW 13.1 billion in 2025. The Pyeongtaek deck works contract of KRW 11.66 billion, disclosed in November 2026 and running through May 31, 2028, supports the revenue outlook.

On the other side, the largest shareholder group holds 44.92% with a 47.48% free float, and raw material and product price swings remain variables for margins. The share price trades below one times book value, but until the pace of earnings recovery is confirmed, the reading of multiples remains mixed. The next checkpoints are the operating margin in the 2026 annual report and Q1 2027 quarterly results.
