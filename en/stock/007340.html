Machine Tools Anchor DN Automotive's Earnings Mix

02

Business structure

DN Automotive runs three lines: machine tools, automotive parts, and batteries with tubes. Alphasquare's recent sales mix shows machine tools at 57.49%, automotive parts at 34.96%, and automotive batteries at 7.55%. The machine tool arm is DN Solutions, formerly Doosan Machine Tools.

A company earnings comment dated 31 August 2026 says its subsidiary acquired 100% of Germany's Heller, which the company describes as strengthening the unit's global competitiveness. Automotive parts split into anti-vibration components and batteries.

Anti-vibration parts supply global automakers including General Motors and Stellantis. Battery deliveries to North American customers expanded, lifting battery sales to KRW 3.73 billion in 2024, up 35.1%, which equaled 28.2% of parts sales.

The tube business came into the group through a 2024 merger with a tire and tube maker. Key customers include Hyundai Motor and KIA at home, plus overseas automakers in North America, Europe and China. The group runs production and sales bases in 11 countries and has 24 consolidated subsidiaries including DN Solutions.

In batteries, demand for automotive batteries rose, but added supply has intensified competition. Machine tools serve precision machining demand from automotive, aerospace and IT industries. In 2024, machine tools accounted for 61% of revenue and 78% of operating profit.

04

Earnings analysis

On filed figures, revenue went from KRW 3.16 trillion in 2022 to KRW 3.27 trillion in 2023 (up 3.6% year on year), KRW 3.43 trillion in 2024 (up 5.1%) and KRW 3.68 trillion in 2025 (up 7.0%). Revenue has risen three years in a row from 2023 through 2025, and 2025 was the largest year of 2022 to 2025.

The jump from the pre-2022 scale is explained by the consolidation of the machine tool business after the DN Solutions acquisition, according to Dealsite's reporting.

Operating profit rose from KRW 421.5 billion in 2022 to KRW 490.4 billion in 2023 (up 16.3%), then KRW 522.9 billion in 2024 (up 6.6%) and KRW 527.9 billion in 2025 (up 1.0%), so growth narrowed quickly. The operating margin climbed from 13.4% in 2022 to 15.2% in 2024 before easing to 14.4% in 2025.

Net income attributable to controlling shareholders went from KRW 180.9 billion in 2022 to KRW 274.8 billion in 2023 (up 51.9%), peaked at KRW 284.9 billion in 2024 (up 3.7%), then slipped to KRW 281.6 billion in 2025 (down 1.2%).

Total net income was KRW 325 billion in 2025, above the controlling-shareholder figure, with the gap belonging to non-controlling interests. Operating cash flow peaked at KRW 356.3 billion in 2023, then came in at KRW 197.1 billion in 2024 and KRW 259.3 billion in 2025.

The debt ratio fell from 305.8% in 2022 to 115.4% in 2025, and total liabilities at the end of 2025 were KRW 2.83 trillion. Quarterly, Q2 2026 marked the peak with revenue of KRW 1.25 trillion (up 22.8% from the prior quarter) and operating profit of KRW 205.4 billion (up 42.1%).

Q3 2026 revenue was KRW 1.16 trillion, down 7.2% from the prior quarter but up 26.1% from a year earlier. Q3 2026 operating profit was KRW 159.5 billion (down 22.4% from the prior quarter), and net income attributable to controlling shareholders was KRW 62.3 billion (down 55.7%).

Over the last four quarters, net income attributable to controlling shareholders totaled KRW 383.2 billion. First-half 2026 figures showed revenue up 26.8%, operating profit up 35.0% and net income up 87.8% on a consolidated basis, according to a company earnings comment. One-off items by quarter can be traced in the notes to the business report.

05

Industry analysis

The machine tool business ties into the smart manufacturing trend and is sensitive to capital spending cycles. According to a Samsung Securities field-visit note, new machine tool orders rose from a low of about KRW 200 billion in 2023 to about KRW 2.1 trillion in 2024 and about KRW 2.3 trillion in 2025.

Q1 2026 orders were KRW 653.1 billion, up 13.5% from a year earlier, and KRW 778.2 billion including Heller orders. These order figures come from a brokerage tally and are not filed results. Automotive batteries are described as having a strong aftermarket character, which tends to smooth out swings in vehicle output.

Added supply has, however, intensified competition, leaving pricing pressure in place. A 2025 press report said anti-vibration parts kept order backlogs intact on steady demand centered in the United States.

Within the auto parts sector, Alphasquare places the 2025 full-year operating margin of 14.36% second among 137 companies, while the sector average margin sits slightly below zero. Peers such as Hyundai Mobis, Hanon Systems and Hankook Tire & Technology operate in the same supply chain. A 2025 report also described new product development aimed at the shift toward electric vehicles.

Outlook

The company has not issued formal guidance, according to a Samsung Securities field-visit note from July 2026. The same note said that, factoring in rising new machine tool orders and the Heller acquisition, 2026 revenue could grow more than 20% from the prior year, with profitability broadly similar to last year.

The FnGuide consensus as of July 2026 put 2026 revenue at around KRW 4.4 trillion (up 20.8%) and operating profit at KRW 623.0 billion (up 18%). First-half 2026 results, up 26.8% in revenue and 35.0% in operating profit, point in the same direction.

Operating profit fell from the prior quarter in Q3, so the Q4 trend will be central to the full-year picture. Capacity expansion for lead-acid batteries carries KRW 440 billion of investment through 2030, and the note said there is no other capex beyond that.

A 2025 press report, however, described a KRW 62 billion battery line expansion in Ulsan and a KRW 397.6 billion new plant in Busan. The two accounts differ on investment scale, so the capex execution detail in the business report should be checked against both.

The pace of Heller's profit improvement should appear in the segment commentary of the annual report. The company holds 11.53% in treasury shares, and the timing of their cancellation bears on shareholder returns.

Bull factors

Machine tool orders recovering

A Samsung Securities field-visit note traced new machine tool orders from a low of about KRW 200 billion in 2023 to about KRW 2.3 trillion in 2025. Q1 2026 orders were KRW 653.1 billion, up 13.5% from a year earlier, and KRW 778.2 billion including Heller.

If orders convert into sales, they would support growth in a structure where machine tools carry the largest revenue share.

Balance sheet normalization

The debt ratio fell from 305.8% in 2022 to 115.4% in 2025. Operating cash flow rose from KRW 153 billion in 2022 to KRW 259.3 billion in 2025. The operating margin also climbed from 13.4% in 2022 to 15.2% in 2024, widening the profit base.

Treasury shares and dividend trend

A Samsung Securities note said the company holds 11.53% in treasury shares and cited a mandatory cancellation deadline of within September 2027 for shares held by the government. Dividends have risen since 2022. If the cancellation is carried out, the scale of shareholder returns will become visible in filings.

Bear factors

Concentration in machine tools and slowing profit

In 2024 machine tools accounted for 61% of revenue and 78% of operating profit. When profit is concentrated in one unit, swings in orders or utilization drive group results. Operating profit grew only 1.0% in 2025, and net income attributable to controlling shareholders fell 1.2%.

High quarterly volatility

Operating profit peaked at KRW 205.4 billion in Q2 2026, then fell to KRW 159.5 billion in Q3, down 22.4% from the prior quarter. Net income attributable to controlling shareholders dropped from KRW 140.6 billion to KRW 62.3 billion, down 55.7%. Quarterly swings are large, so the figures need to be read alongside the full-year trend.

Battery competition and margin pressure

The operating margin eased to 14.4% in 2025 from 15.2% in 2024. Total liabilities of KRW 2.83 trillion at the end of 2025 remain large, so battery investment and borrowing costs together are a weak point to watch.

Risk factors

Trade environment

Anti-vibration and battery units rely on demand centered in the United States, so changes in trade conditions could feed into results. A 2025 report noted that anti-vibration parts kept backlogs intact on steady US-centered demand.

As overseas customers grow in weight, the effect of tariffs and exchange rates on margins needs quarterly tracking.

Uncertainty over acquisition payoff

The Heller acquisition lifts revenue, but whether its profit contribution grows in step can only be confirmed through quarterly filings. The machine tool unit's profitability after the deal should be checked against 2026 quarterly results and the segment commentary in the business report. If the payoff falls short, it could weigh on the group's overall operating margin.

Subsidiary listing and governance

A March 2025 Hana Securities report said that under a planned listing of subsidiary DN Solutions, DN Automotive's stake would fall from 84.8% to 69.4%. Whether the listing happens and how the stake changes must be confirmed through later filings.

In a holding company structure, changes in subsidiary ownership also alter how consolidated profit is attributed to shareholders.

Overall view

DN Automotive is an auto parts maker whose machine tool subsidiary now carries a larger share of the group. Revenue has risen three years in a row since 2022, and 2025 operating profit of KRW 527.9 billion was the largest of 2022 to 2025.

Growth in operating profit narrowed after 2024, though, and controlling-shareholder net income peaked in 2024. The debt ratio fell from 305.8% to 115.4%, which improved the balance sheet.

Quarterly results peaked in Q2 2026 and fell in Q3, and net income attributable to controlling shareholders over the last four quarters totaled KRW 383.2 billion. Rising machine tool orders and the Heller acquisition are cited as support for 2026 sales growth.

Battery competition, reliance on the US market and quarterly volatility are the main weak points. With no formal guidance, the 2026 full-year results and the business report due around March 2027 are the next checkpoints.

Weighing strengths and weaknesses equally and tracking the filing trail is the current research focus for this stock.
