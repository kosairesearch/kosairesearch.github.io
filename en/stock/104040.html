DSM: Auto-Parts Growth Alongside Leisure Investment

02

Business structure

DSM was founded in 2000 to make auto parts and molds and listed on KOSDAQ in 2009. After merging with the next-generation leisure platform developer Monolith in 2025, it changed its name and reorganized into a mobility division and a Monolith division.

The mobility division supplies door locks and seat recliners to first-tier suppliers of Hyundai Motor and KIA, and generated KRW 33.431 billion of cumulative revenue through the third quarter of 2026, or 71.2% of the total.

That division's cumulative sales rose 50.3% year on year, which the company attributed to higher orders for major auto parts. The leisure division runs the 9.81 Park in Jeju, with cumulative revenue of KRW 13.519 billion through the third quarter of 2026, up 10% year on year.

Foreign visitors to the Jeju site rose 83% year on year, and their share of total visitors increased from 8.5% to 15.1%. According to the company, on-site sales at the Jeju site (tickets, retail and F&B combined) hit a monthly record in August 2026 since the park opened in 2020.

On the mobility side, DSM is supplying a swivel seat mechanism for the Genesis GV90, co-developed over about four years with a Hyundai Motor Group seating partner, with mass production set for Q4 2026.

The auto-parts business depends on keeping first-tier supplier positions with automakers, while the leisure business depends on visitor demand tied to its Jeju and Incheon Airport sites.

The company ended a co-branded KBO program on November 12, 2026, and said it confirmed a model for widening content and visitor demand without large facility investment.

04

Earnings analysis

On the disclosed basis, annual revenue was KRW 27.3 billion in 2022, KRW 29.4 billion in 2023 (up 7.9% year on year), KRW 36.7 billion in 2024 (up 24.7%) and KRW 28.5 billion in 2025 (down 22.4%). Among 2022–2025, 2024 was the largest year and 2022 the smallest.

Operating results were an operating loss of KRW 2.32 billion in 2022 and KRW 2.02 billion in 2023, a loss that narrowed but persisted, before turning to operating profit of KRW 749 million in 2024.

In 2025 the company returned to an operating loss of KRW 7.93 billion, and the operating margin fell from 2.0% in 2024 to -27.8%.

Net income attributable to controlling shareholders was KRW 2.02 billion in 2025, up 164.0%, moving in the opposite direction from the operating result; total net income for the year was the same KRW 2.02 billion.

The company has said merger accounting effects are embedded in year-on-year comparisons, so changes in reported figures should be read with that in mind. Operating cash flow went from KRW 582 million in 2024 to -KRW 4.01 billion in 2025.

The debt ratio rose from 37.6% in 2024 to 102.0% in 2025, with total liabilities of KRW 116.7 billion exceeding total equity of KRW 114.4 billion at year-end 2025. On a quarterly basis, Q4 2025 revenue was KRW 13.5 billion with an operating loss of KRW 2.77 billion.

Q1 2026 revenue fell 7.5% to KRW 12.5 billion and the operating loss widened to KRW 3.33 billion. Q2 2026 revenue rose 31.4% to KRW 16.4 billion and the operating loss narrowed to KRW 154 million.

Q3 2026 revenue reached KRW 18 billion, up 9.4%, with an operating loss of KRW 795 million, and revenue has risen for two consecutive quarters since Q1 2026. Net losses attributable to controlling shareholders over the last four quarters totaled KRW 6.97 billion.

The company's preliminary cumulative release on November 17, 2026 gave revenue of KRW 46.949 billion, up 35.9% excluding merger accounting effects, and an operating loss of KRW 4.275 billion, narrowed 18.5%.

05

Industry analysis

DSM's core end market is the vehicle market centered on Hyundai Motor and KIA. As a first-tier supplier to both automakers, the company supplies door locks and seat recliners, and this division accounted for 71.2% of cumulative revenue through the third quarter of 2026.

Auto-parts volumes move with vehicle production and model-level orders. On the automaker side, rising demand for parts in electrified models lines up with the direction of the company's new projects.

The Genesis GV90 is an electrified flagship SUV, and the company says it began mass-producing the swivel seat module for this model from Q4. The company describes its expansion into electrified mobility parts as building on its existing mold and fine-blanking technology.

Listed peers in the same sector include Hyundai Mobis, HL Mando, Sungwoo Hitech and Hankook Tire & Technology, and the revenue scale gap with large players such as Hyundai Mobis is wide. Share and order-win comparisons remain qualitative. In leisure, the sector outlook depends on foreign demand for tourism in Jeju.

Foreign visitors to the 9.81 Park in Jeju rose 83% year on year, and they made up 15.1% of total visitors. This points to a model that leans on tourism demand more than on local leisure demand.

06

Outlook

The company set a 2026 revenue target of KRW 600 billion and said that mass-production sales of the GV90 swivel seat module from Q4 would push results past that target. This outlook is management's view, and full-year revenue will be fixed in the 2026 annual report.

The key leisure milestone is the 9.81 Incheon Airport site, targeted to open in spring 2027. Press reports say the project involves about KRW 1.3 hundred billion, and the company has presented a first-year visitor target of 1 million and annual revenue potential of KRW 80 billion.

Construction progress has been reported differently, with one report near 90% and another citing 78%. A Chinese licensing business is also in preparation.

On the mobility side, the company said in its August 2026 first-half results that it is preparing mass supply of swivel seat parts for a Hyundai Motor premium electric vehicle and a supercapacitor manufacturing business.

In May 2026, a domestic research house said the company's shift toward a physical-AI platform business is under way after the Monolith merger.

On the capital structure, the company acquired KRW 1.06 billion worth of the second convertible bond tranche before maturity, for a face value of KRW 1 billion, according to a corrected filing dated November 9, 2026.

Bull factors

Mobility sales expansion

The company attributes the gain to higher orders for door locks and seat recliners through first-tier supply to Hyundai Motor and KIA. Mass-production sales of the GV90 swivel seat module are set to be added from Q4.

Foreign demand for the leisure platform

Foreign visitors to the 9.81 Park in Jeju rose 83% year on year, lifting their share of total visitors to 15.1%. The company said on-site sales in August 2026 were the highest monthly figure since the park opened in 2020.

A domestic research house said in a May 2026 report that the company's transition toward a platform business is under way.

A narrowing loss trend

The operating loss in Q2 2026 narrowed to KRW 154 million from the prior quarter, and Q3 2026 revenue rose for a second straight quarter to KRW 18 billion. The cumulative operating loss through Q3 2026 was KRW 4.275 billion, a loss that narrowed 18.5% year on year. The company credits growth in existing businesses and operating efficiency for the smaller loss.

Losses have continued for four consecutive quarters from Q4 2025 through Q3 2026, so a return to quarterly profit remains to be shown.

Balance sheet pressure

The debt ratio rose from 37.6% in 2024 to 102.0% in 2025, with total liabilities of KRW 116.7 billion above total equity of KRW 114.4 billion at year-end 2025. Operating cash flow turned to an outflow of -KRW 4.01 billion in 2025. Revenue for 2025 was KRW 28.5 billion, down 22.4% year on year.

New investment and timing risk

The 9.81 Incheon Airport project involves about KRW 1.3 hundred billion, with a spring 2027 opening targeted. Reported construction progress differs, with one report near 90% and another at 78%, so the timing can only be confirmed at opening. Development costs for new projects continue, and losses could persist in the meantime.

Risk factors

Customer and product concentration

Auto parts account for 71.2% of cumulative revenue, so results depend on volumes from first-tier supply to Hyundai Motor and KIA. Lower automaker output or weaker orders for specific models could increase revenue swings. Whether the GV90 seat module adds revenue on the planned mass-production schedule is the next item to confirm.

New-project spending and timing

The 9.81 Incheon Airport project requires about KRW 1.3 hundred billion, with a spring 2027 opening targeted. Differing construction reports and any delay could push back the point at which the leisure division returns to profit.

The Chinese licensing and supercapacitor businesses are still in preparation, so the timing of results is uncertain.

Financial and capital structure

The debt ratio rose to 102.0% in 2025, with total liabilities exceeding total equity. Operating cash flow was -KRW 4.01 billion in 2025.

The company acquired KRW 1 billion face value of its second convertible bond tranche before maturity, and the largest shareholder group holds 33.53% as of October 19, 2026, with a free float ratio of 65.89%.

Overall view

DSM is running auto-parts sales growth and leisure platform investment at the same time. Cumulative revenue through the third quarter of 2026 was KRW 46.949 billion, up 35.9% excluding merger accounting effects, and the cumulative operating loss narrowed 18.5% to KRW 4.275 billion.

However, after swinging to an operating loss of KRW 7.93 billion in 2025, net losses attributable to controlling shareholders totaled KRW 6.97 billion over the last four quarters, and the debt ratio rose to 102.0%.

Bullish factors include order growth in mobility, the expected addition of GV90 mass-production sales from Q4, and rising foreign demand at the 9.81 Park. Bearish factors include consecutive losses, balance sheet pressure, and uncertainty over the scale of spending and timing at the Incheon Airport project.

The weight of these factors will be shown in full-year results in the 2026 annual report and the outcome of the spring 2027 opening. Valuation sits below book value, but net losses over the last four quarters mean no earnings-based PER is calculated.
