02

Business structure

Lemon Healthcare operates a real-time, bidirectional medical-data relay platform connecting hospitals, patients and insurers. The business is split into three segments. The smart-hospital platform LDB-H (LemonCare, LemonCare Plus and LemonTalkTalk) earns revenue from system installation and subscriptions.

The medical-data ecosystem relay platform LDB-E (the Claim Shin app, Silson24 and LemonPharmLink) earns data brokerage fees, and press reports describe it as the largest revenue segment. LDB-D (Health Shin) is structured around personalized health-data relay and sales of processed data.

In 2024, revenue from building the Silson24 claims system was KRW 12.2 billion of total revenue of KRW 14.9 billion, and the largest single customer accounted for 81.8% of sales that year. Public-sector and medical-institution customers accounted for 83.6% of revenue in 2024 and 62.4% in 2025.

As of March 2026, the company said it had contracts with 38 of Korea's 47 tertiary hospitals, a share of 80.8%. The company said the Claim Shin app had 1.9 million cumulative subscribers and 10 million cumulative claims.

On competition, the company points to its standardized API layer for hospital data and live-operation references as advantages. The company also acknowledges that similar smart-hospital and medical-data platform operators could emerge as competitors.

Overseas, it began commercial service of LemonCare at Vietnam's 22-12 Hospital, its first overseas commercial deployment.

03

Recent trends

Based on first-half 2026 cumulative DART filings, revenue was KRW 2.93 billion, down 35.3% from the same period last year. Over the same period, the operating loss of KRW 2.13 billion turned the company into deficit, and the net loss of KRW 2.15 billion also swung into deficit versus the prior-year period.

On an annual basis, 2024 revenue of KRW 14.9 billion was the first year above KRW 10 billion, and the company turned profitable. Press reports put 2025 revenue at KRW 15.9 billion or KRW 16.0 billion, and cited a 2025 operating loss of KRW 0.6 billion.

First-quarter 2026 revenue was KRW 2.6 billion, slightly lower year on year, with an operating loss reported at KRW 4.0 billion. One outlet reported the same quarter's operating loss at KRW 1.44 billion, so figures vary across reports. Balance-sheet quality is also a key issue.

Total equity at the end of 2024 was negative KRW 31.2 billion, a full capital impairment, but conversion of redeemable preferred shares into common shares in 2025 lifted equity to KRW 2.2 billion. At the end of the first quarter of 2026, total equity fell back to KRW 0.7 billion and the impairment ratio rose to 87.7%.

The stock listed on KOSDAQ on July 6, 2026 at an offering price of KRW 10,000, and the current price at the time of this report is KRW 2,515.

04

Outlook

The company targets 2026 revenue of KRW 24.2 billion and operating profit of KRW 6.6 billion, although some reports cite KRW 24.1 billion and KRW 6.5 billion. Given first-half cumulative revenue of KRW 2.93 billion, reaching the target depends heavily on revenue recognized in the second half.

The company has said that revenue in this industry tends to concentrate in the fourth quarter. The targets rest on higher awards of public-sector system projects, stable maintenance revenue from Silson24, and higher advertising and data-sales revenue from the Health Shin service launch.

Press reports also cite 2027 targets of KRW 29.6 billion in revenue and KRW 11.3 billion in operating profit. Over the medium term, the company is building a marketplace for medical-AI training data.

From 2027, it plans to roll out medical-data distribution and trading services and data-analysis services for insurers and pharmaceutical companies in sequence.

Overseas, after the Vietnam rollout, it plans to open two more local hospitals within the year, bringing the total to three LemonCare sites, and targets up to ten hospitals in Vietnam over the medium term.

Press reports describe use of offering proceeds as KRW 3.2 billion for facilities, KRW 9.7 billion for operations and KRW 1.8 billion for debt repayment. The trajectory of cumulative third-quarter 2026 results and the impairment ratio is the next point to track.

05

Bull factors

API-based expansion anchored in tertiary hospitals

The company says that adding tertiary-hospital contracts increases medical-data API calls, which in turn lifts data transfer volumes and platform traffic in a recurring revenue structure. It reported a 100% response rate to the standard specification for API responses and reuse of private API specifications above 84%.

Through LemonTalkTalk and strategic partnerships with 13 electronic medical record companies, it is extending its customer base to general hospitals and small clinics. The Claim Shin app's 1.9 million cumulative subscribers and 10 million cumulative claims are cited as indicators of its consumer reach.

National-scale Silson24 reference and technology ratings

The company carried out the Silson24 project, the insurance industry's claims-digitization system built with the Insurance Development Institute, and gained experience running national-scale relay infrastructure.

During its technology-based listing, it received an A grade in technology evaluations from two external agencies. The agencies viewed favorably that the LDB platform addresses interoperability between closed hospital information systems. Commercial operation in real-use environments at more than 130 large hospitals was also rated highly.

Subscription renewals and first overseas commercial launch

Press reports put the subscription renewal rate at about 95% as of the first quarter of 2026. On November 3, 2026, the company held a local launch event at Vietnam's 22-12 Hospital, and it highlights that hospitals can adopt the app without replacing their existing hospital information systems.

Working with Megazone Cloud, it built a private-cloud environment and LDB relay infrastructure in a local data center. The company says its operating experience at 179 domestic medical institutions reduces development burden for overseas rollouts.

06

Bear factors

First-half revenue decline and swing to losses

First-half 2026 cumulative revenue was KRW 2.93 billion, down 35.3% from the same period last year. The operating loss of KRW 2.13 billion and the net loss of KRW 2.15 billion both swung the company into deficit versus the prior-year period.

One outlet reported that the company's ability to sustain profitability after its 2024 turn to profit has not yet been proven. Silson24 project revenue fell to KRW 5.4 billion in 2025, which is cited as a weakness in revenue continuity.

Renewed capital impairment and delisting-risk concerns

Total equity at the end of the first quarter of 2026 fell back to KRW 0.7 billion, and the impairment ratio rose to 87.7%. In its prospectus, the company said that if technology commercialization falls short or additional funding becomes difficult, the balance sheet could deteriorate again.

Concerns have been raised that continued partial impairment could lead to designation as a managed issue after listing. At the end of 2024, a full impairment reflected the treatment of redeemable preferred shares as debt.

Concentration in public and project-based revenue

In 2024, the largest single customer accounted for 81.8% of sales, so one Silson24 project largely determined results that year. Public-sector and medical-institution customers made up 83.6% of revenue in 2024 and 62.4% in 2025, so results track government policy and budget execution schedules.

First-quarter 2026 revenue declined slightly year on year. Quarterly volatility could widen if the timing of public-sector system awards shifts.

07

Risk factors

Policy & budget

The company's heavy reliance on public-sector system builds and medical-institution customers makes results sensitive to government policy and budget execution timing. Public and medical-institution customers accounted for 62.4% of revenue in 2025.

The company has said its 2026 targets require more public-sector system awards and stable Silson24 maintenance revenue. Changes in the regulatory environment for medical-data use could also delay the timing of its data-sales business.

Supply & trading

In the offering, institutional lock-up commitments covered 6.91% of applied shares, well below the 40% threshold used for preferential allocation. Immediately after listing, tradable shares were reported at 33.19% of the total. The CEO's post-offering stake was expected at 27.42%. The current price of KRW 2,515 is far below the KRW 10,000 offering price.

Financial & regulatory

With partial capital impairment continuing, the possibility of designation as a managed issue after listing is cited as a concern. The medical-data relay and sales businesses depend on strengthening regulatory compliance systems, which the company has identified as a precondition for expansion.

The company previously sought a listing through a growth-track route in December 2020 and withdrew in July 2021, when profitability issues were reported to be a factor.

08

Overall view

Lemon Healthcare has a clear strength in having secured medical-data relay infrastructure centered on tertiary hospitals. Its Claim Shin and Silson24 experience and the A grade in technology evaluations are cited as evidence of its business foundation.

On the other hand, first-half 2026 cumulative DART results showed lower revenue and a swing into operating and net losses. The 2026 targets of KRW 24.2 billion in revenue and KRW 6.6 billion in operating profit depend heavily on second-half revenue recognition.

The renewed rise in the capital impairment ratio to 87.7% is a key financial variable. Concentration in public-sector and medical-institution revenue and in individual projects is cited as a source of earnings volatility.

The current price of KRW 2,515 against the KRW 10,000 offering price is one indicator of how the market is weighing the durability of the return to profit. This report is a qualitative summary for information purposes only.

Because the capital impairment ratio may shift with the scale of second-half revenue recognition, whether these two items move together in the cumulative third-quarter filing is the variable.
