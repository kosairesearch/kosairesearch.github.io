02

Business structure

Lemon Healthcare operates a relay platform that connects participants in the medical-data ecosystem, including hospitals, patients, insurers and drugmakers. Its core technology is LDB, which pulls in medical data held in differing hospital systems in real time.

The business is organized into a smart-hospital platform (LDB-H), a medical-data ecosystem relay platform (LDB-E) and a personalized health-data relay platform for mobile healthcare (LDB-D). A representative service is its indemnity-insurance claims app. Its experience building the national Silson24 claims system is also cited as support for its market position.

The share of revenue from public-sector and medical institutions was 83.6% in 2024 and 62.4% in 2025. That weight on public system-building projects means revenue can swing with the timing of contract awards.

Newstop reported that the company is regarded as a leader in the medical-data platform market, backed by an 80% share of tertiary general hospitals. The same report put the renewal rate for subscription services at around 95%.

The company has said it recognizes that competition may emerge in smart-hospital platforms, medical-data relay and AI-based healthcare.

The exchange classifies it under bio and pharmaceuticals, but its actual business is closer to software and IT services, such as a mobile healthcare platform and smart-hospital solutions. Its earnings drivers therefore differ from those of drug manufacturers such as Samsung Biologics and Celltrion.

03

Recent trends

Based on filed results, first-half 2026 cumulative revenue was KRW 2.93 billion, down 35.3% from a year earlier. The operating loss was KRW 2.13 billion and the net loss was KRW 2.15 billion, both swings to a loss from the prior-year period.

First-quarter 2026 revenue was KRW 2.6 billion, with an operating loss of KRW 1.4 billion. Comparing first-quarter revenue with the half-year total suggests that second-quarter revenue was very small. Full-year 2025 revenue was KRW 15.9 billion, with an operating loss of KRW 600 million.

The company's pre-IPO estimates for 2026 were revenue of KRW 24.2 billion and operating profit of KRW 6.6 billion. On first-half figures alone, reaching the full-year estimates would require a sharp rise in second-half revenue and profit.

The offer price was set at KRW 10,000, the top of the range, and the shares listed on July 6, 2026. The close on July 7, the next trading day, was KRW 12,220. The shares later traded well below the offer price. On October 21 the stock closed at the daily limit of KRW 3,825, up KRW 880 (29.88%) from the prior close.

No disclosure or company news explaining the move was identified then either. On October 27 the close was KRW 4,050 (+12.81%), with a market capitalization of KRW 54.4 billion and trading value of KRW 66.7 billion. Jaekyung Ilbo attributed the latest jump to a possible technical rebound or sector price co-movement.

04

Outlook

The first item to watch is second-half 2026 earnings. With a cumulative first-half operating loss of KRW 2.13 billion already reported, the odds of meeting the full-year operating-profit estimate depend on how quickly revenue is recognized in the second half.

The company's estimate assumed more public system-building orders, stable Silson24 maintenance revenue and growing advertising and data-sales revenue from its health-data service.

Medical-data projects must meet hospital-network integration, security and privacy requirements, so a lag can open between contract signing and revenue recognition.

The second item is how the IPO proceeds are spent. At the bottom of the price band, the company earmarked KRW 3.2 billion for facilities, KRW 9.7 billion for operations and KRW 1.8 billion for debt repayment.

The facilities portion is for data-processing infrastructure for medical AI training, upgrades to LDB-based services and cloud capacity. The third item is the capital structure.

In its prospectus the company said its finances could deteriorate again if commercialization falls short or further funding becomes hard to raise. The effect of the first-half net loss of KRW 2.15 billion on equity should be checked against the balance sheet in the half-year report.

The shares are moving sharply without an identified catalyst, so volatility around earnings and filings may persist.

Bull factors

Early position in data-connection infrastructure

Newstop reported that the company is seen as a leader in medical-data platforms, citing an 80% share of tertiary general hospitals and Silson24 experience. The subscription renewal rate was put at around 95%.

Connecting to hospital systems takes time and meets security requirements, so an installed base of connected institutions is a barrier for new entrants. Whether that base converts into profit still needs separate confirmation.

Growth record and profit target

Revenue rose from KRW 6.1 billion in 2023 to KRW 14.9 billion in 2024 and KRW 16.0 billion in 2025. The company expected real earnings growth from expanding core services, stronger public system-building work and advertising revenue from its health-data service.

Operating results also turned to a profit of KRW 127 million in 2024. Room to improve the operating margin depends on order timing and cost control.

IPO proceeds and AI data investment

The offering was sized at KRW 15 billion to KRW 20 billion on the price band. Planned uses include working capital, debt repayment and building data-processing infrastructure for medical AI training. The inflow eases financial pressure and gives the company room to prepare new data-based services. Actual spending and results must still be confirmed in later filings.

Bear factors

First-half loss and revenue decline

First-half 2026 cumulative revenue was KRW 2.93 billion, down 35.3% year on year. The operating loss of KRW 2.13 billion and the net loss of KRW 2.15 billion are both swings to a loss. On first-half results, the gap to the company's full-year operating-profit estimate is wide. The estimate also rests on several assumptions, such as public project orders, which adds to the uncertainty.

Capital impairment and designation risk

Total equity fell to KRW 700 million at the end of the first quarter of 2026, and the capital impairment ratio rose to 87.7%. Because it remained partially impaired, concerns were raised that the company could be designated an administrative issue after listing.

How the first-half net loss of KRW 2.15 billion affected equity needs to be checked in the half-year report. If losses continue, financial stability could be tested again.

Public-sector reliance and revenue volatility

The share of revenue from public-sector and medical institutions was 83.6% in 2024 and 62.4% in 2025. Project-type revenue varies widely by quarter depending on order and acceptance timing. A lag can arise between contract signing and revenue recognition. The first-half 2026 revenue decline can be read as an example of this structural volatility.

Medical-data businesses must meet hospital-network integration, security and privacy requirements. Changes in regulation or public-project policy could affect the timing of orders and revenue recognition. The company itself recognizes that competition may emerge in smart-hospital platforms and medical-data relay.

Financial and trading

In its prospectus the company said its finances could deteriorate again if losses persist. According to Jaekyung Ilbo, there were no official announcements of new contracts, ownership changes or earnings outlooks around the surge. A price that moves without an identified catalyst could also reverse at a similar speed. Daily swings are very large, as the October 21 limit-up and the October 27 jump show.

08

Overall view

Lemon Healthcare is a medical-data relay platform company with a base of connected tertiary hospitals and experience building Silson24. On filed results, first-half 2026 cumulative revenue fell 35.3% to KRW 2.93 billion, and the company swung to an operating loss of KRW 2.13 billion and a net loss of KRW 2.15 billion.

The gap to the company's pre-IPO estimate of an operating profit for 2026 is wide on first-half results, so second-half orders and revenue recognition are the key variables.

With the capital impairment ratio at 87.7% at the end of the first quarter of 2026, the balance sheet and the equity position in the half-year report also need to be checked.

The shares moved sharply at KRW 4,050, well below the KRW 10,000 offer price, without an identified catalyst, and trading value exceeded market capitalization, pointing to heavy short-term volatility.

The bullish side cites infrastructure positioning and IPO proceeds, while the bearish side cites continued losses, capital impairment and volatile public-sector revenue. This report is for information only and leaves the judgment to the reader.
