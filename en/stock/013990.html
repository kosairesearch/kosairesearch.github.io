02

Business structure

AGABANG&COMPANY was founded in 1979 as Korea's first apparel and goods company specializing in infants and young children and listed on KOSDAQ in 2002. Its core is children's apparel under the Agabang brand and other labels, supplemented by an infant skincare brand and a subsidiary that makes play mats and furniture.

Most revenue comes from domestic children's apparel sales. Overseas sales fell from KRW 5.93 billion in 2023 to KRW 5.12 billion in 2024, and reached KRW 2.915 billion in the first half of 2025, or 3.3% of revenue.

The company runs sales networks through units in Yantai, Beijing and the United States, yet the overseas business posted an operating loss of KRW 1.425 billion in the first half of 2025.

Its subsidiaries include a logistics unit, the mat and furniture unit and a management consulting unit, but combined subsidiary sales were about KRW 3.5 billion in the first quarter of 2026, below 10% of consolidated revenue of KRW 48.5 billion.

Domestic rental income was KRW 1.82 billion in the first half of 2025, or 2.0% of revenue. The company pairs online distribution with a premium strategy, and has focused on the domestic market since 2021 after its 2014 acquisition by a unit of Chinese conglomerate Lersi Group.

Competition in Korea has intensified as global brands expand their children's lines, and the company is responding by separating value-oriented and premium products.

04

Earnings analysis

On the basis of DART periodic reports, annual revenue rose from KRW 174.3 billion in 2022 to KRW 186.4 billion in 2023, a 6.9% increase, then fell 2.0% to KRW 182.7 billion in 2024 before recovering 3.5% to KRW 189.1 billion in 2025, the largest year of 2022 to 2025.

Operating profit peaked at KRW 16.6 billion in 2023, a 14.1% rise, then declined 8.3% to KRW 15.2 billion in 2024 and 8.0% to KRW 14 billion in 2025, two consecutive annual declines.

The operating margin moved from 8.3% in 2022 and 8.9% in 2023 to 8.3% in 2024 and 7.4% in 2025, so revenue growth did not carry through to margins.

Profit attributable to controlling shareholders was KRW 10.4 billion in 2022, KRW 13.4 billion in 2023, KRW 11.2 billion in 2024 and KRW 11.6 billion in 2025, while net profit including non-controlling interests (KRW 9.85 billion, KRW 12.7 billion, KRW 10.2 billion and KRW 10.6 billion) followed a different path.

Operating cash flow rose from KRW 7.43 billion in 2022 to KRW 22.1 billion in 2023, then eased to KRW 13.6 billion in 2024 and KRW 13.4 billion in 2025. Quarterly results swung widely.

Operating profit was KRW 2.02 billion in the third quarter of 2025 and KRW 5.72 billion in the fourth quarter, up 182.5% quarter on quarter. Revenue in the first quarter of 2026 was KRW 48.5 billion, down 14.4% quarter on quarter, with operating profit of KRW 2.14 billion.

In the second quarter, revenue was KRW 50.4 billion and operating profit KRW 7.19 billion, a 236.6% quarterly increase.

Third-quarter 2026 revenue was KRW 46.7 billion, up 7.1% year on year, but operating profit fell 34.0% quarter on quarter to KRW 4.74 billion and profit attributable to controlling shareholders dropped 90.4% year on year to KRW 151 million.

A preliminary release dated October 29, 2026 showed pre-tax profit of about KRW 210 million in the third quarter, far below operating profit. Over the last four quarters, profit attributable to controlling shareholders totaled KRW 19.2 billion, with the second quarter of 2026 contributing KRW 12.1 billion of that.

05

Industry analysis

The infant and children's goods market faces a structural constraint from Korea's declining birth rate, and an October 2026 press report noted that AGABANG&COMPANY achieved both sales growth and margin improvement despite that backdrop.

Domestic competition has intensified as global brands widen their children's lines, while consumers increasingly weigh value and premium features together. The company is responding by strengthening premium price points and online channels.

Overseas, progress has been limited since the 2016 Chinese retaliation over THAAD and the COVID-19 disruption, and overseas sales have declined since 2023. The company says it intends to expand in China through cooperation with its parent.

On cost, press coverage credited the company with lowering its cost ratio by closing inefficient stores, cutting inventory production and shifting mix toward apparel and away from lower-margin goods. That approach puts more weight on defending profitability than on pursuing volume growth.

Outlook

Press coverage credited the first-half 2026 profit improvement partly to a narrower overseas operating loss and higher rental income. Consolidated revenue in the first half rose 11.2% year on year, while selling, general and administrative expenses grew only 5.6%.

The company is pointing to expansion of its product lines, including infant skincare and furniture, and to stronger online channels as growth drivers. Still, after two annual declines in operating profit, commentary has questioned whether the quarterly rebound will become sustained medium-term growth.

Third-quarter 2026 pre-tax profit fell well short of operating profit, so how non-operating items are explained in upcoming filings will be a key factor for earnings. The next checkpoint is whether the 2026 annual report shows an operating margin above the 7.4% recorded in 2025.

After that, the fourth-quarter 2026 and first-quarter 2027 preliminary results will show whether quarterly profit recovery holds. With overseas sales still a small share of revenue, the effect of the China cooperation on the revenue mix is also worth tracking.

Operating profit peaked at KRW 16.6 billion in 2023 and then declined to KRW 15.2 billion in 2024 and KRW 14 billion in 2025, two consecutive annual drops. The operating margin fell from 8.9% in 2023 to 7.4% in 2025. Revenue grew in 2025 while the margin fell, which is a burden on earnings.

Small overseas and subsidiary contribution

Risk factors

Birth rate and demand base

Domestic demand for infant products depends on the number of births, so a prolonged decline in births would lower the ceiling on revenue growth. Press coverage has pointed to the long-running low birth rate as a constraint on the business. Whether premium positioning and online channels can offset this constraint is the central question.

Quarterly earnings volatility

Operating profit ranged from KRW 5.72 billion in the fourth quarter of 2025 to KRW 2.14 billion in the first quarter of 2026, KRW 7.19 billion in the second and KRW 4.74 billion in the third.

In the third quarter of 2026, operating profit of KRW 4.74 billion compared with pre-tax profit of about KRW 210 million in a preliminary release. How non-operating items are explained in upcoming periodic reports matters for reading earnings.

Overseas business and competition

With overseas sales at only about 2% to 3% of revenue, the benefit of the China cooperation may take time to show up in results. Domestic competition has intensified as global brands expand children's lines, which could keep pricing pressure in place.

The China business is also exposed to bilateral relations and policy risk, as the 2016 THAAD-related retaliation showed.
