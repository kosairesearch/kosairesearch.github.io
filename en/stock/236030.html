02

Business structure

Ssial Food was established in September 2007, focuses on manufacturing and selling cereal, and listed on the KONEX market on December 14, 2017. It makes cereal, cereal bars and whole-grain products from grains, nuts and vitamins.

Its business is split between making and selling its own brands and supplying products developed with its patented technology to major domestic retailers. The company's official online store lists cereals, energy bars, protein bars, organic whole grains and character-themed cereals.

In August 2021 it obtained GMP certification as a health functional food manufacturer and launched an organic whole-grain product, widening its range.

FnGuide describes the cereal and bar manufacturing process as technology that is hard to develop in-house, and says the company holds the only royalty-free proprietary core technology in Korea.

The revenue split between own brands and retailer supply, and the breakdown by product line, could not be verified, so no figures are given.

In the competitive landscape, large food groups such as Orion, Nongshim and Dong SUH Companies, along with imported brands, share the market, and Ssial Food, with a market capitalization of KRW 4.7 billion, trails them heavily in scale and distribution reach.

Its competitiveness therefore rests on manufacturing technology, retailer-specific product development and the expansion of functional product lines rather than on price.

Recent trends

According to the 19th-term standalone statements disclosed on March 31, 2026, 2025 revenue was KRW 18,335 million, operating profit KRW 749 million and net profit KRW 817 million.

The same filing shows total assets of KRW 14,901 million, total liabilities of KRW 13,465 million and total equity of KRW 1,436 million, and with net profit positive, earnings per share was also positive. In 2024, revenue fell 8% year on year and operating profit turned negative.

On an FnGuide basis, 2024 revenue was KRW 17.8 billion, with an operating loss of KRW 0.6 billion and a net loss of KRW 1.1 billion.

The same source shows 2023 revenue of KRW 19.4 billion, operating profit of KRW 0.1 billion and a net loss of KRW 0.8 billion, and 2022 with an operating loss of KRW 1.7 billion and a net loss of KRW 3.7 billion. The 2022 net loss reflected items such as impairment of property, plant and equipment.

Revenue growth in 2025 was modest, and net profit exceeding operating profit appears to reflect a small net contribution from non-operating items. The share price was KRW 2,400 on March 31, 2026, and KRW 4,200 on June 12, 2026. The 52-week high is KRW 4,300.

The current price of KRW 2,290 on October 27, 2026 is well below the June level, and market capitalization is KRW 4.7 billion. With trading value of only KRW 75,280, small orders alone can move the price. No verified results for any 2026 quarter or half-year were found.

Outlook

No 2026 guidance from the company or broker estimates could be found. The outlook therefore hinges on whether the company can hold an operating margin near its 2025 level.

Because 2025 profit was earned on a small revenue base, even modest sales growth could ease the fixed-cost burden and leave room for operating margin improvement. Conversely, rising raw material and labor costs could erode the thin profit quickly.

On the balance sheet, equity grew on the 2025 net profit, but debt remains large, so interest costs and the management of debt maturities matter. A 2023 report said the company had pursued a third-party rights offering and raised its new-share issuance limit to 50% of total issued shares.

Should fresh funding become necessary, potential dilution is a factor shareholders should watch. As a KONEX stock with a small market capitalization and sparse trading, the share price can swing sharply regardless of operating results.

05

Bull factors

Return to profit and a larger equity base

The company recorded standalone 2025 operating profit of KRW 749 million and net profit of KRW 817 million. In 2024 it had an operating loss of KRW 0.6 billion and a net loss of KRW 1.1 billion.

As profit accumulated, total equity rose from roughly KRW 0.6 billion at the end of 2024 to KRW 1,436 million at the end of 2025. Total equity now exceeds paid-in capital of KRW 1,216 million.

In-house technology and certifications

The cereal and bar manufacturing process is technology that is hard to develop in-house, and the company holds royalty-free core technology. It obtained health functional food GMP certification in 2021. This base can be applied to supplying retailers' own-brand products and developing functional products. The absence of royalty payments is a favorable factor for the cost structure.

Room for margin improvement on a small base

Revenue for 2025 was KRW 18,335 million and operating profit KRW 749 million. On such a small revenue base, spreading fixed costs over more sales could leave room for operating margin improvement. This is a view on the business metrics only and does not imply a direction for the share price.

Given that 2023 operating profit was slim, the size of any improvement depends on whether sales growth is sustained.

06

Bear factors

Debt far above equity

Total liabilities at the end of 2025 were KRW 13,465 million against total equity of KRW 1,436 million. At the end of 2024, total equity was KRW 0.6 billion and total liabilities KRW 14.1 billion.

Even with a profit, debt is roughly nine times equity, leaving the company vulnerable to higher interest rates or weaker operations. The key question is whether cash generation can keep covering interest costs.

Volatile and unproven earnings

On an FnGuide basis, operating losses ran from 2020 through 2022, with operating profit of KRW 0.1 billion in 2023 and an operating loss of KRW 0.6 billion in 2024. The 2025 profit is a single-year improvement within this record, so it is hard to call it a trend reversal. Non-operating items also affect net profit and should be separated from core earnings.

Illiquidity and price swings

Market capitalization is KRW 4.7 billion and trading value is only KRW 75,280. The share price was KRW 2,400 on March 31, 2026 and KRW 4,200 on June 12. With such shallow trading, small changes in quotes can move the price sharply. It may be hard to buy or sell the desired quantity at the desired time.

07

Risk factors

Macro

Raw material prices for grains and nuts, as well as exchange rates, directly affect manufacturing costs. With a low operating margin, the company could see profit shrink quickly if it cannot pass higher costs into selling prices.

The high share of borrowings also makes interest rate levels a key driver of financing costs. Weaker consumer sentiment would weigh on processed food demand as well.

Industry and competition

Large food companies and imported brands compete in cereal and bars, and a small manufacturer has relatively weak bargaining power with retailers. Supplying retailers' own-brand products can raise dependence on major retailers. Lower orders or price adjustments from customers could directly affect revenue and profit. If product differentiation weakens, the company is exposed to price competition.

Financing

A 2023 report said the company sought funding to avoid capital impairment, and with paid-in capital of KRW 1.2 billion, losses above KRW 0.5 billion would have triggered impairment. Because total equity at the end of 2024 was below paid-in capital, its financial cushion is still not thick.

If extra funds are needed, a rights offering or borrowing are the options, and an offering could dilute existing shareholders. Low trading liquidity on KONEX also weighs on funding conditions.

08

Overall view

Ssial Food is a small cereal maker with in-house manufacturing technology and a functional product line, and it disclosed standalone 2025 operating profit of KRW 749 million and net profit of KRW 817 million. Moving out of the prior year's operating loss and growing total equity are positive facts.

On the other hand, total liabilities are about nine times total equity, and earnings have been uneven over recent years. A market capitalization of KRW 4.7 billion and trading value of KRW 75,280 show that market liquidity is extremely shallow.

No verified results for any 2026 quarter or half-year were found, so whether profitability continues must be checked in future filings. The judgment is left to the reader, and the key items to monitor are whether the operating margin holds, how the debt burden changes and whether additional funding is raised.
