02

Business structure

Teraview Holdings is a UK-incorporated holding company, and it brought its subsidiary TeraView Ltd into the group in 2024. The wholly owned subsidiary supplies inspection solutions for semiconductors, automotive and batteries. Its core technology is terahertz imaging and spectroscopy.

The technology can reveal defects in nanometer-scale semiconductor circuits without damaging them, and is expected to complement or replace existing electromagnetic, ultrasonic and X-ray inspection. The company works with several global firms, including NVIDIA, AMD and Samsung Electronics.

Its products span the EOTPR semiconductor packaging inspection tool, automotive and high-value coating inspection tools, battery electrode inspection tools, and maintenance services.

At listing, the company set a fiscal 2026 revenue target of KRW 25.1 billion, split into KRW 13.9 billion for semiconductor packaging tools, KRW 5.7 billion for automotive and coating tools, KRW 3.0 billion for battery tools and KRW 2.5 billion for maintenance.

In the IPO-era projections, EOTPR was the largest component of fiscal 2027 revenue at 53.44%. Over the medium to long term, management aims to extend the product range to defense, aviation, appliances, 6G communications and medical uses.

The market classifies the stock under finance, but the actual business is inspection-equipment manufacturing, so direct comparison with banks and brokerages is not appropriate.

03

Recent trends

In reported results, cumulative revenue for the third quarter of 2026 was KRW 1.27 million, up 43.5% year on year. The operating loss for the period was KRW 1.34 million and the net loss was KRW 1.69 million, so losses continued and were wider than a year earlier.

The scale of these figures differs sharply from the earlier fiscal-year numbers that the press converted into won and reported in billions, so the units and conversion basis should be checked in the original filing.

The fiscal year ends in April, so the quarterly split also differs from companies with December year-ends. For the fiscal year from May 1, 2025 to April 30, 2026, consolidated revenue was KRW 7.83 billion and the operating loss was KRW 9.33 billion.

The company attributed the wider loss to higher fees tied to becoming a listed company and higher payroll from hiring, which pushed up selling and administrative expenses.

The registration statement had forecast an operating profit and net profit of KRW 3.5 billion each, but the company posted losses of KRW 9.3 billion and KRW 8.8 billion. On the share price, the stock reached KRW 20,800 intraday on December 10, 2025, just after listing.

On April 17, 2026, it closed at KRW 7,010, below the KRW 8,000 IPO price. On September 29, 2026, it closed at the daily limit of KRW 4,355 after a supply-contract disclosure.

The October 27, 2026 close was KRW 2,620 (-9.19% from the prior day), with a market capitalization of KRW 94.1 billion and trading value of KRW 800 million. No specific disclosure or report explaining the decline has been identified.

04

Outlook

The key variable for future results is how quickly orders convert into recognized revenue. The company said delays in customers' site acceptance tests pushed part of its equipment revenue into the next fiscal year. The order backlog at April 30, 2026 was GBP 2.62 million, or about KRW 5.0 billion.

Four supply contracts were disclosed from January 1 to September 28, 2026, totaling KRW 6.24 billion. Another contract of about KRW 1.4 billion was disclosed on October 13, 2026, and five disclosures with the same title have been reported since June 30.

The company intends to use the Covalent Metrology contract as a customer reference in AI semiconductor inspection and to widen EOTPR supply to global foundries and outsourced inspection firms.

Of KRW 29.7 billion raised for working capital, KRW 5.4 billion had been used by the end of April 2026, and the rest is planned for overseas expansion and R&D through fiscal 2028.

Disclosed contracts are in the range of KRW 1 billion each, which is small relative to the revenue scale assumed in the registration statement. Whether the operating loss narrows will have to be checked in the next periodic report through revenue recognition and the trend in selling and administrative expenses.

05

Bull factors

Accumulating supply-contract disclosures

Four contracts worth KRW 6.24 billion were disclosed between January 1 and September 28, 2026, versus no disclosed amount in the same period a year earlier. Several outlets also reported further contract disclosures in the KRW 1 billion range in October. This can be read as a sign of a widening customer base. The timing of revenue recognition remains a separate matter.

Differentiated non-destructive technology

Terahertz technology can reveal defects without damaging circuits and is expected to replace existing inspection methods. Collaboration with firms such as NVIDIA, AMD and Samsung Electronics is cited as evidence of technical validation.

Inspection demand is expected to grow in areas with complex packaging, such as AI semiconductors and high-bandwidth memory. This view comes from the company and the press and still needs to be confirmed in revenue.

Financial cushion from IPO proceeds

Total equity rose from GBP 1.43 million (about KRW 2.8 billion) to GBP 15.98 million (about KRW 30.7 billion) between the April 2025 and April 2026 year-ends. After listing, debt was repaid and reliance on borrowings fell to about 6%. Funds remain available for R&D and overseas expansion. Near-term capital-erosion concern is relatively low.

06

Bear factors

Gap to the targets set at listing

The registration statement forecast an operating profit of KRW 3.5 billion, but the company posted an operating loss of KRW 9.3 billion. Revenue of KRW 7.8 billion also fell far short of the KRW 25.1 billion forecast, a shortfall of 68.83%. The gap fueled debate over the reliability of management projections. Some reports also questioned the earnings-based valuation used for the technology-special listing.

Overheads exceeding revenue

Selling and administrative expenses in fiscal 2026 rose by quarter to about KRW 2.8 billion, KRW 3.0 billion and KRW 4.0 billion. Through the third quarter, about KRW 4.4 billion went to payroll and about KRW 3.8 billion to listing-related fees.

In reported cumulative results for the third quarter of 2026, the operating and net losses also widened. If revenue growth does not keep pace with cost growth, losses could persist.

Accumulated deficit and thin liquidity

The accumulated deficit had grown to about KRW 133.0 billion by the end of January 2026. Trading value on October 27, 2026 was only KRW 800 million, small relative to a market capitalization of KRW 94.1 billion. The shares have swung widely and reacted sharply to supply-contract disclosures. After the limit-up close, they fell within weeks, indicating high volatility.

07

Risk factors

Macro

Revenue depends on capital-spending schedules at semiconductor and battery manufacturers. Investment delays or a slowdown in the cycle could push back equipment adoption. The company's financial statements are prepared in pounds sterling and converted into won under disclosure rules. Exchange-rate moves can therefore change won-denominated results and asset values.

According to reporting at the time of the IPO, much of the projected revenue was still at the stage of customers evaluating purchases. Equipment revenue is recognized according to customer installation and site acceptance test schedules. Longer customer validation delays recognition and raises earnings volatility.

Competition from incumbent inspection methods and the pace at which the new technology becomes a standard are further variables.

At listing, 65.9% of shares were under lock-up and 34.1% were freely tradable. The tradable float could grow as lock-ups expire, which may add supply pressure. One report also noted a run of governance-related disclosures, including a correction to a shareholder-meeting resolution and changes in major shareholdings.

As a UK-incorporated company, the way domestic shareholders exercise rights and the way disclosures are handled may differ from those of Korean companies.

08

Overall view

Teraview Holdings has a differentiated terahertz non-destructive inspection technology and a record of collaboration with global semiconductor firms.

In reported cumulative results for the third quarter of 2026, however, revenue of KRW 1.27 million rose 43.5% while the operating loss of KRW 1.34 million and net loss of KRW 1.69 million widened. The prior fiscal year also ended with an operating loss of about KRW 9.3 billion, far from the forecast made at listing.

Supply-contract disclosures continue, but individual contracts are in the KRW 1 billion range and revenue recognition depends on customer acceptance-test schedules. IPO proceeds improved the capital structure and much of the money remains unspent, but the accumulated deficit keeps growing.

The shares closed at KRW 2,620 on October 27, 2026, far below the KRW 8,000 IPO price, and volatility is high. The indicators to watch in the next periodic report are revenue recognition, selling and administrative expenses, and changes in the order backlog.
