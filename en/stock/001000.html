Margins rose, sales fell, and a listing-status issue remains

02

Business structure

SillaTextile was founded in 1976. In May 2024 its main business classification changed from retail of home appliances and information communication equipment to real estate leasing and supply. Despite the company name, its main businesses are now property leasing and mobile phone sales.

The company overview page shows a revenue mix of 58.25% leasing and 41.75% telecom, without stating the reference date. The same page records both mobile phone sales and leasing as fully domestic revenue. The leasing unit rents out property to tenants, so earnings are sensitive to tenant demand and the economic cycle.

The phone sales unit depends on mobile carrier distribution channels, so it is exposed to changes in subscriber policies and handset demand. Both segments have shown weak results recently.

Among listed property companies, many REITs are compared on asset-based dividend structures, whereas SillaTextile is an ordinary corporation rather than a REIT. On the shareholder side, Silla, a shareholder holding 10% or more, ended with 1,000,070 shares and kept its 20.60% stake. The filing attributed the share count change to a stock consolidation.

04

Earnings analysis

Annual sales went from KRW 4.43 billion in 2022 to KRW 3.78 billion in 2023 (down 14.7% year on year), KRW 3.67 billion in 2024 (down 2.9%), and KRW 3.59 billion in 2025 (down 2.2%). Sales declined for three consecutive years from 2023 through 2025. 2022 was the largest year and 2025 the smallest within 2022 to 2025.

Operating profit fell from KRW 465 million in 2022 to KRW 392 million in 2023 (down 15.7%), then rose to KRW 417 million in 2024 (up 6.5%) and KRW 488 million in 2025 (up 17.0%), increasing for two consecutive years.

Because operating profit grew while sales shrank, the operating margin rose from 10.5% in 2022 to 10.4% in 2023, 11.4% in 2024 and 13.6% in 2025.

Net income attributable to controlling shareholders moved from a loss of KRW 35.84 million in 2022 and a loss of KRW 672 million in 2023 (losses widened) to a loss of KRW 302 million in 2024 (losses narrowed), before turning to a profit of KRW 71.72 million in 2025.

Net income including non-controlling interests followed the same path in 2025, at KRW 71.72 million. Operating cash flow was KRW 915 million in 2022, KRW 476 million in 2023, KRW 620 million in 2024 and KRW 720 million in 2025, positive in all four years.

At the end of 2025, total equity was KRW 14.5 billion, all of it attributable to controlling shareholders, and total liabilities were KRW 16.3 billion. The debt-to-equity ratio moved from 107.5% in 2022 to 110.2%, 112.8% and 112.4% in 2025.

Quarterly sales were KRW 971 million in Q3 2025, KRW 946 million in Q4 2025 (down 2.6% quarter on quarter), and KRW 832 million in Q1 2026 (down 12.1%). They rebounded to KRW 926 million in Q2 2026 (up 11.4%) before falling to KRW 813 million in Q3 2026 (down 12.2% quarter on quarter and down 16.2% year on year).

Operating profit moved to a loss of KRW 531 million in Q2 2026, a swing from profit in Q1, and returned to a profit of KRW 341 million in Q3, up 5.5% year on year. The causes of the Q2 loss and the quarterly cost structure are points to check in the 2026 annual report.

05

Industry analysis

SillaTextile's core businesses are exposed to the economic and interest-rate environment. The company's results commentary says leasing and phone sales have continued to underperform amid a slowing economy and interest-rate swings.

On the leasing side, the commentary expects rental demand to rise as large corporations enter the used-car market. That view comes from the company-profile commentary, not from a brokerage forecast. On the phone side, the same commentary describes an ecosystem forming around AI and IoT.

However, no disclosed figure yet shows these trends translating into higher company sales. In mid-October 2026, the stock approached its daily price limit on KOSDAQ, a sign of short-term volatility.

In scale, the company's 2025 sales of about KRW 3.59 billion are far smaller than those of the larger listed property and REIT companies. The sector picture is therefore mixed, with signs of leasing-demand recovery alongside continued weakness.

The two segments also respond to different drivers, since phone sales depend on carrier policy and leasing income depends on occupancy.

Bull factors

Recovering operating margin

The operating margin rose from 10.5% in 2022 to 13.6% in 2025. Operating profit returned to KRW 341 million in Q3 2026, up 5.5% year on year. Improving profitability while sales shrink is a constructive signal, though quarterly results have been volatile, including the Q2 2026 loss.

Turn to profit and steady operating cash flow

Net income attributable to controlling shareholders turned from a loss to a profit of KRW 71.72 million in 2025. Operating cash flow was positive in every year from 2022 to 2025, reaching KRW 720 million in 2025.

Steady cash generation helps absorb borrowing costs, although the profit base is small and its persistence still needs confirmation.

Possible recovery in leasing demand

The company-profile commentary expects leasing demand to rise as large corporations enter the used-car market. Leasing is shown as the larger of the two revenue lines, so any recovery would feed directly into results. Whether this shows up in lease contracts and sales will be visible only in later quarterly figures.

Bear factors

Three years of falling sales

Sales fell from KRW 4.43 billion in 2022 to KRW 3.59 billion in 2025, declining for three straight years from 2023. Q3 2026 sales of KRW 813 million were down 16.2% year on year. If the shrinking revenue base persists, the room for margin gains may be limited. The Q2 2026 rebound was not large enough to reverse the trend.

Unstable recent quarterly earnings

Q2 2026 posted an operating loss of KRW 531 million and a net loss attributable to controlling shareholders of KRW 683 million. The net loss attributable to controlling shareholders totals KRW 26.79 million over the last four quarters.

The Q3 profit is a sign of recovery, but profits and losses have alternated by quarter, so earnings persistence is low. The drivers of the Q2 loss cannot be separated from the disclosed table and need follow-up.

Administrative-issue status and borrowing load

The low-price issue has been resolved, but the designation continues because market capitalization remains below the threshold. Short-term borrowings are reported at KRW 14.6 billion, with no long-term borrowings.

Total liabilities of KRW 16.3 billion against total equity of KRW 14.5 billion leave limited financial room. Together, these two factors could weigh on both financing and market assessment.

Risk factors

Listing and regulatory risk

KRX has said one designation ground remains. If market capitalization does not recover to the threshold, the designation continues, and a longer period could affect trading and fund-raising. This ground moves separately from earnings, so each new exchange notice should be checked against the threshold.

Financial and borrowing risk

The debt-to-equity ratio was 112.4% at the end of 2025, with total liabilities of KRW 16.3 billion. Short-term borrowings of KRW 14.6 billion are concentrated, so the maturity profile could become a burden. Operating cash flow is positive but at around KRW 720 million, a modest scale. Debt repayment capacity depends on profits and cash generation holding up together.

Business concentration and ownership risk

Sales are concentrated in two businesses, leasing and phone sales, so weakness in either weighs heavily on overall results. Silla, a shareholder holding 10% or more, reduced its share count through the August 2026 stock consolidation.

Its stake stayed at 20.60%, but how the change in share count affects the free float and trading structure still needs to be confirmed.

12

Overall view

SillaTextile is a small company built on two businesses, property leasing and mobile phone sales. From 2022 to 2025 its operating margin rose from 10.5% to 13.6%, and net income attributable to controlling shareholders turned to a profit in 2025.

Sales, however, have declined for three straight years since 2023, and Q2 2026 posted an operating loss of KRW 531 million.

The Q3 2026 operating profit of KRW 341 million marks a return to the black, but net losses attributable to controlling shareholders total KRW 26.79 million over the last four quarters, so earnings persistence is still to be confirmed.

The administrative-issue designation was partly lifted after the low-price issue was resolved, but it remains because market capitalization is below the threshold. On the balance sheet, total liabilities of KRW 16.3 billion and short-term borrowings of KRW 14.6 billion remain burdens.

The PBR is below 1x, but the company pays no dividend, so how the valuation reads depends on whether profits recover and the listing-status issue is resolved. The next checkpoint is the 2026 annual report, followed by the Q1 2027 quarterly report to check leasing demand and earnings continuity.
