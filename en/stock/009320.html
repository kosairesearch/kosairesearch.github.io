02

Business structure

Ajin Electronic Components is an auto parts maker listed on KOSPI since August 1989. After Ajin Industry (A-Jin Industry) acquired it in 2010, the company shifted from conventional electronic components to automotive electronic components.

In the nine months through the third quarter of 2025, on a separate-financials basis, automotive product sales accounted for 96.9% of revenue. Domestic general customers made up 89.2% of sales and exports 10.8%.

Core products include heater units for EVs, mood lamp controllers and AAUs (Auxiliary Air Unit) for electric and autonomous vehicles, which are in mass production. A 48V TMD (integrated thermal management controller) for the Ioniq 5 was decided on October 20, 2026, with delivery to Hyundai WIA scheduled for 2027.

In the U.S., a Georgia unit set up in early 2024 entered the North American supply chain for Hyundai Motor and KIA, and a Suzhou unit in China is building a current sensor line for the domestic Chinese market. The customer base is concentrated in the Hyundai Motor and KIA chain, so volumes follow OEM production plans.

In thermal management, the company competes for new-model adoption with specialists such as Hanon Systems. At its March 31, 2026 general meeting, the company added robot parts manufacturing and sales to its business purposes.

04

Earnings analysis

Annual sales rose from KRW 100.5 billion in 2022 to KRW 113.4 billion in 2023 (up 12.9% year on year), KRW 143.5 billion in 2024 (up 26.5%) and KRW 151.8 billion in 2025 (up 5.8%), three straight years of increases from 2023 through 2025, with 2025 the largest year of the span.

Operating results moved from an operating loss of KRW 5.76 billion in 2022 to operating profit of KRW 2.04 billion in 2023, then KRW 5.94 billion in 2024 (up 191.4%) and KRW 6.3 billion in 2025 (up 6.0%), two straight years of gains.

Operating margin went from -5.7% in 2022 to 1.8% in 2023 and 4.1% in both 2024 and 2025. Net income attributable to controlling shareholders swung from a net loss of KRW 8.74 billion in 2022 to KRW 230 million in 2023, then KRW 919 million in 2024 (up 300.2%) and KRW 4.41 billion in 2025 (up 379.4%).

Net income including non-controlling interests was KRW 4.35 billion in 2025, a different figure, and the gap comes from the non-controlling share. Operating cash flow was KRW 5.27 billion in 2022, KRW 3.63 billion in 2023, KRW 13.2 billion in 2024, the largest of the four years, and KRW 4.46 billion in 2025.

The debt ratio went from 255.6% in 2022 and 239.1% in 2023 to 254.7% in 2024 and 228.7% in 2025. Quarterly sales fell to KRW 35.8 billion in Q4 2025 (down 13.0% from the prior quarter), then rose three straight quarters to KRW 36.5 billion, KRW 38.6 billion and KRW 39 billion in Q3 2026.

Operating profit dropped to KRW 1.09 billion in Q4 2025 (down 44.4% quarter on quarter), fell to KRW 881 million in Q1 2026 and KRW 625 million in Q2 2026, then recovered to KRW 2.46 billion in Q3 2026 (up 25.4% year on year) even though sales were down 5.2% year on year.

Net income attributable to controlling shareholders included a loss of KRW 463 million in Q4 2025 and a loss of KRW 113 million in Q2 2026, so the last four quarters total only KRW 1.64 billion. The disclosed figures do not separate the drivers of these quarterly swings.

05

Industry analysis

The company's growth axis is electrification-driven electronics for EVs and fuel-efficiency products. It developed and mass-produced electrified and efficiency-focused electronic parts, and applications on new Hyundai and KIA models were cited as a driver of the earnings recovery.

Next-generation EV heater units, mood lamp controllers and AAUs are presented as products in mass production. Electrified parts are tied to OEM new-model launch schedules, so industry conditions show up quickly in quarterly sales.

The three consecutive quarters of sales growth after the Q4 2025 low may reflect such launch timing. Overseas, U.S. unit sales rose from KRW 600 million in 2024 to KRW 5.9 billion in 2025, showing that the North American OEM link is translating into revenue.

Among listed peers, Hyundai Mobis, HL Mando and Seoyon E-Hwa compete in the same OEM supply chain in different product lines. Measured by revenue, Ajin Electronic Components is smaller than large parts makers and depends heavily on domestic OEM volumes. In thermal management, new-model adoption determines volume.

06

Outlook

The forward business item confirmed in company disclosures is the 48V TMD supply to Hyundai WIA for the Ioniq 5, with delivery scheduled for 2027. The disclosure states that supply timing and volume may change according to the customer's development and production plans.

At the March 2026 general meeting, the company added robot parts manufacturing and sales to its business purposes, a step toward diversification. The same meeting elected two outside directors, so outside directors make up 50% of the four-member board.

U.S. unit sales reached about KRW 5.9 billion in 2025, so whether North American volumes continue to flow into results is the next thing to track. The Q3 2026 profit recovery came with lower sales, so the direction of sales and whether the operating margin holds around 4% in the next annual report are the key checks.

Given the small earnings base, the contribution of new businesses such as robot parts is hard to gauge from disclosed figures. On ownership, the 10% shareholder raised its stake through on-market purchases of 215,500 shares on October 12, 2026, to 14.17%.

Bull factors

Profit recovery after the turn to profit

After an operating loss of KRW 5.76 billion in 2022, the company turned to operating profit of KRW 2.04 billion in 2023, then grew it to KRW 5.94 billion in 2024 and KRW 6.3 billion in 2025. The operating margin rose from -5.7% in 2022 to 4.1% in 2025.

In Q3 2026 sales fell 5.2% year on year, yet operating profit rose 25.4% to KRW 2.46 billion, an improvement in quarterly profitability.

EV electronics and new supply contracts

EV heater units, mood lamp controllers and AAUs are presented as products in mass production, broadening the electrified product line. A 48V TMD supply to Hyundai WIA for the Ioniq 5, decided on October 20, 2026, is scheduled for delivery in 2027. U.S. unit sales rose from KRW 600 million in 2024 to KRW 5.9 billion in 2025.

Gradual balance sheet improvement

The debt ratio fell from 255.6% in 2022 to 228.7% in 2025. Accumulated deficit shrank from KRW 11.8 billion at the end of 2022 to around KRW 5.9 billion in the first half of 2026. Equity stood at KRW 33.3 billion at the end of 2025, against total liabilities of KRW 76.2 billion.

Bear factors

Sales decline and volatile quarterly profit

Q3 2026 sales were KRW 39 billion, down 5.2% year on year. Operating profit in Q4 2025 was KRW 1.09 billion, down 44.4% from the prior quarter, and the Q2 2026 net loss attributable to controlling shareholders of KRW 113 million turned the quarter back into a loss. Sales have risen for three straight quarters since Q1 2026, but they remain below the year-earlier level.

Thin earnings base and margins

The operating margin was 4.1% in both 2024 and 2025, a low single-digit level. Net income attributable to controlling shareholders totaled KRW 1.64 billion over the last four quarters, and Q4 2025 posted a loss of KRW 463 million.

Because the earnings base is small, one or two quarters of cost or revenue shifts can change the direction of the result.

High leverage and reliance on group support

Total liabilities of KRW 76.2 billion exceeded equity of KRW 33.3 billion at the end of 2025, a debt ratio of 228.7%. Press reports said a group affiliate provided KRW 3 billion in short-term loans at the end of 2022.

With leverage still high and the earnings base small, repayment capacity and interest burden are items to check in the next annual report.

Ownership and governance changes

The largest shareholder changed on July 16, 2026, from the former controlling holder to a group affiliate that now holds 15.42%. A founder-related holder and its related parties reported a combined stake of 25.84%, and 4.2 million shares (8.52%) are pledged under a contract running to June 24, 2027. The ownership transition may lead to differing market interpretations of the direction of control.

Customer concentration and supply timing

The disclosure states that the timing and volume of the TMD supply may change with the customer's plans. If new-model timing slips, sales and profit in the affected quarters could move with it.

Overall view
