02

Business structure

HB Investment is a first-generation Korean venture capital firm founded in 1999 and listed on KOSDAQ in January 2025. Its revenue comes from management fees earned on funds it forms and from performance fees tied to investment exits, and management fees accumulate each year in line with the size of its managed assets.

Managed assets were reported at about KRW 700 billion, generating management fees of more than KRW 7 billion per year. As of February 2025, managed assets stood at KRW 691.8 billion across 19 funds, with cumulative fund formation of KRW 1.12 trillion across 37 funds.

Per a 2025 report, the portfolio was split 40% deep tech and manufacturing, 40% software and services, and 20% bio and healthcare. Investment targets have extended to AI semiconductor firms and a robotics specialist.

A first close of a KRW 100 billion fund was completed in late December 2025, with a multi-closing aimed at up to KRW 150 billion. A private equity division was established in 2025. Institutional capital from the Korea Fund of Funds and the Growth Finance Corporation accounts for about 40%.

The largest shareholder is the group holding company HB Corporation, and the company says group affiliates can join as strategic investors.

04

Earnings analysis

Revenue rose 28.8% to KRW 20.5 billion in 2023, the largest year of 2022 to 2025, then fell 26.1% to KRW 15.2 billion in 2024, the smallest year. Revenue grew 14.8% to KRW 17.4 billion in 2025, still below the 2023 level.

Operating profit was KRW 9.29 billion in 2022, KRW 10.6 billion in 2023, KRW 6.49 billion in 2024 and KRW 7.26 billion in 2025, falling 38.8% in 2024 before rising 11.8% in 2025. The operating margin declined from 58.3% to 41.7% over the same period.

In 2025, operating profit grew more slowly than revenue, so profitability ratios did not recover. Profit attributable to owners of the parent was KRW 7.47 billion in 2022, KRW 8.88 billion in 2023, KRW 6.04 billion in 2024 and KRW 6.14 billion in 2025.

Net profit is disclosed at the same amounts as profit attributable to owners of the parent in each year. Operating cash flow was -KRW 437 million in 2022, KRW 8.34 billion in 2023, -KRW 3.5 billion in 2024 and KRW 3.68 billion in 2025.

Total equity at end-2025 was KRW 88.6 billion, all attributable to owners of the parent, with total liabilities of KRW 6.15 billion.

Quarterly operating profit moved from KRW 1.64 billion in Q3 2025 to KRW 1.32 billion in Q4 2025, KRW 883 million in Q1 2026, then KRW 2.84 billion in Q2 2026 and KRW 2.02 billion in Q3 2026. Profit attributable to owners of the parent for the latest four quarters totaled KRW 5.95 billion. The breakdown behind the Q2 2026 jump can be checked in the semi-annual report filed on August 14, 2026.

05

Industry analysis

A January 2026 report said venture fund-raising rebounded to the KRW 8 trillion range after three years, led by private equity funds. A 2025 brokerage view expected easier fundraising under policy support, with capital flowing into AI, robotics and beauty investments.

The same coverage said the company targets AI, materials, parts and equipment, and skin care, positioning it to benefit from the venture upcycle. Listed venture capital firms, however, face structural limits: small market capitalization and earnings volatility restrict institutional investor inflows.

Coverage also noted a diversified portfolio that has held up relatively well in weak venture markets. In an early 2026 report, one competing venture firm had managed assets of about KRW 1.5 trillion, nearly twice the size of HB Investment.

HB Investment has a first-generation VC track record and group financial backing, but it trails larger houses in managed assets. Its position is that of a mid-sized house, and reaching KRW 1 trillion in managed assets is cited as the threshold for scale.

06

Outlook

The company has set a goal of KRW 1 trillion in managed assets. CEO Hwang Yu-seon described the multi-closing as a turning point from a mid-sized venture firm to a large house, noting managed assets now exceed KRW 700 billion.

The head of the third investment division said reaching KRW 1 trillion is possible around 2027 to 2028. The CEO said the private equity business would be fully launched from the second half of 2026, with hiring of semiconductor, AI and robotics specialists and a list of potential M&A targets.

Whether this translated into higher managed assets and fee income can be checked in the annual report. On exits, press coverage said further sales of the remaining 65% stake in a cosmetics portfolio company listed in 2025 were expected. A brokerage said the success of exits will be decisive.

The company said it will maintain a high-dividend policy. In May 2026 it filed a voluntary corporate value-up plan.

08

Bull factors

Expanding fee base from managed assets

Managed assets are around KRW 700 billion, generating management fees of more than KRW 7 billion a year. Cumulative fund formation reached KRW 1.12 trillion across 37 funds as of February 2025. Expanding the flagship fund through a multi-closing could widen the fee base further.

Quarterly operating profit rebound in 2026

Operating profit rose 11.8% in 2025 to KRW 7.26 billion, reversing the 2024 decline. Q2 2026 operating profit reached KRW 2.84 billion, up 222.0% from the prior quarter, and Q3 2026 was KRW 2.02 billion, up 22.6% year on year. Quarterly profit moved away from the Q1 2026 low.

Large exit gains and shareholder returns

After a cosmetics portfolio company listed, the firm sold about 35% of its stake for more than KRW 16.7 billion, a 24-times return on invested capital. Coverage expects further exits from the remaining stake. The company has said it will keep a high-dividend policy.

09

Bear factors

Structural decline in operating margin

Operating margin fell from 58.3% in 2022 to 41.7% in 2025, dropping to 42.9% in 2024 when operating profit declined 38.8%. In 2025, operating profit grew more slowly than revenue, so the margin did not recover.

Quarterly volatility tied to exit timing

Quarterly operating profit ranged from KRW 883 million in Q1 2026 to KRW 2.84 billion in Q2 2026. Results swing with performance fees and exit timing. A brokerage described exit success as the decisive factor.

2024 profit decline and small market capitalization

Profit attributable to owners of the parent fell 32.0% to KRW 6.04 billion in 2024. Small market capitalization is cited as limiting institutional investment, and the CEO has named improving the share price level as a priority.

10

Risk factors

Exit timing risk

Operating profit can vary sharply depending on when and how much portfolio value is realized. Fair value changes in held stakes can also affect profit and loss. Concentrated exits can widen quarterly swings, while delayed exits reduce income beyond management fees.

Venture cycle and policy funding risk

Fund formation depends on policy capital and institutional commitments. With about 40% from the Korea Fund of Funds and the Growth Finance Corporation, a policy shift could slow fundraising. A downturn in the venture cycle could slow both new fund size and fee growth.

Ownership structure and liquidity risk

At listing, HB Corporation and four related holders together held 56.2% of issued shares. A high insider stake limits free float and can thin trading volume. With a small market capitalization, share price volatility can also be large.

12

Overall view

HB Investment is a first-generation venture capital firm with a fee-based earnings structure, and its managed assets are growing toward a KRW 1 trillion target from about KRW 700 billion.

Based on disclosed results, revenue peaked at KRW 20.5 billion in 2023 and recovered to KRW 17.4 billion in 2025, while operating profit rose to KRW 7.26 billion in 2025. However, the operating margin declined from 58.3% in 2022 to 41.7% in 2025, and profit swings sharply by quarter depending on exit timing and size.

Operating profit of KRW 2.84 billion in Q2 2026 and KRW 2.02 billion in Q3 2026 reads as a recovery, but whether it continues depends on exits.

The share price trades at a low level relative to net assets, and coverage describes the payout yield as relatively high, while small market capitalization and limited institutional inflows are cited as reasons for the low multiple.

Strengths are fund scale-up and shareholder return policy; weaknesses are the falling operating margin and reliance on exits. The next checkpoints are the operating margin in the 2026 annual report and Q1 2027 quarterly results.
