Rising Sales, Falling Net Profit at a Holding Company

02

Business structure

Dongwon Industries is the business holding company of the Dongwon Group, founded in 1969, and oversees fisheries, food processing and distribution, logistics, packaging and construction subsidiaries.

According to the company's profile, its roots lie in offshore fishing and seafood sales, and it is now pursuing new businesses such as secondary battery materials and smart port operations.

Based on a press report on first-quarter 2026 results, food processing and distribution is the largest segment at about 68% of revenue, followed by logistics at 12%, packaging at 11%, and fisheries at 3 to 4%.

Another press report using second-quarter 2026 data put the food segment at 55.8% of revenue, so the mix depends on the reference period and method. The core of the food segment is the food subsidiary, which sells canned tuna, seasoning sauces such as tuna-based liquid seasoning, ready meals and pet food.

The US tuna subsidiary StarKist is described in a Hana Securities report as holding about 40% of the US canned tuna market.

The home-food subsidiary handles food service ingredients and catering, while the packaging subsidiary Dongwon Systems produces flexible packaging, bottles, cans and industrial films and is expanding exports of eco-friendly packaging.

The logistics subsidiary runs port handling, warehousing and cold chain logistics, and the construction subsidiary is selectively bidding for projects centered on logistics centers.

Within the competitive landscape, the food subsidiary's sales scale is the second largest among Korean food companies after CJ Cheiljedang, but its overseas sales share remains in the low single digits.

04

Earnings analysis

On a DART-filed annual basis, revenue went from KRW 9.03 trillion in 2022 to KRW 8.95 trillion in 2023 (down 0.9%) and KRW 8.94 trillion in 2024 (no change), before reaching KRW 9.58 trillion in 2025 (up 7.1%), the largest year in the 2022 to 2025 window.

Operating profit fell from KRW 494.4 billion in 2022 to KRW 464.7 billion in 2023 (down 6.0%), then rose to KRW 501.3 billion in 2024 (up 7.9%) and KRW 516.1 billion in 2025 (up 3.0%), growing for two consecutive years from 2024 to 2025. Operating margin stayed in a narrow band of 5.2% to 5.6% across the four years.

One Korean daily described 2025 revenue as a record since the company's founding. Profit attributable to controlling shareholders fell from KRW 296.5 billion in 2022 to KRW 225 billion in 2023 (down 24.1%) and KRW 75.3 billion in 2024 (down 66.5%), then recovered to KRW 367 billion in 2025 (up 387.6%).

In a financial statement table published by Hana Securities, the 2024 pre-tax profit was well below operating profit because non-operating items, financial items and equity-method results were all in loss. The same table shows non-operating items turning positive in 2025, which appears to be one driver of the recovery.

Net profit including non-controlling interests was KRW 387.1 billion in 2025, higher than the controlling-shareholder figure because of the non-controlling share. In quarterly terms, the fourth quarter of 2025 was the low point, with revenue of KRW 2.32 trillion and operating profit of KRW 109.5 billion.

In the third quarter of 2026, revenue reached KRW 2.76 trillion (up 6.6% year on year) and operating profit KRW 149.7 billion (up 1.1%), but profit attributable to controlling shareholders was KRW 59.3 billion, down 41.3% from the prior quarter.

Hana Securities attributed the weakness to higher food cost burdens from high fish prices, which it estimated at about 1,950 dollars per ton for the quarter, and to lower catches in the standalone fishing business. Profit attributable to controlling shareholders over the latest four quarters totals KRW 391.4 billion.

05

Industry analysis

Fisheries, one of the holding company's core businesses, is strongly affected by fishing grounds and catch volumes. In a May 2026 press report, Heungkuk Securities cited prolonged fishing downturns and greater volatility in fish prices as burdens on the marine business.

In a November 2026 report, Hana Securities estimated the third-quarter average fish price at about 1,950 dollars per ton and said it pressured food costs.

In the food business, StarKist is described as holding about 40% of the US canned tuna market, and the company is responding with price increases and more efficient promotions.

Even so, the food subsidiary's overseas sales share is in the 3% range, and a press report said it was the only company among Korean food makers with annual sales above KRW 4 trillion whose overseas share is in single digits, as of 2025.

Packaging is affected by higher aluminum and naphtha costs and weak liquor demand, as Hana Securities noted in a February 2026 report. The logistics business has grown sales in double digits by adding new industrial and consumer customers even as global cargo volumes declined, according to the company.

In construction, selective bidding and cost control lifted operating profit more than threefold in 2025, according to press coverage. Overall, the food business pairs export growth against cost pressure, while the non-food units show customer gains and margin improvement.

06

Outlook

The company guided for high single-digit revenue growth in 2026 and an improvement in operating margin, as Hana Securities reported in a February 2026 note. The same report expected a back-half-weighted pattern for the year, with the food subsidiary's cost burden from the strong dollar easing from the second half.

The third-quarter 2026 result, however, fell short of that path because high fish prices raised food costs, according to Hana Securities' November 2026 report.

In a May 2026 press report, Heungkuk Securities projected 2026 revenue of KRW 10.318 trillion and operating profit of KRW 541.3 billion, up 7.7% and 5.0% year on year, and said it had lowered its earlier forecast.

In its November 2026 report, Hana Securities set a target price of KRW 60,000 and said that a sustained share price recovery would require visible shareholder return measures and a clearer plan to broaden overseas presence.

The company has pursued expansion of strategic food products such as tteokbokki, seaweed snacks and sauces overseas, and M&A has been cited as a strategic option.

The next confirmation points are the annual filed results and dividend decision in the 2026 business report, and whether the food operating margin recovers in the first quarter of 2027.

08

Bull factors

Export and B2B customer growth

A press report on 2025 results said US exports of the Dongwon tuna brand rose close to 30% year on year, while domestic seasoning sauce lines grew 40%. In the third quarter of 2026, new B2B customers across food distribution, packaging and logistics supported sales growth of 6.6% year on year.

The logistics subsidiary's sales and operating profit grew in double digits after it added industrial and consumer customers, according to press coverage.

Stated target for margin improvement

Hana Securities reported in a February 2026 note that management guided for single-digit percentage revenue growth and an improvement in operating margin. In 2025, the construction subsidiary's operating profit more than tripled through selective bidding and cost control. With a 2025 operating margin of 5.4%, there is room for margin improvement as a business metric.

Shareholder return policy

Hana Securities noted in a February 2026 report that management committed to raising its dividend payout ratio to around 30%. Profit attributable to controlling shareholders recovered to KRW 367 billion in 2025, giving the policy a broader earnings base than in 2024. The method and timing of implementation still need to be confirmed through filings.

09

Bear factors

Sharp drop in third-quarter net profit

Profit attributable to controlling shareholders was KRW 59.3 billion in the third quarter of 2026, down 41.3% from the prior quarter and 40.2% year on year. It has declined for three consecutive quarters since peaking at KRW 120.1 billion in the fourth quarter of 2025.

Operating profit in the same quarter rose 1.1% year on year to KRW 149.7 billion, so the gap between operating and net profit widened.

Food cost and currency pressure

In a November 2026 report, Hana Securities said high fish prices in the third quarter raised cost burdens at the food subsidiary. A Deal Site report cited the high exchange rate and intensifying promotional competition in retail channels as drags on food margins.

Press coverage said the food subsidiary's operating profit fell sharply in the third quarter alone, even though its nine-month figure grew.

Concerns over a possible HMM acquisition

Hana Securities noted in a February 2026 report that a possible HMM acquisition, reported in the press, has been weighing on the share price. The same report estimated HMM's enterprise value at about KRW 10 trillion and said at least KRW 5 trillion in funding would be needed. It also questioned whether the dividend policy could be carried out if borrowings rise.

10

Risk factors

Raw materials and currency

A high exchange rate and rising prices for aluminum and naphtha are pressing costs in both food and packaging. In a February 2026 report, Hana Securities noted that packaging sales at Dongwon Systems are affected by weak total liquor demand.

How much of the price increases at StarKist offset cost inflation can only be confirmed through quarterly results.

Fishing and tariffs

In a May 2026 press report, Heungkuk Securities cited fishing downturns and fish price volatility as burdens on the marine business. Tariff effects on StarKist's Ecuador plant were flagged as a variable. In a November 2026 report, Hana Securities said standalone results were weak because catches declined.

Governance and M&A

If the HMM acquisition advances, the funding required would be large and could affect borrowing capacity and dividend room. Hana Securities said in a November 2026 report that a sustained share price recovery would require visible shareholder return measures and a clearer overseas expansion plan. Whether the company formalizes M&A plans will need to be confirmed through disclosures.

Overall view

Filed results for 2025 show revenue of KRW 9.58 trillion and operating profit of KRW 516.1 billion, with both sales and core profit growing together. Profit attributable to controlling shareholders recovered from KRW 75.3 billion in 2024 to KRW 367 billion in 2025.

However, sales have risen for three straight quarters through the third quarter of 2026 while controlling-shareholder profit has fallen for three straight quarters, so the profit trend is mixed.

In the food business, US exports and seasoning sauces are growing, but high exchange rates and elevated fish prices are weighing on costs. The non-food units are showing customer gains and margin improvement in logistics and construction.

On valuation, the price-to-book ratio sits below 1x and the company has committed to a higher payout ratio, but a possible HMM acquisition and higher borrowings are cited as variables for carrying that out.

Brokers' views also diverge, with Hana Securities setting a KRW 60,000 target in November 2026 and tying a sustained recovery to visible shareholder returns and overseas expansion. The next confirmation points are the fourth-quarter 2026 results and the dividend decision in the 2026 annual business report.
