02

Business structure

SV Investment is a venture capital company established in April 2006 that forms investment partnerships and invests in founders and small and mid-sized ventures. It earns management fees as the operating general partner and aims to recover capital through secondary sales, M&A, or listings of portfolio companies.

Its funds are sourced from pension funds and financial institutions. On Alphasquare's breakdown, investment fund income accounts for about 69.10%, fund investment income about 30.20%, and other operating income about 0.70%.

Its investment areas span biotech and healthcare as well as ICT and cultural content, with life sciences and high-tech forming the core of the portfolio. According to tracxn, the firm had invested in 127 companies as of August 2026, and 35 of them have gone public.

Its strategy is to back a few leading companies in a given sector with large checks and take significant stakes as a second-largest shareholder. Assets under management grew from about KRW 530 billion in the first half of 2019 to about KRW 1.9 trillion at end-2025.

It is an independent venture capital firm, not part of a financial group or conglomerate. The firm has offices in Boston, Shanghai, and Singapore.

04

Earnings analysis

On an annual basis, revenue rose from KRW 25.4 billion in the March 2023 fiscal year to KRW 29.7 billion in the March 2024 fiscal year, a 17.3% increase and the largest year in the period. It then fell to KRW 21.1 billion in the March 2025 fiscal year, down 29.2% and the smallest year.

The March 2026 fiscal year revenue was KRW 21.4 billion, up 1.7%. Operating profit went from KRW 6.33 billion in the March 2024 fiscal year, up 39.6%, to an operating loss of KRW 6.05 billion in the March 2025 fiscal year, a swing into loss. In the March 2026 fiscal year the operating loss narrowed to KRW 2.72 billion.

The operating margin moved from 21.3% in the March 2024 fiscal year to -28.7% in the March 2025 fiscal year and -12.7% in the March 2026 fiscal year.

Net profit attributable to controlling shareholders was KRW 4.29 billion in the March 2024 fiscal year, then turned into a loss of KRW 5.23 billion in the March 2025 fiscal year and a loss of KRW 1 billion in the March 2026 fiscal year.

Net income including non-controlling interests followed the same direction, with a net loss of KRW 1 billion in the March 2026 fiscal year. Quarterly results ran in the opposite direction at points.

Revenue fell from KRW 6.51 billion in Q2 2025 to KRW 4.32 billion in Q3 2025, a 33.7% quarterly drop, then rose to KRW 4.59 billion in Q4 2025, KRW 6 billion in Q1 2026, and KRW 9.15 billion in Q2 2026.

Operating profit moved from an operating loss of KRW 266 million in Q3 2025 and KRW 1.44 billion in Q4 2025 to operating profit of KRW 325 million in Q1 2026 and KRW 1.57 billion in Q2 2026, up 382.8% from the prior quarter.

Net profit attributable to controlling shareholders was KRW 396 million in Q1 2026 and KRW 776 million in Q2 2026. Over the last four quarters, the combined net loss attributable to controlling shareholders was KRW 345 million.

Operating cash flow improved from -KRW 6.85 billion in the March 2023 fiscal year to -KRW 1.2 billion in the March 2026 fiscal year. The filed figures alone do not separate how much of the Q2 2026 result came from investment valuation gains versus management fees.

05

Industry analysis

Venture capital earnings depend heavily on exit timing, meaning listings and M&A of portfolio companies. The firm holds investments in biotech and deep-tech companies, so the rise in portfolio values and exit outcomes feed directly into results.

The firm's CEO has said venture capital performance should be judged over periods of seven to ten years or more. Among domestic players, the company is classed as an independent house, not part of a financial group or conglomerate. It is regarded as one of the most active domestic venture firms in overseas expansion.

Forming co-GP funds with overseas managers gives access to local deal flow and spreads risk. In AI semiconductors, expectations of a Rebellions listing were reported as a driver of the share price rise in January 2026. The company's view is that only some companies may survive a reshuffle driven by AI.

06

Outlook

The company's stated goal is to push assets under management above KRW 2 trillion. A January 2026 report projected that AUM would exceed KRW 2 trillion. The firm formed about KRW 174.1 billion in new funds in 2025, and in 2026 fundraising for the year already exceeded KRW 100 billion, according to press reports.

Additional government fund-of-funds allocations could expand the final fundraising total. For global funds, the firm was reported to be pursuing a KRW 50 billion open innovation fund as co-GP with an investment arm of a large Chinese medical device group.

A USD 10 million Web3.0 fund targeting Southeast Asia was set for formation in the second half of 2026. On exits, preparations for the listings of Rebellions and a global copper-foil materials company were mentioned. The timing of the Rebellions listing has not been fixed in reports. Whether the Q2 2026 turn to profit continues after Q3 will be checked in the next quarterly filing.

08

Bull factors

Q2 2026 turned profitable as revenue recovered

Q2 2026 revenue reached KRW 9.15 billion, up 40.4% from a year earlier, and operating profit was KRW 1.57 billion, a swing to profit from a year earlier. Revenue has risen for three consecutive quarters from Q4 2025 through Q2 2026, which supports the view of a quarterly recovery.

However, the filed figures alone do not separate how much came from investment valuation gains versus management fees.

Growing AUM and fund formation

Assets under management grew from about KRW 530 billion in the first half of 2019 to about KRW 1.9 trillion at end-2025. New fund formation was KRW 174.1 billion in 2025, and 2026 fundraising already exceeded KRW 100 billion by early 2026, according to press reports. Government fund-of-funds allocations could enlarge the final total.

Expected exits from large holdings

Rebellions, an AI semiconductor fabless company, received more than KRW 45 billion from the firm, and press reports covered expectations of its listing. A global copper-foil materials company was reported to be preparing a US listing.

Realized exits would feed directly into valuation gains and earnings volatility, which makes timing an important variable.

09

Bear factors

Two loss-making years and a cumulative net loss

The March 2025 fiscal year turned to an operating loss of KRW 6.05 billion, and the March 2026 fiscal year still posted an operating loss of KRW 2.72 billion. The net loss attributable to controlling shareholders narrowed to KRW 1 billion in the March 2026 fiscal year, but the loss continued.

Over the last four quarters, the combined net loss attributable to controlling shareholders was KRW 345 million.

Reliance on fund income and valuation swings

Most revenue comes from fund-related income and fund investment income. A research summary attributed the weak 2025 results mainly to lower fund income, lower management fees, and a smaller equity-method contribution from funds. Another analysis noted that higher valuation gains were offset by rising costs, leaving profitability weaker.

Operating cash outflows and rising leverage

Operating cash flow improved from -KRW 6.85 billion in the March 2023 fiscal year to -KRW 1.2 billion in the March 2026 fiscal year, but it remains negative. The debt ratio rose from 10.3% in the March 2023 fiscal year to 32.6% in the March 2026 fiscal year. At end-March 2026, total equity was KRW 69.9 billion and total liabilities KRW 22.8 billion.

10

Risk factors

Exit timing and listing schedules

Investment returns depend on when portfolio companies list or are sold. Press reports differ on the Rebellions investment size, with one citing about KRW 40 billion and another more than KRW 45 billion. Listing timing has not been fixed in reports, so any delay in exits would weigh on valuation gains and results.

Fund formation and management fee base

Management fees move with assets under management and the size of new funds. Lower management fees were cited as a drag on the 2025 results. The planned USD 10 million Web3.0 fund was targeted for formation in the second half of 2026.

Concentrated bets and industry reshuffling

Because the firm concentrates on a few top-notch companies, changes in individual company values feed heavily into valuation gains. The company itself has noted that AI-driven change could leave only some companies standing. Listing-based valuations can also shift with conditions in the IPO market.

Overall view

SV Investment is a venture capital firm that earns income from forming investment partnerships and exiting portfolio investments, with most revenue coming from fund-related income and fund investment income.

The March 2025 and March 2026 fiscal years both recorded operating and net losses, and the debt ratio rose from 10.3% to 32.6%. In contrast, Q2 2026 posted revenue of KRW 9.15 billion and operating profit of KRW 1.57 billion, a swing to profit, and revenue has risen for three consecutive quarters.

AUM of about KRW 1.9 trillion at end-2025 has a stated target above KRW 2 trillion, and the exit timing of holdings such as Rebellions is a key swing factor. Strengths include the quarterly recovery and expanding fund formation.

Weaknesses include volatility from valuation gains and exit timing, along with the cumulative net loss over the last four quarters.

The next checkpoints are the Q3 2026 quarterly report and the annual report for the March 2027 fiscal year, where continued operating profit and the share of fund income can be checked together.
