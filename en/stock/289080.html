02

Business structure

SV Investment is a Korean venture capital firm founded in April 2006 that forms and manages investment partnerships, earning management and performance fees along with valuation and disposal gains on portfolio companies.

The company says it supports portfolio firms in strategy, operations and finance, and realizes returns through value creation and recovery of invested capital.

According to AlphaSquare's aggregation, investment partnership income accounts for about 69% of revenue, operating investment income about 30%, and other operating income about 1%, with partnership income the largest component.

Bloter reported in June 2025 that management fees for the year ended March 2025 declined slightly to KRW 15.7 billion, and no performance fees were paid for a second consecutive year. Assets under management reached KRW 1.9025 trillion at end-2025 and have risen each year since 2020 in the reported series.

The Bell reported in January 2026 that new fund formation in 2025 totaled KRW 174.1 billion, roughly four times the KRW 46.5 billion of the prior year. Its focus areas are biotech, healthcare and deep tech, and its U.S. arm plans a bio-focused portfolio.

Its cited exits include an AI chip designer and a cybersecurity firm now in the KOSDAQ listing process. thevc.kr lists 38 employees as of September 2026. DSC Investment and LB Investment are separate listed companies with similar names.

04

Earnings analysis

Annual revenue rose from KRW 25.4 billion in the year ended March 2023 to KRW 29.7 billion in the year ended March 2024, an increase of 17.3% that made that year the largest of the four.

Revenue fell 29.2% to KRW 21.1 billion in the year ended March 2025, the smallest year, and reached KRW 21.4 billion in the year ended March 2026, up 1.7%.

Operating profit climbed from KRW 4.54 billion to KRW 6.33 billion in the year ended March 2024, a 39.6% increase, before the year ended March 2025 turned into an operating loss of KRW 6.05 billion. The loss narrowed to KRW 2.72 billion in the year ended March 2026.

The operating margin moved from 17.9% and 21.3% in the first two years to -28.7% and -12.7% in the last two.

Controlling-shareholder net income went from KRW 4.29 billion in the year ended March 2024 to a net loss of KRW 5.23 billion in the year ended March 2025 and a net loss of KRW 1 billion in the year ended March 2026.

Bloter attributed the year ended March 2025 swing to two consecutive years without performance fees, lower management fees and larger equity-method valuation losses, while the company pointed to fair-value declines.

Operating cash flow was negative in all four years, at KRW -6.85 billion, KRW -8.19 billion, KRW -1.51 billion and KRW -1.2 billion.

Quarterly, revenue of KRW 6.51 billion and an operating loss of KRW 1.34 billion in Q2 2025 were followed by a low point in Q3 2025, with revenue of KRW 4.32 billion (down 33.7% from the prior quarter) and an operating loss of KRW 266 million.

Q4 2025 brought an operating loss of KRW 1.44 billion, and Q1 2026 returned to operating profit of KRW 325 million on revenue of KRW 6 billion.

Q2 2026 revenue reached KRW 9.15 billion, up 52.5% from the prior quarter and 40.4% from a year earlier, and operating profit reached KRW 1.57 billion, up 382.8% from the prior quarter. Controlling-shareholder net income in Q2 2026 was KRW 776 million.

The combined controlling-shareholder net loss for the last four quarters, from Q3 2025 to Q2 2026, was KRW 345 million.

The causes behind the quarterly swings were not confirmed in filings or reliable press reports in this review, so the role of performance fee recognition and fund valuation gains should be checked in the Q3 2026 quarterly report.

Industry analysis

Venture capital earnings depend heavily on the exit market and the pace of technology listings. In an interview with The Bell, CEO Hong Won-ho said 2026 is the year the firm must prove its exit results through listings and M&A.

The same interview said AI-driven change is likely to leave only some companies standing, and the firm will focus on selecting top-notch companies carefully. A press headline on thevc.kr said Korean VC shares moved with expectations of AI and deep-tech exits.

AlphaSquare's comparison of 17 venture capital companies shows SV Investment's operating margin for the year ended March 2026 in the lower ranks of the sector, and its dividend yield below the sector average. Its price-to-book ratio, by contrast, ranks near the top of the sector and sits above the sector average.

Because the aggregation dates and methods differ across platforms, these comparisons are better read as direction than as precise levels. Assets under management stand at KRW 1.9025 trillion, but this review did not confirm where that ranks against peers.

Conditions in the IPO market and venture fund inflows were not confirmed with figures, so this section remains qualitative.

Bull factors

Quarterly Turnaround and Rising Revenue

Operating profit reached KRW 1.57 billion in Q2 2026, turning positive from a year-earlier operating loss. Revenue has risen for three consecutive quarters since Q4 2025 to KRW 9.15 billion.

Operating profit of KRW 325 million in Q1 2026 was followed by a second profitable quarter, which is the main bullish point in the quarterly trend.

Exit Expectations Tied to a Cybersecurity Listing

A November 2026 report estimated the stake in a cybersecurity portfolio company at KRW 26.3 billion to KRW 33.3 billion on the indicative offering range, against KRW 7.1 billion invested. The report said the listing could ease uncertainty about returns for limited partners.

Actual realized proceeds will depend on post-listing share prices, the timing of sales and the lock-up commitment ratio.

AUM Growth and Fundraising Momentum

Assets under management reached KRW 1.9025 trillion at end-2025 and have risen each year since 2020 in the reported series. The Bell reported that new fund formation in 2025 totaled KRW 174.1 billion, about four times the KRW 46.5 billion of the prior year.

With the company targeting above KRW 2 trillion in AUM in 2026, a larger base could widen the management fee foundation.

Bear factors

Two Straight Loss Years and No Performance Fees

Operating losses were KRW 6.05 billion in the year ended March 2025 and KRW 2.72 billion in the year ended March 2026, two consecutive loss years. Bloter reported that no performance fees were paid for two straight years through the year ended March 2025.

The operating margin of -12.7% for the year ended March 2026 ranks in the lower part of the sector, which is the main bearish point.

Fund Maturities and Pressure on Management Fees

A 2025 report said six funds maturing over the following two years held roughly KRW 350 billion in assets. The report noted that concern existed that shrinking AUM from maturities would reduce management fees.

The company is responding with new fund formation, but the results of the fund closings targeted for end-March 2026 were not confirmed in this review.

Valuation Swings and Growing Pains in Some Units

Equity-method valuation losses widened in the year ended March 2025, and the company attributed the profit decline to lower fair values. CEO Hong Won-ho said the PEF and global units went through growing pains in 2025.

Because the Q2 2026 turnaround could also depend on valuation gains, the durability of quarterly profits needs further confirmation.

10

Risk factors

Valuation and Exit Timing Risk

Earnings are directly affected by fair-value changes in portfolio assets. If listings are delayed or M&A fails, exit timing shifts and valuation gains may shrink. For the cybersecurity listing, post-listing overhang concerns and the lock-up commitment ratio have been cited as issues.

Fund Maturity and Management Fee Risk

Many funds are reaching maturity, which could shrink the management fee base. Management fees for the year ended March 2025 declined slightly to KRW 15.7 billion. If new fund formation falls short of targets, AUM declines could accumulate into lower management fee income.

Performance Fee Recovery and Market Volatility

No performance fees were paid for two straight years, and their return depends on exit results. Press coverage shows venture capital shares moving together with AI and deep-tech themes, which adds volatility. If growing pains in the global unit persist, the earnings base from overseas funds could stay limited.

Overall view

SV Investment showed quarterly improvement, with operating profit turning positive in Q2 2026 and revenue rising for three consecutive quarters.

However, both the year ended March 2025 and the year ended March 2026 recorded operating losses and controlling-shareholder net losses, so annual earnings power is not yet in a clear recovery.

Total equity of KRW 69.9 billion at end-March 2026 is entirely attributable to controlling shareholders, and the debt ratio of 32.6% suggests limited financial strain. AUM growth to KRW 1.9025 trillion and expanded fund formation could widen the management fee base.

On the other hand, no performance fees were paid for two straight years, and concern about AUM shrinkage from fund maturities remains, so earnings volatility stays high. Whether portfolio exits such as the cybersecurity listing materialize is the key variable for a return of performance fees and valuation gains.

The P/E cannot be calculated, and the price-to-book ratio sits above the sector average, so whether the market is pricing in exit potential beyond book value will be shown by listing and realization outcomes. The next checkpoints are the Q3 2026 quarterly report and the annual report for the year ending March 2027.
