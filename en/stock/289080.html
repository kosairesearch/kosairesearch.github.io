02

Business structure

SV Investment is an independent venture capital firm founded in 2006 and listed on KOSDAQ in 2018.

It raises capital from limited partners such as pension funds and financial institutions to form venture investment funds, and it takes a second-largest shareholder position in portfolio companies while providing strategic and financial support.

Fund management fees and fund-level investment gains form the core of its income. According to Alphasquare's breakdown as of early November 2026, fund investment income accounted for 69.10% of revenue, investment income from operations for 30.20%, and other operating income for 0.70%.

Assets under management rose from KRW 1.1586 trillion in 2020 to KRW 1.9025 trillion in 2025. Press reports in early 2026 said fundraising for the year had passed KRW 100 billion, roughly four times the KRW 46.5 billion of the prior year.

The portfolio includes AI semiconductor fabless company Rebellions, semiconductor design house Semifive, and a global copper-foil materials maker. Press reports said the company invested more than KRW 40 billion in Rebellions, the largest amount among domestic venture capital firms.

It has subsidiaries in Boston, Shanghai and Shenzhen, and Singapore. The co-CEOs are Park Sung-ho and Hong Won-ho. Revenue is structurally tied to exit timing and valuation of portfolio companies, so it can vary widely from quarter to quarter.

Earnings analysis

Annual revenue was 254 hundred million won for the fiscal year ended March 2023, 297 hundred million won for the fiscal year ended March 2024 (up 17.3% year on year), 211 hundred million won for the fiscal year ended March 2025 (down 29.2% year on year), and 214 hundred million won for the fiscal year ended March 2026 (up 1.7% year on year).

The largest year in the period was the fiscal year ended March 2024, and the smallest was the fiscal year ended March 2025.

Operating profit rose to 45.4 hundred million won for the fiscal year ended March 2023 and 63.3 hundred million won for the fiscal year ended March 2024 (up 39.6% year on year), but it swung to an operating loss of 60.5 hundred million won in the fiscal year ended March 2025.

The operating loss for the fiscal year ended March 2026 was 27.2 hundred million won, so the losses continued, but the size of the loss narrowed. The operating margin moved as 17.9%, 21.3%, -28.7%, and -12.7%.

Net profit attributable to owners of the parent was 42.9 hundred million won for the fiscal year ended March 2024, turned into a net loss attributable to owners of the parent of 52.3 hundred million won for the fiscal year ended March 2025, and then narrowed to a net loss attributable to owners of the parent of 10 hundred million won for the fiscal year ended March 2026.

Net income including non-controlling interests for the same years was 34.5 hundred million won, 42.9 hundred million won, a net loss of 52.3 hundred million won, and a net loss of 10 hundred million won.

Cash flow from operating activities was negative in all four fiscal years, from the fiscal year ended March 2023 through the fiscal year ended March 2026.

The debt-to-equity ratio rose from 10.3% to 32.6%, and as of the end of March 2026, total equity was 699 hundred million won and total liabilities were 228 hundred million won.

For the fiscal 2025 results, an external analysis site cited decreases in investment partnership income, decreases in partnership management fees, and a reduction in equity-method gains from partnerships as the main causes.

On a quarterly basis, revenue increased for four consecutive quarters, from 43.2 hundred million won in Q3 2025 to 45.9 hundred million won in Q4 2025, 60 hundred million won in Q1 2026, 91.5 hundred million won in Q2 2026, and 97 hundred million won in Q3 2026.

Operating profit went from an operating loss of 14.4 hundred million won in Q4 2025 to a return to profit with an operating profit of 3.3 hundred million won in Q1 2026, and continued with 15.7 hundred million won in Q2 and 11 hundred million won in Q3 (down 29.8% quarter on quarter).

The sum of net profit attributable to owners of the parent over the latest four quarters (Q4 2025 to Q3 2026) is 6.4 hundred million won. The swings in quarterly earnings stem from a structure in which partnership income moves with the timing of investment valuations and exits.

Industry analysis

Venture capital firms are sensitive to fund formation by limited partners and to the pace of IPOs and M&A among portfolio companies. AI semiconductors drew sector-wide attention in 2026, and SV Investment holds positions in fabless chip designer Rebellions and semiconductor design house Semifive.

In an interview in early 2026, the co-CEO said rapid AI-driven change would likely leave only a few companies standing. The same interview framed 2026 as the year the firm must prove results through exits.

In a sector comparison of 17 listed venture capital firms, SV Investment ranked in the lower group on sales and operating profit, while its price-to-book ratio ranked near the top. That means valuations sit high relative to reported earnings, so changes in portfolio valuations weigh heavily on the share price.

Press reports that its Rebellions investment is the largest among domestic venture capital firms also point to concentration in a single holding. As an independent firm not owned by a financial group or conglomerate, it competes for fund formation and anchor investors on its own strength.

Its expansion in global fundraising ties it to overseas investor demand, which should be read alongside sector trends.

Outlook

The company's stated goal is to lift assets under management to KRW 2 trillion or more. From the KRW 1.9025 trillion level at end-2025, roughly KRW 97.5 billion remains to reach KRW 2 trillion.

Press reports in early 2026 said fundraising for the year had passed KRW 100 billion, and the final amount could grow with support from government fund-of-funds and other allocation programs. On exits, management expects results from IPOs and M&A of portfolio companies.

Rebellions and a global copper-foil materials maker were cited as candidates, and the copper-foil company was reported to be preparing a US listing in 2026. US fundraising is reported to run alongside a plan to build a portfolio centered on biotech companies.

Items to confirm are whether AUM reaches KRW 2 trillion by end-2026 and whether an IPO or M&A involving Rebellions or the copper-foil company is disclosed. Whether revenue growth and operating profit direction continue in the fourth quarter of 2026 also needs to be checked.

Bull factors

Quarterly earnings recovery

Sales rose for four consecutive quarters from the fourth quarter of 2025 through the third quarter of 2026. Operating profit was positive in each of the first three quarters of 2026, at KRW 1.57 billion in the second quarter and KRW 1.1 billion in the third. The shift from quarterly losses to profits lines up with the narrowing of the annual loss.

AUM growth and fundraising

Assets under management grew steadily from KRW 1.1586 trillion in 2020 to KRW 1.9025 trillion in 2025. Press reports said 2026 fundraising passed KRW 100 billion, about four times the KRW 46.5 billion of the prior year. A larger asset base widens the fee base for fund management and gives the revenue model more scale.

Expected exits from large holdings

The size of the Rebellions investment, the largest among domestic venture capital firms, means a successful listing would weigh heavily on exit results. The company set exits through IPOs and M&A as a key task for 2026. Reports also cited a case in which a KRW 1.5 billion stake from 2019 grew to about KRW 9.9 billion in value.

Bear factors

Annual losses and negative operating cash flow

The company posted operating losses in the fiscal years ended March 2025 and March 2026. The operating margin improved from -28.7% to -12.7% but remains negative. Cash from operations was negative throughout the four fiscal years, so it is not confirmed that the profit improvement has translated into cash inflow. The debt ratio rose from 10.3% to 32.6%.

Volatile quarterly results

The fourth quarter of 2025 recorded an operating loss of KRW 1.44 billion and a net loss attributable to controlling shareholders of KRW 1.2 billion. Third-quarter 2026 operating profit fell 29.8% from the prior quarter.

Net income attributable to controlling shareholders over the last four quarters totaled KRW 638 million, so whether quarterly profits persist still needs confirmation.

Concentration and share count growth

Because the Rebellions stake is among the largest in the sector, a delay in its listing or a revaluation could have an outsized effect on results. Records show that convertible bond conversions added listed shares in May 2026.

Risk factors

Portfolio valuation and exit timing

Revenue and profit depend on the valuation of portfolio companies and on the timing of IPOs and M&A. If listings are delayed or valuations are marked down, quarterly results could return to losses. Whether the stated exit plans turn into actual disclosures remains to be seen.

Fund formation and management fees

Outside analysis attributed the 2025 weakness mainly to lower fund investment income, lower management fees, and a smaller equity-method gain from funds. If fund formation slows or the fee base weakens, fund-related income, which makes up most of revenue, could fall as well.

Major shareholder stake and liquidity

Park Sung-ho and two related parties hold 24.78%, a relatively high major-shareholder stake. The free float is reported at about 75.22%. A high insider stake can affect trading liquidity and the drivers of share price movement.

Overall view

SV Investment recorded annual losses in the two fiscal years after March 2025, but quarterly sales rose for four consecutive quarters through 2026, and operating profit stayed positive from the first to the third quarter of 2026.

The annual operating loss narrowed to KRW 2.72 billion for the year ended March 2026, and the net loss attributable to controlling shareholders shrank to KRW 1 billion.

Operating cash flow, however, was negative throughout the disclosed period, and net income attributable to controlling shareholders over the last four quarters totaled only KRW 638 million, so the earnings base remains thin.

Assets under management reached KRW 1.9025 trillion and the company has targeted KRW 2 trillion, while whether portfolio exits such as Rebellions convert into results is the biggest variable. Bullish factors include the quarterly recovery, asset growth, and expected exits from large holdings.

Bearish factors include the annual losses, negative cash flow, concentration in a few holdings, and volatility in fund income. Both sets of factors deserve equal weight, and valuation readings will shift with changes in portfolio valuations.

The next checkpoints are the fourth-quarter 2026 results, the annual report for the year ending March 2027, and disclosures on IPOs or M&A.
