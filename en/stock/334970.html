02

Business structure

Prestige Biologics is a biologics CDMO company with a total of 154,000 liters of antibody drug production capacity at its Osong Biovalley campus in North Chungcheong Province.

Through its proprietary ALITA Smart BioFactory platform, the company offers a one-stop service spanning drug substance (DS) and drug product (DP) manufacturing as well as analytical testing, flexibly adapting to client needs from clinical to commercial stages.

The company holds GMP certifications from Korea's Ministry of Food and Drug Safety (MFDS) and the European Medicines Agency (EMA). Its controlling shareholder is parent company Prestige BioPharma, which holds a 40.03% stake, while Chairwoman So Yeon Park holds 10.04% and CEO Jinwoo Kim holds 9.92%.

The revenue base has historically depended heavily on manufacturing volumes for parent Prestige BioPharma's biosimilar pipeline, but the company has recently been broadening its client roster to include Teva, Asian big pharma, a Japanese pharmaceutical company, and domestic biosimilar makers.

Revenue is composed of bulk drug substance sales tied to the parent's approved products, development service revenue from contract development (CDO), and packaging and analytical service fees.

In May 2026 the company signed its first CDMO production contract with a Japanese pharmaceutical company, becoming the first among domestic CDMO players to be selected as a production partner in Japan.

Its core commercial approach is the "Scale-Bridge CDMO" strategy, which links clinical-stage production experience to subsequent clinical and commercial manufacturing contracts. The company's fiscal year ends in June.

04

Earnings analysis

Annual revenue rose from KRW 15.57 million in 2022 to KRW 1.73 billion in 2023 (up 11,015.9% year-on-year), KRW 2.17 billion in 2024 (up 25.4%), and KRW 12.5 billion in 2025 (up 477.8%), marking three consecutive years of growth from 2023 through 2025.

Operating profit, however, stayed negative for four straight years from 2022 to 2025: an operating loss of KRW 36.6 billion in 2022, KRW 22.1 billion in 2023 (loss continued but narrowed), KRW 33.4 billion in 2024 (loss continued and widened), and KRW 33.7 billion in 2025 (loss continued and widened).

Controlling shareholders' net loss also persisted for four consecutive years, from KRW 49.7 billion in 2022 to KRW 18 billion in 2023 (narrowed), KRW 29.4 billion in 2024 (widened), and KRW 27.5 billion in 2025 (narrowed).

The operating margin moved from -235,333.1% in 2022 to -268.7% in 2025, a pattern reflecting the extremely small initial revenue base rather than a deterioration, with the ratio moderating substantially once the revenue base expanded in 2025.

On a quarterly basis, revenue rose from KRW 1.17 billion in Q1 2025 to KRW 8.9 billion in Q2, up 662.0% quarter-on-quarter, before coming in at KRW 5.69 billion in Q4.

The quarterly operating loss widened from KRW 1.88 billion in Q4 2024 to KRW 13.6 billion in Q1 2025, then narrowed for three straight quarters to KRW 9.74 billion, KRW 6.16 billion, and KRW 4.19 billion in Q2, Q3, and Q4 2025, respectively.

Controlling shareholders' net loss was KRW 5.04 billion in Q4 2024, widened to KRW 12.9 billion in Q1 2025, then narrowed to KRW 8.28 billion and KRW 6.69 billion in Q2 and Q3, before turning to a net profit of KRW 438 million in Q4, a swing from both the prior quarter and the year-earlier quarter.

Still, the trailing four quarters from Q1 to Q4 2025 posted a cumulative controlling shareholders' net loss of KRW 27.5 billion, indicating that a single profitable quarter has not yet altered the annual loss pattern.

05

Industry analysis

Korea's biologics CDMO industry is led by large players such as Samsung Biologics and Celltrion, which command overwhelming production capacity, while smaller companies carve out positions in specific client segments or niche areas.

Prestige Biologics built its business around manufacturing for parent Prestige BioPharma's biosimilar pipeline and has recently been diversifying its client base to include Teva, Asian big pharma, and a Japanese pharmaceutical company.

In Europe, the Herceptin biosimilar market has reportedly grown to surpass the originator drug and now accounts for more than half of a roughly KRW 5 trillion total market, suggesting structurally expanding demand for biosimilar CDMO services.

Some observers note that supply-chain realignment following the enactment of the U.S. BIOSECURE Act, aimed at reducing reliance on Chinese CDMOs, has created a favorable environment for Korean CDMO firms.

At the same time, some in the industry have pointed out that regulatory risks such as drug price cuts are holding back the growth of domestic CDMOs despite their globally competitive potential.

Prestige Biologics holds 154,000 liters of production infrastructure along with MFDS and EMA GMP certifications, but raising utilization rates and securing new clients remain key to improving profitability.

Its relatively small scale compared with larger rivals and its dependence on the parent company for revenue are challenges it must overcome to establish itself as a fully independent CDMO.

Outlook

The company ran a dedicated booth at Bio Korea 2026 in April, promoting global partnering based on its 154,000-liter production infrastructure and ALITA Smart BioFactory.

The following month it attended Interphex Week Tokyo 2026 to discuss cooperation with Japanese and Asian pharma and biotech firms, around which time it signed its first CDMO production contract with a Japanese global pharmaceutical company.

In June 2026, it secured a follow-on order from a large domestic biosimilar company for clinical sample production running through the end of March 2027, opening a path toward expanded commercial production cooperation.

The company stated that its cumulative order value reached about KRW 65 billion as of the end of March 2026 and said it had entered a stage of converting the order backlog into actual production and revenue.

For fiscal year 2026 (July 2025 to June 2026) as a whole, it signed six new CDMO contracts worth a combined approximately KRW 41.2 billion, and announced that fourth-quarter (April–June 2026) revenue reached KRW 11.47 billion, exceeding KRW 10 billion in a single quarter for the first time (announced August 19, 2026, preliminary).

In the same release, annual revenue was reported at KRW 23.81 billion, up 90.0% year-on-year (preliminary).

However, an audit report filed on September 18, 2026 showed the operating loss for that fiscal year at KRW 49.3 billion, an even wider loss, and despite an unqualified audit opinion, it also disclosed uncertainty regarding the company's ability to continue as a going concern.

The company decided to increase short-term borrowings by KRW 15 billion for working capital in April 2026 and, in September 2026, extended the maturity of its KRW 45.5 billion third-series convertible bonds by one year as part of its liquidity management.

Bull factors

Diversifying CDMO Clients and a Growing Order Backlog

Prestige Biologics is moving away from a revenue structure centered on parent Prestige BioPharma by broadening its contract counterparties to include Teva, Asian big pharma, and a Japanese pharmaceutical company.

Its cumulative order value reached about KRW 65 billion as of the end of March 2026, up steadily from KRW 19.3 billion in March 2025. The first CDMO contract with a Japanese pharmaceutical company is notable as it could serve as a reference for future global order wins. Continued diversification of orders would be expected to reduce the company's reliance on its parent.

Revenue Growth Momentum and Loss-Narrowing Quarters

Revenue grew for three consecutive years from 2022 to 2025, rising 477.8% year-on-year in 2025. The quarterly operating loss narrowed sequentially from Q1 2025 through Q4, and controlling shareholders' net income turned positive in Q4.

According to preliminary figures, fourth-quarter revenue for fiscal year 2026 exceeded KRW 10 billion for the first time in a single quarter. If the trend of order volumes converting into production and revenue continues, there is room for quarterly earnings volatility to gradually ease.

Production Competitiveness Based on Facilities and Certifications

The company holds a total of 154,000 liters of production infrastructure at its Osong campus in North Chungcheong Province and has secured GMP certifications from both the Ministry of Food and Drug Safety and the European Medicines Agency.

Its proprietary ALITA Smart BioFactory is designed to flexibly handle everything from clinical-stage to commercial-stage production. Accumulated experience responding to global regulatory inspections underpins confidence in its quality management capabilities. This infrastructure serves as a foundation for attracting new clients.

Bear factors

Going-Concern Uncertainty and Capital Erosion Concerns

An audit report filed in September 2026 retained an unqualified opinion but newly flagged uncertainty about the company's ability to continue as a going concern. The operating loss for that fiscal year was reported at KRW 49.3 billion, a sharp widening from KRW 33.7 billion in the prior year.

Total equity was also reported to have fallen from KRW 135.2 billion in the prior year to about KRW 90.5 billion. Whether the financial structure deteriorates further needs to be monitored through subsequent disclosures.

Revenue Structure Dependent on the Parent Company

Prestige Biologics' revenue has long depended heavily on CMO contracts with parent Prestige BioPharma. While client diversification has been progressing recently, a significant portion of new contracts has yet to fully convert into revenue.

The license agreement with the affiliated parent company has been cited as providing a stable business environment while also limiting profitability through cost burdens. A revenue structure concentrated on a specific customer can heighten sensitivity to external variables.

Frequent External Fundraising and Financial Strain

The company has repeatedly raised debt-repayment funds in the past through third-party allotment rights offerings to its parent company.

In April 2026 it decided to increase short-term borrowings by KRW 15 billion for working capital, and in September it extended the maturity of KRW 45.5 billion in convertible bonds by one year. The recurring use of such financing tools illustrates the difficulty of covering funding needs through operations alone. Operating cash flow has also remained negative every year from 2022 through 2025.

Risk factors

Financial Risk

An audit report filed in September 2026 disclosed uncertainty regarding going-concern status, with the operating loss widening to KRW 49.3 billion. If equity continues to shrink while liabilities grow, further capital raising or borrowing may become unavoidable.

The convertible bond maturity extension and increase in short-term borrowings ease near-term liquidity pressure, but financial strain could recur without a fundamental improvement in profitability.

Customer Concentration Risk

A substantial portion of revenue still stems from contracts tied to parent Prestige BioPharma, leaving customer concentration relatively high. Many new client contracts remain at early stages such as clinical sample production, and the possibility that they do not convert into commercial production cannot be ruled out. Termination or delay of a single contract could increase quarter-to-quarter earnings volatility.

Regulatory and Competitive Risk

Some industry participants note that despite strong potential, Korea's CDMO sector could see profitability constrained by regulatory conditions such as drug price cuts.

As capacity and price competition with large global CDMOs intensifies, a slower pace of utilization improvement relative to peers could widen the profitability gap.

Variables in the parent company's pipeline, such as delays in biosimilar approvals or distribution partnerships, could also indirectly affect production volumes.

Overall view

Prestige Biologics has begun to show tangible results from its CDMO order expansion, with revenue growing steadily from 2022 through 2025 and controlling shareholders' net income briefly turning positive in Q4 2025.

At the same time, operating profit has remained negative for four consecutive years, with the 2025 operating loss widening to KRW 33.7 billion from the prior year, and the trailing four quarters still showing a cumulative controlling shareholders' net loss of KRW 27.5 billion.

An audit report filed in September 2026 carried an unqualified opinion but newly disclosed uncertainty about the company's ability to continue as a going concern, heightening caution around its financial stability.

The order backlog grew from KRW 19.3 billion in March 2025 to about KRW 65 billion in March 2026, with visible client diversification into Japan and Asian big pharma, but a significant share of new contracts remains at an early stage that has not yet fully converted into revenue.

Amid ongoing liquidity measures such as the convertible bond maturity extension and the increase in short-term borrowings, the pace of order-to-revenue conversion and whether the going-concern uncertainty is resolved remain the key variables for gauging future performance and financial structure.

Readers should track upcoming quarterly reports and further disclosures to verify for themselves whether these trends persist.
