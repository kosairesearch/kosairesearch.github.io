02

Business structure

Prestige Biologics is a CDMO focused on antibody drug manufacturing, operating a 154,000-liter production complex in Osong Bio Valley, Chungbuk.

The complex has a 34,000-liter first campus and a 120,000-liter second campus, and press reports say it holds GMP certifications from Korea's MFDS and the European Medicines Agency (EMA). Services extend beyond manufacturing into contract development (CDO) and clinical packaging (CPO).

Press reports citing the business report, dated September 2026, split fiscal year ended June 2026 revenue of KRW 26.7 billion into KRW 14.5 billion of product sales and KRW 12.2 billion of other sales.

Known customers include Celltrion and Alteogen, along with a global pharmaceutical company that signed a long-term contract in 2025. In May 2026 the company said it had signed its first CDMO contract with a Japanese global pharmaceutical company.

Most counterparties and key terms remain undisclosed, so customer-level revenue shares cannot be confirmed. On its own pipeline, the trastuzumab biosimilar Tuznue (HD201) is reported to be receiving ongoing orders.

Compared with large domestic CDMO players such as Samsung Biologics and Celltrion, its production scale is smaller.

04

Earnings analysis

On a disclosed-results basis, revenue has grown quickly while the operating and net losses have grown alongside it. Revenue was KRW 1.73 billion in the fiscal year ended June 2023, KRW 2.17 billion in June 2024 (up 25.4%), KRW 12.5 billion in June 2025 (up 477.8%), and KRW 26.7 billion in June 2026 (up 112.8%).

Sales have risen for three straight fiscal years since June 2024, and the fiscal year ended June 2026 is the largest of the period.

The operating loss was KRW 22.1 billion, KRW 33.4 billion, KRW 33.7 billion and KRW 49.3 billion, marking four straight fiscal years of losses, with the fiscal year ended June 2026 the largest loss.

The operating margin improved from -268.7% in June 2025 to -184.9% in June 2026, because revenue growth outpaced the rise in losses.

The net loss attributable to controlling shareholders was KRW 18 billion, KRW 29.4 billion, KRW 27.5 billion and KRW 51.2 billion, narrowing in June 2025 before widening again in June 2026.

Operating cash flow moved from -KRW 4.45 billion, -KRW 8.35 billion and -KRW 18.3 billion to KRW 3.2 billion in the fiscal year ended June 2026.

Press reports citing the business report say cost of sales rose from KRW 28.2 billion to KRW 46.0 billion and selling and administrative expenses from KRW 18.0 billion to KRW 29.9 billion, while depreciation booked in cost of sales went from KRW 6.0 billion to KRW 12.0 billion.

Quarterly, the operating loss has persisted for five straight quarters from Q2 2025 through Q2 2026, and the Q2 2026 operating loss of KRW 9.64 billion was larger than the KRW 4.19 billion a year earlier.

Some press reports gave different April-June 2026 revenue figures, so this analysis uses only the regular-report values. The company attributed the widening loss to inventory and convertible bond (CB) related charges and impairment of some production facilities, and said the cash impact is limited.

05

Industry analysis

Korea's biopharmaceutical CDMO market is organized around large production players such as Celltrion and Samsung Biologics, where competition centers on production scale.

Prestige Biologics' 154,000-liter capacity is smaller than those of the largest domestic expansion players, but the company promotes an end-to-end model that covers antibody clinical production through commercial supply.

In 2024 the company described how US biosecure legislation had led overseas clients that used Chinese CDMOs to look for alternatives, and said it aimed to convert that demand into orders.

Whether that demand has turned into signed contracts cannot be confirmed from disclosures, because counterparties remain undisclosed. In terms of cycle position, the company says orders secured since 2024 have moved into production, lifting utilization.

Press reports citing the business report, dated September 2026, show effective capacity expanding from 48 to 60 batches and actual production rising from 15 to 28 batches. Depreciation burdens have grown, however, so whether higher utilization will translate into better profitability has not yet been confirmed.

Relative to peers, the company is still building a track record with large clients, and no verified market share figure was found.

06

Outlook

No verified formal earnings guidance from the company was found in the search results. Instead, order backlog and production volume serve as the observable indicators.

Cumulative contract wins reached about KRW 58.0 billion as of 2025, and the company said in early 2026 that it had signed a KRW 4.3 billion manufacturing contract with a global pharmaceutical company.

A KRW 2.1 billion contract signed in November 2025 runs through December 31, 2026, which is also the end of the confidentiality deferral period. After the May 2026 Japanese contract, the company said it would pursue follow-on projects and expand its client portfolio in Japan.

Continued orders for its own trastuzumab biosimilar Tuznue are also cited as a growth factor. On production, a 2025 report said the first, second and fourth plants were expected to run for the new contracts.

However, the KRW 13.2 billion contract has unconfirmed raw material and shipping amounts, and the disclosure notes possible cancellation, so whether each contract converts into reported revenue needs quarterly confirmation.

With the debt burden higher after June 2026, how quickly order wins translate into revenue is the next checkpoint.

Bull factors

Scale of revenue growth

Q2 2026 revenue was KRW 14.3 billion, up 151.9% from a year earlier. The company said cumulative contract wins reached about KRW 58.0 billion as of 2025.

Rising production utilization

Press reports citing the business report say actual production in the fiscal year ended June 2026 rose to 28 batches from 15 a year earlier. Effective capacity expanded from 48 to 60 batches, and utilization rose from 31.3% to 46.7%. Bulls point to production volume flowing through to revenue as evidence of the model working.

Efforts to diversify clients

In November 2025 a KRW 2.1 billion first purchase order under a long-term contract with a global pharmaceutical company was disclosed. Whether the first contract with the Japanese client leads to follow-on orders is the next point to check. Press reports also cited six new contracts worth about KRW 41.2 billion during the fiscal year ended June 2026.

Risk factors

Contract volatility

Even large contract values are hard to track from outside, because counterparties and terms are undisclosed, making the timing of revenue recognition unclear. Quarterly revenue swings, such as the move from KRW 2.27 billion in Q1 2026 to KRW 14.3 billion in Q2 2026, reflect when specific projects enter production. Terminations or changes to contracts could widen quarterly swings further.

Financial burden

Total liabilities at the end of June 2026 were KRW 176.1 billion, and the debt ratio stood at 194.7%. The company cited convertible bond charges and inventory-related items as factors behind the widening loss. If operating losses continue, equity could shrink and financial flexibility could narrow.

Policy and client dependence

Expectations that Chinese CDMO volume will shift to Korean providers depend on policy developments such as the US biosecure legislation. A 2024 report cited the law as a possible tailwind for domestic firms, but no conversion into orders has been confirmed. If production concentrates on a few large clients, changes in their orders could quickly affect utilization.

Overall view

Prestige Biologics' disclosed results show revenue growth and wider losses arriving at the same time. Revenue in the fiscal year ended June 2026 reached KRW 26.7 billion and rose for three straight fiscal years, while Q2 2026 revenue of KRW 14.3 billion was up 151.9% year on year.

On the other side, the operating loss widened to KRW 49.3 billion and the net loss attributable to controlling shareholders to KRW 51.2 billion, and the four-quarter net loss also totals KRW 51.2 billion.

The operating margin improving from -268.7% to -184.9% reflects revenue growth outpacing the increase in losses, and operating cash flow turning positive is also confirmed.

Bullish factors include the larger contract base, higher utilization and overseas clients such as the Japanese partner, while bearish factors include depreciation and cost of sales burdens, a higher debt ratio and the possibility of changes to undisclosed contracts.

On valuation, with no earnings-based multiple available because of recent net losses, the market value sits close to total equity, and whether losses narrow remains the key variable.

The next checkpoint is whether the operating loss in the Q3 2026 quarterly report shrinks as production volume rises enough to absorb cost of sales.
