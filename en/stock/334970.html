02

Business structure

Prestige Biologics is a biopharmaceutical contract development and manufacturing organization (CDMO) founded in 2015 and listed on KOSDAQ in 2021. The company provides integrated manufacturing, process development and analytical testing of advanced biologics for domestic and overseas pharmaceutical customers.

Its production base is in Osong, Chungbuk, with a total capacity of 154,000 liters across Campus 1 (34,000 liters) and Campus 2 (120,000 liters).

According to the business report, operating revenue of KRW 26.7 billion in the fiscal year ended June 2026 consisted of KRW 14.5 billion in product sales and KRW 12.2 billion in other sales. JobKorea's financial analysis shows antibody drug sales at 52% and service sales at 48% as of March 2026.

Its key product is Tuznue (HD201), a Herceptin biosimilar developed by parent Prestige BioPharma, for which the company handles drug substance supply. Most orders still come through the parent, although the company has been widening its counterparties.

The company disclosed six new CDMO contracts totaling about KRW 41.2 billion with domestic and overseas clients during fiscal 2026. Competitively, its scale is well below large CDMOs such as Samsung Biologics and SK Bioscience. Its strategic partnership with Prestige BioPharma in Singapore is cited as a base for overseas expansion.

04

Earnings analysis

Annual revenue was KRW 1.73 billion in the fiscal year ended June 2023, KRW 2.17 billion in fiscal 2024 (up 25.4% year on year), KRW 12.5 billion in fiscal 2025 (up 477.8%), and KRW 26.7 billion in fiscal 2026 (up 112.8%).

Revenue has grown three years in a row from fiscal 2024 through fiscal 2026, and fiscal 2026 is the largest year in the fiscal 2023 to fiscal 2026 span.

Operating losses were KRW 22.1 billion, KRW 33.4 billion, KRW 33.7 billion and KRW 49.3 billion over the same years, a four-year run of losses, and because revenue grew faster than the loss the operating margin improved from -268.7% to -184.9%.

According to the business report, cost of sales rose from KRW 28.2 billion to KRW 46.0 billion and selling, general and administrative expenses rose from KRW 18.0 billion to KRW 29.9 billion.

R&D spending increased from KRW 2.6 billion to KRW 20.2 billion, and depreciation of tangible assets rose from KRW 12.9 billion to KRW 16.6 billion, of which the portion booked in cost of sales doubled from KRW 6.0 billion to KRW 12.0 billion.

The company attributes the widening quarterly loss to inventory, convertible bonds and impairment charges on some production facilities, and says the effect on actual cash flow is limited.

Net losses attributable to controlling shareholders were KRW 18 billion, KRW 29.4 billion, KRW 27.5 billion and KRW 51.2 billion over the four years, narrowing in fiscal 2025 before widening again in fiscal 2026, and the net loss including non-controlling interests is at the same level.

Operating cash flow moved from negative KRW 18.3 billion in fiscal 2025 to positive KRW 3.2 billion in fiscal 2026.

Quarterly revenue went from KRW 5.69 billion in the second quarter of 2025 to KRW 1.17 billion in the third quarter (down 79.5% from the prior quarter), KRW 8.9 billion in the fourth quarter (up 662.0%), KRW 2.27 billion in the first quarter of 2026 (down 74.5%) and KRW 14.3 billion in the second quarter of 2026 (up 531.1%).

The operating loss has been reported in each of the five quarters from the second quarter of 2025 through the second quarter of 2026, and the net loss attributable to controlling shareholders has been reported in each of the four quarters from the third quarter of 2025.

05

Industry analysis

Demand for CDMO services is being shaped by the reorganization of global supply chains. The company said it is in business discussions with overseas customers as outsourced manufacturing demand grows from that reorganization.

An analysis note on a corporate information service pointed to the expansion of the biosimilar market and rising outsourcing demand as growth opportunities. The same note said that large capital investment, process validation and regulatory approval require upfront costs that keep fixed-cost burdens high.

In cycle terms, utilization rose from 31.3% to 46.7%, so equipment use is increasing, but it remains below half of capacity. On policy, a CDMO special act has been under discussion, and as of a September 2025 report the bill had been referred to a subcommittee of the National Assembly's Health and Welfare Committee.

Media coverage has often named the company as the biggest beneficiary of such a law after the large CDMOs such as Samsung Biologics and SK Bioscience. Its strength is the link between development and manufacturing through its parent's pipeline, while its weaknesses are the pace of scale-up and customer diversification.

06

Outlook

The company said it is focusing resources on raising utilization of its production facilities and securing new customers. The new CDMO contracts signed during fiscal 2026 number six, worth about KRW 41.2 billion, and the company said that volume is moving sequentially into production and revenue.

Cumulative orders as of 2025 were about KRW 58 billion, and in early 2026 the company signed a KRW 4.3 billion manufacturing contract with a global pharmaceutical company.

In Europe, parent Prestige BioPharma signed a commercialization license for Tuznue with Teva, and the company has the role of producing and supplying that product. The company also said business discussions with overseas customers are ongoing.

The next checkpoint is how inventory, convertible bond and facility impairment items flow through profit and loss in coming quarters. With R&D spending up to KRW 20.2 billion, whether this cost level continues into the next fiscal year matters.

A early 2025 report pointed out that orders excluding parent-company volume were around KRW 9 billion, so the share of orders from outside customers is the next point to track.

08

Bull factors

Production turning into revenue

Revenue for the fiscal year ended June 2026 reached KRW 26.7 billion, up 112.8%, with product sales of KRW 14.5 billion and other sales of KRW 12.2 billion both rising. Actual production increased 86.7% to 28 batches and utilization climbed to 46.7%. Evidence that the facilities are generating actual revenue is the main bull point.

Expanding order book

The company said cumulative orders as of 2025 were about KRW 58 billion. During fiscal 2026 it signed six new CDMO contracts with domestic and overseas customers worth about KRW 41.2 billion. In early 2026 it also signed a KRW 4.3 billion manufacturing contract with a global pharmaceutical company. Whether these orders convert into revenue on production schedules is the core of the bull case.

Policy tailwind and cash flow turn

Media reports have described the company as likely the largest beneficiary of a CDMO special act if it passes, excluding the large CDMOs. Its parent holds a new drug pipeline, and a commercialization license for Tuznue in Europe was signed with Teva. Operating cash flow turned positive at KRW 3.2 billion in the fiscal year ended June 2026.

09

Bear factors

Cost and expense burden

Cost of sales for the fiscal year ended June 2026 was KRW 46.0 billion, above revenue of KRW 26.7 billion. Selling, general and administrative expenses rose to KRW 29.9 billion and the operating loss widened to KRW 49.3 billion. The rise in R&D spending to KRW 20.2 billion signals that cost pressure has not yet eased.

Parent dependence and customer concentration

Media reports have said most of the company's revenue relates to parent Prestige BioPharma, raising doubts about its independent sales capability. A large part of the revenue increase in fiscal 2025 also came from manufacturing contracts with the parent. An early 2025 report said orders excluding parent volume were around KRW 9 billion.

Financial strain and dilution risk

Total liabilities were KRW 176.1 billion and the debt ratio rose to 194.7%, from 99.9% in fiscal 2025. Net losses attributable to controlling shareholders over the last four quarters total KRW 51.2 billion.

In June 2026 a material matters report on convertible bond issuance was filed, along with a notice on adjusting the conversion price.

10

Risk factors

Customer concentration

A large portion of revenue depends on volume tied to the parent, so a drop in parent orders could hit utilization and revenue together. External contracts are growing, but the key question is how fast they can replace parent volume. The pace of customer diversification is the main indicator for this risk.

Cost and facility burden

Cost of sales and depreciation are rising faster than revenue, which could delay an improvement in profit and loss. The company booked impairment charges on some production facilities and said inventory-related losses were reflected in results.

As long as the fixed-cost structure persists, it is uncertain whether higher utilization alone will shrink the operating loss.

Capital structure risk

The debt ratio rose to 194.7%, and convertible bond disclosures continue. Liabilities are large relative to total equity of KRW 90.5 billion, so the terms of any additional funding become important. Even with operating cash flow back in positive territory, the annual loss is large, so financial room remains limited.

Overall view

Prestige Biologics is a CDMO entering the stage where production converts into revenue. Revenue for the fiscal year ended June 2026 was KRW 26.7 billion, rising for three straight years, and actual production and utilization both rose from the prior year.

The operating loss in the same year was KRW 49.3 billion, the fourth straight year of losses, with higher cost of sales and R&D as the main drivers. Net losses attributable to controlling shareholders over the last four quarters total KRW 51.2 billion, and the debt ratio is 194.7%.

On valuation, no earnings-based multiple can be calculated, and the price-to-book ratio sits slightly below 1x. Bullish factors are order growth, higher utilization and policy tailwind expectations, while bearish factors are cost burden, parent dependence and financial strain.

The next checkpoints are the operating loss in the third-quarter 2026 report, the utilization trend, and how quickly new contracts convert into revenue. This report is for information only and contains no buy or sell opinion.
