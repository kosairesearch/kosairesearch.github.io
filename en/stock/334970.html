02

Business structure

Prestige Biologics is a CDMO that manufactures antibody drugs on contract. Its production base consists of four plants with a total capacity of 154,000 liters, and the first plant is a campus with EU GMP certification.

An external corporate data aggregator shows antibody drugs at 52% of sales and service and other revenue at 48% as of March 2026, but this is a third-party classification and is treated here as reference only.

On regulation, the company has secured GMP approval from Korea's MFDS and Europe, followed by approval from Brazil's ANVISA. Its customers range from domestic drugmakers such as Alteogen and Celltrion to new-technology biotech firms, as the company stated in April 2024.

Besides contract manufacturing, the company runs contract testing and contract packaging (CPO) services, and reported more than four CPO orders. A contract disclosed on November 12, 2026 covers a commercial API manufacturing deal worth about KRW 15.5 billion, with a contract period running through December 31, 2027.

Half of the contract value is to be received as an advance payment. The company says its single-use culture method, which uses disposable culture bags, reduces cross-contamination risk and supports cost competitiveness.

In competition, plant scale and commercial production track record determine order competitiveness, and the gap is most visible against large CDMO peers such as Samsung Biologics and Celltrion. The company is strengthening a scale-bridge strategy that links clinical development to commercial production.

04

Earnings analysis

Annual revenue rose from KRW 1.73 billion in the fiscal year ended June 2023 to KRW 26.7 billion in the fiscal year ended June 2026.

Revenue was KRW 2.17 billion in the fiscal year ended June 2024 (up 25.4%), KRW 12.5 billion in the fiscal year ended June 2025 (up 477.8%), and KRW 26.7 billion in the fiscal year ended June 2026 (up 112.8%), so it has grown for three straight fiscal years since June 2024.

Over the same period the operating loss was KRW 22.1 billion, KRW 33.4 billion, KRW 33.7 billion and KRW 49.3 billion, so losses have continued each year. The operating margin improved from -1,277.6% to -184.9%, but that reflects the larger revenue base, while the absolute loss widened.

The net loss attributable to controlling shareholders grew from KRW 18 billion in the fiscal year ended June 2023 to KRW 51.2 billion in the fiscal year ended June 2026, after narrowing to KRW 27.5 billion in the fiscal year ended June 2025.

The company attributes the increase in losses mainly to impairment charges on inventory, convertible bonds and some production facilities. The company argues that these accounting impairments have a limited effect on actual cash flow.

Operating cash flow moved from outflows of KRW 4.45 billion in the fiscal year ended June 2023 and KRW 18.3 billion in the fiscal year ended June 2025 to an inflow of KRW 3.2 billion in the fiscal year ended June 2026.

Quarterly revenue was KRW 1.17 billion in the third quarter of 2025, KRW 8.9 billion in the fourth quarter of 2025, KRW 2.27 billion in the first quarter of 2026, KRW 14.3 billion in the second quarter of 2026 and KRW 4.03 billion in the third quarter of 2026.

The company said revenue exceeded KRW 10 billion for the first time in the April to June 2026 quarter. The swings appear to reflect timing, since new contract volumes convert into production and revenue in sequence.

The operating loss was KRW 4.39 billion in the third quarter of 2026, down from KRW 13.6 billion a year earlier, but the net loss attributable to controlling shareholders over the last four quarters totals KRW 44.1 billion.

05

Industry analysis

Demand for CDMO services is expanding alongside biosimilar production. The company is described as growing its biosimilar CDMO orders, including contracts from overseas firms.

Contract testing is described as a market that emerged as demand grew for early-stage work in GMP facilities that meet international standards from the outset.

The company's statement that it is moving from securing orders to converting them into production and revenue suggests the industry is in a middle phase, shifting from order expansion to utilization management.

Large CDMO companies focus on large long-term contracts, while this company targets clinical supply and scale-up volumes with mid-sized capacity. Celltrion both produces its own drugs and is a customer of this company, which shows how orders and production are intertwined in the sector.

GMP inspection readiness is a precondition for orders, and in 2025 reporting the company said it was preparing for a US FDA cGMP certification at its fourth plant.

Utilization management and cost pressure from the shift to commercial production are common industry challenges, and the company's impairment cases can be read in that context. Whether order growth translates into earnings should be judged together with utilization and cost trends.

06

Outlook

The company said cumulative orders stood at KRW 65 billion as of the end of March 2026. During the fiscal year ended June 2026 it signed a total of six new CDMO contracts with domestic and overseas customers, worth about KRW 41.2 billion.

The KRW 15.5 billion contract disclosed in November 2026 runs through December 31, 2027, so production and revenue recognition are likely to extend through 2027. The follow-on clinical supply contract runs through the end of March 2027.

The company expects an operating margin improvement from restructuring its cost recognition, in which raw material costs had been booked ahead of revenue. Reporting in 2025 said the first plant exceeded 70% utilization at peak batch capacity, and that the second and fourth plants were producing contracted volumes.

Because that figure dates from 2025, it should not be read as current utilization. Three points remain to be checked. First, how quickly the order backlog converts into quarterly revenue. Second, whether inventory and facility impairments recur. Third, how much higher utilization lowers the operating margin loss. All three can be checked in the next periodic reports.

Bull factors

Improving operating margin trend

The operating loss in the third quarter of 2026 was KRW 4.39 billion, down from KRW 13.6 billion in the third quarter of 2025. The company said it raised cost-control efficiency by changing how raw material costs were booked.

Cash flow turn and utilization headroom

Operating cash flow turned to an inflow of KRW 3.2 billion in the fiscal year ended June 2026. Reporting in 2025 said the first plant exceeded 70% utilization at peak batch capacity. Higher utilization could ease fixed-cost pressure, which is the core of the bullish case.

Large swings in quarterly revenue

Quarterly revenue swung from KRW 2.27 billion in the first quarter of 2026 to KRW 14.3 billion in the second quarter and KRW 4.03 billion in the third quarter. Earnings depend on when new contract volumes move into production and revenue. If production schedules slip, quarterly revenue could fall again, which limits the predictability of results.

Risk factors

Financial and liquidity

The debt ratio is 194.7%, with total liabilities close to twice total equity. The company says the cash impact of impairments is limited, but the repayment schedule and refinancing options will determine the funding burden. Conversion or redemption terms of the convertible bonds also affect the capital structure.

Contract execution and production

The company must deliver on the KRW 15.5 billion contract disclosed in November 2026 and on about KRW 41.2 billion of contracts signed during the fiscal year ended June 2026. Customers and products are largely undisclosed under confidentiality terms, which makes order quality hard to verify from outside. Production delays could affect revenue recognition and the settlement of advance payments.

Regulatory and quality

A CDMO business depends on passing GMP inspections and meeting quality standards. Reporting in 2025 said preparations for US FDA cGMP certification were underway, but the timing and outcome of that certification have not been confirmed. Inspection findings or quality problems could affect both new orders and existing contracts.

Overall view

Prestige Biologics recorded revenue of KRW 26.7 billion in the fiscal year ended June 2026, up 112.8% year on year, so its top-line expansion is now showing up in reported results.

The same year brought an operating loss of KRW 49.3 billion and a net loss attributable to controlling shareholders of KRW 51.2 billion, and the loss amount grew alongside revenue. The operating margin's improvement to -184.9% reflects the larger revenue base.

The narrower operating loss in the third quarter of 2026 and the turn to positive operating cash flow are read as bullish signals. Meanwhile, the 194.7% debt ratio, the convertible bonds and the quarterly revenue swings remain items to verify for financial stability and earnings predictability.

The company says impairments have a limited cash effect, but that explanation should be tested against the size and recurrence of impairments in the next periodic reports.

Cumulative orders of KRW 65 billion and the KRW 15.5 billion contract show the order base, but the speed at which they convert into revenue is the key to earnings improvement.
