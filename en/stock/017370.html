02

Business structure

Wooshin Systems is a Korean maker of automotive production equipment and parts, founded in 1984.

Its business consists of an automation equipment segment that builds car body welding lines and EV battery equipment, an automotive parts segment that makes doors, seatbelts and interior trim, and other segments including leasing.

Butler, a financial data site, puts the 2Q 2026 revenue mix at 79% automotive parts, 17% body-shop automation equipment and 4% other. The company has supplied EV battery equipment to customers including Hyundai Mobis since 2020, and it said it won a project worth about USD 150 million in 2023.

Hanyang Securities noted in an April 2025 report that the company counts GM and Ford among more than 40 global OEM and ODM customers. Overseas subsidiaries were set up in China (2002), India (2006) and the United States (2015), and overseas sales account for close to 60% of revenue.

Export results rose from KRW 28.6 billion in 2022 to KRW 49.7 billion in 2023 and KRW 379.6 billion in 2024. Equipment sales depend on orders and are considered volatile with hard-to-manage margins, while seatbelts and other parts are described as a steadily growing business.

Overall earnings therefore depend on the timing of equipment orders and the profit margin of the parts business.

04

Earnings analysis

Annual revenue fell from KRW 254.5 billion in 2022 to KRW 242.2 billion in 2023 (down 4.8% year on year), then jumped to KRW 563.6 billion in 2024 (up 132.7%) before declining to KRW 388.6 billion in 2025 (down 31.0%). Among 2022 to 2025, 2024 was the largest year and 2023 the smallest for revenue.

Operating profit rose from KRW 7.97 billion in 2022 to KRW 12.4 billion in 2023 (up 55.7%) and KRW 36.1 billion in 2024 (up 190.6%), then fell to KRW 20.9 billion in 2025 (down 42.2%). The operating margin climbed from 3.1% in 2022 to 5.1% in 2023 and 6.4% in 2024, before easing to 5.4% in 2025.

Hanyang Securities described 2024 as a record-level result driven by the full recognition of large secondary-battery automation orders in an April 2025 report, while the company attributed the 2025 decline to lower orders.

Profit attributable to owners of the parent was KRW 2.77 billion in 2022 and grew to KRW 33.9 billion in 2024 (up 356.2% from the prior year), and then fell to KRW 15 billion in 2025 (down 55.7%). Net income including non-controlling interests was KRW 15 billion in 2025.

Operating cash flow went from -KRW 81.3 billion in 2024 to KRW 90.1 billion in 2025, turning positive. On a quarterly basis, revenue declined in each of the last four quarters, from KRW 101 billion in 3Q 2025 and KRW 90.9 billion in 4Q 2025 to KRW 70.6 billion in 3Q 2026, which was down 30.1% year on year.

Operating profit reached KRW 6.08 billion in 4Q 2025 (up 39.3% quarter on quarter), then turned to an operating loss of KRW 335 million in 1Q 2026, widened to an operating loss of KRW 3.55 billion in 2Q 2026 and reached an operating loss of KRW 4.65 billion in 3Q 2026.

ThinkPool noted that the 2Q 2026 operating loss was the weakest among the last three years. Profit attributable to owners of the parent moved from KRW 6.62 billion in 3Q 2025 to a net loss of KRW 16.3 billion in 3Q 2026, and the four-quarter total for the last four quarters is a net loss of KRW 9.25 billion.

05

Industry analysis

The company's end demand is tied to investment in automotive body-shop automation and EV battery production lines. It has supplied EV battery equipment to customers including Hyundai Mobis since 2020 and said it won a project worth about USD 150 million in 2023.

Secondary-battery assembly equipment is order-driven, so revenue tends to cluster in certain periods and margins are hard to control, a pattern that recurs in industry analysis. In a March 2026 report, Kiwoom Securities cited a sharp drop in battery-related automation orders as the cause of lower automation revenue.

The same note said the seatbelt segment kept growing and returned to profit. A 2025 company profile cited expected growth in equipment orders tied to shifts in global markets including North America.

Hanyang Securities said the company's competitiveness lies in its body-shop equipment technology and turnkey supply capability. On the customer side, the company counts GM and Ford among more than 40 customers, which ties it to automaker investment cycles.

The current cycle position splits in two: equipment is in an order lull, while parts are in a return-to-profit phase.

Operating leverage if equipment orders recover

Kiwoom Securities said that if earnings improve, conditions are in place for operating leverage to show up. The USD 150 million project win in 2023 and the 2024 results that Hanyang Securities described as record-level serve as past reference points.

If the 2024 operating margin of 6.4% were to return, the improvement from the 2026 operating losses could be large. The key is whether equipment revenue recovers and when that happens.

Improving balance sheet and overseas base

Overseas subsidiaries and export growth (KRW 379.6 billion in 2024) are seen as having widened the revenue base. This financial capacity could ease working capital needs when equipment orders resume.

Risk factors

Order volatility

A large part of revenue depends on automaker customers and equipment orders, so a gap in a single project feeds directly into results. The company attributed the 2025 decline to lower orders, which illustrates this. Despite a customer base of more than 40, the timing gap in orders remains.

Profitability and cash flow

Operating losses have run for three quarters since 1Q 2026, so the timing of a return to profit is uncertain. The four-quarter net loss attributable to owners of the parent totals KRW 9.25 billion. Since operating cash flow was negative in 2024, working capital pressure could rise again.

Overseas operations and balance sheet

Overseas sales account for close to 60% of revenue, so results are exposed to exchange rates and local production conditions. Total liabilities of KRW 228.3 billion at end-2025 exceed total equity of KRW 166.6 billion.

High shareholdings by the largest shareholder and treasury shares limit the number of freely tradable shares, which can add to trading volatility.

Overall view

By contrast, the automotive parts segment, including seatbelts, has returned to profit and is partly cushioning the equipment weakness. On the balance sheet, the debt ratio fell to 137.1% at end-2025 from 2022, and operating cash flow turned positive at KRW 90.1 billion.

The earnings burden is also clear, with a 3Q 2026 net loss attributable to owners of the parent of KRW 16.3 billion and a four-quarter total of KRW 9.25 billion. The price-to-book ratio sits well below book value per share, but the price-to-earnings ratio is not calculated while losses continue.
