Hyungji I&C: Quarterly Profit Returns, Revenue Still Shrinking

Hyungji I&C returned to both operating profit and net profit attributable to owners in the second quarter of 2026, but the structural burden of three straight years of revenue decline from 2023 to 2025 and two straight years of annual losses in 2024-2025 has not yet been resolved.

02

Business structure

Hyungji I&C is a fashion company under the Hyungji Fashion Group, built around the menswear brands YEZAC and BON and the womenswear brand CARRIES NOTE.

YEZAC has been expanding beyond its core shirt lineup into knitwear, sweaters and cardigans, and runs a product line developed in collaboration with broadcaster Kim Chang-ok. BON pursues a two-track strategy that simultaneously expands its premium suit line and casual outerwear.

The company has said that in the first half of 2025, online sales of CARRIES NOTE rose 182% year on year, helping drive double-digit overall sales growth for the brand in the same period.

The company operates an omnichannel structure combining department store and outlet locations with its own online mall, Haijin.com, and major open-market platforms, and it has recently introduced combined menswear-womenswear stores in a single space.

In April 2026, it reorganized its former online sales team into a CEO-reporting VC (Velocity Commerce) division to strengthen e-commerce-centered management, and in September of the same year it launched a new online-exclusive brand.

Its competitive landscape overlaps with numerous small and mid-cap fashion companies listed on KOSDAQ and KOSPI, with distribution-channel fee burdens and brand loyalty cited as key competitive factors. The company once generated more than KRW 100 billion in annual revenue, but its scale has contracted since 2020.

04

Earnings analysis

Annual revenue declined for three straight years, from KRW 70.5 billion in 2022 to KRW 65.3 billion in 2023 (down 7.3% year on year), KRW 56.7 billion in 2024 (down 13.1%), and KRW 50.8 billion in 2025 (down 10.5%).

Operating profit fell sharply from KRW 2.39 billion in 2022 to KRW 653 million in 2023, then swung to an operating loss of KRW 5.02 billion in 2024 and widened to an operating loss of KRW 7.04 billion in 2025. The operating margin also deteriorated sharply, from 3.4% in 2022 to -13.9% in 2025.

Net profit attributable to owners swung to a profit of KRW 1.86 billion in 2023, but the company then posted a net loss attributable to owners of KRW 3.87 billion in 2024 (a swing to loss) and a net loss of KRW 8.91 billion in 2025 (the loss continuing), marking two consecutive years of annual losses.

On a quarterly basis, the operating loss widened from KRW 1.01 billion in Q2 2025 to KRW 2.03 billion in Q3 and KRW 2.29 billion in Q4, before narrowing to an operating loss of KRW 1.28 billion in Q1 2026 and then swinging to an operating profit of KRW 51.7 million in Q2 2026.

Net profit attributable to owners followed a similar path, widening to a net loss of KRW 3.5 billion in Q1 2026 before swinging to a net profit of KRW 29.97 million in Q2 2026.

The company attributed the improvement to reduced selling and administrative expenses and a restructured store network following the closure of low-efficiency locations, noting that cumulative first-half SG&A expenses fell roughly 4% year on year.

Still, cumulative net losses attributable to owners over the most recent four quarters (Q3 2025 to Q2 2026) totaled KRW 8.14 billion, showing that a single quarter of profit has not yet offset the accumulated losses.

Operating cash flow also deteriorated from a positive KRW 1.13 billion in 2022 to negative KRW 2.81 billion in 2023 and negative KRW 6.01 billion in 2024, before the loss narrowed to negative KRW 3.4 billion in 2025, though it remained in negative territory.

05

Industry analysis

Small and mid-cap domestic fashion companies face a common trend of slowing domestic apparel consumption and a shift in purchasing toward online and direct-to-consumer channels.

Within the KOSDAQ textile and apparel sector that Hyungji I&C belongs to, aggregate sector revenue and operating profit are smaller in scale than those of large KOSPI-listed apparel companies, and while the KOSDAQ textile and apparel sector as a whole maintained positive aggregate operating profit, Hyungji I&C posted consecutive annual losses in 2024-2025, lagging the sector average in profitability.

Traditional offline channels such as department stores and outlets are being restructured around store renewals and pop-up formats to improve sales efficiency per location, and Hyungji I&C has responded in kind by closing low-efficiency stores and shifting toward key flagship locations.

At the same time, omnichannel and D2C strategies that combine owned online malls with open-market platforms have become a broadly used industry tool for lowering fee costs and defending margins.

The fall-winter season, the industry's peak period due to higher average product prices, is considered critical for both revenue and profitability, and many apparel companies including Hyungji I&C weight a large share of their business plans toward this period.

Still, the pace of recovery in overall domestic consumer sentiment and fluctuations in raw material and logistics costs remain common variables across the sector.

06

Outlook

In an October 2026 announcement, the company set a fourth-quarter 2026 revenue target of KRW 21.0 billion, roughly 33% higher than the same period a year earlier.

However, this figure is a management target and should be distinguished from the confirmed results that will later appear in the fourth-quarter regulatory filing.

To reach this target, the company cited expanded sales of key fall-winter products across YEZAC, BON and CARRIES NOTE, along with more efficient inventory management using combined online and offline sales data.

On the online side, the company set a 2026 e-commerce division revenue target of KRW 6.0 billion, and said sales at its owned mall Haijin.com rose 52% in the most recent year compared with its first year after launch.

Whether the VC division established in April 2026 and the new online-exclusive brand launched in September contribute meaningfully to that target remains to be confirmed.

On the financing side, a rights offering decided in February 2026 (2.8 million common shares at an issue price of KRW 4,670) combined with a 10-for-1 reverse stock split reshaped the capital structure, following an earlier KRW 20.0 billion shareholder rights offering in March 2025.

Outstanding convertible bonds and bonds with warrants remain, so the potential for further share-count changes from future conversion requests also warrants monitoring.

Whether the Q2 swing to profit reflects a temporary improvement driven by cost efficiency or a trend that continues through the fall-winter peak season is something that will become clearer in upcoming quarterly results.

Bull factors

Quarterly Return to Profit via Cost Efficiency

In Q2 2026, operating profit of KRW 51.7 million and net profit attributable to owners of KRW 29.97 million both swung to profit versus the prior quarter and the same quarter a year earlier.

The company cited reduced selling and administrative expenses and the closure of low-efficiency stores as drivers, with cumulative first-half SG&A down roughly 4% year on year. The scale of operating losses had also been narrowing since Q1 2026 after widening in the third and fourth quarters of 2025.

This is still a single-quarter result, however, and whether the trend continues needs confirmation in coming quarters.

Growth in Online and D2C Channels

Online sales at the womenswear brand CARRIES NOTE rose 182% year on year in the first half of 2025, and sales at the company's own mall Haijin.com grew 52% in the most recent year compared with its first year after launch.

The VC division established in April 2026 now oversees online operations, pursuing channel-specific product lineups and inventory efficiency. Strengthening D2C sales to reduce reliance on external distribution fees is a factor that could help defend margins. Still, the absolute scale of online sales appears limited relative to the company's total revenue.

Fall-Winter Season Demand and Store Network Restructuring

The company is diversifying its product lineup for the fall-winter season, including expanded knitwear and sweaters under YEZAC and a blouson collection under BON.

It is simultaneously restructuring its distribution network by closing low-efficiency stores and shifting toward renewed flagship stores, combined stores and pop-up formats. Based on this strategy, the company set a fourth-quarter revenue target of KRW 21.0 billion.

This remains a management target rather than a confirmed result, and whether it is actually achieved will need to be verified in a future regulatory filing.

Bear factors

Three Years of Revenue Decline, Two Years of Annual Losses

Annual revenue fell for three consecutive years, from KRW 70.5 billion in 2022 to KRW 50.8 billion in 2025. Operating profit swung to an operating loss of KRW 5.02 billion in 2024 and the loss widened to KRW 7.04 billion in 2025.

Net profit attributable to owners also posted losses for two straight years, a net loss of KRW 3.87 billion in 2024 and KRW 8.91 billion in 2025. It is premature to conclude that this multi-year trend has reversed based on a single quarter of profit in Q2 2026.

Persistently Negative Operating Cash Flow

Operating cash flow deteriorated from a positive KRW 1.13 billion in 2022 to negative KRW 2.81 billion in 2023 and negative KRW 6.01 billion in 2024. In 2025, the negative figure narrowed to KRW 3.4 billion but remained in negative territory.

This indicates that difficulties have persisted not only in the income statement but also in actual cash flow. Whether operating cash flow turns positive is a metric worth watching in future quarterly results.

History of Frequent Capital Raises and Share-Count Changes

Hyungji I&C decided on a KRW 20.0 billion shareholder rights offering in March 2025 and an additional rights offering of 2.8 million common shares in February 2026. Around the same period, a 10-for-1 reverse stock split was also carried out, substantially changing the share count and the per-share value basis.

Outstanding convertible bonds and bonds with warrants remain, raising the possibility of further dilution from future conversion requests. In July 2026, the stock was also designated an investment caution issue following large-volume selling from a specific account.

With operating cash flow negative for multiple consecutive years, the company has become more reliant on external financing.

Revenue Slowdown and Pace of Profitability Recovery

If the recovery in domestic consumer sentiment is slow, cost-cutting alone may have limits in normalizing profitability.

Trading Volatility Linked to Small Market Capitalization

Overall view

Hyungji I&C posted its first numerical evidence of cost efficiency and distribution-network restructuring paying off when both operating profit and net profit attributable to owners swung to profit in Q2 2026.

However, the multi-year downtrend—three straight years of revenue decline from 2022 to 2025 and two straight years of operating losses and net losses attributable to owners in 2024-2025—has not been resolved by a single quarter of profit.

Cumulative net losses attributable to owners of KRW 8.14 billion over the most recent four quarters underscore this structural burden.

On the financing side, two rights offerings in March 2025 and February 2026, along with a 10-for-1 reverse stock split, substantially reshaped the capital structure and share count, and further dilution remains possible from outstanding convertible bonds and bonds with warrants.

The company has set a fourth-quarter 2026 revenue target of KRW 21.0 billion, centering its planned recovery on strengthening online channels and fall-winter product competitiveness, but this figure should be understood as a management target rather than a confirmed result.

The key points to watch going forward are whether the Q2 improvement carries through as a trend and whether the multi-year revenue decline stabilizes, both of which will become clearer in upcoming quarterly and annual regulatory filings.
