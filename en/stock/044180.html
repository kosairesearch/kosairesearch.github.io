KD: Court Receivership Clouds Listing Status

Construction firm KD is undergoing both court-led rehabilitation proceedings and a KOSDAQ listing eligibility review following a sharp simultaneous decline in 2025 revenue and profit.

  1. 1

    2025 revenue fell 82.4% year over year to KRW 27.8 billion, with the company swinging to an operating loss of KRW 4.27 billion and a controlling-interest net loss of KRW 25.6 billion.

  2. 2

    Over the last four quarters (3Q2025–2Q2026), the cumulative controlling-interest net loss reached KRW 29.9 billion, with only 1Q2026 showing a temporary profit.

  3. 3

    KD received a court rehabilitation commencement order in March 2026, was designated an administrative issue, and subsequently faced a trading halt and improvement period tied to a listing eligibility review related to audit opinion issues.

  4. 4

    The debt-to-equity ratio surged to 2,041.1% at the end of 2025 from 690.1% in 2024, while total equity shrank to KRW 14.1 billion.

  5. 5

    Repeated principal and interest delinquencies occurred on project-financing-type loans from regional credit unions, and KD was also penalized with unfaithful disclosure points for a disclosure reversal.

02

Business structure

KD was founded in 1974 and listed on KOSDAQ in November 2000, with headquarters in Danwon-gu, Ansan.

The company's core is its construction segment, covering housing and officetel construction and sales, and it has historically also operated electronics component manufacturing and a fashion segment making and distributing golf wear and women's apparel.

However, the mold manufacturing business was discontinued after rising raw material and labor costs eroded its cost structure.

The vast majority of revenue now comes from the construction segment, where KD wins orders through bidding on self-developed, government, and private projects and recognizes revenue as permitting, pre-sales, and completion proceed.

In the 2024 revenue mix, the Gunsan New Station Area project alone accounted for 73.31% of sales, with completed-building sales contributing 17.65%, the Cheonan Jiksan Lakemark apartment construction 4.59%, and the Haeundae Hallavivaldi Penville project 4.05%, reflecting heavy concentration in a small number of projects.

This project concentration means quarterly revenue can swing sharply depending on the progress and pre-sale rate of individual developments.

Within the KOSDAQ construction sector, KD's business model differs from materials producers and more closely resembles that of small and mid-sized homebuilders pursuing self-developed housing projects. By revenue scale, however, KD remains among the smaller names in the KOSDAQ construction segment.

04

Earnings analysis

Annual revenue rose 73.4% from KRW 103.5 billion in 2022 to a peak of KRW 179.4 billion in 2023, then fell for two straight years to KRW 157.6 billion in 2024 (down 12.1%) and KRW 27.8 billion in 2025 (down 82.4%).

Operating profit swung from an operating loss of KRW 2.43 billion in 2022 to an operating profit of KRW 4.4 billion in 2023, before falling 33.6% to KRW 2.92 billion in 2024 and then reversing into an operating loss of KRW 4.27 billion in 2025.

Controlling-interest net income followed a similar path, moving from net losses of KRW 2.86 billion in 2022 and KRW 3.77 billion in 2023 to a net profit of KRW 4.52 billion in 2024, before swinging back to a net loss of KRW 25.6 billion in 2025 as the loss widened sharply.

The operating margin stayed in low single-digit territory at 2.5% in 2023 and 1.9% in 2024 before deteriorating sharply to -15.4% in 2025. The company has attributed the widening loss to lower pre-sale revenue from its housing projects together with higher non-operating expenses tied to provisions for liabilities.

On a quarterly basis, revenue fell 48.1% from KRW 7.02 billion in 2Q2025 to KRW 3.64 billion in 3Q2025, then rose 76.8% to KRW 6.44 billion in 4Q2025, before surging 613.1% to KRW 45.9 billion in 1Q2026 on project completion and pre-sale recognition, producing an operating profit of KRW 6 billion and a controlling-interest net profit of KRW 5.38 billion.

Revenue then dropped 81.3% quarter over quarter to KRW 8.57 billion in 2Q2026, operating profit fell to KRW 212 million, and the controlling-interest result swung back to a net loss of KRW 14 billion, leaving the trailing four quarters (3Q2025–2Q2026) with a cumulative controlling-interest net loss of KRW 29.9 billion.

Operating cash flow also swung wildly, from outflows of KRW 42.1 billion in 2022 and KRW 15.6 billion in 2023 to a large inflow of KRW 90.8 billion in 2024, before reverting to an outflow of KRW 11.7 billion in 2025.

The 2025 annual business report underwent multiple subsequent corrective filings, and the figures in this report follow the final disclosed periodic report values.

Industry analysis

South Korea's housing construction cycle, governed largely by pre-sale rates and project-financing (PF) market conditions, is a major driver of earnings volatility for small builders.

Mid- and small-cap pre-sale developers like KD, whose revenue is concentrated in a handful of regional projects, commonly show sharp quarter-to-quarter swings tied to the timing of pre-sales and project completion recognition, a pattern seen broadly across the KOSDAQ construction sector.

A market data provider has noted that KD's performance deterioration has been persistent amid construction-cycle swings, observing that on a cumulative basis through 3Q2025, consolidated revenue fell 79.0% year over year with both operating profit and net income swinging to losses.

The KOSDAQ construction sector's combined revenue of roughly KRW 10,162.3 billion is a small slice of total KOSDAQ revenue of about KRW 190,533.2 billion, and KD sits toward the smaller end of that sector by revenue scale.

Interest rate levels, unsold housing inventory, and financing conditions for regional small-scale projects remain common variables affecting builders similar in size to KD.

Reports over the past two years indicate a rising number of KOSDAQ listing eligibility reviews and delistings tied to non-standard audit opinions, disclosure violations, and deteriorating financial structures, which has heightened market wariness toward small builders like KD that are experiencing capital erosion.

Competitively, smaller builders with limited brand power or large-scale order capacity tend to be more vulnerable to liquidity pressure during downturns in the pre-sale market.

The company received a court rehabilitation commencement order in March 2026, and related disclosures have since been corrected multiple times, indicating the process remains ongoing.

At the same time, trading has been halted over a listing eligibility review tied to audit opinion issues, with an improvement period granted whose end date is defined as 10 days after the statutory filing deadline for the next annual business report, meaning the specific timeline should become clearer through future disclosures.

Within what could be confirmed through search, no recent official guidance on new housing project orders or capital-raising plans was found; notably, a rights offering pursued in early 2026 was subsequently withdrawn.

On the financial side, multiple loan delinquencies on inter-cooperative project financing loans were disclosed in 2026, with some delinquent amounts reaching double-digit percentages of equity, keeping short-term liquidity management a central issue.

If a rehabilitation plan is ultimately approved by the court, debt adjustment could open a path to an improved balance sheet, but approval and the specific terms remain undetermined.

Under these conditions, investors following KD are better served tracking the sequential progress of the court process and the exchange's listing eligibility determination than any near-term business momentum.

Bull factors

Potential for sharp quarterly swings

In Q1 2026, revenue reached KRW 45.9 billion, operating profit KRW 6.0 billion, and net income attributable to controlling shareholders KRW 5.38 billion, turning profitable as completion and sales recognition for a specific project were concentrated in the quarter.

This case demonstrates the potential for KD's earnings structure to swing into profit within a short period depending on project progress rates.

However, the fact that this profit immediately reverted to a loss in the following quarter (Q2 2026) also shows that the sustainability of such profitability has not been verified.

Possible debt restructuring via court process

In March 2026, KD received a court decision to commence rehabilitation proceedings, entering a process in which its debt-credit relationships can be restructured under court supervision. If the rehabilitation plan is approved, a structural path may open to ease the heavy debt ratio and overdue burden. However, whether approval will be granted and the specific adjustment ratios remain undetermined.

Limited gap versus book net asset value

The current price-to-book ratio is near 1x, trading in a range where the gap between book net asset value and stock price is not large. This suggests the market is pricing the stock on the premise that additional capital impairment has been limited.

However, it should be noted that this comparison is based on a significantly reduced equity base, with total equity standing at only KRW 14.1 billion in 2025.

Bear factors

Sharp 2025 earnings decline and deepening equity erosion

In 2025, revenue plunged 82.4% year-on-year to KRW 27.8 billion, while operating loss of KRW 4.27 billion and net loss attributable to controlling shareholders of KRW 25.6 billion both turned negative in tandem. During the same period, the debt ratio surged to 2,041.1%, nearly tripling from 690.1% in 2024. Total equity also shrank to KRW 14.1 billion, significantly weakening the company's financial buffer.

Cumulative net loss over four quarters

The cumulative net loss attributable to controlling shareholders over the most recent four quarters, from Q3 2025 to Q2 2026, totaled KRW 29.9 billion. Only Q1 2026 posted a profit, while the other three quarters all recorded net losses, indicating that the sustainability of profits has not been confirmed.

In Q2 2026, the net loss widened again to KRW 14.0 billion, showing that the scale of losses itself remains substantial.

Going-concern uncertainty tied to rehabilitation and listing review

Following its designation as an administrative issue due to the rehabilitation proceeding filing in March 2026, KD has continuously undergone procedures directly tied to the maintenance of its listing status, including the rehabilitation proceeding commencement decision, trading suspension, and the granting of an improvement period.

After the improvement period ends, the exchange's corporate examination committee will deliberate and decide on delisting, meaning the final outcome has not yet been determined. The possibility of additional trading suspensions or disclosure-related sanctions during this process cannot be ruled out.

Risk factors

Listing continuity risk

Trading in KD shares was suspended from March 24, 2026 due to grounds for substantive review of listing eligibility related to the audit opinion, and an improvement period has been granted.

Since the end of the improvement period is defined as 10 days after the day following the statutory filing deadline for the next business report, the subsequent deliberation and decision on delisting by the exchange's corporate examination committee remains pending.

Additionally, in January-February 2026, penalty points for unfaithful disclosure were imposed or announced due to disclosure retractions and delayed litigation disclosures, raising the possibility of further trading suspensions or inclusion in substantive review subjects due to additional penalty points.

Financial and liquidity risk

Since January 2026, multiple disclosures have been made regarding principal and interest arrears on joint loans among credit unions involving regional credit unions such as Asan Credit Union and Asan Future Credit Union, with some overdue amounts reaching double-digit percentages relative to equity capital.

The debt ratio at the end of 2025 rose sharply to 2,041.1% from 690.1% in 2024, while total equity shrank to KRW 14.1 billion, warranting attention to the possibility of further capital impairment.

Operating cash flow also recorded an outflow of KRW 11.7 billion in 2025, suggesting that liquidity pressure could recur depending on external funding conditions.

Business concentration and sector cycle risk

In 2024, 73.31% of revenue was concentrated in a single project, the Gunsan Sinyeok Station Area project, creating a structure in which the sales rate or construction delay of a specific project directly impacts overall performance.

The domestic housing construction market is sensitive to interest rates, unsold inventory, and PF market conditions, and the KOSDAQ construction sector as a whole is classified as a group of stocks with high earnings volatility.

As a small-scale sales developer, the company has limited funding capacity compared to larger players, making it more vulnerable during downturns in the sales market.

Overall view

KD's revenue has declined for two straight years since peaking in 2023, and in 2025 the company swung simultaneously into an operating loss and a controlling-interest net loss, marking a significant deterioration in its financial structure.

Through 2026, a series of disclosures beyond core business performance have emerged, including delinquencies on inter-cooperative project-financing loans, an unfaithful disclosure designation, a court rehabilitation commencement order, and a trading halt with an improvement period. 1Q2026 saw a temporary swing to profit driven by completion and pre-sale recognition on a specific project, but the company reverted to a net loss in 2Q2026, leaving the trailing four-quarter cumulative controlling-interest net loss at KRW 29.9 billion.

The price-to-book ratio sits close to 1x, but this comparison must be read against the backdrop of total equity having shrunk to just KRW 14.1 billion.

The key variables going forward are whether the court confirms a rehabilitation plan and how the exchange's listing eligibility review concludes, with the trajectory of both processes likely to determine both the company's balance-sheet recovery and its continued listing status. Investors should directly verify quarterly disclosures and exchange/court filings before forming any judgment.
