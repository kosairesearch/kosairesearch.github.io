02

Business structure

Founded in 1974 and listed on KOSDAQ in 2000, KD is a construction and real estate development company whose revenue is derived mostly from housing construction and pre-sale activities.

The company has operated apartment and officetel pre-sale businesses under its own brands, including Aramchae, KDU Plaza, and Pentville.

It also engages in telecommunication parts manufacturing and fashion (golf and women's apparel) distribution, though these contribute a far smaller share of revenue than construction, and its mold-base manufacturing operation has already been discontinued due to deteriorating margins from rising raw material and labor costs.

The company holds five consolidated subsidiaries and had disclosed plans to absorb-merge four of them during 2025.

More recently, it has provided joint and several guarantees for interim-payment loans tied to buyers of the Haeundae Halla Vivaldi Pentville project in Busan, adding financial-guarantee contingent liabilities beyond its core construction and sales business.

Within the KOSDAQ construction sector, KD is classified as a small-cap builder ranking near the bottom in both market capitalization and revenue scale.

Because a portion of its housing business is structured through regional housing associations, the business carries relatively high variability tied to permitting and member-recruitment stages.

The controlling shareholder is KD Technology Investment, and in 2026 its stake fell sharply from 26.34% to 11.72% after forced sales triggered by unpaid stock-collateralized loans.

04

Earnings analysis

On an annual basis, KD posted revenue of KRW 103.480 billion with an operating loss of KRW 2.429 billion in 2022, before revenue rose to KRW 179.411 billion in 2023 with operating profit turning positive at KRW 4.402 billion, even as the bottom line remained a net loss of KRW 3.768 billion.

In 2024, revenue declined to KRW 157.635 billion, yet operating profit of KRW 2.922 billion (a 1.9% operating margin) and net income of KRW 4.523 billion were achieved, alongside a strongly positive operating cash flow of KRW 90.842 billion.

In 2025, however, revenue collapsed to KRW 27.787 billion, producing an operating loss of KRW 4.267 billion (a -15.4% operating margin) and a net loss that widened sharply to KRW 25.587 billion, while operating cash flow turned negative at KRW 11.701 billion.

The company attributed the wider 2025 loss to declining pre-sale revenue in its housing business along with higher non-operating expenses tied to provisions for contingent liabilities.

Quarterly, revenue was KRW 7.021 billion in Q2 2025 with an operating loss of KRW 2.699 billion and a net loss of KRW 2.095 billion, followed by a further decline to KRW 3.642 billion in Q3 with an operating loss of KRW 1.931 billion and a net loss of KRW 2.684 billion.

Revenue recovered to KRW 6.439 billion in Q4 with a small operating profit of KRW 141 million, yet the net loss ballooned to KRW 18.540 billion, suggesting a possible one-off, non-operating item.

Q1 2026 saw revenue surge to KRW 45.916 billion on large pre-sale revenue recognition, with operating profit of KRW 6.004 billion and net profit of KRW 5.379 billion marking a clear turnaround, but Q2 revenue fell back to KRW 8.566 billion with only a small operating profit of KRW 212 million while the net loss widened again to KRW 14.005 billion, repeating the pattern of divergence between operating results and the bottom line.

As a result, cumulative net loss attributable to owners over the trailing four quarters (Q3 2025 through Q2 2026) reached roughly KRW 29.851 billion, underscoring that large non-operating items, rather than quarterly operating swings, have been driving final results.

05

Industry analysis

South Korea's housing construction and pre-sale market continues to face tight project-financing conditions and a burden of unsold inventory, keeping the funding environment difficult for small and mid-sized builders overall.

In this environment, a small builder like KD, which has exposure to regional housing association-type projects, faces relatively greater risk from delays in member recruitment and permitting.

The company's repeated disclosures of loan principal and interest arrears on inter-association joint loans illustrate this funding pressure concretely.

Within the KOSDAQ construction sector, KD ranks near the bottom in both market capitalization and revenue scale, and it is distinguished from most peers by simultaneously carrying structural risks of court rehabilitation and capital impairment.

As pre-sale demand remains polarized by region and product type, the progress of specific projects such as the one in Haeundae has an outsized influence on the company's results.

Revenue recognition in the construction sector tends to concentrate in particular quarters given the nature of project wins and pre-sales, a structural feature of the industry that is reflected in KD's volatile quarterly performance.

06

Outlook

KD received a rehabilitation commencement decision from the Suwon Rehabilitation Court on March 18, 2026, with the company's CEO serving concurrently as the court-appointed receiver.

After the registration and investigation periods for rehabilitation claims, collateral rights, and shares were extended multiple times, the deadline for submitting the rehabilitation plan was likewise pushed back from October 2 to October 16, 2026.

The forthcoming rehabilitation plan is expected to address debt restructuring methods, including the possibility of debt-to-equity conversion, a matter that could directly affect existing shareholders' equity value.

On the listing front, a delisting cause tied to the audit opinion arose in March, triggering a trading halt, and following the company's objection the Korea Exchange granted an improvement period through April 10, 2027, after which a final delisting decision will be made based on whether the improvement plan has been implemented.

On the business side, the company has again extended, through the end of this year, its joint and several guarantee for interim-payment loans of buyers in the Haeundae Halla Vivaldi Pentville project in Busan, and the progress of occupancy and settlement at that site, alongside the contractor's own guarantee history, will be a factor in whether this contingent liability is resolved going forward.

No specific disclosures of new order wins or capacity expansion plans have been confirmed to date, and for now the progress of the rehabilitation process and the maintenance of listing eligibility remain the company's top priorities.

Bull factors

Temporary turnaround from Q1 2026 pre-sale revenue recognition

In Q1 2026, concentrated revenue recognition from a large pre-sale project lifted sales to KRW 45.916 billion with operating profit of KRW 6.004 billion and net profit of KRW 5.379 billion, a clear quarterly turnaround.

This illustrates how pre-sale projects can generate large one-time revenue and profit recognition once they reach the occupancy and settlement stage. Similar revenue-recognition effects could recur depending on the settlement schedule of remaining pre-sale projects.

Track record of large positive swings in operating cash flow

In 2024, operating cash flow reached a large positive KRW 90.842 billion, demonstrating that cash flow can improve substantially in a short period once projects reach the settlement stage. This is a past example suggesting capacity for balance-sheet improvement when collection of pre-sale proceeds accelerates. However, such cash-flow improvement does not recur every quarter and depends on project timing.

Court-led rehabilitation offers a debt-restructuring pathway

KD obtained a rehabilitation commencement decision from the Suwon Rehabilitation Court in March 2026, securing a procedural framework for debt restructuring under court supervision.

If the rehabilitation plan is approved, elements such as interest reduction or repayment deferral could help ease the company's elevated debt ratio to some degree. This offers a more systematic restructuring path than relying solely on individual creditor negotiations outside of court protection.

Bear factors

Delisting uncertainty from capital impairment and audit disclaimer

As of first-half 2026, the capital impairment ratio reached 56.8% and the auditor's review opinion was confirmed as a disclaimer, adding to the grounds for an investment-alert designation.

Separately, a delisting cause tied to the audit opinion arose in March, halting trading, and the exchange has granted an improvement period through April 10, 2027. A delisting outcome cannot be ruled out depending on the Corporate Review Committee's determination after the improvement period ends.

Large net losses recurring independent of operating performance

In both Q4 2025 and Q2 2026, operating profit was modestly positive, yet net losses reached KRW 18.540 billion and KRW 14.005 billion respectively, repeating a pattern in which non-operating items dominate the final result.

The cumulative net loss attributable to owners over the trailing four quarters was roughly KRW 29.851 billion, making it difficult to explain the overall profit-and-loss structure through operating improvement alone.

This volatility appears to stem from hard-to-predict factors such as provisioning for contingent liabilities or litigation-related costs.

Forced sale of controlling shareholder stock and governance instability

The controlling shareholder, KD Technology Investment, failed to repay stock-collateralized loans, resulting in the forced sale of more than 3.9 million KD shares, which cut the controlling stake from 26.34% to 11.72%.

The decline in the controlling shareholder's stake could become a variable affecting management stability going forward.

The company has also been designated an unfaithful disclosure entity multiple times, including for reversing a disclosed capital raise and for delayed disclosure related to litigation, raising separate concerns about disclosure reliability.

Risk factors

Listing maintenance and rehabilitation risk

The company received a rehabilitation commencement decision from the Suwon Rehabilitation Court in March 2026, and the rehabilitation plan submission deadline has been extended to October 16.

Separately, a delisting cause tied to the audit opinion has resulted in an improvement period through April 10, 2027, meaning both listing status and shareholder rights could be affected depending on how the two processes unfold.

If the approved rehabilitation plan includes debt-to-equity conversion, further dilution of existing shareholders cannot be ruled out.

Financial soundness and contingent liability risk

The debt ratio surged to 2,041.1% at the end of 2025, and the company has repeatedly disclosed arrears on principal and interest for inter-association joint loans.

Its joint and several guarantee for interim-payment loans of buyers in the Haeundae Halla Vivaldi Pentville project in Busan has been extended again through year-end, meaning additional guarantee liabilities could materialize depending on the settlement outcome at that site.

Such contingent liabilities could also affect the claims-confirmation process within the rehabilitation proceedings.

Disclosure reliability and governance risk

The company has been designated or preliminarily designated as an unfaithful disclosure entity on multiple occasions, including for reversing a disclosed rights offering and for delayed disclosure related to litigation.

The sharp reduction in the controlling shareholder's stake due to forced sales adds governance uncertainty in connection with potential future changes in control. These disclosure and governance issues could heighten information-asymmetry risk for investors.

12

Overall view

KD has shown extreme earnings volatility, swinging from profitability in 2023-2024 to a large loss in 2025 and alternating between quarterly profits and losses through the first half of 2026, driven largely by the timing of pre-sale revenue recognition and shifts in non-operating items.

At the same time, the company faces multiple structural issues simultaneously: court-led rehabilitation proceedings, a first-half capital impairment ratio of 56.8%, a disclaimer of audit opinion, and an improvement period tied to an audit-related delisting cause running through April 10, 2027.

The sharp reduction in the controlling shareholder's stake due to forced sales, along with a history of multiple unfaithful-disclosure designations, adds further uncertainty from a governance and disclosure-reliability standpoint.

On the other hand, the clear turnaround in Q1 2026 and the large positive swing in operating cash flow seen in 2024 are past examples showing that financial conditions can improve depending on the timing of project settlements.

The specific content of the rehabilitation plan due in October and the final listing-eligibility determination expected around April 2027 will likely be the key inflection points for the company's continued existence.

Investors should continue monitoring disclosures closely in light of these structural risks alongside the earnings volatility.
