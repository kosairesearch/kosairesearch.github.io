02

Business structure

KD is a construction and real estate development company listed on KOSDAQ in 2000. It exited its former electronic parts and mold base businesses and has concentrated on housing sales and contract construction.

The company sells apartments and officetels it builds itself and also takes construction orders for buildings. On June 30, 2025, it absorbed four subsidiaries through a no-capital-increase small merger to simplify its consolidated group.

Revenue in 2024 was led by the Gunsan Sinyeok Station area project at 73.31%, followed by completed building sales and merchandise at 17.65%, the Cheonan Jiksan Lakemark apartment construction at 4.59%, and Haeundae Hanla Vivaldi Pentville at 4.05%.

Because revenue depends on a few sites, the timing of completions and sales drives annual results. As a result, a delay at one site can make quarterly revenue jump and then fall sharply. As of September 2025, five subsidiaries were counted in the consolidated group.

The former electronic parts business run through a Vietnamese subsidiary was discontinued after raw material and labor cost increases worsened its profitability.

Compared with large contractors in the same sector such as Hyundai Engineering & Construction, DL E&C and Daewoo Engineering & Construction, the gap in capital scale and order backlog is very wide. Competition centers on securing sites among small and mid-sized housing developers and builders.

04

Earnings analysis

Annual revenue rose from KRW 103.5 billion in 2022 to KRW 179.4 billion in 2023, up 73.4% year on year, before falling to KRW 157.6 billion in 2024, down 12.1%, and KRW 27.8 billion in 2025, down 82.4%.

The year 2023 was the largest year in the 2022–2025 period, and revenue fell for two consecutive years from 2024 through 2025. Operating results moved from an operating loss of KRW 2.43 billion in 2022 to an operating profit of KRW 4.4 billion in 2023, a swing to profit.

Operating profit then fell to KRW 2.92 billion in 2024, down 33.6%, and the company posted an operating loss of KRW 4.27 billion in 2025, a swing to loss. The operating margin was 2.5% in 2023 and 1.9% in 2024, both low single digits, before dropping to -15.4% in 2025.

In 2025, revenue fell 82.4% while the company still posted an operating loss, which points to a heavier fixed-cost burden.

Net income attributable to controlling shareholders went from a loss of KRW 2.86 billion in 2022 and a loss of KRW 3.77 billion in 2023, which widened, to a profit of KRW 4.52 billion in 2024, and then turned to a loss of KRW 25.6 billion in 2025.

Because the 2024 net profit exceeded operating profit of KRW 2.92 billion, non-operating items may have contributed. Net income including non-controlling interests moved in the same direction, from KRW 4.52 billion in 2024 to a net loss of KRW 25.6 billion in 2025.

At the end of 2025, total liabilities were KRW 288 billion and total equity was KRW 14.1 billion, with a debt ratio of 2,041.1%, compared with 707.3% in 2022, 805.4% in 2023 and 690.1% in 2024.

Operating cash flow was -KRW 42.1 billion in 2022, -KRW 15.6 billion in 2023, KRW 90.8 billion in 2024 and -KRW 11.7 billion in 2025, so only 2024 was positive.

On a quarterly basis, the third quarter of 2025 showed revenue of KRW 3.64 billion and an operating loss of KRW 1.93 billion, and the fourth quarter showed revenue of KRW 6.44 billion and operating profit of KRW 141 million, a swing to profit from the prior quarter.

Revenue of KRW 45.9 billion in the first quarter of 2026, up 613.1% from the prior quarter, and operating profit of KRW 6 billion, up 4,161.4%, marked the peak.

Revenue then fell to KRW 8.57 billion in the second quarter, down 81.3%, and to KRW 5.46 billion in the third quarter, down 36.3%, which is two consecutive quarters of decline.

The third quarter of 2026 recorded an operating loss of KRW 642 million, and the sum of net losses attributable to controlling shareholders over the latest four quarters was KRW 31.7 billion.

05

Industry analysis

The construction industry is sensitive to economic cycles and is shaped by government financial and economic policy, housing policy and social infrastructure investment policy.

KD's revenue is tied to housing sales and contract construction, so sales conditions and the permit and construction start pipeline directly drive its results.

When housing supply policy loosens, sales conditions can improve, but interest rates and funding conditions work alongside policy, so the direction depends on the combination of both.

In terms of position, large contractors such as Hyundai Engineering & Construction, DL E&C and Daewoo Engineering & Construction operate with large order backlogs and financial capacity, while KD has total equity of KRW 14.1 billion and a debt ratio of 2,041.1%.

Smaller developers need construction guarantees and financing to secure new sites, and during rehabilitation that access may be constrained. The 2024 structure, in which 73.31% of revenue came from the Gunsan Sinyeok Station area project, also means that progress at one site largely determines company-wide results.

If small and mid-sized builders face unsold inventory amid a weak sales market, KD's results could also swing with the pace of sales at each project.

Losses continued with a net loss of KRW 14 billion in the second quarter of 2026 and KRW 4.52 billion in the third quarter. A weaker earnings base could reduce the resources available to sustain the business during rehabilitation.

Until the debt structure is reorganized under the rehabilitation plan, this burden continues to weigh on results and credibility.

Revenue concentration and quarterly volatility

After revenue of KRW 45.9 billion in the first quarter of 2026, quarterly revenue fell quickly to KRW 8.57 billion in the second quarter and KRW 5.46 billion in the third. When quarterly revenue shifts this sharply, results are hard to predict.
