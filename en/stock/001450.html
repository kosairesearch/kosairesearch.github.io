02

Business structure

HYUNDAI MARINE&FIRE INSURANCE CO is a non-life insurer centered on long-term, general and automobile insurance, and its earnings rest on two pillars: insurance profit from underwriting and investment income from investing premium float.

The company reports profit by business line, and in the third quarter of 2026 long-term insurance profit was KRW 211.0 billion, up 16.2% year on year, while general insurance profit was KRW 65.3 billion, up 85.5%.

Automobile insurance posted a KRW 13.1 billion loss in the same quarter, a sharp narrowing from the KRW 55.3 billion loss a year earlier. Long-term insurance earnings are supported by amortization of the contractual service margin (CSM), which reached KRW 264.0 billion in the quarter, up 7.4%.

The CSM balance at the end of September 2026 was KRW 10.17 trillion, 14.2% higher than at the end of 2025. On new business, cumulative third-quarter new-contract CSM was KRW 1.40 trillion, down 5.8% year on year, and the company said it keeps a profitability-first approach focused on high-CSM product groups.

The investment book covers equities, bonds and alternative investments, and rising rate volatility produced valuation losses in bonds and alternatives in the third quarter, leaving valuation gains and losses at a KRW 125.9 billion loss. The automobile combined ratio improved to 101.6% from 105.7% a year earlier.

On the health insurance side, the company said claims for physical therapy fell by about KRW 6.0 billion a month in connection with the managed-benefit system.

04

Earnings analysis

In the annual figures from DART filings, insurance revenue rose from KRW 12.1 trillion in 2022 to KRW 13.1 trillion in 2023 (up 8.3%), KRW 13.7 trillion in 2024 (up 4.5%) and KRW 14.1 trillion in 2025 (up 3.1%).

Insurance revenue has risen for three consecutive years from 2023 through 2025, with 2025 the largest year and 2022 the smallest in the 2022–2025 period.

Operating profit fell from KRW 1.79 trillion in 2022 to KRW 788.2 billion in 2023 (down 56.0%), then recovered to KRW 1.24 trillion in 2024 (up 57.8%) and KRW 1.26 trillion in 2025 (up 1.4%). Revenue kept growing while profit dipped in 2023 and rebounded in 2024, and the 2025 increase narrowed to 1.4%.

Profit attributable to owners of the parent fell from KRW 1.29 trillion in 2022 to KRW 607.8 billion in 2023 (down 53.1%), then rose two consecutive years through 2025 to KRW 1.02 trillion, with 2024 up 39.9% and 2025 up 19.9%.

The debt-to-equity ratio was 877.8% at the end of 2025, below 923.0% in 2024 but well above 386.5% in 2022. Operating cash flow reached KRW 3.53 trillion in 2025, against KRW 910.8 billion in 2022.

On a quarterly basis, operating profit was KRW 707.7 billion in the third quarter of 2025, then the fourth quarter posted an operating loss of KRW 127.9 billion.

The first quarter of 2026 returned to operating profit at KRW 324.5 billion, rose 62.9% to KRW 528.7 billion in the second quarter, and fell 47.8% to KRW 275.8 billion in the third quarter.

Profit attributable to owners of the parent also fell from KRW 418.3 billion in the second quarter to KRW 210.3 billion in the third quarter.

On a separate basis, as the company announced, third-quarter investment income turned into a KRW 27.8 billion loss, driven mainly by valuation losses on bonds and alternative assets. Attributable net profit for the last four quarters totals KRW 750.5 billion.

05

Industry analysis

The non-life insurance cycle is currently driven more by loss ratios and policy variables than by premium levels.

For health insurance, the company said claims for physical therapy fell by about KRW 6.0 billion a month and claims for extracorporeal shockwave therapy by about KRW 1.0 billion a month under the managed-benefit system, while claims for alternative treatments rose by KRW 3.0 to 4.0 billion a month, meaning part of the policy effect is offset by a shift in treatment type.

Automobile insurance posted a cumulative loss of KRW 23.3 billion for the first nine months, as accumulated premium cuts and higher repair costs weighed on results, although the third-quarter combined ratio improved to 101.6% from 105.7%.

The gap between expected and actual claims narrowed to a KRW 47.8 billion loss in the third quarter of 2026 from KRW 90.5 billion a year earlier, which the company attributed to a smaller loss in its health insurance portfolio.

On the financial-market side, wider interest-rate volatility has enlarged valuation losses on bonds and alternative investments, raising the volatility of insurers' investment income.

In cycle terms, loss ratios have stabilized and underwriting profit is recovering, but automobile and health policy variables can still change results from quarter to quarter.

Other listed non-life insurers face the same policy and rate exposures, so Hyundai Marine's position is best gauged by the pace of long-term insurance profit improvement and the narrowing of its automobile insurance loss.

06

Outlook

The company reiterated a target of KRW 100 billion in investment income per quarter and KRW 400 billion for the year, and it confirmed that target in its third-quarter results briefing.

Cumulative third-quarter investment yield was 2.22%, down 0.45 percentage points from 2.67% a year earlier, and the company said it plans to raise yields by focusing on high-quality assets such as corporate loans.

On capital, the K-ICS ratio stood at 210.3% at the end of the third quarter of 2026, up 20.2 percentage points from the end of 2025. The base capital ratio was 81.4%, down 2.4 percentage points from the second quarter, and it would be 93.2% if policy reserves for surrender values were fully recognized.

On dividends, KB Securities said in an August 2026 report that distributable profit could be secured in 2027 without special policy changes. The same firm's November report said distributable profit had widened to roughly minus KRW 1.0 trillion because of larger OCI valuation losses.

On new business, the company said it will keep its portfolio centered on high-CSM products, and the new-business CSM multiple fell to 16.4 times in the third quarter from 17.2 times in the prior quarter.

The next checkpoints are the distributable profit and capital ratios recorded in the 2026 annual report, and whether investment income turns positive in the first quarter of 2027.

08

Bull factors

Structural improvement in underwriting profit

Long-term insurance profit reached KRW 211.0 billion in the third quarter, up 16.2% year on year, supported by steadily rising CSM amortization. General insurance profit rose 85.5% to KRW 65.3 billion.

Narrower losses in automobile insurance and in the gap between expected and actual claims point to improvement in underwriting.

Capital headroom and CSM accumulation

The K-ICS ratio rose to 210.3% at the end of the third quarter of 2026, up 20.2 percentage points from the end of 2025. The CSM balance reached KRW 10.17 trillion, up 14.2% from the end of 2025, building the base for future insurance profit. The company attributed part of the capital improvement to growth in its held CSM.

Broker view: expected continuation of earnings improvement

KB Securities kept a buy rating in its November 16, 2026 report. The same report said it expects the earnings improvement momentum in underwriting profit to continue into 2027. KB Securities set a target price of KRW 53,000 in its November 16, 2026 report.

09

Bear factors

Volatility in investment income

Third-quarter investment income turned into a KRW 27.8 billion loss from a profit a year earlier. Valuation losses were KRW 125.9 billion, and cumulative investment yield fell 0.45 percentage points to 2.22%. Investment weakness was cited as the reason third-quarter net profit declined from a year earlier.

Uncertainty over dividend resumption

KB Securities pointed out that distributable profit widened to roughly minus KRW 1.0 trillion as OCI valuation losses grew. The same report said this is expected to weaken the momentum of the share price recovery. The timing of any dividend resumption depends on when distributable profit recovers.

Softer new-business profitability indicators

Cumulative third-quarter new-business CSM was KRW 1.40 trillion, down 5.8% year on year. The new-business CSM multiple fell to 16.4 times in the third quarter from 17.2 times in the prior quarter. Automobile insurance remained in loss at KRW 23.3 billion cumulatively and has not yet returned to profit.

10

Risk factors

Quarterly profit volatility risk

Operating profit was a loss of KRW 127.9 billion in the fourth quarter of 2025, so whether the fourth quarter of 2026 stays in profit is not assured.

Quarterly operating profit in 2026 was KRW 324.5 billion, KRW 528.7 billion and KRW 275.8 billion, a wide spread that can change the annual profit path from quarter to quarter. The size of the underwriting recovery may also depend on how long-term and automobile insurance results are reflected at year-end. The preliminary fourth-quarter and full-year 2026 results are the first checkpoint for this.

Policy risk (health and automobile insurance)

Loss ratios and the gap between expected and actual claims can shift with policy variables such as the managed-benefit system and automobile premium rules. According to the company, physical therapy claims fell while alternative treatment claims rose by KRW 3.0 to 4.0 billion a month. Whether the policy effect is offset by a shift in treatment type is the next item to verify.

Reserve policy and capital risk

If the policy on reserves for surrender values is not revised, the base capital ratio may remain near the 81.4% recorded at the end of the third quarter of 2026. If the reserves were fully recognized, the converted ratio would be 93.2%, so the policy decision could change how much capital headroom is perceived.

No timing has been set for the announcement, but once it is made, the K-ICS ratio and base-capital measures will need to be reassessed together.

12

Overall view

The DART-based annual figures show insurance revenue rising to KRW 14.1 trillion in 2025, with operating profit recovering in 2024 and 2025, which points to a stronger core underwriting base. Even after the 2023 low, quarterly profit remains highly sensitive to investment and valuation results.

Operating profit in 2026 was KRW 324.5 billion in the first quarter, KRW 528.7 billion in the second and KRW 275.8 billion in the third, a wide spread from quarter to quarter.

Improvement in long-term and general insurance profit, a narrower automobile loss and a smaller gap between expected and actual claims support the underwriting case.

On the other side, valuation losses on alternative assets, distributable profit in negative territory and a slowdown in new-business CSM are the weak links for earnings quality and shareholder returns.

Broker views are mixed; KB Securities kept a buy rating with a KRW 53,000 target on November 16, 2026, and expects the earnings improvement to continue into 2027. The next checkpoints are distributable profit and capital ratios in the 2026 annual report, and investment income in the first quarter of 2027.
