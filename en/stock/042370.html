02

Business structure

Vitzro Tech is an electric and electronics company organised around three business segments: power equipment, batteries and special applications. According to a third-party breakdown by Butler, the share of 2026 second-quarter revenue was 63% batteries, 32% power equipment and 5% special applications.

The power equipment segment develops and manufactures medium-voltage equipment such as electrical control devices and switchgear, with Korea Electric Power Corporation (KEPCO) and power generation subsidiaries among its main customers.

The battery segment sits under the subsidiary Vitzrocell, whose main product is industrial primary batteries used in defence, medical and industrial applications. Securities coverage describes its lithium primary batteries as holding the global number one share in utility meters and smart grid applications.

The special applications segment develops equipment and solutions based on plasma application technology and ultra-precise vacuum joining, extending into space and fusion energy.

Space-related business is run by the subsidiary Vitzro Nextech, in which Vitzro Tech held 69% of the common shares as of its November 2025 filing.

In January 2026 Vitzro Nextech signed a KRW 27.27 billion integrated wind tunnel equipment contract with the Space Aerodynamics Integrated Wind Tunnel Center, running to 31 October 2029. In December 2024 the subsidiary Vitzro Mill-Tech was sold, and in April 2025 the chief executive changed.

In power equipment, the company is compared with LS ELECTRIC, Hyosung Heavy Industries and HD Hyundai Electric in the same electrical equipment industry.

04

Earnings analysis

Revenue was KRW 347.7 billion in 2022, KRW 352.9 billion in 2023 (up 1.5% year on year), KRW 428.3 billion in 2024 (up 21.4%) and KRW 432.3 billion in 2025 (up 0.9%). It rose for three consecutive years from 2023 to 2025, with 2025 the largest year and 2022 the smallest in the 2022 to 2025 period.

Growth was concentrated in 2024 and slowed to 0.9% in 2025. Operating profit was KRW 32.7 billion in 2022, fell to KRW 28.8 billion in 2023 (down 11.9%), then rose to KRW 51.1 billion in 2024 (up 77.4%) and KRW 55.7 billion in 2025 (up 8.9%), increasing for two consecutive years.

In 2024 operating profit grew far faster than revenue, so margin gains drove the improvement more than sales growth. The operating margin was 9.4% in 2022, 8.2% in 2023, 11.9% in 2024 and 12.9% in 2025.

Net income attributable to controlling shareholders moved from KRW 19.5 billion in 2022 to a net loss of KRW 811 million in 2023, turned to a profit of KRW 41.5 billion in 2024, and reached KRW 42.7 billion in 2025 (up 2.8%).

Net income including non-controlling interests was KRW 22.4 billion in 2023, a wide gap from the controlling-shareholder loss, which reflects the holding structure in which part of subsidiary profit belongs to outside shareholders.

The debt-to-equity ratio fell from 54.1% in 2022 to 32.8% in 2025, and operating cash flow bottomed at KRW 16.5 billion in 2023 before reaching KRW 80.6 billion in 2025.

Quarterly revenue was KRW 102.5 billion with operating profit of KRW 15.3 billion in the third quarter of 2025, then rose to KRW 127.6 billion (up 24.6% quarter on quarter) with operating profit of KRW 18.3 billion (up 19.4%) in the fourth quarter.

It fell to KRW 105.6 billion and KRW 13.3 billion in the first quarter of 2026 (down 17.2% and 27.2%), recovered to KRW 123.2 billion revenue in the second quarter with KRW 11.8 billion operating profit (down 11.2%), and in the third quarter of 2026 reached KRW 113.8 billion revenue (down 7.6% quarter on quarter, up 11.1% year on year) and KRW 18 billion operating profit (up 52.0% quarter on quarter, up 17.4% year on year).

Net income attributable to controlling shareholders for the last four quarters, from the fourth quarter of 2025 to the third quarter of 2026, totalled KRW 36.9 billion.

05

Industry analysis

The power equipment segment sells mainly to a procurement structure centred on KEPCO and power generation companies, so quarterly revenue tends to swing with the timing of orders.

The battery segment belongs to the lithium battery market, where demand from electric vehicles and energy storage systems is rising, and a securities report carried by Pinpoint News said that technological barriers and quality competitiveness could support long-term growth.

In lithium primary batteries, Vitzro Tech is described as one of the world's three largest producers, according to WiseReport company information.

FnGuide's company summary puts the expected annual growth of the space launch market at 13.22% and of fusion energy at 7.4%, citing reusable rockets, private entrants and rising investment.

Domestically, Daily Money reported that the success of the fourth Nuri launch and the opening of the Korea Aerospace Administration increased interest in space-related stocks.

In power equipment, the company operates in the same industry as large electrical equipment makers such as LS ELECTRIC, Hyosung Heavy Industries and HD Hyundai Electric, with a position centred on specific devices such as switchgear and electrical control equipment.

Pinpoint News also reported that the battery segment is expanding exports of high-reliability batteries specialised for defence and aviation. A company analysis report describes its differentiation as specialty products built on in-house technology and certifications.

Outlook

The representative space-related order is the integrated wind tunnel equipment contract signed on 16 January 2026 with the Space Aerodynamics Integrated Wind Tunnel Center, worth KRW 27.27 billion.

Because the contract runs for about three years and nine months until 31 October 2029, the pace at which it shows up in results will depend on project progress each quarter.

On 26 December 2025, a contract with the Korea Institute of Fusion Energy for helium refrigeration and recovery equipment for a superconducting conductor test facility, worth KRW 6.31 billion, was disclosed.

A supply contract for high-pressure combustors for next-generation launch vehicles with Hanwha Aerospace, starting in March 2026, was reported at about KRW 4.5 billion.

Pinpoint-style coverage by The Elec reported that the subsidiary Vitzrocell has set a 2030 target of KRW 600 billion in revenue and KRW 160 billion in operating profit, and these are the subsidiary's own targets.

The same report said third-quarter revenue rose 27% and that annual revenue of KRW 300 billion could be reached for the first time in its history. In June 2026, filings on a subsidiary share cancellation decision and a treasury share disposal decision showed capital structure changes continuing at the subsidiary level.

Second-quarter 2026 revenue of KRW 123.2 billion recovered from the first quarter, and third-quarter operating profit rose to KRW 18 billion, so the next point to check is how much order-based revenue feeds into quarterly results.

08

Bull factors

Operating profit recovery and a higher margin

Operating profit rose for two consecutive years from KRW 28.8 billion in 2023, reaching KRW 51.1 billion in 2024 and KRW 55.7 billion in 2025. The operating margin was 12.9% in 2025, the highest of the 2022 to 2025 period.

Third-quarter 2026 operating profit of KRW 18 billion, up 17.4% year on year, shows the recovery continuing in quarterly results.

Expanding space and fusion-related orders

Subsidiary Vitzro Nextech signed a KRW 27.27 billion wind tunnel contract in January 2026 and a KRW 6.31 billion fusion-related contract in December 2025. A combustor supply contract with Hanwha Aerospace, reported at about KRW 4.5 billion, has also been reported. Space-related revenue could be added to the battery and power equipment mix, which is the main bullish factor.

Improved balance sheet and cash generation

The debt-to-equity ratio fell from 54.1% in 2022 to 32.8% in 2025, while operating cash flow rose from KRW 51.7 billion to KRW 80.6 billion over the same period. Total equity stood at KRW 487.6 billion at the end of 2025. This cash generation could support investment in the business and shareholder returns.

09

Bear factors

Slower revenue growth and quarterly swings

Revenue rose only 0.9% in 2025 to KRW 432.3 billion, a sharp slowdown from the 21.4% increase in 2024. First-quarter 2026 revenue of KRW 105.6 billion fell 17.2% quarter on quarter, recovered to KRW 123.2 billion in the second quarter, and then dropped to KRW 113.8 billion in the third. The lack of a steady direction in quarterly revenue is the main bearish factor.

A capital structure with a large non-controlling share

The controlling-shareholder net loss of KRW 811 million in 2023 contrasted sharply with net income of KRW 22.4 billion including non-controlling interests. Controlling-shareholder equity of KRW 258.8 billion is only a little over half of total equity of KRW 487.6 billion at the end of 2025.

Because part of subsidiary ownership sits with outside holders, part of the earnings does not fully accrue to parent shareholders.

Concentration and group restructuring

The battery segment accounts for 63% of second-quarter 2026 revenue on Butler's breakdown, so results are sensitive to demand for industrial primary batteries and to export conditions. Mergers and disposals within the group recurred through 2024 and 2025, changing the boundaries of the businesses. Order-based revenue can be lumpy, because individual contracts are large relative to the subsidiary's sales.

10

Risk factors

Subsidiary mid-term targets

Vitzrocell has been reported to target KRW 600 billion in revenue and KRW 160 billion in operating profit by 2030, and these are the subsidiary's own targets. The same reports said third-quarter revenue rose 27% and that annual revenue of KRW 300 billion could be reached for the first time in its history.

Reaching the targets requires battery demand to continue over the medium term, so slower growth than expected could weaken the growth contribution the battery segment makes to consolidated results. Any gap between the targets and actual results will need to be checked against later disclosures.

Operating margin sustainability

The operating margin rose from 8.2% in 2023 to 12.9% in 2025, and the 2024 profit increase came largely from margin gains. Because the profit recovery rests on margin, a lower margin could limit operating profit growth even if revenue expands.

If space-related orders change the revenue mix, the existing margin structure could also shift. Whether the annual operating margin in 2026 holds near 12.9% will determine the direction of profit.

Industry and policy dependence

The battery business depends on defence and industrial demand and on export conditions, and it is still early to confirm whether expected demand from electric vehicles and energy storage turns into reported results. Space-related business is highly dependent on government budgets and launch schedules. Power equipment orders follow the investment plans of KEPCO and power generation companies.

12

Overall view

Vitzro Tech's operating profit rose in both 2024 and 2025 after its 2023 low, and the operating margin of 12.9% in 2025 was the highest of the 2022 to 2025 period. Net income attributable to controlling shareholders also moved from a 2023 loss to a 2024 profit and reached KRW 42.7 billion in 2025.

On the other side, revenue growth slowed to 0.9% in 2025, and quarterly revenue in 2026 has moved down, up and down again. The business mix is weighted to batteries at 63% of second-quarter 2026 revenue on Butler's breakdown, with space-related business added on top.

Space orders such as the wind tunnel contract have improved visibility, but quarterly results could shift with the pace of contract execution. The large non-controlling interest is also a structural feature to read alongside consolidated results.

The next checkpoints are the annual operating margin to be fixed in the 2026 annual report and the revenue direction in the first-quarter 2027 report, with how much order-based revenue feeds into quarterly results as the key item to track.
