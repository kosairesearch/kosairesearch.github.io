Kyobo Securities: Cumulative Profit Up, Q3 Sharply Lower

Kyobo Securities' cumulative operating profit and profit attributable to owners rose through the first three quarters of 2026, but Q3 profit attributable to owners fell to KRW 8.51 billion as fair-value financial instrument losses widened.

02

Business structure

Kyobo Securities is a financial investment company founded in 1949 that engages in investment trading, brokerage, collective investment, advisory, discretionary investment and trust businesses.

Its main segments are brokerage, proprietary trading, derivatives and investment banking, and it holds 63 unlisted subsidiaries. Its parent is a life insurer.

In Q1 2026, fair-value financial instruments accounted for 83.73% of operating revenue, so the top line can move sharply while net profit swings with valuation and disposal gains and losses. The wealth management (WM) unit expanded its high-net-worth client base and diversified customized products to raise fee income.

The investment banking (IB) unit secured IPO and rights offering underwriting mandates and widened project financing (PF) focused on the Seoul metropolitan area and public and rental housing.

In Q1 2026, brokerage operating profit from entrusted trading reached KRW 43.8 billion, more than five times the KRW 8.3 billion of a year earlier, and listed and OTC derivatives swung to KRW 46.6 billion of profit from a KRW 24.7 billion loss.

Proprietary trading profit fell to KRW 17.1 billion from KRW 72 billion, and IB profit fell to KRW 19.1 billion from KRW 22.9 billion.

It competes with Mirae Asset Securities, NH Investment & Securities and Samsung Securities, and industry observers highlight its focus on WM, trading and derivatives as higher-margin areas.

04

Earnings analysis

Annual results show a clear turnaround after 2022. Revenue rose from KRW 4.07 trillion in 2022 to KRW 3.74 trillion in 2023 (down 7.9%) and KRW 3.17 trillion in 2024 (down 15.4%), then reached KRW 4.51 trillion in 2025 (up 42.3%), the largest year in the 2022 to 2025 span.

Operating profit rose from KRW 51.7 billion in 2022 to KRW 70.3 billion in 2023 (up 36.1%), KRW 113.9 billion in 2024 (up 62.1%) and KRW 190.4 billion in 2025 (up 67.2%), with operating profit increasing three years in a row from 2023 through 2025.

Operating margin moved from 1.3% to 1.9%, 3.6% and 4.2% over the same period. Profit attributable to owners rose from KRW 43.3 billion to KRW 67.6 billion, KRW 117.7 billion and KRW 142.9 billion, with 2025 up 21.4%.

Operating cash flow was KRW -929.1 billion in 2022, KRW -1.49 trillion in 2023, KRW 710.8 billion in 2024 and KRW -897 billion in 2025, negative in every year except 2024.

The debt-to-equity ratio moved from 759.1% in 2022 and 700.6% in 2024 to 809.5% at end-2025, when total liabilities were KRW 17.2 trillion against equity of KRW 2.12 trillion.

Quarterly, revenue went from KRW 830.4 billion and operating profit of KRW 40.6 billion in Q3 2025 to KRW 12.1 billion operating profit in Q4 2025. Q1 2026 revenue was KRW 2.4 trillion with operating profit of KRW 95.9 billion, and Q2 2026 operating profit of KRW 116 billion was the highest quarter.

In Q3 2026, revenue was KRW 1.65 trillion (up 98.6% year on year) while operating profit was KRW 17.7 billion (down 56.4%). Fair-value financial instrument losses of KRW 1.43 trillion exceeded gains of KRW 1.29 trillion, SG&A rose 34.6% to KRW 87.4 billion, and operating expenses rose 106.5%. Profit attributable to owners over the latest four quarters totals KRW 173.1 billion.

05

Industry analysis

Brokerage earnings in the securities industry depend heavily on trading volumes and market volatility. In its Q1 2026 results, Kyobo Securities cited a favorable domestic equity market as a factor behind growth in WM and trading.

In Q3 2026, losses on financial instrument valuation and disposal widened as market volatility increased.

Cumulative entrusted trading fee income for the first three quarters reached KRW 215 billion, up 98.2% from KRW 108.5 billion a year earlier, showing how a stronger equity market lifted brokerage income in the first half.

Volatility in bonds and financial markets widens the range of gains and losses in the trading book. IB fees depend on IPO and rights offering conditions, and PF is sensitive to property markets and interest rates, with the company focusing on the Seoul metropolitan area and public and rental housing.

Industry observers see the company's focus on WM, trading and derivatives as higher-margin areas and expect its growth to stand out among mid-sized securities firms if IB recovers.

Compared with listed peers such as Mirae Asset Securities, NH Investment & Securities and Samsung Securities, its larger share of fair-value instruments means its earnings are more volatile.

06

Outlook

The company has said it will keep expanding its earnings base through a more diversified business portfolio and a stable risk management system.

Reporting in 2025 described a medium-term goal of KRW 3 trillion in equity by 2029 and KRW 4 trillion by 2031, which would support a designation as a super-large investment bank.

As a comprehensive investment business company, it could offer prime brokerage services and raise its corporate credit extension limit from 100% to 200% of equity. The pace of equity growth depends on earnings accumulation and gains or losses on fair-value instruments.

Through the first nine months of 2026, core operating income from fees and interest held roughly at prior-year levels. Whether the Q3 rise in SG&A proves temporary is the next point to watch. Continued growth in high-net-worth clients and IPO and rights offering mandates could raise the floor of fee income.

Repeated losses on fair-value instruments would narrow the room for cumulative earnings gains. Full-year results will be confirmed in the 2026 annual report, and for Q1 2027 the key test is whether brokerage and derivatives profit holds near Q1 2026 levels.

08

Bull factors

Cumulative profit growth and fee income expansion

Cumulative operating profit for the first three quarters reached KRW 229.6 billion, up 28.7%, and profit attributable to owners reached KRW 167.0 billion, up 22.0%. Cumulative entrusted trading fee income rose 98.2% to KRW 215 billion.

A stronger first-half equity market lifted these fees, while WM's broader high-net-worth client base and more customized products added support.

Share price relative to book equity

The share price trades below 1x price-to-book, so it sits below reported book equity. Equity of KRW 2.12 trillion at end-2025 is entirely attributable to owners of the parent. This level is cited by some as a basis for a constructive reading of the stock.

Medium-term equity growth and comprehensive investment business goal

Reporting in 2025 described a goal of KRW 3 trillion in equity by 2029 and KRW 4 trillion by 2031, leading to super-large investment bank status. Comprehensive investment business status would allow prime brokerage services and raise the credit extension limit. Operating profit rose 67.2% to KRW 190.4 billion in 2025, and operating margin reached 4.2%.

09

Bear factors

Sharp single-quarter decline in Q3

Q3 2026 operating profit fell to KRW 17.7 billion, down 56.4% year on year and 84.7% quarter on quarter. Losses of KRW 1.43 trillion on fair-value instruments exceeded gains of KRW 1.29 trillion, producing a net loss of KRW 141.7 billion in that segment. Profit attributable to owners fell to KRW 8.51 billion, down 72.4%.

Rising costs and thin operating margin

Operating expenses in Q3 2026 rose 106.5% to KRW 1.63 trillion from KRW 789.8 billion, outpacing revenue growth. SG&A increased 34.6% to KRW 87.4 billion. Operating margin improved to 4.2% in 2025 but remains thin, so cost increases weigh directly on profit.

Higher leverage and volatile cash flow

The debt-to-equity ratio rose to 809.5% at end-2025 from 700.6% in 2024, with total liabilities of KRW 17.2 trillion. Whether operating cash flow turns positive is the next point to check.

IB operating profit in Q1 2026 fell to KRW 19.1 billion from KRW 22.9 billion, so recovery in investment banking remains an open task.

10

Risk factors

Market volatility and trading volume dependence

Brokerage and entrusted trading fee income depend heavily on trading volumes. The Q1 2026 results cited a favorable domestic equity market as a growth driver, while Q3 saw higher market volatility. A shrinking trading volume could reverse the brokerage gains.

Fair-value financial instrument valuation gains and losses

Fair-value financial instruments accounted for 83.73% of operating revenue in Q1 2026. This segment posted a net loss of KRW 141.7 billion in Q3 2026, raising earnings volatility. Quarterly results can shift sharply with the direction of valuation gains and losses.

Financial leverage and PF exposure

The debt-to-equity ratio stood at 809.5% at end-2025, with total liabilities of KRW 17.2 trillion. The IB unit has been expanding PF focused on the Seoul metropolitan area and on public and rental housing. If property markets or interest rates raise provisioning burdens, capital capacity could narrow.

12

Overall view

Full-year 2025 results rose on all three measures: revenue of KRW 4.51 trillion (up 42.3%), operating profit of KRW 190.4 billion (up 67.2%) and profit attributable to owners of KRW 142.9 billion (up 21.4%).

Cumulative operating profit of KRW 229.6 billion and profit attributable to owners of KRW 167.0 billion for the first three quarters of 2026 also rose year on year.

However, Q3 2026 single-quarter results fell sharply, with operating profit of KRW 17.7 billion and profit attributable to owners of KRW 8.51 billion, due to fair-value instrument losses and higher SG&A.

Fee-based brokerage, derivatives and WM income has grown, but the share of fair-value instruments remains high, keeping earnings volatile. On the balance sheet, the 809.5% debt-to-equity ratio and negative operating cash flow at end-2025 remain open questions.

Valuation sits below book equity, but earnings volatility and leverage define how that gap should be interpreted. The next checkpoints are full-year operating margin and fair-value instrument gains in the 2026 annual report, and brokerage and derivatives profit in Q1 2027. Growth in cumulative earnings and the equity expansion target are weighed against the Q3 loss and debt burden.
