02

Business structure

Winpac, founded in 2002, is an outsourced semiconductor back-end company that performs both packaging, which mounts and seals chips on substrates, and testing, which verifies the performance of finished semiconductors.

Many domestic back-end providers focus on one of packaging or testing, whereas Winpac has both processes in place and can receive back-end orders from chipmakers and fabless companies in a single contract.

Its production base is in Yongin, Gyeonggi Province, and its P campus packages products such as DRAM, NAND flash and embedded universal flash memory (eUFS). Press reports said packaging capacity rose from 44 million units a month to 50 million units.

Its main processes cover packaging products such as FBGA, WLP and MCP, along with semiconductor testing. Winpac resumed business with SK hynix after four years, and according to press reports DDR4 and DDR5 DRAM flip-chip packaging volume was reallocated to Winpac.

The company plans to raise the share of test revenue through a turnkey service that links wafer test, package and final test. Reports also said it repaid more than KRW 40 billion of debt by the end of 2025, easing its financing cost burden. Its newly pursued business includes a diabetic insulin patch project.

04

Earnings analysis

On a disclosed basis, revenue fell from KRW 152.6 billion in 2022 to KRW 86.2 billion in 2023 (down 43.5% year on year), then to KRW 74.1 billion in 2024 (down 14.0%), before edging up to KRW 75.5 billion in 2025 (up 1.9%); 2022 was the largest year and 2024 the smallest.

Operating profit of KRW 1.92 billion in 2022 turned into an operating loss of KRW 22.9 billion in 2023, followed by a KRW 23.3 billion loss in 2024 (losses continued, with the loss widening) and a KRW 14.3 billion loss in 2025 (losses continued, with the loss narrowing).

The operating margin was -26.5% in 2023 and -31.4% in 2024 before easing to -18.9% in 2025. Net loss attributable to controlling shareholders was KRW 1.57 billion in 2022, KRW 33.3 billion in 2023, KRW 30 billion in 2024 and KRW 13.7 billion in 2025, which is four consecutive years of losses from 2022 to 2025.

The net loss including non-controlling interests was disclosed at the same amounts over the same period. Operating cash flow went from KRW 18.9 billion in 2022 to outflows of KRW 10.4 billion in 2023, KRW 10.1 billion in 2024 and KRW 6.23 billion in 2025.

The company attributed the main 2025 change to operating efficiency and business structure improvement, and press reports said unnecessary facilities and assets were sold and voluntary retirements reduced fixed costs.

Quarterly, revenue of KRW 21.4 billion and an operating loss of KRW 2.96 billion in 3Q 2025 widened to an operating loss of KRW 2.99 billion on revenue of KRW 21.2 billion in 4Q 2025; 1Q 2026 revenue was KRW 22.2 billion with an operating loss of KRW 2.06 billion, and 2Q 2026 revenue rose 36.1% from the prior quarter to KRW 30.2 billion with operating profit of KRW 795 million.

In 3Q 2026 revenue was KRW 27.9 billion (down 7.5% from the prior quarter, up 30.2% year on year), operating profit was KRW 220 million (down 72.3% from the prior quarter), and the net loss attributable to controlling shareholders was KRW 1.12 billion, a return to loss. The net loss attributable to controlling shareholders over the last four quarters totals KRW 6.82 billion.

05

Industry analysis

Demand for memory back-end work has clearly recovered in 2026. According to the company's earnings commentary, demand for back-end processing of DRAM, NAND flash and MCP products increased, and first-half 2026 revenue rose sharply. The recovery is attributed to a stabilization in wafer supply.

In 2025, volatile wafer prices made customers wary of inventory risk and reduce orders, which cut back-end volume. In 1Q 2026 the packaging utilization rate was 64.88% and the test utilization rate was 32.54%, both well above the same period of 2025.

Press coverage said test demand has not fully recovered and is expected to grow as memory volume increases. HBM is drawing attention, but most HBM is produced in-house by chipmakers, so the company sees the recovery in general-purpose memory as the more direct benefit.

Winpac is also pursuing pass-through of higher gold wire prices into its product pricing. Order volume, plant utilization and depreciation burden determine how fast profitability recovers. Within domestic back-end providers, the ability to handle both packaging and testing is presented as Winpac's differentiator.

Outlook

The company described the 2Q 2026 return to profit as evidence that its business structure improvements have taken hold. It said it aims to raise shareholder value through sustained revenue growth and improved profitability.

It plans to expand its non-memory wafer test business, which it cites as a new growth driver, to raise the share of test revenue. The company explained that it intends to grow revenue through a turnkey service linking wafer test, package and final test.

In a first-half 2026 press report, a company official said packaging requests from customers were normalizing as wafer supply stabilized. In the same report, the official said the company expects 2026 results to improve meaningfully over 2025 if current trends continue.

The company is also pursuing pass-through of higher gold wire prices into product pricing. When the fiscal year ends on December 31, 2026, fourth-quarter utilization and the trend in test revenue share will be totaled into annual results.

The next checkpoint is how the SK hynix volume shows up in revenue in 4Q 2026 and in 2027 quarters. Whether the company's stated direction matches its reported quarterly results will be checked against the periodic report figures.

Bull factors

Memory volume reallocation and utilization recovery

Press reports said SK hynix reallocated DDR4 and DDR5 DRAM flip-chip packaging volume to Winpac for the first time in four years. Packaging utilization was 64.88% in 1Q 2026, well above 20.6% in the same quarter of 2025. A company official said customer orders are normalizing as wafer supply stabilizes.

Cost reduction and balance sheet repair

The operating loss narrowed to KRW 14.3 billion in 2025 from KRW 23.3 billion in 2024. Press reports said the company sold facilities and assets, cut fixed costs through voluntary retirements, and repaid more than KRW 40 billion of debt to ease interest burden. The debt-to-equity ratio fell from 220.0% in 2023 to 107.8% in 2025.

2Q profit turn and test expansion

Revenue was KRW 30.2 billion in 2Q 2026, and operating profit was KRW 795 million, a turn to profit. The company said it plans to raise the share of test revenue through a turnkey service linking wafer test to final test. Test utilization was 32.54% in 1Q 2026, up from 11.0% in the same quarter of 2025.

Bear factors

Profit pulled back in 3Q

Operating profit fell to KRW 220 million in 3Q 2026, down 72.3% from the prior quarter. Net income attributable to controlling shareholders swung from a KRW 127 million profit in 2Q 2026 to a KRW 1.12 billion loss in 3Q. The failure of the 2Q profit to carry into the next quarter is cited as a bearish point.

Accumulated losses and cash outflows

Operating cash flow was negative in each year from 2023 to 2025, and total liabilities stood at KRW 67.8 billion at the end of 2025. Revenue of KRW 75.5 billion in 2025 is roughly half the KRW 152.6 billion recorded in 2022.

Dependence on the cycle and a fixed-cost structure

Press reports said memory back-end utilization fell to 20 to 25% when customer losses coincided with a chip downturn. When utilization drops, fixed costs such as depreciation on expensive equipment flow straight into losses. Some view the direct benefit from HBM as limited because most HBM is produced in-house by chipmakers.

Risk factors

Customer and cycle concentration

Revenue depends heavily on the memory cycle and wafer supply. In 2025, customers reduced orders as wafer price volatility increased, according to the company's explanation. Whether the reallocated volume is temporary or a full-year trend will become clear from the revenue trend in the annual report.

Financial liquidity

Operating cash flow was negative from 2023 through 2025, and total liabilities were KRW 67.8 billion at the end of 2025. Press reports say debt repayment and asset sales eased the burden, but whether cash generation has recovered alongside the profit turn will show in operating cash flow in the next annual report.

Cost and utilization variables

The pass-through of higher gold wire costs into product prices is described only as an ongoing effort. How much of it shows up in results will be visible in later quarterly reports. A drop in utilization could again swing profit sharply because of depreciation costs.

Overall view

Winpac, founded in 2002, is a semiconductor back-end company that combines packaging and testing. Revenue fell from KRW 152.6 billion in 2022 to KRW 75.5 billion in 2025, and operating losses and net losses attributable to controlling shareholders continued from 2023 through 2025.

The operating loss narrowed to KRW 14.3 billion in 2025 from KRW 23.3 billion in 2024, and operating profit of KRW 795 million in 2Q 2026 marked a turn to profit.

In 3Q 2026 operating profit fell to KRW 220 million and the company posted a net loss of KRW 1.12 billion, so whether profit can persist quarter to quarter is still a short data series. Press reports say packaging utilization recovered to 64.88% in 1Q 2026, and SK hynix volume reallocation has been reported.

Strengths include volume recovery and cost reduction, while weaknesses include slow test demand recovery, a KRW 6.82 billion net loss attributable to controlling shareholders over the last four quarters, and negative operating cash flow.

The debt-to-equity ratio fell from 220.0% in 2023 to 107.8% in 2025, but total liabilities were KRW 67.8 billion at the end of 2025. The next checkpoints are the annual results released after the 2026 fiscal year closes and the operating margin and operating cash flow in the 2026 annual report.
