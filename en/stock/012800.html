02

Business structure

Daechang is a copper-alloy non-ferrous metal manufacturer founded in 1974, centered on brass rod. According to company profile data, brass rod made up 94.60% of the main product sales mix, with merchandise and other items at 5.40%.

In the domestic and export breakdown as of December 2025, brass rod sales were split 45.79% domestic and 54.21% export, so exports exceed half. A 2022 news report described brass rod as a base material for semiconductors, electrical and electronic components, automobiles, ships and industrial machinery.

In the third quarter 2022 report, brass rod accounted for 90.9% of sales, and the same period's coverage cited a 44% share of the domestic brass rod market. Apart from brass rod, other items are only about 5% of sales, so product concentration is high.

In February 2026, a company official said demand from semiconductors and automobiles and pre-buying ahead of copper price increases were lifting order volumes. Because copper is the key raw material, the gap between copper cost and selling price directly drives margins.

Poongsan and Korea Zinc operate in the same metals sector but with broader business mixes, which limits direct comparison. The headquarters is in Siheung, Gyeonggi, and headcount was 328 as of June 2026.

04

Earnings analysis

Annual revenue fell from KRW 1.44 trillion in 2022 to KRW 1.32 trillion in 2023, down 8.2% year on year, then rose to KRW 1.34 trillion in 2024, up 1.0%, and KRW 1.47 trillion in 2025, up 10.0%.

After the 2023 low, revenue has grown for two consecutive years from 2024 through 2025, and 2025 was the largest year of the 2022 to 2025 period.

Operating profit went from KRW 9.09 billion in 2022 to an operating loss of KRW 9.27 billion in 2023, turned to a profit of KRW 31.3 billion in 2024, and reached KRW 51.5 billion in 2025, up 64.4%.

The operating margin moved from 0.6% in 2022 to -0.7% in 2023, 2.3% in 2024 and 3.5% in 2025, the highest of the four years. Net profit attributable to controlling shareholders was KRW 3.7 billion in 2022, a net loss of KRW 19.4 billion in 2023, KRW 4.39 billion in 2024, and KRW 2.13 billion in 2025, down 51.5%.

Operating profit rose while profit attributable to controlling shareholders fell because of non-operating items. According to Hankyung financial data, non-operating expenses in 2025 far exceeded non-operating income.

In February 2026, the company said it had fully reflected one-off losses tied to subsidiaries in its books for 2025. Net income including non-controlling interests was KRW 3.29 billion in 2025, which differs from profit attributable to controlling shareholders, so the non-controlling share must be considered.

By quarter, operating profit peaked at KRW 30.1 billion in the fourth quarter of 2025, then fell to KRW 23.8 billion in the first quarter of 2026, KRW 22.4 billion in the second and KRW 8.71 billion in the third, a decline for three consecutive quarters.

Third-quarter 2026 revenue of KRW 480.4 billion was up 33.9% year on year, yet operating profit fell 61.1% from the prior quarter. The third-quarter net loss attributable to controlling shareholders was KRW 6.23 billion, a continued loss with the deficit widening.

Over the last four quarters, from the fourth quarter of 2025 to the third quarter of 2026, profit attributable to controlling shareholders totaled KRW 17.4 billion.

05

Industry analysis

Brass rod demand comes from downstream industries including semiconductors, automobiles, electrical and electronic components, ships and industrial machinery.

In February 2026, a company official said demand from semiconductors and automobiles, combined with pre-buying ahead of copper price increases, was pushing order volumes up. This is the company's own view and not a verified industry statistic.

A 2022 news report said China is the largest copper consumer, accounting for about 50% of global copper use, so copper prices are heavily influenced by Chinese demand. From April to October 2022, a sharp fall in copper prices lowered product prices relative to purchase costs and caused losses.

Even if downstream demand recovers, a sharp copper price decline can thin margins. In competition, Daechang is described as a leading domestic brass rod producer, with a 44% domestic share cited in 2022 coverage.

Its claim to be No. 1 in Asia and among the top five globally comes from company profile text and is the company's own assertion. The company cited higher copper prices and recovering plant utilization as reasons for the first-quarter 2026 improvement.

The key question is whether the 2025 operating recovery and the slowdown in quarterly profit through the second half of 2026 reflect industry conditions or cost factors. Poongsan and Korea Zinc have broader business mixes, which makes direct comparison with Daechang difficult.

08

Bull factors

Recovery in earnings capacity

Operating profit moved from a KRW 9.27 billion loss in 2023 to a KRW 31.3 billion profit in 2024 and KRW 51.5 billion in 2025, up 64.4%, a two-year recovery. The operating margin improved from -0.7% in 2023 to 3.5% in 2025.

After peaking at KRW 30.1 billion in the fourth quarter of 2025, operating profit still reached KRW 23.8 billion in the first quarter of 2026.

Top-line growth and export base

Revenue has grown for two consecutive years, from 2024 through 2025, reaching KRW 1.47 trillion in 2025. As of December 2025, exports made up 54.21% of brass rod sales, so overseas demand supports more than half of that product line. Third-quarter 2026 revenue of KRW 480.4 billion was up 33.9% year on year, extending the top-line growth.

Utilization and copper price recovery (company view)

The company said plant utilization in January 2026 was recovering year on year and that order volumes were rising. It also cited margin gains from higher copper prices as a driver of the first-quarter turnaround.

This is the company's own explanation, and whether the effect persists matters, given that quarterly profit slowed through the third quarter.

Bear factors

Quarterly profit slowdown in 2026

Operating profit declined for three consecutive quarters, to KRW 23.8 billion in the first quarter of 2026, KRW 22.4 billion in the second and KRW 8.71 billion in the third. The third quarter fell 61.1% from the prior quarter and moved to a net loss attributable to controlling shareholders of KRW 6.23 billion. Over the same span revenue rose from KRW 429.1 billion to KRW 480.4 billion, but profitability moved lower.

High debt and weaker cash flow

Total liabilities at the end of 2025 were KRW 623 billion, above total equity of KRW 302.2 billion, leaving a debt-to-equity ratio of 206.2%. That ratio had improved to 165.9% in 2024 before rising again.

Operating cash flow swung from a KRW 36.5 billion inflow in 2024 to a KRW 16.9 billion outflow in 2025, a gap against reported profit.

Recurring losses and one-off charges

In 2023 the company posted an operating loss of KRW 9.27 billion and a net loss attributable to controlling shareholders of KRW 19.4 billion. In 2025 operating profit rose, yet profit attributable to controlling shareholders fell 51.5% to KRW 2.13 billion.

The company said one-off losses tied to subsidiaries were reflected in 2025 books, and whether such charges recur is the next point to watch in filings.

10

Risk factors

Copper price volatility

Copper is the core raw material for brass rod, so copper prices move both costs and selling prices. When copper fell sharply from April to October 2022, product prices dropped relative to purchase costs and losses followed. Part of the 2026 improvement was tied to higher copper prices, so a reversal could compress margins quickly.

Export and overseas demand dependence

As of December 2025, exports made up 54.21% of brass rod sales, which exposes the company to overseas demand and exchange-rate changes. A slowdown in semiconductor or automobile demand, its main downstream markets, could reduce orders. With product sales concentrated in brass rod, earnings are hard to diversify across other product lines.

Balance sheet and cash flow

At the end of 2025 the debt-to-equity ratio was 206.2% and total liabilities were KRW 623 billion. Operating cash flow was negative in both 2023 and 2025. If profit continues to fall while borrowing costs remain, room for investment and working-capital funding could narrow.

Overall view

Daechang is a non-ferrous metal manufacturer centered on brass rod. In 2025 it posted revenue of KRW 1.47 trillion and operating profit of KRW 51.5 billion, recovering earnings capacity after the 2023 operating loss.

However, profit attributable to controlling shareholders fell 51.5% to KRW 2.13 billion that year, and the company cited one-off losses tied to subsidiaries as a factor.

Quarterly operating profit was KRW 23.8 billion in the first quarter of 2026, KRW 22.4 billion in the second and KRW 8.71 billion in the third, a decline for three consecutive quarters, and the third quarter widened into a net loss attributable to controlling shareholders of KRW 6.23 billion.

Strengths include a brass rod business in which exports exceed half of sales, the company's account of higher copper prices and recovering utilization, and the annual profit recovery since 2024.

Weaknesses include the 206.2% debt-to-equity ratio at the end of 2025, the 2025 operating cash flow deficit, and past losses when copper prices fell sharply. These factors deserve equal weight. Profit attributable to controlling shareholders totaled KRW 17.4 billion over the last four quarters.

The next checkpoints are the operating margin and debt structure in the 2026 annual report and the first-quarter 2027 results. This report offers no investment opinion or target price and is provided for information only.
