02

Business structure

Jusung Engineering is a KOSDAQ-listed maker of deposition equipment used in semiconductor, display and solar manufacturing.

Of cumulative revenue of KRW 192.8 billion for January to September 2026 (preliminary, announced November 4, 2026), the semiconductor segment accounted for KRW 189.4 billion, about 98%, while the solar and display segment contributed KRW 3.4 billion.

Its core product line is ALD (atomic layer deposition) equipment, which builds thin films one atomic layer at a time and grows in importance as circuits shrink. In memory, production-grade ALD tools for high-k capacitor processes at the 1c and 1d nm nodes are cited as a key application.

Korea Investment & Securities said in a March 2026 report that these orders were expected to begin in the second half of 2026.

The same report said the company ran qualification tests with a North American semiconductor company on glass-substrate deposition equipment in 2025, and qualification is the last step before a production tool is adopted. The solar business centers on next-generation HJT (heterojunction) cell equipment.

A May 2026 report said Tesla was negotiating solar equipment purchases worth about USD 2.9 billion with Chinese companies, and one securities analyst described Jusung as an alternative supplier that has completed HJT mass-production validation.

In domestic deposition and front-end equipment, Wonik IPS, Eugene Technology and PSK are the names most often used for comparison. Results depend on customers' capex timing and on delivery and acceptance schedules, which makes quarterly results volatile.

04

Earnings analysis

Annual results show large swings: revenue fell from KRW 437.9 billion in 2022 to KRW 284.7 billion in 2023 (down 35.0%), recovered to KRW 409.4 billion in 2024 (up 43.8%), then fell to KRW 310.7 billion in 2025 (down 24.1%).

Operating profit was KRW 123.9 billion in 2022, KRW 28.9 billion in 2023 (down 76.6%), KRW 97.2 billion in 2024 (up 235.9%) and KRW 31.3 billion in 2025 (down 67.8%). The operating margin moved from 28.3% to 10.2%, 23.7% and 10.1% over the same years.

Net profit attributable to controlling shareholders was KRW 106.2 billion, KRW 34 billion, KRW 106.8 billion and KRW 35.7 billion, with 2024 the largest year and 2023 the smallest among 2022 to 2025. Net income including non-controlling interests was reported at the same figures for each year.

Cash generation swung more than earnings: operating cash flow was KRW 101 billion in 2022, KRW 887 million in 2023, KRW 224.7 billion in 2024 and -KRW 30.7 billion in 2025.

The debt ratio was 71.9%, 56.4%, 74.2% and 50.4% in those years, and at the end of 2025 total equity was KRW 590.5 billion against total liabilities of KRW 297.4 billion.

Quarterly results show a trough in Q4 2025, when revenue was KRW 52.3 billion (down 11.0% quarter on quarter) and the company posted an operating loss of KRW 12.6 billion. In Q1 2026 revenue was KRW 54.9 billion (up 5.0%) and the operating loss narrowed to KRW 7.03 billion.

In Q2 2026 revenue was KRW 59.9 billion and operating profit was KRW 1.42 billion, a return to profit.

Q3 2026 revenue was KRW 78.1 billion (up 30.4% quarter on quarter and 32.8% year on year) and operating profit was KRW 17.7 billion (up 1,141.3% quarter on quarter and 426.4% year on year), with revenue rising for three consecutive quarters from Q1 2026 through Q3 2026.

Net profit attributable to controlling shareholders was KRW 28 billion in Q3 2026, up 267.4% year on year, and the last four quarters total KRW 27.7 billion.

The company attributed the first-half revenue decline to customers' new fab infrastructure timing, equipment move-in delays and delivery-basis revenue recognition. Why controlling net profit exceeded operating profit in Q3 2026 is a line-item question for the periodic filings.

05

Industry analysis

Demand for front-end semiconductor equipment such as Jusung's tools moves with the size and timing of memory and foundry capex. ALD, the company's core line, controls film thickness and uniformity at fine geometries, and its importance rises as processes shrink.

Demand is tied to customers' investment timing, so quarterly results can swing widely. In 2026 expectations tied to AI chips have been reflected in the share price.

A June 2026 report from Korea Financial News described the stock as a rising AI-chip beneficiary but noted that the order backlog and contract liabilities, which indicate future earnings, had not clearly turned. The same report named Wonik IPS, Eugene Technology and PSK as the main comparison points.

A March 2026 report from Korea Investment & Securities said the higher average selling price of ALD production tools could be positive for profitability. Semiconductors account for about 98% of revenue, so the business is concentrated in the chip investment cycle.

Solar HJT and glass-substrate opportunities have uncertain market size and production timing, so they offer limited evidence about where the cycle stands. Among front-end equipment makers, visibility of earnings depends heavily on backlog size and customers' investment plans.

Outlook

A company representative said the first-half revenue decline came from the timing of customers' new fab infrastructure, equipment move-in delays and delivery-basis revenue recognition, and expected revenue to rise in the second half as the order backlog converts (Digital Times and THE ELEC coverage).

Q3 2026 revenue of KRW 78.1 billion and operating profit of KRW 17.7 billion are consistent with that direction. However, the backlog had fallen to KRW 74.7 billion at the end of Q1 2026, so revenue after Q4 depends heavily on new orders.

One named order pipeline is production ALD tools for high-k capacitors at the 1c and 1d nm nodes for a memory customer, which Korea Investment & Securities said was expected to start in the second half of 2026 (March 2026 report).

The glass-substrate qualification with a North American chipmaker ran in 2025, and passing it has been described as likely to lead to medium- to long-term supply contracts. In solar, the reported Tesla negotiation worth about USD 2.9 billion is the largest variable, and it remains at the reporting stage.

The company presented first-half results and business status to institutional investors on August 28, 2026. The next checkpoints are Q4 2026 results, the backlog and contract liabilities in the 2026 annual report, and the size and counterparties of any disclosed supply contracts.

Contract liabilities are volatile, but the speed of the decline is cited as a bearish sign. With a smaller backlog, the revenue recovery relies on new orders.

Cumulative decline and negative cash flow

Operating cash flow was -KRW 30.7 billion in 2025, a net outflow. Whether the Q3 2026 profit carries into later quarters cannot yet be confirmed.

Earnings volatility and premium valuation

Risk factors

Customer capex cycle

If memory and foundry customers delay or cut investment plans, orders and delivery schedules slip together. The company cited fab infrastructure timing and equipment move-in delays for the first-half decline.

Telling whether the Q3 rebound reflects a short delivery cluster or sustained demand requires tracking the backlog.

Order gaps and cash flow

With the backlog and contract liabilities shrinking, operating cash flow was -KRW 30.7 billion in 2025. A heavier working capital burden could constrain room for R&D and capacity investment. Operating cash flow was only KRW 887 million in 2023, so cash generation has been highly variable from year to year.

New businesses and policy variables

The reported solar HJT negotiation is at the reporting stage and its timing and scale could shift with US-China export rules and customer contract terms. The timing and size of revenue from glass-substrate and capacitor ALD orders are also undetermined.

Because these businesses are small relative to semiconductors, they are unlikely to change total results alone, but they drive expectations.
