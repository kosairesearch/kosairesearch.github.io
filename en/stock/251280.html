02

Business structure

AngioLab is a Daejeon-based biotech founded in 1999 that pursues drug development and health functional foods around its angiogenesis inhibition technology.

The company holds an in-house screening platform called ANGIOTOS, which measures and screens angiogenesis inhibition, and uses it to develop natural product drugs and antibody therapeutics. Revenue is concentrated in health functional foods.

According to business reports, sales of the Ob-X lemon balm extract blend as an ingredient ranged from the high 50s to the high 70s percent of total sales in different years, with finished products accounting for the rest. The lead pipeline asset is ALS-L1023 for wet age-related macular degeneration.

The same compound completed a Phase 2a trial in adults with otitis media with effusion, and related composition patents were granted in the United States and Japan.

In antibodies, the company is developing a photoimmunotherapy anticancer platform that pairs a photosensitizer with an antibody, and it has reportedly secured in vitro proof of concept and is preparing in vivo efficacy studies.

At the end of 2025, the company filed a domestic patent for a new cell-penetrating peptide technology and proposed applications in skin, eye, and hair loss. On the cosmetic side, its SPACE peptide skin-penetration platform obtained an International Nomenclature of Cosmetic Ingredients name.

Compared with large pharmaceutical and biotech peers, its capital and headcount base is small, with roughly 16 to 17 employees reported. The business therefore remains weighted toward health functional food sales rather than clinical-stage pipeline revenue.

Recent trends

According to the separate financial statements for fiscal 2025 (the 27th term), filed on March 26, 2026, revenue was KRW 2,265 million. The operating loss was KRW 603 million and the net loss was KRW 554 million.

Outlook

The outlook will depend on clinical progress and whether the company secures funding. ALS-L1023 has approval for its Phase 3 IND, so the start of Phase 3 and its results are the next major milestones.

For the otitis media with effusion indication, the entry into a later stage after the completed Phase 2a trial is the item to watch.

On patents, the lemon balm fraction patent has been granted in the United States and Japan, while the new cell-penetrating peptide technology remains at the domestic filing stage, leaving registration and overseas filings ahead.

For the photoimmunotherapy anticancer platform, the transition from in vitro proof of concept to in vivo efficacy studies is an important turning point.

Health functional food sales showed growth in 2025, but whether ingredient and finished product sales are sustained depends on expansion of sales channels and customers.

In cosmetics, the International Nomenclature of Cosmetic Ingredients name has been obtained, so whether co-development with beauty brands translates into actual revenue is the point to track.

On capital, with total equity at KRW 1,641 million and an annual operating loss around KRW 600 million, repeated fundraising to cover clinical costs is possible. This could lead to dilution and changes in share count, so rights offering and convertible share disclosures should be read together.

Advancing candidates into clinical stages is viewed by supporters as meaningful progress for a company of this size.

Platform expansion and diversified applications

At the end of 2025, the company filed a domestic patent application for a new cell-penetrating peptide technology and presented potential delivery in skin, eye, and hair applications. The photoimmunotherapy anticancer platform is reported to have secured in vitro proof of concept.

The SPACE peptide cosmetic ingredient obtaining an INCI name is cited as an example of the platform being applied beyond medicines. Supporters argue that diversified applications could soften the impact of any single pipeline setback.
