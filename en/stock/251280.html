A Micro-Cap Biotech Tied to One Phase 3 Asset

02

Business structure

AngioLab was founded in 1999 and develops treatments for angiogenesis-related diseases using angiogenesis inhibitors. It listed on the Konex market on October 25, 2016.

Its core technology is the ANGIOTOS platform, which measures and screens angiogenesis-inhibiting activity through image-based assays on cells and tissues.

The natural-product drug ALS-L1023, derived from this base, is a fraction of lemon balm leaf extract. It is an oral candidate that suppresses angiogenic factors such as VEGF and bFGF while also targeting inflammation and oxidative stress.

At an investor event, the company described results from a Phase 2 wet AMD trial, a Phase 2a trial in non-alcoholic steatohepatitis, and a Phase 2 trial in periodontal disease. It is also developing antibody photosensitizer conjugate (APC) therapeutics, which pair an antibody with a photosensitizer.

Its revenue source is the sale of its in-house lemon balm extract powder (Ob-X) as a functional ingredient. The revenue split by segment could not be confirmed in this research, and the yearly revenue mix can shift depending on whether licensing payments are recognized.

The company follows a model of proving clinical efficacy and then pursuing out-licensing, rather than commercializing new drugs itself. Existing wet AMD drugs are all injected directly into the eye, and critics point to low convenience, high cost, and the risk of resistance with long-term use.

AngioLab is trying to differentiate with an oral drug used in combination, but the market has many established pharmaceutical companies with far greater funding and clinical capacity. Its business structure and scale also differ greatly from large listed biotech companies such as Samsung Biologics and Celltrion. Morningstar's company profile lists 16 employees, which makes it a very small organization.

04

Outlook

The key near-term business variable is the progress of the ALS-L1023 Phase 3 trial. The Phase 3 trial plan was approved by the Ministry of Food and Drug Safety, and the trial is structured to be run jointly by a subsidiary of a domestic drugmaker, which holds the license, and its parent.

A 2026 news report states that the drug is in Phase 3 development as a wet AMD treatment. The detailed clinical timeline and the timing of results could not be confirmed in this research.

The KRW 11 billion in licensing fees includes a KRW 1 billion upfront payment and is paid as milestones are met. Royalties on sales are received separately. Additional income therefore depends on clinical progress and commercial results, and both its timing and size are uncertain.

The company is also pursuing further research to extend ALS-L1023 to diabetic retinopathy. It has also stated a goal of out-licensing overseas.

News reports said the European patent (EP 4134091) was registered in 21 countries. Securing intellectual property can serve as a basis for overseas negotiations. The company has cited a move to the KOSDAQ as a goal for many years, but it remains a Konex-listed company.

With small revenue and continuing losses, financial capacity and the way additional funding is raised are items to check in later annual and quarterly reports.

Room for improvement in business metrics lies in higher functional-ingredient sales and a narrower operating loss as licensing income is recognized, though the timing of both is not fixed.

Bull factors

Milestone-based licensing structure

The ALS-L1023 licensing deal is worth KRW 11 billion in total, including a KRW 1 billion upfront payment, with royalties on top. With approval of the Phase 3 trial plan, the KRW 1 billion upfront payment already received became eligible for revenue recognition.

If milestones are realized, they could be a meaningful income source relative to the company's current revenue scale of roughly KRW 2 billion. The structure in which the partner runs the Phase 3 trial also lightens the load for a small company.

Higher revenue and narrower losses

Revenue rose from KRW 496.65 million in 2023 to KRW 2,264.61 million in 2025, while the operating loss narrowed from KRW 2,433.86 million to KRW 602.61 million. Sales of functional-food ingredients form the base that supports revenue.

As a small organization with a light cost structure, additional revenue can translate into smaller losses relatively quickly. Room for improvement in the operating margin depends on ingredient supply volumes and how much licensing income is recognized.

Multiple indications and patents

The company has run Phase 2 trials across several diseases, including wet AMD, non-alcoholic steatohepatitis, exudative otitis media, and periodontal disease. It was reported to hold about 60 domestic and overseas patents related to angiogenesis inhibitors.

News reports also said its European patent registration in 21 countries was completed. The ability to extend one candidate to several indications gives the pipeline structural room to expand.

Bear factors

Persistent losses and a small balance sheet

For fiscal 2025 on a standalone basis, the operating loss was KRW 603 million, the net loss was KRW 554 million, and total equity was KRW 1,641 million. Losses are shrinking, but a return to profit has not been confirmed.

With such a small capital base, higher clinical and research costs could raise the need for external funding. The company filed disclosures related to a rights offering in February and March 2025.

Gap between past projections and results

In a 2020 news report, the company projected that earnings would grow in earnest from 2023 on licensing deals, estimating 2023 revenue of KRW 20.2 billion and an operating profit of KRW 14.2 billion. Actual 2023 revenue was KRW 496.65 million, with an operating loss of KRW 2,433.86 million.

This shows that licensing timelines and clinical results can run later than planned. The current milestone outlook carries the same uncertainty.

Risk factors

Macro

A report on October 27, 2026 said gaps in market demand had widened in the domestic stock market as investor deposits declined and large-cap buybacks ended. If sentiment weakens, trading in Konex micro-cap biotech stocks can thin out further.

Changes in interest rates and risk appetite also affect the funding environment for clinical-stage biotech companies.

Industry and clinical

The Phase 3 results are not yet determined, and a failure or delay could change the timing and size of milestone receipts. Eye-injection therapies are already established for wet AMD, so an oral drug must demonstrate the benefit of combination use. The possibility of added regulatory requirements during the approval review also cannot be ruled out.

Company-specific

Core value is concentrated in a single candidate and a single partner, so results depend on the partner's pace and commitment in running the trial. As a small organization with 16 employees, the loss of key staff or limits to research capacity could weigh heavily on the business.

At the March 2026 shareholders' meeting, approval of the financial statements was recorded at 49.3% of total voting shares issued. The shareholder turnout structure is also worth noting.

Overall view

Based on filed results, fiscal 2025 revenue was KRW 2,265 million and the operating loss was KRW 603 million, so losses have narrowed but a return to profit has not been confirmed.
