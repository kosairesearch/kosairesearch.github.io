02

Business structure

Iljin Hysolus was founded in 2012 and operates hydrogen and environmental businesses; it listed on the KOSPI main market in 2021.

Its main products are passenger and commercial hydrogen tanks, hydrogen transport storage systems, and emission-reduction devices, and it has been the exclusive supplier of hydrogen tanks for Hyundai Motor's NEXO since 2018. Passenger-segment sales therefore move closely with NEXO volumes.

The company is expanding commercial-vehicle capacity at its Wanju plant in line with government hydrogen-bus policy. Based on a 2024 brokerage report, hydrogen storage tanks accounted for KRW 68.3 billion of the KRW 78.7 billion in 2023 sales, the large majority of the total.

Customers have broadened from Hyundai Motor to commercial vehicles such as hydrogen buses. Press coverage describes the company as the leading domestic maker of hydrogen storage tanks. It is seeking to diversify across vehicle types and overseas markets.

Press reports also cite a new fuel-cell plant in Ulsan as a long-term growth driver. The largest shareholder group holds 70.00% of the shares.

04

Earnings analysis

On a DART-filed basis, annual sales fell from KRW 109.1 billion in 2022 to KRW 78.7 billion in 2023, a 27.8% decline, making 2023 the smallest year in the period.

Sales then rose to KRW 79.3 billion in 2024 (up 0.7%) and KRW 81.6 billion in 2025 (up 2.9%), two straight years of growth, while 2022 was the largest year of the 2022–2025 span.

Operating profit of KRW 2.77 billion in 2022 turned into an operating loss of KRW 9.8 billion in 2023, followed by losses of KRW 9.52 billion in 2024 and KRW 11.2 billion in 2025, three straight years of losses. The operating margin moved from 2.5% in 2022 to -12.4% in 2023, -12.0% in 2024 and -13.7% in 2025.

Press reports attribute the weakness to early costs of starting new-model production, raw-material price swings and fixed-cost burden.

Net profit attributable to controlling shareholders was KRW 7.04 billion in 2022, KRW 1.41 billion in 2023 (down 80.0%) and KRW 1.82 billion in 2024 (up 28.9%), before turning into a net loss of KRW 2 billion in 2025. Net loss including non-controlling interests was also KRW 2 billion in 2025.

Operating cash flow went from KRW 9.95 billion in 2024 to -KRW 4.59 billion in 2025. The auditor issued unqualified opinions on both consolidated and separate statements for 2025.

By quarter, the third quarter of 2025 showed sales of KRW 28.2 billion, an operating loss of KRW 4.45 billion and a net loss attributable to controlling shareholders of KRW 2.05 billion.

The fourth quarter of 2025 brought sales of KRW 24.2 billion (down 14.1% from the prior quarter), an operating loss of KRW 969 million that narrowed, and a controlling-shareholder profit of KRW 1 billion.

In the first quarter of 2026, sales rose to KRW 27.1 billion (up 12.1%), but the operating loss widened to KRW 2.03 billion and the controlling-shareholder net loss was KRW 50.44 million.

The second quarter of 2026 recorded sales of KRW 25.9 billion (down 4.6%), an operating loss of KRW 646 million and a controlling-shareholder profit of KRW 966 million.

The third quarter of 2026 recorded sales of KRW 22 billion (down 14.9% from the prior quarter and 21.8% from a year earlier), an operating loss of KRW 2.64 billion and a controlling-shareholder net loss of KRW 648 million. Over the latest four quarters, net profit attributable to controlling shareholders totaled KRW 1.27 billion.

05

Industry analysis

Revenue moves on two axes: passenger volumes tied to the NEXO and commercial volumes from buses and trucks, and both depend on government policy and vehicle rollout speed.

Reports note that the refreshed NEXO launched in 2025 lifted passenger hydrogen demand, and that Hyundai Motor ranked first in the global hydrogen fuel-cell vehicle market in 2026.

In contrast, a Shinhan Securities report from December 2025 pointed to budgeted volumes falling from 7,300 to 6,000 hydrogen passenger cars and from 2,000 to 1,800 hydrogen buses for the following year. The same report said continued strong sales could lead to budget expansion through a supplementary budget.

A government official was quoted as saying hydrogen industry priorities had slipped somewhat relative to the past. The company is pursuing commercial-vehicle expansion, large-capacity tube trailers, next-generation liquid hydrogen tanks, and cooperation with global partners such as Volvo.

Its strength is exclusive NEXO tank supply, but that also ties earnings to Hyundai's hydrogen-car sales volumes. Hydrogen commercial vehicles are ordered in large units such as buses and trucks, which makes them sensitive to policy budget cycles.

Doosan Fuel Cell, a fuel-cell maker in the same sector, has different products and customers, so direct comparison metrics are limited.

Net profit attributable to controlling shareholders was a net loss of KRW 50.44 million in the first quarter, a profit of KRW 966 million in the second, and a net loss of KRW 648 million in the third. A May 2026 press report said some market participants expected a full recovery from the second half of 2026.

However, third-quarter sales fell and the operating loss widened, so the second-half recovery case needs confirmation in fourth-quarter results. Press coverage cites the new Ulsan fuel-cell plant as a long-term growth driver, and its start-up timing and investment size are items to check in periodic filings.

A single-sales supply contract corrected on March 30, 2026 listed a contract value of KRW 9.1 billion, equal to 11.56% of sales. The next periodic report is the 2026 annual report, and the 2026 fourth-quarter and full-year operating margin are the next checkpoints.

Profitable quarters and balance-sheet capacity

Bear factors

Three straight years and five straight quarters of operating losses

Quarterly sales turning lower

Sales peaked at KRW 27.1 billion in the first quarter of 2026, then fell for two straight quarters to KRW 25.9 billion and KRW 22 billion. Third-quarter sales were down 21.8% from a year earlier. A December 2025 Shinhan Securities outlook on budgeted hydrogen vehicle volumes is cited as a bearish factor.

Breakeven sales level and cash flow

Shinhan Securities estimated in December 2025 that breakeven would require sales of around KRW 110 billion. DART-filed 2025 sales were KRW 81.6 billion. Operating cash flow turned negative at -KRW 4.59 billion in 2025, and the net loss attributable to controlling shareholders was KRW 2 billion.

Risk factors

Customer concentration

Because the company is the exclusive NEXO tank supplier to Hyundai Motor, a drop in the automaker's hydrogen-car sales would hit revenue directly. Passenger-segment recovery depends on a single customer's sales flow. The pace of broadening supply to other automakers or overseas customers is a key item to watch.

Policy and budget variables

Hydrogen car and bus deployment depends on government budget volumes. An official acknowledged that hydrogen industry priorities have slipped somewhat relative to the past. Budget reductions and any supplementary budget will shape the direction of commercial-segment results.

Costs and cash flow

Early costs of new-model production, raw-material price swings and fixed-cost burden were reported as reasons for the weak results. Operating cash flow turned negative in 2025. If operating losses keep widening, working-capital funding needs could rise. The large equity base provides a buffer, but restoring operating cash generation is the central issue.

Overall view

Iljin Hysolus is a hydrogen-tank maker whose earnings depend heavily on supplying Hyundai Motor's NEXO. Sales rose for two straight years to KRW 81.6 billion in 2025, but the operating loss widened to KRW 11.2 billion, and operating losses have continued for five straight quarters through the third quarter of 2026.

Quarterly sales have declined for two straight quarters after peaking in the first quarter of 2026. Supporting factors include the refreshed NEXO, wider commercial-vehicle tank supply, expansion plans such as the Ulsan plant, and a low debt ratio against equity.

Negative factors include persistent operating losses, uncertainty in policy and budget volumes, and operating cash flow turning negative. Both sets of factors coexist, and the DART-filed results alone do not show when a sustained return to profit might occur.

The next checkpoints are the full-year operating margin in the 2026 annual report and the fourth-quarter 2026 results.
