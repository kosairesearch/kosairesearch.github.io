Report overview

Square Tech: Expanding From LTCC Components Amid Continued Losses

Square Tech, which changed its name and relisted on KOSDAQ on November 9, 2026, is expanding into semiconductors, defense and digital assets, while reported operating losses have continued for five consecutive quarters.

  1. 1

    Annual sales rose 30.4% year on year to KRW 18.7 billion in 2025, but the company posted an operating loss of KRW 3.43 billion, the third straight loss-making year from 2023.

  2. 2

    Third-quarter 2026 sales were KRW 5.14 billion, up 3.2% year on year, with an operating loss of KRW 1.35 billion.

  3. 3

    Over the last four quarters (Q4 2025 to Q3 2026), the net loss attributable to controlling shareholders totaled KRW 10.2 billion.

  4. 4

    The company has set a target of returning to operating profit in 2027 and is pursuing cost reduction and profitability improvement through a management innovation task force.

  5. 5

    Non-core asset sales and a capital increase have both been rescheduled several times, with fund inflows now split between late November 2026 and January 2027 or later.

02

Business structure

Square Tech was founded in March 2002 as a ceramic materials and components company, and according to a Money Today report dated October 26, 2026, its core business is making LTCC (low-temperature co-fired ceramic) based RF components for mobile base stations and multilayer ceramic substrates.

The English-language outlet Newsfield also lists multilayer ceramic substrates for medical devices and defense among its product areas. The company says it covers the full chain from in-house development of LTCC core materials to finished component production.

After the shareholders' meeting on October 14, 2026 approved an amendment to its articles, the company adopted the name Square Tech Co., Ltd., and the renamed listing took effect on November 9, 2026.

Drawing on nearly two decades of LTCC materials and process technology, the company plans to expand into semiconductors and defense, according to a Datanet report.

Its Digital Solutions (DS) unit is also pursuing institutional digital asset custody and infrastructure services, dollar stablecoin distribution, and a stablecoin-based trading and payment platform.

An MOU with GOAT Network, announced October 23, 2026, covers a review of AI-agent stablecoin payments in Korea, with a foreign tourist payment program as the first candidate.

The MOU is a framework agreed to confirm cooperation direction, not a contract that fixes business terms, and support for a won-denominated stablecoin is not settled.

On this structure, the existing LTCC business, including base-station RF parts, remains the center of reported results, while semiconductors, defense and digital assets appear to be at an early stage.

04

Earnings analysis

On reported annual figures, sales fell from KRW 23.7 billion in 2022 to KRW 14.7 billion in 2023 (down 38.1% year on year) and KRW 14.3 billion in 2024 (down 2.6%), before recovering to KRW 18.7 billion in 2025 (up 30.4%). Among 2022 to 2025, 2022 was the largest year and 2024 was the smallest.

Operating profit of KRW 32.68 million in 2022 (operating margin 0.1%) turned into an operating loss of KRW 1.45 billion in 2023 (operating margin -9.9%). The losses continued with an operating loss of KRW 2.69 billion in 2024 (operating margin -18.8%) and KRW 3.43 billion in 2025 (operating margin -18.4%).

Net profit attributable to controlling shareholders was KRW 204 million in 2022, then turned into a loss of KRW 3.69 billion in 2023, narrowed to a loss of KRW 1.72 billion in 2024, and widened again to a loss of KRW 3.86 billion in 2025.

Net income including non-controlling interests, which is a different figure, showed a net loss of KRW 119 million in 2022. Sales rose in 2025 while the operating loss widened, which indicates that the revenue recovery did not absorb cost and selling and administrative burdens.

Operating cash flow moved from KRW 5.47 billion in 2022 to negative KRW 235 million in 2023, KRW 2.73 billion in 2024 and negative KRW 4.32 billion in 2025. The debt ratio rose from 28.5% in 2024 to 60.8% in 2025.

At the end of 2025, total equity was KRW 39.4 billion, of which KRW 39.3 billion belonged to controlling shareholders, and total liabilities were KRW 24 billion.

On a quarterly basis, sales were KRW 4.99 billion in Q3 2025, KRW 4.5 billion in Q4 2025 (down 9.9% quarter on quarter), KRW 4.71 billion in Q1 2026, KRW 4.61 billion in Q2 2026 and KRW 5.14 billion in Q3 2026 (up 11.6% quarter on quarter).

Over the same five quarters, operating losses were KRW 1.58 billion, KRW 805 million, KRW 1.34 billion, KRW 821 million and KRW 1.35 billion, and net losses attributable to controlling shareholders were KRW 1.88 billion, KRW 1.42 billion, KRW 3.33 billion, KRW 3.13 billion and KRW 2.31 billion, with the widest loss in Q1 2026.

In several quarters the net loss attributable to controlling shareholders exceeded the operating loss, which suggests that non-operating items and financing costs weighed on results.

05

Industry analysis

Within the electronic components industry, Square Tech is classified as a small component maker that produces LTCC substrates and RF parts. Demand for RF parts used in mobile base stations moves with telecom equipment investment, and RF component peers in the same industry include KMW and Rfhic.

Large component makers such as Samsung Electro-Mechanics and LG Innotek have greater scale and production capacity, so Square Tech operates more as a niche supplier focused on specific product lines.

Multilayer ceramic substrates for medical and defense uses involve lengthy certification and delivery procedures, which makes rapid revenue growth in those areas difficult.

The expansion into semiconductors is at the planning stage, grounded in the applicability of LTCC technology, and customer wins and volume production results have not been disclosed.

The digital asset business depends on domestic regulation and licensing requirements, with dollar stablecoins under review first and a won-denominated stablecoin deferred until the regulatory framework is in place.

As a result, end-market demand and the regulatory environment are likely to determine the pace of the business. The position in the industry cycle should be read alongside telecom equipment investment and defense demand, and it is difficult to separate from the company's own order flow.

06

Outlook

Among the company's public goals, the one confirmed is a return to operating profit in 2027. To pursue it, a management innovation task force was set up in September 2026 to work on cost reduction and profitability improvement, according to a Datanet report.

At the October 14, 2026 shareholders' meeting, the existing board was dismissed and a new board was elected, and the leadership under CEO Kim Kang-ho was reinforced, according to Money Today.

On the financing side, the company made an early repayment of a KRW 5.1 billion convertible bond and plans to reduce further borrowings through the sale of non-core assets and cost efficiency measures.

The planned sale of the financial subsidiary Apex Investment was moved twice from its original September 3, 2026 date and is now scheduled for November 30, 2026, which is also the final payment date. The sale terms allow the deadline to be extended if legal approval is delayed.

The third-party capital increase has been moved from October 30, 2026 to January 29, 2027, with new shares expected to list on February 18, 2027. Once subscription is complete, the Jentechnology Investment Fund would become the largest shareholder with a 19.45% stake.

The digital asset business is built on the GOAT Network MOU, but these are all functions still at the planning stage, and support for a won-denominated stablecoin and an expansion into gift cards and vouchers are under review.

Whether the 2027 operating profit target is reached will therefore depend on whether the asset sale and capital increase proceed as scheduled and whether cost reduction narrows quarterly operating losses.

Bull factors

Extending LTCC technology into semiconductors and defense

The company plans to extend its LTCC materials and process technology, built over nearly two decades, into semiconductors and defense. Multilayer ceramic substrates for defense have already been introduced as a production area. If realized, the expansion could diversify revenue beyond base-station RF parts. Specific order volumes and mass production timing have not yet been disclosed.

Balance sheet repair has started

The company says it made an early repayment of a KRW 5.1 billion convertible bond, reducing concerns about potential dilution. It plans to repay additional debt through non-core asset sales and intensive efficiency measures.

Since the management innovation task force began in September 2026, cost reduction has been pursued company-wide. Whether this narrows quarterly operating losses will become clear in later reported quarters.

Governance and management structure reset

At the October 14, 2026 shareholders' meeting, the existing board was dismissed and a new board was elected. Reports say this reinforced CEO Kim Kang-ho's leadership and eased concerns over a management rights dispute.

A proposal to dismiss the CEO as an inside director was rejected, so the management structure was maintained. A more stable management could make it easier to carry out asset sales and capital raising consistently.

Bear factors

Five straight quarters of operating losses and cumulative net losses

Operating losses have continued for five consecutive quarters from Q3 2025 through Q3 2026. The net loss attributable to controlling shareholders totaled KRW 10.2 billion over the last four quarters. It widened to KRW 3.33 billion in Q1 2026 and was KRW 2.31 billion in Q3 2026. The fact that the loss structure has not changed even as sales rose is the largest bearish factor.

Shrinking financial capacity

The debt ratio rose to 60.8% at the end of 2025 from 28.5% in 2024. Operating cash flow was negative KRW 4.32 billion in 2025. Total liabilities of KRW 24 billion weighed more heavily against total equity of KRW 39.4 billion.

If asset sales and the capital increase are not completed as scheduled, pressure on additional borrowing and funding could increase.

Early-stage pivot and repeated schedule delays

The digital asset business is at the MOU stage with GOAT Network, which confirms the direction of cooperation rather than fixing business terms. Support for a won-denominated stablecoin has not been settled, and the expansion into gift cards and vouchers is still under review.

The sale of Apex Investment has been postponed several times. Time is needed before the new businesses are confirmed in revenue, which is a bearish factor.

Risk factors

Funding and schedule risk

The sale of Apex Investment is scheduled for November 30, 2026, with conditions that allow the deadline to be extended if legal approval is delayed. The subscription date for the third-party capital increase has moved to January 29, 2027.

If fund inflows are delayed, operating funds and the debt repayment plan would also slip. Whether these two schedules proceed as planned is central to near-term financial risk.

Commercialization and regulatory risk

Expansion into semiconductors and defense is at the planning stage, applying LTCC technology, and customer wins and volume production results have not been disclosed. The digital asset business must meet domestic regulatory and licensing requirements, and even the dollar stablecoin under review is at an early stage. If the regulatory environment changes, the business timetable and revenue model could shift.

Governance and shareholder structure risk

Around the October 2026 extraordinary shareholders' meeting, a management rights dispute surfaced and the existing board was dismissed. Once the capital increase is completed, the Jentechnology Investment Fund is reported to become the largest shareholder with a 19.45% stake.

Successive changes in the largest shareholder and the board could shift management direction and business priorities.

Overall view

Square Tech is in a transition phase, using its LTCC ceramic component business as a base to expand into semiconductors, defense and digital assets. On reported figures, sales rose to KRW 18.7 billion in 2025, but an operating loss of KRW 3.43 billion extended a third straight year of losses.

Third-quarter 2026 sales were KRW 5.14 billion, up 3.2% year on year, yet an operating loss of KRW 1.35 billion remained. The net loss attributable to controlling shareholders totaled KRW 10.2 billion over the last four quarters, and the debt ratio rose to 60.8% at the end of 2025.

On the bullish side, the plans for semiconductors and defense, the early repayment of convertible bonds and asset sales, and the reset of the management structure after the board change are the main points.

On the bearish side, five consecutive quarters of operating losses, negative operating cash flow and repeated rescheduling of the asset sale and capital increase stand out. The digital asset business remains at the MOU and review stage, so it will take time before it shows up in results.

The next points to check are the November 30, 2026 final payment date for the asset sale, the January 29, 2027 subscription date for the capital increase, and whether the 2027 operating profit target is achieved.
