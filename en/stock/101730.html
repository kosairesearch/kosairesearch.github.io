02

Business structure

Wemade Max is a game developer subsidiary within the Wemade group that is widening its portfolio by releasing new titles across several genres.

Of its Q3 2026 revenue of KRW 29.9 billion, mobile revenue was KRW 11.2 billion, up 25.8% from the prior quarter, driven by the launch of the idle RPG Wind Runner and a large update to Lost Sword.

In the subculture segment, Make Drama: MAD, serviced by its publishing subsidiary, launched domestically on June 4, 2026 with a 14+ rating, and the same subsidiary signed a publishing deal for the subculture RPG OZ targeting a 2027 global launch.

The core in-development title is Night Crow W, developed by the development subsidiary Mad Engine as the official sequel to Night Crow, to be served globally on a single build. The company's first AAA title, the open-world action RPG Project TAL, is also advancing.

The PC extraction shooter Midnight Walkers went to Steam early access in January 2026. Revenue combines new titles, updates to existing games and license income, so stabilizing license sales shapes quarterly sales.

The company attributed the Q3 2026 sales decline from the prior quarter to a base effect from the Prefer Universe update and stabilizing license revenue. Its core battleground is the mobile MMORPG market, where it competes with large game makers such as Netmarble and NC.

MIR5, a PC MMORPG under the Mir IP, is being developed by affiliate Wemade Next, and its target timing has been moved to 2028 or later.

04

Earnings analysis

In the annual disclosed record, sales fell from KRW 86.2 billion in 2022 to KRW 69.8 billion in 2023, a 19.1% decline, then rose 7.5% to KRW 75 billion in 2024 and 98.5% to KRW 148.8 billion in 2025. Sales grew for two consecutive years from 2024 through 2025, and 2025 was the largest year in the 2022 to 2025 period.

Operating profit fell from KRW 25.3 billion in 2022 to KRW 3.76 billion in 2023, an 85.1% drop, then turned to an operating loss of KRW 8.09 billion in 2024 and widened to KRW 48.7 billion in 2025.

The operating margin moved from 29.3% in 2022 to 5.4% in 2023, -10.8% in 2024 and -32.8% in 2025, so losses grew even as sales rose in 2025.

Attributable net income fell from KRW 25.3 billion in 2022 to KRW 6.41 billion in 2023, a 74.6% decline, then turned to an attributable net loss of KRW 9.49 billion in 2024 and KRW 35.5 billion in 2025, with losses in both years.

Net income including non-controlling interests, a different figure, was a net loss of KRW 35.8 billion in 2025. Operating cash flow declined from KRW 35.2 billion in 2022 to KRW 2.43 billion in 2024 before turning to -KRW 24.2 billion in 2025.

The debt-to-equity ratio was 54.1% in 2022, 58.6% in 2023, 19.2% in 2024 and 22.3% in 2025, with total liabilities at KRW 103 billion at year-end 2025.

Quarterly, Q3 2025 revenue of KRW 38.2 billion and an operating loss of KRW 11.7 billion were followed by Q4 2025 revenue of KRW 30.5 billion, down 20.0% from the prior quarter, and an operating loss of KRW 17.9 billion.

In Q1 2026 revenue rose 7.0% to KRW 32.7 billion and the operating loss narrowed to KRW 15.3 billion, while in Q2 2026 revenue rose 3.1% to KRW 33.7 billion and the operating loss stayed near the prior level at KRW 15.4 billion.

In Q3 2026 revenue fell 11.2% from the prior quarter to KRW 29.9 billion and the operating loss narrowed to KRW 13.5 billion, though year on year revenue fell 21.6% and the loss widened.

Attributable net losses went from KRW 8.36 billion in Q3 2025 to KRW 10.8 billion in Q4 2025, KRW 11.7 billion in Q1 2026, KRW 14.4 billion in Q2 2026 and KRW 10.8 billion in Q3 2026.

The company's announced Q3 2026 net loss, including non-controlling interests, was KRW 11.9 billion, a figure different from the attributable loss.

05

Industry analysis

Mid-sized Korean game companies face the burden of developing several new titles at once while managing loss-making periods, and Wemade Max's cost management reads within that backdrop. Operating expenses fell 11.6% from the prior quarter in Q3 2026, while investment in new-title development was maintained.

In the mobile MMORPG market, demand for IP-based titles remains strong, and Night Crow W is positioned to draw on that demand. The original Night Crow received a Chinese foreign publishing license and is preparing a 2027 reveal under the local name Ya-A.

Some analysts see entering China with a game already run globally as an opportunity for additional revenue. Subculture is a key genre for the company, though Make Drama: MAD launched with a rating restricting users under 18, narrowing its audience.

Because quarterly revenue swings widely around launch timing, sales continue to lean on existing titles and license income until new releases contribute meaningfully. Its position against large peers will be judged mainly on performance after new launches.

Outlook

The company is developing Night Crow W toward a Q2 2027 launch target and said the release schedule will be fixed according to development progress. It expects additional sales growth in Q4 2026 from the global launch of Make Drama: MAD and the e-sports event FWC 2026.

Project TAL is advancing development and preparing a new trailer. The subculture RPG OZ targets a 2027 launch across global markets including Korea, North America and Taiwan. The Chinese version of Night Crow, Ya-A, is being prepared for a public reveal in China in 2027.

By contrast, MIR5 has been pushed to 2028 or later after its development direction was reset. The company said operational efficiency has built a foundation for steadily developing several new projects. All of these timelines come from company statements, and the launch dates are not yet fixed.

The next checkpoint is the full-year and Q4 performance recorded in the 2026 annual report, followed by whether a Night Crow W release date is confirmed.

Bull factors

Cost control and a smaller loss

The Q3 2026 operating loss narrowed to KRW 13.5 billion from the prior quarter, and operating expenses fell 11.6% from the prior quarter. The company said it kept investing in its core development organization while reallocating development resources.

The loss widened again in Q2 2026, so whether the narrowing continues will be visible in disclosures after Q4 2026. The smaller loss could be read as a first sign of recovery.

Night Crow W and the China entry

Night Crow W is the official sequel to Night Crow, and the company said two recent internal tests drew positive feedback on its photorealistic graphics, optimization and character design. Its target launch is set for Q2 2027.

The original Night Crow received a Chinese foreign publishing license in June 2026 and is preparing a 2027 reveal under the local name Ya-A. If the sequel performs well, it could rebuild the mobile MMORPG revenue base.

A wider subculture lineup and revenue base

Make Drama: MAD launched domestically on June 4, 2026, and the company said pre-registrations passed 500,000. The company expects Q4 2026 sales to grow further from the game's global launch and the FWC 2026 event.

The publishing deal for the subculture RPG OZ and updates to Lost Sword broaden the genre-based revenue sources. Actual results will be confirmed in later disclosures.

With its large new titles not yet launched, the company still relies on existing titles and license income, which is the core weakness in this view.

Schedule changes and uncertainty

Project TAL and OZ also target 2027, so several new titles are concentrated in one year. Any slippage could lengthen the period of losses.
