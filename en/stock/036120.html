02

Business structure

Seoul Information Service was founded in 1992 and listed on KOSDAQ in 1999 as a comprehensive credit information company.

It runs credit inquiry, credit investigation, debt collection and identity verification, and its main services include the personal credit management service SIREN24, corporate credit rating BIZRATING and technology credit evaluation TCB.

In the first nine months of 2026, credit inquiry made up 90.2% of revenue, debt collection 6.8% and credit investigation 3.0%. A Hana Securities report from March 2026 showed a similar tilt toward credit inquiry, at 88%, although the periods differ.

Its subsidiary Seoul Credit Rating rates CP, ABS and electronic short-term bonds. Core demand comes from corporate finance, financial and public-sector clients. Corporate credit ratings must be renewed every year, which gives the business a strong recurring-revenue character.

The company has widened TCB into public R&D and procurement bid evaluations and the preliminary review for KOSDAQ technology listings, and has raised fees, according to press reports. It launched the BIZOneClick corporate information platform to widen its higher-margin content business.

Hana Securities described it as the only comprehensive credit information services company in Korea. Because it needs little capital investment, it is reported to generate free cash flow in the KRW 7 billion range each year.

04

Earnings analysis

Annual revenue fell from KRW 40.9 billion in 2022 to KRW 34.4 billion in 2023 (down 16.0% year on year), then rose to KRW 38.1 billion in 2024 (up 10.8%) and KRW 41.3 billion in 2025 (up 8.3%). 2023 was the low point, and revenue rose in each of 2024 and 2025, with 2025 the largest year of 2022 to 2025.

Operating profit fell from KRW 4.58 billion in 2022 to KRW 401 million in 2023 (down 91.2%), then recovered to KRW 5.16 billion in 2024 (up 1,186.6%) and KRW 6.76 billion in 2025 (up 31.0%). The operating margin ran at 11.2% in 2022, 1.2% in 2023, 13.6% in 2024 and 16.4% in 2025.

Operating profit growth in 2025 far outpaced revenue growth of 8.3%, which points to cost efficiency as a contributor. Management attributed the 2025 improvement to business-unit co-CEO structures, a restructuring around core businesses and AI-based work efficiency.

Net profit attributable to controlling shareholders was KRW 2.34 billion in 2022, KRW 439 million in 2023, KRW 2.76 billion in 2024 and KRW 6.93 billion in 2025, while net profit including non-controlling interests was KRW 6.79 billion in 2025, a separate figure.

Quarterly, operating profit was KRW 772 million in Q3 2025, an operating loss of KRW 764 million in Q4 2025, KRW 268 million in Q1 2026, KRW 7.4 billion in Q2 2026 and KRW 1.64 billion in Q3 2026. Q1 2026 posted a net loss attributable to controlling shareholders of KRW 461 million despite positive operating profit.

Net profit attributable to controlling shareholders over the last four quarters totaled KRW 6.72 billion. Quarterly revenue moved from KRW 17.3 billion in Q2 2026 to KRW 8.11 billion in Q3 2026, so quarter-level margin comparisons are volatile.

05

Industry analysis

Demand for credit information moves with changes in the lending environment. Growth in credit evaluation demand from internet banks entering the individual business loan market, together with rising demand for corporate information inquiry from digital transformation, is seen as driving the improvement.

Hana Securities judged that revisions to the technology finance guidelines could widen the pool of evaluated companies and create room to raise report prices. Automation has given the corporate credit evaluation unit structural efficiency and rising margins, according to analysts.

The company is also pushing AI evaluation models to cut costs while entering new businesses. Hana Securities also credited the company with a competitive edge in credit data-based business. Direct evidence of sensitivity to the economic cycle is limited to the sharp swings in quarterly revenue.

Analysts argue that recurring credit-inquiry revenue dampens the impact of economic shocks. Expansion into digital finance is cited as a mid-term growth driver. New lenders such as internet banks can lift evaluation demand while also reshaping competition among incumbents.

06

Outlook

In July 2026 the company announced a corporate value enhancement plan that targets operating profit of KRW 10 billion within three years. The same plan said it would keep a high-dividend policy to strengthen shareholder returns.

In a March 2026 report, Hana Securities estimated 2026 revenue at about KRW 44 billion and operating profit at KRW 10 billion. Nine-month 2026 operating profit of KRW 9.3 billion sits close to that full-year estimate.

Because quarterly swings are large, whether the full-year estimate is met can only be judged once full-year 2026 preliminary results are out. The company says it will keep pushing AI-based efficiency to raise profitability and competitiveness. New initiatives include accelerating entry into AI model and STO markets.

TCB is expanding into public R&D and procurement evaluations. The company is also gradually pursuing buybacks and cancellation of treasury shares. Whether the de facto controlling shareholder's open-market purchases continue is the next item to watch.

08

Bull factors

Recurring revenue and strong cash generation

Credit inquiry made up 90.2% of revenue in the first nine months of 2026, giving the business a deep recurring base. Analysts say the model needs little capital spending and produces free cash flow in the KRW 7 billion range each year.

Cash and equivalents were about KRW 13.1 billion at end-Q3 2026, and the company is described as effectively debt-free. Operating cash flow rose to KRW 7.25 billion in 2024 and KRW 7.92 billion in 2025.

Operating margin recovery

The operating margin rose from 1.2% in 2023 to 13.6% in 2024 and 16.4% in 2025. Operating profit reached KRW 6.76 billion in 2025, up 31.0%, which management tied to business-unit leadership changes and AI-based efficiency. Nine-month 2026 operating profit was reported at KRW 9.3 billion, up 17.3%.

Shareholder returns and insider buying

The company has paid a dividend every year for seven consecutive years and has said it will keep its high-dividend policy. It is gradually pursuing share buybacks and cancellations.

Open-market purchases by the de facto controlling shareholder's director were disclosed for 5 to 10 November 2026, and an analyst described them as a positive signal.

09

Bear factors

Large swings in quarterly results

Operating profit turned into an operating loss of KRW 764 million in Q4 2025, and Q1 2026 posted a net loss attributable to controlling shareholders of KRW 461 million. Q3 2026 revenue of KRW 8.11 billion fell 53.2% from the prior quarter, and operating profit of KRW 1.64 billion fell 77.8%.

Strong Q2 2026 results carried much of the full-year flow, so quarterly continuity is low. Given the wide swings, one quarter's figures are a weak guide to the trend.

Limited top-line growth

Revenue of KRW 41.3 billion in 2025 is close to the KRW 40.9 billion of 2022. Q3 2026 revenue fell 9.0% year on year. If much of the profit recovery came from cost efficiency, whether top-line growth follows matters. The timing at which new businesses show up in revenue is not yet clear.

Ownership concentration and float

The largest shareholder group held 57.98% and treasury shares 4.39% as of 14 September 2026. The free float ratio was 37.63% and foreign ownership 1.78%. The limited float can leave trading thin, which is a weakness.

10

Risk factors

Policy and regulation

Credit information businesses are subject to personal data and credit data protection rules. If rules change the scope of collection or the fee structure, the impact could concentrate on credit inquiry, which accounts for about 90% of revenue. Higher compliance costs could slow margin recovery.

Technology and competition

New lenders such as internet banks add evaluation demand but can also reshape competition and pricing among incumbents. If returns on AI evaluation automation fall short, the cost-efficiency contribution would shrink. Shifting into new businesses could also dilute the profitability of the core.

Earnings volatility and liquidity

Sharp swings in quarterly revenue and profit can make annual and quarterly readings diverge. The gap between KRW 17.3 billion in Q2 2026 and KRW 8.11 billion in Q3 2026 shows this. With a free float ratio of 37.63%, trading volume can be thin.

12

Overall view

Seoul Information Service has used recurring credit-inquiry revenue to recover revenue, operating profit and net profit attributable to controlling shareholders since its 2023 low. The 2025 operating margin of 16.4% is above 11.2% in 2022.

Sharp quarterly swings, such as revenue of KRW 17.3 billion in Q2 2026 and KRW 8.11 billion in Q3 2026, mean annual and quarterly trends need to be read together. Press tallies put nine-month 2026 operating profit at KRW 9.3 billion, up 17.3%.

Credit inquiry at about 90% of revenue leaves the business exposed to regulatory or competitive change. Dividend history, treasury share policy and the de facto controlling shareholder's open-market buying are cited as shareholder-return positives.

On the other side, the largest shareholder group holds 57.98% and the free float ratio is 37.63%, which constrains liquidity. On valuation, one report places the P/E at the lower end of its historical band, while market capitalization exceeds the book value attributable to controlling shareholders.

The next checkpoints are Q4 and full-year 2026 preliminary results around February 2027 and the 2026 annual business report by end-March 2027.
