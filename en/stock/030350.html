02

Business structure

Dragonfly GF is a first-generation Korean game developer built around the 'Special Force' FPS franchise, operating across PC and mobile. Its main PC title, the Special Force remaster, was reported to use Unreal Engine for a major graphics upgrade, with the original developer serving as executive producer.

Reports said the remaster began official service on Steam and the Epic Games Store on March 25, 2026, and in June 2026 the company said it aimed for a full release in September.

The mobile title Down Down Town was co-developed with a Japanese game company, with a Japan soft launch planned for August 2026 and a full launch planned for October. ABYSSMATE, a mobile tag-action RPG developed by Japan's FUNCREW, is served by Dragonfly GF, and its teaser was released in July 2024.

The company plans overseas publishing deals to extend its global reach. Quarterly revenue ran between KRW 2.72 billion and KRW 2.93 billion across the first three quarters of 2026, so the timing of new launches drives the revenue path.

Compared with large Korean-listed peers such as Krafton, Netmarble and PearlAbyss, Dragonfly GF is a much smaller company whose results lean on a single IP and a few new titles.

04

Earnings analysis

Annual revenue rose from KRW 7.1 billion in 2022 to KRW 16.6 billion in 2023 (up 134.2% year on year) and reached KRW 25.6 billion in 2024 (up 54.2%), the largest year of the 2022 to 2025 period. It fell to KRW 15.9 billion in 2025, down 38.0%.

The operating loss widened from KRW 9.6 billion in 2022 to KRW 13.3 billion in 2023, then narrowed to KRW 9.65 billion in 2024 and KRW 1.94 billion in 2025. Operating losses continued for four consecutive years from 2022 through 2025.

Even as revenue declined after 2024, the operating loss shrank, which may reflect cost restructuring, though the line-item drivers would need to be checked in the full annual report. The operating margin improved from -135.2% in 2022 to -12.2% in 2025.

The net loss attributable to controlling shareholders was KRW 14.4 billion in 2022, KRW 27.7 billion in 2023, KRW 14.1 billion in 2024 and KRW 8.86 billion in 2025, and the net loss including non-controlling interests followed the same pattern.

Operating cash outflow narrowed from -KRW 8.8 billion in 2022 to -KRW 1.49 billion in 2025. The debt-to-equity ratio rose from 17.9% in 2022 to 139.3% in 2025, and at year-end 2025 total liabilities of KRW 7.72 billion exceeded total equity of KRW 5.54 billion.

Quarterly revenue moved from KRW 4.15 billion in the third quarter of 2025 to KRW 3.38 billion in the fourth quarter (down 18.6% from the prior quarter), then KRW 2.93 billion, KRW 2.72 billion and KRW 2.76 billion through the third quarter of 2026.

The net loss attributable to controlling shareholders was largest in the fourth quarter of 2025 at KRW 6.36 billion, then narrowed to KRW 489 million and KRW 317 million in the first two quarters of 2026.

Even so, the third-quarter 2026 operating loss of KRW 1.17 billion was larger than the KRW 279 million of the second quarter and the KRW 434 million of the third quarter of 2025. The net loss attributable to controlling shareholders over the last four quarters totaled KRW 8.4 billion.

05

Industry analysis

The domestic game industry has a strong hit-driven structure centered on large new titles, so smaller developers often see results swing sharply on the performance of one or two releases.

Dragonfly GF's long-running FPS IP is its main strength, but it competes for players with rival titles in the same genre that have built long-lived hit franchises. Releasing a PC remaster through global distribution platforms tests a revenue path that differs from a mobile-centered domestic market.

In mobile, co-development with a Japanese studio and a soft launch in Japan appear designed to test overseas player response while avoiding direct domestic competition.

Among listed peers, Krafton, Netmarble, PearlAbyss and NC have large development teams and multiple IPs, while Dragonfly GF's 2025 revenue of KRW 15.9 billion is far smaller.

Set against other mid-sized listed companies such as Wemade, Webzen, Neowiz and Com2us, the company's reliance on recycled IP is a point of difference. For smaller developers, release cadence and platform fees shape profitability.

Because management has said it will pursue fixed-cost reduction alongside cost control, how flexibly costs fall during a revenue decline is a key variable.
