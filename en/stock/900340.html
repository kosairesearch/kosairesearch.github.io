02

Business structure

Wing Yip Food Holdings is a holding company established in Hong Kong in 2015 that processes pork and poultry through operations centered in Guangdong province, China.

Its operating subsidiary Guangdong Wing Yip Food was founded in 2010, and its sub-subsidiary Hainan Wing Yip Food was founded in 2020, both handling production.

Core products span traditional foods, convenience foods, and frozen foods, including a line of five convenience products developed around traditional sausage flavors to diversify the portfolio.

Distribution combines an offline network of 67 dealers with online platforms such as Tmall and JD.com and placements in large supermarkets. The company has invested in production mechanization and quality control, obtaining ISO22000 and HACCP certifications.

It became the first Chinese food company to list on KOSDAQ in November 2018, and it added a Nasdaq listing in November 2024, giving it a dual-listing structure.

As a regional Guangdong brand, its strength lies in localized distribution relationships, though it remains smaller in scale than nationwide meat-processing brands. The business is inherently sensitive to swings in raw pork prices, a common characteristic of the industry.

04

Earnings analysis

Annual revenue rose from KRW 177.8bn in 2022 to KRW 191.8bn in 2023 and KRW 210.3bn in 2024, before edging down to KRW 196.3bn in 2025.

Operating profit, however, peaked at KRW 26.6bn in 2023 and then declined for two straight years to KRW 21.7bn in 2024 and KRW 15.3bn in 2025, with the operating margin falling clearly from 13.9% in 2023 to 10.3% in 2024 and 7.8% in 2025.

Owner net profit held near KRW 17.2bn in both 2022 and 2023 before shrinking to KRW 15.3bn in 2024 and KRW 11.3bn in 2025.

On a quarterly basis, revenue of KRW 52.3bn, operating profit of KRW 3.6bn, and net profit of KRW 3.0bn in 2025Q2 gave way to a weaker 2025Q3, when revenue fell to KRW 46.4bn, operating profit was only KRW 0.04bn, and the company posted a net loss of KRW 0.17bn.

The business then recovered clearly in 2025Q4 with revenue of KRW 47.2bn, operating profit of KRW 5.5bn, and net profit of KRW 5.0bn, only to soften again in 2026Q1 to revenue of KRW 44.3bn, operating profit of KRW 2.6bn, and net profit of KRW 0.7bn.

In 2026Q2, revenue of KRW 44.0bn was similar to the prior quarter, but operating profit recovered to KRW 5.5bn and net profit improved to KRW 2.8bn. This quarter-to-quarter volatility appears to reflect a combination of cost pressures and timing effects in sales recognition.

Cumulative owner net profit over the latest four quarters (2025Q3-2026Q2) came to roughly KRW 8.3bn, still below the full-year 2025 figure of KRW 11.3bn, suggesting annualized earnings have not fully recovered.

Operating cash flow also fell sharply, from KRW 36.4bn in 2023 and KRW 23.8bn in 2024 to KRW 4.6bn in 2025, a notable development alongside the profitability slowdown.

Industry analysis

China's meat-processing industry is heavily exposed to cyclical swings in hog prices, with feed costs and pork supply directly affecting individual companies' margins.

On the demand side, urbanization and dual-income households have steadily lifted demand for convenience and frozen foods, while the expansion of online distribution channels has become a common industry trend.

The competitive landscape features large nationwide brands coexisting with regional players, with Wing Yip Food falling into the latter category, leveraging a localized distribution network centered on Guangdong province.

Compared with major nationwide brands, the company may be at a disadvantage in scale economics, but it maintains a stable sales base through regional consumer relationships and its dealer network.

Sustained cost pressures in recent years have produced margin compression across the sector, consistent with Wing Yip Food's own declining operating margin trend. Expanding into major online platforms such as Tmall and JD.com is a common industry response aimed at reducing reliance on offline dealers.

Outlook

Since July 2026, Wing Yip Food has been subject to disclosures flagging concerns over falling below KOSDAQ's tightened market-cap and share-price maintenance thresholds, prompting the CEO to purchase treasury shares in the open market and the controlling shareholder and others to subscribe to a third-party placement totaling 2.3 million shares.

These actions are largely defensive in nature, aimed at meeting listing-maintenance requirements rather than directly targeting earnings improvement, a distinction worth noting.

Separately, a stock consolidation decision was disclosed on March 30, 2026, and the actual implementation timeline of that process warrants further confirmation. The company appears to be continuing to expand online distribution channels such as Tmall and JD.com while diversifying its offline dealer network.

The dual-listing structure established through the November 2024 Nasdaq listing could potentially serve as an additional capital-raising channel going forward.

However, no official guidance on future revenue or profit, nor concrete capacity-expansion or new-product timelines, has been confirmed, making upcoming regular disclosures and quarterly results important points to verify.

Bull factors

Diversified Online and Offline Distribution

The company is diversifying its sales channels by expanding into online platforms such as Tmall and JD.com alongside offline distribution through 67 agents. This can be seen as a factor that reduces dependence on specific distribution channels and enhances responsiveness to changes in consumption trends. Expansion into large discount stores is also underway, broadening points of contact with consumers.

Secured Capital-Raising Channels via Dual Listing

Following its 2018 KOSDAQ listing, the company also listed on NASDAQ in 2024, establishing a dual-listing structure. This can be interpreted as adding one more channel that can be utilized for future capital raising. In fact, there has been a recent case of capital expansion through a third-party allotment capital increase.

Active Response from Management and Controlling Shareholder

As concerns arose over failing to meet listing maintenance requirements, the CEO directly purchased treasury shares, and the largest shareholder participated in a third-party allotment capital increase, acquiring 2.3 million shares.

This can be interpreted as a case showing management's and the major shareholder's willingness to respond immediately to listing maintenance issues. However, it should be noted separately that such measures are defensive in nature and distinct from actual earnings improvement.

Bear factors

Operating Margin Decline for Three Consecutive Years

The operating margin continued to decline, from 13.9% in 2023 to 10.3% in 2024 and 7.8% in 2025. Despite revenue growth, cost pressures persisted, resulting in structurally weakening profitability. If this trend continues, the recovery of absolute profit levels could be further delayed.

High Quarterly Earnings Volatility

In Q3 2025, the company recorded a net loss (-KRW 170 million), which sharply rebounded to a net profit of KRW 5.0 billion in Q4, then contracted again to KRW 670 million in Q1 2026, before improving once more to KRW 2.8 billion in Q2, showing significant quarter-to-quarter volatility. Such volatility is a factor that makes it difficult to predict earnings trends.

Potential Persistence of Listing-Maintenance Risk

Under the strengthened KOSDAQ regulations effective July 2026, if market capitalization falls below KRW 20 billion or share price stays below KRW 1,000 for 30 consecutive trading days, the company could be designated as an administrative issue.

Even though recent management responses have temporarily alleviated the situation, the same concern could recur if the share price or market capitalization falls below the threshold again in the future.

Risk factors

Exchange Regulatory Risk

Under the strengthened KOSDAQ listing maintenance requirements effective from July 2026, if market capitalization or share price remains below the threshold for a certain period, the company could be designated as an administrative issue, potentially leading to delisting procedures.

Given that a warning disclosure has already occurred once, the possibility of a similar situation recurring in the future cannot be ruled out. This is a factor that could affect the continuity of stock trading itself, separate from earnings performance.

Raw Material (Pork Price) Volatility Risk

The meat processing industry has an inherent characteristic of being heavily exposed to cost fluctuations driven by the cyclical nature of pork prices. The continuous decline in operating margin over the past three years may have been influenced by such cost pressures. If raw material prices rise further, margin pressure could intensify again.

Dual-Listing and Information Asymmetry Risk

The dual-listing structure on both KOSDAQ and NASDAQ has the advantage of broadening capital-raising channels, but differences in disclosure timing and information delivery speed between the two markets could create information asymmetry among investors.

In addition, changes in the accounting, customs, and regulatory environment of the China-based subsidiary are also variables that could affect earnings.

Overall view

Wing Yip Food is a Guangdong-based pork and poultry processor with a distinctive dual-listing structure spanning KOSDAQ and Nasdaq.

Financially, its operating margin has declined for three consecutive years from 13.9% in 2023 to 7.8% in 2025, shrinking overall profit, and owner net profit over the trailing four quarters still falls short of the full-year 2025 figure, indicating an incomplete earnings recovery.

That said, quarterly profitability did improve in both 2025Q4 and 2026Q2.

The key recent issue for this stock has been the concern over administrative-issue designation stemming from tightened KOSDAQ listing-maintenance rules from July 2026, to which management and the controlling shareholder responded with treasury share purchases and a third-party placement.

Distribution diversification and the dual-listing structure could serve as a longer-term growth foundation, but raw material cost volatility and exchange regulatory risk remain variables requiring ongoing observation.

Ahead of any investment decision, it would be prudent to check both listing-maintenance compliance and upcoming quarterly results.
