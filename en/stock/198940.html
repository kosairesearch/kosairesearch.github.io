02

Business structure

Hanjoo Light Metal was founded in 1987 as an aluminum casting company and listed on KOSDAQ in 2023. Its core business is aluminum automotive parts, including engine components, lightweight body parts and electric vehicle (EV) parts.

In 2022 it set up a subsidiary in Slovakia, becoming the first Korean aluminum caster to enter Europe. According to preliminary cumulative figures announced on 16 November 2026, lightweight parts sales for January to September 2026 reached KRW 103.5 billion, up 8.5% year on year and roughly 51% of total sales.

Marine parts sales over the same period were KRW 12.5 billion, up 31.0%. Hyundai Motor Group is the core customer, and the company was selected as a common-parts supplier for Hyundai's eM electric vehicle platform. Its technology centers on a semi-solid casting process based on electromagnetic stirring (EMS).

The company says cross members and control arms for the GV90 are ready for mass production and supply, and four parts for the GM pickup truck are entering production. It describes itself as an ultra-lightweight aluminum total solution provider and is extending into hydrogen vehicle and robot parts.

Within the KOSDAQ metals sector, its focus on lightweight automotive components is the main point of differentiation.

04

Earnings analysis

On a reported annual basis, sales were KRW 232.5 billion in 2022, KRW 221 billion in 2023 (down 5.0% year on year), KRW 250.8 billion in 2024 (up 13.5%) and KRW 259.5 billion in 2025 (up 3.5%). Sales rose in both 2024 and 2025, and 2025 was the largest year of the 2022 to 2025 period while 2023 was the smallest.

Operating profit was KRW 5.25 billion in 2022, turned to an operating loss of KRW 13.6 billion in 2023, narrowed to an operating loss of KRW 8.66 billion in 2024, and turned to an operating profit of KRW 1.33 billion in 2025, an operating margin of 0.5%.

Attributable net income was KRW 93.18 million in 2022, and attributable net losses of KRW 15.3 billion, KRW 7.28 billion and KRW 2.74 billion followed in 2023, 2024 and 2025, making three consecutive loss years.

At the end of 2025, total liabilities were KRW 166.8 billion against total equity of KRW 46.5 billion, a debt-to-equity ratio of 358.7%. Operating cash flow was KRW -2.33 billion in 2023, KRW 21.7 billion in 2024 and KRW 11.6 billion in 2025.

Quarterly, the third quarter of 2025 showed sales of KRW 65.2 billion and an operating loss of KRW 170 million, and the fourth quarter showed sales of KRW 59 billion (down 9.5% quarter on quarter) and an operating loss of KRW 2.89 billion.

The first quarter of 2026 turned to an operating profit of KRW 1.22 billion on sales of KRW 63.1 billion, and the second quarter delivered sales of KRW 72.6 billion (up 14.9%), an operating profit of KRW 2.6 billion and attributable net income of KRW 938 million.

The third quarter fell to KRW 67.2 billion in sales (down 7.4% quarter on quarter, up 3.1% year on year) with an operating loss of KRW 1.88 billion and an attributable net loss of KRW 10.1 billion.

The company said aluminum bought at high prices in the second quarter was consumed while the third-quarter drop in aluminum prices reached selling prices late, raising cost pressure. It also cited currency and share-price valuation losses as a factor in the wider cumulative net loss and described them as non-cash. Attributable net losses over the last four quarters total KRW 11.7 billion.

05

Industry analysis

Demand in the downstream market rests on automakers' push for lighter bodies and the expansion of electric vehicle platforms. Hanjoo Light Metal's products began with combustion-engine parts such as intake manifolds, but its sales mix is shifting toward body structure parts and EV-dedicated parts.

The roughly 51% share of lightweight parts in January to September 2026 sales shows this direction. Aluminum prices are the key raw-material variable.

The company attributed its third-quarter loss to the timing lag between raw-material costs and selling-price adjustments, so falling aluminum prices can temporarily weigh on profit. In North America, the company expects GM pickup parts to lift shipments and export share to recover.

In Europe, the Slovakia subsidiary raised its supply share of its main product from 60% to 100% from August 2026. Its customer base centers on Hyundai Motor Group, so volumes move with the production plans of that group. An earlier securities report flagged that slowing EV demand could lower capacity utilization.

Compared with other metals-sector peers on KOSDAQ, its specialization in lightweight automotive parts is the main point of difference, though the concentration on one automaker group means it is directly exposed to EV demand trends.

06

Outlook

The company plans to expand supply under newly won orders and pursue earnings improvement in 2027. In its August 2026 release it expected sales from the European volume expansion and new GM and GV90 programs to be reflected from the fourth quarter.

In its November 2026 release it said the GV90 launch schedule had been adjusted, pushing back the timing of initial sales. The Slovakia subsidiary was expected to add knuckle and carrier supply once new items enter mass production in November 2026.

It also plans to start supplying new parts to Hyundai and Kia in Europe from January 2027, which it expects to improve the subsidiary's profitability and its contribution to consolidated results. Growth is pursued without large new capacity, using existing spare production lines.

On the pipeline side, it signed a non-disclosure agreement with ZF in May 2026 and is reviewing three new parts for the North American market. Core parts for next-generation hydrogen fuel cell vehicles are being developed for direct supply to Hyundai Motor.

In robotics, it has begun advance development of humanoid robot parts for North America and finalized a GATC advanced robot national project agreement on 29 June 2026. The next point to verify is whether new program revenue in 2027 translates into a higher operating margin.

08

Bull factors

Return to Profit and Cost Restructuring

Operating profit reached KRW 1.33 billion in 2025, reversing the 2024 operating loss. The company says it completed restructuring through labor-management agreement and improved its cost structure.

Operating profit in the second quarter of 2026 rose to KRW 2.6 billion, up 113.8% from the prior quarter, and attributable net income was also positive that quarter.

Visibility of New Program Volume

Chassis parts for the GV90 and four GM pickup parts are entering mass production. The Slovakia subsidiary raised its main product supply share to 100% from August 2026 and plans to start new supply to Hyundai and Kia in Europe in January 2027. If these programs are reflected in results, a larger sales base could follow.

Optionality from New Business Areas

Core parts for next-generation hydrogen fuel cell vehicles are being developed for direct supply to Hyundai Motor. Advance development of humanoid robot parts for North America has started, and a GATC advanced robot national project agreement was finalized in June 2026. A non-disclosure agreement with ZF in May 2026 supports technical review of three new North American parts.

Bear factors

High Quarterly Earnings Volatility

Operating losses occurred in three of the last five quarters. The third quarter of 2026 turned to an operating loss of KRW 1.88 billion. The company also cited seasonal operating effects in the third quarter, and the timing lag between raw-material costs and selling prices can swing quarterly profit sharply.

Net Losses and Valuation Burden

The attributable net loss in the third quarter of 2026 was KRW 10.1 billion, much larger than the operating loss. The company said valuation losses from currency and share-price changes widened the cumulative net loss and described them as non-cash.

Balance Sheet and Cash Generation

At the end of 2025 the debt-to-equity ratio was 358.7%, with total liabilities of KRW 166.8 billion well above total equity of KRW 46.5 billion. Operating cash flow fell from KRW 21.7 billion in 2024 to KRW 11.6 billion in 2025. With net losses in three consecutive years from 2023 to 2025, the pace of equity rebuilding matters.

10

Risk factors

Customer Program Timing Risk

The GV90 launch schedule was adjusted, pushing back when initial sales are recognized. Customers are concentrated among Hyundai Motor Group and GM, so changes in automaker production plans move volumes directly. Even confirmed orders can shift revenue timing depending on mass-production schedules.

Raw Material and Currency Risk

The timing gap between aluminum purchase and selling prices was cited as the cause of the third-quarter operating loss. A rise in the exchange rate widened valuation losses and expanded swings in net results.

The company expects raw-material input costs to stabilize from the fourth quarter, but cost pressure could return if metal prices move again.

Dilution and Financing Risk

The company disclosed the exercise of conversion rights on its 7th series of convertible bonds on 30 June 2026. Continued conversions would increase the share count and affect per-share metrics. In a capital structure with a high debt-to-equity ratio, repayment burden and dilution risk should be watched together.

12

Overall view

Hanjoo Light Metal is in a phase where its earnings direction has improved, with an operating profit in 2025 and a recovery in the second quarter of 2026. However, the third-quarter 2026 operating loss and an attributable net loss of KRW 10.1 billion show that quarterly profit remains highly volatile.

The company attributes the swings to the timing of raw-material price pass-through and valuation losses. On the business side, whether new mass-production programs, including the GV90, GM pickup and Slovakia volumes, translate into 2027 results is the central question.

On the financial side, the debt-to-equity ratio of 358.7% and total liabilities of KRW 166.8 billion at the end of 2025 remain burdens. Attributable net losses of KRW 11.7 billion over the last four quarters should be read in the same context.

The share consolidation and the executive share purchases reflect the company's shareholder-value efforts. The next points to verify are the fourth-quarter result in the 2026 annual report, the start of European supply in January 2027, and the operating margin in the first quarter of 2027.
