02

Business structure

Wizit, founded in 1997 and listed on KOSDAQ in 1999, is a core parts supplier for semiconductor and display manufacturing equipment.

The company is credited with localizing display top electrodes for the first time in Korea and operates precision machining, anodizing and CP lines that produce parts for deposition, exposure and etching equipment.

It positions its in-house surface treatment technology as its core competitive edge and supplies high-quality parts to equipment makers in Korea and abroad. The business is split between display equipment parts and semiconductor equipment parts.

Semiconductor segment revenue rose 46% year on year in the first nine months of 2026 and drove overall growth, which the company attributes to expanded parts supply to new Chinese customers.

Consolidated results also include subsidiaries Powernet, a power conversion device maker, and Omnitel, an application software company, whose swings affect quarterly consolidated operating profit.

On customers, a June 2026 report said the company is supplying five TSMC sample types and evaluating two, and evaluating two sample types with Kioxia. Display remains a large share of revenue, so the business is still exposed to panel investment cycles.

04

Earnings analysis

Annual results moved in an expansion direction from 2022. Filed revenue went from KRW 35.2 billion in 2022 and KRW 35.2 billion in 2023 (no change year on year) to KRW 357.8 billion in 2024 (up 915.4%) and KRW 483 billion in 2025 (up 35.0%).

Operating profit rose from KRW 2.17 billion in 2022 to KRW 3.65 billion in 2023 (up 68.5%), KRW 13.8 billion in 2024 (up 278.9%) and KRW 25.8 billion in 2025 (up 86.6%), increasing for three consecutive years from 2023 to 2025, with 2025 the largest year of 2022 to 2025.

Operating margin was 6.2% in 2022, 10.4% in 2023, 3.9% in 2024 and 5.3% in 2025, dipping in 2024 when revenue expanded sharply and partly recovering in 2025.

Net income attributable to controlling shareholders moved from KRW 13.7 billion in 2022 to a net loss of KRW 24.1 billion in 2023, a turn to profit at KRW 851 million in 2024, and KRW 7.79 billion in 2025 (up 815.1%).

Total net income including non-controlling interests was KRW 24 billion in 2025, higher than the controlling-shareholder figure, with the gap reflecting non-controlling interests.

Quarterly revenue rose to KRW 133.5 billion in Q4 2025 (up 21.0% from the prior quarter), but operating profit fell to KRW 4.49 billion (down 38.1%). Q1 2026 operating profit of KRW 4.03 billion was the low point, and Q2 recovered to KRW 7.81 billion (up 94.0%) before Q3 reached KRW 10 billion (up 28.3%).

The first-quarter consolidated decline was attributed to the base effect at subsidiary Powernet and a temporary rise in labor costs. Even so, net income attributable to controlling shareholders swung to a KRW 634 million loss in Q3 2026, so operating and controlling-shareholder profit moved in different directions.

Operating cash flow was steady at KRW 26.2 billion in 2024 and KRW 23.7 billion in 2025. The debt ratio rose from 15.4% in 2022 to 67.4% in 2025 (74.5% in 2024), and at end-2025 controlling-shareholder equity was KRW 144.7 billion within total equity of KRW 406.1 billion.

05

Industry analysis

Demand from downstream markets is being led by semiconductors. An August 2026 report said AI server and data center investment has lifted chip demand, and that new facility investment and capacity expansion at major Korean chipmakers is expected to raise demand for related parts.

That points to a phase of expanding semiconductor capital spending. In displays, first-half 2026 revenue grew 6% year on year, and the first nine months of 2026 showed 4% growth, providing a stable base.

A December 2022 brokerage company analysis, however, recorded a case where falling display industry conditions reduced orders from key customers and revenue, so the display segment remains sensitive to industry cycles.

In semiconductor equipment parts, Wizit highlights its in-house surface treatment technology and its record of localizing parts, and it is advancing qualification with overseas customers. Other listed machinery and equipment companies in the same sector include People & Technology and Sunic System.

Because equipment makers' investment cycles determine parts order timing, announcements of semiconductor capex and customer fab schedules serve as leading indicators for revenue.

Outlook

In its third-quarter cumulative release on November 17, 2026, the company projected 2026 revenue growth of more than 10% year on year and operating profit more than doubling. The projection assumes continued expansion of high-value chip product supply and efficiency gains in production.

A company official said 2027 could see a further step up in growth, combining supply expansion from new fab operations at major domestic customers with a possible entry by a Taiwanese global customer.

The company plans to use funds raised in a June 2026 third-party allotment rights offering to expand production capacity. The same June 2026 report described a mid-term roadmap to generate about KRW 50 billion of additional overseas revenue by 2029.

Whether sample evaluations with TSMC and Kioxia convert into volume supply is the key gate for that overseas roadmap. Supplier registration could lift overseas revenue, but the timing of evaluation completion and the volume involved have not been disclosed.

Fourth-quarter 2026 results have not yet been filed in a periodic report, so the attainability of the annual projection will be checked against the 2026 annual report figures.

Because the projection is conditional, judging results will also require tracking the share of high-value products and production efficiency metrics. The timing at which capacity expansion shows up in revenue, and how the capital raised changes the balance sheet, are further points to track.

Bull factors

Semiconductor growth and operating profit recovery

Third-quarter 2026 operating profit of KRW 10 billion was the largest of the last five quarters, and it rose for two consecutive quarters from Q2 to Q3 2026.

The company reported that semiconductor revenue in the first nine months grew 46% year on year, with expanded supply to new Chinese customers lifting both sales and profitability. Whether the 2025 trend, when operating profit reached KRW 25.8 billion, continues is the core of the bullish case.

Sample evaluations with overseas chipmakers

A June 2026 report said the company is supplying five TSMC sample types and evaluating two, and evaluating two sample types with Kioxia. The company said it expects a possible entry by a Taiwanese global customer in 2027.

If the evaluations convert into volume supply, the overseas customer share of revenue could rise and customer diversification would follow.

Balance sheet repair and subsidiary value

A 3-to-1 reverse stock split in May 2026 and a roughly KRW 4 billion third-party allotment rights offering in June 2026 were used to repair the balance sheet. The largest shareholder, JSI Holdings, raised its stake from 35.2% to 38.9%.

Value Finder, an independent research firm, reported that combined subsidiary stake values exceeded market capitalization at that time, but this is a third-party assessment rather than an official company valuation.

Bear factors

Continued losses for controlling shareholders

Net losses attributable to controlling shareholders were KRW 658 million in Q2 2026 and KRW 634 million in Q3 2026, two consecutive quarters of losses.

Net income attributable to controlling shareholders totaled only KRW 301 million over the last four quarters, so operating profit gains have not yet reached the controlling shareholders. Without improvement in non-operating items and subsidiary results, operating gains alone would not confirm a return to profit.

Slower growth in the display segment

Display segment revenue grew 6% in the first half of 2026 and 4% in the first nine months, which is modest compared with the 46% semiconductor growth over the same nine months. In Q3 2022, falling display industry conditions reduced orders from key customers and cut revenue, a precedent for cyclical exposure. A structure weighted toward displays remains directly exposed to panel industry swings.

Rising leverage and reliance on capital raises

The debt ratio rose from 15.4% in 2022 to 67.4% in 2025, and in 2026 the company bolstered equity through a reverse split and a rights offering. If capacity expansion does not translate into revenue, reliance on outside funding could raise financial burden. Total liabilities at end-2025 were KRW 273.9 billion.

Risk factors

Qualification and volume conversion uncertainty

Sample evaluations with TSMC and Kioxia are ongoing, but whether and when volume supply starts has not been disclosed. If evaluations do not convert into revenue, the mid-term goal of about KRW 50 billion in additional overseas revenue by 2029 could be delayed. Customer volumes still at evaluation stage can remain volatile until they show up in quarterly results.

Ownership structure and share count changes

The 2026 reverse split and rights offering changed the share count, and the largest shareholder's stake rose to 38.9%. Because the float structure has changed, trading characteristics and supply-demand patterns may differ from before, which warrants monitoring.

Changes in the largest shareholder's stake can also bear on management direction and how capacity expansion is executed.

Subsidiary earnings volatility

The first-quarter 2026 consolidated operating profit decline was attributed to the base effect at subsidiary Powernet and a temporary rise in labor costs. Because consolidated results mix in subsidiary swings, the core chip-parts earnings trend is harder to read on its own. Subsidiary profit swings that steer the direction of controlling-shareholder net income are also a risk.

Overall view

Wizit is a company that built its name on display equipment parts and is now raising the weight of semiconductor parts.

On a filed basis, 2025 revenue reached KRW 483 billion and operating profit KRW 25.8 billion, a clear step up from 2022, and Q3 2026 operating profit of KRW 10 billion was the largest of the last five quarters.

Over the same period, net income attributable to controlling shareholders posted two consecutive quarterly losses from Q2 2026, and the four-quarter total was only KRW 301 million. The contrast between operating recovery and weak controlling-shareholder earnings is the most important dividing line at present.

Bullish factors are the 46% year-on-year growth in semiconductor revenue for the first nine months (preliminary, released November 17, 2026), sample evaluations with overseas chipmakers, and the conditional 2026 annual projection.

Bearish factors are controlling-shareholder losses, modest display growth and the rising debt ratio. Valuation sits well below book value per share, but earnings-based multiples rest on a small denominator, and the conclusion depends on how subsidiary stake values are weighed.

The next checkpoints are the confirmed results in the 2026 annual report, Q1 2027 results, new fab operations, and whether overseas customer evaluations convert into volume supply.
