02

Business structure

Haesung Optics is a KOSDAQ-listed maker whose core business is optical image stabilization (OIS) actuators for smartphone cameras. After exiting its lens module and camera module businesses in September 2021, it refocused on actuators, and according to a The Elec report the actuator share of sales exceeded 90% in 2022.

Production runs at its Vietnam subsidiary Haesung Vina and at Haehwa Vina, which it acquired in 2023, while a Tianjin subsidiary in China supports local customers with quality control and after-sales service.

The company said it brought the SUB process, a core sub-step in actuator manufacturing, in-house from May 2023, which improved cost competitiveness and yield. Samsung Electro-Mechanics is the center of the customer base.

According to the same report, sales to Samsung Electro-Mechanics were KRW 138.1 billion (98%) in 2021, KRW 151.5 billion (92%) in 2022, KRW 114.1 billion (94%) in 2023 and KRW 87.2 billion (80%) in 2024.

Other major customers include camera module makers that supply smartphone manufacturers in China, the United States and Japan.

In competition, OIS actuators have become essential parts of high-megapixel cameras and are spreading from flagship to mid-range models, and patents and precision manufacturing know-how are seen as barriers to new entrants.

Its subsidiary TKENS makes dehumidifiers for automotive lamps, which the company presents as a new automotive business. Exiting the lens and camera module businesses simplified the structure, but sales remain concentrated in one product family and one major customer.

04

Earnings analysis

The largest year for sales over 2022–2025 was 2022 at KRW 163.8 billion, and the smallest was 2024 at KRW 108.5 billion.

The 2024 decline traced back to sales to Samsung Electro-Mechanics falling to KRW 87.2 billion, and the company attributed this to delayed approval of key models by its end customer and a delayed entry into the folded zoom sub-component market. Sales rose 47.7% to KRW 160.3 billion in 2025, approaching the 2022 level.

Operating results moved from an operating loss of KRW 5.12 billion in 2022 to an operating profit of KRW 3.99 billion in 2023, a turnaround from the prior year, before returning to an operating loss of KRW 13.4 billion in 2024; the 2025 operating loss of KRW 6.74 billion was a continued loss with a narrower size.

Operating margins were -3.1% in 2022, 3.3% in 2023, -12.3% in 2024 and -4.2% in 2025. Press coverage attributes the 2023 profit largely to the in-house SUB process and the resulting cost gains.

Net losses attributable to controlling shareholders were KRW 16.1 billion in 2022, KRW 1.36 billion in 2023, KRW 27.8 billion in 2024 and KRW 8.61 billion in 2025, while net losses including non-controlling interests were KRW 17.4 billion in 2022 and KRW 2.14 billion in 2023, a different measure.

Quarterly, operating profit of KRW 676 million in Q4 2025 turned positive from a loss in Q3 2025, then came in at KRW 704 million in Q1 2026, KRW 406 million in Q2 2026 and KRW 97.33 million in Q3 2026.

Sales peaked at KRW 69.5 billion in Q1 2026 and then fell to KRW 55.9 billion in Q2 and KRW 52.6 billion in Q3, although Q3 sales were still up 24.4% from a year earlier.

In Q1 2026, attributable net profit of KRW 3.47 billion far exceeded operating profit of KRW 704 million, and in Q2 2026 an attributable net loss of KRW 7.29 billion far exceeded operating profit of KRW 406 million, so non-operating items had a large effect in both quarters.

At end-2025 total liabilities were KRW 117.5 billion and total equity was KRW 28.3 billion, of which KRW 18.6 billion belonged to controlling shareholders. Operating cash flow was -KRW 9.38 billion in 2022, KRW 12.3 billion in 2023, -KRW 6.74 billion in 2024 and -KRW 722 million in 2025.

05

Industry analysis

Demand in the camera module sector depends heavily on the shift toward higher-megapixel, higher-performance smartphone cameras. In these cameras, OIS actuators are essential components, and their use is spreading from flagship phones to mid-range models.

Smartphone market cycles are seen as the largest variable for demand, and price competition among global suppliers is also flagged as a business risk.

The actuator market has short product cycles and intense competition, but patents on specific driving methods and manufacturing processes are viewed as limiting entry by new players. Recently, AI-based image recognition and growth in automotive cameras have been cited as new growth drivers for the camera module sector.

In the folded zoom actuator segment, competition with the leading supplier of the sub-component market has become serious, and the company said in 2025 that it had entered that market. Press coverage describes the company as the main OIS supplier to a global smartphone camera client.

Because sales move with the mass-production timing of specific flagship models, quarterly revenue has tended to swing sharply.

06

Outlook

The company said it aims to raise the share of higher-value products by expanding mass production of folded zoom actuators and winning new model orders.

In its 2025 securities registration statement, the company set a 2025 sales target for Samsung Electro-Mechanics of KRW 123.0 billion, while total company sales for 2025 were disclosed at KRW 160.3 billion.

The same filing included a planned rights offering of KRW 9.5 billion, of which KRW 5.3 billion was to fund facilities. The company had earlier described a delayed sales amount of KRW 30.2 billion for two products that began mass production in December 2024.

Press reports said monthly sales in January 2026 set a record for the company, and that supply of key Galaxy S26 models improved volume stability. The automotive business began with the acquisition of a stake in TKENS in February 2024, and its main product is dehumidifiers for automotive headlamps.

Press coverage has flagged a time lag before automotive and new businesses reach meaningful revenue shares as a risk. The next checkpoint is the 2026 annual report, which should present Q4 2026 sales, full-year operating results and automotive-related figures as disclosed results. Whether sales recover from the Q3 2026 level in Q4 2026 is also a near-term test.

08

Bull factors

Main OIS supplier status at a global smartphone client

Press reports describe the company as the main OIS actuator supplier to a global smartphone camera client. Supply of key Galaxy S26 models has grown, moving the company beyond China-centered volumes, and management said this reduced earnings volatility. Press coverage of a record monthly sales figure in January 2026 points in the same direction.

In-house process gains and overseas production efficiency

Press coverage reports that bringing the SUB process in-house from May 2023 improved material ratios, yields and labor costs, cutting the break-even point by about KRW 8 billion. The company also reportedly built an automated next-generation line with a robotics equipment firm at its Vietnam base. This is read as a factor that supports earnings in periods of lower sales.

Automotive business as a second growth leg

Press coverage reported that the company entered the global automotive market through its 2024 acquisition of a stake in TKENS, which makes dehumidifiers for automotive lamps. It holds a 50.1% stake.

The company describes automotive as a second pillar alongside smartphone components and has outlined plans to expand affiliated businesses, including digital twin operations. The scale of automotive sales will become visible in upcoming periodic reports.

09

Bear factors

Slower sales and renewed losses in Q2–Q3 2026

Quarterly sales fell to KRW 55.9 billion in Q2 2026 and KRW 52.6 billion in Q3 2026, a two-quarter decline. The attributable net loss turned into a KRW 7.29 billion loss in Q2 2026 and continued at KRW 5.11 billion in Q3 2026.

Q3 sales still rose 24.4% from a year earlier, so year-over-year and sequential trends point in different directions.

Dependence on Samsung Electro-Mechanics and client approval timing

According to a The Elec report, sales to Samsung Electro-Mechanics fell from KRW 151.5 billion in 2022 to KRW 87.2 billion in 2024. The company cited delayed approval of key models by its end customer and a delayed entry into the folded zoom sub-component market as causes. A structure in which a single customer's model approval timing can move company-wide sales is a bearish factor.

Heavy liabilities and negative operating cash flow

Total liabilities were KRW 117.5 billion at end-2025 against total equity of KRW 28.3 billion, a liabilities-to-equity ratio of 416.0%. Equity attributable to controlling shareholders was KRW 18.6 billion.

Operating cash flow was negative in 2022, 2024 and 2025, at -KRW 9.38 billion, -KRW 6.74 billion and -KRW 722 million respectively. A rights offering was included in the 2025 registration statement, so the funding path is a financial watch item.

10

Risk factors

Customer concentration

Sales to Samsung Electro-Mechanics account for roughly 80–90% of revenue, so changes in a single customer's model approvals and volumes flow directly into results. Press coverage notes that sales of key Samsung models can drive earnings volatility. The pace of customer diversification is the key variable for earnings stability.

Smartphone demand and price competition

Smartphone market cycles drive OIS actuator demand, and price competition among global suppliers is intensifying, which is cited as a business risk. Unstable supply of key optical materials such as rare earths could weigh on profitability. The automotive business also faces a time lag before its revenue share becomes meaningful.

Financial structure and funding

The liabilities-to-equity ratio was high at 416.0% at end-2025, and operating cash flow was negative in 2022, 2024 and 2025. Any additional funding could lead to dilution or higher debt.

The company said KRW 5.3 billion of the planned KRW 9.5 billion rights offering in its 2025 registration statement would go to facility funding.

Overall view

Haesung Optics disclosed 2025 sales of KRW 160.3 billion and an operating loss of KRW 6.74 billion after concentrating on a single OIS actuator business. The return to quarterly operating profit in Q4 2025 and sales of KRW 69.5 billion in Q1 2026 are cited as evidence of a healthier business.

On the other side, sales declined in Q2 and Q3 2026, and net losses attributable to controlling shareholders continued for two quarters. On the customer side, sales to Samsung Electro-Mechanics account for roughly 80–90% of revenue, so results depend heavily on a single customer's model approvals and volumes.

On the financial side, a liabilities-to-equity ratio of 416.0% and total equity of KRW 28.3 billion at end-2025 remain burdens. Strengths and weaknesses coexist, and disclosures alone do not settle which side dominates.

The next checkpoints are the Q4 2026 results in the 2026 annual report and the Q1 2027 quarterly report. The key items are whether quarterly operating profit stabilizes in profitable territory and whether Samsung flagship supply continues into 2027.
