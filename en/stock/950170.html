China Group-Tour Gap Tests JTC's Margins

02

Business structure

JTC is a specialist post-purchase tax-free retailer that runs tax-free shops for foreign group tourists visiting Japan. Its business is in-city consumption-tax-free shopping, not airport duty-free.

Customers are Chinese, Korean, Taiwanese and Southeast Asian package tourists who arrive through travel agencies and ground operators. The share of private-brand (PB) products in sales was reported to have risen to 79%. That report is a September 16, 2026 article by The Buyer.

Segment revenue is not part of the reported results, so this paragraph describes shares only. Japan has more than 64,000 tax-free stores, so JTC shares customers with drugstores, department stores and electronics retailers. The company is also extending into Korea.

It plans to start a duty-free business in Korea in the second half, beginning with Jeju. Press reports also describe a plan to use self-owned real estate as stores to avoid rent.

04

Earnings analysis

Annual revenue was KRW 11.4 billion in 2022, KRW 31.3 billion in 2023 (up 174.3% year on year), KRW 137.9 billion in 2024 (up 341.1%) and KRW 288.4 billion in 2025 (up 109.2%), all KRW-converted. Revenue increased for three consecutive years from 2023 to 2025, and 2025 was the largest year within 2022 to 2025.

Operating results were an operating loss of KRW 73.5 billion in 2022, an operating loss of KRW 13.1 billion in 2023 (losses continued but narrowed), operating profit of KRW 20.1 billion in 2024 (turnaround to profit) and operating profit of KRW 44.4 billion in 2025 (up 120.7%).

The operating margin moved from -644.9% to -41.9%, 14.6% and 15.4%. Net profit attributable to owners was KRW 72.2 billion in 2025 (up 283.8%), and net income including non-controlling interests was also KRW 72.2 billion.

Because that exceeds operating profit of KRW 44.4 billion, non-operating items added to earnings, and their makeup should be checked in the notes.

Quarterly revenue was KRW 66.7 billion in the second quarter of 2025, KRW 77.6 billion in the third quarter (up 16.3% from the prior quarter), KRW 58.9 billion in the fourth (down 24.2%), KRW 41.5 billion in the first quarter of 2026 (down 29.4%) and KRW 30.5 billion in the second quarter of 2026 (down 26.6% from the prior quarter and 54.3% from a year earlier).

Operating profit dropped from KRW 26.3 billion in the fourth quarter of 2025 to KRW 1.93 billion in the first quarter of 2026 (down 92.7%), and the second quarter of 2026 brought an operating loss of KRW 2.37 billion, a swing to loss against both the prior quarter and the prior-year quarter.

Net profit attributable to owners peaked at KRW 59.4 billion in the fourth quarter of 2025 and then fell for two consecutive quarters, to KRW 897 million and KRW 149 million.

Of the KRW 65.4 billion earned over the last four quarters (third quarter of 2025 to second quarter of 2026), KRW 59.4 billion came from the fourth quarter of 2025.

As a backdrop, press reports tie the slump to the sharp contraction in Chinese group tour dispatches after relations between China and Japan soured in November 2025. The company began headcount adjustments and the closure or suspension of low-profit stores from the fourth quarter of 2025.

Its provisional results for the fiscal year from March 2025 to February 2026, announced on April 15, 2026, included other operating expenses of KRW 16.2 billion, including an impairment loss of about KRW 9.5 billion. That figure is on a fiscal-year basis and may not map directly onto the annual figures above.

On the balance sheet, 2025 operating cash flow was KRW 45.6 billion and the debt ratio was 95.6%. Total equity at the end of 2025 was KRW 161.4 billion, all attributable to owners, against total liabilities of KRW 154.3 billion.

05

Industry analysis

JTC's demand depends on the number and nationality mix of foreign visitors to Japan. The Japanese government has set a goal of raising inbound visitors from 42.68 million in 2025 to 60 million by 2030, with spending of 15 trillion yen.

Even if overall tourism grows, operators with a heavy Chinese group share are exposed to swings by nationality. In June to August 2025, rumors of a major earthquake, heat waves, typhoons and anti-Japan sentiment in China weighed on group travel to Japan. The policy backdrop is also changing.

The Japanese government is introducing a refund-method tax-free system to curb resale of tax-free goods. Tourists will pay the tax-inclusive price first and receive the consumption tax back after customs confirms the goods are leaving the country.

The 500,000 yen daily cap on consumables is abolished, while the 5,000 yen minimum purchase stays. Some worry that the more complex refund process could reduce purchases. A tourism marketing support company projected that department store tax-free sales could fall about 10% if resellers decline.

That projection concerns department stores and does not carry over directly to group-tour-focused post-purchase tax-free shops.

06

Outlook

The company says a recovery in Chinese group tourism is unlikely in the short term. It is therefore consolidating the existing business while diversifying customer nationalities toward Korea, Taiwan and Southeast Asia and strengthening ground-operator sales.

Sales to non-Chinese customers rose 39.3% year on year in March to May 2026, yet reported revenue fell further to KRW 30.5 billion in the second quarter of 2026. Growth outside China has not yet filled the gap left by Chinese customers.

As a new revenue source, the company plans to start a duty-free business in Korea in the second half, beginning with Jeju, but the opening schedule and sales scale have not been confirmed.

On shareholder returns, the company decided to cancel all 2,051,675 treasury shares bought on the market from May 7 to July 29, 2026. The actual reduction in issued shares will be reflected after local administrative procedures in Japan are completed.

Results for quarters after the fourth quarter of 2025 have no periodic report yet, and no quantitative guidance from the company has been confirmed.

08

Bull factors

Customer diversification is progressing

Sales to non-Chinese customers rose 39.3% year on year in March to May 2026. The Buyer reported that non-China sales grew for three consecutive quarters. Korean, Taiwanese and Thai customers are the lever that reduces reliance on China. If the trend continues, the demand base could be broader when Chinese group travel returns.

Balance sheet strength and shareholder returns

The debt ratio fell sharply to 95.6% at the end of 2025 from 222.8% at the end of 2024. Operating cash flow in 2025 was KRW 45.6 billion. Treasury shares acquired and cancelled in two rounds in 2026 amount to about KRW 25 billion. The capacity to pursue capital policy even as revenue declines is cited as a strength.

Cost restructuring and a new revenue source

The company has reworked its operating structure through headcount adjustments and closures or suspensions of low-profit stores. In reported results, the first quarter of 2026 still produced operating profit of KRW 1.93 billion even though revenue fell to KRW 41.5 billion.

It plans to launch a duty-free business in Korea in the second half, starting with Jeju. A lower fixed-cost burden leaves room for the operating margin to improve if revenue recovers.

09

Bear factors

Falling revenue and a swing to operating loss

Quarterly revenue fell for three consecutive quarters from the fourth quarter of 2025 to the second quarter of 2026. Revenue in the second quarter of 2026 was down 54.3% from a year earlier. The same quarter produced an operating loss of KRW 2.37 billion, interrupting the run of profits.

Net profit attributable to owners in the first and second quarters of 2026 was KRW 897 million and KRW 149 million, far below the fourth quarter of 2025.

The China gap may last

Sales to Chinese nationals fell 96.2% year on year in March to May 2026. The company itself believes a recovery in Chinese group tourism will be hard in the short term. Non-Chinese sales are growing, but reported total revenue kept shrinking. The longer the gap lasts, the more fixed-cost burden and further restructuring costs become variables.

Demand uncertainty from the tax-free overhaul

From November 1, 2026, tourists will pay tax-inclusive prices first and be refunded at departure. There is concern that extra steps at departure and refund fees could make shoppers reluctant to buy. There is no grace period running the old and new systems in parallel. The effect on group-tour-focused post-purchase tax-free shops has not yet appeared in results.

10

Risk factors

Policy and regulation

Japan's refund-method system aims to eliminate resale of tax-free goods. Because customer payment and refund procedures change, post-purchase tax-free shops may face both operating adaptation costs and shifts in purchasing behavior. Fee burdens can also differ depending on who processes refunds and how. How the company responds to the overhaul should be checked in its filings and investor materials.

Geopolitics and demand

Worsening relations between China and Japan were reported as the direct cause of the sharp fall in Chinese group tour dispatches. In summer 2025, natural events such as earthquake rumors, heat waves and typhoons also weighed on group travel demand.

Diplomatic relations and natural disasters are beyond the company's control. Easing in one factor does not prevent another from disrupting demand.

Currency and earnings composition

The company reports in Japanese yen, so KRW-converted amounts move with exchange rates. Provisional results for the fiscal year from March 2025 to February 2026 included an FX loss of about KRW 3.1 billion within other operating expenses.

Because the fourth quarter of 2025 accounts for most of the profit earned over the last four quarters, the durability of the earnings mix is also a point to check. The share count reduction from cancellations will be reflected after Japanese administrative procedures are completed.

12

Overall view

After revenue and the operating margin rose sharply from 2022 to 2025, JTC is in a phase of declining quarterly revenue that began after the third-quarter 2025 peak. In the second quarter of 2026 it swung to an operating loss of KRW 2.37 billion on revenue of KRW 30.5 billion.

Sales to Chinese nationals have collapsed while sales to non-Chinese customers are growing, but that growth has not yet stopped the decline in total revenue.

On the financial side, a debt ratio of 95.6% and 2025 operating cash flow of KRW 45.6 billion act as a cushion, and shareholder returns such as share cancellations are under way.

External variables remain: net profit concentrated in the fourth quarter of 2025, Japan's tax-free overhaul effective November 1, 2026, and China-Japan relations. The next things to check are purchase trends after the new system starts, whether the Jeju duty-free store opens, and the next quarter's operating result.
