Diversifying Beyond Chinese Group Tourists Amid Earnings Swings

JTC is reducing its reliance on Chinese group tourists, while its operating profit fell sharply in the fiscal year ended February 2026 and it swung to an operating loss in the quarter ended August 2026.

02

Business structure

JTC began in 1993 in Beppu, Kyushu, and its core business is operating duty-free shops that sell to foreign travelers visiting Japan. Its headquarters are in Fukuoka, and the company runs 24 stores across Japan according to its company profile. The stores operate under six duty-free brands.

The company has staffed each store with sales personnel who can consult in the customer's native language, building a sales base centered on group travelers. Because the earnings decline coincided with the drop in Chinese group tourists, the number of visitors from that segment has had a large effect on results.

In response, the company has pushed to expand customers from countries other than China, and reports said sales to Korean customers rose 39.0% and sales to Taiwanese customers rose 11.6% in the first half of 2026 (March to August), both the highest first-half results on record.

In Korea, the company opened a duty-free store on Jeju Island in 2026 to widen its domestic footprint.

On competition, the securities information site FnGuide noted in a June 2025 comment that, unlike competitors that closed most of their stores during the COVID-19 period, JTC kept stores in each base and can respond quickly to inbound recovery.

The company has been listed on KOSDAQ since April 2018 as a foreign company, which is also useful context for its business structure.

04

Earnings analysis

The company reports in yen (JPY), and the amounts below are KRW-converted values. Annual revenue rose from KRW 31.4 billion in the fiscal year ended February 2023 to KRW 138.6 billion in the fiscal year ended February 2024, up 341.1% year on year.

It then reached KRW 289.9 billion in the fiscal year ended February 2025, up 109.2%, which was the largest year among the fiscal years ended February 2023 through February 2026. Revenue for the fiscal year ended February 2026 was KRW 270.9 billion, down 6.6% from the prior year.

Operating results moved from an operating loss of KRW 13.2 billion in the fiscal year ended February 2023 to an operating profit of KRW 20.2 billion in the fiscal year ended February 2024, a swing to profit. Operating profit then rose to KRW 44.7 billion in the fiscal year ended February 2025, up 120.7%.

For the fiscal year ended February 2026, operating profit was KRW 5.31 billion, down 88.1%, and the operating margin fell to 2.0%. Over the same period, net profit attributable to controlling shareholders fell 94.0% from KRW 72.6 billion to KRW 4.35 billion.

Profit declined far more steeply than revenue, and a press report citing the company's disclosure pointed to other operating expenses, including impairment losses on assets and foreign exchange losses, along with one-time costs from restructuring efficiency measures.

Operating cash flow fell from KRW 45.8 billion in the fiscal year ended February 2025 to KRW 23.7 billion in the fiscal year ended February 2026, but stayed positive. By quarter, the quarter of September to November 2025 had the highest revenue at KRW 78 billion and operating profit of KRW 6.54 billion.

The quarter of December 2025 to February 2026 saw revenue drop to KRW 40.2 billion and an operating loss of KRW 12.9 billion. The quarter of March to May 2026 returned to operating profit of KRW 1.94 billion.

The quarter of June to August 2026 recorded revenue of KRW 30.7 billion, down 54.3% year on year, and an operating loss of KRW 2.38 billion, a swing to loss compared with the same quarter a year earlier.

Net profit attributable to controlling shareholders was KRW 900 million in the quarter of March to May 2026 and KRW 150 million in the quarter of June to August 2026, but was a loss of KRW 8.44 billion in the quarter of December 2025 to February 2026.

The sum of controlling-shareholder net losses over the last four quarters (September to November 2025 through June to August 2026) is KRW 2.49 billion.

05

Industry analysis

JTC's end demand comes from overseas visitors to Japan, and its results are strongly affected by spending patterns of Chinese group tourists. Reports on first-half 2026 results said revenue fell as Chinese group tourist numbers declined, while the company is responding by expanding its customer base outside China.

Changes in relations between Japan and China have been cited as a variable that can shift the direction of Chinese tourist demand, and this is a common external factor across Japanese duty-free retail.

The company has broadened its customer base to Korea, Taiwan and Thailand, and sales to non-Chinese customers in the quarter of March to May 2026 rose 39.3% year on year.

On competition, a June 2025 FnGuide comment said that JTC kept stores in each base, unlike competitors that closed most of their stores during COVID-19. The same source does not provide market share or total industry size figures, so comparisons with competitors are limited to store operating models.

Strengths are the results of customer diversification and the retained store network, while weaknesses are volatility in Chinese group tourist demand and in spending by foreign visitors.

06

Outlook

On January 7, 2026, the company decided to buy back shares worth KRW 15 billion, and it conducted open-market purchases on KOSDAQ from February 2 to July 31, 2026. On April 30, 2026, it disclosed a further buyback of about KRW 10 billion and said the repurchased shares would be cancelled.

On August 19, 2026, the board decided to cancel all shares from the second buyback. The shares repurchased and cancelled in two rounds this year total 5,024,908 shares, equal to 9.7% of total issued shares.

The actual reduction in issued shares from the cancellation will be reflected after Japanese administrative procedures are completed.

On the business side, the Jeju duty-free store opening has begun the company's push into the Korean market, and the company says it will expand its customer base to match changes in demand by nationality while growing its Korean duty-free business.

On costs, personnel expenses fell 28.2% in the first half of 2026, and asset efficiency work including sales of idle assets is ongoing. Whether Chinese group tourist demand recovers, and how much the Korean stores contribute, will show up in the next quarters' revenue and operating margin.

08

Bull factors

Results of customer diversification

Sales to non-Chinese customers rose 38.8% in the first half of 2026, with Korean customer sales up 39.0% and Taiwanese customer sales up 11.6%. Reports described both as first-half records. The trend partly fills the revenue gap caused by fewer Chinese group tourists. The Jeju store opening has also broadened the Korean business base.

Expanded shareholder returns

A KRW 15 billion buyback was decided in January 2026, and an additional buyback of about KRW 10 billion was disclosed in April 2026. In August 2026, the board decided to cancel all shares from the second round. Shares bought back and cancelled this year total 5,024,908, equal to 9.7% of issued shares. The reduction in issued shares will be reflected after Japanese administrative procedures are completed.

Cost efficiency and cash generation

Personnel expenses fell 28.2% in the first half of 2026, and asset efficiency measures including sales of idle assets were pursued alongside. Operating cash flow stayed positive at KRW 23.7 billion in the fiscal year ended February 2026.

Net profit attributable to controlling shareholders was positive in the quarters of March to May and June to August 2026. Reports said the cost adjustments helped maintain profitability even as revenue declined.

09

Bear factors

Dependence on Chinese group tourists and falling sales

Revenue for the fiscal year ended February 2026 fell 6.6% from the prior year. Revenue in the quarter of June to August 2026 was KRW 30.7 billion, down 54.3% year on year. Reports cited the drop in Chinese group tourists as the direct cause of the sales decline.

Customer diversification is underway, but its scale in replacing the lost Chinese demand has not yet been reflected in the disclosed results.

Weaker earnings capacity

The operating margin for the fiscal year ended February 2026 was 2.0%, down sharply from 15.4% in the fiscal year ended February 2025. Net profit attributable to controlling shareholders fell 94.0%. The sum of controlling-shareholder net losses over the last four quarters is KRW 2.49 billion.

The company posted an operating loss of KRW 12.9 billion in the quarter of December 2025 to February 2026 and KRW 2.38 billion in the quarter of June to August 2026.

One-time costs and expansion burden

Other operating expenses, including impairment losses on assets and foreign exchange losses, were cited as factors behind the profit decline in a press report based on the company's disclosure. One-time costs from restructuring efficiency measures also reduced profit.

Expansion in Korea, including the Jeju store, may bring initial investment and operating costs. If one-time costs recur, the recovery of the operating margin could be slower.

10

Risk factors

Diplomatic and tourism demand risk

Changes in relations between Japan and China can shift the direction of Chinese tourist demand. Chinese group tourists have been an important customer group for the company. If diplomatic conditions worsen again, visits to Japan could decline. How much the expansion to non-Chinese customers absorbs this risk needs to be confirmed in quarterly results.

Earnings volatility and cost risk

Impairment losses on assets and foreign exchange losses have appeared as factors that move profit. Quarterly operating results swung from KRW 6.54 billion in operating profit in the quarter of September to November 2025 to an operating loss of KRW 12.9 billion in the quarter of December 2025 to February 2026.

Yen-to-won conversion also creates differences in won-based results. When quarterly profit moves sharply, the reliability of annual earnings forecasts declines.

Free float and ownership structure risk

According to a September 2025 disclosure, the largest shareholder and related parties held roughly 70% of shares. A small free float can increase share price volatility. In June 2026, the company disclosed the cancellation of a share-pledge agreement tied to a change in the largest shareholder.

How the reduction in issued shares from cancellations is reflected in the share structure needs to be confirmed.

Overall view

JTC operates after-sales duty-free shops in Japan and moved from a loss to a profit in the fiscal year ended February 2024, but its profit fell sharply in the fiscal year ended February 2026.

Annual revenue fell from KRW 289.9 billion in the fiscal year ended February 2025 to KRW 270.9 billion in the fiscal year ended February 2026, and the operating margin dropped to 2.0%.

In the quarter of June to August 2026, revenue was KRW 30.7 billion and the company posted an operating loss of KRW 2.38 billion, a swing to loss on a quarterly basis.

Strengths include fast growth in sales to non-Chinese customers, shareholder returns through buybacks and cancellations, and cost efficiency from personnel and idle-asset measures.

Weaknesses include dependence on Chinese group tourists, external factors such as Japan-China relations and exchange rates, and one-time costs such as impairment losses and foreign exchange losses.

The price-to-earnings ratio cannot be calculated because of the net loss over the last four quarters, and the price-to-book ratio sits slightly above book value per share.

The operating margin in the next annual report, the performance of the Korean business, and changes in issued shares after cancellation are the points to confirm. This report is intended to organize facts and does not present an investment opinion or target price.
