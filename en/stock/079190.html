Operating Profit Back in the Black; Net Loss and New Businesses Remain Tasks

02

Business structure

Kespion was founded in 1998 as an R&D-focused wireless communications manufacturer and supplies smartphone antennas and related parts to Samsung Electronics. As of the end of the third quarter of 2025, about 96% of total revenue came from smartphone antenna products.

The CEO has said that a structure relying on Samsung for 90% of sales cannot sustain growth on its own. Its antenna modules are built on smartphone materials such as ferrite and LTCC. Slower smartphone market growth and intensifying price competition in communication parts have weighed on antenna profitability.

In response, the company is pursuing new businesses through M&A. Press reports said it used proceeds from a convertible bond decided in January 2026 to acquire a 93.2% stake in MBTB, a maker of hydrocolloid acne patches and fabrics.

The company argues that the film and coating know-how used in smartphone antennas underpins the patch process. Several established domestic hydrocolloid patch makers already hold positions in the market, which makes securing customers harder for a newcomer.

The company is also developing zinc-air batteries for military use with a university research partner and has filed patent applications. Revenue from the new businesses is still small relative to the antenna business, and the pace of change depends on customer approvals.

04

Earnings analysis

Annual revenue rose from KRW 55.6 billion in 2022 to KRW 63.2 billion in 2023, up 13.7% year on year, then fell 48.4% to KRW 32.6 billion in 2024 before climbing 35.4% to KRW 44.1 billion in 2025. Among 2022 to 2025, 2023 was the largest year and 2024 the smallest.

Operating results moved from an operating loss of KRW 3.78 billion in 2022 to an operating profit of KRW 1.07 billion in 2023, a swing to profit, then back to an operating loss of KRW 5.37 billion in 2024, a swing to loss.

The 2025 operating loss narrowed to KRW 2.74 billion, and the company has posted operating losses for two consecutive years from 2024 through 2025. Operating margin was -6.8% in 2022, 1.7% in 2023, -16.5% in 2024 and -6.2% in 2025, with 2024 the lowest as revenue fell.

Net income attributable to controlling shareholders turned to profit at KRW 2.72 billion in 2023, then turned to losses of KRW 3.87 billion in 2024 and KRW 4.58 billion in 2025, a widening deficit.

In 2025 the company cited higher revenue and a smaller operating loss, while noting that higher non-operating expenses, including impairment losses, weighed on results. Operating cash flow fell from KRW 4.02 billion in 2023 to KRW 163 million in 2024 and turned to an outflow of KRW 3.35 billion in 2025.

On a quarterly basis, the operating loss narrowed from KRW 452 million in the third quarter of 2025 to KRW 13.43 million in the fourth quarter, and the first quarter of 2026 returned to operating profit of KRW 228 million.

Second-quarter 2026 operating profit rose 147.3% from the prior quarter to KRW 563 million, and third-quarter operating profit of KRW 302 million, down 46.4% quarter on quarter, was a swing to profit from a year earlier.

Third-quarter 2026 revenue of KRW 11.3 billion fell 1.9% year on year, and the net loss attributable to controlling shareholders of KRW 1.45 billion was a swing to loss from a year earlier.

The net loss attributable to controlling shareholders over the latest four quarters totals KRW 3.04 billion, and a gap remains between operating profit and bottom-line results.

05

Industry analysis

Kespion's end market is smartphones, and slower growth in the smartphone market is noted in external coverage. Communication parts face intensifying price competition, which the company cited as the reason for its business restructuring.

On the customer side, dependence on Samsung Electronics, at roughly 90% of sales by the CEO's account, is a structural feature. The hydrocolloid acne patch market that the new business is entering is seeing advancing manufacturing techniques, including functional ingredients and fine structures.

Several established domestic makers already hold positions in this market, making it difficult for a late entrant to add customers quickly. Overseas end-line suppliers reportedly responded relatively positively to Kespion products in early quality testing.

However, approval procedures have lengthened as a domestic cosmetics maker moves to replace its supplier. In defense, the company says zinc-air batteries carry lower explosion risk than lithium batteries, fitting military demand for backup power.

Competitively, its revenue scale and business scope are narrower than those of larger components makers in the sector, and revenue remains concentrated in one product line of smartphone antennas, so diversification progress will shape its position.

06

Outlook

In a February 2026 interview, the company set a target of returning annual operating profit to the black in 2026. In the same interview it said MBTB revenue would begin to be recognized from May 2026.

However, a May 2026 press report said that supply contracts with a domestic cosmetics maker are being pursued, but product testing and supply-chain approval procedures have lengthened and revenue timing is slipping. A company official said time is needed for the supplier replacement to be approved.

As a medium-term goal, the company aims to expand new-business revenue to around KRW 10 billion in 2027 and KRW 20 billion in 2028.

Zinc-air batteries have completed high- and low-temperature performance and environmental evaluations and are in patent filing, and the company expects new revenue to become visible with entry into a domestic defense program in 2027.

The roughly KRW 13.0 billion third-party rights offering completed in 2026 is intended to sustain the existing communication-parts and beauty businesses while funding new projects.

Shares issued in the rights offering to a newly designated largest shareholder investment partnership are subject to one-year mandatory custody.

The next checkpoint is whether annual operating profit in the 2026 annual report meets the return-to-profit target, and whether new-business revenue appears in quarterly results will become clear in the 2027 quarterly reports.

08

Bull factors

Operating profit back in the black

Operating profit in the first quarter of 2026 was KRW 228 million, a swing to profit from the prior quarter, followed by KRW 563 million in the second quarter and KRW 302 million in the third.

Second-quarter operating profit rose 147.3% from the prior quarter, and third-quarter operating profit swung to profit from a year earlier. The move from operating losses to operating profit is the first indicator of a structural change.

New-business revenue targets set

The company has set new-business revenue targets of about KRW 10 billion in 2027 and KRW 20 billion in 2028. MBTB produces and sells hydrocolloid acne patches and fabrics, and the company says it draws on existing film and coating technology.

The effort aims to diversify revenue beyond a structure centered on smartphone antennas for Samsung Electronics.

Capital structure reinforced

A capital reduction in 2026 at a 50% ratio was decided to improve the capital structure by offsetting accumulated deficits. New shares from a roughly KRW 13.0 billion third-party rights offering were then listed, strengthening the capital base.

The proceeds are planned to sustain the existing communication-parts and beauty businesses while funding new projects.

Bear factors

Samsung dependence and price competition

As of the end of the third quarter of 2025, about 96% of revenue came from smartphone antennas, and the CEO acknowledged the limits of a structure in which 90% of sales depend on Samsung Electronics.

Slowing smartphone growth and intensifying price competition in communication parts have weakened antenna profitability, according to analysis. Revenue rose 35.4% in 2025, yet an operating loss of KRW 2.74 billion persisted.

Delayed timing of new-business revenue

In a February 2026 interview, the company said MBTB revenue would begin in May 2026. A May 2026 press report said product testing and supply-chain approvals during contract negotiations have lengthened, pushing back revenue timing.

Because the company has no prior hydrocolloid experience, it must go through the full process from production to customer approval, which is seen as a burden.

Continued net losses and managed-stock designation

The net loss attributable to controlling shareholders over the latest four quarters totals KRW 3.04 billion, and the third quarter of 2026 recorded a net loss of KRW 1.45 billion, a swing to loss from a year earlier.

The company was designated a managed stock on August 20, 2026 because its market capitalization fell short. Operating profit that does not carry through to net profit remains a bearish factor.

Risk factors

Listing requirements

The KOSDAQ listing-maintenance threshold is KRW 20 billion in market capitalization, and new shares listed in September 2026 pushed market capitalization above that level. However, the managed-stock designation was made on August 20, 2026 because market capitalization fell short.

Market capitalization moves with the share price and the number of listed shares, so whether the requirement is met can keep changing.

Dilution and share overhang

The convertible bonds carry a conversion price of KRW 500, and shares issued on conversion would equal 9.85% of total shares outstanding. The new shares held by the investment partnership that became the largest shareholder in the rights offering are subject to one-year mandatory custody. Once that custody ends, shares could reach the market, so the timing of the supply pressure is set in advance.

Concentration and execution

Most revenue depends on supply to Samsung Electronics, so customer concentration is high. If new-business revenue does not grow enough to offset any decline in antenna sales, structural dependence will persist.

Zinc-air battery plans rest on patent filings and entry into a 2027 defense program, so the schedule could slip.

Overall view

Kespion's revenue moved sharply, from KRW 55.6 billion in 2022 to KRW 63.2 billion in 2023, KRW 32.6 billion in 2024 and KRW 44.1 billion in 2025, with operating and net losses persisting through 2025.

Quarterly operating profit was positive from the first quarter through the third quarter of 2026, but the third-quarter net loss attributable to controlling shareholders of KRW 1.45 billion brought the four-quarter total to KRW 3.04 billion.

Bullish factors include the swing to operating profit, the broader new-business portfolio and a capital base strengthened by the rights offering and capital reduction.

Bearish factors include heavy dependence on Samsung Electronics for smartphone antennas, delayed customer approvals for new businesses, the managed-stock designation and market-capitalization requirement, and the overhang from convertible bonds and new shares.

Both sets of factors coexist, and neither settles the outcome on its own. On valuation, the price-to-earnings ratio cannot be calculated and the price-to-book ratio sits below 1x.
