02

Business structure

Hyundai Hyms makes ship blocks and ship component parts, and ship component parts is its core business. Production is split between plants in Pohang and the Daebul industrial complex in Mokpo, South Jeolla Province, and the company says its main facilities have annual block capacity of about 200,000 tons.

A 2026 report said engine rooms made at Daebul No. 2 account for roughly half of the plant's output, and the company handles half of the engine room production for HD Hyundai Samho. Its main customers are HD Hyundai shipbuilding affiliates, and their order backlogs underpin its production volumes.

In port cranes, the company says it produces 70% of the crane structures built by HD Hyundai Samho, and makes them at Daebul No. 4 plant. CEO Choi Ji-yong expects port crane revenue to rise from about KRW 20 billion in 2025 to about KRW 27 billion in 2026.

In eco-friendly ships, the company is preparing independent cargo tanks and fuel tanks for LPG and ammonia carriers, with KRW 29 billion earmarked for related equipment. A subsidiary handles gas generators for eco-friendly marine systems.

Competitively, Hyundai Hyms works as an outsourced block producer for large shipbuilders, so its earnings track the volumes of its group customers rather than independent orders.

04

Earnings analysis

On a disclosed annual basis, revenue rose from KRW 144.8 billion in 2022 to KRW 248.2 billion in 2025. Revenue reached KRW 189.2 billion in 2023 (up 30.7% year on year), KRW 223.2 billion in 2024 (up 18.0%) and KRW 248.2 billion in 2025 (up 11.2%), rising three years in a row from 2023 through 2025.

Operating profit went from KRW 3.76 billion in 2022 to KRW 14.5 billion in 2023 (up 285.0%), KRW 21.5 billion in 2024 (up 49.0%) and KRW 28.8 billion in 2025 (up 33.5%), also increasing three years in a row.

The operating margin moved from 2.6% to 7.6%, 9.7% and 11.6% over the same period, so profit growth outpaced revenue growth. The company attributed the 2025 revenue increase to expanded orders from major customers and new businesses, and port crane revenue began to be recognized in earnings in 2025.

Net profit attributable to controlling shareholders rose from KRW 4.37 billion in 2022 to KRW 10.1 billion in 2023, KRW 16.6 billion in 2024 and KRW 21.4 billion in 2025 (up 29.2%).

Net profit including non-controlling interests was reported at KRW 4.37 billion, KRW 10.1 billion, KRW 16.6 billion and KRW 21.4 billion over the same years.

On a quarterly basis, revenue went from KRW 60.6 billion with operating profit of KRW 6.79 billion in the third quarter of 2025 to KRW 64.5 billion with operating profit of KRW 8.1 billion in the third quarter of 2026.

Operating profit reached KRW 8.53 billion in the first quarter of 2026 (up 31.7% from the prior quarter) and KRW 12.2 billion in the second quarter (up 42.8%), the highest among the last five quarters.

In the third quarter, revenue fell 11.4% and operating profit fell 33.5% from the prior quarter, though both rose year on year, with revenue up 6.4% and operating profit up 19.4%. Net profit attributable to controlling shareholders over the last four quarters totaled KRW 25.9 billion.

At the end of 2025, total equity was KRW 252.9 billion, all of it attributable to controlling shareholders, and total liabilities were KRW 80.5 billion. Operating cash flow was KRW 19 billion in 2022, KRW 17 billion in 2023, KRW 31.4 billion in 2024 and KRW 37 billion in 2025.

05

Industry analysis

Hyundai Hyms' end market is the construction volume of large Korean shipbuilders. HD Hyundai Heavy Industries set a 2026 order target of USD 14.5 billion, 21.8% above its prior-year order intake of USD 11.9 billion, with USD 11.5 billion for commercial vessels and USD 3 billion for naval and other special vessels.

A November 2025 report said South Korea's share of global shipbuilding orders rose from 10% in 2024 to 39% in 2025. The US Trade Representative has announced measures that impose port fees on Chinese-built ships, which has raised demand for alternative suppliers of port equipment.

A March 2026 report said a US investment project could expand the role of Korean shipbuilders, naming Hyundai Hyms as one of the potential beneficiaries. On the supply side, a severe shortage of skilled welders across the shipbuilding industry limits production growth.

The company has responded with dormitory construction, shuttle buses and investment in intelligent automatic welding equipment. Among competitors, its Pohang No. 1 plant is cited as the only site in the Yeongnam region able to handle advance outfitting work, which the company presents as an advantage.

Because it is a block and equipment maker, however, its volumes move with the order cycles and production schedules of large shipbuilders. Whether the upcycle continues will show up in those customers' order results and delivery schedules.

Outlook

The company's CEO set a target of growing 2026 revenue by more than 10% from the prior year. In the same remarks he said port crane revenue would rise to roughly KRW 27 billion in 2026 and that new-business production would start in the second half of 2026.

A report dated July 30, 2026 described a KRW 5.5 billion order for port crane structures bound for the United States, which the company presented as a step into the North American market. A tally as of September 7, 2026 showed first-half 2026 consolidated revenue up 13.8% year on year and operating profit up 33.6%.

The eco-friendly ship business is still in the investment phase, with KRW 29 billion earmarked for independent cargo tank and fuel tank equipment. The company says the new Daebul No. 4 plant has expanded production capacity and that production efficiency in crane structures is improving.

Investment bankers have described the profitability and order durability of the new businesses as still early-stage.

The next checkpoints are the 2026 preliminary annual results and the full-year operating margin reported in the annual report, along with whether port crane and eco-friendly tank orders are disclosed and how the sale of the largest shareholder's stake proceeds.

Bull factors

Profitability Has Improved for Three Years

The operating margin rose from 2.6% in 2022 to 11.6% in 2025, and operating profit has increased three years in a row through 2025. Second-quarter 2026 operating profit of KRW 12.2 billion was the largest of the last five quarters. Profit growth has outpaced revenue growth in disclosed results.

Port Crane Business Is Generating Revenue

Port crane structure revenue began in 2025, and the CEO expects it to rise from about KRW 20 billion in 2025 to about KRW 27 billion in 2026. A July 30, 2026 report described a KRW 5.5 billion order for US-bound port crane structures. Restrictions on Chinese equipment could also widen demand for alternative suppliers.

Balance Sheet and Cash Generation Have Strengthened

The debt ratio fell from 39.3% in 2022 to 31.8% in 2025. Operating cash flow rose from KRW 19 billion in 2022 to KRW 31.4 billion in 2024 and KRW 37 billion in 2025. Total equity of KRW 252.9 billion at the end of 2025 gives the company more internal capacity to fund plant and eco-friendly equipment investments.

Bear factors

Quarterly Profit Has Become Volatile

Third-quarter 2026 revenue of KRW 64.5 billion fell 11.4% from the prior quarter, and operating profit of KRW 8.1 billion fell 33.5%. Fourth-quarter 2025 operating profit also declined 4.6% from the prior quarter.

Because quarterly results can swing sharply within a few quarters, the annual trend needs to be checked as a separate matter.

Dependence on Group Customer Volumes

The company secures production volumes based on order backlogs at HD Hyundai shipbuilding affiliates. The fact that a shipyard within the group accounts for 70% of the port crane structures it produces also points to customer concentration. A decline in group orders or a delay in construction schedules could affect its volumes and utilization.

New Businesses Are Still Early-Stage

Investment banking analysts have said the new businesses remain early-stage and need validation of profitability, order durability and cost control. Eco-friendly tank equipment carries a KRW 29 billion investment.

Until new-business revenue grows to a larger share, margins are likely to continue depending on the established block business.

Risk factors

Parent Shipbuilder Order Cycle

HD Hyundai Heavy Industries set a 2026 order target of USD 14.5 billion, but whether it is reached depends on actual orders and construction schedules at large shipbuilders. A slowdown in orders or a shift in the order mix could affect block volumes and utilization. A global slowdown and lower shipping volumes are also cited as risks to the shipbuilding cycle.

Ownership Change and Stake Sale

Reports have continued about a sale of the 52.88% stake held by the largest shareholder, a private equity fund. A change in control could alter customer relationships and investment plans.

The July 2026 disclosure list includes a material report on an equity acquisition, so follow-up filings should be tracked together with the sale.

Labor and Capacity Constraints

The shipbuilding industry is widely described as facing a serious shortage of skilled welders. The company is responding with dormitory construction and automation investment, but labor availability could still limit production speed when volumes rise.

Extending the AI safety system from the Daebul plant to other sites also remains a task for operations and safety management.
