Report overview

Revenue Stabilizing, Profitability Still in the Red

Coreana Cosmetics swung to an operating loss in 2025 and has posted operating losses for five straight quarters through Q2 2026, even as revenue has risen for three consecutive quarters since bottoming in Q3 2025.

  2. 2

  3. 3

  4. 4

02

Business structure

Founded in 1988, Coreana Cosmetics manufactures and sells skincare and color cosmetics, running both its own brands and OEM/ODM contract manufacturing in parallel.

According to its 2021 annual report, revenue was split across door-to-door and direct sales (27.9%), retail (4.8%), online (9.9%), exports (29.1%), brand contract manufacturing/wholesale (26.6%), and skin-care shops and rental income (1.7%), reflecting a channel mix spread fairly evenly across direct sales, exports, and contract manufacturing.

The company operates a China subsidiary (Tianjin) covering both production and sales, meaning greater China's economic conditions and local consumption trends feed directly into results.

In the domestic OEM/ODM industry, large players such as Kolmar Korea, Cosmax, Cosmecca Korea, and C&C International dominate the top of the revenue ranking, leaving Coreana with comparatively smaller production scale that relies more on niche demand and its own distribution network.

Competitively, it sits as a mid-to-small player squeezed between large-scale ODM manufacturers and independent brand companies across both skincare and color cosmetics.

The company has traditionally carried a relatively high share of door-to-door sales, and contraction in that channel has recently been cited as a drag on performance.

Its main customers span domestic consumers and export counterparties including greater China, with brand contract manufacturing and wholesale also contributing a meaningful share of revenue historically.

The founding Yoo Sang-ok family remains involved in management, with eldest son Yoo Hak-soo, the CEO, holding the largest shareholder position.

04

Earnings analysis

Annual revenue rose from KRW 80.4 billion in 2022 to a peak of KRW 86 billion in 2023 (up 7.0%), then declined for two straight years to KRW 82.5 billion in 2024 (down 4.2%) and KRW 73.7 billion in 2025 (down 10.6%).

Operating profit held modestly positive at KRW 876 million in 2022 and KRW 936 million in 2023, before plunging 52.9% to KRW 441 million in 2024 and swinging to an operating loss of KRW 4.39 billion in 2025.

Net income attributable to controlling shareholders followed a similar path, peaking at KRW 1.79 billion in 2023 (up 18.0%), falling to KRW 822 million in 2024 (down 54.1%), and reversing into a net loss of KRW 7.74 billion in 2025.

The company attributed the widening losses to structurally entrenched cost pressure from rising raw material prices, prolonged weakness in greater China and declining K-beauty competitiveness that stalled exports, and accumulated revenue declines from a shrinking door-to-door sales channel.

On a quarterly basis, the operating loss widened from KRW 507 million in Q2 2025 to KRW 1.56 billion in Q3 2025, then narrowed to KRW 1.39 billion in Q4 2025 and KRW 465 million in Q1 2026, before widening again to KRW 1.06 billion in Q2 2026, extending the streak to five consecutive quarters of operating losses.

Revenue fell 18.0% from KRW 20.5 billion in Q2 2025 to KRW 16.8 billion in Q3 2025, then rose for three straight quarters to KRW 17.3 billion in Q4 2025, KRW 18.6 billion in Q1 2026, and KRW 18.9 billion in Q2 2026.

Net income attributable to controlling shareholders has swung sharply by quarter, turning profitable at KRW 215 million in Q1 2026 before reverting to a net loss of KRW 1.14 billion in Q2 2026, showing no stable return to profitability.

Operating cash flow also weakened steadily, from KRW 5.6 billion in 2022 to KRW 1.04 billion in 2024, before turning negative for the first time at -KRW 1.55 billion in 2025, indicating the slowdown extended beyond reported earnings into cash generation.

05

Industry analysis

Since 2026 began, Korean cosmetics exports have shown clear strength centered on the US and Europe.

Preliminary figures released in October 2026 put January-to-September exports at $11.1 billion, up 31.1% year over year, with US-bound exports up 40.6% and now accounting for 21.1% of the total, making the US the largest single export market.

Much of the sector's growth momentum has concentrated in large OEM/ODM players such as Kolmar Korea and Cosmax and brand companies such as APR, and Hana Securities projected that Korea's 2026 cosmetics export growth would reach around 33%, well above its initial 15% forecast.

However, this growth has not been evenly distributed: since 2023, large-cap cosmetics stocks have fallen while small and mid-cap and ODM stocks have risen sharply, underscoring pronounced differentiation within the sector.

The domestic OEM/ODM industry that Coreana belongs to benefited broadly from the K-beauty boom in 2025, with 81 companies' combined revenue up 11.8%, operating profit up 24.5%, and net income up 9.8% year over year, but that growth concentrated among top players including Kolmar Korea, Cosmax, Cosmecca Korea, and C&C International.

China, by contrast, remains weak, with a substantial share of Korean companies operating there considering scaling back or withdrawing, which weighs on firms with heavier exposure to greater China.

Despite having both export and OEM exposure, Coreana posted a revenue decline and operating loss in 2025 that diverged from the broader industry's upswing, making it a notable case of the gap between sector-wide conditions and company-specific results.

06

Outlook

In its 2025 earnings disclosure, Coreana attributed the widening losses to structurally entrenched cost pressure from rising raw material prices, export stagnation from prolonged weakness in greater China, and accumulated revenue declines from a shrinking door-to-door sales channel, and whether these factors ease will be key to any earnings recovery.

Revenue rising for three consecutive quarters through Q2 2026 after bottoming in Q3 2025 could be read as a sign that the revenue decline has stabilized, but operating results have not reached breakeven over the same period and the loss widened again in Q2 2026, meaning the revenue recovery has not yet translated into improved profitability.

At the industry level, multiple brokerage forecasts point to continued strong Korean cosmetics export growth centered on the US and Europe through 2026, which represents a favorable external backdrop for a company with export exposure like Coreana.

However, the benefits of this export boom have so far concentrated in large ODM manufacturers and major brand companies, so whether Coreana actually participates in this trend will need to be confirmed through upcoming quarterly results.

The founding family maintained its combined special-related-party stake at 16.08% into 2026 with no major change to management control, suggesting any near-term strategy shift is more likely to come from cost efficiency and channel restructuring within the existing business than a major strategic pivot.

No company-issued sales or earnings guidance, nor any new capacity expansion or major investment plan, was identified within the scope of this research.

08

Bull factors

Low Leverage and a Stable Capital Structure

The debt-to-equity ratio stood at 18.2% at end-2025, the lowest level across 2022-2025, with total equity of KRW 83.7 billion well above total liabilities of KRW 15.2 billion. Even amid ongoing losses, financial leverage itself has stayed low, limiting near-term funding pressure. This structural buffer means poor earnings have not translated directly into acute financial risk.

Revenue Rising for Three Straight Quarters

Revenue has risen for three consecutive quarters, from a low of KRW 16.8 billion in Q3 2025 to KRW 17.3 billion in Q4 2025, KRW 18.6 billion in Q1 2026, and KRW 18.9 billion in Q2 2026. This rebound in quarterly revenue could be read as a sign that the top line has passed its trough, at least in terms of scale. Whether this translates into a profitability recovery, however, has not yet been confirmed.

A Tailwind from the K-Beauty Export Boom

Korean cosmetics exports rose 31.1% year over year from January to September 2026, with US-bound exports up 40.6%, giving the broader industry a favorable external backdrop. As a company with export and OEM exposure, Coreana could see this favorable environment reflected in future results.

So far, however, the benefits of this growth have concentrated in large ODM manufacturers and major brand companies.

Bear factors

Five Straight Quarters of Operating Losses

The company has posted operating losses for five consecutive quarters from Q2 2025 through Q2 2026, with the Q2 2026 loss of KRW 1.06 billion widening again from KRW 465 million in the prior quarter.

Even as revenue has grown, operating results have failed to return to profit, suggesting no clear improvement in the cost and expense structure. Net income attributable to controlling shareholders has also been volatile, turning profitable in Q1 2026 before reverting to a net loss in Q2 2026.

Two Straight Years of Revenue Decline Since the 2023 Peak

Annual revenue peaked at KRW 86 billion in 2023, then fell for two straight years to KRW 82.5 billion in 2024 and KRW 73.7 billion in 2025, with the 2025 decline rate of 10.6% exceeding the prior year's 4.2% drop.

The company cited rising raw material costs, stalled exports to greater China, and a shrinking door-to-door sales channel as the main causes. With the revenue base itself clearly contracting, restoring profitability through cost cuts alone looks difficult.

Stalled China Sales and Shifting Distribution Structure

Coreana stated that exports have stalled due to prolonged weakness in greater China and declining K-beauty competitiveness, a trend that coincides with a broader pattern of Korean companies in China considering scaling back or withdrawing operations.

At the same time, the traditionally significant door-to-door sales channel has been shrinking, compounding revenue declines. Both factors appear structural in nature and are unlikely to be resolved quickly.

10

Risk factors

Cost and Currency Risk

The company has stated that cost pressure from rising raw material prices has become structurally entrenched, meaning input cost swings directly affect gross margin. Currency fluctuations could also affect overseas sales, including in China, and raw material sourcing costs. If cost pressure persists, operating profit recovery could lag even if revenue continues to grow.

Greater China Exposure Risk

Coreana operates a China-based subsidiary and has exposure to exports and sales in greater China, so local economic weakness and declining K-beauty competitiveness there directly affect its results.

A survey by the Korea Institute for Industrial Economics & Trade also found that a substantial share of Korean companies operating in China are considering scaling back or withdrawing, pointing to a broader structural challenge across the industry. The uncertain timing of any recovery in greater China demand is a variable that warrants continued monitoring.

Distribution Channel and Competitive Risk

The traditionally significant door-to-door sales channel is shrinking, requiring distribution restructuring, and the time and cost involved in that transition could add further strain.

Within the OEM/ODM industry, competitive pressure persists between large players such as Kolmar Korea and Cosmax and newer independent brands, squeezing the space available to a mid-sized player. If this structural competitive intensification continues, revenue recovery could slow.

12

Overall view

Coreana has moved through a trough phase, with revenue declining for two straight years since the 2023 peak and both operating and net results swinging to losses in 2025.

Operating losses have continued for five consecutive quarters through Q2 2026, yet revenue has risen for three straight quarters over the same period, leaving a timing gap between the top line and the bottom line.

On the financial structure side, the debt-to-equity ratio has stayed low, meaning the key variable to watch is less near-term financial risk than whether profitability actually recovers.

At the industry level, Korea's cosmetics exports have shown strong growth centered on the US and Europe through 2026, but those benefits have so far concentrated in large OEM/ODM manufacturers and major brand companies rather than showing up clearly in Coreana's results.

The founding family appears to be maintaining its ownership stake and managing within the existing business structure rather than pursuing a major strategic shift.

Going forward, upcoming quarterly and annual reports will be important for tracking whether the revenue rebound translates into a profitability improvement and whether structural pressures tied to greater China and distribution channels ease.
