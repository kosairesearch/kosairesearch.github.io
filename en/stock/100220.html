Report overview

Textbook cycle lifts profits, but two loss quarters follow

Visang Education rebounded on textbook replacements under the 2022 revised curriculum and cost efficiency, posting 2025 revenue of KRW 268.8 billion and operating profit of KRW 21.6 billion, yet it recorded operating losses in both Q2 and Q3 2026.

02

Business structure

Visang Education's business spans publishing, T-learning, and other operations such as early-childhood teaching aids and facilities.

Publishing covers paper-based textbooks for elementary, middle and high school, AI digital textbooks, the teaching-support platform Bibsam, and supplementary workbooks such as Hanggeut and Oto.

According to the company, publishing revenue rose to KRW 179.9 billion in 2025 from KRW 147.7 billion in 2024 and accounted for 66.9% of consolidated revenue. Publishing operating profit also increased to KRW 29.1 billion in 2025 from KRW 21.4 billion.

T-learning, which covers online and smart learning services, saw revenue fall to KRW 81.6 billion in 2025 from KRW 93.6 billion in 2024, while its operating loss narrowed to KRW 3.9 billion from KRW 6.3 billion. The company said cost efficiency in its smart learning product OnlyOne improved profit and loss.

EdTech remains loss-making, so textbooks and workbooks remain the core of earnings. A news report said 46.6% of consolidated revenue in Q1 2026, about KRW 48.5 billion, came from a single external customer, a textbook association.

The education sector shares a challenge from a shrinking school-age population, and the company's heavier textbook weighting has produced comparatively better results than many peers.

The company said development of AI digital textbooks for middle school math, English and informatics and for high school math and English has been completed.

04

Earnings analysis

Annual revenue went from KRW 253 billion in 2022 to KRW 252.4 billion in 2023 (down 0.3% year on year) and KRW 246.5 billion in 2024 (down 2.3%), before rising to KRW 268.8 billion in 2025 (up 9.1%). Among 2022 to 2025, 2025 was the largest year and 2024 the smallest.

Operating profit moved from KRW 3.55 billion in 2022 to an operating loss of KRW 5.81 billion in 2023, a swing into loss, then returned to operating profit of KRW 10.2 billion in 2024 and reached KRW 21.6 billion in 2025, up 111.5%. Operating margin was 1.4% in 2022, -2.3% in 2023, 4.1% in 2024 and 8.0% in 2025.

Net income attributable to controlling shareholders was a loss of KRW 1.02 billion in 2022, a loss of KRW 12.4 billion in 2023 (losses widened), a loss of KRW 2.9 billion in 2024 (losses narrowed), and a profit of KRW 21.3 billion in 2025, a swing to profit.

Net income including non-controlling interests was KRW 21.3 billion in 2025. A news report said other income of KRW 9.3 billion in 2025 included proceeds from the partial sale of the former headquarters building in Guro-gu, Seoul, so part of the bottom line rested on non-operating items.

The company attributed the operating profit increase to higher gross profit combined with cost and SG&A efficiency. Operating cash flow was KRW 2 billion in 2022, -KRW 4.3 billion in 2023, KRW 22.2 billion in 2024 and KRW 25.5 billion in 2025.

Total equity at end-2025 was KRW 199.8 billion, all of it attributable to controlling shareholders, against total liabilities of KRW 199.7 billion. The debt ratio was 56.8% in 2022, 100.1% in 2023, 118.5% in 2024 and 100.0% in 2025.

Quarterly, an operating loss of KRW 14.4 billion in Q3 2025 (controlling net loss of KRW 14.1 billion) turned into operating profit of KRW 26.4 billion in Q4 2025.

Q1 2026 revenue was KRW 104.2 billion (up 10.4% from the prior quarter), operating profit KRW 35 billion (up 32.8%) and controlling net income KRW 25.8 billion (up 17.8%), and a news report described the operating profit as the highest in three years.

Q2 2026 revenue fell to KRW 52.6 billion (down 49.6% from the prior quarter), with an operating loss of KRW 860 million. Q3 2026 revenue was KRW 54.8 billion (up 4.2% from the prior quarter and up 27.6% year on year), with an operating loss of KRW 5.66 billion, narrower than a year earlier.

Quarterly revenue appears concentrated in the first quarter, when textbook deliveries cluster, which makes quarterly results volatile. Controlling net income for the last four quarters, from Q4 2025 to Q3 2026, totaled KRW 43.4 billion.

05

Industry analysis

The education market faces a structural drag from a shrinking school-age population. According to Ministry of Education projections, the number of elementary, middle and high school students will fall from 5,015,310 in 2025 to 4,836,890 in 2026, and the total is projected to drop below five million.

Major education companies posted broadly weak results in Q1 2026, and digital learning-material and visiting-tutor businesses were hit by membership declines and changes in the timing of academy enrollment. Visang Education posted results that diverged from this trend thanks to its textbook-centred structure.

The 2022 revised curriculum expanded to grades 5 and 6 of elementary school and grade 2 of middle and high school in 2026, creating replacement demand, and analysts expect the cycle to continue through grade 3 of middle and high school in 2027.

A supply contract for elementary integrated-subject state-authored textbooks, placed through the Public Procurement Service, runs from 2026 to 2028 with a total value of KRW 59.6 billion, signed in November 2025.

The pace of the AI digital textbook market depends on government adoption schedules, and one competitor withdrew from that business and recorded restructuring costs. According to a Thinkpool report, Visang Education had the highest operating margin among peers in Q1 2026.

On the other side, analysts describe the textbook market itself as stagnant, and R&D spending fell to 9.6% of revenue in Q1 2026 from 21.7% in the prior-year period.

06

Outlook

The company's identifiable schedule centres on the textbook replacement cycle and the state-authored textbook supply contract. Textbook changes for grade 3 of middle and high school in 2027 mean that publishing revenue trends will be a key variable for that year's results.

The supply contract for 2026 to 2028, worth KRW 59.6 billion in total, adds visibility to publishing revenue over its term. The AI digital textbook development for major middle and high school subjects has been completed, so government adoption timing and classroom adoption scale are the next checkpoints.

By contrast, T-learning revenue was KRW 21 billion in Q1 2026, down 7.7% year on year, and its operating loss structure continues. How quickly the digital and subscription businesses take hold will determine the growth axis after textbooks.

Because Q2 2026 revenue fell 49.6% from the prior quarter, the timing of textbook deliveries and revenue recognition will need to be tracked in quarterly results.

On the financing side, a May 2026 news report said about KRW 129.5 billion of borrowings had maturities concentrated at the end of May, so the spread of maturities and repayment capacity are the next items to watch. A news report also said cash and cash equivalents rose to KRW 63.5 billion at the end of Q1 2026.

08

Bull factors

Extended textbook replacement cycle

Replacement demand under the 2022 revised curriculum extended to grades 5 and 6 of elementary school and grade 2 of middle and high school in 2026, and analysts expect it to continue through grade 3 of middle and high school in 2027.

The state-authored textbook contract, worth KRW 59.6 billion, runs from 2026 to 2028 and adds visibility. Publishing operating profit rose to KRW 29.1 billion in 2025 from KRW 21.4 billion in 2024.

Recovery in operating margin

Operating margin improved from 1.4% in 2022 and -2.3% in 2023 to 4.1% in 2024 and 8.0% in 2025. A news report put the Q1 2026 operating margin at 33.6%, the highest quarterly level in three years. The company attributed the gains to higher gross profit combined with efficiency in costs and SG&A.

Cash generation and lower borrowings

Operating cash flow rose to KRW 22.2 billion in 2024 and KRW 25.5 billion in 2025. A news report said Q1 2026 operating cash flow was about KRW 40.4 billion, up 123% year on year, and that borrowings fell by KRW 10 billion from the prior year-end. The debt ratio fell to 100.0% at end-2025 from 118.5% in 2024.

09

Bear factors

Two consecutive quarters of operating losses

In Q2 2026, revenue shrank to about half of the prior quarter's level, and the company swung to an operating loss of KRW 860 million. Q3 2026 extended the losses with an operating loss of KRW 5.66 billion, narrower than a year earlier but still a loss.

Controlling net income also moved from KRW 880 million in Q2 to a controlling net loss of KRW 5.06 billion in Q3.

Textbook dependence and peak-cycle concerns

Publishing accounted for 66.9% of consolidated revenue in 2025, and 46.6% of Q1 2026 revenue came from a single external customer. An education-industry official said that as the textbook boost passes its peak, the speed at which digital and subscription businesses take hold will be decisive. If textbook revenue stalls, the other pillars supporting the profit recovery remain thin.

T-learning losses and a shrinking school-age population

The T-learning business remained loss-making in 2025 with an operating loss of KRW 3.9 billion, and its Q1 2026 revenue fell 7.7% year on year to KRW 21 billion. The school-age population is projected to fall to 4,836,890 students in 2026, a structural drag on demand.

Across the sector, Q1 2026 results were weak, and digital learning-material companies continued to lose members.

Risk factors

Policy and regulatory risk

Slower-than-expected activation of the AI digital textbook market and policy uncertainty around entry into public education are cited as obstacles. Analysts describe the textbook market itself as stagnant, so changes in regulation or adoption rules could affect revenue directly. The timing of textbook deliveries and adoption outcomes also adds volatility to quarterly results.

Financial and liquidity risk

A May 2026 news report said about KRW 129.5 billion of borrowings had maturities concentrated at the end of May. The debt ratio was 100.0% at end-2025, and total liabilities were close to total equity.

Dividends were suspended for the fiscal years 2023 and 2024, so cash distribution policy needs to be tracked alongside earnings.

Earnings quality and one-off items

Net income of KRW 21.3 billion in 2025 included other income of KRW 9.3 billion, which the company said contained proceeds from the partial sale of its former headquarters. Profits are concentrated in the first quarter, when textbook deliveries cluster, which adds quarter-to-quarter volatility.

If operating earnings outside textbooks do not recover, questions about the durability of the earnings recovery could grow.

12

Overall view

Visang Education returned to profit in 2024 after an operating loss in 2023, and reached operating profit of KRW 21.6 billion in 2025, up 111.5%, on textbook replacements under the 2022 revised curriculum and cost efficiency.

Q1 2026 operating profit of KRW 35 billion was described in news reports as the highest quarterly level in three years, but Q2 and Q3 2026 each recorded operating losses.

The bullish points are that the textbook replacement cycle may extend into grade 3 of middle and high school in 2027, and that the state-authored textbook contract and completed AI digital textbook development add revenue visibility.

The bearish points are high textbook dependence, with publishing at 66.9% of 2025 revenue and 46.6% of Q1 2026 revenue from a single customer, which makes results sensitive to delivery timing and policy. T-learning remains loss-making, and the school-age population is shrinking.

The 2025 bottom line included other income such as the sale of part of the former headquarters, so the durability of the recovery needs confirmation on an operating basis.

Financially, operating cash flow rose to KRW 25.5 billion in 2025 and borrowings declined, but the concentration of debt maturities and the 100.0% debt ratio remain items to monitor. The next checkpoints are the full-year operating margin in the 2026 annual report and the textbook revenue trend in Q1 2027.
