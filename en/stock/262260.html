Apro: Five Straight Loss-Making Quarters Amid Volatile Sales

Apro has posted operating losses in five straight quarters from the third quarter of 2025 through the third quarter of 2026, and the key issues for its financial structure are demand swings in its core battery equipment business and the subsidiary funding absorbed by its GaN growth project.

  2. 2

  3. 3

02

Business structure

Apro develops and manufactures activation equipment for lithium-ion secondary batteries. Its main products are charge-discharge equipment, and high-temperature pressurized charge-discharge systems are reported as its core high-performance line.

The company has grown into a major supplier to LG Energy Solution, so the pace of capital spending by large battery cell makers is the starting point for its orders and sales.

Its growth project is GaN (gallium nitride) power semiconductors, and it has also outlined an expansion into EV fast chargers and diagnostic equipment for used batteries. For the GaN business, reports describe a production facility built in Gumi, North Gyeongsang, and the start of sales of 8-inch GaN epiwafers.

A summary of the first nine months of 2025 said the company was in supply discussions with several customers. Because consolidated results include the profit and loss of its core subsidiary and other affiliates, the core equipment business and the new business need to be viewed separately.

The equipment business is sensitive to battery investment cycles, while the new business has not yet translated into visible revenue.

04

Earnings analysis

Annual revenue rose from KRW 79.4 billion in 2022 to KRW 236 billion in 2023, up 197.2% year on year, making 2023 the largest year of the 2022–2025 span. It then fell two years in a row, to KRW 200.2 billion in 2024 (down 15.2%) and KRW 147.1 billion in 2025 (down 26.5%).

Operating results moved from an operating loss of KRW 2.51 billion in 2022 to operating profit of KRW 15.5 billion in 2023, a swing back to profit, before falling to KRW 3.36 billion in 2024 (down 78.3%) and turning into an operating loss of KRW 5.83 billion in 2025.

Net income attributable to controlling shareholders went from KRW 12.5 billion in 2023 to KRW 6.54 billion in 2024 (down 47.7%) and then to a net loss of KRW 5.43 billion in 2025.

Net income including non-controlling interests was a net loss of KRW 10.8 billion in 2025, a larger loss than the figure attributable to controlling shareholders. The operating margin fell from 6.6% in 2023 to 1.7% in 2024 and -4.0% in 2025.

According to a DigitalToday report, the company attributed the 2025 swing to higher selling, general and administrative expenses and higher R&D expenses at subsidiaries.

Quarterly revenue was KRW 31 billion in the third quarter of 2025, KRW 14.1 billion in the fourth quarter (down 54.5% from the prior quarter), KRW 43.9 billion in the first quarter of 2026 (up 211.0%), KRW 16.1 billion in the second quarter (down 63.4%), and KRW 24.1 billion in the third quarter (up 50.2% from the prior quarter but down 22.2% year on year).

Operating losses were KRW 2.44 billion, KRW 1.97 billion, and KRW 973 million in the third and fourth quarters of 2025 and the first quarter of 2026, widened to KRW 5.62 billion in the second quarter of 2026, and reached KRW 4.84 billion in the third quarter.

Net income attributable to controlling shareholders turned positive at KRW 996 million in the first quarter of 2026 but then recorded losses of KRW 6.9 billion and KRW 8.78 billion in the following two quarters.

The fact that operating profit did not turn positive even in the quarter when revenue rebounded remains a structural weakness in the earnings base.

05

Industry analysis

Apro's battery equipment market is tied directly to capital spending by battery cell makers. Reporting on a listed cathode-materials company also cited slowing growth in North American electric vehicle demand as a drag on results.

At the same time, reports linked demand for energy storage systems to the expansion of AI data center construction, so the mix of demand is shifting from EV-centered to ESS-centered. The timing and scale of equipment orders may move with this shift.

Compared with other listed electrical equipment companies, Apro is concentrated in one niche of secondary battery activation equipment, while its GaN power semiconductor project is linked to the semiconductor cycle.

Whether equipment orders recover and whether the GaN business moves into customer supply are the main markers of its position against the industry. The rise in selling and R&D costs cited for 2025 is a cost-structure issue that remains separate from any industry recovery.

Outlook

Based on confirmed facts, the starting point for the outlook is the financial structure in the first half of 2026. According to press reports, the company was profitable on a standalone basis in the first half of 2026 but posted a loss on a consolidated basis that reflects subsidiary losses.

Consolidated revenue fell from a year earlier, and consolidated retained earnings are reported to have turned negative by the end of the first half of 2026 after being positive at the end of 2025.

Reports said the core subsidiary had fallen into full capital impairment, and the parent has continued supporting its subsidiaries through capital injections, loans, and guarantees. The current ratio declined from 90.5% at the end of 2024 to 65.1% at the end of 2025 and 56.7% at the end of the first half of 2026.

The GaN business is reported to be at the stage of building a production facility in Gumi and starting sales of 8-inch GaN epiwafers, with its contribution to earnings described as not yet clear.

Criticism of repeated disclosure omissions has also been raised, which points to a need to review subsidiary management and disclosure processes.

Whether the first-quarter sales rebound will carry into later quarters remains uncertain, since third-quarter revenue rose from the prior quarter but was still below the year-earlier level.

The next checkpoints are the operating margin in the 2026 annual report and the movements in consolidated retained earnings and the current ratio.

Bull factors

A rebound quarter in sales

Quarterly sales jumped to KRW 43.9 billion in the first quarter of 2026, up 211.0% from the prior quarter. Third-quarter sales rose 50.2% from the second quarter to KRW 24.1 billion, but were still down 22.2% from the same quarter a year earlier, so the recovery remains limited.

Quarters with narrower losses

The operating loss narrowed from KRW 1.97 billion in the fourth quarter of 2025 to KRW 973 million in the first quarter of 2026. Net income attributable to controlling shareholders turned positive at KRW 996 million in the same first quarter, which shows that losses have narrowed in at least one period.

The GaN power semiconductor project

GaN power semiconductors are the growth project the company is pursuing. Reports say it has built a production facility in Gumi, North Gyeongsang, and started selling 8-inch GaN epiwafers. The project is seen as a possible second business axis that could offset the volatility of equipment sales.

Bear factors

Five straight quarters of operating losses

The company has posted operating losses in five straight quarters, from the third quarter of 2025 through the third quarter of 2026. The operating loss widened to KRW 5.62 billion in the second quarter of 2026 and was KRW 4.84 billion in the third quarter. The net loss attributable to controlling shareholders totaled KRW 16.1 billion over the last four quarters.

Subsidiary capital impairment and financial strain

Reports say the core subsidiary has fallen into full capital impairment. Consolidated retained earnings turned negative by the end of the first half of 2026, and the current ratio fell to 56.7% by the same point. The debt-to-equity ratio was 434.2% at the end of 2025, well above 134.1% in 2022.

Delayed contribution from the new business and slower end demand

Reports say the new business has not yet translated into visible earnings contribution. Growth in EV-centered battery demand, particularly in North America, has been described as slowing. If equipment orders decline, sales volatility in the core business could widen again.

Risk factors

Financial liquidity risk

Reports say the parent has continued to support subsidiaries through capital injections, loans, and guarantees. If that support continues, the parent's financial burden accumulates, and the current ratio has been on a downward path.

Because subsidiary losses flow into consolidated results, the scale of funding directly affects earnings volatility.

Disclosure management risk

Reporting has pointed to repeated omissions in subsidiary disclosures. If subsidiary management and disclosure systems fail to keep pace while the new business expands, it becomes harder to assess the company's financial condition. Whether disclosure processes improve can be checked through the periodic reports the company files.

Customer concentration and battery investment cycle risk

Apro is known as a major supplier to LG Energy Solution, so the pace of capital spending by a large customer is reflected heavily in its sales. If slower North American EV demand delays battery makers' capacity expansion, equipment orders could also be affected. Because orders can be lumpy from quarter to quarter, the uncertainty around sales forecasts is high.

Overall view

Apro's reported results show revenue peaking in 2023, two straight years of declines in 2024 and 2025, and a swing to an operating loss in 2025.

The company has posted operating losses in five straight quarters from the third quarter of 2025 through the third quarter of 2026, and the net loss attributable to controlling shareholders totaled KRW 16.1 billion over the last four quarters.

The first quarter of 2026 brought KRW 43.9 billion in sales and a return to positive attributable net income, but losses widened again afterward. On the financial side, the debt-to-equity ratio of 434.2%, subsidiary capital impairment, and the falling current ratio are the confirmed concerns.

The GaN power semiconductor business is reported to be at the stage of building production facilities and starting sales, but its contribution to earnings is not yet clear. On the industry side, slowing EV demand alongside rising ESS demand makes the direction of equipment orders a key variable.
