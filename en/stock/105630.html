02

Business structure

Hansae is an OEM and ODM apparel exporter that manufactures and ships garments for U.S. retailers and global brands.

For the first nine months of 2026, the apparel OEM and ODM segment reported sales of KRW 1.48 trillion and operating profit of KRW 505 million, while the fabric business posted sales of KRW 292.4 billion and an operating loss of KRW 40 million, according to a November 13, 2026 report based on Financial Supervisory Service filings.

Segment sales include intra-group transactions, so they cannot be simply added to consolidated sales. U.S. customers accounted for 95.8% of 2024 sales, according to a securities report from April 2025, which points to very high concentration in the U.S. market.

Production is spread across Vietnam, Nicaragua, Indonesia, Guatemala and Haiti, and the 2024 production split was 39.2% Vietnam, 30.5% Nicaragua, 17.7% Indonesia and 9.2% Guatemala. Reporting says the Vietnam share of production fell from 49% in 2021 to 39% in 2024.

A 2026 report says Vietnam, counting company-owned plants and outsourced partner factories together, accounts for about 55% of total sales and produces roughly 100 million garments a year. In fabrics, a dyeing plant in Vietnam that began operating in December 2024 extends vertical integration.

A company official said automation has cut the workforce per regular knit production line from about 55 to about 45. Key customers reported in 2026 include Target, Old Navy, Gap and Walmart.

04

Earnings analysis

On an annual basis, sales went from KRW 2.2 trillion in 2022 to KRW 1.71 trillion in 2023, down 22.5%, which was the smallest year in the period, and then rose to KRW 1.8 trillion in 2024 and KRW 1.94 trillion in 2025, up 5.2% and 8.0% respectively.

Operating profit went from KRW 179.6 billion in 2022 to KRW 168.2 billion in 2023, KRW 142.2 billion in 2024 and KRW 83.4 billion in 2025, a decline for three consecutive years starting in 2023.

The operating margin moved from 8.1% in 2022 and 9.8% in 2023 down to 7.9% in 2024 and 4.3% in 2025, so margins fell sharply in 2025 even as sales grew 8.0%.

Net income attributable to controlling shareholders peaked at KRW 112 billion in 2023 and then fell to KRW 58 billion in 2024 and KRW 57.3 billion in 2025, a two-year decline.

Net income including non-controlling interests was KRW 58.4 billion in 2024 and KRW 57.3 billion in 2025, a pattern similar to attributable profit. The debt ratio fell from 130.7% in 2022 to 92.2% in 2023, then rose to 100.7% in 2024 and 109.0% in 2025.

Operating cash flow dropped from KRW 196.7 billion in 2022 to KRW 56.3 billion in 2024 before recovering to KRW 85.4 billion in 2025.

Quarterly results show sales of KRW 543.4 billion and operating profit of KRW 37 billion in the third quarter of 2025, followed by a fourth-quarter operating profit of KRW 13.8 billion, down 62.6% from the prior quarter.

In the first quarter of 2026, operating profit was KRW 10.5 billion and the company swung to a controlling-shareholder net loss of KRW 3.74 billion. In the second quarter, operating profit rose to KRW 36.1 billion and controlling-shareholder net income returned to a profit of KRW 13.1 billion.

The third-quarter drop reflects cost of sales of KRW 526.7 billion, up 14.0% year on year, selling and administrative expenses of KRW 61.8 billion, up 38.7%, and bad-debt charges of about KRW 17.4 billion versus about KRW 0.7 billion a year earlier, according to a November 13, 2026 report. Controlling-shareholder net income for the last four quarters totals KRW 16.1 billion.

05

Industry analysis

Apparel OEM demand is sensitive to inventory adjustments and order volumes at U.S. retailers and brands. Industry reporting explains that when inventories fall, new orders shrink, while a recovery in orders after inventory adjustments lifts production and earnings.

On tariffs, the U.S. reciprocal tariff rates announced in April 2025 cited 46% for Vietnam, 37% for Bangladesh and 32% for Indonesia, with Cambodia at 49%, the highest level. These figures reflect the announcement at the time, and any later changes need separate confirmation.

To reduce reliance on Vietnam, Hansae is using Nicaragua and Guatemala, and a company official said it would actively use factories in El Salvador and Guatemala, which carry lower tariffs, according to 2025 reporting.

On currency, a securities report from October 2026 cited a sharp shift toward a stronger won as a factor lowering third-quarter estimates, alongside raw-material cost pressure linked to the war.

For comparison, reporting for 2024 put Hwaseung Enterprise's Vietnam production share at 47% and Youngone's at 26%, so Hansae's 39% sits in the middle of the peer range.

Outlook

No quantitative company guidance was confirmed, so this outlook relies on facts from filings and reporting. A November 13, 2026 report identifies cost and selling-expense control, and whether bad-debt charges ease, as the main variables for profit recovery.

With the allowance for doubtful receivables rising from about KRW 11.9 billion to about KRW 32.9 billion, how quickly impaired receivables are resolved will directly affect fourth-quarter earnings.

In an October 2026 report, SK Securities lowered its third-quarter sales estimate to KRW 571.4 billion and its operating profit estimate to KRW 21.4 billion, a 4% margin. Reported third-quarter operating profit of KRW 247 million fell well short of that estimate, and the same report also cut fourth-quarter estimates.

On the production side, expanding Central American sites and automation are changing the cost structure, and a company official has described plans to use Central American production more strategically. Whether this translates into margin improvement will show up in reported operating margins.

The next periodic report, the 2026 annual report, is the key checkpoint for the full-year operating margin and the bad-debt allowance balance. The first-quarter 2027 quarterly report will then allow comparison with the KRW 10.5 billion operating profit posted in the first quarter of 2026.

08

Bull factors

Top-line growth continues

Cumulative sales for the first nine months of 2026 reached KRW 1.62 trillion, up 8.9% year on year, according to reporting based on Financial Supervisory Service filings. Sales have risen for three consecutive quarters since the fourth quarter of 2025.

Continued top-line growth is seen by some as a starting point for profit recovery, but whether it reaches earnings depends on cost control.

Second-quarter swing back to profit

Second-quarter 2026 operating profit reached KRW 36.1 billion, up 245.4% from the prior quarter, and controlling-shareholder net income turned to a profit of KRW 13.1 billion. The swing came one quarter after a controlling-shareholder net loss of KRW 3.74 billion.

Controlling-shareholder net income over the last four quarters totals KRW 16.1 billion, which shows that earnings capacity has not fully disappeared despite large quarterly swings.

Diversified production and automation

Vietnam's share of production fell from 49% in 2021 to 39% in 2024, while Central American sites such as Nicaragua and Guatemala are gaining weight. Reporting says these sites sit in lower-tariff countries, which helps manage tariff risk.

Automation has also reduced the workforce per regular knit line from about 55 to about 45, which supports the cost base. Whether this production restructuring feeds into margin recovery is the next thing to watch.

Bear factors

Sharp drop in third-quarter profitability

Third-quarter 2026 operating profit was KRW 247 million, down 99.3% year on year, and net income fell 98.2%. Cost of sales rose 14.0% and selling and administrative expenses rose 38.7%, both faster than sales growth.

The 2025 operating margin of 4.3% is less than half the 8.1% recorded in 2022, so sales growth is not converting into profit.

Bad-debt and financial pressure

Third-quarter 2026 bad-debt charges were about KRW 17.4 billion, compared with about KRW 0.7 billion a year earlier. The allowance for doubtful receivables rose from about KRW 11.9 billion at the end of 2025 to about KRW 32.9 billion at the end of September 2026.

The debt ratio climbed from 92.2% in 2023 to 109.0% in 2025, and total liabilities were KRW 800.9 billion at the end of 2025. If more receivables become hard to collect, both earnings and financial flexibility could come under pressure.

Simultaneous exposure to tariffs and cost pressure

U.S. customers accounted for 95.8% of 2024 sales, leaving the company directly exposed to changes in U.S. trade policy. The reciprocal tariff rate for Vietnam was cited at 46% in the April 2025 announcement.

An October 2026 SK Securities report cited a sharp won appreciation and war-related raw-material costs as reasons for lowering estimates. When cost, currency and tariffs act at the same time, the path to margin recovery becomes less certain.

Risk factors

Trade and tariffs

With 95.8% of 2024 sales going to the United States and Vietnam still accounting for a large share of production, changes in U.S. tariff policy directly affect the company. Reciprocal rates cited in April 2025 were 46% for Vietnam and 49% for Cambodia.

Expanding Central American sites reduces this exposure, but tariff levels and policy direction there also need separate confirmation.

Currency and raw-material costs

An October 2026 SK Securities report cited sharp won appreciation and war-related raw-material costs as factors lowering third-quarter estimates. Currency movements affect both the won value of overseas sales and the cost base of production.

Because adjusting delivery prices and production plans takes time, fast currency moves widen the gap between forecasts and results.

Customer credit and balance sheet

The allowance for doubtful receivables rose from about KRW 11.9 billion at the end of 2025 to about KRW 32.9 billion at the end of September 2026. The debt ratio stood at 109.0% at the end of 2025, above 92.2% in 2023. Operating cash flow was KRW 85.4 billion in 2025, well below KRW 196.7 billion in 2022. If customer payments slow for longer, cash flow and financial flexibility could weaken together.

Overall view

Hansae kept growing sales through the third quarter of 2026, but third-quarter operating profit was KRW 247 million, down 99.3% year on year. Rising cost of sales and selling expenses combined with a sharp increase in bad-debt charges.

On an annual basis, the operating margin fell from 8.1% in 2022 to 4.3% in 2025, and operating profit has declined for three consecutive years since 2023. Operating profit recovered to KRW 36.1 billion in the second quarter of 2026, but quarterly swings remain large.

Factors on the positive side include continued sales growth, production spread into Central America beyond Vietnam, and workforce efficiency from automation.

Factors on the negative side include a rising allowance for doubtful receivables, concentration in U.S. sales and tariff exposure, and the burden of a stronger won and raw-material costs. The debt ratio stood at 109.0% at the end of 2025, above 92.2% in 2023.

The next checkpoints are the full-year operating margin and allowance balance in the 2026 annual report, and the change in earnings capacity in the first-quarter 2027 report.
