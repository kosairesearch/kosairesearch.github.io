Bluecom: Rental Income Turns Profit, Core Business Gap Remains

Bluecom swung to an operating profit in 2025 on rental income after its wireless earphone business ended, and whether new businesses materialize is the next key test.

02

Business structure

Bluecom was founded in 1990 and listed on KOSDAQ in 2011 as a Bluetooth headset maker. It supplied LG Electronics' TONE models on an ODM basis from 2006 and has supplied the fully wireless TONE FREE earphones since 2020.

Production is handled by its Vietnamese subsidiary Bluecom Vina, which is reported to make its own components in a vertically integrated structure. However, the wireless earphone contract with LG Electronics ended in December 2025, removing the base of its manufacturing revenue.

The business has since centered on real estate leasing, and according to coverage of the March 2026 annual shareholder meeting, the leasing segment accounts for most of revenue.

Its subsidiary BLUECOM VINA resolved to dispose of land and buildings worth KRW 20.665 billion to improve efficiency and its financial structure.

The lithium-ion battery lead-tab business that the company pursued after relocating its factory from Songdo, Incheon to Cheongna was withdrawn without reaching mass production. The company added renewable energy power generation to its articles of incorporation but has put that plan on hold, citing funding and timing. The largest shareholder and related parties hold 47.62%, with the latest change recorded on July 22, 2024.

04

Earnings analysis

Revenue on a disclosed basis fell from KRW 58.6 billion in 2022 to KRW 32.8 billion in 2023 (down 44.1% year on year), KRW 18.3 billion in 2024 (down 44.1%), and KRW 5.81 billion in 2025 (down 68.3%).

It declined for three consecutive years from 2023 through 2025, with 2022 the largest year and 2025 the smallest in the 2022 to 2025 period.

Operating results were an operating loss of KRW 1.33 billion in 2022, an operating loss of KRW 7.71 billion in 2023 (losses widened), and an operating loss of KRW 2.8 billion in 2024 (losses narrowed). In 2025 the company posted an operating profit of KRW 648 million, a swing to profit.

Revenue fell because the wireless earphone business was discontinued, yet operating profit turned positive because rental income was recognized as revenue, which the company cited as the cause.

Parent-attributable net income rose to KRW 16.4 billion in 2025, up 589.0% year on year, and the company pointed to the sale of its headquarters building as a driver of the increase in net income. That figure includes a one-time gain from the building sale and should be separated when assessing underlying earnings.

The operating margin moved from -2.3% in 2022 and -23.5% in 2023 to -15.3% in 2024 and 11.2% in 2025. Operating cash flow went from KRW 3.9 billion in 2022, KRW 9.17 billion in 2023 and KRW 10.6 billion in 2024 to negative KRW 119 million in 2025.

The debt ratio went from 9.4% in 2022 and 10.8% in 2023 to 26.0% in 2024 before falling to 18.1% in 2025. Quarterly revenue bottomed at KRW 642 million in the fourth quarter of 2025 (down 61.9% from the prior quarter) and then rose for three consecutive quarters through the third quarter of 2026.

Third-quarter 2026 operating profit was KRW 690 million, up 259.5% year on year, while parent-attributable net income of KRW 2.63 billion fell 26.6% year on year in the same quarter.

Fourth-quarter 2025 operating profit of KRW 1.54 billion exceeded revenue of KRW 642 million, so whether one-off items drove that quarter should be checked against the quarterly filings.

05

Industry analysis

Bluecom's former core products, Bluetooth headsets and wireless earphones, belong to an audio device market that grew with the spread of smart devices. An earlier securities company field-trip report described Bluecom as a leader in the neckband-type Bluetooth headset market.

However, the company found it hard to protect manufacturing margins as unit prices fell quickly. At the March 27, 2026 annual shareholder meeting, CEO Kim Tae-jin said unit price declines had been faster than expected.

At the same meeting he explained that the business was loss-making from the moment mass production began, after vendor registration and line setup. One shareholder described the market as saturated and a unit-price battle and questioned the business judgment.

Bluecom's main customer was LG Electronics, and the wireless earphone contract with it ended in December 2025. A market data service counted ODM supply as 90.5% of Bluecom's sales during its wireless earphone business.

Compared with larger components makers in the same sector, Bluecom's key difference is that it lost its in-house production lines and its manufacturing scale shrank.

06

Outlook

Beyond leasing, the businesses Bluecom has discussed are M&A and renewable energy. At the March 2026 shareholder meeting, CEO Kim Tae-jin said the company was working with consultants and screening M&A targets.

He said the company was looking at a deal size of around KRW 20 billion but that it could change depending on its own situation and available targets. The renewable energy business was added to the articles of incorporation but has been postponed due to funding and timing.

The company spent KRW 59.8 billion on land and factory construction in Cheongna, and borrowed KRW 16.0 billion from Chairman Kim Jong-gyu for this project. At an annual interest rate of 3.7%, annual interest payable to the chairman is reported at about KRW 592 million.

The company said share buybacks are more effective than dividends for supporting the share price. As of February 12, 2026, treasury shares stood at 9.99%, and the company said it plans to buy about KRW 4.0 billion more by August 2026.

The company explained that it must meet revenue requirements under exchange standards going forward. The market is watching whether Bluecom can find new businesses and restore its scale as a manufacturer, or whether the leasing-centered structure becomes entrenched.

08

Bull factors

Financial Capacity and Low Debt

Total equity at the end of 2025 was KRW 170.2 billion and total liabilities were KRW 30.7 billion. The debt ratio fell from 26.0% in 2024 to 18.1% in 2025. A thick capital base means the company has more financial room in any new business or M&A process.

Swing to Profit on Rental Base

Operating profit turned from a loss to KRW 648 million in 2025, with an operating margin of 11.2%. Revenue rose for three consecutive quarters starting in the first quarter of 2026. Third-quarter 2026 operating profit of KRW 690 million was up 259.5% year on year.

Share Buybacks Under Way

The company acquired about KRW 5.5 billion of treasury shares through trust contracts in two rounds from 2025 to February 2026. It said it plans to buy about KRW 4.0 billion more by August 2026. The company has said buybacks are more effective than dividends for supporting the share price.

09

Bear factors

Core Revenue Gap

Revenue fell from KRW 58.6 billion in 2022 to KRW 5.81 billion in 2025 after the wireless earphone business was discontinued. It declined for three consecutive years from 2023 to 2025. The 2025 turn to profit relied on rental income, so it is hard to read as a recovery of the manufacturing core.

New Business Uncertainty and Investment Burden

KRW 59.8 billion went into the Cheongna factory, but the lithium-ion lead-tab business was withdrawn without mass production. The renewable energy plan is on hold. Part of the Cheongna funding is a KRW 16.0 billion loan from the chairman carrying 3.7% interest.

Dividend Halt and Governance Concerns

No dividend has been paid since 2017, and a KRW 200 per share dividend proposal was voted down at the March 2026 annual meeting. Minority shareholders questioned executive pay of about KRW 1 billion. The auditor publicly flagged a procedural defect in agenda item 3.

10

Risk factors

Reliance on Rental Income and Earnings Durability

The 2025 profit and the increase in quarterly operating profit in 2026 depend heavily on rental income recognition. Lease terms and renewals determine how durable earnings are. Without one-off gains such as the building sale, the earnings base could look very different.

Listing Maintenance Requirements

The company said it must meet future revenue requirements under exchange standards. Commentary notes that a structure relying only on rental income has long-term limits. If new businesses fail to rebuild scale, the burden of maintaining the listing could grow.

Related-Party Funding and Governance

The largest shareholder and related parties hold 47.62%. The company carries interest on the KRW 16.0 billion borrowed from the chairman, and minority shareholders disagree with executive pay. The rejection of a shareholder dividend proposal showed how hard it is for minority holders to influence decisions.

Overall view

Bluecom is reshaping its revenue and earnings base around rental income after its wireless earphone business ended. On a disclosed basis, revenue fell from KRW 58.6 billion in 2022 to KRW 5.81 billion in 2025, and the 2025 operating profit of KRW 648 million reflects rental income recognition.

Quarterly revenue has risen for three consecutive quarters in 2026, and third-quarter operating profit was up 259.5% year on year. However, parent-attributable net income in the same quarter was KRW 2.63 billion, down 26.6% year on year, so operating performance and bottom-line results moved in different directions.

Strengths include total equity of KRW 170.2 billion, a low debt ratio, and ongoing share buybacks, while weaknesses include the core business gap, uncertainty over new businesses, and the dividend halt and governance concerns.

The weight of these factors will depend on whether new businesses start generating actual revenue. The next checkpoints are the annual operating margin in the 2026 annual business report and any disclosures on new businesses.
