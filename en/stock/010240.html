02

Business structure

Heungkuk Metaltech was founded in 1974 and has been listed on KOSDAQ since 2009. Its core business on a consolidated basis is making lower-body drive parts for construction machinery and hot-forged products.

Demand for these parts is sensitive to economic conditions, and domestic and overseas infrastructure investment policy largely determines volumes. The company set up HK JAPAN in Tokyo in 2018 as part of its push to widen overseas sales channels.

The forging unit draws on more than three decades of hot-forging know-how to supply construction machinery, industrial machinery and automotive parts. Even so, the forging unit accounted for only about 3% of sales as of 2019, so automotive parts play a supporting role in the overall revenue mix.

The cyclical exposure to construction is visible in 2019, when sales declined after the domestic construction market contracted from the second half of 2018.

At the March 2026 general shareholders' meeting, an amendment adding solar power generation to the business purpose was approved, broadening the scope of activities. The value-enhancement plan disclosed in March 2026 also includes higher overseas sales and better profitability at subsidiaries.

On a consolidated basis, 2025 sales were KRW 137 billion and operating profit was KRW 14.9 billion, which gives a sense of scale. The segment sales mix and customer composition are covered in the business section of periodic filings.

Industry analysis

Heungkuk Metaltech's end markets are construction machinery and infrastructure investment. Demand for construction machinery parts swings with economic phases and depends on the direction of infrastructure budgets.

Sales declined in 2019 after the domestic construction market contracted from the second half of 2018, which shows the cycle sensitivity. To gauge where the cycle stands in 2026, the most direct link to company earnings is the flow of domestic construction investment and public infrastructure budgets.

Automotive is still a small share for the company. The forging unit was about 3% of sales as of 2019, so changes in vehicle production have limited impact on the group.

On technology, the company has operated a corporate research institute since December 2019, and a 2020 report listed five dedicated R&D staff working on construction machinery and automotive forgings. Its position against peers is best framed along two axes: hot-forging experience and an overseas sales network.

Expanding overseas sales is part of the value-enhancement plan and reads as an attempt to reduce reliance on domestic construction equipment demand. Adding solar power generation to the business purpose at the March 2026 meeting can be read as an attempt to expand beyond construction machinery parts.

The company is classified under automotive, but its earnings track construction activity and infrastructure policy more closely.
