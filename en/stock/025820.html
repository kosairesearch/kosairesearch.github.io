02

Business structure

Leeku Industrial was established in 1971 and is a KOSPI-listed manufacturer of copper, brass, and phosphor bronze sheets and strips. Its core products are copper sheets and strips, brass sheets and strips, and phosphor bronze sheets and strips, alongside processing services and real estate leasing.

The company's annual production capacity is reported at around 60,000 tons, supplying more than 20% of domestic demand.

Its customer base spans automotive parts, semiconductor lead frames, electrical and electronic components, and construction materials, with connectors, terminals, and battery terminals among its core items. More recently it has expanded EV-related material facilities into busbars, motors, and heat sinks.

It is also addressing demand for ESS and switchgear products tied to the spread of AI data centers. South Korea's copper-processing industry is concentrated among a small number of players, and a 2021 media report cited Leeku Industrial as ranked second in production-based market share behind Poongsan. As of the end of 2025, the company had five affiliates in total, one listed and four unlisted.

04

Earnings analysis

Annual revenue rose from KRW 429.6 billion in 2022 to KRW 433.4 billion in 2023 (up 0.9% year-on-year), KRW 472.3 billion in 2024 (up 9.0%), and KRW 507.8 billion in 2025 (up 7.5%), marking three consecutive years of growth from 2023 through 2025.

Operating profit fell from KRW 13.7 billion in 2022 to KRW 10.2 billion in 2023 (down 25.4%), rebounded sharply to KRW 25.2 billion in 2024 (up 146.4%), then eased to KRW 24.4 billion in 2025 (down 3.0%), with 2024 the highest and 2023 the lowest among the 2022-2025 period.

Net profit attributable to owners followed a similar pattern, falling to KRW 656 million in 2023, rebounding to KRW 12.6 billion in 2024 (up 1,820.9%), then declining to KRW 10.9 billion in 2025 (down 13.4%), with 2024 again the highest and 2023 the lowest in the period.

On a quarterly basis, revenue bottomed at KRW 121.6 billion in Q4 2025 (down 7.4% from KRW 131.3 billion in Q3 2025, which itself rose 4.2% from KRW 126 billion in Q2 2025), then rose for two consecutive quarters to KRW 151.6 billion in Q1 2026 (up 24.7%) and KRW 190.8 billion in Q2 2026 (up 25.8% quarter-on-quarter and 51.4% year-on-year).

Operating profit jumped from KRW 9.2 billion in Q4 2025 to KRW 21.3 billion in Q1 2026 (up 131.1%), then held at an elevated KRW 20.3 billion in Q2 2026 (down 4.4% quarter-on-quarter but up 363.8% year-on-year).

Net profit attributable to owners swung to a loss of KRW 564 million in Q3 2025, turned profitable at KRW 1.69 billion in Q4 2025, surged to KRW 14.6 billion in Q1 2026 (up 762.8%), then fell to KRW 8.69 billion in Q2 2026 (down 40.5% quarter-on-quarter but up 55.2% year-on-year).

The sum of net profit attributable to owners over the last four quarters (Q3 2025 through Q2 2026) totaled KRW 24.4 billion. The operating margin slipped from 5.3% in 2024 to 4.8% in 2025, suggesting revenue growth did not fully outpace increases in cost of sales and SG&A.

Operating cash flow flipped from an inflow of KRW 26.5 billion in 2023 to an outflow of KRW 4.84 billion in 2025, reflecting a gap between reported profit improvement and cash generation.

05

Industry analysis

Copper is widely cited as facing structurally rising demand tied to the expansion of electric vehicles, AI data centers, and renewable power grids. Hana Securities projected a global refined copper supply deficit of roughly 550,000 tons in 2026 and set its average 2026 price forecast at USD 13,417 per ton.

However, 2026 copper price forecasts diverge widely among global investment banks depending on their supply-demand views, with Citigroup projecting an average of USD 10,500 per ton, Goldman Sachs seeing a 2026 peak of USD 11,500 per ton, and JPMorgan putting the average near USD 10,000 per ton.

Whether the United States introduces phased tariffs on refined copper is seen as a key variable for trade flows and the price gap between exchanges. Exchange inventory levels also influence price direction; combined stocks across the LME, COMEX, and SHFE stood at a one-year high in early 2026.

South Korea's copper-processing industry remains split mainly between Leeku Industrial and Poongsan, and securing metal margins during periods of rising raw-material prices determines relative profitability.

Given the nature of non-ferrous metal processing, the lag in passing through raw-material costs adds to quarterly earnings volatility. Despite concerns over slowing EV adoption growth, data center and power infrastructure demand is viewed as an emerging growth driver for copper consumption.

Outlook

In its value-up plan disclosed in March 2026, Leeku Industrial set a target of maintaining a dividend payout ratio of 20% or higher. The same plan listed capacity expansion and production line upgrades for efficiency, along with expansion of high-value-added products, as execution tasks.

Governance improvement items, including the introduction of electronic voting at shareholder meetings, were also included. EV-related material offerings are expanding into busbars, motors, and heat sinks, while the company is also pursuing demand from ESS and switchgear products for data centers.

Beyond the dividend and governance targets, no separate quantitative guidance on revenue or profit has been publicly confirmed.

With copper prices fluctuating at elevated levels through 2026, periods of rising raw-material costs could expand both reported revenue and the volatility of inventory valuation gains or losses.

The rise in operating profit to the KRW 20 billion-plus range in both Q1 and Q2 2026 appears to reflect a combination of improved product mix and captured metal margins, but whether this trend continues will require confirmation in upcoming quarterly disclosures.

Bull factors

Revenue Expansion on Rising Copper Prices

Revenue rose for two straight quarters in 2026 to KRW 151.6 billion in Q1 and KRW 190.8 billion in Q2, up 51.4% year-on-year in the latter. Hana Securities projected an average 2026 refined copper price of USD 13,417 per ton amid a continuing supply deficit. Continued top-line growth tied to this trend could also help spread fixed costs.

Expanding EV and Data Center Product Portfolio

A product lineup once centered on battery terminals and lead frames is expanding into busbars, motors, and heat sinks. Addressing ESS and switchgear demand for data centers is cited as a new growth axis. Diversifying end markets can reduce reliance on any single customer or product.

Formalized Shareholder Return Policy

The 2026 value-up plan formalized a target of maintaining a dividend payout ratio of 20% or higher. Electronic voting adoption is also being pursued as a governance improvement task. Continued profit recovery could support dividend capacity.

Bear factors

Quarterly Earnings Volatility

Net profit attributable to owners swung to a loss of KRW 564 million in Q3 2025, turned profitable in Q4 2025, then fell 40.5% quarter-on-quarter to KRW 8.69 billion in Q2 2026. The large quarter-to-quarter variance makes it difficult to treat any single strong quarter as a settled trend. Earnings remain driven by raw-material prices and inventory valuation gains or losses.

Operating Margin Softening

The operating margin eased to 4.8% in 2025 from 5.3% in 2024. Despite revenue growth, the pace of profitability improvement slowed. Intensified competition and lags in passing through raw-material costs may have weighed on margins.

Balance Sheet Pressure

The debt-to-equity ratio rose again to 158.8% in 2025 from 141.2% in 2024. Operating cash flow also flipped to an outflow of KRW 4.84 billion in 2025. Working-capital needs tied to revenue growth appear to have affected cash generation.

Risk factors

Raw Material Price Volatility

Copper prices carry significant uncertainty, with global investment banks' 2026 average forecasts ranging from roughly USD 10,000 to USD 11,500 per ton. A sharp price decline could trigger inventory valuation losses, while a sharp rise could increase working-capital burdens.

An analysis suggesting China's 2026 copper demand growth could be the weakest since 1988 is also cited as a downside factor.

Trade Policy Variables

The possibility of phased U.S. tariffs on refined copper has been raised. If implemented, such tariffs could widen price gaps between exchanges and reshape trade flows. For a processor with export exposure, these shifts could have indirect effects.

End-Market Demand Uncertainty

If EV adoption slows more than expected, demand for related materials could also be affected. A slowdown in the data center investment cycle could require adjustments to the strategy of expanding into new product lines. High cyclicality in the automotive and electronics end markets adds to earnings volatility.

Overall view

Leeku Industrial recorded three straight years of revenue growth through 2025 and continued to post larger quarterly revenue into 2026.

However, the operating margin eased slightly after peaking in 2024, and net profit attributable to owners swung between losses and gains across quarters, showing substantial volatility.

The rise of operating profit to the KRW 20 billion-plus range in both Q1 and Q2 2026 appears to reflect a combination of higher copper prices and product mix changes, but the increase in the debt ratio and the shift to negative operating cash flow are metrics that warrant equal attention.

The company set formal targets in its 2026 value-up plan to maintain a dividend payout ratio above 20%, invest in facilities, and expand high-value-added products.

Copper prices face widely diverging 2026 forecasts among investment banks amid the interplay of AI data center and EV demand with supply constraints, leaving raw-material price trends as a key variable for upcoming earnings.

The quality and durability of profit should be assessed through the next quarterly report and ongoing news on tariffs and copper prices.
