02

Business structure

Leeku Industrial produces industrial copper products such as copper plate, brass plate and phosphor bronze plate. The company has annual production capacity of 60,000 tons and said in a disclosure reported on May 15, 2026 that it supplies more than 20% of domestic demand.

In Q1 2026, copper products accounted for KRW 76.06 billion (50.17%), brass for KRW 59.26 billion (39.09%) and phosphor bronze for KRW 11.49 billion (7.58%), and product sales made up 96.84% of total revenue, with processing and other income making up the rest.

In 2025, product sales split 82.37% domestic and 17.63% export, which points to a domestic-weighted customer base. Demand from automotive electronics and batteries is the main driver, and the company said demand for copper products used in EV battery busbars and lead tabs has grown sharply.

It supplies first- and second-tier vendors of major domestic EV battery makers. The company has also widened its offering from automotive parts into semiconductor and electronics materials and is focusing on higher value-added lines. A 2021 press report described selling prices as linked to LME copper prices. Its competitive set includes copper rolling peers such as Poongsan.

Earnings analysis

Based on reported results, revenue rose from KRW 429.6 billion in 2022 to KRW 433.4 billion in 2023 (up 0.9% year over year), KRW 472.3 billion in 2024 (up 9.0% year over year) and KRW 507.8 billion in 2025 (up 7.5% year over year), increasing for three consecutive years from 2023 to 2025, and the highest year over 2022-2025 is 2025.

Operating profit was KRW 13.7 billion in 2022, KRW 10.2 billion in 2023 (down 25.4% year over year), KRW 25.2 billion in 2024 (up 146.4% year over year) and KRW 24.4 billion in 2025 (down 3.0% year over year), and the highest year over 2022-2025 is 2024.

The operating margin was 3.2% in 2022, 2.4% in 2023, 5.3% in 2024 and 4.8% in 2025, so margins stayed low even during the period when revenue was growing.

Net income attributable to owners of the parent was KRW 5.72 billion in 2022, KRW 660 million in 2023 (down 88.5% year over year), KRW 12.6 billion in 2024 (up 1,820.9% year over year) and KRW 10.9 billion in 2025 (down 13.4% year over year), and net income including non-controlling interests was reported at the same figures for the same years.

In 2023, the decline in net income was far larger than the decline in operating profit, which suggests that non-operating factors and swings in the small profit base worked together.

Cash flow from operating activities was KRW -1.69 billion in 2022, KRW 26.5 billion in 2023, KRW 4.75 billion in 2024 and KRW -4.84 billion in 2025, turning negative again in 2025.

The debt-to-equity ratio fell from 165.1% in 2022 to 141.2% in 2024 and then rose to 158.8% in 2025, and total liabilities at the end of 2025 stood at KRW 231.3 billion, exceeding total equity of KRW 145.6 billion.

On a quarterly basis, the company swung from a net loss attributable to owners of the parent of KRW 560 million in the third quarter of 2025 to a net profit attributable to owners of the parent of KRW 1.69 billion in the fourth quarter, and in the first quarter of 2026 it posted record-high levels, with revenue of KRW 151.6 billion, operating profit of KRW 21.3 billion and net income attributable to owners of the parent of KRW 14.6 billion.

Revenue in the second quarter of 2026 increased to KRW 190.8 billion (up 25.8% quarter over quarter), but operating profit of KRW 20.3 billion (down 4.4% quarter over quarter) and net income attributable to owners of the parent of KRW 8.69 billion (down 40.5% quarter over quarter) declined.

Third-quarter 2026 revenue of KRW 177.4 billion fell 7.0% quarter over quarter but rose 35.2% year over year, with operating profit of KRW 11.8 billion (down 42.0% quarter over quarter and up 273.0% year over year) and net income attributable to owners of the parent of KRW 13.6 billion (a swing to profit from the same period a year earlier).

The fact that net income attributable to owners of the parent exceeded operating profit in the third quarter is interpreted as a result of non-operating gains and losses being reflected as well.

The sum of net income attributable to owners of the parent over the last four quarters is KRW 38.6 billion, far exceeding the full-year 2025 figure of KRW 10.9 billion.

Industry analysis

The copper rolling industry earnings depend on copper prices and end-market demand. On May 13, 2026, copper-related stocks drew buying as copper prices rose and expectations of Chinese economic stimulus built up, even as the broader non-ferrous sector traded mixed.

Demand is supported by growth in copper parts for EV battery busbars and lead tabs, while semiconductor process upgrades and expanding automotive electronics add demand for higher value-added products. If the company's claim of supplying more than 20% of domestic demand holds, it is a leading domestic supplier.

Because selling prices track copper prices, revenue grows when prices rise and faces pressure when they fall. Operating income peaked in Q1 2026 and then declined in Q2 and Q3, a pattern consistent with copper prices moving past a peak.

Peers include Poongsan, and Leeku Industrial is trying to differentiate through EV battery components and higher value-added materials. With 82.37% of 2025 product sales in the domestic market, the outlook is more tied to domestic end-market demand than to export growth.

On May 13, 2026, the board approved a KRW 15 billion increase in short-term borrowings from financial institutions for working capital, lifting the total from about KRW 1,403.8 billion to about KRW 1,553.8 billion.

On November 16, 2026, the board approved cancelling 1,211 repurchased common shares, with an estimated value of about KRW 3.02 million and a scheduled cancellation date of November 30, 2026, and the company said capital stock will not decline.

The next confirmed full-year data will come from the 2026 annual business report, which will include Q4 and full-year 2026 results. Because operating income declined in Q2 and Q3 2026, whether the facility expansion and higher value-added products lift margins is the key item to track.

Whether EV battery component demand keeps growing and how copper price adjustments affect the revenue mix are also points to follow.

Bull factors

Earnings leverage in a rising copper price phase

Because selling prices track LME copper prices, revenue rises when copper rises. Q1 2026 revenue of KRW 151.6 billion and operating income of KRW 21.3 billion, and Q2 revenue of KRW 190.8 billion, reflect that link in reported results. In May 2026, rising copper prices and hopes for Chinese stimulus drew buying into copper-related stocks.

Growing demand for EV battery components

The company said demand for copper products used in EV battery busbars and lead tabs has grown significantly. It supplies first- and second-tier vendors of major domestic EV battery makers, so its volumes are tied directly to that end market.

Copper and brass products account for most of product sales, which makes EV component demand a large driver of results.

Shareholder return plan and capital policy

In a plan disclosed on March 24, 2026, the company set a target of keeping its dividend payout ratio at 20% or more. The 2025 payout ratio was 27.56%. On November 16, 2026, the board approved cancelling 1,211 repurchased shares, with a scheduled cancellation date of November 30, 2026.

Bear factors

Operating income fell in Q2 and Q3 2026

Operating income fell from KRW 21.3 billion in Q1 2026 to KRW 20.3 billion in Q2 and KRW 11.8 billion in Q3, two consecutive quarterly declines. In Q2, revenue rose 25.8% quarter on quarter yet operating income and net income attributable to controlling shareholders both fell. Margin pressure offsetting revenue growth means the quality of earnings needs continued monitoring.

Financial burden and weaker cash flow

The debt-to-equity ratio rose from 141.2% in 2024 to 158.8% in 2025, when total liabilities of KRW 231.3 billion exceeded total equity of KRW 145.6 billion. Operating cash flow was KRW -4.84 billion in 2025. In May 2026, the board approved an additional KRW 15 billion of short-term borrowings for working capital.

Copper price swings and a thin margin structure

Because selling prices track copper, falling metal prices squeeze revenue and inventory valuations. The operating margin stayed low, at 3.2%, 2.4%, 5.3% and 4.8% from 2022 to 2025. In 2023, net income attributable to controlling shareholders fell 88.5% year on year, showing how large earnings swings can be.

Risk factors

Capacity expansion execution risk

The company set facility expansion and production line upgrades for efficiency as execution items in its value-up plan. If the investment built on its 60,000-ton annual capacity is delayed or costs exceed expectations, the efficiency gains may be postponed.

Expansion of higher value-added products is also tied to plant utilization, so the point at which capacity additions show up in margins could slip.

New customer qualification risk

The company is widening its supply of core materials from automotive parts into semiconductor and electrical and electronic products, and cites customer qualification as a point of differentiation.

If qualification with new-field customers takes longer than expected, electrical and electronics material sales may not grow as planned. Fixed costs may continue during the qualification period, which could delay profitability gains.

Customer concentration and end-market demand risk

The company supplies first- and second-tier vendors of major domestic EV battery makers, tying demand to one end market. In 2025, 82.37% of product sales were domestic, so changes in domestic demand carry significant weight. A slowdown in EV demand or a change in customers' sourcing could quickly affect volumes and margins.

Overall view

Leeku Industrial focuses on copper, brass and phosphor bronze rolled products, and its selling prices track copper prices. On DART-filed figures, revenue grew for three consecutive years from 2023 through 2025 to KRW 507.8 billion, and operating income was largest in 2024 at KRW 25.2 billion.

Operating income peaked at KRW 21.3 billion in Q1 2026 and then fell in Q2 and Q3, yet net income attributable to controlling shareholders over the last four quarters totaled KRW 38.6 billion.

Supporting factors include demand for EV battery components, earnings leverage when copper prices rise, and a plan to keep the payout ratio at 20% or more.

Weighing against these are an operating margin that has stayed in the 2% to 5% range, a debt-to-equity ratio of 158.8% and negative operating cash flow in 2025, and the declines in Q2 and Q3 2026.

The next confirmed points are Q4 2026 and the 2026 annual business report, and whether the operating margin recovers will show whether the structure is improving.
