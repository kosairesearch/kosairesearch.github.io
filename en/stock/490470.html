Backlog at KRW 245.5 billion; revenue conversion is the test

02

Business structure

SemiFive designs and supplies custom ASIC (application-specific integrated circuit) chips and organizes its business into three segments: turnkey ASIC development, mass production, and IP (design assets).

In the first half of 2026, ASIC development revenue reached KRW 54.1 billion, more than double the prior-year level, while the IP segment, run through its subsidiary Analog Bits, booked KRW 32.5 billion, already above the KRW 30.5 billion it recorded for all of 2025.

The turnkey model covers chip specification definition, IP selection, logic and physical design, packaging, testing and software development, with mass production assumed from the outset.

Mass-production new orders reached KRW 42.3 billion in the first half of 2026, about twice the KRW 21.2 billion booked for all of 2025, with KRW 15.6 billion in the first quarter and KRW 26.7 billion in the second. Overseas customers accounted for about 45% of second-quarter orders, or KRW 12.1 billion.

The company is a design solutions partner of the Samsung Electronics foundry business.

The KRW 70.3 billion (US$52 million) North American contract signed in September 2026 follows a spec hand-off model, in which the customer supplies performance requirements and SemiFive handles detailed design through packaging, testing, mass production and software, a bigger role than the conventional approach where the customer completes the design and only outsources manufacturing.

The company cites cases in which large cloud companies developed their own AI chips with design partners such as Broadcom and Marvell to argue that specialist ASIC firms are taking a larger role.

Third-quarter 2026 product revenue rose to KRW 12 billion from KRW 4.5 billion in the prior quarter, as supply of high-value AI chips began in earnest, according to the company.

04

Earnings analysis

Revenue rose 8.2% in 2025 to KRW 121 billion from KRW 111.8 billion in 2024, but the operating loss widened from KRW 22.9 billion to KRW 53.4 billion, extending the losses. The operating margin fell from -20.5% in 2024 to -44.1% in 2025, meaning revenue growth did not absorb cost and operating expense pressure.

Against the 2025 guidance the company had given (revenue of KRW 139.7 billion and an operating loss of KRW 33.8 billion, as cited by DB Securities), actual revenue came in lower and the operating loss was larger.

The net loss attributable to controlling shareholders narrowed from KRW 290.9 billion in 2024 to KRW 56.2 billion in 2025, and DB Securities' consolidated income data indicate that the 2024 loss was driven largely by a large non-operating loss.

Operating cash flow improved from -KRW 19.4 billion in 2024 to -KRW 8.88 billion in 2025. In the first quarter of 2026 revenue was KRW 47.9 billion with an operating profit of KRW 105 million, and the net profit attributable to controlling shareholders was KRW 1.34 billion.

In the second quarter revenue fell 2.2% to KRW 46.8 billion, the company swung to an operating loss of KRW 11 billion, and the net loss attributable to controlling shareholders was KRW 12.2 billion.

In the third quarter revenue rose 9.9% to KRW 51.4 billion, the operating loss narrowed to KRW 9.67 billion and the net loss attributable to controlling shareholders was KRW 8.22 billion.

The company's first-half 2026 operating loss of KRW 10.9 billion was smaller than the KRW 28.1 billion loss of the prior-year first half, according to the company's disclosures.

Before the results, Kiwoom Securities had expected third-quarter revenue to be limited because new orders clustered at quarter-end; actual third-quarter revenue of KRW 51.4 billion came in above that estimate.

The net loss attributable to controlling shareholders for the last four reported quarters (first quarter to fourth quarter of 2025) totaled KRW 56.2 billion.

05

Industry analysis

Demand for custom ASIC chips underpins SemiFive's order growth. The company says that rising global ASIC demand and the full-scale entry into markets such as North America and Japan drove a sharp increase in orders.

As demand for AI inference chips grows, the company points to the technical maturity and mass-production track record of its 4-nanometer process as a competitive advantage. On advanced nodes, the company says IP revenue on 2- and 3-nanometer processes is rising.

Kiwoom Securities analyzed that foundry bottlenecks and raw-material supply disruptions are lengthening delivery times, so part of fourth-quarter revenue could slip into early 2027.

The company's view is that big cloud players developing their own AI chips while working with design partners creates opportunities for specialist ASIC firms. In terms of competition, SemiFive differentiates itself by widening its turnkey scope from specification definition through mass production.

On the other side, its heavy reliance on the foundry ecosystem and a decline in valuation multiples at global peers were cited by Kiwoom Securities as reasons for its target price adjustment.

The company also said it has signed a turnkey contract for AI chips for industrial robots, extending its application areas beyond data centers and edge AI.

06

Outlook

The company said the KRW 245.5 billion backlog at the end of the third quarter of 2026 will be recognized as revenue in sequence, and it expects revenue conversion to accelerate from the fourth quarter.

Purchase orders for mass production keep arriving, and the company expects this production portfolio to contribute to earnings growth from 2027.

The North American KRW 70.3 billion contract is planned to move through tape-out in the first half of 2027 and into volume production from 2028 for global hyperscalers and cloud service providers.

In a report published just before the results, Kiwoom Securities forecast a full-year 2026 operating loss of KRW 19.6 billion and a 2027 operating profit of KRW 26.4 billion.

Adding the reported first-to-third-quarter operating results gives a cumulative loss of about KRW 20.6 billion, so reaching Kiwoom's full-year 2026 estimate would require roughly KRW 1 billion of operating profit in the fourth quarter (an arithmetic calculation from reported figures).

Kiwoom also flagged that part of fourth-quarter revenue could move into early 2027. How much of the backlog is recognized in the fourth quarter is therefore the first checkpoint for the full-year outlook.

The full-year operating result in the 2026 annual report, and whether the North American project reaches tape-out in the first half of 2027, are the next checkpoints.

08

Bull factors

Backlog about twice 2025 revenue

The company announced that third-quarter 2026 new orders of KRW 178.4 billion and a backlog of KRW 245.5 billion were record levels. ASIC development orders alone reached KRW 136.9 billion in the quarter, up 705% from a year earlier. The backlog is more than twice the KRW 121 billion of 2025 revenue, which is the core of the bullish case.

KRW 70.3 billion North American contract

SemiFive announced a KRW 70.3 billion (US$52 million) contract with a North American AI fabless company and stressed that it is the largest single contract in its history. The amount exceeds 40% of the company's full-year 2025 orders.

Under the spec hand-off model it covers the work from detailed design through packaging, testing, mass production and software, which could lay a foundation for follow-on orders. Whether the tape-out in the first half of 2027 and volume production in 2028 proceed on schedule is the point to verify.

Signs of improving unit economics

According to the company, the gross margin for the first half of 2026 was 30.6%, up 18.7 percentage points from 11.9% a year earlier. The cumulative third-quarter 2026 operating margin of -14% improved by 25 percentage points year on year.

IP revenue at subsidiary Analog Bits reached KRW 32.5 billion in the first half, broadening its recurring revenue base. Operating losses have continued since the second quarter, so whether the improvement reaches operating profit must be confirmed in upcoming results.

Bear factors

Operating losses continue

The 2025 operating loss was KRW 53.4 billion, and the operating margin of -44.1% was lower than the -20.5% of 2024. Operating losses have continued for two consecutive quarters, from the second quarter to the third quarter of 2026.

The third-quarter operating loss narrowed to KRW 9.67 billion, but it is too early to say the loss-making structure has been resolved, which is the bearish point.

Quarter-end orders and delayed revenue recognition

Before the results, analysts noted that new orders clustering at quarter-end would limit how much revenue was recognized in the third quarter. Kiwoom Securities said foundry bottlenecks and raw-material supply disruptions are lengthening delivery times, so part of fourth-quarter revenue could slip into early 2027. If the backlog converts to revenue slowly, the pace at which losses narrow could slow as well.

Concentration in customers and foundry

The counterparty to the KRW 70.3 billion contract has not been disclosed, and ASIC development accounted for most of the KRW 178.4 billion of third-quarter orders, at KRW 136.9 billion. The company is a design solutions partner of Samsung Electronics' foundry business and therefore depends on that ecosystem.

Because it relies on both a small set of customers and one foundry ecosystem, a delay in a single project could weigh noticeably on quarterly results.

Risk factors

Supply chain and process risk

Kiwoom Securities analyzed that foundry bottlenecks and raw-material supply disruptions are extending delivery times. In that case part of fourth-quarter revenue could move into early 2027.

A project whose costs rose after a specification change in the second quarter was also cited as delaying additional revenue recognition. Even with rising orders, quarterly results could swing more if revenue recognition timing slips.

Customer concentration and contract structure

The KRW 70.3 billion contract is a single large project that accounts for a substantial share of third-quarter 2026 orders, and its counterparty is undisclosed. The spec hand-off model widens the scope of design work, which can add cost and schedule management burden during development. A specification change or schedule adjustment by one customer could affect profitability directly.

Financial and cash flow

The 2025 operating loss of KRW 53.4 billion equaled a 44.1% margin on KRW 121 billion of revenue, and operating cash flow was -KRW 8.88 billion. Operating losses have continued from the second quarter to the third quarter of 2026, so the loss structure is not yet resolved.

The debt-to-equity ratio fell to 40.3% in 2025, but how quickly order revenue turns into cash will determine financial stability.

Overall view

Based on its ASIC turnkey expansion, SemiFive announced third-quarter 2026 new orders of KRW 178.4 billion and a backlog of KRW 245.5 billion.

Over the same quarter, reported results showed revenue of KRW 51.4 billion and an operating loss of KRW 9.67 billion, so revenue grew but no operating profit has appeared for the third quarter.

The 2025 operating margin of -44.1% was lower than the -20.5% of 2024, and operating losses have continued for two consecutive quarters from the second quarter of 2026.

On the bullish side, the backlog is more than twice 2025 revenue, the North American KRW 70.3 billion contract has been added, and the first-half gross margin improved to 30.6%.

On the bearish side, Kiwoom Securities has analyzed that quarter-end order concentration and foundry bottlenecks could push part of revenue recognition into early 2027, and the company depends heavily on a single large contract.

Kiwoom Securities lowered its target price while maintaining a Buy rating, according to its report. The next checkpoints are the scale of fourth-quarter 2026 revenue recognition, the full-year operating result in the 2026 annual report, and the tape-out schedule of the North American project in the first half of 2027.
