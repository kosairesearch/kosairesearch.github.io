02

Business structure

Genohco supplies satellite communications parts, avionics, electrical ground support equipment (EGSE) and test gear, and core defense components.

According to a KB Securities report dated June 30, 2026, the first-quarter 2026 revenue mix was 34.4 percent core defense parts, 31.6 percent EGSE and test equipment, 20.7 percent satellite communications and 13.4 percent avionics.

KB Securities noted that core parts are developed for military operating conditions and then delivered in production volumes, which gives the line long-tail supply once development is complete. EGSE is ground equipment used to check satellites electrically before launch, so its sales tend to move with satellite orders.

In satellite communications, the company's track record includes an X-band transmitter for satellites, which a 2021 KB Securities report described as the first domestic unit with 720 Mbps transmission speed.

In February 2026 the company won an on-board computer development contract for a Korean low-earth-orbit satellite communications program, worth KRW 5.6 billion and running through October 31, 2028. KAI acquired a 37.95 percent stake in Genohco in July 2025, tightening its ties to the aerospace value chain.

Overseas, the company is expanding satellite communications and MRO work with Viasat, is cooperating with Northrop Grumman on a Korean minesweeping helicopter program, and has delivered ground operations station work for Airbus D&S.

KB Securities judged that many of these overseas tie-ups are still at the MOU stage, so their earnings contribution is too early to assess. Listed peers in satellite communications terminals and antennas include Intellian Technologies.

04

Earnings analysis

In the annual disclosed results, revenue rose from KRW 50.7 billion in 2022 to KRW 55.1 billion in 2023 (up 8.7 percent year on year), KRW 56.8 billion in 2024 (up 3.1 percent) and KRW 59.6 billion in 2025 (up 4.9 percent), and 2025 was the largest year of the 2022–2025 span.

Over the same period the operating margin moved from 6.2 percent to 2.1 percent, then -3.8 percent and -3.5 percent. Operating profit of KRW 3.15 billion in 2022 fell to KRW 1.15 billion in 2023 (down 63.5 percent), and the company swung to an operating loss of KRW 2.17 billion in 2024.

The 2025 operating loss of KRW 2.09 billion continued, but the loss narrowed from 2024.

Profit attributable to controlling shareholders was KRW 2.72 billion in 2022 and KRW 1.65 billion in 2023 (down 39.1 percent), then turned into a net loss of KRW 3.09 billion in 2024 and narrowed to a KRW 2.12 billion net loss in 2025.

Net income including non-controlling interests is disclosed separately and shows the same amounts for each year. Operating cash flow swung from KRW 10.2 billion in 2022 to -KRW 3.56 billion in 2023, KRW 12.2 billion in 2024 and -KRW 19.8 billion in 2025, with the 2025 outflow the largest in the period.

The debt ratio stood at 69.3 percent at the end of 2025, down from 154.4 percent in 2024, with total equity of KRW 51.8 billion, all attributable to controlling shareholders, and total liabilities of KRW 35.9 billion.

On a quarterly basis, revenue went from KRW 17.9 billion in the third quarter of 2025 to KRW 17.2 billion in the fourth quarter (down 4.0 percent quarter on quarter), KRW 11.6 billion in the first quarter of 2026 (down 32.6 percent), KRW 10.9 billion in the second quarter (down 6.3 percent) and KRW 9.84 billion in the third quarter (down 9.4 percent), a fourth straight quarterly decline.

Operating profit of KRW 378 million in the third quarter of 2025 turned into an operating loss of KRW 195 million in the fourth quarter, then losses of KRW 1.23 billion, KRW 696 million and KRW 1.11 billion in the first three quarters of 2026.

Wisereport attributed the first-quarter 2026 loss to revenue swings tied to the timing of large system programs and production schedules, plus fixed-cost burdens typical of R&D-intensive businesses.

KB Securities judged the margin pressure as temporary, linked to expanded national projects and higher costs from supplier investment. Profit attributable to controlling shareholders was KRW 193 million in the second quarter of 2026, a return to profit, but the third quarter swung back to a loss of KRW 1.01 billion.

The combined controlling-shareholder net loss for the last four quarters, from the fourth quarter of 2025 to the third quarter of 2026, is KRW 2.2 billion.

05

Industry analysis

Genohco operates in the satellite, aerospace and defense parts market, where demand leans heavily on government-led space programs and defense system procurement. A 2021 KB Securities report cited the third basic space development plan, which envisions 69 satellites launched from Korea by 2040.

In a June 2026 report, the same broker pointed to satellite miniaturization, multi-satellite launch trends and demand for domestic avionics as medium-term drivers. Defense revenue is notably uneven from quarter to quarter because it depends on the timing of system programs and production schedules.

In terms of cycle position, KB Securities judged that Genohco is in a transition in which development projects are moving toward production and revenue recognition.

Competitively, satellite communications terminals and antennas overlap with listed peers such as Intellian Technologies, but Genohco supplies satellite payload parts, avionics, ground test equipment and core defense components together.

This mix can spread revenue across segments if one program slips, but segment-level margin swings also feed into consolidated results. Its position within KAI's value chain means volumes are tied to the production schedules of Korean programs.

06

Outlook

For the medium term, KB Securities pointed to whether Genohco can join the supply chains of global prime contractors and shift toward export-type revenue.

The same report noted that the company secured new KAI orders worth KRW 8.4 billion in the first half of 2026, and KB Securities expected the year-end order backlog could rise to around KRW 150 billion. It also expected conversion of the existing backlog into revenue to concentrate in the second half of 2026.

With third-quarter 2026 revenue at KRW 9.84 billion, down from the prior quarter, the pace at which this conversion shows up in quarterly results is the most direct checkpoint.

Among first-half 2026 broker reports, Daishin Securities estimated 2026 revenue of KRW 84.7 billion and an operating profit of KRW 3.8 billion, a return to profit, while Yuanta Securities forecast 2026 revenue of KRW 85.0 billion with a turn to operating profit.

Quarterly results disclosed for the first three quarters of 2026 show operating losses, so the gap between these forecasts and actual results is wide.

The company is carrying out the 6G low-earth-orbit OBC development through October 31, 2028, and KB Securities expected EGSE revenue from this program to be recognized in sequence. The 2026 annual report, the next regular filing, will show whether full-year operating profit returns to the black.

Because most overseas tie-ups are still at the MOU stage, moving them into final contracts and production supply is the key condition for an export-type revenue shift.

Bull factors

The order base is building

The company won a KRW 5.6 billion OBC development contract for a 6G low-earth-orbit satellite in February 2026 and secured new KAI orders worth KRW 8.4 billion in the first half. KB Securities expected the year-end backlog could reach around KRW 150 billion. The core of this trend is that a broader revenue base forms as development projects move into production.

KAI stake and cooperation synergies

KAI acquired a 37.95 percent stake in July 2025, creating a framework for cooperation in avionics and satellites. KB Securities expected synergies to strengthen gradually after the acquisition. If demand for smaller satellites and domestic avionics continues, there is room for cooperation volumes to expand.

Overseas partnership references

The company is expanding satellite communications and MRO work with Viasat and is cooperating with Northrop Grumman on a Korean minesweeping helicopter program. It has also delivered ground operations station work for Airbus D&S.

KB Securities said that if these tie-ups move to final contracts and production supply, the company could be viewed as a parts supplier with overseas customers.

Bear factors

Four straight quarters of falling sales

Quarterly revenue fell from KRW 17.9 billion in the third quarter of 2025 to KRW 9.84 billion in the third quarter of 2026, a fourth straight quarterly decline. Third-quarter 2026 revenue was down 45.1 percent from a year earlier, a steep drop. If order growth does not reach quarterly revenue, the annual growth trend will be hard to restore.

Persistent losses and cash outflow

Operating losses have run for four consecutive quarters from the fourth quarter of 2025 through the third quarter of 2026, and the third-quarter loss of KRW 1.11 billion marked a swing to losses from a year earlier. Operating cash flow was a KRW 19.8 billion outflow in 2025.

The combined controlling-shareholder net loss over the last four quarters is KRW 2.2 billion, so the pace of earnings improvement is slow.

Delayed conversion and reliance on MOUs

KB Securities said many overseas tie-ups are still at the MOU stage, making their earnings contribution too early to judge. Its expectation that backlog conversion would concentrate in the second half of 2026 sits uneasily with third-quarter revenue falling.

If the turn to profit that broker reports projected earlier in 2026 is delayed, the gap between forecasts and reported results could widen.

Risk factors

Timing of order conversion

Even if the backlog grows, revenue recognition slows if development projects shift into production late. Third-quarter 2026 revenue fell to KRW 9.84 billion. Whether the second-half conversion KB Securities highlighted shows up in quarterly results is the first thing to verify.

Fixed costs and program timing

Because the business is R&D-intensive, fixed-cost burdens remain even when sales decline. Quarterly revenue swings with the timing of large system programs and production schedules. In the first quarter of 2026, costs rose from expanded national projects and supplier investment.

Dependence on KAI and cash flow

After KAI's stake acquisition, KAI-related volumes could account for a larger share, tying results to one customer's ordering schedule. Operating cash flow was a KRW 19.8 billion outflow in 2025, so working-capital pressure could persist until earnings recover.

Overall view

Revenue grew through 2025 and the operating loss narrowed that year from 2024, but quarterly sales have fallen for four straight quarters into 2026, and operating losses have run for four consecutive quarters.

Supportive factors include the February 2026 KRW 5.6 billion 6G low-earth-orbit OBC contract, new KAI orders of KRW 8.4 billion in the first half of 2026, KAI's 37.95 percent stake acquired in July 2025, and overseas partnership references.

Negative factors include the 45.1 percent year-on-year drop in third-quarter 2026 revenue, the KRW 19.8 billion operating cash outflow in 2025, and a combined controlling-shareholder net loss of KRW 2.2 billion over the last four quarters.

The debt ratio fell to 69.3 percent at the end of 2025 from 154.4 percent a year earlier, an improvement in financial structure. On valuation, the PER cannot be calculated, and the price-to-book ratio sits below the lower end of its quarter-end range, which is a factual comparison only.

The next checkpoints are the year-end 2026 order backlog, full-year 2026 operating results in the annual report, and first-quarter 2027 results. Whether order conversion into revenue and a narrowing of losses appear together in these three points will be the reference for reading this company.
