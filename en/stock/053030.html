Binex: CDMO Transition Amid Persistent Losses

Binex's revenue rebounded to KRW 168.5 billion in 2025, but the company has posted three consecutive quarters of operating losses from the fourth quarter of 2025 through the second quarter of 2026, and four consecutive quarters of net losses attributable to controlling interests since the third quarter of 2025, amid heavy spending on its Osong plant expansion.

02

Business structure

Binex operates two business segments: a chemical pharmaceuticals division producing synthetic drugs, and a biologics division handling biopharmaceutical contract development and manufacturing (CDMO).

Its Busan plant produces chemical pharmaceuticals, while the Songdo plant in Incheon and the Osong plant in North Chungcheong serve as its core biologics CDMO production sites. The Songdo plant was among the earlier Korean CDMO facilities to obtain both European Medicines Agency (EMA) and U.S.

Food and Drug Administration (FDA) commercial cGMP certifications.

In early 2025, in connection with manufacturing the biosimilar Aptozma (referencing Actemra) developed by Celltrion for autoimmune disease treatment, Binex received FDA approval for commercial antibody drug production, marking the fourth such FDA approval among domestic CDMO companies.

Its major customers include large domestic biosimilar makers such as Celltrion and multiple global pharmaceutical companies, and it has expanded its overseas customer base, including signing a KRW 20.8 billion contract manufacturing deal for a biosimilar with a global biotech company, the largest single-product contract in its history.

As of the second quarter of 2025, the chemical division generated revenue of KRW 21.5 billion and the biologics division KRW 23.0 billion, indicating a roughly balanced contribution between the two segments.

In the competitive landscape, large-scale CDMO players such as Samsung Biologics dominate blockbuster-scale commercial manufacturing, while Binex has positioned itself as a mid-size CDMO specializing in small-batch, multi-product manufacturing, including expanding its then-7,000-liter Osong plant to preemptively boost capacity.

It competes with other domestic mid-size CDMOs such as ST Pharm in the niche targeting early-stage clinical materials and mid-sized commercial volumes.

04

Earnings analysis

Binex's annual revenue fell for two straight years from KRW 156.7 billion in 2022 to KRW 154.8 billion in 2023 (down 1.2% year-on-year) and KRW 130.1 billion in 2024 (down 16.0%), before rebounding to KRW 168.5 billion in 2025 (up 29.5%).

Operating profit plunged from KRW 17.2 billion in 2022 to KRW 1.04 billion in 2023 (down 93.9%), then turned into an operating loss of KRW 30.8 billion in 2024, and the company recorded an operating loss of KRW 4.53 billion in 2025, extending losses for a second consecutive year with a narrower deficit.

Net income attributable to controlling interests followed a similar pattern, falling from KRW 12.4 billion in 2022 to KRW 4.67 billion in 2023 (down 62.5%), turning into a net loss of KRW 35.1 billion in 2024, and posting a loss of KRW 3.23 billion in 2025, a second straight year of losses with a smaller deficit.

The operating margin deteriorated sharply from 11.0% in 2022 to -2.7% in 2025.

On a quarterly basis, the operating profit of KRW 1.02 billion in the second quarter of 2025 shrank to KRW 242 million in the third quarter before turning into an operating loss of KRW 6.34 billion in the fourth quarter, followed by operating losses of KRW 8.97 billion in the first quarter of 2026 and KRW 9.15 billion in the second quarter of 2026 — three consecutive quarters of losses that widened each period.

Net income attributable to controlling interests turned negative in the third quarter of 2025 at a loss of KRW 965 million, and the loss widened over four consecutive quarters to KRW 3.68 billion, KRW 9.62 billion, and KRW 15.0 billion by the second quarter of 2026.

A report by the Korea Investors Relations Service explained that in 2025 Binex recorded an operating loss as one-off costs concentrated around FDA and EMA inspection response, facility upgrades, staffing increases, and expansion preparations, underscoring why profitability failed to recover despite revenue growth.

On the balance sheet, total equity stood at KRW 187.5 billion (controlling interest equity of KRW 187.3 billion) and total liabilities at KRW 124 billion at the end of 2025, while operating cash flow shifted from an inflow of KRW 17.1 billion in 2022 to outflows of KRW 9.43 billion in 2024 and KRW 23.3 billion in 2025, reflecting the cash drain from expansion investment and working capital needs.

05

Industry analysis

The global biopharmaceutical CDMO market continues to expand on growing demand for biosimilars and antibody drugs, with large domestic CDMOs such as Samsung Biologics continuing massive capacity expansions while mid-size CDMOs including Binex carve out a differentiated niche in small-batch, multi-product manufacturing.

On the policy front, Korea's CDMO special act, set to take effect at the end of this year, is focused on reducing the administrative bottleneck of approval and quality certification procedures that arise after commercial production infrastructure is built, structurally supplementing the regulatory responsiveness and quality trust that global clients demand when evaluating new CDMO partners.

Potential tightening of U.S. restrictions on China-linked biotech supply chains is frequently cited as a factor that could benefit domestic CDMOs holding EMA and FDA certifications, though the legislative outcome remains uncertain.

Binex still relies heavily on domestic customers such as Celltrion, as export revenue accounted for only about 2.2% of its cumulative third-quarter revenue in the prior year, making the expansion of global orders a key factor for strengthening its industry position.

While some in the industry view the company as a mid-size CDMO production infrastructure that combines global certifications with facility competitiveness, it remains structurally disadvantaged relative to large CDMOs in terms of economies of scale.

The downstream biosimilar market is expected to see expanding production demand over the medium to long term as original biologics patents expire, though delays in clients' clinical or approval timelines, or weaker investment sentiment, could slow the pace of new orders.

06

Outlook

The company's medium-term outlook hinges heavily on completing the Osong plant expansion and securing FDA approval.

Binex is expanding the Osong site by 10,000 liters, targeting completion in November of this year, combining the addition with existing 1,000-liter and 5,000-liter animal cell culture facilities to strengthen commercial production capability, which would bring total capacity to roughly 22,000 liters once complete.

The company is targeting an FDA facility inspection in 2026, aiming for a commercial production transition as early as the first half of 2026 and the start-up of the 10,000-liter (two 5,000-liter lines) expansion in the second half.

Once approval is secured, analysts note that having already obtained EMA and FDA certifications, the company could see the approval act more as a trust-building factor in global contract negotiations rather than an additional certification hurdle.

The Songdo plant continues producing commercial volumes including Celltrion's Aptozma biosimilar, and some observers suggest that once the Osong plant reaches normal operation, the combined production track record of both plants could be leveraged to attract new global customers.

However, neither the expansion completion nor the FDA inspection and approval timeline is confirmed, and any delay would push back both the commercial production transition and the associated revenue recognition.

On the policy side, the CDMO special act is set to take effect by year-end, and if support measures such as shortened approval procedures materialize, it could influence the pace of business expansion for mid-size CDMOs.

Whether the company discloses new order contracts and whether the expansion is completed on schedule will be key indicators of how quickly any earnings turnaround actually materializes.

08

Bull factors

Global Certification-Backed CDMO Track Record

The Songdo plant has obtained commercial production cGMP certifications from both EMA and FDA, and has also received commercial production approval for Celltrion's biosimilar 'Aptozma,' making it one of the few domestic CDMOs to secure certification from both major regulatory agencies.

These certifications are cited as a factor that enhances credibility when attracting new global clients. Analysts note that if the Osong plant additionally obtains FDA approval, the combined production records of the two plants could serve as a stepping stone for expanding overseas orders.

Osong Expansion Set to Roughly Double Capacity

The Osong plant is undergoing a 10,000-liter capacity expansion, targeting completion in November 2026. Once the expansion is complete, the company's total production capacity is expected to expand to approximately 22,000 liters.

This capacity expansion serves as a business foundation that could lead to diversification of commercial production volumes and strengthened mid-to-long-term order response capability.

Policy Tailwinds

With the CDMO Special Act set to take effect by the end of the year, there is discussion that administrative bottlenecks in licensing and quality certification procedures could be reduced.

Analysts also suggest that if the U.S. moves to re-strengthen regulations on the biotech supply chain targeting China, this could open up opportunities for small and mid-sized domestic CDMOs holding EMA and FDA certifications to secure new clients.

However, the actual impact of such policy variables may vary depending on the timing of legislation and implementation.

09

Bear factors

Three Straight Quarters of Widening Operating Losses

The company recorded operating losses for three consecutive quarters from Q4 2025 through Q2 2026, with the loss size expanding each quarter from KRW 6.34 billion to KRW 9.15 billion.

Net loss attributable to controlling shareholders also increased for four consecutive quarters since Q3 2025, reaching KRW 15 billion in Q2 2026. The timing for profitability recovery remains unclear.

Rising Financial Burden

The debt ratio rose from 41.7% in 2023 to 66.1% in 2025, and operating cash flow outflow widened from -KRW 9.43 billion in 2024 to -KRW 23.3 billion in 2025. With expansion investment and working capital burden overlapping, the structure could increase the need for external financing. If the expansion is prolonged without improvement in cash flow, financial burden may intensify.

Structural Slowdown in the Chemical Division

The Chemical (synthetic drugs) division showed slowing growth due to drug price cuts and base effects from past quality issues. This division is considered to have a relatively lower operating margin compared to the Bio division, and could act as a factor holding back overall profitability improvement.

If the profitability gap between the two divisions continues, it could affect the pace of consolidated profit recovery.

10

Risk factors

Facility and Certification Schedule Risk

Both the completion of the 10,000-liter expansion at the Osong plant and the FDA inspection and approval remain unconfirmed schedules. If completion or approval is delayed beyond the planned timeline, the timing of transition to commercial production and revenue recognition could also be delayed together.

In this case, a gap could emerge between market expectations regarding the timing of earnings turnaround and actual results.

Customer Concentration Risk

A significant portion of the Bio division's revenue is concentrated among a small number of domestic clients such as Celltrion, and the proportion of exports in total revenue remains low.

If a specific client experiences changes in clinical trial or approval schedules, or adjusts orders, quarterly earnings volatility could increase. If the expansion of overseas client share is slow, this concentration may not be easily resolved.

Funding and Balance Sheet Risk

With expansion investment continuing amid negative operating cash flow, there is a possibility that additional borrowing or capital raising will be needed. If the debt ratio continues to rise, market sensitivity to financial soundness could increase.

The method of fundraising is also a variable to watch, as it could affect the equity value of existing shareholders.

12

Overall view

Despite a revenue rebound in 2025, Binex is in a phase where operating losses and net losses attributable to controlling shareholders have continued through Q2 2026 and widened, due to the burden of expansion and certification-related costs at the Osong plant.

The EMA and FDA certifications at the Songdo plant and the commercial production record for Celltrion are evidence of a qualitative transformation as a CDMO, but this has not yet clearly translated into improved consolidated profits.

The completion of the Osong plant expansion (targeted for November 2026) and the subsequent FDA inspection and approval are cited as key variables for the next phase, and if this process is delayed, the timing of the turnaround could also be pushed back.

On the financial side, a rising debt ratio and continued outflow of operating cash flow are occurring together, raising the possibility that financing burden could increase if the expansion is prolonged.

On the policy side, the CDMO Special Act and the potential re-push of the Biosecure Act are cited as variables that could create a favorable environment for small and mid-sized CDMOs, but the actual legislation and implementation timing remain uncertain.

Before making investment decisions, it is necessary to directly verify disclosures regarding expansion completion, FDA approval, new orders, and the profit and loss trends in the next quarterly report.
