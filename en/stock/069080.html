02

Business structure

Webzen develops and publishes online and mobile games, and its revenue still rests heavily on the long-running MU franchise. Mu-related revenue was KRW 22.3 billion in Q3 2026, about 62% of game revenue, a share roughly unchanged from Q3 2025.

Beyond Mu, the lineup includes R2, Metin2, Shot Online and S.U.N New Edition. In the company's 2025 third-quarter report, eight of the 13 services it operated were based on Mu IP. Overseas sales were about 51% of revenue in Q3 2026, down from about 55% a year earlier but still above half.

For full-year 2025 the overseas share rose to about 49%, which reports attributed to steady overseas demand for Mu and Metin IP offsetting domestic declines. The new-title pipeline has three main parts.

The first is The Defense Game Tyrant: LAST STAGE, a strategy RPG based on a Naver webtoon that a development subsidiary is building, targeting a first-half 2027 launch. The second is MU THEOS, a Mu-based MMORPG that has completed internal testing and targets a 2027 launch.

The third is Project G, an Unreal Engine 5 project based on Mu IP that another development subsidiary is building for Western markets, with business plans to be disclosed as development progresses. A subculture title, Terbis, is also in development.

04

Earnings analysis

Annual revenue was highest in 2022 at KRW 242.1 billion and lowest in 2025 at KRW 174.4 billion. It fell to KRW 196.3 billion in 2023 (down 18.9% year on year), rebounded to KRW 214.7 billion in 2024 (up 9.4%), then dropped to KRW 174.4 billion in 2025 (down 18.8%).

Operating profit fell from KRW 83 billion in 2022 to KRW 29.7 billion in 2025 (down 45.5% year on year), and the operating margin moved from 34.3% in 2022 to 25.4% in 2023, 25.4% in 2024 and 17.0% in 2025.

Profit attributable to owners of the parent was KRW 72.4 billion in 2022, KRW 57.6 billion in 2023 (down 20.4%), KRW 56.9 billion in 2024 (down 1.2%) and KRW 24 billion in 2025 (down 57.8%), a decline for three consecutive years from 2023 through 2025.

Net profit including non-controlling interests was KRW 23.5 billion in 2025, a different figure that should be read separately from profit attributable to owners of the parent. Operating cash flow fell from KRW 72.4 billion in 2024 to KRW 19.1 billion in 2025.

The balance sheet remains light, with a debt ratio of 12.3% at end-2025, total liabilities of KRW 84.2 billion and total equity of KRW 683.9 billion.

On a quarterly basis, revenue peaked at KRW 49.9 billion in Q4 2025 (up 13.9% from the prior quarter) and then declined for three consecutive quarters to KRW 39.4 billion in Q1 2026, KRW 38 billion in Q2 and KRW 36 billion in Q3. Operating profit in Q3 2026 was KRW 5.39 billion, down 29.3% year on year.

Advertising spending was cut sharply in the same period, but personnel costs rose to about 56% of operating expenses, which analysts said made it hard to defend profitability through cost reduction alone.

Profit attributable to owners of the parent peaked at KRW 8.88 billion in Q1 2026 and then fell for two consecutive quarters to KRW 6.32 billion and KRW 5.25 billion, with a trailing four-quarter total of KRW 27.4 billion.

The revenue decline is centered on falling Mu IP sales and a gap in new releases, so recovery depends more on new titles filling that gap than on cost adjustments.

05

Industry analysis

Reports on Webzen's results repeatedly describe the domestic game market as in a broad slump, and the company's domestic revenue also fell to KRW 17.6 billion in Q3 2026.

Mu IP accounted for about 63% of revenue in 2025, down from a level above 70% in earlier years, and industry commentary treats reducing dependence on Mu as a medium-term task. Mu IP revenue declined from KRW 192.1 billion in 2021 to KRW 150.9 billion in 2024, a long-term downward trend.

Overseas markets have acted as a cushion. The overseas share rose to about 49% in 2025, and overseas results in Q4 2025 were reported up about 27% year on year.

However, in Q3 2026 overseas revenue fell 23.0%, more than the 11.6% drop in domestic revenue, a sign that overseas growth can no longer fully offset weakness at home.

In the new-title cycle, Webzen is in a period of extended release gaps, and with its main new titles targeted for the first half of 2027, media reports suggest that the earnings gap could persist for some time.

Compared with listed peers in the same sector, Webzen owns its own IP lineup as both developer and publisher, but its concentration on a single IP makes its earnings more volatile.

06

Outlook

The nearest checkpoint the company has set is G-STAR 2026. Webzen is running a 100-booth exhibit at BEXCO in Busan from November 19 to 22, including a playable siege-battle zone for The Defense Game Tyrant: LAST STAGE and a gameplay section covering exploration, defense battles and territory management.

On November 21 the development subsidiary's CEO and key developers will hold an open talk show. The webtoon-based strategy RPG uses Unreal Engine 5 with a diorama pixel-art style, and its launch target is the first half of 2027.

MU THEOS revealed its game name and brand identity in early November 2026, has completed internal testing, and is set to show a cinematic teaser at G-STAR, with a 2027 launch targeted. Project G, based on Mu IP and aimed at Western markets, will have its business plan disclosed according to development progress.

Terbis has released OST and web novel content while development continues. CEO Kim Tae-young said the company is focusing on supporting its development subsidiaries and expanding overseas business models to secure medium-term growth.

The next formal disclosure is the 2026 annual report, due by March 31, 2027, where full-year revenue, operating margin and the Mu IP share can be checked against filed figures.

Bull factors

Expanding new-title lineup

Project G is an Unreal Engine 5 project aimed partly at Western markets. Bulls argue that a broader lineup beyond Mu could reduce single-IP dependence.

Financial capacity and shareholder returns

Total equity was KRW 683.9 billion at end-2025 against total liabilities of KRW 84.2 billion, a debt ratio of 12.3%. Reports say the company has pursued shareholder returns through large share retirements and high dividends.

Operating cash flow fell to KRW 19.1 billion in 2025, but low liabilities leave comparatively more room to absorb the decline.

Overseas base and cost flexibility

The overseas share of revenue held near half, at about 49% in 2025 and about 51% in Q3 2026. Reports cite overseas results up about 27% year on year in Q4 2025. Advertising spending was cut sharply in Q3 2026, showing room to adjust costs. If overseas demand for Mu and Metin IP holds, it could serve as a defense line until new titles arrive.

Bear factors

Concentration on the Mu IP and long-term decline

Mu-related revenue was KRW 22.3 billion in Q3 2026, down 17.7% year on year, and about 62% of game revenue. Mu IP revenue fell from KRW 192.1 billion in 2021 to KRW 150.9 billion in 2024, a long-term downtrend. The higher the share of a single franchise, the more a decline in that title drags down total earnings.

Three straight quarters of falling revenue

Revenue peaked at KRW 49.9 billion in Q4 2025 and then fell to KRW 39.4 billion in Q1 2026, KRW 38 billion in Q2 and KRW 36 billion in Q3. Q3 2026 operating profit was KRW 5.39 billion, down 29.3% year on year.

With personnel costs at about 56% of operating expenses, analysts said advertising cuts alone could not protect profitability.

Release gap and concentration of launches

With major launch targets clustered in 2027, media reports suggest the earnings gap could persist for some time. New titles are still at demo and teaser stages, so there is limited data on their likely commercial scale.

Reports also point to a publishing dispute with a developer over one title, adding to pipeline management strain.

Risk factors

Single-IP concentration

Mu IP accounts for about 62% of game revenue, so swings in the flagship franchise pass straight through to the whole company. Mu IP revenue has trended down since 2021. If MU THEOS underperforms, the dependence on one franchise could persist for longer.

Launch timing and commercial performance of new titles

The Defense Game Tyrant: LAST STAGE and MU THEOS both target 2027 launches, and any delay would extend the gap in releases. Because major titles are clustered in the same period, shifts in launch timing could shape the earnings path. The timing of business-plan disclosures for subsidiary projects also depends on development progress.

Slowdown in overseas and domestic markets

Overseas revenue fell 23.0% year on year to KRW 18.4 billion in Q3 2026, and domestic revenue also declined to KRW 17.6 billion. The domestic game market's slump is repeatedly cited in earnings coverage. There is a risk that the overseas expansion which offset domestic weakness loses its effect.

Overall view

Webzen's filed results show revenue and operating profit trending down since 2022, apart from a rebound in 2024. Revenue fell from KRW 242.1 billion in 2022 to KRW 174.4 billion in 2025, and operating profit from KRW 83 billion to KRW 29.7 billion.

Quarterly revenue has declined for three consecutive quarters through Q3 2026, and Q3 operating profit of KRW 5.39 billion was down 29.3% year on year, which the company attributes to falling Mu IP sales and a gap in new releases.

Mu-related revenue remained about 62% of game revenue in Q3 2026, so single-IP dependence is still high. On the financial side, the debt ratio was 12.3% and total equity KRW 683.9 billion at end-2025, and shareholder-return measures have been reported.

Bull factors are the 2027 lineup led by The Defense Game Tyrant: LAST STAGE and MU THEOS, and an overseas share that has held near half of revenue. Bear factors are that major titles are clustered in 2027 and that the operating margin has trended lower.

The next checkpoints are the reception of the demo at G-STAR 2026 on November 19–22, the full-year figures in the 2026 annual report, and whether The Defense Game Tyrant launches in the first half of 2027.
