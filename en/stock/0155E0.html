Haechitech: Testing Whether Non-Mobile Sensors Can Lift Results

Haechitech has built a large share in smartphone magnetic sensors, but operating losses widened in 2025 and continued into the third quarter of 2026, so whether non-mobile applications convert into earnings is the central test.

02

Business structure

Haechitech is a fabless semiconductor company founded in 2017 that designs sensor integrated circuits. Its chips detect changes in magnetic fields to measure position, direction, angle, speed and current, while wafer fabrication and back-end processes are outsourced to external manufacturers.

The company says it retains in-house capabilities in device and circuit design, algorithms, mass-production testing and quality control. Geomagnetic sensors accounted for 63.7% of 2025 sales, followed by digital magnetic sensors at 31.6%, temperature and humidity sensors at 3% and analog magnetic sensors at 1%.

The core geomagnetic product is used in the electronic compasses of smartphones, and the company states that product life runs 10 to 15 years. Mobile dependence is high, with mobile making up 85% of 2024 revenue.

The company said its share among major global smartphone makers in Korea rose from 0.3% in 2022 to 43% in 2025. Customers have expanded to Chinese smartphone makers OPPO and vivo, European premium appliance makers such as Philips and De'Longhi, and a domestic memory chip company.

The company ships about 200 million sensor ICs a year and says cumulative shipments exceed 700 million units. It also reports mass-production yields above 98% for Hall switches and geomagnetic sensors.

After the listing, the company plans to broaden applications into robot encoder ICs, data center temperature sensors and current sensors for EV and energy storage systems.

04

Earnings analysis

On a disclosed basis, revenue fell 13.1% year on year, from KRW 16.2 billion in 2024 to KRW 14.1 billion in 2025. The company attributed the decline to its response to supply-chain adjustments.

The operating loss widened from KRW 275 million in 2024 to KRW 2.4 billion in 2025, and the operating margin moved from -1.7% to -17.0%. The company has posted losses for two consecutive years, from 2024 through 2025.

The net loss attributable to controlling shareholders widened from KRW 54.78 million in 2024 to KRW 8.03 billion in 2025, and the total net loss was the same KRW 8.03 billion. The net loss is far larger than the operating loss, which is a notable feature of the 2025 results.

Press coverage pointed to R&D spending as a factor behind the operating loss. Operating cash flow swung from KRW 1.66 billion in 2024 to -KRW 1.53 billion in 2025, moving from cash generation to cash outflow. The balance sheet has changed, however.

Press reports say the company was in full capital impairment at the end of 2024, and that total equity recovered to KRW 7.97 billion at the end of 2025 after preferred shares converted into common stock and a rights offering.

Total liabilities at the end of 2025 were KRW 6.06 billion, and the debt ratio moved from -545.2% in 2024, reflecting negative equity, to 76.0% in 2025.

On a quarterly basis, the third quarter of 2026 showed revenue of KRW 4.55 billion, an operating loss of KRW 497 million and a net loss attributable to controlling shareholders of KRW 1 billion.

The cumulative net loss attributable to controlling shareholders over the last four quarters (first quarter to fourth quarter of 2025) totaled KRW 8.03 billion. Press reports say monthly operating profit turned positive from May 2026, but the operating loss continued through the third quarter of 2026.

05

Industry analysis

Haechitech's business rests on sensors for smartphone electronic compasses. The company plans to reduce mobile's share of revenue from 85% in 2024 to 40% by 2030, while lifting industrial and appliance shares to 26% each and mobility to 8%.

In robotics it is developing encoder ICs that measure joint position and rotation angle, and it supplies high-voltage Hall switches for robot joint motors to a Chinese motor maker. In data centers it has started mass production of temperature sensors used in enterprise SSDs from a domestic memory chip company.

It is also developing high-precision current sensors for EV battery management, energy storage and data center power management. A KB Securities analyst, as reported in August 2026, forecast that the non-mobile share of revenue would rise from about 25% in 2026 to 44.2% in 2028.

A Yuanta Securities analyst assessed a competitive edge built on technology, mass production and customer qualification. The company says sensor products can take more than five years to pass customer qualification and reach volume production, which it cites as a barrier to entry.

Peers named in the IPO valuation included Analog Integrations Corp (AIC), Elmos Semiconductor and Melexis. All are fabless semiconductor design companies, but AIC focuses on power management chips while Haechitech focuses on chips that sense motion and environment.

06

Outlook

The company set a 2026 revenue target of KRW 21.1 billion and, in the same August 2026 reporting, expected a 2026 operating margin of around -2%. Disclosed third-quarter 2026 revenue was KRW 4.55 billion, and whether the annual target is met will be visible in the 2026 annual business report.

The medium-term targets call for an operating profit in 2027 of KRW 4.3 billion on revenue of KRW 31.7 billion, and for 2028 revenue of KRW 46.1 billion with an estimated net profit of KRW 11.3 billion.

A 2030 target of KRW 76.4 billion in revenue and KRW 23.4 billion in operating profit has also been reported, with a 37.9% average annual revenue growth rate from 2026 to 2030 and operating margins in the 30% range. All of these are company targets, not confirmed results. Product timelines follow.

The company said in August 2026 that it planned to begin supplying temperature sensors to a domestic memory chip maker from the second half of 2026. Current sensor development has been underway with a Swiss company since 2019. Medical sensors are being prepared for market entry after about two years of development.

In its securities filing the company stated that its estimated revenue and profit could change, and it noted that for technology-growth IPOs over the past three years the gap between estimates and actual results had been substantial.

The next checkpoints are whether 2026 revenue reaches KRW 21.1 billion and whether operating profit turns positive in 2027.

Bull factors

Share gains in mobile and a proven mass-production base

Cumulative shipments have passed 700 million units, and it reports mass-production yields above 98% for Hall switches and geomagnetic sensors. It attributes this to in-house design through mass-production testing. Volume production proven through customer qualification can act as a barrier for new entrants.

Non-mobile applications moving into production

The company says it has started mass production of temperature sensors for enterprise SSDs at a domestic memory chip maker for data center use. In robotics it supplies high-voltage Hall switches for robot joint motors to a Chinese motor maker.

Encoder ICs and current sensors for EVs and energy storage are still in development. The company says more than half of its R&D staff and spending is directed to non-mobile fields.

External technology ratings and broker forecasts

Press reports say the company's core technology, a high-precision three-dimensional magnetic sensor single-chip system, received A ratings from Korea Technology Finance Corporation and NICE Information Service.

A KB Securities analyst forecast in August 2026 that the non-mobile share of revenue would rise from about 25% in 2026 to 44.2% in 2028. A Yuanta Securities analyst cited a competitive edge built on technology, mass production and customer qualification. These are assessments by brokers and rating agencies, and results have not yet confirmed them.

Bear factors

Continued losses and a wider net loss

The operating loss widened from KRW 275 million in 2024 to KRW 2.4 billion in 2025, marking two consecutive years of losses from 2024 through 2025. The net loss attributable to controlling shareholders widened from KRW 54.78 million to KRW 8.03 billion over the same period.

Third-quarter 2026 disclosures show an operating loss of KRW 497 million and a net loss attributable to controlling shareholders of KRW 1 billion. The cumulative figure for the last four quarters is KRW 8.03 billion. The shift to profit still sits at a future target date, which is the core of the bear case.

A gap between projections and realized results

The company projects KRW 46.1 billion in 2028 revenue, against KRW 14.1 billion in 2025. Press reports say realized revenue as of end-May 2026 was KRW 7.4 billion, or 35.1% of the KRW 21.1 billion annual projection. The securities filing also notes that estimated revenue and profit may change.

Because the IPO valuation relied on estimated future profit, whether the gap between projections and results narrows is the key test for the bear case.

Mobile concentration and the 2025 revenue decline

Mobile accounted for 85% of 2024 revenue, and geomagnetic sensors made up 63.7% of 2025 sales. Revenue fell 13.1% in 2025, and the company cited its response to supply-chain adjustments. The bear case argues that share gains in mobile did not offset that decline.

Whether the plan to cut mobile's share to 40% by 2030 translates into an actual shift in revenue mix remains to be seen.

Risk factors

Financial cushion

Equity was in full capital impairment at the end of 2024 and recovered to KRW 7.97 billion at the end of 2025. Press reports say the recovery came from preferred-share conversion and a rights offering, and that accumulated deficit remained KRW 12.5 billion as of end-March 2026.

With operating losses continuing, how long the equity cushion holds will show up in upcoming periodic reports. The debt ratio also rose to 76.0% in 2025 from negative territory in 2024.

Post-listing supply overhang

Press reports say institutions were allocated 750,000 shares but applied for 710,000, leaving 40,000 shares that DB Securities, the lead underwriter, took on itself. The lock-up commitment ratio was 15.08%, with no six-month commitments.

A put-back right runs for six months from listing, and shares free to trade immediately after listing equal 38.35% of the total. Four major shareholders, including the largest shareholder, hold 44.49% under a three-year lock-up.

Customer concentration and qualification risk

The company says sensor products can take more than five years to clear customer qualification and reach volume production. That sets a high barrier to entry, but when results depend on a few customers or product lines, any loss or volume change has an outsized effect.

Press coverage has flagged customer concentration and inventory risk as items to watch. If non-mobile applications do not convert into volume revenue, the mobile-centered structure will persist.

Overall view

Haechitech is a sensor IC fabless company founded in 2017 that has substantially raised its share among major global smartphone makers in Korea for smartphone geomagnetic sensors. Its manufacturing base, including mass-production yields and cumulative shipments, is presented as a strength.

On the other side, revenue fell 13.1% to KRW 14.1 billion in 2025 and the operating loss widened to KRW 2.4 billion. Disclosed third-quarter 2026 results still show an operating loss of KRW 497 million, so a profitable run has not yet been confirmed.

The company targets a 2027 operating profit and KRW 46.1 billion in 2028 revenue, but press reports put realized sales at 35.1% of the 2026 target as of end-May 2026, making the gap between projections and results the central question.

In non-mobile areas, data center temperature sensor mass production has started, and robot encoder and current sensor development is ongoing, while brokers forecast the non-mobile share rising into the 40% range by 2028.

On the financial side, total equity recovered to KRW 7.97 billion at the end of 2025, but accumulated deficits remain. After the listing, supply factors such as the put-back right and low lock-up commitments drew coverage.

The next checkpoints are the annual revenue and operating results in the 2026 annual business report and the trend in the operating loss in the first quarter of 2027. This report does not present an investment opinion or a target price.
