Haechitech: Sensor Fabless Navigates Diversification and Losses

Haechitech is expanding from smartphone geomagnetic sensors into robot, current, and temperature sensors, but it posted operating and net losses in both 2024 and 2025.

  1. 1

    2025 revenue fell 13.1% year over year to KRW 14.1 billion, while the operating loss widened from KRW 275 million to KRW 2.4 billion.

  2. 2

    Net loss attributable to controlling shareholders jumped from KRW 54.78 million in 2024 to KRW 8.03 billion in 2025, reflecting non-operating factors including derivative valuation losses tied to redeemable convertible preferred shares.

  3. 3

    The company completed a technology-track IPO on KOSDAQ in August 2026, and the stock has since traded below the IPO price of KRW 23,000.

  4. 4

  5. 5

02

Business structure

Founded in 2017, Haechitech is a fabless semiconductor sensor IC company that outsources wafer fabrication and packaging to external foundries and subcontractors.

Its core products are geomagnetic sensors used as electronic compasses in smartphones, alongside digital and analog magnetic sensors, Hall sensors, and temperature/humidity sensors. Geomagnetic sensors currently account for 63.7% of revenue.

The company's current core business is semiconductor sensors attached to mobile phones, holding a 43% share of the domestic smartphone market. Its primary customers are Android-camp smartphone makers in Korea and China, and overseas customers account for more than 96% of revenue, reflecting high export dependence.

However, revenue from a specific customer fell sharply from KRW 7.3 billion in 2024 to KRW 2.6 billion, contributing to the decline in annual revenue.

In terms of competitive positioning, domestic ABOV Semiconductor along with Belgium's Melexis, Germany's Elmos Semiconductor, and Taiwan's Analog Integrations are cited as comparables, as the IPO price was set using four domestic and overseas sensor semiconductor peers—ABOV Semiconductor, Analog Integrations of Taiwan, Elmos Semiconductor of Germany, and Melexis of Belgium—as comparable companies.

The company is expanding into encoder ICs for robot joints, current sensors for electric vehicles, and temperature sensors for servers and data centers, and has been co-developing current sensors with a Swiss firm since 2019, anticipating rising demand as the electric vehicle market expands.

04

Earnings analysis

Haechitech's consolidated revenue fell 13.1% from KRW 16.2 billion in 2024 to KRW 14.1 billion in 2025. The operating loss widened from KRW 275 million in 2024 to KRW 2.4 billion in 2025, marking operating losses in both years. The operating margin deteriorated sharply from -1.7% in 2024 to -17.0% in 2025.

Net loss attributable to controlling shareholders surged from KRW 54.78 million in 2024 to KRW 8.03 billion in 2025, and total net loss including non-controlling interests was likewise KRW 8.03 billion.

The much larger expansion of net loss relative to operating loss was driven in part by the fact that derivative valuation losses tied to redeemable convertible preferred shares rose sharply to KRW 3.4 billion ahead of the listing, inflating the net loss, and such losses are financial in nature and temporary.

Operating cash flow swang from a net inflow of KRW 1.66 billion in 2024 to a net outflow of KRW 1.53 billion in 2025, indicating weaker cash generation.

On the balance sheet, the debt-to-equity ratio was -545.2% at end-2024, reflecting full capital impairment, but total equity was negative KRW 2.8 billion at end-2024 under full capital impairment, which was resolved last year through the conversion of redeemable convertible preferred shares into common stock and a rights offering.

The debt-to-equity ratio improved to 76.0% at end-2025, with total equity of KRW 7.97 billion, entirely attributable to controlling shareholders. The sum of controlling-shareholder net losses across the most recent four quarters (Q1-Q4 2025) was KRW 8.03 billion, matching the full-year 2025 figure.

05

Industry analysis

The semiconductor sensor IC market is expanding beyond smartphones into robots, electric vehicles, and data centers. Through its listing, Haechitech has laid out plans to expand into encoder IC development needed for precise motion control in physical AI applications.

The company positions itself as having localized geomagnetic sensor technology, noting that it acquired the sensor business of Magnachip Semiconductor, formerly the non-memory division of SK hynix.

Its IPO lead underwriter, a DB Securities official, said the company supplies geomagnetic sensor chips to Samsung Electronics domestically and that its technology assessment results were favorable.

However, the global sensor semiconductor market is competitive, with long-established players such as Belgium's Melexis, Germany's Elmos Semiconductor, and Taiwan's Analog Integrations.

In the mobile market, demand swings and customer-specific order changes add to revenue volatility, and indeed a decline in revenue from a specific customer was one factor behind the drop in full-year 2025 revenue.

New applications in robotics, EVs, and servers have not yet reached a meaningful revenue share, meaning time is needed before they can offset volatility in the existing mobile business.

06

Outlook

At its IPO briefing, the company set 2030 targets of KRW 76.4 billion in revenue and KRW 23.4 billion in operating profit, with plans to cut the mobile revenue mix from 85% in 2024 to 40% by 2030 while raising industrial and appliance mix to 26% and mobility to 8%.

For 2026, the company had presented a target of KRW 21.1 billion in revenue and an operating loss of KRW 300 million; this is a company-stated goal, not a figure confirmed in a regular disclosure.

Among new products, the company completed development of an encoder IC that controls the rotation angle and position of robot joints and expects to secure customer evaluation samples in October, while also finalizing fourth-quarter mass production of a temperature sensor for enterprise SSDs (Etoday, October 3, 2026).

Based on a preliminary disclosure (per an IR presentation on August 5, 2026), the company reportedly posted first-half revenue of KRW 9.2 billion and an operating loss of KRW 700 million, and a later report noted that first-half 2026 revenue rose year over year but the operating loss continued (Job Post, October 26, 2026).

These figures are preliminary media reports rather than a formal semiannual disclosure and may differ from eventual confirmed filings.

Bull factors

Diversification Strategy Moving Into Products

The completed development of a robot-joint encoder IC and progress toward mass production of a temperature sensor for SSDs show non-mobile applications entering actual product stages. Current sensors have been co-developed with a Swiss firm since 2019.

The company has stated that more than 50% of its R&D headcount and spending is now directed at non-mobile areas, backing its diversification intent with concrete figures.

Balance Sheet Repair

The debt-to-equity ratio, which reflected full capital impairment at end-2024, improved to 76.0% by end-2025 through the conversion of redeemable convertible preferred shares into common stock and a rights offering. Total equity also rose to KRW 7.97 billion, moving the company out of capital impairment. IPO proceeds provide additional funding capacity for R&D investment.

Localization Technology and Existing Customer Base

Haechitech points to its localization of geomagnetic sensor technology, built on its history of having acquired the sensor business of Magnachip Semiconductor, formerly the non-memory division of SK hynix, and states that it supplies geomagnetic sensor chips to Samsung Electronics domestically.

The company explains that product lifespans of 10 to 15 years create a structure of accumulating stable revenue.

Bear factors

Two Consecutive Years of Losses

The company posted both operating and net losses in 2024 and 2025, with the loss size actually widening in 2025. The operating margin fell to -17.0%, showing clear profitability deterioration. Operating cash flow also swung to a net outflow of KRW 1.53 billion in 2025.

Customer Concentration and Mobile Dependence

Revenue from a specific customer reportedly fell from KRW 7.3 billion in 2024 to KRW 2.6 billion in 2025, driving the annual revenue decline. With the mobile revenue mix still high, contributions from new applications have yet to become meaningful.

Post-Listing Supply Overhang

Shares available for trading one month after listing were reported at about 2.67 million, equivalent to 48.3% of total shares outstanding. The stock traded down 24.35% from the IPO price at KRW 17,400 on its first trading day, falling below the offering price from the outset.

10

Risk factors

Delayed Profitability Recovery Risk

The company has targeted a smaller operating loss in 2026 and a return to profitability in 2027, but its securities registration statement itself acknowledged the possibility that projected revenue could fluctuate or projected earnings might not be achieved due to unforeseen internal or external factors. If mass production of new sensor products is delayed, losses could persist longer.

Customer and Revenue Concentration Risk

Overseas customers account for more than 96% of revenue, reflecting high export dependence, and there is a precedent of a single customer's order changes directly affecting results. Reliance on Android-camp smartphone makers means shifts in their orders flow directly into revenue.

Float Expansion and Supply-Demand Risk

Tradable shares one month after listing reached 48.3% of total shares outstanding, while the stake held by largest shareholder and CEO Choi Seong-min along with related parties stood at 48.7%.

Future lock-up releases could add selling pressure, and as a technology-track listing, supply-demand volatility could increase if projected earnings are not met.

12

Overall view

Haechitech is in a transition period, moving from a business structure concentrated in smartphone geomagnetic sensors toward broader applications in robots, current sensors, and temperature sensors.

It is a disclosed fact that the company posted operating and net losses in both 2024 and 2025, with the loss widening in 2025. At the same time, the conversion of redeemable convertible preferred shares into common stock, a rights offering, and IPO proceeds have moved the company out of full capital impairment.

New products such as the completed robot-joint encoder IC and the SSD temperature sensor moving toward mass production remain at early stages, with the scale and timing of actual revenue contribution still unconfirmed.

Revenue concentration in a specific customer and the supply overhang from early post-listing float are additional factors to weigh. Upcoming quarterly results and the pace at which new products convert to mass production will be key indicators of how the diversification strategy is progressing.
