02

Business structure

INFINITT Healthcare is focused on medical imaging information solutions, and its main revenue comes from selling PACS and 3D medical imaging software. An equity information site classifies its revenue mix as 100% software, with radiology and cardiology PACS cited as flagship products.

The company has long held the top position in the domestic PACS market, and on the basis of the Health Insurance Review and Assessment Service's list of hospitals, it is described as holding 75% of the top-tier hospital market and 70% of the general hospital market.

Its customers are mostly hospitals, and because the products store imaging data and provide reading environments, maintenance and service contracts tend to follow after installation. Service revenue reached KRW 36.8 billion in H1 2026, or 63.8% of total revenue, which shows this structure in the reported numbers.

Overseas sales are also growing, with European revenue rising 47.5% to KRW 6.8 billion in H1 2026 from KRW 4.6 billion a year earlier, the fastest growth among overseas regions. Reports say the product scope is widening into North American PACS with AI integration and digital pathology.

According to the company's profile, it has repeatedly been selected as a category leader in a global PACS evaluation. The competitive picture rests on a high share among large domestic hospitals, so growth depends on retaining existing customers and expanding overseas and into AI.

04

Earnings analysis

Annual revenue rose from KRW 88.3 billion in 2022 to KRW 89.4 billion in 2023 (up 1.3%), KRW 95.8 billion in 2024 (up 7.1%), and KRW 102.4 billion in 2025 (up 6.9%). Revenue has increased for three consecutive years from 2023 through 2025, and 2025 was the largest year of 2022 to 2025.

Operating profit fell from KRW 7.69 billion in 2022 to KRW 3.09 billion in 2023 (down 59.9%), the smallest year of the period, then rose to KRW 7.72 billion in 2024 (up 150.2%) and KRW 14.2 billion in 2025 (up 84.1%), growing two consecutive years from 2024 through 2025.

The operating margin was 8.7% in 2022, 3.5% in 2023, 8.1% in 2024 and 13.9% in 2025, the highest of the period. Controlling-shareholder net income rose from KRW 132 million in 2022 to KRW 16.5 billion in 2023 (up 12,411.0%) and KRW 37.8 billion in 2024 (up 129.6%), then fell to KRW 26.4 billion in 2025 (down 30.1%).

The small 2022 base explains why the growth rates look so large. In 2025 operating profit rose while controlling-shareholder net income fell, so operating performance and bottom-line results moved in opposite directions.

One analysis of the nine-month 2025 disclosure said profitability was constrained by a smaller reversal of inventory valuation losses and higher one-off costs.

Quarterly, the company moved from an operating loss of KRW 1.54 billion and revenue of KRW 22.1 billion in Q3 2025 to operating profit of KRW 11.6 billion and revenue of KRW 30 billion in Q4 2025.

Operating profit then declined for three consecutive quarters, to KRW 9.47 billion in Q1 2026, KRW 4.93 billion in Q2 and KRW 4.18 billion in Q3. Q3 2026 revenue was KRW 25.9 billion, up 17.4% year on year, but controlling-shareholder net income swung from KRW 25.2 billion in Q2 to a KRW 16.9 billion net loss in Q3.

Controlling-shareholder net income for the latest four quarters totals KRW 33.7 billion. In H1 2026, the cost of revenue ratio fell from 38.6% to 32.4% and selling and administrative expenses declined, which lifted operating profit sharply.

05

Industry analysis

Demand for medical imaging software continues alongside the expansion of digital clinical infrastructure in hospitals. One equity information site cited rising demand for medical software and sustained demand for patient monitoring and data management as the background to the earnings recovery.

In the domestic market the company is described as holding high shares among both top-tier and general hospitals, so revenue builds more from maintaining and upgrading existing customers than from competing for new orders.

This structure produces stable service revenue, but with domestic share already high, room for top-line growth tends to shift toward overseas markets and new products.

Since the operating profit trough in 2023, results have been in a recovery phase through 2024 and 2025, and revenue and operating profit both rose in H1 2026. In cycle terms, demand looks closer to a gradual pattern driven by hospital IT budgets and policy changes than to an equipment investment cycle.

That pattern carries the limitation that order timing can slip with hospital investment capacity and changes in healthcare policy. Overseas, PACS and AI-linked products are becoming the competitive focus in advanced markets such as North America and Europe, and European revenue grew fastest among overseas regions.

The competitive position will depend on whether a strong domestic share translates into a larger overseas share.

06

Outlook

Three growth axes are confirmed in the company's disclosures and reports. The first is overseas markets: in its Q1 2026 results commentary, expansion overseas and new products were cited as drivers, and European revenue rose 47.5% year on year in H1 2026.

The second is new products and AI, with reports saying the Smart Endo product has entered clinical settings and that the AI business is being strengthened. The third is product linkage, with North American PACS integrated with AI and expansion into digital pathology mentioned.

In H1 2026, expanded maintenance service contracts and cost efficiency were cited as drivers of operating profit improvement, and revenue for the same period rose 12.6% year on year. On shareholder returns, quarterly dividends run alongside the year-end dividend in the disclosure listings.

Still, Q3 2026 operating profit sits on the tail of three consecutive quarterly declines, and until the cause of the controlling-shareholder net loss is confirmed in a periodic report, the direction of earnings cannot be stated with confidence.

The next checkpoints are how quickly overseas revenue grows in share and whether AI-related products convert into revenue. The 2026 annual preliminary results expected in early 2027 and the 2026 annual report will show how the quarterly trend resolves at full-year level.

Earnings recovery and financial capacity

Controlling-shareholder net loss in Q3

Controlling-shareholder net loss in Q3 2026 was KRW 16.9 billion, ending the profitable run that began in Q4 2025. Operating profit stayed positive at KRW 4.18 billion over the same period, so operating and bottom-line results moved in different directions. Three straight quarters of lower operating profit

Operating profit peaked at KRW 11.6 billion in Q4 2025 and then fell for three consecutive quarters, to KRW 9.47 billion in Q1 2026, KRW 4.93 billion in Q2 and KRW 4.18 billion in Q3. Q3 operating profit fell 15.1% from the previous quarter. It remains well below the peak level.

Earnings volatility and one-off factors

Controlling-shareholder net income fell 30.1% from KRW 37.8 billion in 2024 to KRW 26.4 billion in 2025. The latest four quarters total KRW 33.7 billion, but the quarterly figures vary widely, so a single quarter can change the trend.

The nine-month 2025 disclosure analysis cited a smaller inventory valuation reversal and higher one-off costs as limits on profitability.

Risk factors

Earnings volatility and one-off items

In the nine months of 2025, inventory valuation reversals shrank and one-off costs rose. Operating profit rose 84.1% in 2025 while controlling-shareholder net income fell 30.1%, which suggests non-operating items may have grown in weight. Whether the Q3 2026 net loss was one-off or recurring must be confirmed in the periodic report.

Governance and litigation

A ruling confirming the invalidity of a resolution from an extraordinary shareholders' meeting tied to a June 23, 2025 meeting appears in the disclosures. The ruling outcome, the company's response and whether costs have been recognized are matters for later disclosures. Governance issues can affect shareholder confidence separately from earnings.

Overseas expansion and dependence on healthcare policy

European revenue is growing, but country-level regulation and certification procedures can delay when results show up. Domestic revenue depends on hospital investment capacity and healthcare policy, which can shift order timing. Both axes appear as quarterly swings, so quarterly revenue has to be read together with them.

Overall view

INFINITT Healthcare is a medical imaging software company with a high share of the domestic PACS market, and its service revenue, 63.8% of the total in H1 2026, gives it a recurring revenue base.

Revenue has grown for three consecutive years from 2023 through 2025, and the 13.9% operating margin in 2025 was the highest of 2022 to 2025. The debt ratio was 27.8% at end-2025, so the balance sheet is not heavily leveraged.

Earnings direction is mixed, however, with controlling-shareholder net income declining in 2025 and turning into a net loss in Q3 2026.

Operating profit has fallen for three consecutive quarters since peaking in Q4 2025, and the detailed cause of the KRW 16.9 billion Q3 net loss has to be confirmed in the periodic report.

Overseas expansion and AI and new products are growth drivers, but with domestic share already high, room for top-line growth tends to shift overseas.

The next checkpoints are the 2026 annual preliminary results expected in early 2027 and the 2026 annual report, and the key question is where the operating margin and controlling-shareholder net income settle.
