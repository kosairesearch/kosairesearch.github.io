02

Business structure

Samsung Heavy Industries operates in two segments: commercial shipbuilding, which includes LNG carriers, crude oil tankers, container ships, ethane carriers and gas carriers, and offshore plants, where floating LNG production units (FLNG) are the core.

The 2026 order target of USD 13.9 billion is split into USD 5.7 billion for commercial vessels and USD 8.2 billion for offshore.

As of late August 2026, cumulative commercial orders stood at 36 vessels worth USD 6.1 billion, including 14 LNG carriers (one of them an LNG-FSRU), 12 crude oil tankers, four gas carriers, four container ships and two ethane carriers.

In offshore, two FLNG units were part of the cumulative orders, and offshore orders stood at USD 4.4 billion as of early July 2026. In the first half of 2026 the company signed FLNG contracts with Italy's ENI worth USD 2.39 billion and with Delfin Midstream of the United States worth USD 2.88 billion.

Its customers are spread across shipowners in Europe, Africa and Bermuda and overseas energy developers. The company is also pursuing floating small modular reactors and floating data centers.

In competition, it faces domestic large shipbuilders such as HD Korea Shipbuilding & Offshore Engineering, HD Hyundai Heavy Industries and Hanwha Ocean, as well as overseas yards building the same vessel types.

04

Earnings analysis

On an annual basis, revenue rose from KRW 5.94 trillion in 2022 to KRW 10.7 trillion in 2025, with three consecutive years of growth from 2023 through 2025. Among 2022 to 2025, 2025 was the largest year for revenue and 2022 the smallest.

Operating profit moved from an operating loss of KRW 854.4 billion in 2022 to a turn to profit at KRW 233.3 billion in 2023, then rose to KRW 502.7 billion in 2024 and KRW 862.2 billion in 2025, two straight years of gains.

The operating margin improved from -14.4% in 2022 to 2.9% in 2023, 5.1% in 2024 and 8.1% in 2025. Net income attributable to controlling shareholders went from a net loss of KRW 619.4 billion in 2022 and KRW 148.3 billion in 2023 to a profit of KRW 63.9 billion in 2024, then rose 754.1% to KRW 545.5 billion in 2025.

Net income including non-controlling interests in 2025 was KRW 535.8 billion, a different figure from the controlling-shareholder number. The debt ratio fell from 305.7% in 2022 to 265.0% in 2025, when total equity stood at KRW 4.1 trillion and total liabilities at KRW 10.9 trillion.

Operating cash flow shifted from negative KRW 1.69 trillion in 2022 to KRW 1.56 trillion in 2025. Third-quarter 2026 revenue of KRW 3.15 trillion rose 19.6% year on year, and operating profit of KRW 338.3 billion rose 42.1%.

Operating profit climbed from KRW 273.1 billion in 1Q 2026 to KRW 325 billion in 2Q and KRW 338.3 billion in 3Q, two straight quarters of gains, after a 7.8% quarterly dip in 1Q that marked the low point.

Controlling-shareholder net income of KRW 470.6 billion in 3Q rose 109.8% from the prior quarter, but the preliminary release of 29 October 2026 said pre-tax profit of KRW 610.7 billion included KRW 272.3 billion of non-operating income, including gains on the settlement of forward contracts, while the company attributed margin resilience to the full recognition of higher-value vessel sales.

The non-operating items therefore account for a meaningful part of the net income increase. Controlling-shareholder net income for the last four quarters combined totals KRW 893.8 billion.

05

Industry analysis

The commercial shipbuilding order cycle is showing signs of recovery. A July 2026 report said that surpassing USD 10 billion in annual orders was the first such outcome since 2021, a year in which commercial ordering had surged.

A company official said in late August 2026 that container ship ordering is concentrated in the 8,000 to 13,000 TEU class, driven by replacement demand for eco-friendly vessels. Cumulative orders of USD 12.4 billion as of 17 November 2026 include 20 LNG carriers and two FLNG units.

Industry commentary reads the commercial ordering cycle as reviving again. On profitability, HD Korea Shipbuilding & Offshore Engineering reported 3Q 2026 revenue of KRW 8.29 trillion, operating profit of KRW 1.45 trillion and an operating margin of about 17.5%.

Over the same period, Samsung Heavy Industries' operating margin was 10.7%, a gap that likely reflects differences in offshore weighting, vessel mix and the timing of revenue recognition across projects. The company says it is pursuing commercial and offshore orders in parallel to manage market volatility.

06

Outlook

The company's 2026 revenue guidance is KRW 12.8 trillion. Cumulative revenue for the first three quarters was KRW 9.3 trillion on the preliminary basis, so how much 4Q revenue contributes toward the guidance is the year-end checkpoint.

Against the USD 13.9 billion annual order target, cumulative orders were USD 12.4 billion as of 17 November 2026, leaving about USD 1.5 billion to fill. The company said it will accelerate additional offshore orders centered on FLNG in the second half.

A January 2026 report had anticipated more than USD 7 billion of FLNG orders during the year, and the ENI and Delfin Midstream contracts signed in June made part of that pipeline concrete. In January 2026 the company announced the launch of Coral Norte FLNG, scheduled for delivery in 2028.

Because the commercial order target was exceeded early, the company said it will focus on selective orders with better profitability. Floating data centers are viewed as a medium-term order challenge, so whether related orders materialize is the next point to track.

08

Bull factors

Expanding order book

Cumulative 2026 orders reached USD 12.4 billion, about 89% of the USD 13.9 billion target. Commercial orders surpassed their annual target early, including 20 LNG carriers. Large FLNG contracts with ENI and Delfin Midstream support the order expansion.

Earnings recovery

Operating profit turned positive in 2023, rose in both 2024 and 2025, and increased again in 2Q and 3Q 2026. The operating margin moved from 8.1% in 2025 to the low double digits in 3Q 2026. Controlling-shareholder net income also turned to profit in 2024 and grew sharply in 2025.

Improving balance sheet

The debt ratio fell from 305.7% in 2022 to 265.0% in 2025. Operating cash flow turned from negative KRW 1.69 trillion in 2022 to positive KRW 1.56 trillion in 2025. The KRW 12.8 trillion revenue guidance sits above the KRW 10.7 trillion recorded in 2025.

09

Bear factors

Reliance on non-operating gains

Third-quarter pre-tax profit of KRW 610.7 billion included KRW 272.3 billion of non-operating income. Compared with operating profit of KRW 338.3 billion, non-operating items account for a large share of the earnings increase. Those gains include forward contract settlements, which are currency-related and not clearly recurring.

Margin gap with peers

The 3Q 2026 operating margin of 10.7% was below the roughly 17.5% reported by HD Korea Shipbuilding & Offshore Engineering. Operating profit fell 7.8% quarter on quarter in 1Q 2026, so the quarterly path has been uneven. Whether this profitability gap narrows is one measure of the earnings base.

Concentration in offshore orders

Of the USD 13.9 billion annual order target, USD 8.2 billion is for offshore, a larger share than commercial. Analysts have noted that reaching the total target depends on the timing of FLNG contracts. A delay in large offshore contracts could weigh on achieving the annual order target.

10

Risk factors

FX and hedging gains

Contracts are signed in dollars, so exchange-rate moves affect revenue and profit. Third-quarter results included forward contract settlement gains in non-operating income. A reversal in exchange rates could widen swings in non-operating results and net income.

Project execution and cost

Large offshore projects recognize revenue as construction progresses, so cost changes or schedule delays can move quarterly results. Working days fell in 3Q 2026 because of summer holidays and the Chuseok break, reducing revenue. Seasonal factors combined with project progress can increase quarterly swings.

Ordering cycle and contract timing

Commercial ordering moves with global shipping conditions and shipowners' investment decisions. FLNG contract timing depends on customers' final investment decisions. A slowdown in ordering or a delay in large contracts would affect both the order target and later revenue flow.

What to watch next

  1. Late December 2026

    The checkpoint is whether the remaining gap between USD 12.4 billion in cumulative orders and the USD 13.9 billion target is filled. In offshore, additional FLNG contracts in the second half are the key.

  2. Late December 2026

    The KRW 12.8 trillion revenue guidance will be tested by how much 4Q revenue adds to the KRW 9.3 trillion cumulative figure for the first three quarters.

  3. Early 2027

    The 4Q and full-year 2026 preliminary results will show whether the operating margin holds near the 3Q level and how large non-operating items are within net income.

  4. By late March 2027

    The 2026 annual report is the next regular filing and will confirm full-year results, operating cash flow and the debt ratio. The filing deadline is 90 days after fiscal year-end.

12

Overall view

Samsung Heavy Industries moved from an operating loss in 2022 to KRW 862.2 billion in operating profit in 2025, and operating profit rose again in 2Q and 3Q 2026. Cumulative 2026 orders of USD 12.4 billion equal about 89% of the annual target, built mainly on commercial vessels and FLNG.

On the other side, a KRW 272.3 billion non-operating gain contributed a meaningful part of 3Q net income, so the operating trend should be read separately from the net income trend.

The operating margin has moved into the low double digits, but a gap remains against peers such as HD Korea Shipbuilding & Offshore Engineering.

The items to track are the remaining order target, whether 4Q 2026 revenue reaches the KRW 12.8 trillion guidance, and the full-year results to be confirmed in the 2026 annual report. This report is provided for information only and does not include a buy or sell opinion.
