Capstone Partners: Earnings Swing With Fund Exit Timing

02

Business structure

Capstone Partners is a Korean venture capital firm founded in 2008 that forms venture investment partnerships and invests in small and mid-sized startups.

A 2025 press report described management fees earned from partnership operations as its core revenue source, while equity-method gains from partnerships, fair value gains and disposal gains on investments move its profit more.

Its first-half 2026 standalone operating profit growth was attributed to new partnership formation and higher equity-method gains.

Major funds include the Capstone 2026 AI Innovation Investment Partnership, formed in April 2026 with KRW 50 billion, and the Capstone 2025 Deep Tech Camp Investment Partnership, a KRW 22.5 billion fund under the Korea Growth Finance program formed in August 2025.

A June 2026 report put assets under management at about KRW 576.5 billion. Among its holdings, Karrot is cited as the core asset, and broker analysis also named DeepX, Wrtn and Rebellions as major investments.

The firm focuses on early-stage investments in AI, deep tech, mobility and ICT, and in early 2026 it reportedly made a KRW 500 million Pre-A investment alone in an AI platform developer. It had 16 employees as of September 2026.

Among domestic venture capital firms in the same sector, it ranks in the lower tier, 16th out of 17, by market capitalization and revenue scale. Peers commonly mentioned alongside it include Tees Investment, Now IB Capital, Lindeman Asia Investment and Company K Partners.

04

Earnings analysis

Annual revenue moved from KRW 12.2 billion in 2022 to KRW 9.51 billion in 2023 (down 22.2% year on year), KRW 9.69 billion in 2024 (up 1.9%) and KRW 7.47 billion in 2025 (down 22.9%). Among 2022-2025, 2022 was the largest year and 2025 the smallest.

Operating profit of KRW 7.1 billion in 2022 turned into an operating loss of KRW 2.43 billion in 2023, which marked a swing back into the red, then returned to a profit of KRW 2.2 billion in 2024 and rose 6.5% to KRW 2.34 billion in 2025.

The operating margin was 58.1% in 2022, -25.6% in 2023, 22.7% in 2024 and 31.3% in 2025, so margins recovered in 2024-2025 even as revenue shrank.

Net income attributable to controlling shareholders was KRW 6.12 billion in 2022, a net loss of KRW 4.32 billion in 2023, KRW 1.83 billion in 2024 and KRW 2.3 billion in 2025 (up 25.7%).

Total net income including non-controlling interests was reported as KRW 6.12 billion, a net loss of KRW 4.32 billion, KRW 1.83 billion and KRW 2.3 billion for the same years.

On a quarterly basis, Q3 2025 revenue of KRW 2.9 billion, operating profit of KRW 1.72 billion and controlling-shareholder net income of KRW 1.39 billion fell sharply in Q4 2025, when revenue was KRW 1.25 billion (down 57.1% from the prior quarter), an operating loss of KRW 134 million was recorded and controlling-shareholder net income was KRW 299 million.

Q1 2026 revenue recovered to KRW 1.54 billion with operating profit of KRW 387 million, and Q2 2026 revenue rose to KRW 2.2 billion (up 42.5% quarter on quarter) with operating profit of KRW 1.08 billion (up 179.6%).

Q3 2026 revenue was KRW 1.65 billion (down 24.9% quarter on quarter and 43.0% year on year), operating profit was KRW 426 million (down 60.6% quarter on quarter and 75.2% year on year), and controlling-shareholder net income was KRW 350 million (down 74.9% year on year).

Net income attributable to controlling shareholders over the last four quarters (Q4 2025 to Q3 2026) totaled KRW 1.8 billion, below the KRW 2.3 billion for full-year 2025. Quarterly profit moves with the timing of fund exits and disposals, so the gaps between quarters are wide.

At the end of 2025, total liabilities were KRW 638 million, the debt ratio was 18.6%, and total equity was KRW 34.4 billion, all attributable to controlling shareholders. Operating cash flow was -KRW 3.88 billion in 2022, KRW 3.22 billion in 2023, -KRW 2.88 billion in 2024 and KRW 1.01 billion in 2025.

05

Industry analysis

Korea's venture investment market is described as entering a recovery phase in fund formation. NH Investment & Securities analyzed in June 2026 that cumulative new venture capital fund formation through April 2026 reached KRW 3.4 trillion, approaching the record pace of 2022 on an annualized basis.

The same analysis expected Capstone Partners' position in the venture sector to strengthen as public funding for venture firms increased. A 2025 press review, by contrast, noted that the venture investment industry as a whole was weak and that fundraising was difficult.

Management fees, the core revenue of a venture capital firm, fall when funds are liquidated, so forming new funds is essential to sustaining the fee base.

In an industry comparison on an annual basis, the firm's operating margin of 31.30% was below the venture investment industry average of 34.42%, and its debt ratio of 18.57% was below the industry average of 33.51%. The same source showed its dividend yield below the industry average.

On the portfolio side, the CEO's view that startup formation is shifting from platforms toward AI and deep tech lines up with the firm's stated investment focus. Its position within the industry depends on the mix of fund formation results and liquidation gains.

Outlook

NH Investment & Securities said it plans to set up at least two new funds this year, including ones of KRW 50 billion and KRW 60 billion, focused on mobility, AI and ICT, with about 75% of capital going to early-stage investments.

The firm is also competing to be selected as a fund manager in several public investment programs, including a national growth fund program.

On the realization side, the broker expects about one year for the liquidation of the fund holding Karrot, and it expected disposal gains from partial sales of Karrot and DeepX stakes in the second half of 2026. Two portfolio companies awaiting IPOs were named, and their listings could lift equity-method gains.

New partnership formation and higher equity-method gains were cited as drivers of first-half 2026 operating profit. In the 2026 annual report, the next regular filing, the scale of new fund formation and disposal results will show how these items feed into full-year figures.

Bull factors

Expected investment realizations

In a June 2026 analysis, NH Investment & Securities expected disposal gains from partial sales of Karrot and DeepX stakes in the second half. The same analysis described Karrot as the core holding of a fund due for liquidation. One report cited a last-round valuation for Karrot of about KRW 3 trillion. If these gains are recognized, operating margin has room to improve, which is cited as a bullish argument.

Balance sheet and margin recovery

Total liabilities at the end of 2025 were KRW 638 million, with a debt ratio of 18.6%. Total equity was KRW 34.4 billion, all attributable to controlling shareholders. The operating margin rose from 22.7% in 2024 to 31.3% in 2025, and the margin recovery despite lower revenue is cited as a strength.

Expanding fund formation

Assets under management were KRW 576.5 billion in a June 2026 report, and the company targets an increase of more than KRW 100 billion within 2026. A KRW 50 billion AI innovation fund was formed in April 2026.

New partnership formation and higher equity-method gains were cited as factors behind first-half 2026 operating profit growth.

09

Bear factors

Shrinking revenue and volatile quarters

Annual revenue was KRW 7.47 billion in 2025, below KRW 12.2 billion in 2022. Q3 2026 revenue of KRW 1.65 billion was down 43.0% from a year earlier.

Net income attributable to controlling shareholders totaled KRW 1.8 billion over the last four quarters, and the size of quarter-to-quarter swings is cited as a bearish point.

Weaker management fee base

A 2025 press report noted that management fees disappear as funds are liquidated, and that new fund formation had stalled, weakening the fee base. The same report said that the decline in management fees could deepen as more funds reach maturity.

A new partnership was formed in April 2026, and whether the concern eases will show up in future quarterly management fee trends.

Risk of delayed realizations

NH Investment & Securities estimated that liquidating the fund holding Karrot would take about one year. No fund was liquidated in Q1 2026, which was attributed partly to some assets falling short of target returns.

If realizations are delayed, disposal gain recognition would also slip, which could widen quarterly earnings swings.

10

Risk factors

Changing listing requirements

A 2025 press report said the Korea Exchange is gradually tightening KOSDAQ delisting criteria, with the market capitalization threshold rising to KRW 30 billion by 2028. The revenue threshold is set to rise to KRW 10 billion in 2029.

Revenue in 2025 was KRW 7.47 billion, below that level, and the current market capitalization is also close to the future threshold.

Reliance on valuation gains and disposals

Fair value changes in startup stakes and the timing of disposals drive profit and loss. A 2025 press report noted that the firm stayed profitable thanks to fair value gains and asset disposal gains. Such gains can vary widely with market conditions and IPO timing.

Fundraising and key-person concentration

Because fundraising across the venture industry has been described as difficult, whether planned new funds are actually formed is the key question. The firm relies heavily on its CEO, who serves as lead fund manager, and a small core investment team. Changes in that team could directly affect fund formation and investment pace.

Overall view

Capstone Partners is a venture capital firm founded in 2008. Based on DART-filed results, revenue fell to KRW 7.47 billion in 2025 while operating profit rose to KRW 2.34 billion.

Quarterly revenue in 2026 was KRW 1.54 billion in Q1, KRW 2.2 billion in Q2 and KRW 1.65 billion in Q3, and Q3 operating profit of KRW 426 million fell 75.2% from a year earlier. Net income attributable to controlling shareholders totaled KRW 1.8 billion over the last four quarters.

Bullish factors include expected disposal gains, assets under management of about KRW 576.5 billion and a stable capital structure. Bearish factors include shrinking revenue, a weakening management fee base and the possibility of delayed realizations.

Gradual tightening of listing requirements is also a medium-term variable. The next checkpoints are new fund formation by the end of December 2026, whether Q4 disposal gains are recognized, and the 2026 annual report due by the end of March 2027.
