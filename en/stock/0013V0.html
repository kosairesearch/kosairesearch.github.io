Testing Global Expansion Amid Off-Season Loss

02

Business structure

Founded in Busan in 1953, Samjin Food is Korea's longest-operating fish cake manufacturer, having taken its current corporate name after merging with Samjin Eomuk Co., Ltd. in 2020.

As of 2024, its product mix consisted of soup/stew-use products at 37.6%, side-dish products at 21.7%, packaged snack products at 20.7%, gift sets at 14.8%, and ready-to-eat snacks at 8.1%, while sales channels were split across retail (29.1%), B2B (27.0%), stores (21.4%), online (19.5%), and exports (2.8%).

The company operates two production bases, the Jangnim plant and the Gamcheon plant (Amazing Factory), with combined annual capacity of 11,104 tons, and runs an in-house value chain spanning R&D, raw material sourcing, production, distribution, and sales.

In 2013 it opened Korea's first fish-cake bakery store, elevating fish cake from a side-dish ingredient to a premium snack category, and this brand strength helped it rank first in the fish-based processed food category of Korea's Brand Power Index (K-BPI) for five consecutive years.

The domestic retail fish cake market remains an oligopoly where CJ Cheiljedang's "Samho Eomuk" and Sajo Daerim's "Daerimsun" lineups occupy most of the top-10 brand rankings, and Samjin Food has carved out its own position outside that structure through gift sets, bakery stores, and B2B channels.

Overseas, it operates fish-cake bakery stores in Singapore, Indonesia, and Vietnam and exports to 13 countries, while pursuing entry into Costco and Walmart in the United States on top of its existing H Mart presence.

The company listed on KOSDAQ on December 22, 2025, and has allocated KRW 11.822 billion of IPO proceeds to facility investment aimed at upgrading production equipment. In 2026 it has accelerated its push into Greater China, opening a directly operated flagship store in Changsha and running a pop-up store in Shanghai.

04

Earnings analysis

Full-year 2025 revenue came to KRW 109.5 billion, up 13.6% from KRW 96.4 billion in 2024, while operating profit rose 22.9% from KRW 4.84 billion to KRW 5.96 billion, lifting the operating margin from 5.0% to 5.4%.

Net income attributable to owners of the parent surged 408.2%, from KRW 1.02 billion in 2024 to KRW 5.2 billion in 2025, and total net income (including non-controlling interests) likewise rose from KRW 1.02 billion to KRW 5.19 billion, suggesting the jump stemmed primarily from improved core operating performance rather than non-operating items.

Operating cash flow also increased from KRW 6.27 billion in 2024 to KRW 8.24 billion in 2025, indicating the earnings improvement translated into actual cash generation. Quarterly results, however, show pronounced volatility.

First-quarter 2026 was strong, with revenue of KRW 33.3 billion, operating profit of KRW 3.89 billion, and net income attributable to owners of KRW 2.89 billion, but in the seasonally weak second quarter of 2026 revenue fell 35.5% quarter-on-quarter to KRW 21.5 billion, operating profit swung to an operating loss of KRW 524 million, and net income attributable to owners turned to a net loss of KRW 224 million.

The sum of net income attributable to owners over the trailing four quarters (Q1 2025 through Q4 2025) was KRW 5.2 billion, matching the full-year 2025 result.

At end-2025, total equity stood at KRW 41.7 billion (of which equity attributable to owners was KRW 41.9 billion) against total liabilities of KRW 41.4 billion, with the debt-to-equity ratio dropping sharply from 1,248.8% at end-2024 to 99.2% at end-2025, reflecting the capital-raising effect of the listing.

The swing to a loss in the second quarter of 2026 appears to reflect a combination of the fish cake industry's inherent summer seasonality along with raw material and foreign exchange cost pressure and one-off selling and administrative expenses tied to brand renewal initiatives.

05

Industry analysis

Korea's fish cake (processed fisheries product) market is an oligopoly in which CJ Cheiljedang and Sajo Daerim occupy most of the top-10 brand rankings, with the two companies' combined share reaching the mid-60% range.

Sajo Daerim has pushed into peak-season demand by expanding its "Daerimsun" lineup into soup, convenience, and camping-oriented products, and its own analysis found that average monthly sales of soup-type products rose about 87% from December 2025 through February 2026 compared with the preceding summer, confirming winter seasonality as a common feature across the market.

At the same time, Sajo Daerim's domestic fish cake and crab-stick revenue in the first half of 2026 fell 12.2% year-on-year to KRW 82.8 billion from KRW 94.3 billion, pointing to stagnating growth in the domestic retail fish cake market overall.

Samjin Food has maintained an independent position outside this large-food-company-led retail competition by focusing on bakery stores, premium gift sets, and B2B distribution.

The spread of the K-food wave from instant noodles and seaweed into K-street food items such as tteokbokki and gimbap is seen as an opportunity for protein-rich processed fisheries products like fish cake, though Japanese fish cake brands are said to retain strong recognition and market power overseas.

Samjin Food's overseas revenue mix stood at below 3% around the time of its listing, and its U.S. business has reportedly lagged, indicating that international growth remains at an early stage.

Still, 2026 has brought signs of change in the overseas business structure, with new entry into the Chinese distribution market and the completion of room-temperature fish cake development.

06

Outlook

The company has indicated plans to expand Chuseok gift set sales and continue export- and B2B-focused channel expansion in the second half of 2026, while preparing for winter demand in the traditionally strong fourth quarter.

Gift set revenue has grown steadily from KRW 9.3 billion in 2023 to KRW 11.4 billion in 2024 and KRW 13.3 billion in 2025, making it one of the key variables for fourth-quarter results.

Samjin Food has completed development of a room-temperature fish cake that does not require refrigerated or frozen distribution and is currently in export discussions with three countries, which management views as a core product for expanding into new overseas markets by reducing cold-chain burdens.

In China, following its first Changsha store, the company is running a Shanghai pop-up store and preparing to open a deli shop, while pursuing additional hotbar product supply to large local retail channels such as Costco and Freshippo.

The company said first-half 2026 export revenue reached KRW 2.463 billion, already about 73% of full-year 2025 export revenue of KRW 3.388 billion.

On the production side, KRW 11.822 billion of IPO proceeds was allocated to facility investment, of which KRW 361 million had been executed as of end-June 2026, with production line layout redesign, equipment investment, and logistics facility investment under review for the second half.

In Busan's Gwangalli area, the company is operating a flagship store in pop-up form with plans for a formal opening in February-March 2027, alongside a renovation of its Yeongdo headquarters store.

Over the longer term, the company has outlined plans to expand into marine-protein-based wellness food, positioning products such as a room-temperature fish cake bar as a strategic offering.

08

Bull factors

Accelerating Overseas Channel Expansion

Samjin Food's first Changsha store ranked No. 1 among newly opened Korean restaurant category listings on a major local review platform, and the company is preparing a Shanghai pop-up store and a deli shop.

With room-temperature fish cake development complete, cold-chain constraints have eased and export discussions are underway with three countries, while in the U.S. the company is pursuing Costco and Walmart entry on top of its H Mart presence.

First-half 2026 export revenue already reached about 73% of the full 2025 annual figure, pointing to an accelerating pace of overseas revenue growth.

Improved Financial Structure Post-Listing

Net income attributable to owners rose 408.2% year-on-year to KRW 5.2 billion in 2025 from KRW 1.02 billion in 2024, a marked improvement in profitability.

The debt-to-equity ratio also fell sharply from 1,248.8% at end-2024 to 99.2% at end-2025, reflecting the capital-raising effect of the listing on the balance sheet. Operating cash flow likewise rose from KRW 6.27 billion to KRW 8.24 billion, showing the earnings improvement was backed by actual cash generation.

Premium Brand Strength

Samjin Food has ranked first in the fish-based processed food category of Korea's Brand Power Index (K-BPI) for five consecutive years, maintaining an independent brand position even within a market dominated by large food companies.

A premium gift set launched in 2011 has reportedly sold out every major holiday since, and gift set revenue has grown steadily from KRW 9.3 billion in 2023 to KRW 13.3 billion in 2025.

Its pioneering fish-cake bakery store model is credited with creating a new consumption culture in the market, prompting competitors to follow suit.

Bear factors

Pronounced Seasonal Volatility

In the second quarter of 2026, revenue fell 35.5% quarter-on-quarter to KRW 21.5 billion, and the company swung to an operating loss of KRW 524 million and a net loss attributable to owners of KRW 224 million.

The fish cake industry has a structurally pronounced gap between summer off-season and winter peak-season demand, repeatedly producing large quarter-to-quarter swings in results. This volatility is a characteristic that must be weighed whenever quarterly performance is assessed.

Small Share in an Oligopolistic Market

Korea's domestic fish cake retail market is an oligopoly in which CJ Cheiljedang- and Sajo Daerim-affiliated brands occupy most of the top-10 rankings, and Samjin Food does not appear on that retail brand ranking.

Sajo Daerim continues to broaden its product range with items such as fish-cake noodles and camping-oriented products while pursuing aggressive marketing, intensifying competition.

Samjin Food's reliance on differentiated niche channels such as gift sets, bakery stores, and B2B means it has relatively limited exposure in large-scale retail channel competition.

Early-Stage Overseas Revenue Base

At the time of listing, overseas revenue accounted for less than 3% of the total, and the U.S. market had reportedly shown a relatively weak trend. Japanese fish cake brands are also said to retain strong recognition and market power overseas, suggesting new market entry may take time.

The China business also remains at an early testing stage with its Changsha store and Shanghai pop-up store, and the results of distribution network expansion have yet to become clearly visible.

10

Risk factors

Raw Material and FX Costs

In the second quarter of 2026, rising surimi prices, a higher won-dollar exchange rate, and geopolitical risk in the Middle East weighed on raw material procurement costs.

The company said it offset much of this through supplier diversification and process innovation, but renewed volatility in raw material prices could affect profitability. Given the fish cake production process's reliance on imported surimi, exchange rate sensitivity remains a variable that needs ongoing management.

Possible Recurrence of One-Off Costs

In the second quarter of 2026, selling and administrative expenses temporarily increased due to upfront spending on brand renewal, design consulting, and external financial advisory services aimed at longer-term growth.

The company described these as investment-related costs, but if similar expenses recur as overseas expansion and production facility investment continue, quarterly profitability swings could widen further.

With new store openings and infrastructure investment proceeding simultaneously, the pace of cost execution warrants ongoing monitoring.

Supply-Demand and Share Overhang

At the time of listing, freely tradable shares amounted to about 37.6% of total shares outstanding, with a 12-month lock-up applied to major shareholder stakes.

Given the listing date of December 22, 2025, the lock-up release is approaching and could become a supply-and-demand variable as the pool of tradable shares changes. As a smaller-cap stock, limited trading volume could also amplify price volatility, which is worth factoring in.

12

Overall view

Samjin Food showed clear improvement in both revenue and profit on a full-year 2025 basis, along with a sharply lower debt-to-equity ratio, pointing to a stabilizing financial structure following its listing.

However, as the swing to an operating loss in the second quarter of 2026 illustrates, quarter-to-quarter volatility driven by the fish cake industry's inherent seasonality remains a key variable.

Growth drivers hinge on maintaining differentiated channels such as gift sets and bakery stores outside the domestic oligopolistic market, alongside overseas expansion centered on China and the new room-temperature fish cake product.

At the same time, the still-low share of overseas revenue at this early stage, and the potential impact of raw material, FX, and investment-related cost burdens on quarterly profitability, should be weighed in a balanced way.

With preliminary third-quarter results, fourth-quarter peak-season demand, and the lock-up release all converging in the period ahead, each of these indicators warrants separate monitoring. The final investment decision rests with the reader.
