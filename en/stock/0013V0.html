Samjinfood: Peak Season Ahead After a Q2 Operating Loss

02

Business structure

Samjinfood is a Busan-based fish cake manufacturer that listed on KOSDAQ in December 2025. By product, the 2024 mix was Soup-use fish cake 37.6%, side dish 21.7%, packaged snacks 20.7% and gift sets 14.8%.

Packaged snack sales on a standalone basis more than doubled from KRW 12.8 billion in 2023 to KRW 26.9 billion in 2025, and reached KRW 14.2 billion in the first half of 2026, equal to 52.6% of 2025 annual sales.

Soup-use fish cake sales fell 4.7% year on year in the nine months to Q3 2026, which the company linked to weaker demand at large discount stores. B2B sales, including franchise and food-service distributors, rose 21.9% over the same nine-month period.

Exports remain a small share: standalone export value was KRW 3.4 billion in 2025 and KRW 2.5 billion in the first half of 2026, up 90% year on year. Products are sold through H Mart stores in the United States, Emart24 in Cambodia, and a Shanghai outlet that opened on October 16, 2026.

Competitively, the company is a small or mid-sized player focused on one category, with a narrower product range than large listed food groups. Seasonality is pronounced, so winter demand anchors the business model.

04

Earnings analysis

On an annual basis, 2025 sales reached KRW 109.5 billion, up 13.6% from KRW 96.4 billion in 2024. Operating profit rose 22.9% to KRW 5.96 billion from KRW 4.84 billion, and operating margin edged up from 5.0% to 5.4%, so profit grew faster than sales.

Operating margin was 2.6% in 2023, so the improvement has run across the 2023 to 2025 period. Net income attributable to controlling shareholders rose 408.2% to KRW 5.2 billion from KRW 1.02 billion, while net income including non-controlling interests was KRW 5.19 billion.

The company credited a shift toward higher-value products and productivity gains, and a decline in one-off non-operating costs also supported net income. At year-end 2025, total equity was KRW 41.7 billion, of which KRW 41.9 billion belonged to controlling shareholders, and total liabilities were KRW 41.4 billion.

Operating cash flow rose from KRW 6.27 billion in 2024 to KRW 8.24 billion in 2025. By quarter, Q1 2026 sales were KRW 33.3 billion, operating profit KRW 3.89 billion and controlling-shareholder net income KRW 2.89 billion, with operating margin up 4.0 percentage points year on year.

Q2 2026 sales fell 35.5% from the prior quarter to KRW 21.5 billion and the company posted an operating loss of KRW 524 million, a swing from profit in the prior quarter.

The company pointed to off-season fixed costs, higher raw material purchase prices and delayed price increases for B2B channels, while listing-related infrastructure costs and a bad-debt charge tied to the Homeplus rehabilitation procedure also weighed.

In Q3 2026 sales were KRW 28.6 billion, up 32.9% from the prior quarter, and operating profit returned to KRW 1.32 billion with controlling-shareholder net income of KRW 1.08 billion.

05

Industry analysis

Fish cake demand is strongly seasonal, falling in spring and summer and rising as temperatures drop. On a three-year average basis, Q4 has accounted for about 31% of annual sales, so the cycle peaks in winter. On the cost side, higher fish paste prices and currency pressure persisted into 2026.

The company’s own filing data showed imported raw material prices per kilogram in the first half of 2026 up more than 13%. In Q2 2026 the company said raw material purchase prices rose more than 7% from the prior quarter, citing geopolitical risk and overseas catch regulation.

On demand, large discount store weakness has pressured traditional soup-use products, while packaged snacks and gift sets have led growth. Abroad, demand for Korean processed foods such as ramen and seaweed is widening, and fish cake is viewed as a high-protein seafood product with room to expand.

The company’s export share was about 3% of sales as recently as 2024, and exports have been growing quickly since then. Its position rests on a fish-cake specialist identity and a growing B2B channel, while its production scale and distribution reach remain limited compared with large domestic food groups.

06

Outlook

The company says it has completed its capacity expansion and plans to use the larger capacity to meet peak-season orders in Q4 2026. Because more than 30% of annual sales have typically landed in Q4, the fourth quarter largely determines full-year operating profit.

One financial news outlet reported that strong second-half results on top of a record first half could make a full-year record possible.

In overseas expansion, the focus is China: the company said it would add three to five stores in China in the second half of 2026 if its deli shop performs at target, and reach more than 20 stores by the first half of 2027. It is also considering a dedicated frozen export line and logistics automation.

Capital spending timing has shifted. Of the KRW 11.8 billion in facility funds from the IPO, only KRW 400 million had been spent by the end of June 2026, as the logistics building expansion at the Jangrim plant needed coordination with ongoing production.

The company said the existing capacity and third-party logistics partners would cover the 2026 peak season. It also issued a KRW 10 billion private bond with warrants, carrying 0% coupon and maturity rates, to fund the new plant.

Over the medium term, the company plans to develop seafood-protein wellness products and ambient-temperature fish cake bars as strategic items.

08

Bull factors

Structural Q4 peak season

On a three-year average basis, Q4 has accounted for about 31% of annual sales, concentrating winter fish cake demand. The company says capacity expansion is complete and that it plans to serve Q4 2026 orders. If peak-season demand holds, the concentration of annual results in Q4 supports earnings improvement.

Expanding overseas and B2B channels

Standalone exports rose from KRW 3.4 billion in 2025 to KRW 2.5 billion in the first half of 2026, up 90% year on year. The company said North American sales rose more than 60% in the nine months to Q3 2026. B2B sales grew 21.9% over the same period, reducing reliance on any single consumer channel.

Improving profitability and balance sheet

Operating margin rose from 5.0% in 2024 to 5.4% in 2025 and was up 4.0 percentage points year on year in Q1 2026. Debt-to-equity fell from 1,248.8% in 2024 to 99.2% in 2025. Operating cash flow increased to KRW 8.24 billion in 2025, so profits and cash generation both improved.

09

Bear factors

Q2 loss and cost pressure

The Q2 2026 operating loss of KRW 524 million reversed a profit in the prior quarter. The company cited off-season fixed costs, higher raw material purchase prices and delayed B2B price increases.

Raw material purchase prices rising more than 7% in a single quarter means whether the cost pressure is temporary remains to be confirmed.

Weakness in traditional products

Soup-use fish cake sales fell 4.7% year on year in the nine months to Q3 2026. The company attributed this to weaker demand at large discount stores. Soup-use products were the largest category in 2024, so their decline can cap overall growth.

Inventory build and delayed capex

Consolidated inventory at the end of the first half of 2026 was KRW 14.5 billion, up 52.4% from the end of 2025. Only KRW 400 million of IPO facility funds had been spent by the end of June 2026, so the spending schedule is being adjusted. Combining inventory build with delayed capex raises questions about post-peak cash flow and cost structure.

10

Risk factors

Raw materials and FX

Higher fish paste prices and a weaker won are pressing costs. The company says its formulation technology, process innovation and new suppliers offset much of the increase, but the offset may vary by quarter. How quickly cost increases pass through to B2B prices is a key variable for margins.

Seasonality and channel concentration

With Q4 averaging about 31% of annual sales, results can swing sharply on peak-season demand or weather. The Q2 loss from off-season fixed costs shows how quarterly variance can drive the full-year result. Weaker consumption at large discount stores directly affects soup-use sales.

Overseas expansion and dilution

The company issued a KRW 10 billion bond with warrants to fund its new plant. The exercise price carries a 40% premium and there is no repricing clause. If exercised, the share count would rise and dilution could follow, while overseas store expansion may add near-term selling and administrative costs.

Overall view

Samjinfood’s sales, operating profit and controlling-shareholder net income all rose from 2024 to 2025, and debt-to-equity fell sharply. In 2026 quarterly results alternated, with a Q1 profit, a Q2 operating loss and a Q3 profit.

Nine-month sales rose 9.6%, but raw material prices and off-season fixed costs weighed on Q2. Strengths include the structural Q4 peak season, growing exports and B2B channels, and better profitability. Weaknesses include the Q2 loss and cost pressure, lower soup-use sales, and rising inventory alongside delayed capex.

The price-to-book ratio is below 1x, while earnings-based multiples are affected by one-off items. The next checkpoints are Q4 2026 peak-season results and the 2026 full-year preliminary release.
