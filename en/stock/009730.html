02

Business structure

Irem is a specialty steel materials company that manufactures stainless steel pipes and integrated deck plates. Its stainless steel pipe business produces pipes in a range of sizes with strong corrosion resistance, supplying construction, shipbuilding and other industries.

The company has supplied specialty stainless pipes to semiconductor and display manufacturers, which it cites as evidence of its technical position.

In May 2025 it completed vendor registration with a large domestic shipbuilder, and reports at the time said it was adding specialty pipe capacity to match a strong shipbuilding order cycle.

On the raw material side, it secured the equipment and facilities of a leading domestic coil center and now operates the coil center directly. The company has said the move should stabilize material supply, reduce costs and improve profitability.

Its affiliate XRB, a specialist in vanadium redox flow battery energy storage systems, began full operation of a 100MWh annual production plant in Eumseong, Chungbuk, as announced in October 2025. The stainless pipe business is the core, with the deck business and the affiliate energy storage business complementing it.

Listed competitors in stainless pipes include SeAH Steel, so differentiation in specialty pipes is the key to competitiveness.

04

Earnings analysis

Annual revenue rose from KRW 66.6 billion in 2022 to KRW 76.8 billion in 2023 (up 15.3% year on year) and KRW 109.8 billion in 2024 (up 43.0%), then fell to KRW 92 billion in 2025 (down 16.2%). 2024 was the largest year and 2022 the smallest in the 2022 to 2025 period. Profitability moved in the opposite direction.

Operating profit fell from KRW 1.99 billion in 2022 to KRW 979 million in 2023 (down 50.7%), then turned into an operating loss of KRW 4.72 billion in 2024.

The 2025 operating loss widened to KRW 13.9 billion, making 2024 and 2025 two consecutive loss years, and the operating margin fell from 3.0% in 2022 to -15.1% in 2025. The net loss attributable to controlling shareholders widened from KRW 14.9 billion in 2024 to KRW 16.8 billion in 2025.

The 2024 deterioration was attributed to higher financing costs from the transfer of the deck business and an impairment of goodwill. The debt ratio rose from 63.0% in 2022 to 147.5% in 2024 and 151.6% in 2025, with total liabilities of KRW 60.4 billion and total equity of KRW 39.8 billion at year-end 2025.

Operating cash flow dropped from KRW 5.82 billion in 2024 to KRW 409 million in 2025. Quarterly, the operating loss went from KRW 5.13 billion in the fourth quarter of 2025 to KRW 2.36 billion in the first quarter of 2026 and KRW 4.42 billion in the second quarter.

In the third quarter of 2026, revenue was KRW 17.7 billion (up 12.3% from the prior quarter, down 25.8% year on year) and the operating loss was KRW 1.39 billion, narrower than KRW 2.8 billion in the third quarter of 2025.

Steel & Metal News reported that cutting labor costs by 21% and improving the cost of goods sold ratio helped defend profitability in that quarter, while the same report said losses in the super deck segment widened as construction activity weakened.

Losses have persisted for five consecutive quarters from the third quarter of 2025 through the third quarter of 2026, and the net loss attributable to controlling shareholders over the last four quarters totals KRW 18.3 billion.

05

Industry analysis

Stainless steel pipe is a specialty segment within steel. Its listed peers in the steel and metals sector include Dongkuk Steel Mill, Hyundai Steel Company and SeAH Steel, along with several smaller pipe makers.

On FnGuide's sector comparison, Irem's 2025 revenue of KRW 92 billion is small relative to the combined revenue of the KOSDAQ metals sector. On the demand side, reports noted that the domestic shipbuilding order boom is a favorable backdrop for specialty pipe demand, including pipes for LNG vessels.

Construction weakness, by contrast, widened losses in the deck-related business. On raw materials, the company previously said nickel supply disruptions increased price volatility in stainless steel and responded by securing coil center operations.

Its customers include domestic semiconductor and display manufacturers and shipbuilders, and it has built a niche in specialty-use pipes that it cites as a differentiator.

The cost improvement in the third quarter of 2026 was reported to come from labor and cost-of-sales management rather than a sector recovery, so company-specific and industry factors should be read separately.

Shipbuilding is in an upcycle while construction is weak, so the industry's position in its cycle is difficult to pin down. The energy storage market entered by the affiliate XRB is presented as a growth axis separate from the pipe business.

The narrowing operating loss is attributed to cost management, which is an improvement the company controls independent of sector conditions. Specialty pipe demand base

Delivery history to semiconductor and display makers, combined with added capacity, forms the base for specialty pipe demand. The operation of the affiliate XRB plant in Eumseong adds energy storage as a new axis.
