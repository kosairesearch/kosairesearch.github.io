Report overview

Irem rebuilds its balance sheet as losses widen

Irem, a stainless steel pipe and deck plate maker, has posted two consecutive annual losses since 2024 and is now pursuing a 9-for-1 free capital reduction and a KRW 11.3 billion rights offering to repair its balance sheet.

  1. 1

    Disclosed results show two consecutive loss-making years from 2024 to 2025. Operating loss in 2025 was KRW 13.9 billion and net loss attributable to controlling shareholders was KRW 16.8 billion.

  2. 2

    Quarterly revenue fell for three consecutive quarters from Q4 2025 to Q2 2026. Q2 2026 revenue was KRW 15.8 billion, down 31.7% year on year.

  3. 3

    The debt ratio reached 151.6% at the end of 2025, up sharply from 60.5% at the end of 2023. In response Irem carried out a 9-for-1 free capital reduction on common shares and is separately running a rights offering.

  4. 4

    Press reports flagged that Irem's market capitalization fell below the KRW 20 billion level cited as the KOSDAQ administrative-issue threshold, and the possible withdrawal of the rights offering was raised. Whether Irem is designated could affect whether the offering is completed.

  5. 5

    End markets are polarized, with construction still weak while shipbuilding and semiconductor plants support demand. The vanadium ESS venture was reported to be at the demonstration stage, and no meaningful revenue contribution has been confirmed.

02

Business structure

Irem is a steel materials company that manufactures stainless steel pipes and integrated deck plates. Its company profile confirms these two product lines as the core of the business.

The pipes serve corrosion-sensitive uses in chemicals, energy and construction, and the company has said it supplies specialty stainless pipes to semiconductor and display makers and has completed vendor registration with a shipbuilder.

The deck plate business (Super Deck) was acquired in 2024 through a business transfer from Kostil's operations.

No verified disclosure of segment revenue shares was found, so none is quoted here, while a Dealsite report said the pipe and Super Deck segments posted losses of KRW 6.0 billion and KRW 2.8 billion respectively on a 2025 basis.

The new business is vanadium-based ESS through affiliate XRB, but at the time of that report the BESS business was still at the demonstration stage. On ownership, the largest shareholder and related parties held 21.05% as of April 14, 2026.

Competition includes pipe makers such as Nexteel, Husteel and TCC Steel as well as deck plate suppliers, so price competition is hard to avoid in commodity grades.

04

Earnings analysis

Annual revenue rose from KRW 66.6 billion in 2022 to KRW 76.8 billion in 2023 (up 15.3%) and KRW 109.8 billion in 2024 (up 43.0%), the largest year in the 2022-2025 span, before falling to KRW 92 billion in 2025 (down 16.2%).

Operating profit went from KRW 1.99 billion in 2022 to KRW 979 million in 2023 (down 50.7%), then swung to an operating loss of KRW 4.72 billion in 2024 and widened to an operating loss of KRW 13.9 billion in 2025. The operating margin fell from 3.0% in 2022 to -15.1% in 2025.

Net income attributable to controlling shareholders was KRW 2.92 billion in 2022 and KRW 95.38 million in 2023 (down 96.7%), followed by net losses of KRW 14.9 billion in 2024 and KRW 16.8 billion in 2025, two consecutive loss-making years.

Press reports tied the 2024 swing to a loss, despite higher sales, to one-off items such as goodwill impairment and investment costs, and the company attributed the wider 2025 operating loss to lower sales from changed market conditions and one-off costs.

By quarter, revenue peaked at KRW 23.8 billion in Q3 2025 and then fell to KRW 18.4 billion in Q4 2025, KRW 17 billion in Q1 2026 and KRW 15.8 billion in Q2 2026, three consecutive quarterly declines.

The operating loss has persisted for five consecutive quarters from KRW 3.07 billion in Q2 2025 to KRW 4.42 billion in Q2 2026; it was KRW 5.13 billion in Q4 2025, narrowed to KRW 2.36 billion in Q1 2026, and widened again in Q2 2026.

Net loss attributable to controlling shareholders was KRW 8.09 billion in Q4 2025, KRW 3.01 billion in Q1 2026 and KRW 5.39 billion in Q2 2026, and the sum for the last four quarters (Q3 2025 to Q2 2026) was KRW 20.1 billion.

Operating cash flow was barely positive at KRW 409 million in 2025, which is small relative to the loss, and total liabilities of KRW 60.4 billion against total equity of KRW 39.8 billion put the debt ratio at 151.6%. Periodic reports for quarters after Q2 2026 have not yet been filed.

05

Industry analysis

The stainless welded pipe market is split, with construction weak and demand concentrated in shipbuilding and plants. Domestic industry players described the 2026 market as facing structural uncertainty from oversupply combined with slowing demand.

In the same interview, building-sector recovery was seen as limited, while relatively strong shipbuilding and expanding semiconductor plant investment were expected to create meaningful demand.

For steel overall, one analysis said construction-related steel such as rebar, sections and pipes would have weak rebound strength because of its sensitivity to rates, property and public works budgets.

On November 6, 2026, a press report said small and mid-cap steel stocks were mostly weak on slowing construction and delayed global steel demand recovery, and pipe-related companies were among those falling.

On global supply, the World Stainless Association counted 2025 stainless melt shop output at 64.2 million tons, up 2.1% from a year earlier.

Irem has meaningful exposure to construction deck plates and general-purpose pipes, which makes it sensitive to this weak segment, while high-value pipes for shipbuilding and semiconductors become visible in revenue only as certification and delivery records build up.

Within the sector, it is a small company with an operating loss on a disclosed basis and, unlike the large steelmakers, has limited borrowing capacity.

06

Outlook

No separate earnings guidance from the company has been confirmed. What can be confirmed is the schedule of balance sheet repair. On April 24, 2026 the board decided on a 9-to-1 free capital reduction to improve the financial structure by covering accumulated deficits.

After the reduction, the total number of common shares was adjusted to 7,975,503. A press report said the accumulated deficit at the end of 2025 was KRW 38.8 billion and would shrink to about KRW 6.5 billion after reflecting the KRW 32.3 billion capital reduction surplus.

The rights offering was decided as 4.8 million common shares at an indicative issue price of KRW 2,360, raising KRW 9.328 billion for working capital and KRW 2 billion for debt repayment, but the timetable slipped, with the share allocation record date amended from October 1 to October 13.

The final issue price and subscription results need to be confirmed through filings. On the business side, the key questions are whether demand for shipbuilding and semiconductor plant pipes translates into revenue and whether the vanadium ESS moves from demonstration to orders.

The operating loss narrowed in Q1 2026 but widened again in Q2, so it is hard to say that quarterly losses have stabilized.

08

Bull factors

Capital increase and deficit clean-up

The free capital reduction cuts share capital to absorb deficits, and per press reports the accumulated deficit would fall from KRW 38.8 billion to about KRW 6.5 billion. If the KRW 11.3 billion rights offering is completed, KRW 9.328 billion for working capital and KRW 2 billion for debt repayment would be secured.

With the debt ratio at 151.6%, a capital increase is a precondition for easing the financial burden. The next check is whether the offering closes and how much cash actually comes in.

A high-value demand axis exists

Industry participants see shipbuilding and semiconductor and data center plant investment as likely main demand axes for stainless pipes even while building demand is weak. Irem has disclosed a shipbuilder vendor registration and a record of supplying semiconductor and display makers.

A larger revenue share from these areas could leave room to improve the operating margin. Segment revenue would first need to be verified.

Low trading level versus net assets, plus a new venture

The price-to-book ratio is well below 1x, so shares trade at a low price relative to net assets. The largest shareholder and related parties hold 21.05%, so the control structure is fairly clear.

Affiliate XRB's vanadium ESS is at the demonstration stage, but if it succeeds it is an option that could change the business mix. The timing of each of these factors remains unconfirmed.

09

Bear factors

Falling revenue and widening losses

Q2 2026 revenue of KRW 15.8 billion was down 31.7% year on year, and the operating loss of KRW 4.42 billion was larger than both the previous quarter and the year-earlier quarter. Revenue has declined for three straight quarters since Q3 2025, so the bottom in demand is not yet confirmed. With operating losses for five consecutive quarters, the fixed-cost burden may be becoming structural.

Debt burden and liquidity

Total liabilities were KRW 60.4 billion at the end of 2025 and the debt ratio was 151.6%. Dealsite reported cash and equivalents of only KRW 3.8 billion against gross borrowings above KRW 40 billion as of Q3 2025. Operating cash flow was only KRW 409 million in 2025, so losses are hard to cover with cash. The history of repeated delays in capital raising also adds to funding uncertainty.

Dilution and listing-maintenance variables

The rights offering restores the share count that the capital reduction cut, which dilutes existing holders. A press report said that if the company is designated an administrative issue after the reduction, further reverse splits or capital reductions cannot be carried out during the improvement period.

The market capitalization requirement and the record of unfaithful disclosure add to the burden of managing the share price.

10

Risk factors

Financing and funding

The rights offering timetable has been pushed back several times, and an earlier third-party allocation was also paid in with a long delay. Dealsite reported that the cash actually raised in the earlier offering was limited, leaving a continuing need for additional funding.

If the offering fails or shrinks, debt repayment and working capital are directly affected. Filings on debt clean-up such as convertible bond buybacks are also continuing, so follow-up disclosures should be checked.

Listing and regulation

A Korean press report noted that market capitalization below the KRW 20 billion administrative-issue threshold raises the possibility that the rights offering is withdrawn.

In its registration statement the company said that if it is designated an administrative issue before subscription rights certificates are listed, listing of the certificates becomes impossible.

On April 30, 2026 the company received a notice of prospective designation as an unfaithful disclosure corporation over a delayed disclosure of an advance payment decision. Whether designations and additional penalty points follow must be confirmed through filings.

Business and cycle

Demand for construction-related steel is weak and oversupply in stainless pipes has been cited. Nickel price swings affect both costs and selling prices. The vanadium ESS venture was reported at the demonstration stage, so the timing of any revenue contribution is uncertain. Expansion in high-value pipes can only be assessed once order results are confirmed.

Overall view

On a disclosed basis Irem has posted losses for two consecutive years since 2024, and operating losses have continued for five consecutive quarters through Q2 2026. Revenue has fallen for three consecutive quarters since Q3 2025, and the debt ratio was 151.6% at the end of 2025.

At the same time, the 9-for-1 free capital reduction has begun the clean-up of accumulated deficits and a KRW 11.3 billion rights offering is under way. On the bullish side are the capital increase, shipbuilding and semiconductor plant demand, and a low trading level relative to net assets.

On the bearish side are falling revenue, the debt burden, dilution, and the administrative-issue and offering-withdrawal variables.
