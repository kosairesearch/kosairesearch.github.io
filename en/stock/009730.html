02

Business structure

Irem is a specialty steel materials company that makes stainless steel pipes and integrated deck plates. Its stainless steel pipe business produces corrosion-resistant pipes in a range of specifications for construction, shipbuilding and other uses.

Reports say the company has supplied specialty stainless steel pipes to domestic semiconductor and display manufacturers. In May 2025 it said it had completed vendor registration with a major domestic shipbuilder.

In June 2025 it announced it had acquired the equipment and plant of a leading domestic coil center operator and would run the coil center itself. The company said this should stabilize material supply and reduce costs, supporting profitability in its pipe business.

In shipbuilding it is pursuing supply of pipes for LNG-fueled vessels, extending its reach into eco-friendly ships. Reports attributed the first year of revenue above KRW 100 billion in 2024 to expansion of its super deck and thick-walled stainless steel pipe operations.

Its affiliate XRB, in which Irem holds a stake, makes energy storage systems based on vanadium redox flow batteries and started a plant with 100 MWh of annual capacity in Eumseong, Chungbuk, in October 2025.

An August 2026 report said Irem used convertible bonds in its process of securing management control of a target company. Segment revenue shares could not be confirmed in this search, so they are not stated. Share or sales comparisons with peers were also not confirmed.

04

Earnings analysis

On a disclosed basis, annual revenue rose from KRW 66.6 billion in 2022 to KRW 76.8 billion in 2023 (up 15.3% year on year). It reached KRW 109.8 billion in 2024 (up 43.0%), the largest year in the 2022 to 2025 period, and fell to KRW 92 billion in 2025 (down 16.2%).

Operating profit fell from KRW 1.99 billion in 2022 to KRW 979 million in 2023 (down 50.7%), and the company swung to an operating loss of KRW 4.72 billion in 2024. The operating loss widened to KRW 13.9 billion in 2025, and the company has posted losses in two consecutive years, 2024 and 2025.

The operating margin declined from 3.0% in 2022 and 1.3% in 2023 to -4.3% in 2024 and -15.1% in 2025. Revenue grew in 2024, but reports attributed the profit decline to higher investment costs, one-off expenses and weaker steel market conditions.

Reports also said the 2024 results reflected higher financing costs tied to the transfer of a deck business and an impairment of goodwill.

Net profit attributable to controlling shareholders fell from KRW 2.92 billion in 2022 to KRW 95.38 million in 2023 (down 96.7%), then turned to losses of KRW 14.9 billion in 2024 and KRW 16.8 billion in 2025.

Net income including non-controlling interests was a loss of KRW 14.9 billion in 2024 and KRW 16.8 billion in 2025. Quarterly, the operating loss went from KRW 2.8 billion in Q3 2025 on KRW 23.8 billion of revenue to KRW 5.13 billion in Q4 2025 on KRW 18.4 billion of revenue (down 22.7% from the prior quarter).

The operating loss narrowed to KRW 2.36 billion in Q1 2026, then widened to KRW 4.42 billion in Q2, and narrowed to KRW 1.39 billion in Q3 2026 on revenue of KRW 17.7 billion (up 12.3% from the prior quarter, down 25.8% from a year earlier).

Reports put first-half 2026 revenue at KRW 32.7 billion, down 34.2% year on year, which is close to the sum of the disclosed Q1 and Q2 revenue.

05

Industry analysis

Stainless steel pipe demand spans piping and structural uses in construction, shipbuilding, and semiconductor and display equipment. Reports said the company was expanding its specialty pipe equipment to match a boom in domestic shipbuilding orders.

This trend lines up with demand for higher-value products such as pipes for LNG-fueled vessels, but whether that demand has translated into quarterly revenue growth in 2026 is not yet confirmed.

On raw materials, the company said a nickel supply disruption caused by unrest in New Caledonia had increased price volatility in the stainless steel market in 2025. Citing that assessment, it said it decided to acquire the coil center to secure a stable supply chain.

Reports cited weaker steel market conditions as a factor in the 2024 profit decline, but this search did not confirm a basis for placing the company within the industry cycle. Operating margin comparisons with listed peers could not be confirmed and are therefore not stated.

The company's cited strengths are its record of supplying specialty stainless pipes, its shipbuilding vendor registrations, and a raw material sourcing structure built around the coil center. Independent data on its scale or price competitiveness versus peers was not found.

With revenue down to KRW 92 billion in 2025, whether it regains market position should be checked against future quarterly sales.

Overall view

Revenue peaked at KRW 109.8 billion in 2024, fell to KRW 92 billion in 2025, and rebounded on a quarterly basis to KRW 17.7 billion in Q3 2026.
