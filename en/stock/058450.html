Revenue rebounds, losses persist, governance risk looms

02

Business structure

Hanjoo ART is a Kosdaq-listed producer of drama and film props, listed in January 2002. Its roots lie in mobile phone parts and mold manufacturing, and later in plastic injection molding, a business that reportedly generated virtually no revenue after 2022.

After a change of largest shareholder in 2024, the company added entertainment operations including broadcast equipment rental, and its business classification moved to content production and distribution. It also runs restaurant franchises.

The company says lighting, refurbished mobile phones and notebooks, and platform businesses drove its Q1 2026 revenue.

It acquired the lighting distribution platform Vittz to widen its distribution and platform activities, and it has completed registration as a supplier for secondary-battery logistics automation equipment. Management describes a shift from low-margin operations toward profit-focused businesses.

In May 2026 it opened a public call for roughly KRW 30 billion of growth investments aimed at AI, robotics, secondary batteries and data-driven businesses. Because its activities span manufacturing, distribution, entertainment and food franchising, it does not map neatly onto a single competitive set.

04

Earnings analysis

Annual revenue went from KRW 5.58 billion in 2022 to KRW 10.5 billion in 2023 (up 88.1% year on year) and KRW 14.3 billion in 2024 (up 35.8%), then fell to KRW 6.29 billion in 2025 (down 55.9%). 2024 was the largest year and 2022 the smallest in the 2022–2025 window.

Operating losses were KRW 5.61 billion in 2022, KRW 2.93 billion in 2023, KRW 6.51 billion in 2024 and KRW 5.99 billion in 2025, which is four consecutive years of losses. Operating margins were -100.5%, -27.9%, -45.6% and -95.3% from 2022 to 2025, so the margin deteriorated again in 2025 as revenue shrank.

Net losses attributable to controlling shareholders were KRW 8.02 billion, KRW 3.11 billion, KRW 9.99 billion and KRW 10.4 billion from 2022 to 2025. Net loss including non-controlling interests was KRW 10.5 billion in 2025, and that figure is reported separately.

The company attributed the 2025 change to lower revenue, higher selling and administrative expenses and costs for expanding business areas. The debt ratio fell from 336.4% in 2022 to 83.0% in 2024 and rose to 153.2% in 2025, when total equity was KRW 10 billion.

Operating cash flow was negative in all four years, at -KRW 3.69 billion, -KRW 670 million, -KRW 2.16 billion and -KRW 1.03 billion.

On a quarterly basis, Q1 2026 revenue was KRW 7.27 billion with an operating loss of KRW 2.66 billion, while the company's own release put that loss at KRW 27 billion won, a 1.4% improvement year on year. Q2 2026 revenue was KRW 6.44 billion and the operating loss narrowed to KRW 27.08 million.

The company said consolidated net income turned positive in Q2, but the disclosed net loss attributable to controlling shareholders for Q2 was KRW 214 million, and the two measures differ, so they should be read separately. Q3 2026 revenue was KRW 5.65 billion and the operating loss widened again to KRW 1.93 billion.

Industry analysis

Hanjoo ART is classified in the media and entertainment sector, but its business has moved from drama and film prop production into lighting, refurbished phones, distribution platforms and restaurant franchising.

Large entertainment companies in the same sector are typically organized around content intellectual property and artist activity, whereas Hanjoo ART combines manufacturing- and distribution-type businesses. That difference limits direct comparison with sector-average multiples or growth rates.

The areas the company has named as growth candidates, including secondary batteries, renewable energy and AI-based robotics and drones, show the direction management has chosen, but disclosed figures do not show how much revenue these areas contribute.

The company says lighting and refurbished phone businesses drove Q1 2026 revenue growth, but data to compare that business's market size and competitor share is limited. On listing maintenance, market capitalization is a key variable.

In September 2026 the Kosdaq market operations office issued a notice that the company was at risk of designation as a managed issue because its market capitalization was below KRW 20 billion. The company is still restructuring, so its position within the sector has not yet settled into reported results.

Outlook

In May 2026 the company said it aimed for a turnaround in the second half of 2026. In the same period, when it released Q1 results, it set a target of improving Q2 results and returning to profit in Q3.

Q2 operating loss narrowed to KRW 27.08 million, but the Q3 operating loss widened to KRW 1.93 billion, so the Q3 profit target is not yet reflected in disclosed results. The company had expected major receivables to be collected within Q3 2026 and said some real estate sales were underway.

Q3 has already passed, so the outcome of those collections is a matter for the next periodic report. The growth investment plan targets about KRW 30 billion, to be funded in stages from rights-offering cash, asset sales and receivables collections.

The public call proceeds through a first document review of business plans and company profiles before follow-up steps. Beyond capital, the company says it will provide incubation support in management, finance and legal affairs, and it is also considering joint ventures with existing businesses.

Since October 2026, the suspected embezzlement and breach of trust matters and the listing-eligibility review have been in progress, so management stability, which underpins the investment plan, is now a question for upcoming disclosures.

The 2026 annual business report, the next periodic filing, will show Q4 2026 results and the full-year operating margin trend.

Bull factors

Revenue recovery

Q1 2026 revenue of KRW 7.27 billion exceeded full-year 2025 revenue of KRW 6.29 billion. Revenue then fell to KRW 6.44 billion in Q2 and KRW 5.65 billion in Q3, but both remain well above the KRW 1.74 billion recorded in Q4 2025.

The company describes a reorganization around businesses with a revenue base, including lighting, refurbished devices and platforms. Whether that reorganization sustains revenue scale is a point to check in the 2026 annual report.

Narrowing operating loss

Q2 2026 operating loss narrowed to KRW 27.08 million from KRW 2.66 billion in Q1. In its Q1 release the company attributed the improvement to reduced low-margin business and a shift toward profit-focused operations. Press coverage described the same trend as a result of that strategy.

However, the Q3 operating loss widened again to KRW 1.93 billion, so the quarterly direction of losses is mixed.

Capital injection and funding for restructuring

A third-party rights offering in early 2026 brought in about KRW 3 billion and made an investment partnership the largest shareholder. In March 2026 the company decided on a 4-for-1 capital reduction to cover accumulated deficits. A further rights offering decision was disclosed at the end of July 2026. The company has said the funds will be used for growth investments and business restructuring.

Bear factors

Persistent losses and renewed widening

Operating losses were KRW 6.51 billion in 2024 and KRW 5.99 billion in 2025, extending four consecutive years of losses from 2022. The Q3 2026 operating loss of KRW 1.93 billion was wider than a year earlier. Net losses attributable to controlling shareholders over the last four quarters total KRW 7.67 billion.

The company's announced Q2 net income turnaround uses a different measure from the disclosed controlling-shareholder net loss and should be interpreted separately.

Governance and accounting concerns

In October 2026 the company disclosed suspected embezzlement and breach of trust, followed by a disclosure of a prosecution referral tied to accounting-standard violations and a listing-eligibility review notice from the exchange.

Press reports say goodwill tied to a drama-set producer acquired in 2023 lost about KRW 4.6 billion in value across 2024 and 2025. The same reporting says the company had not given a specific explanation of those questions.

Equity erosion and financial strain

Total equity fell from about KRW 20.4 billion at year-end 2024 to KRW 10 billion at year-end 2025. The debt ratio rose from 83.0% in 2024 to 153.2% in 2025. Operating cash flow was negative in each year from 2022 to 2025. Revenue in 2025 was KRW 6.29 billion, down 55.9% from the prior year.

Risk factors

Listing and governance risk

The listing-eligibility review and the market-capitalization shortfall are both in play at the same time. If the market-capitalization shortfall continues, the company could be designated as a managed issue, and failure to meet the standard afterward could lead to delisting procedures.

Several changes of largest shareholder within three years have been cited as a question about consistency of management direction.

Accounting and asset valuation risk

Press reports say goodwill impairments reached about KRW 3.6 billion in 2024 and about KRW 1 billion in 2025. The 2023 financial statements were restated for a revenue recognition error, reducing net assets by about KRW 2.1 billion, according to reporting. Audit opinions were reported as unqualified each year. The appropriateness of accounting treatment will be reviewed again in the 2026 annual report and audit results.

Execution risk

Commentary has pointed to frequent shifts in business direction across manufacturing, entertainment, restaurants and distribution. The roughly KRW 30 billion investment plan is still at the public-call stage, and its timing and profitability are not yet visible in disclosed figures.

Real estate sales and receivables collections that fund the plan could also run behind schedule. Because quarterly revenue has moved sharply, predictability of business results is limited.
