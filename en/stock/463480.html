02

Business structure

Motivelink is an automotive electronics company that handles electrified vehicle power conversion systems from design through validation and mass production.

Its core products are transformers and inductors for inverters, electric power steering (EPS) parts, onboard chargers (OBC) and low-voltage DC-DC converters (LDC), which are reported to be fitted to the Hyundai Ioniq 5, Genesis GV60, and Kia EV6 and EV9. Its sales are concentrated on Hyundai Mobis.

Press reports put Hyundai Mobis at 85.6% of sales in 2023 and 82.7% in the first nine months of 2024, while the Hyundai Motor Group accounted for 78.3% of Hyundai Mobis sales in 2023. The company says it is widening joint development with Hyundai KEFICO and Hyundai Transys to reduce that dependence.

Its center of gravity is shifting from parts toward modules. It is developing two electromagnetic interference filter modules with Hyundai Mobis and two with Hyundai KEFICO, and co-developing FDC and ICCU modules with customers. Vietnam Plants 1 and 2 are running, and a third plant was planned at the 2025 listing.

In September 2025 it set up a wholly owned subsidiary in Chennai, India. A July 2026 report said the company has built a system spanning component design, module manufacturing and mass production, using an external manufacturing partner's infrastructure.

04

Earnings analysis

Under disclosed annual results, sales fell from KRW 82.9 billion in 2023 to KRW 61.7 billion in 2024 (down 25.5% year on year) before rising to KRW 67.4 billion in 2025 (up 9.2%). Within 2023 to 2025, 2023 was the largest year and 2024 the smallest.

Operating profit dropped from KRW 4.52 billion in 2023 to KRW 1.72 billion in 2024 (down 61.9%), and then became an operating loss of KRW 1.72 billion in 2025, a swing from profit to loss year on year. The operating margin fell from 5.5% in 2023 to 2.8% in 2024 and -2.6% in 2025.

Net income attributable to controlling shareholders fell from KRW 4.25 billion in 2023 to KRW 1.75 billion in 2024 (down 58.8%), and became a net loss of KRW 113 million in 2025. Net income including non-controlling interests moved in the same direction, ending at a net loss of KRW 113 million in 2025.

Sales grew in 2025, but margins slid into loss. Press coverage has pointed to research and development spending as a driver: a first-quarter 2025 report said R&D approached 10% of sales, up from 4.4% in 2023 and 6.8% in 2024.

The company said it was temporarily sacrificing profitability because development costs were concentrated in that period.

By quarter, the operating loss of KRW 538 million in the third quarter of 2025 widened to KRW 1.32 billion in the fourth quarter and KRW 1.44 billion in the first quarter of 2026, then narrowed to KRW 1.12 billion in the second quarter and KRW 829 million in the third quarter of 2026.

The third quarter of 2026 nonetheless showed a wider loss than a year earlier. Over the last four quarters, the net loss attributable to controlling shareholders totaled KRW 3.97 billion, while quarterly sales ranged from KRW 13 billion to KRW 20.2 billion. One media outlet had forecast 2025 sales of KRW 91 billion, against disclosed sales of KRW 67.4 billion.

05

Industry analysis

Demand from downstream markets is tied to Hyundai Motor Group's electrified vehicle output. A 2025 report said the EV market's temporary demand slowdown, known as the chasm, caused growth to contract in 2024. The same report carried expectations that orders for Hyundai and Kia eco-friendly vehicles would increase.

The CEO argued that automakers' push to localize production is raising demand for local manufacturing from suppliers. A July 2026 report cited Global Market Insights' forecast that the integrated automotive module market would reach about USD 450 billion by 2032.

Demand is also building from the shift of data center power systems to 800V high-voltage architectures, which the company cites as a new market. Competitively, Hyundai's in-vehicle electronics supply is splitting between Hyundai Mobis and Hyundai KEFICO, and Motivelink is pursuing supply to both. Its claim of a widening technology gap comes from company statements.

06

Outlook

Three timelines appear in company statements and press reports. First, a March 2026 report said mass production for Hyundai, Kia and Saudi CEER would start in the second half of 2026, with Hyundai Mobis volume fully reflected from 2027.

Second, the same report said the NE-2 (Ioniq 5) bidding remained open in the second half of 2026, and that the outcome would affect secured production volume through 2035. Third, the India timeline varies by report.

An August 2025 report said mass production could begin as early as the end of 2026, while a November 2025 report set a target of initial mass-production testing in the first quarter of 2026. The CEO said Indian sales could grow to a level similar to or above the home base after 2030.

In modules, FDC and ICCU modules are being co-developed with customers, and a July 2026 report described plans to expand the module lineup. Server power conversion parts are being developed with a semiconductor company, and the company said results should become visible within two to three years. Whether awarded volume converts into sales on schedule will determine future earnings.

08

Bull factors

Large Affiliated Orders Add Volume Visibility

The company was reported to have secured all volume for the Hyundai Mobis SX-3 (third-generation Kona) platform, scheduled for mass production in 2027. A March 2026 report pointed to this award supporting secured production volume through 2035.

An industry source also said the Hyundai KEFICO project is worth more than KRW 40 billion, with some possible change due to specification revisions.

Expanding India and Vietnam Production Bases

The company set up a wholly owned subsidiary in Chennai in September 2025 and said a large share of new orders is shifting to production in India. It said Indian sales could grow to a level similar to or above the home base after 2030.

A plan at the listing, as reported, said that adding a third Vietnam plant would raise total capacity 2.2 times.

Module and New Business Push to Shift the Earnings Mix

FDC and ICCU modules are being co-developed with customers, and four electromagnetic interference filter modules are in development with Hyundai Mobis and Hyundai KEFICO.

The CEO said server power conversion parts for 800V data center systems are being developed with a semiconductor company, with results expected within two to three years. Global Market Insights forecasts the integrated automotive module market at about USD 450 billion by 2032.

09

Bear factors

Five Straight Quarters of Losses and Cumulative Shortfall

Operating and net losses attributable to controlling shareholders have continued for five consecutive quarters, from the third quarter of 2025 through the third quarter of 2026. The first-quarter 2026 operating loss of KRW 1.44 billion was the largest in that stretch.

The net loss attributable to controlling shareholders totaled KRW 3.97 billion over the last four quarters. The third-quarter 2026 loss narrowed sequentially but widened from a year earlier.

Dependence on the Hyundai Group Affiliates

Press reports put Hyundai Mobis at 82.7% of sales in the first nine months of 2024, and the Hyundai Motor Group at 75.9% of Hyundai Mobis sales over the same period. Because revenue is tied to the group's operating performance, changes in affiliate production or orders feed directly into results.

The latest share reported in press coverage is the first nine months of 2024, so later trends need to be checked against the customer breakdown in periodic filings.

R&D Burden and Overseas Investment Costs

R&D spending rose from 4.4% of sales in 2023 and 6.8% in 2024 to near 10% in a first-quarter 2025 report. The company attributed this to development costs being concentrated in that period.

For India, a November 2025 report said the joint venture structure was not yet settled, so the investment cap was set at up to USD 1.5 million, and the roughly USD 5 million factory construction cost had not been finalized.

10

Risk factors

Customer concentration and affiliate dependence

Most sales flow through Hyundai Mobis to the Hyundai Motor Group, so any cut in affiliate orders or shift in EV demand flows straight into revenue.

The company is expanding co-development with Hyundai KEFICO and Hyundai Transys, but any reduction in concentration has to be confirmed in the customer breakdown in periodic filings. Until module sales scale up, volume changes at existing customers remain the main driver of results.

Costs, FX and tariffs

A 2025 report flagged raw material prices, exchange rates and overseas tariffs as variables for second-half results. As overseas production grows, exposure to FX and tariffs rises.

The India and Vietnam bases require local staffing and equipment investment, and the company said in November 2025 that the India factory construction cost was not yet final.

Execution of overseas expansion and new businesses

The India joint venture set an investment ceiling before its equity structure was settled, and mass-production timing differs across reports.

New businesses such as server power, satellites and defense are expected by the company to bear fruit within two to three years, so the timing of any revenue contribution is uncertain. If the EV demand slowdown persists, lower utilization of new capacity could raise the fixed-cost burden.

Overall view

Motivelink's sales fell from KRW 82.9 billion in 2023 to KRW 61.7 billion in 2024 before recovering to KRW 67.4 billion in 2025, while operating profit went from KRW 4.52 billion in 2023 to an operating loss of KRW 1.72 billion in 2025.

Losses have continued for five quarters through the third quarter of 2026, and the net loss attributable to controlling shareholders over the last four quarters totaled KRW 3.97 billion.

Bullish factors include large awards from Hyundai Group affiliates, the SX-3 platform volume, production bases in India and Vietnam, and the shift toward modules. Bearish factors include the ongoing losses, heavy concentration on Hyundai Mobis, R&D costs, and mass-production timelines that differ across reports.

On the balance sheet, total liabilities were KRW 27.3 billion and total equity KRW 36.7 billion at end-2025, and the debt ratio fell from 167.4% in 2023 to 74.3% in 2025. Operating cash flow in 2025 was -KRW 382 million.

The next checkpoints are the second-half 2026 mass-production start, the India timeline, and the operating margin in the 2026 annual report.
