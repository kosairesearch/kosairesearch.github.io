02

Business structure

Hwail Pharm is a active pharmaceutical ingredient maker founded in 1980 and listed on KOSDAQ in 2002. Built on more than 40 years of organic synthesis expertise, it develops and produces raw materials used across respiratory, cardiovascular and digestive therapeutic areas.

Production rests on a cGMP-certified facility in Hwaseong, Gyeonggi, and the company describes a portfolio of about 200 products. It supplies raw materials to roughly 300 domestic pharmaceutical companies and is expanding exports to Japan and Europe.

In 2005 it acquired a cephalosporin antibiotic plant, and it has also run a functional food ingredient business using nano-emulsion technology. Recently it has been diversifying by splitting operations into a food ingredient unit, including coenzyme Q10, and an antibiotic unit.

As of the third quarter of 2020, cough suppressants accounted for about 7 percent of total revenue and were the largest share among key products, according to contemporaneous press coverage. Raw material earnings depend on the production plans and drug approval timing of customer pharmaceutical companies.

Competitive differentiation rests on cGMP facilities, synthesis capability and long-term customer relationships. As of November 2026, the business direction is widening through the addition of new business objectives beyond the core raw materials.

04

Earnings analysis

Annual revenue fell from KRW 132.1 billion in 2022 to KRW 122.5 billion in 2023 (down 7.2% year on year), KRW 119.8 billion in 2024 (down 2.3%) and KRW 100.8 billion in 2025 (down 15.8%).

Revenue declined for three consecutive years from 2023 to 2025, with 2022 the largest year and 2025 the smallest in the 2022–2025 period. Daily Pharm also reported that revenue has fallen for three straight years from a 2022 peak.

Operating profit dropped from KRW 4.77 billion in 2022 to KRW 2.05 billion in 2023 (down 57.1%) and KRW 755 million in 2024 (down 63.1%), before turning into an operating loss of KRW 2.28 billion in 2025.

Operating margin went from 3.6 percent in 2022 to 1.7 percent in 2023, 0.6 percent in 2024 and -2.3 percent in 2025.

Net income attributable to controlling shareholders swung from a loss of KRW 6.33 billion in 2022 to a profit of KRW 3.86 billion in 2023, rose to KRW 6.48 billion in 2024 (up 67.8%), and then fell back into a loss of KRW 233 million in 2025. Net income including non-controlling interests followed the same pattern.

The company attributes the 2025 loss partly to lower output and higher costs following the 2022 Sangsin-ri plant fire. Operating cash flow moved from -KRW 234 million in 2022 to KRW 7.02 billion in 2023, KRW 11.6 billion in 2024 and KRW 11.9 billion in 2025.

Total equity at the end of 2025 was KRW 206.9 billion, all attributable to controlling shareholders, against total liabilities of KRW 6.29 billion, a debt ratio of 3.0 percent.

Quarterly revenue went from KRW 24.3 billion in Q3 2025 to KRW 18.6 billion in Q4 2025 (down 23.6% quarter on quarter), then KRW 23.4 billion in Q1 2026 (up 26.3%), KRW 24.5 billion in Q2 (up 4.4%) and KRW 24 billion in Q3 2026 (down 1.9% quarter on quarter and down 1.3% year on year).

Quarterly operating results moved from an operating profit of KRW 165 million in Q3 2025 to an operating loss of KRW 1.3 billion in Q4 2025, and losses have continued through Q3 2026, four quarters in a row.

The operating losses were KRW 566 million in Q1 2026, KRW 912 million in Q2 (wider) and KRW 781 million in Q3 (narrower, a swing to loss from a year earlier).

Net income attributable to controlling shareholders was KRW 4.37 billion in Q2 2026, up 655.5% from the prior quarter, before a net loss of KRW 1.93 billion in Q3 2026. The divergence between an operating loss and positive net income in Q2 makes non-operating items and one-off factors the next point to check. Net income attributable to controlling shareholders over the last four quarters totals KRW 2.51 billion.

05

Industry analysis

The raw materials market is sensitive to drug pricing policy at finished-drug makers and to exchange rate movements. In April 2026 Daily Pharm reported that raw material suppliers' earnings deteriorated together and pointed to drug price revisions and a strong dollar as background.

Hwail Pharm's revenue decline can be read as the combined effect of these industry factors and the 2022 fire that the company cites. Based on disclosed figures alone, it is difficult to separate how much each factor contributed.

On the customer side, relationships with about 300 domestic pharmaceutical companies are cited as a strength. Exports to Japan and Europe are affected by local regulation and currency movements.

A raw material supplier does not set the price of finished products, so it is exposed to procurement policy changes at its customers. Compared with innovative drug developers in the same bio and pharma sector, its R&D scale and earnings structure differ.

The newly added business objectives in semiconductors, AI and tourism have no direct link to the raw materials market. Whether the industry is recovering can be tracked through revenue trends in the raw materials segment and the operating margin.

Outlook

The share sale was disclosed on September 15, 2026. Two existing shareholders agreed to sell 2,343,750 shares, a 27.84 percent stake, to a technology investment fund for KRW 75 billion. A deposit of KRW 20 billion has been paid, and the remaining KRW 55 billion is due on November 19, 2026.

The fund becomes the largest shareholder once the balance is paid and shares are transferred. On the same timeline, the company decided on a third-party rights offering of about KRW 10 billion and a convertible bond issue of about KRW 10 billion, both disclosed as operating funds.

In a November 5, 2026 disclosure, the company proposed adding 23 business objectives to its articles, including semiconductor elements, AI data services, hot spring and spa operations, and hotel and resort operations.

These proposals will be voted on at an extraordinary shareholders meeting in Pyeongtaek on November 20, 2026, alongside the election of four directors and one standing auditor. According to InfoStock Daily, cash and financial assets on a standalone basis at the end of the first half of 2026 totaled KRW 92.7 billion.

In the same report, the company said it had not received any plan for using funds after the change of control. The next periodic report is the 2026 annual report, which will include fourth-quarter operating results. Follow-up proceedings may continue after the first-instance ruling on the 2022 fire case on May 28, 2026.

Risk factors

Production safety and legal risk

A 2022 fire at the Sangsin-ri plant killed one person and injured more than ten. Production was halted in October 2022 and partially resumed in March 2023. On May 28, 2026 the first-instance court sentenced the former CEO to two years in prison, suspended for three years, and fined the company KRW 200 million. The company cites the 2022 fire as a factor in the 2025 loss.

Dilution and funding

In September 2026 the company decided on a third-party rights offering of about KRW 10 billion and a convertible bond issue of about KRW 10 billion, both for operating funds.

A bond investment firm acquired 1,205,690 convertible bonds on October 8, 2026, raising its holding ratio to 12.53 percent, as disclosed on November 2, 2026. Conversion would increase the number of shares outstanding.

Control transition and business expansion

Daily Pharm reported that the largest shareholder is changing for the fourth time in about five years. The investor fund's main contributor is Dynamic Solution, and a separate investment firm operates the fund.

The new business objectives disclosed on November 5, 2026 cover semiconductors, AI and resorts, which are far from raw materials. With both the controlling party and the business direction changing, uncertainty over management continuity remains.
