Two Businesses, Persistent Losses and Volatile Quarters

02

Business structure

Mhethanol, founded in 1978, manufactures industrial ethanol and runs its operations from Changwon in South Gyeongsang Province through two business units, ethanol and golf. Based on second-quarter 2026 sales, the mix is 64% ethanol and 36% golf.

The ethanol unit supplies alcohol to beverage, food and pharmaceutical companies and accounts for a majority of revenue. Ethanol is a largely standardized input, so cost levels and stable supply are the main competitive factors.

The golf unit operates both membership and public golf courses in Changwon, and its proximity to Busan and the wider South Gyeongsang region is cited as an advantage. Golf revenue moves with visitor numbers, so it is directly exposed to consumer conditions and travel demand.

On a consolidated basis the group includes four subsidiaries: MH Biotech (machinery manufacturing), Yongwon Development (golf operations), Jinhae Ocean Resort and Jinhae Ocean. Because golf and equipment results sit in the same consolidated figures as ethanol, segment profit and loss should be read separately. Choi Dong-ho and four related parties held 52.37% as of October 14, 2026.

04

Earnings analysis

Annual sales fell from KRW 110.7 billion in 2022 to KRW 105.5 billion in 2023 (down 4.7% year on year), KRW 100.4 billion in 2024 (down 4.8%) and KRW 94.7 billion in 2025 (down 5.7%), a decline in each of the last three years. 2022 is the largest year in the 2022–2025 window and 2025 is the smallest.

Operating profit went from KRW 21.9 billion in 2022 to KRW 18.9 billion in 2023, KRW 10.7 billion in 2024 (down 43.6%) and KRW 9.49 billion in 2025 (down 11.1%), and the operating margin narrowed from 19.8% to 10.0%.

Net profit attributable to controlling shareholders swung from KRW 4.67 billion in 2023 to a net loss of KRW 3.11 billion in 2024, then a net loss of KRW 2.38 billion in 2025, a smaller loss.

Net loss including non-controlling interests was KRW 19.6 billion in 2024 and KRW 11.8 billion in 2025, a wider loss than the controlling-shareholder figure.

The company attributed the 2025 decline to lower ethanol sales and fewer golf visitors, and cited lower financial costs and a deferred tax effect for the narrower net loss. That makes the 2025 loss reduction worth reading separately from any recovery in operations.

Quarterly, an operating loss of KRW 441 million in the third quarter of 2025 turned into an operating profit of KRW 1.12 billion in the fourth quarter, then KRW 500 million in the first quarter of 2026, KRW 3.21 billion in the second quarter (up 541.6% quarter on quarter) and an operating loss of KRW 1.39 billion in the third quarter.

Sales peaked at KRW 23.4 billion in the fourth quarter of 2025 and have declined for three straight quarters since, with the third quarter of 2026 down 22.9% year on year. Net loss attributable to controlling shareholders over the last four quarters totals KRW 4.08 billion.

05

Industry analysis

Ethanol is an intermediate input used by beverage, food and pharmaceutical producers, so demand follows final-product consumption, particularly alcohol, and production plans in food and pharmaceuticals.

A corporate monitoring summary as of June 2026 described lower alcohol sales in the ethanol unit, citing a weaker economy and changing drinking habits. The same summary said gross profit improved on cost reductions.

In volume terms demand is under pressure, while unit profitability is being defended through cost management. This matches the direction of reported sales, which have declined for three consecutive years since 2023.

The golf unit faces rising overseas golf travel and weaker domestic visitor numbers amid the economic slowdown, according to the same summary. Among listed food and beverage companies, Hite Jinro and Lotte Chilsung Beverage operate in alcohol and beverages and face similar consumption trends.

Mhethanol does not disclose detailed ethanol volume and pricing by product in a way that allows a clean share comparison with rivals. By sector classification the company sits within food and tobacco on the KOSPI, and it is grouped with consumer staples in the broader index.

06

Outlook

Future earnings can be assessed along three lines. The first is ethanol volume and cost. Alcohol sales fell in the first quarter of 2026, but gross profit improved on cost cuts, so the question is whether margins can hold without a volume recovery.

The second is golf visitor flows: with overseas golf demand rising, how well the company captures demand from its Busan and South Gyeongsang catchment will shape revenue. The third is financing costs and tax items.

The lower financial costs and deferred tax effect cited for the narrower 2025 net loss need to recur before they can be read as operating improvement.

On governance, following a share-cancellation resolution disclosed on August 25, 2026, the stake held by Choi Dong-ho and related parties rose to 52.37% as of October 14, 2026. That reflects the cancellation of 1,177,093 treasury shares, which reduced voting shares outstanding to 6,201,433.

Second-quarter operating profit of KRW 3.21 billion and third-quarter operating loss of KRW 1.39 billion point in opposite directions, so the next annual report will show whether the gap was one-off or structural.

08

Bull factors

Second-quarter profit rebound and cost gains

Operating profit rose to KRW 3.21 billion in the second quarter of 2026, up 541.6% from the prior quarter. Gross profit also improved in the first quarter of 2026 on cost reductions even as alcohol sales declined. If these trends hold, there is room to recover some of the margin decline since 2022.

The third-quarter operating loss of KRW 1.39 billion, however, means sustainability will be tested by coming quarters.

Operating cash flow improved in 2025

Operating cash flow was KRW 16.5 billion in 2022, KRW 19.2 billion in 2023, KRW 17 billion in 2024 and KRW 23.1 billion in 2025, the highest of the four years. Cash generation held up in 2024 and 2025 even as the company posted net losses.

Because accounting losses and cash flow diverge, the question is whether that cash supports debt repayment capacity.

Larger controlling stake after share cancellation

Choi Dong-ho and four related parties held 52.37% as of October 14, 2026, up 8.48 percentage points from the June 1, 2026 report date. The increase partly reflects the cancellation of 1,177,093 treasury shares, which reduced total voting shares.

On February 2, 2026, executive director Kwon Kyung-rok disclosed open-market purchases that increased his holding. These are factual changes in ownership structure.

Bear factors

Sales have fallen for three straight quarters

Sales peaked at KRW 23.4 billion in the fourth quarter of 2025 and then fell to KRW 21 billion in the first quarter of 2026, KRW 17.5 billion in the second and KRW 17.4 billion in the third, a third straight quarterly decline. Third-quarter 2026 sales were down 22.9% year on year.

Lower alcohol sales in ethanol and fewer golf visitors are pressuring revenue at the same time. If declines continue, a fixed cost base could limit profit recovery.

Losses for controlling shareholders persist

Net losses attributable to controlling shareholders were KRW 3.11 billion in 2024 and KRW 2.38 billion in 2025, two straight loss years. The third quarter of 2026 posted a controlling-shareholder net loss of KRW 791 million. A profit of KRW 1.87 billion in the first quarter of 2026 was not sustained into the following two quarters.

Heavy debt burden

The debt ratio improved from 421.0% in 2024 but remains high. With annual operating profit near KRW 10 billion, financing costs are a structural constraint on earnings. Because lower financial costs helped narrow the 2025 net loss, changes in rates and borrowing structure feed directly into reported profit.

Risk factors

Alcohol demand and drinking habits

Ethanol sales track alcohol consumption, so a weaker economy and changing drinking habits affect volumes directly. A June 2026 summary pointed to lower alcohol sales in the ethanol unit for these reasons. Cost cuts have offset part of the pressure, but cumulative volume declines could eventually limit margin defense.

Fewer golf visitors

The golf unit has seen lower revenue as domestic visitors decline amid overseas golf travel and a weaker economy. The company also cited fewer golf visitors among the drivers of the 2025 sales decline.

Proximity to Busan and South Gyeongsang is an advantage, but if the travel shift is structural, recovery could be slow.

Earnings quality and balance sheet

The company cited lower financial costs and a deferred tax effect as reasons the 2025 net loss narrowed. These sit outside core operations and need to be read separately from any operating recovery.

With liabilities of KRW 198.6 billion, continued reliance on such non-operating items would add uncertainty to earnings interpretation.

Overall view

Mhethanol's sales have declined for three consecutive years, with ethanol at 64% and golf at 36% of the mix, and net losses attributable to controlling shareholders have continued for two years.

Second-quarter 2026 operating profit of KRW 3.21 billion points to a possible recovery, but the third quarter swung back to an operating loss of KRW 1.39 billion. Net losses attributable to controlling shareholders over the last four quarters total KRW 4.08 billion.

Positive factors include gross profit gains from cost cuts, the highest operating cash flow of the 2022–2025 period in 2025 and a higher stake held by the controlling family. Negative factors include continued sales declines, fewer golf visitors and a debt ratio of 317.5%.

The price-to-book ratio sits below its past year-end range, which can be read as reflecting both the losses and the heavy liability structure. The next checkpoints are the 2026 annual preliminary results and the 2026 annual report, which will show whether operating margin and the effect of lower financing costs hold.
