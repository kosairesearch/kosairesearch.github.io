02

Business structure

THE E&M was founded in 2002 and listed on KOSDAQ in 2006. The company operates the one-person media platform Popcorn TV and also runs MCN and talent management businesses.

According to WiseReport, the company holds seven consolidated subsidiaries, including the KOSDAQ-listed Bessel, which manufactures inkjet media and display equipment.

A JobKorea aggregation as of March 2026 shows display materials and system manufacturing at 63 percent, other personal broadcasting platform activities at 27 percent, other talent management activities at 8 percent, and MCN and management at 1 percent.

Another category on the same site shows semiconductor manufacturing machinery at 60 percent and other platform activities at 31 percent, so the segment weights differ depending on the classification basis.

WiseReport also noted that eco-solvent inkjet media and high-function film product lines are expanding within other business segments. On the platform side, the company ran a reward event for Popcorn TV paying users in July 2026 to attract more users.

The SOM:CITY video and culture complex in Cheongna was selected by IFEZ, with the THE E&M consortium named as preferred negotiating party, and the company has spent more than three years consulting with foreign investors and video industry specialists on participation.

The company overlaps with SOOP in live streaming and with CJ ENM in media content, but its total equity of KRW 55.3 billion is smaller than those large media listed companies.

Accurate segment sales should be read from segment information in the periodic reports, and the aggregator weights are best treated as a reference.

04

Earnings analysis

Annual results show falling sales and persistent losses at the same time. Sales fell from KRW 46.6 billion in 2022 to KRW 31.7 billion in 2023 (down 32.0% year on year), KRW 22.2 billion in 2024 (down 30.0%), and KRW 12.3 billion in 2025 (down 44.4%), declining for three consecutive years from 2023 through 2025.

Among the years 2022 to 2025, 2022 was the largest year by sales and 2025 the smallest.

The operating loss was KRW 2.31 billion in 2022, KRW 7.72 billion in 2023 (losses widened year on year), KRW 7.47 billion in 2024 (losses narrowed) and KRW 8.58 billion in 2025 (losses widened), marking four consecutive years of operating losses from 2022 to 2025.

The operating margin moved from -5.0% in 2022 to -24.3% in 2023, -33.7% in 2024 and -69.5% in 2025. As sales shrank, the burden of costs that do not scale down with revenue appears to have grown relative to sales.

Attributable net losses were KRW 22.6 billion in 2022, KRW 33.6 billion in 2023, KRW 13.2 billion in 2024 and KRW 19.9 billion in 2025, and the net loss including non-controlling interests was reported at the same amounts.

In 2023 the net loss of KRW 33.6 billion was much larger than the operating loss of KRW 7.72 billion, which points to a heavy burden from non-operating items and income taxes. The debt ratio rose from 68.4% in 2022 to 190.0% in 2023 before easing to 158.0% in 2024 and 113.4% in 2025.

At the end of 2025, total equity stood at KRW 55.3 billion, of which KRW 22 billion was attributable to controlling shareholders, and total liabilities were KRW 62.8 billion.

Operating cash flow went from KRW 5.39 billion in 2022 to negative KRW 1.62 billion in 2023, negative KRW 4.93 billion in 2024 and negative KRW 8.86 billion in 2025.

In the quarterly data, third-quarter 2026 sales of KRW 6.74 billion rose 58.3% from a year earlier, and the operating loss of KRW 1.23 billion in the same quarter narrowed from a year earlier.

In the second quarter of 2026 the operating loss widened to KRW 4.07 billion, yet attributable net income turned positive at KRW 2.23 billion. The gap between the operating result and attributable net income in that quarter comes from non-operating items and taxes.

05

Industry analysis

THE E&M's core one-person media and live streaming business sits in a digital content market where individual creators produce content and viewers generate revenue through donations and payments.

In this market, the size of the viewer base and demand for donations and payments are key, and the company competes directly with large platforms such as SOOP. The sales decline from 2023 through 2025 suggests that growth in platform users has not translated into revenue recovery.

Profitability pressure is also visible across the media sector, and CJ ENM, another listed media company, disclosed a net loss of KRW 25.02 billion for the third quarter of 2026, turning it into a loss.

The SOM:CITY video and culture complex in Cheongna may connect to production demand as video industry infrastructure, but figures for project cost and completion schedule are hard to find in current public materials.

The inkjet media and display equipment business of subsidiary Bessel is exposed to the display investment cycle, and because display materials and system manufacturing shows the largest weight in the JobKorea aggregation, that cycle matters for total sales.

With total equity of KRW 55.3 billion, the company is small relative to large listed media companies, so its expansion depends meaningfully on external funding.

The Cheongna SOM:CITY project signed a financing memorandum with Korea Investment & Securities in July 2026, and a late-July 2026 report said the development plan change was advancing with a submission to a review committee targeted for September 2026.

The April disclosure listed the counterparty for that acquisition as undetermined, so the target selection remains open.

At an extraordinary shareholders meeting on October 12, 2026, two independent directors and one auditor were elected. The next periodic report is the 2026 annual report, and whether the operating margin and operating cash flow improve from 2025 levels is the next checkpoint.

Bear factors

Sales decline and operating loss structure

Sales fell from KRW 46.6 billion in 2022 to KRW 12.3 billion in 2025, declining for three consecutive years. The operating margin deteriorated from -5.0 percent in 2022 to -69.5 percent in 2025.

The 2025 operating loss of KRW 8.58 billion was larger than the KRW 2.31 billion loss in 2022, and the company has posted losses for four consecutive years.

Cash outflow and debt burden

Operating cash flow was negative KRW 1.62 billion in 2023, negative KRW 4.93 billion in 2024 and negative KRW 8.86 billion in 2025. At the end of 2025, total liabilities of KRW 62.8 billion exceeded total equity of KRW 55.3 billion, leaving a debt ratio of 113.4 percent.

Attributable equity was KRW 22 billion, and press reports put year-end 2025 cash and equivalents at about KRW 4.5 billion.

Convertible bond valuation losses and funding reliance

A May 2026 disclosure showed a cumulative valuation loss of KRW 12.7 billion on derivatives tied to convertible bonds, equal to 17.36 percent of equity. The company decided to issue convertible bonds of KRW 5.0 billion in March 2026 and KRW 10.0 billion in April 2026. It also agreed to buy back its 20th convertible bond before maturity for KRW 4.27 billion.

Risk factors

Regulation, permits and project execution

The SOM:CITY project requires a development plan change and review procedures, and the company has spent more than three years negotiating participation by foreign investors and video industry specialists.

If the review is delayed or investor participation falls through, the project timeline and funding plan could both change. Whether the July 2026 financing memorandum with Korea Investment & Securities turns into actual funding is also central to execution.

Conversion dilution and acquisitions of other companies

Conversion requests for the KRW 5.0 billion convertible bond become possible from April 10, 2027, and for the KRW 10.0 billion bond from May 8, 2027. Shares issuable on conversion equal 8.45 percent and 10.82 percent of total shares outstanding, so dilution is material.

A large portion of proceeds was allocated to acquiring other companies' securities, while the counterparty was listed as undetermined in the disclosure for the KRW 10.0 billion bond.

Subsidiary demand and display investment

Subsidiary Bessel manufactures inkjet media and display equipment, and the JobKorea aggregation showed display materials and system manufacturing as the largest segment weight. If display equipment investment slows, subsidiary sales and group results could both be affected.

With the platform business still loss-making, dependence on the manufacturing subsidiary's results also adds to the burden.

Overall view

From 2022 to 2025, THE E&M's sales fell from KRW 46.6 billion to KRW 12.3 billion, and operating losses persisted for four consecutive years.

Operating cash flow has been negative since 2023, and while the debt ratio eased from 190.0 percent in 2023 to 113.4 percent in 2025, total liabilities of KRW 62.8 billion remain large.

In 2026, quarterly sales rose 58.3 percent year on year in the third quarter and the operating loss narrowed, and attributable net income turned positive in the second quarter.

On the business side, the Cheongna SOM:CITY video and culture complex and product expansion at the manufacturing subsidiary are presented as new drivers. However, both depend on funding and review timelines, and the convertible bonds issued in 2026 can convert into shares from 2027, remaining a dilution factor.

Attributable net losses over the last four quarters total KRW 25.6 billion, so one profitable quarter is not enough to treat the annual trend as changed. The next key checkpoint is whether the operating margin and operating cash flow improve from 2025 levels in the 2026 annual report.
