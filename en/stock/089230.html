02

Business structure

THE E&M was founded in 2002, listed on KOSDAQ in 2006, and was renamed THE E&M in 2016. The company describes itself as a provider of live-streaming-based media and entertainment services.

Its core platform is the real-time personal broadcasting service Popcorn TV, which runs a donation-based revenue loop between broadcasters and viewers. Through subsidiaries it operates an MCN business that manages live-streaming creators and a management business centered on actors.

Reported segments are Internet, Entertainment and Other, and in the first half of 2026 their revenue shares were 27.3%, 9.4% and 63.2%, according to an Asia Economy report dated October 29, 2026.

The Other segment is more than twice the size of Internet, so the revenue mix has moved away from the platform, although the detailed composition of Other is hard to pin down from press reports alone.

Separately, the company holds a 30.1% stake in the project financing vehicle (PFV) for the Cheongna SOM CITY development, adding a real estate development axis.

On competition, a February 2024 DART disclosure, as summarized by a financial information site, cited intensifying competition in the media platform market as a reason for lower revenue. The largest shareholder and related parties held 13.86% as of August 10, 2026. Popcorn TV has run promotions for paying users that offer rewards of up to 10%.

04

Earnings analysis

Based on disclosed annual results, revenue went from KRW 46.6 billion in 2022 to KRW 31.7 billion in 2023 (down 32.0% year on year), KRW 22.2 billion in 2024 (down 30.0%), and KRW 12.3 billion in 2025 (down 44.4%).

Revenue has declined for three consecutive years from 2023 through 2025, with 2022 the largest year and 2025 the smallest in the 2022–2025 range.

Operating losses were KRW 2.31 billion in 2022, KRW 7.72 billion in 2023, KRW 7.47 billion in 2024 and KRW 8.58 billion in 2025, which the filings record as four consecutive years of losses.

Operating margins moved from -5.0% to -24.3%, -33.7% and -69.5%, which reflects costs that did not fall in step with shrinking revenue. The 2024 loss narrowed slightly, but in 2025 the operating loss widened again as revenue fell 44.4%.

Net loss attributable to controlling shareholders was KRW 22.6 billion, KRW 33.6 billion, KRW 13.2 billion and KRW 19.9 billion over 2022–2025, and total net loss is recorded at the same values.

The debt-to-equity ratio jumped from 68.4% in 2022 to 190.0% in 2023 before easing to 113.4% in 2025, yet total liabilities of KRW 62.8 billion at year-end 2025 still exceeded total equity of KRW 55.3 billion.

Operating cash flow was KRW 5.39 billion positive in 2022, turned negative in 2023, and reached KRW -8.86 billion in 2025, the largest outflow in the period.

On a quarterly basis, revenue was KRW 6.2 billion in the first quarter of 2026, KRW 7.33 billion in the second (up 18.2% quarter on quarter) and KRW 6.74 billion in the third (down 8.0% quarter on quarter, up 58.3% year on year). No quarterly revenue data is available for the fourth quarter of 2025.

Operating losses have run for five consecutive quarters from the third quarter of 2025 through the third quarter of 2026, and the third quarter of 2026 saw the loss narrow as revenue grew.

Net loss attributable to controlling shareholders was KRW 3.92 billion in the third quarter of 2025, KRW 8.4 billion in the fourth quarter and KRW 16.7 billion in the first quarter of 2026.

It then turned to a KRW 2.23 billion profit in the second quarter of 2026 before returning to a KRW 2.7 billion loss in the third quarter.

The net loss in the first quarter of 2026 was far larger than the operating loss, which suggests non-operating items had a large effect, although the quarterly figures alone cannot pin down the cause. Cumulative net loss attributable to controlling shareholders over the last four quarters is KRW 25.6 billion. The financial information site WiseReport recorded first-quarter 2026 revenue up 97.8% year on year.

05

Industry analysis

Live-streaming and personal broadcasting platforms run on donation-driven revenue between broadcasters and viewers, and in Korea several platforms compete for users and creators.

In a February 2024 disclosure, the company cited intensifying competition in the media platform market as a cause of lower revenue, and said the resulting fixed-cost burden enlarged its operating loss. That explanation comes from a financial information site summarizing the filing.

The same site classifies the company under IT services, within software, on KOSDAQ. Among listed peers, SOOP (Soop) is the closest comparison as another live-streaming platform operator.

CJ ENM reported third-quarter 2026 revenue of KRW 1.1904 trillion and operating profit of KRW 221 billion, but its mix of content production and distribution differs enough that direct metric comparisons are limited.

Platform businesses depend on user acquisition and promotion spending, and the reward promotion run for paying users in July and August 2026 is an example of that cost structure.

Quarterly revenue has stayed in the KRW 6–7 billion range in 2026, but whether that growth comes from platform users or from other businesses needs to be checked against the body of each quarterly filing. On an annual basis, the three-year revenue decline through 2025 has not yet reversed.

The core industry questions are therefore whether the platform user base stabilizes and whether promotion costs scale with revenue.

06

Outlook

The most significant business variable for the company is the Cheongna SOM CITY video and cultural complex development.

On October 19, 2026, the Free Economic Zone Committee under the Ministry of Trade, Industry and Energy approved the revised Cheongna development plan as submitted, which created the administrative basis for the project (Asia Economy report dated October 29, 2026).

The total project cost is KRW 1.7894 trillion, funded by PFV equity of KRW 100 billion, financial institution borrowings of about KRW 776.9 billion and sales proceeds from unit sales. The company holds a 30.1% stake in the PFV and has committed to a 10-year long-term operation of the video production facilities.

Reports cite development profit from unit sales and long-term operating income from the production facilities as the main future growth drivers.

Two large construction firms are responsible for construction, Shadowbox Studios, a global studio operator, will run the facilities, and a Korean brokerage will design the financing structure and raise funds. In July 2026 the company signed a financing memorandum of understanding with that brokerage.

The Incheon Free Economic Zone Authority has said it intends to publish the revised development plan within this year. The next checkpoints are whether permitting and construction steps proceed after the notice, whether a sales schedule is disclosed, and whether the debt financing closes.

Because much of the project cost relies on borrowings and sales proceeds, financing progress will determine the timeline. The core platform business, Popcorn TV, has not yet shown a clear user growth or monetization trend in quarterly results.

Bull factors

Cheongna project moves forward

The revised development plan was approved as submitted in October 2026, which created the administrative basis for the project. A research analyst said concerns about delays that ran for about four years after the 2022 tender had largely been eased.

The total cost is KRW 1.7894 trillion, and the company's 30.1% PFV stake means development results would flow through its ownership structure as the project advances.

Quarterly revenue rebound and narrower losses

Third-quarter 2026 revenue was KRW 6.74 billion, up 58.3% year on year. The operating loss that quarter was KRW 1.23 billion, narrower than the KRW 2.92 billion loss in the fourth quarter of 2025. Second-quarter 2026 also recorded a return to net profit attributable to controlling shareholders of KRW 2.23 billion.

Efforts to broaden revenue beyond the platform

Subsidiaries run an MCN business and an actor management business, which provide revenue sources beyond the platform. According to press reports, the Other segment accounted for 63.2% of first-half 2026 revenue, larger than the 27.3% from Internet. Its profit contribution cannot be separated from summary reports alone.

Bear factors

Cumulative losses and financial strain

The company recorded four consecutive years of losses from 2022 through 2025, with a 2025 operating margin of -69.5%. Total liabilities of KRW 62.8 billion exceeded total equity of KRW 55.3 billion at year-end 2025. Operating cash flow in 2025 was KRW -8.86 billion, the largest outflow in the period.

Dilution from convertible bonds

According to a Saramin company profile, convertible bonds were issued in April and May 2026 with face values of KRW 8.5 billion, KRW 5 billion and KRW 10 billion. Press reports said conversions from several series would lead to the listing of about 3.69 million new shares. Such new supply can dilute existing shareholders' stakes.

Transparency concerns raised in media

One media outlet reported that the stock was designated as a caution-listed issue due to excessive buying by a small number of accounts and described signs of concentrated accumulation.

The same report noted that previously issued convertible bonds were sold to individuals and funds whose stakes stay below 5%, which places them outside mandatory disclosure. These points are press allegations, not regulatory findings.

Risk factors

Financing and funding

Press reports said accumulated deficit at the end of last year stood at KRW 120.8 billion, with cash equivalents of about KRW 4.5 billion. Successive convertible bond issuances in 2026 indicate continued reliance on outside funding.

With operating cash flow negative since 2023, any tightening in funding conditions could reduce operating flexibility.

Dilution and share supply

Listing of new shares from conversions increases the share count, and holders may sell into the market. Press reports said large-scale conversion requests combined with sharp price gains could expand profit-taking supply. The timing and size of that supply are difficult to predict from public information.

Project execution

The Cheongna project saw delay concerns for about four years after its 2022 tender. Because about KRW 776.9 billion of financial institution borrowings is a large part of funding, delays in financing could push back the whole schedule. If permitting and construction slip, the timing of sales proceeds and operating income would also move later.

Overall view

THE E&M posted the hardest year in the disclosed period in 2025, with annual revenue of KRW 12.3 billion, an operating loss of KRW 8.58 billion and an operating margin of -69.5%.

In 2026, quarterly revenue has stayed at or above KRW 6.2 billion, at KRW 6.2 billion, KRW 7.33 billion and KRW 6.74 billion, showing signs of recovery.

The operating loss narrowed to KRW 1.23 billion in the third quarter of 2026, but a net loss attributable to controlling shareholders of KRW 2.7 billion returned, so quarterly swings remain large.

The debt-to-equity ratio of 113.4%, total liabilities of KRW 62.8 billion and operating cash flow of KRW -8.86 billion in 2025 show that financial strain remains. Convertible bond issuance and conversion requests are feeding into new share supply.

The Cheongna SOM CITY project, with a total cost of KRW 1.7894 trillion, has gained a base for progress after its revised plan was approved as submitted in October 2026. Strengths are the revenue recovery and the large development project, while weaknesses are cumulative losses, dilution and reliance on borrowings.

The next items to check are the publication of the development plan, the full-year operating loss and cash flow in the 2026 annual report, and the pace of convertible bond conversions.
