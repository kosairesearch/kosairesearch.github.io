02

Business structure

Newflex Technology Co., Ltd. is a KOSDAQ-listed electronic parts maker focused on flexible printed circuit boards (FPCB), with a market capitalization of roughly KRW 90.4 billion.

Its core products are camera module FPCB and wireless-charging module FPCB for smartphones, and the company maintains a strategy of focusing on flagship models such as the Galaxy S series and foldable phones, targeting high-value-added segments rather than low- and mid-range products.

Its customers include the domestic smartphone supply chain and Meta Platforms, for whose XR devices it has supplied camera-module FPCB. More recently, the company has expanded beyond camera modules into display-module applications, diversifying its product portfolio.

In March 2026, the company said it was co-developing smart-glasses components with Meta and preparing mass-production supply targeting a domestic launch in July.

As the auto industry shifts toward EVs and autonomous driving, demand for automotive electronics and EV battery FPCB is rising, and Newflex is expanding beyond its mobile-centric structure into vehicle components and wearable-robot FPCB.

It was also reported to have won a new order for vision-sensing FPCB from a domestic humanoid robot maker. Production is concentrated in Vietnamese subsidiaries rather than Korea, and a company official said it currently produces no products in Korea, running all production sites in Vietnam.

The domestic FPCB market is described as facing intensifying competition from rigid-PCB makers entering the space, low-cost offerings from China and Southeast Asia, and advanced technology from Japan and the United States.

04

Earnings analysis

Consolidated revenue fell for three straight years, from KRW 260.3 billion in 2022 to KRW 195.1 billion in 2023 (down 25.0% year on year), KRW 175.9 billion in 2024 (down 9.8%), and KRW 154.1 billion in 2025 (down 12.4%).

Over the same period, operating profit dropped from KRW 17.9 billion in 2022 to KRW 10.3 billion in 2023 (down 42.3%), rebounded to KRW 14.6 billion in 2024 (up 41.8%), then fell again to KRW 9.23 billion in 2025 (down 36.9%).

The operating margin fluctuated at 6.9% in 2022, 5.3% in 2023, 8.3% in 2024, and 6.0% in 2025. Controlling-interest net income fell to KRW 6.79 billion in 2023, jumped to KRW 16.1 billion in 2024 (up 137.1%), then shrank again to KRW 8.8 billion in 2025 (down 45.3%).

Quarterly swings were even wider: Q1 2026 operating profit fell to KRW 291 million, down 90.9% quarter on quarter, before rising to KRW 1.42 billion in Q2 2026, up 387.4% quarter on quarter but still down 56.2% year on year.

Analysts attributed the weakness to a combination of declining share within the Galaxy S series, weak sales of the prior year's low-cost Quest model, and a supply bottleneck for a North American customer's new display-equipped AI glasses.

Battery FPCB was also said to be suffering from prolonged weak demand, with revenue recognition continuing to lag behind the order backlog. Operating cash flow fell from KRW 23.7 billion in 2022 to KRW 5.89 billion in 2023, rose to KRW 42.7 billion in 2024, then eased again to KRW 14.7 billion in 2025.

The year-end 2025 debt ratio of 51.7% was sharply lower than 162.3% in 2022, and total equity stood at KRW 108.4 billion, entirely attributable to controlling shareholders.

05

Industry analysis

FPCB is a flexible circuit board that can bend, fold, and curl, used in camera modules, wireless charging, and foldable displays as electronics trend toward miniaturization and lighter weight.

The domestic FPCB market is seen as facing intensifying competition from rigid-PCB makers entering the space, low-cost offerings from China and Southeast Asia, and advanced technology from Japan and the United States.

Smartphone end demand has matured, so the share of high-value flagship and foldable models continues to determine performance. At the same time, end-market applications are diversifying into VR/AR devices, smart glasses, EV and autonomous-vehicle electronics, and robotics.

Meta Platforms said it plans to expand smart-glasses production to more than ten million units by the end of 2026 to meet demand, a variable that could affect supply volumes for related parts makers.

As the auto industry shifts toward EVs and autonomous driving, demand for automotive electronics and EV battery FPCB is also rising rapidly. Amid this application expansion, competition in capacity investment among FPCB makers with Southeast Asian production bases also appears to be intensifying.

Outlook

Meritz Securities forecast in a December 2025 report that 2026 consolidated revenue would reach KRW 184.4 billion (up 17.9% year on year) and operating profit KRW 15.4 billion (up 64.8%), suggesting a possible return to revenue growth after four years.

However, disclosed Q1 2026 revenue (KRW 35 billion) plus Q2 2026 revenue (KRW 37.1 billion) sum to KRW 72.1 billion, a trajectory that would require a substantial increase in the second half to reach that annual estimate.

In March 2026, the company said it was co-developing smart-glasses components with Meta and preparing mass-production supply targeting a domestic launch in July. Two Meta smart-glasses models slated for a 2026 launch were also reported to be under exclusive development by Newflex.

Vision-sensing FPCB for a domestic humanoid robot maker was reported to begin supply from the first half of 2026, though initial volumes were described as small.

In July 2026, the company planned to use the KRW 52.8 billion proceeds from the Ansan plant sale to upgrade its Vietnam production base and fund new process investment, making the pace of that buildout a variable for future cost structure.

EV battery FPCB revenue recognition continues to lag the order backlog, so the timing of actual sales conversion is likely to determine the speed of any earnings recovery.

Bull factors

Diversifying FPCB Applications

Newflex has been identified as the only Korean parts maker supplying core FPCB for Meta's AI glasses. Two Meta smart-glasses models slated for a 2026 launch were also reported to be under its exclusive development.

The company is also expanding into EV battery, automotive electronics, and humanoid robot vision-sensing FPCB, gradually spreading a revenue base once concentrated in smartphones.

Improving Balance Sheet

The debt ratio fell sharply from 162.3% in 2022 to 51.7% in 2025, and year-end 2025 total equity rose to KRW 108.4 billion. In July 2026, the company planned to use the KRW 52.8 billion from the Ansan plant sale for Vietnam production investment.

Operating cash flow also showed swings in cash-generating ability, rising from KRW 5.89 billion in 2023 to KRW 42.7 billion in 2024.

Signs of Earnings Rebound

Controlling-interest net income recovered to KRW 16.1 billion in 2024, up 137.1% year on year. Q2 2026 controlling-interest net income rose to KRW 1.58 billion, up 6.6% quarter on quarter and 47.5% year on year. The trailing-four-quarter total of KRW 10 billion also exceeded the full-year 2025 figure of KRW 8.8 billion.

Bear factors

Third Straight Year of Revenue Decline

Revenue peaked at KRW 260.3 billion in 2022 and declined for three straight years through 2025, falling to KRW 154.1 billion. The 2025 operating margin of 6.0% was lower than 8.3% in 2024, breaking the prior improvement trend.

With revenue weighted toward smartphone flagship models, earnings have repeatedly shown sensitivity to shifts in major customers' market share.

Quarterly Earnings Volatility

Operating profit plunged 90.9% quarter on quarter to KRW 291 million in Q1 2026 before rebounding to KRW 1.42 billion in Q2 2026, still 56.2% below the year-earlier level.

This was attributed to a combination of declining share within the Galaxy S series and a supply bottleneck for a North American customer's new AI glasses. The lag between order bookings and actual revenue recognition is cited as a structural factor behind quarterly volatility.

New Businesses Still Small Contributors

EV battery FPCB was reported to be suffering from prolonged weak demand, with revenue recognition continuing to lag the order backlog. Vision-sensing FPCB for a domestic humanoid robot maker was also described as starting with small initial volumes.

With new businesses still accounting for a limited share of revenue, the smartphone- and XR-centric revenue structure is unlikely to change substantially in the near term.

10

Risk factors

Customer Concentration Risk

Newflex's revenue is heavily dependent on a small number of smartphone set makers and Meta Platforms. Changes in major customers' new-product timing, market share, or order volume feed directly into quarterly results.

While customer diversification is under way, it does not yet appear to have substantially reduced concentration in revenue terms.

FX and Overseas Production Risk

With production concentrated in Vietnamese subsidiaries, fluctuations in the won/dollar and Vietnamese dong exchange rates and local labor/logistics costs can directly affect costs.

With the domestic production base shrinking after the Ansan plant sale, any delay in Vietnam capacity investment could also push back capacity-expansion timelines. A production structure increasingly weighted overseas also raises exposure to external variables such as logistics and tariffs.

Intensifying Competition

The domestic FPCB market is seen as facing intensifying competition from rigid-PCB makers entering the space, low-cost offerings from China and Southeast Asia, and advanced technology from Japan and the United States.

In new applications such as EV battery FPCB, other makers with Southeast Asian production bases are also increasing capacity investment, which could widen competition. Intensifying price competition could weigh on the pace of operating-margin recovery.

12

Overall view

Newflex Technology Co., Ltd. is at a transition point, centered on smartphone camera and wireless-charging FPCB while expanding into Meta Platforms' XR/AI glasses supply and EV battery, automotive electronics, and humanoid robot FPCB applications.

Still, annual revenue declined for three straight years from 2023 through 2025, and the 2025 operating margin fell from the prior year, so the profitability recovery has not been smooth.

On a quarterly basis, swings were wide, with operating profit plunging to KRW 291 million in Q1 2026 before rebounding to KRW 1.42 billion in Q2 2026, reflecting how the lag between order bookings and revenue recognition repeatedly disrupts the earnings trend.

The balance sheet has improved, with the debt ratio falling from 162.3% in 2022 to 51.7% in 2025 and additional liquidity secured from the Ansan plant sale.

New businesses such as EV battery and robot FPCB remain an early-stage, small share of revenue, leaving the timing of actual mass production and revenue recognition as a key variable for future performance.

Results disclosed from Q3 2026 onward, together with progress on Vietnam production investment, warrant continued monitoring.
