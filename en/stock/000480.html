Refractory Holding Company: Quarterly Profit Swings Back to Positive

CR Holdings is a holding company centered on its refractory subsidiary Chosun Refractories, which posted controlling-shareholder losses in 2024 and 2025 before returning to a profit in the third quarter of 2026.

02

Business structure

In July 2023 CR Holdings split its refractory business into a new subsidiary, Chosun Refractories, and the original listed company remained as the holding company.

Chosun Refractories manufactures and sells refractories used in high-temperature industries such as steel, cement and glass, and CR Holdings held a 59.03% stake in it as of December 2024.

The group also includes a sintered machine-parts maker in which it holds a 51.00% stake, a golf course operator in which it holds 50.00%, a fine ceramics research and manufacturing company, and an industrial furnace business. The consolidated group comprised 29 companies as of December 2025.

Local press has reported that the core subsidiary holds a dominant position in the domestic refractory market. Refractory demand tracks steel and cement production, so group earnings depend on the operating rates of those downstream industries.

Diversification into machine parts, ceramics, leisure and real estate broadens the earnings base but also makes the group harder to compare with pure refractory producers. Headcount was 35 as of March 2026.

04

Earnings analysis

Annual revenue rose from KRW 798 billion in 2022 to KRW 816.9 billion in 2023, up 2.4% year on year, then fell to KRW 810.8 billion in 2024, down 0.7%, before reaching KRW 827.8 billion in 2025, up 2.1%, the largest year of 2022 to 2025.

Operating profit peaked at KRW 39.4 billion in 2023, the largest year of the period, and fell to KRW 17.4 billion in 2024, the smallest year, before rising 59.6% to KRW 27.8 billion in 2025. Operating margin moved from 2.8% in 2022 to 4.8% in 2023, 2.2% in 2024 and 3.4% in 2025.

In 2024 revenue slipped slightly while operating profit fell 55.8%, and net income attributable to controlling shareholders turned to a loss of KRW 2.34 billion.

Controlling-shareholder net income fell from KRW 40.9 billion in 2022 to KRW 405 million in 2023, a 99.0% decline, and in 2025 it became a net loss of KRW 27.4 billion, which widened the loss.

Net income including non-controlling interests was a net loss of KRW 18.8 billion in 2025, which differs from the controlling-shareholder figure because of non-controlling interests. Operating cash flow was KRW 81.1 billion in 2024 and KRW 32.4 billion in 2025.

Quarterly operating profit moved from KRW 18.3 billion in Q3 2025 to an operating loss of KRW 5.02 billion in Q4 2025, then KRW 10.2 billion in Q1 2026, KRW 62.45 million in Q2 2026 and KRW 1.56 billion in Q3 2026.

Revenue peaked at KRW 205.7 billion in Q1 2026 and then declined for two straight quarters to KRW 200.8 billion and KRW 195.6 billion, with Q3 revenue down 9.8% year on year. Over the last four quarters, the controlling-shareholder net loss totals KRW 14 billion.

05

Industry analysis

Refractories are consumable materials lining high-temperature equipment such as blast furnaces, converters and cement kilns, so steel and cement production is the starting point of demand. Volumes generally move with operating rates and maintenance cycles rather than new capacity.

Domestic companies grouped with it in a database include Sewoo Steel, Solum, Daehan Steel and Byuksan. Sewoo Steel and Daehan Steel are steel-linked and rely on the same downstream industries.

On the cycle, operating profit peaked in 2023, bottomed in 2024 and recovered in 2025, and quarterly swings have been wide in 2026. Production statistics from downstream steel and cement industries serve as the leading indicator for this group's demand.

The refractory business forms the core of group revenue, and non-steel businesses such as the golf course and real estate leasing absorb part of the earnings swings.

06

Outlook

In the third quarter of 2026 controlling-shareholder net income was positive, but operating profit was only KRW 1.56 billion, so the recovery at the operating level remains limited. Revenue has declined for two straight quarters since its first-quarter 2026 peak.

Operating margin of 3.4% in 2025 was below 4.8% in 2023, so margin recovery is the next challenge even if revenue holds. The operating rate of Chosun Refractories and steel and cement demand will drive next-quarter revenue.

The 2026 annual report is the next regular filing and will show the full-year operating margin and the direction of controlling-shareholder profit. Any preliminary fourth-quarter 2026 figure may differ from the audited result.

On the balance sheet, the debt ratio fell from 96.9% in 2024 to 93.7% in 2025, and whether that trend continues is another point to track.

Asset base and lower leverage

Refractory market position and diversification

Local press has reported that Chosun Refractories holds a dominant position in the domestic refractory market.

Several subsidiaries, including a sintered parts maker, a golf course operator and a ceramics business, partly spread exposure beyond a single industry cycle, and non-steel assets such as the golf course and leasing can buffer earnings swings.

Bear factors

Two straight years of controlling-shareholder losses

Controlling-shareholder net income fell to KRW 405 million in 2023, then turned into a KRW 2.34 billion loss in 2024 and widened to a KRW 27.4 billion loss in 2025. Net income including non-controlling interests was a KRW 18.8 billion loss in 2025, so annual earnings have not yet recovered.

Thin operating margin and sharp quarterly profit drop

Operating margin was 3.4% in 2025, and third-quarter 2026 operating profit of KRW 1.56 billion fell 91.5% year on year. The controlling-shareholder net loss over the last four quarters totals KRW 14 billion, and Q2 2026 recorded a KRW 7.91 billion loss.

Revenue decline and slower cash generation

Revenue peaked at KRW 205.7 billion in Q1 2026 and then declined in the following two quarters. Weaker steel and cement demand could reduce revenue and cash generation together.

Risk factors

Downstream demand

Refractory demand depends on steel and cement production, so lower downstream operating rates would reduce revenue and operating profit together.

Two straight quarterly revenue declines after the first quarter of 2026 show this pressure is already visible, and changes in raw material and energy costs could also affect margins.

Quarterly earnings volatility

Operating profit moved from KRW 18.3 billion in Q3 2025 to an operating loss of KRW 5.02 billion in Q4 2025, and from KRW 62.45 million in Q2 2026 to KRW 1.56 billion in Q3 2026. Such swings between profit and loss make the durability of any recovery harder to judge from a single quarter.

Holding structure and liquidity

Shareholders related to the largest shareholder held 71.31% as of April 20, 2026, and treasury shares were 12.02%, leaving limited free float.

Because a holding company depends on subsidiary earnings and dividends, weaker subsidiary profits flow through to parent-level results, and non-controlling interests create a gap between total and controlling-shareholder profit.
