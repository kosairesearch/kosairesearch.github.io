POSCO Supply-Chain Anchor Holding Co., Earnings Volatility Remains

CR Holdings anchors part of POSCO's refractories supply chain through core subsidiary Chosun Refractories, but owner-attributable net income has posted losses in both 2024 and 2025 due to a subsidiary structure with sizable minority stakes.

02

Business structure

CR Holdings became a holding company in July 2023 when it spun off its refractories business to form Chosun Refractories, and the parent entity now serves as the group's strategic control tower, coordinating subsidiaries and sourcing new growth businesses while earning revenue mainly from brand royalties, dividend income, and management/consulting fees.

Its core consolidated subsidiaries are refractories maker Chosun Refractories (59.03% owned), sintered-parts maker Daehan Sintered Metal (51%), and Hwasun Country Club, which operates a golf course (50%).

By revenue mix, domestic refractories manufacturing accounts for the largest share, machine-parts manufacturing roughly 28%, with overseas refractories and real estate making up the remainder.

Chosun Refractories is widely regarded as the number-one domestic refractories maker and a top-10 global player with over seven decades of history in the industry. The machine-parts segment, run through Daehan Sintered Metal, has supported revenue through higher sales of stainless-steel and home-appliance components.

Beneath the holding company sit numerous related units—including industrial furnace maker Hwain Ro, refractories producer CR Innotech, and plant-construction unit Chosun Refractories E&G—as well as overseas entities in the United States, Indonesia, China, and India, extending the group's footprint across the refractories value chain.

The group is also pursuing refractory recycling and eco-friendly refractory development in response to low-carbon transition pressures and tightening environmental regulation.

04

Earnings analysis

Annual revenue moved gradually in the low-KRW-800-billion range: KRW 798.0 billion in 2022, KRW 816.9 billion in 2023, KRW 810.8 billion in 2024, and KRW 827.8 billion in 2025.

Operating profit rose from KRW 22.6 billion in 2022 to KRW 39.4 billion in 2023, fell to KRW 17.4 billion in 2024, then recovered to KRW 27.8 billion in 2025, with the operating margin oscillating at 2.8%, 4.8%, 2.2%, and 3.4% respectively.

Owner-attributable net income, however, diverged sharply from consolidated net income.

In 2022, KRW 40.9 billion of the KRW 49.6 billion consolidated profit accrued to owners, whereas in 2023 only KRW 0.4 billion of the KRW 26.9 billion consolidated profit was owner-attributable, with most flowing to non-controlling interests.

In 2024 the company posted a consolidated profit of KRW 2.5 billion yet an owner net loss of KRW 2.3 billion, and in 2025 the consolidated net loss of KRW 18.8 billion widened to an owner net loss of KRW 27.4 billion.

Quarterly results were volatile: operating profit of KRW 18.3 billion with an owner net loss of KRW 9.5 billion in Q3 2025, an operating loss of KRW 5.0 billion with an owner net loss of KRW 16.5 billion in Q4 2025, a swing to operating profit of KRW 10.2 billion and owner net income of KRW 6.4 billion in Q1 2026, followed by operating profit shrinking to roughly KRW 0.06 billion and an owner net loss of KRW 7.9 billion in Q2 2026.

Market data also noted that first-quarter 2026 consolidated revenue, operating profit, and net income rose year-on-year by roughly those magnitudes.

Operating cash flow declined to KRW 32.4 billion in 2025 from KRW 81.1 billion in 2024, signaling somewhat weaker cash generation, while the debt ratio eased slightly to 93.7% in 2025 from 96.9% in 2024 but remained in the mid-to-high 90% range.

05

Industry analysis

The refractories industry is a materials business essential to high-temperature steelmaking and steel-refining equipment, with demand closely tied to the steel industry's capacity utilization and capital-investment cycle.

Chosun Refractories is a core supplier of refractories to POSCO's Pohang and Gwangyang steelworks, and refractories produced by the company are regarded as a key technology in POSCO's hydrogen reduction ironmaking process, with steel production reportedly impossible without them.

Hydrogen reduction ironmaking requires a harsher thermal and chemical environment than conventional processes, making refractory durability a decisive factor for process success, and Chosun Refractories is reported to be involved from the design stage in building a next-generation refractory portfolio suited to this process.

Strict quality and technical verification requirements in high-temperature environments make new entry difficult, sustaining an oligopolistic domestic market structure, and Chosun Refractories has expanded exports of customized refractories to Brazil, Turkey, Indonesia, and India.

The machine-parts (sintered-metal) segment is linked to automotive and home-appliance component demand, giving it a different end-market cycle from steel refractories and providing portfolio diversification.

That said, a revenue structure concentrated on a core customer means performance remains tied to the steel industry's cycle and investment pace—a structural feature that is both a strength and a risk.

Outlook

On October 2, 2026, Chosun Refractories signed a supply contract with POSCO for core slab-caster eco-refractories (contract value of KRW 27.86 billion, equal to 5.24% of recent revenue, covering October 1, 2026 through March 31, 2027) and a separate contract for tundish-management refractories (KRW 35.1 billion, or 6.60% of recent revenue, over the same period), together providing partial revenue visibility from the second half of 2026 into the first quarter of 2027.

The combined value of the two contracts comes to roughly KRW 63.0 billion.

Regarding the shift to hydrogen reduction ironmaking, the company is reported to be involved from the design stage in building a next-generation refractory portfolio, suggesting related orders could follow sequentially as POSCO's capital-investment schedule progresses.

Continued investment in low-carbon and eco-friendly refractory development and recycling technology points to an ongoing response to tightening environmental regulation.

At the holding-company level, brand royalty, dividend, and management-consulting income remain the basis for cash flow, and the Q3 2026 quarterly report is due by its statutory filing deadline in mid-November, making it the next data point for gauging second-half performance direction.

On dividends, a March 2024 amendment to the articles of incorporation allows the board to set the dividend record date by resolution rather than fixing it at fiscal year-end, with the 2025 fiscal-year record date announced upon the board's decision—a structure under which future record dates will continue to be set by board resolution rather than a fixed year-end date.

Bull factors

Position within POSCO's Supply Chain

Chosun Refractories is a core supplier to POSCO's Pohang and Gwangyang steelworks and extended that relationship in October 2026 by signing new supply contracts worth roughly KRW 63.0 billion combined.

High quality-verification barriers in high-temperature applications also make new-entrant competition difficult, favoring incumbent suppliers. Refractories are also viewed as a key technology in the shift to hydrogen reduction ironmaking, suggesting the company's role could continue through the process transition.

Diversified Subsidiary Portfolio

The holding company owns businesses with different characteristics—refractories, machine parts, golf course, and real estate—which can partly cushion the impact of a shock in any single end market.

Machine-parts subsidiary Daehan Sintered Metal has maintained revenue through higher sales of stainless-steel and home-appliance parts, contributing to earnings improvement.

Stable cash flow from non-refractory segments, together with brand-royalty and consulting income at the holding-company level, diversifies the group's overall cash-generation base.

Operating-Profit Recovery and New Supply Contracts

Consolidated operating profit rose to KRW 27.8 billion in 2025 from KRW 17.4 billion in 2024, and the improvement continued into Q1 2026 with operating profit of KRW 10.2 billion. The new October 2026 supply contracts with POSCO provide partial revenue visibility through Q1 2027.

Investment in low-carbon, eco-friendly refractory development is seen as a factor that could help sustain competitiveness as environmental regulation tightens.

Bear factors

Structural Losses at the Owner-Net-Income Level

Of the three years from 2023 to 2025, owner-attributable net income was positive only in 2023 (KRW 0.4 billion), and losses actually widened to KRW 2.3 billion in 2024 and KRW 27.4 billion in 2025.

Even in 2024, when consolidated net income was positive, a large non-controlling-interest share left the owner-attributable portion in loss. After turning positive in Q1 2026 (KRW 6.4 billion), owner net income swung back to a loss of KRW 7.9 billion in Q2 2026, illustrating significant quarter-to-quarter volatility.

End-Market Risk from POSCO Dependence

Core subsidiary Chosun Refractories' revenue is heavily weighted toward supply to POSCO's Pohang and Gwangyang steelworks, tying performance closely to the steel industry's utilization and investment cycle.

A slowdown in global steel demand or an adjustment in POSCO's operating rate could directly affect refractory orders and revenue. If the shift to hydrogen reduction ironmaking is delayed or investment scaled back, the pace of new refractory demand creation could also be affected.

Earnings Volatility from Non-Controlling-Interest Stakes

With several subsidiaries held at less than 100%—Chosun Refractories at 59.03%, Daehan Sintered Metal at 51%, and Hwasun Country Club at 50%—a sizable, recurring gap between consolidated net income and owner-attributable net income has appeared.

In certain years consolidated results are positive while the owner-attributable portion is negative, making it difficult to interpret performance from holding-company figures alone. This structure means the way profit is allocated across subsidiaries can amplify volatility in the owner-attributable share.

Risk factors

Industry/Policy Risk

The steel industry's shift to hydrogen reduction ironmaking is a long-term undertaking involving large capital investment and process conversion, so delays or changes in the investment schedule could also shift the timing of related refractory orders.

Growing demand for eco-friendly refractories amid tightening environmental regulation is both an opportunity and a source of additional R&D cost burden. Expanding global steel oversupply or protectionist trade measures could negatively affect the operating rates of customers including POSCO.

Financial Structure Risk

The debt ratio has stayed in the mid-to-high 90% range, moving 95.9% to 96.2% to 96.9% to 93.7% from 2022 through 2025, indicating relatively high balance-sheet leverage. Operating cash flow fell to KRW 32.4 billion in 2025 from KRW 81.1 billion in 2024, confirming year-to-year volatility in cash generation.

A broad consolidation scope covering numerous unlisted subsidiaries and overseas entities makes it difficult to fully assess how individual subsidiaries' financial conditions affect overall results.

Governance/Dividend Predictability Risk

A March 2024 amendment to the articles of incorporation allows the board, rather than a fixed fiscal year-end, to set the dividend record date by resolution, changing the structure under which record dates are announced.

This means the timing required to hold shares to qualify for a dividend can differ from past practice, requiring investors to check in advance. In years when owner net income is negative, uncertainty over the source of dividend funding and the consistency of dividend policy can also increase.

12

Overall view

CR Holdings is a holding company formed through a 2023 spin-off that maintains a business structure supplying refractories to POSCO's Pohang and Gwangyang steelworks through core subsidiary Chosun Refractories.

Consolidated revenue grew gradually between KRW 798.0 billion and KRW 827.8 billion from 2022 through 2025, and the operating margin fluctuated in the 2% to 5% range, but owner-attributable net income posted losses in both 2024 and 2025 given the many subsidiaries held at less than full ownership.

In 2026, owner net income turned positive in Q1 (KRW 6.4 billion) before reverting to a loss (KRW 7.9 billion) in Q2, continuing a pattern of quarterly volatility.

New supply contracts signed with POSCO in October 2026 provide partial revenue visibility through Q1 2027, and refractories are expected to continue playing a role as the industry transitions toward hydrogen reduction ironmaking.

The share price trades at a discount to net asset value per share and the company has a history of paying dividends, though the shift to a board-determined record date introduces a variable into dividend predictability.

The next quarterly report (statutory deadline mid-November 2026), execution of the POSCO supply contracts, and disclosure of the dividend record date are the key items to monitor for gauging future earnings and shareholder-return trends.
