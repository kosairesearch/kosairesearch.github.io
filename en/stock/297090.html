02

Business structure

CS Bearing makes pitch bearings, which connect turbine blades to the rotor and support it, and yaw bearings used in the yaw system of horizontal-axis wind turbines.

In August 2022 the company halted production at its Korean plant to secure global cost competitiveness and has since concentrated output at its Vietnamese plant. Production is handled by CS BEARING VIETNAM, a subsidiary with a 100% stake.

Rather than signing separate supply contracts, the company delivers against customer orders, and fixed customers such as GE generate recurring orders in Europe and the United States.

A brokerage report said the company kept a share of about 70% within GE, and Vestas-related sales have risen since 2025, diversifying its sales regions.

Its core products serve 2–3 MW onshore turbines, and the company plans to raise the share of 5–6 MW products and widen its lineup by passing qualification for offshore wind bearings. Its parent is CS Wind, which held a 53.56% stake as of June 30, 2026.

Wind bearing supply begins only after turbine makers complete qualification, so qualification timing and the speed of customer diversification are the key variables for orders. Within this structure, the cost competitiveness of the Vietnamese production base stands out as the company's clearest advantage.

04

Earnings analysis

Annual revenue rose from KRW 49 billion in 2022 to KRW 76.6 billion in 2023, KRW 105.4 billion in 2024 and KRW 126.1 billion in 2025, growing three years in a row from 2023 through 2025, with 2025 the largest year of 2022 to 2025. The 2025 revenue growth of 19.7% was slower than the 37.6% gain in 2024.

Operating profit moved from an operating loss of KRW 8.83 billion in 2022 to KRW 57.07 million in 2023, turning profitable, then rose to KRW 2.11 billion in 2024 (up 3,596.4%) and KRW 11.1 billion in 2025 (up 425.5%), increasing in both 2024 and 2025.

The operating margin climbed from -18.0% in 2022 to 0.1% in 2023, 2.0% in 2024 and 8.8% in 2025. The company attributed the 2025 gains to a recovery in North American wind demand and a higher share of sales from customers other than GE.

A brokerage report said the Vietnamese plant's utilization recovered from about 20% in 4Q 2023 to about 80% in 4Q 2025 after the relocation was completed in 2024, and cited this as a driver of margin improvement.

Net profit attributable to controlling shareholders moved from net losses of KRW 17.4 billion in 2022 and KRW 1.61 billion in 2023 to KRW 2.51 billion in 2024, turning profitable, and KRW 11.2 billion in 2025 (up 347.8%).

At the end of 2025, total equity was KRW 101 billion and total liabilities were KRW 53.6 billion, and the debt ratio fell from 78.9% in 2022 to 53.1% in 2025.

Quarterly revenue went from KRW 33.7 billion in 4Q 2025 to KRW 28.8 billion in 1Q 2026 (down 14.3% quarter on quarter), then recovered to KRW 37.8 billion in 2Q and KRW 34.7 billion in 3Q 2026 (up 8.0% year on year).

Operating profit bottomed at KRW 1.83 billion in 1Q 2026 and rose for two straight quarters to KRW 3.42 billion in 2Q and KRW 3.87 billion in 3Q 2026, up 15.5% year on year.

Net profit attributable to controlling shareholders was KRW 2.34 billion in 3Q 2026, down 46.7% year on year, moving in the opposite direction to operating profit. The four most recent quarters, from 4Q 2025 to 3Q 2026, total KRW 11.7 billion in such net profit.

05

Industry analysis

According to a company performance commentary, offshore wind is expected to grow at an annual rate of 21% and expand to 441 GW by 2034, and larger turbines are increasing demand for very large offshore wind bearings.

For onshore wind, a Korean brokerage said in a February 2026 report that demand in the United States and Europe remains firm. A recovery in North American wind demand was cited as a reason for the company's 2025 revenue growth.

The Vietnamese plant's 2026 utilization target was reported at 90%, while Samsung Securities projected about 95% in a March 2026 report. The same February 2026 report said the Vietnamese plant's capacity is close to its maximum, so expansion would also be needed.

Wind bearings begin supply only after each turbine maker completes qualification, so qualification timing slows the pace of customer diversification. Samsung Securities said in a 2025 report that delays in Vestas product qualification also pushed back the diversification plan.

Quarterly revenue moves with order timing, and it fell 14.3% quarter on quarter in 1Q 2026 before recovering, which shows substantial short-term volatility.

On competition, the company's strengths are the cost competitiveness of its Vietnamese production and a customer base centered on GE, where its share is cited at about 70%.

Outlook

In a March 2026 corporate day summary, Samsung Securities projected the company's utilization at about 95% for the year and said expansion is under consideration if additional volume is secured.

The same material presented expanding the share of 5–6 MW products and widening the lineup through qualification of offshore wind bearings as ongoing projects. Entry into the Asia-Pacific region was also presented as a plan.

According to company disclosures, a loan to the Vietnamese subsidiary CS BEARING VIETNAM, disclosed on October 2, 2026, had its term extended to October 5, 2027, and its total outstanding balance is KRW 40.79 billion. A loan to the same subsidiary, disclosed on February 27, 2026, had its term extended to March 3, 2027.

The qualification progress of offshore wind products for customers and whether Vietnam capacity expands are the key variables for the next growth phase. The 2026 annual report is the filing that will confirm 4Q 2026 and full-year results, with a submission deadline in late March 2027.

The 1Q 2027 quarterly report has a submission deadline of May 15, 2027. The checkpoint at that time is whether operating profit continues to recover from the 1Q 2026 low of KRW 1.83 billion.

08

Bull factors

Improved profitability structure

The operating margin climbed from -18.0% in 2022 to 8.8% in 2025, and a brokerage estimate puts utilization at about 80% in 4Q 2025 after the Vietnam relocation. Operating profit rose for two straight quarters in 2Q and 3Q 2026, with 3Q up 15.5% year on year. Operating cash flow also grew from KRW 2.02 billion in 2024 to KRW 7.59 billion in 2025.

Room to expand in offshore wind

According to a company performance commentary, offshore wind is expected to grow 21% a year through 2034, and the company plans to widen its lineup with 5–6 MW products and offshore wind bearing qualification. Asia-Pacific expansion was also presented as a plan. Whether these plans translate into a larger revenue share depends on customer qualification progress.

Stronger balance sheet

The debt ratio fell from 78.9% in 2022 to 53.1% in 2025, and at the end of 2025 total equity was KRW 101 billion against total liabilities of KRW 53.6 billion.

Operating profit of KRW 11.1 billion and net profit attributable to controlling shareholders of KRW 11.2 billion in 2025 helped rebuild the equity base after prior losses. Parent CS Wind held a 53.56% stake as of June 30, 2026.

09

Bear factors

Quarterly earnings volatility

Net profit attributable to controlling shareholders was KRW 2.34 billion in 3Q 2026, down 46.7% year on year, and KRW 2.09 billion in 1Q 2026, down 49.4% quarter on quarter.

Quarterly net profit attributable to controlling shareholders ranged from KRW 4.13 billion in 4Q 2025 to KRW 2.09 billion in 1Q 2026, a wide swing. Quarters in which operating profit and net profit moved in opposite directions also appeared.

Customer concentration and qualification delays

The company's share within GE, its main customer, is cited at about 70%, which points to high dependence on GE. Delays in Vestas product qualification push back the customer diversification timeline as well. A slower offshore qualification would lower earnings estimates, according to brokerage views.

Production capacity and funding structure

The Vietnamese plant's capacity is described as close to its maximum, so additional volume would require expansion. Expansion carries investment burden. The total outstanding loan balance to the Vietnamese subsidiary was KRW 40.79 billion, based on the October 2, 2026 disclosure, so the overseas funding flow is large.

10

Risk factors

Customer concentration risk

With a sales structure centered on GE, changes in orders from the main customer feed directly into results. Vestas-related sales are rising, but qualification delays could slow customer diversification. As sales regions diversify, qualification timing determines how visible future earnings are.

Order timing and policy risk

Wind orders depend on customers' installation schedules and the policy environment. As 1Q 2026 revenue fell 14.3% quarter on quarter, quarterly results move sharply with order timing. Because the recovery in North American wind demand supported 2025 results, demand changes in that region remain an important variable.

Overseas production and funding risk

Having halted Korean production and concentrated output in Vietnam, the company is exposed to local labor costs and exchange-rate moves that can affect cost. With loan balances to the subsidiary at KRW 40.79 billion, changes in subsidiary funding could weigh more heavily on consolidated financials.

12

Overall view

CS Bearing makes pitch and yaw bearings for wind turbines, and it has shifted from an operating loss in 2022 to an 8.8% operating margin in 2025.

Revenue grew three years in a row from 2023 through 2025, and operating profit and net profit attributable to controlling shareholders rose sharply in 2025 after turning profitable in 2024.

The shift to Vietnamese production and the recovery in utilization are cited by brokerages as the core drivers of this improvement. The 2026 quarterly picture is mixed, however.

Operating profit bottomed in 1Q 2026 and rose for two straight quarters, but net profit attributable to controlling shareholders fell 46.7% year on year in 3Q 2026, and the four most recent quarters total KRW 11.7 billion.

The strengths are the utilization recovery, the planned expansion of the offshore lineup and a lower debt ratio, while the weaknesses are concentration on GE, qualification delays at Vestas and quarterly earnings volatility.

Valuation is summarized by a multiple below the sector average and a premium over net assets that is not large. The next checkpoints are the 2026 annual report, due in late March 2027, and the progress of offshore wind qualification.
