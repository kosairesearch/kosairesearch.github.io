Report overview

Steel Recovery Lifts Profits for Three Straight Quarters

POSCO Holdings' operating profit has risen for three straight quarters from the 4Q 2025 low, reaching KRW 944.7 billion in 3Q 2026, but annual operating profit has fallen for three consecutive years since 2023 and the lithium-related business remains loss-making.

  1. 1

    Operating profit in 3Q 2026 was KRW 944.7 billion, up 47.9% year on year, on revenue of KRW 19.1 trillion, up 10.9%.

  2. 2

    The core steel subsidiary's standalone operating profit in 3Q 2026 was KRW 455 billion, back in the KRW 400 billion range for the first time in four quarters.

  3. 3

    Profit attributable to owners fell to KRW 657.7 billion in 2025 from KRW 3.14 trillion in 2022, while the four quarters from 4Q 2025 to 3Q 2026 total KRW 1.17 trillion.

  4. 4

  5. 5

02

Business structure

POSCO Holdings is a holding company centered on steel, with battery materials, infrastructure (trading and construction) and energy resources alongside it.

Under the resource-focused 'Triple Core' strategy announced in July 2026, the company says its industrial (steel), strategic (battery materials) and energy (LNG) businesses complement one another's profitability.

The standalone steel operating company recorded revenue of KRW 9.96 trillion and operating profit of KRW 455 billion in 3Q 2026. Combined steel-segment operating profit for the quarter was KRW 566 billion, up KRW 163 billion from the prior quarter. The domestic share of steel sales rose to 55.6% from 51.9% in 2025.

The infrastructure segment kept operating profit at about KRW 495 billion, roughly flat quarter on quarter, supported by gas-field and palm-oil production growth at the trading subsidiary and higher construction orders at the engineering subsidiary.

The battery-materials segment swung to an operating loss of KRW 17 billion as its cathode affiliate improved but lithium subsidiaries lost money.

In the energy segment, the company signed an agreement to acquire 100% of a U.S. shale gas asset held by Chord, securing roughly 1 million tonnes a year of LNG-equivalent production.

At a February 2026 conference call, management said deconsolidating its Chinese Changjiang steel unit in 2026 would reduce losses by roughly KRW 200 billion year on year. The steel price environment rests on lower-priced imports easing after anti-dumping measures on Chinese and Japanese hot-rolled coil and plate.

04

Earnings analysis

Annual results show falling revenue and shrinking margins at the same time. Revenue went from KRW 84.8 trillion in 2022 to KRW 77.1 trillion in 2023 (down 9.0%), KRW 72.7 trillion in 2024 (down 5.8%) and KRW 69.1 trillion in 2025 (down 4.9%), a three-year decline from 2023.

Operating profit fell from KRW 4.85 trillion in 2022 to KRW 1.83 trillion in 2025, and the operating margin declined each year from 5.7% to 4.6%, 3.0% and 2.6%.

Profit attributable to owners dropped 39.9% in 2025 to KRW 657.7 billion, while net profit including non-controlling interests fell from KRW 3.56 trillion to KRW 504.4 billion. Operating cash flow was KRW 4.57 trillion in 2025, below KRW 6.66 trillion in 2024.

The 4Q 2025 quarter bottomed with operating profit of KRW 12.7 billion and a net loss attributable to owners of KRW 225.4 billion. According to a February 2026 conference-call summary, this reflected lower output from major plant overhauls and a one-time employee compensation charge tied to the sale of a Chinese plant.

In an April 2026 company brief, Meritz Securities said the 4Q 2025 one-time cost period appeared to mark the earnings bottom. Operating profit then rose to KRW 706.8 billion in 1Q 2026, KRW 819 billion in 2Q and KRW 944.7 billion in 3Q, three straight quarterly gains.

In steel, higher coking coal costs lifted raw-material expenses by 4.4%, but a rise in average selling prices of carbon steel from KRW 962,000 to KRW 982,000 per tonne offset them.

The swing to profit at the engineering subsidiary, which posted a large loss in 2025, also contributed materially to group operating profit growth. Lithium subsidiaries, by contrast, kept losing money on falling prices and timing gaps between raw-material purchases and product sales.

Profit attributable to owners in 3Q 2026 was KRW 241.5 billion, down 64.7% from the prior quarter and 42.6% from a year earlier, diverging from the operating profit recovery.

05

Industry analysis

The steel market is in a gradual recovery. Management expects domestic steel demand to recover gradually in 2027, while the market as a whole remains in a downturn.

It expects auto demand and shipbuilding orders to improve more clearly, and it has taken a positive view of new areas such as power infrastructure and data centers. Weak property activity in China could keep Chinese steel demand flat or shrinking.

In Korea, low-priced imports have been drawn down since the anti-dumping measures on hot-rolled products, which is restoring pricing leverage. Some analysis notes that steel profitability has not yet returned to its 3Q 2025 level, even though the operating margin rose to 4.6% in 3Q 2026.

Meritz Securities said in an April 2026 brief that higher exchange rates and raw-material costs outpaced price increases, compressing roll margins.

In lithium, electric-vehicle growth has been weaker than expected, but energy-storage demand is strengthening, and management said current prices may be near the cyclical bottom. Some investment banks reportedly expect lithium prices to exceed USD 15,000 per tonne.

Standalone steel operating profit in 3Q 2026 was down 22.1% year on year, a sign that the steel recovery is still below its prior-year level.

06

Outlook

The company has confirmed four lines of activity.

First, steel output and sales are expected to fall in 4Q 2026 because of repairs to the No. 2 blast furnace at Gwangyang and to downstream hot-rolling and casting equipment, with production about 920,000 tonnes lower and sales about 570,000 tonnes lower than in the prior quarter.

Further maintenance is scheduled to reduce output in early 2027. Second, battery materials are shifting toward LFP production to meet energy-storage demand, and the goal is long-term supply agreements with three major battery makers.

Third, lithium prices have fallen below USD 13,000 per tonne, hurting profitability, and press reports say a return to lithium profit is expected in 2027.

Fourth, the company plans to use about KRW 250 billion, or 10% of the sale proceeds from its stakes in POSCO International and POSCO DX, for share buybacks and cancellation within the year. It intends to proceed with shareholder returns without waiting for a sale of its stake in POSCO Future M.

It has also said it holds about KRW 1.5 trillion in additional cash and does not need to wait until all planned asset sales are complete. In energy, the U.S. shale gas acquisition will add roughly 1 million tonnes a year of LNG-equivalent output.

A February 2026 summary put the 2026 investment budget at KRW 11.8 trillion, though actual spending may differ depending on negotiations and execution.

08

Bull factors

Turn in steel profitability

Standalone steel operating profit returned to the KRW 400 billion range in 3Q 2026 for the first time in four quarters, at KRW 455 billion, and the margin rose from 2.9% in 2Q to 4.6%.

Higher selling prices, higher volumes and cost cuts worked together, and the company says price increases and cost reductions offset a sharp rise in raw-material costs.

Contribution from non-steel affiliates

The infrastructure segment held operating profit at about KRW 495 billion in 3Q 2026, roughly flat quarter on quarter, and the engineering subsidiary moved from a large 2025 loss to profit. The trading subsidiary's gas fields and Indonesian palm-oil output supported its results.

In energy, the pending U.S. shale acquisition is set to add roughly 1 million tonnes a year of LNG-equivalent production.

Balance sheet and shareholder returns

Net debt fell by KRW 4.3 trillion in 3Q 2026 from the prior quarter, which the company attributed to asset sales and a stronger won.

About KRW 250 billion of the affiliate-stake sale proceeds is slated for buybacks and cancellation within the year, and the company has said it holds about KRW 1.5 trillion in additional cash. It plans to start shareholder returns before all asset sales are finished.

09

Bear factors

Losses in lithium and battery materials

Lithium prices falling below USD 13,000 per tonne hurt profitability and triggered inventory valuation losses. The battery-materials segment swung to an operating loss of KRW 17 billion in 3Q 2026, and the lithium subsidiaries were hit by lower prices and timing gaps in raw-material purchases. The cathode affiliate improved from the prior quarter, but the segment as a whole still posted a loss.

Lower 4Q output and sales from maintenance

Because of the No. 2 blast furnace repairs and related work, 4Q 2026 production is expected to fall by about 920,000 tonnes and sales by about 570,000 tonnes from the prior quarter. Output is also expected to decline in early 2027 during further maintenance. Management expects the broader domestic steel market to remain in a downturn in 2027.

Medium-term erosion of earnings power

The operating margin narrowed from 5.7% in 2022 to 2.6% in 2025, and profit attributable to owners fell sharply to KRW 657.7 billion in 2025 from 2022. Profit attributable to owners in 3Q 2026 was KRW 241.5 billion, down 42.6% year on year, diverging from the rise in operating profit.

Whether the quarterly recovery translates into a stronger annual earnings base will show up in later annual filings.

10

Risk factors

Macro, rates and FX

Profit recovered in an environment of heightened volatility, including Middle East tensions and U.S. Treasury yields above 5%. A stronger won helped reduce net debt but could weigh on export margins. Exchange rates also feed into raw-material costs and pricing negotiations.

Trade and tariff policy

At the 3Q 2026 earnings call, analysts focused on steel prices, lithium recovery, U.S. trade risk and capital allocation. Anti-dumping measures on hot-rolled products reduced low-priced imports, but the import price structure could change depending on how long and how broadly those measures apply. Chinese steel export flows also affect domestic pricing.

Raw materials, plant uptime and business transition

Higher coking coal prices raise raw-material costs, and steel margins shrink if price increases lag behind. Output lost to equipment maintenance adds volatility to quarterly results. The shift of battery materials toward LFP and other new growth businesses depends on long-term supply contracts and market demand.

12

Overall view

POSCO Holdings' 3Q 2026 results combined a steel profitability recovery with a stronger infrastructure segment, lifting operating profit to KRW 944.7 billion, up 47.9% year on year. Operating profit has risen for three straight quarters from a 4Q 2025 low of KRW 12.7 billion.

On an annual basis, however, revenue, operating profit and profit attributable to owners each declined for three consecutive years from 2023 through 2025, and the operating margin narrowed from 5.7% in 2022 to 2.6% in 2025.

Strengths include recovering steel prices and volumes, profits at infrastructure affiliates, lower net debt and buybacks with cancellation.

Weaknesses include lithium price weakness and the battery-materials loss, output reductions from maintenance in 4Q 2026 and early 2027, and quarterly volatility in profit attributable to owners.

Profit attributable to owners over the four quarters from 4Q 2025 to 3Q 2026 totaled KRW 1.17 trillion, above the 2025 full-year figure, yet the 3Q 2026 figure of KRW 241.5 billion was below 2Q.

The next checkpoints are execution of the buyback and cancellation within the year, preliminary 4Q 2026 results, and the 2026 annual report.
