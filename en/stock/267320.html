Naintech: Order Recovery Is the Key After Return to Profit

Naintech returned to operating profit in 1Q26 and posted operating profit again in 3Q26, but still recorded net losses attributable to controlling shareholders, so the pace at which battery capex resumes and new display orders convert into sales is the next checkpoint.

02

Business structure

Naintech is an equipment maker whose core products are secondary-battery manufacturing equipment and display manufacturing equipment, and it is widening its scope into new businesses such as thermoelectric elements, MXene, and sodium-ion-based ESS.

According to a Money Today report from May 2026, battery equipment accounted for about KRW 11.4 billion (57.9%) of 1Q26 sales and display equipment for about KRW 7.8 billion (39.3%).

Vacuum and nitrogen (Vac/N2) logistics transfer equipment used between display process steps booked KRW 7.5 billion in 1Q26 alone, above the KRW 4.1 billion full-year 2025 total, and the company says this business's share of sales rose from the 5% range to 37.9%.

As of 1H23, battery manufacturing equipment made up 79.2% of sales, so display logistics equipment has emerged as a new pillar. Its display customers include LG Display, BOE, and TIANMA, and Chinese OLED demand lifted 1Q26 sales.

In June 2026 it signed a supply contract with LG Electronics for 8.6-generation inkjet OLED line equipment. Its semiconductor business centers on back-end packaging and glass substrate equipment, and in an interview with THE ELEC the company said semiconductor-related business accounts for 15 to 20% of sales.

A 2022 brokerage field report described battery equipment supply as centered on LG Energy Solution. In both battery and display equipment, the timing of order placement depends on when customers execute their investment plans.

04

Earnings analysis

Annual revenue moved from KRW 88.8 billion in 2022 to KRW 123 billion in 2023 (up 38.5% year on year), KRW 197.2 billion in 2024 (up 60.3%), and KRW 82.4 billion in 2025 (down 58.2%). Among 2022 to 2025, 2024 was the largest year and 2025 the smallest.

Operating profit was KRW 5.22 billion in 2022, turned into an operating loss of KRW 3.1 billion in 2023, returned to operating profit of KRW 4.63 billion in 2024, and then fell back into an operating loss of KRW 8.92 billion in 2025, when the operating margin was -10.8%.

Revenue grew sharply in 2024, yet the operating margin was only 2.3%, so top-line growth did not fully carry through to profitability. Net income attributable to controlling shareholders was KRW 8.87 billion in 2023, up 28,209.5% year on year, but that comparison base was very small at KRW 31.34 million in 2022.

Net loss attributable to controlling shareholders was KRW 5.72 billion in 2024 and KRW 17.7 billion in 2025, so the company posted losses for two consecutive years from 2024 through 2025. Net income including non-controlling interests was a net loss of KRW 17.4 billion in 2025.

The company attributed the 2025 losses to restructuring and temporary costs tied to new business investment, along with the write-off of long-overdue receivables and bad-debt provisions, as reported by Edaily in February 2026.

Operating cash flow went from KRW -6.34 billion in 2023 and KRW -4.04 billion in 2024 to KRW 7.81 billion in 2025. The debt ratio fell from 396.1% in 2022 to 75.8% in 2025, and year-end 2025 total equity was KRW 101.5 billion, of which KRW 91.8 billion belonged to controlling shareholders.

Quarterly, operating loss widened from KRW 1.36 billion in 3Q25 on sales of KRW 22.1 billion to KRW 7.01 billion in 4Q25 on sales of KRW 16.6 billion. 1Q26 returned to operating profit of KRW 1.47 billion on sales of KRW 19.8 billion, 2Q26 posted an operating loss of KRW 470 million on sales of KRW 12.7 billion, and 3Q26 returned to operating profit of KRW 504 million on sales of KRW 16.3 billion, down 26.4% year on year. Net loss attributable to controlling shareholders in 3Q26 was KRW 1.42 billion.

05

Industry analysis

Secondary-battery equipment demand was described by the company as having dipped temporarily in 2025 as battery makers slowed investment (News1 report, June 2026).

In the same reporting, the Korean battery equipment market was described as improving in 1Q26 from a year earlier, with order placements expected to continue in the second half as major battery makers recover profitability (Seoul Shinmun, June 2026).

A JobPost article from October 2026 noted that the pace at which backlog converts into sales, cost of sales ratio, and receivables collection are the conditions for earnings recovery. Even if capex resumes, placing orders, delivery, and customer inspection take time.

In display, Korean firms are still seen as competitive in mid-to-large OLED even as Chinese companies gain influence.

Naintech has supplied vacuum and nitrogen logistics transfer equipment to LG Display, BOE, and TIANMA, which underpinned its selection as a core infrastructure supplier for an 8.6-generation inkjet OLED line. Because Chinese OLED demand lifted 1Q26 sales, Chinese panel investment flows connect directly to its results.

Compared with other listed battery and display equipment makers in the machinery and equipment sector, Naintech still has a large battery share while adding display logistics equipment as a new order stream.

06

Outlook

The company describes 2026 as the third growth phase and said results from new businesses such as thermoelectric elements and MXene would become visible in the second half of 2026 (Money Today, May 2026).

It argued that expanding into new materials and components could move the company away from one-off order structures toward continuous revenue. The LG Electronics contract announced on June 2, 2026 covers KRW 25.4 billion 13 million and runs through December 31, 2027.

The company said the deposit includes advance payments, giving it significance for cash flow as well as order size. How the contract is recognized as sales quarter by quarter will show up in future quarterly results, and whether revenue pace and operating margin move together is the point to watch.

Subsidiary Energy11 has been running a sodium-ion battery-based ESS demonstration project with Korea East-West Power since May 2025, and the company says it is progressing well. Seoul Shinmun reported that the company is also pushing expansion into new areas such as AI cooling.

Meanwhile, the company said at the time of its 2025 results disclosure that thermoelectric and other new businesses had already entered commercialization and that ESS-related revenue recognition was becoming visible.

With 2Q26 sales down 35.7% from the prior quarter, whether new-business sales hold a stable quarterly level is the point to check in upcoming results.

08

Bull factors

Expansion of the display order stream

The LG Electronics contract for 8.6-generation inkjet OLED equipment covers KRW 25.4 billion 13 million and runs through December 31, 2027. The company said advance payments included in the deposit add cash flow significance.

Vacuum and nitrogen logistics equipment booked KRW 7.5 billion in 1Q26 alone, above the KRW 4.1 billion full-year 2025 total. The company says this business's share of sales rose from the 5% range to 37.9%.

Return to operating profit from a loss-making base

After a KRW 8.92 billion operating loss in 2025, operating profit returned at KRW 1.47 billion in 1Q26 and was KRW 504 million in 3Q26. The company cited fixed-cost reduction, normalized bad-debt expenses, and a focus on profitable orders as the drivers of the 1Q26 turnaround. Net loss attributable to controlling shareholders was still KRW 1.42 billion in 3Q26.

Entry of new businesses into revenue

The company said new projects in thermoelectric elements and MXene entered full-scale revenue generation starting 1Q26. Subsidiary Energy11 has been running a sodium-ion battery-based ESS demonstration with Korea East-West Power since May 2025. The company expects the share of new-business sales to grow gradually into the second half of 2026.

09

Bear factors

Two consecutive years of losses and cumulative deficits

Net loss attributable to controlling shareholders was recorded for two consecutive years from 2024 through 2025, reaching KRW 17.7 billion in 2025. The cumulative net loss attributable to controlling shareholders over the last four quarters totals KRW 9.34 billion, and 3Q26 still posted KRW 1.42 billion. Operating profit has not yet translated into net profit attributable to controlling shareholders.

Revenue volatility and uncertain battery orders

2025 revenue was KRW 82.4 billion, down 58.2% from the prior year. 2Q26 sales were KRW 12.7 billion, down 35.7% from the prior quarter, and 3Q26 sales fell 26.4% year on year. JobPost noted that battery customers resuming capex and backlog converting into sales are central to later results.

Customer concentration and financial burden

The company said display sales are highly exposed to Chinese OLED demand. In 2025 it wrote off long-overdue receivables and recognized bad-debt provisions, and operating cash flow was negative in 2023 and 2024. Of KRW 101.5 billion in total equity at year-end 2025, KRW 91.8 billion belonged to controlling shareholders.

10

Risk factors

Timing of customer investment

Orders for both battery and display equipment depend on when customers execute capex. Even if investment resumes, placing orders, delivery, and customer inspection take time. If backlog converts into sales slowly, quarterly results may keep fluctuating.

Dependence on Chinese OLED and panel investment

The company said Chinese OLED demand lifted 1Q26 sales. If Chinese panel investment changes, display equipment revenue would be affected. Industry commentary also describes the display market as broadly weak, so exposure to demand from one region is a risk.

Financial soundness and new-business investment costs

In 2025, restructuring and temporary costs from new-business investment widened losses. The company said write-offs of long-overdue receivables and bad-debt provisions reduced financial uncertainty. If new-business revenue is delayed or investment costs continue, the durability of the return to profit could be tested.

12

Overall view

Naintech was in a loss-making phase in 2025 on both operating and net losses attributable to controlling shareholders, but its earnings structure has been improving, with operating profit returning in 1Q26 and staying positive in 3Q26.

The display logistics equipment share has risen to 37.9%, and the KRW 25.4 billion 13 million LG Electronics supply contract running through December 31, 2027 widens the order base.

On the other hand, net losses attributable to controlling shareholders continued through 3Q26, and the cumulative loss over the last four quarters is KRW 9.34 billion. With battery equipment still at 57.9% of 1Q26 sales, the pace of battery customer capex resumption strongly shapes the earnings direction.

With the price-to-book ratio above 1x and earnings-based metrics not yet stable, asset-based interpretation carries more weight.

The next checkpoints are the net income attributable to controlling shareholders in the 2026 annual report and the 1Q27 results, along with where new-business sales settle on a quarterly basis.
