02

Business structure

Cherrybro is a broiler integrator founded in 1991 that manages chicken production and distribution from grandparent-stock farming through slaughterhouses and processing plants. Its operations span broiler, processed-meat distribution, and breeder and hatchery segments.

Broiler products made up 93.9% of 2025 revenue, followed by goods for resale at 2.3%, chicks at 1.6% and feed at 1.6%, and domestic sales account for 100%. One processing line handles about 8,000 birds per hour, and the three lines together handle about 24,000 birds per hour.

The company identifies swings in live-chicken prices, which move with market supply, as the main source of earnings volatility. To offset this, it is expanding processed foods such as smoked chicken and frozen prepared products, along with franchise sales.

The CEO said direct sales will be combined with consumer-facing channels, including processed products from its food unit and a chicken restaurant brand linked to the owner family. A chicken restaurant brand linked to the owner family is also part of its sales channel plan. The largest shareholder holds a 39.55% stake.

Media compared this related company's margins with Cherrybro's even though it uses Cherrybro chicken. Competition centers on flock scale and cost competitiveness among integrated broiler producers, and the 2025 annual operating margin was above the agricultural industry average.

04

Earnings analysis

Cherrybro's earnings recovered in 2025 after a trough in 2024. Revenue was KRW 402.4 billion in 2022, KRW 399 billion in 2023 (down 0.8% year on year), KRW 382.9 billion in 2024 (down 4.0%) and KRW 405.4 billion in 2025 (up 5.9%), making 2025 the largest year of 2022 to 2025.

Operating profit was KRW 16.5 billion in 2022, KRW 21 billion in 2023 (up 27.6%), KRW 2.87 billion in 2024 (down 86.4%), the smallest year, and KRW 28.5 billion in 2025 (up 894.0%). Operating margin was 4.1%, 5.3%, 0.7% and 7.0% for 2022 to 2025.

Profit attributable to controlling shareholders moved from a net loss of KRW 9.1 billion in 2024 to KRW 23.8 billion in 2025. The company attributed the 2025 recovery to a stronger fresh-chicken business, cost improvements from productivity gains, and better sales competitiveness from quality improvements.

By quarter, operating profit was KRW 11.3 billion in the fourth quarter of 2025, then KRW 3.2 billion in the first quarter of 2026 (down 71.6% quarter on quarter) with a net loss attributable to controlling shareholders of KRW 103 million.

The second quarter of 2026 recovered to KRW 8.28 billion in operating profit on KRW 112 billion in revenue.

Third-quarter 2026 revenue of KRW 116.7 billion rose 7.5% year on year, but operating profit was only KRW 65.61 million, down 98.9%, while profit attributable to controlling shareholders was KRW 5.38 billion, well above operating profit, so non-operating items warrant a check.

WiseReport noted that first-quarter 2026 revenue grew but profitability fell on cost burdens. Profit attributable to controlling shareholders for the four quarters from the fourth quarter of 2025 through the third quarter of 2026 totaled KRW 21.6 billion. Operating cash flow rose from KRW 379 million in 2024 to KRW 41.2 billion in 2025.

05

Industry analysis

The broiler industry's profitability moves sharply with live-chicken supply and feed costs. Media reports cite broiler raw-material price swings and rearing risks as factors that lowered Cherrybro's earnings.

In an agricultural sector comparison covering 2025 annual data, Cherrybro's operating margin of 7.03% was above the sector average of 2.31% and ranked sixth among 16 companies.

Within the company, the broiler segment swung to profit in the first half of 2026, while the processing and distribution segment's profit fell sharply.

On demand, WiseReport reports rising demand for cured and boneless meat and for convenience meals and delivery food, linked to a growing restaurant industry and rising one- and two-person households. Among integrated broiler producers, competition centers on flock scale and cost competitiveness.

Cherrybro is trying to reduce live-chicken price swings by increasing processed foods and franchise sales, and the success of this shift will shape its position. Where broiler prices sit in the cycle should be checked against price trends.

06

Outlook

The company has outlined three directions. In the near term, it plans to add processed-product and consumer-facing channel sales to its supply-led business. It also aims to raise chick and feed production levels to strengthen cost competitiveness.

Over the longer term, it aims to expand into other livestock categories and become a diversified livestock company. On processed foods, it plans to increase the share of smoked chicken and frozen prepared products, and it is reviewing selling sauced grilled chicken through a deli channel.

On the shareholder side, it decided in May 2026 to consolidate common shares at a 2-to-1 ratio and received approval at an extraordinary shareholders' meeting. The company said it would establish and carry out an IR strategy aimed at enhancing shareholder value.

The next periodic report is the 2026 annual report, which will include fourth-quarter 2026 results. Because third-quarter 2026 operating profit fell to KRW 65.61 million, whether the fourth-quarter margin recovers is the most direct item to check.

Revenue rose in both the second and third quarters of 2026, so whether that growth reaches profit is also a point to confirm.

Risk factors

Raw material and rearing risk

Broiler earnings depend directly on live-chicken prices and feed costs. Biosecurity was cited among the factors behind improved earnings in the first half of 2025, showing that flock management affects profit. If rearing risks materialize, output and costs could move together.

Related-party dealings and governance

The largest shareholder holds a 39.55% stake. Media reported that this affiliate uses Cherrybro chicken while earning a higher margin than Cherrybro itself. The transaction structure with affiliates and the concentrated ownership could affect how minority holders assess the company.

Financial position and listing requirements

Total liabilities at end-2025 were KRW 188.3 billion, larger than total equity of KRW 138.8 billion. In May 2026 the company decided on a share consolidation of two shares into one, in response to listing requirements for low-priced stocks.

With a loss in 2024 and a return to a quarterly net loss in the first quarter of 2026, the durability of profits needs to be checked.

Overall view

Cherrybro moved out of its 2024 loss territory in 2025, with operating profit of KRW 28.5 billion and profit attributable to controlling shareholders of KRW 23.8 billion, and its debt-to-equity ratio fell to 135.7% from 228.6% in 2022.

However, third-quarter 2026 operating profit fell to KRW 65.61 million even as revenue rose, and the first quarter of 2026 showed a net loss attributable to controlling shareholders. The key variables are broiler prices and feed costs, with broiler products accounting for 93.9% of 2025 revenue.
