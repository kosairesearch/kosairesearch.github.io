Report overview

KF-21 Production Lifts Sales as Orders Lag

Third-quarter 2026 sales and operating profit rose sharply from a year earlier, but profit attributable to controlling shareholders has fallen for three consecutive quarters, and year-to-date orders stand at 21.2% of the annual target, making order pace and earnings quality the next checkpoints.

  2. 2

  3. 3

02

Business structure

Korea Aerospace Industries runs fixed-wing aircraft, rotary-wing aircraft, civil aircraft structures and space and satellite businesses.

By segment on a separate basis for the cumulative 2025 third quarter, fixed-wing aircraft programs (T-50 and KF-21 families) accounted for the largest share at 40.82%, followed by airframe parts and exports at 30.69%, defense and other at 20.1%, and rotary-wing aircraft (KUH and LAH families) at 8.4%.

Its core models are the T-50 advanced trainer, FA-50 light combat aircraft and KT-1 basic trainer, and its development and production capacity is now concentrated on KF-21. Domestic business sales in the third quarter of 2026 reached KRW 493.7 billion, up 83% year on year.

Fixed-wing aircraft exports rose 78% to KRW 291.4 billion, and civil aircraft structure sales grew 7% to KRW 262.8 billion. Overseas sales account for 53% of total revenue.

Domestic customers are led by the Republic of Korea armed forces, while overseas customers include fighter and trainer buyers in Malaysia, Poland, the Philippines and Indonesia as well as overseas civil aircraft makers.

Nice Investors classifies the company as top-tier in scale, mid-tier in profitability and low in growth within its industry. Backlog stood at KRW 26.29 trillion at the end of the third quarter of 2026, and at the end of the third quarter of 2025, defense and fixed-wing programs made up 58.3% of total orders.

Space and satellite backlog was KRW 794.4 billion, about 3% of the total, and is viewed as a long-dated growth option.

04

Earnings analysis

Annual sales rose from KRW 2.79 trillion in 2022 to KRW 3.82 trillion in 2023 (up 37.0% year on year), eased to KRW 3.63 trillion in 2024 (down 4.9%), and reached KRW 3.7 trillion in 2025 (up 1.7%). Among 2022 to 2025, 2023 was the largest year for sales and 2022 the smallest.

Operating profit went from KRW 141.6 billion in 2022 to KRW 247.5 billion in 2023 (up 74.8%), dipped to KRW 240.7 billion in 2024 (down 2.7%), and rose to KRW 269.2 billion in 2025 (up 11.8%), the largest year in the period.

Operating margin climbed from 5.1% in 2022 to 6.5% in 2023, 6.6% in 2024 and 7.3% in 2025, so profitability improved in 2024 even as sales declined.

Profit attributable to controlling shareholders peaked at KRW 224 billion in 2023 (up 89.1%), fell to KRW 172.1 billion in 2024 (down 23.2%) and recovered to KRW 185.9 billion in 2025 (up 8.0%).

Net income including non-controlling interests was KRW 187.3 billion in 2025, a different figure from profit attributable to controlling shareholders.

Operating cash flow was KRW 1.49 trillion positive in 2022, then -KRW 700.4 billion in 2023, -KRW 728.2 billion in 2024 and -KRW 903.3 billion in 2025, negative in each year from 2023.

The debt ratio rose to 446.6% in 2025 from 364.7% in 2024; at the end of 2025 total liabilities were KRW 8.47 trillion against total equity of KRW 1.9 trillion, of which KRW 1.83 trillion belonged to controlling shareholders.

By quarter, sales went from KRW 702.1 billion in the third quarter of 2025 to a peak of KRW 1.47 trillion in the fourth quarter (up 108.9% from the prior quarter), then KRW 1.09 trillion in the first quarter of 2026, KRW 1.17 trillion in the second and KRW 1.06 trillion in the third.

Operating profit was lowest at KRW 48.4 billion in the second quarter of 2026 and recovered to KRW 79.4 billion in the third (up 63.9% from the prior quarter). Operating margins were 6.1% in the first quarter, 4.1% in the second and 7.5% in the third.

Third-quarter operating profit included a gain of about KRW 40 billion from estimated cost changes on export programs after the won strengthened, and about KRW 30 billion of provisions, including for an Iraq bad debt, booked as one-off losses.

Shinhan Investment Corp. estimated third-quarter operating profit at KRW 69.4 billion excluding exchange effects and one-off costs, 8.7% below consensus. Profit attributable to controlling shareholders was KRW 20.4 billion in the third quarter, down 43.2% from the prior quarter and 47.5% from a year earlier.

Pre-tax profit was KRW 21.8 billion, well below operating profit, which suggests large items were deducted below the operating line.

05

Industry analysis

The main industry driver is KF-21 production. The fighter, developed over about 10.5 years, began deliveries to the Republic of Korea Air Force in September 2026 and has moved into domestic serial production.

In a March 2026 report, Hana Securities projected KF-21 production sales rising from 11.2% of revenue in 2026 to 29.8% in 2028. On export potential, Hana Securities put cumulative demand at 573 to 703 aircraft and expected early wins in countries that already operate KAI fixed-wing aircraft.

Fixed-wing exports are recognized on percentage of completion for FA-50M (Malaysia), FA-50PL (Poland), FA-50PH (Philippines) and KT-1B (Indonesia). The Indonesian KF-21 order is a key variable, since a contract that may slip into next year would shape the company's order target.

In civil aviation, recovery in commercial aircraft parts is diversifying the portfolio. In helicopters, the light armed helicopter (LAH) made no deliveries in the third quarter because of an engine supply problem.

Among domestic defense peers, Hanwha Aerospace, Hyundai-Rotem and LIG Defense&Aerospace are the usual comparisons. Expectations for a U.S. Navy trainer program (UJTS) have also been raised.

06

Outlook

The company has set its annual sales target at KRW 5 trillion or more. A KAI official said it expects to reach annual sales of KRW 5 trillion or more, a record for the company, supported by KF-21, FA-50 and LAH production and export programs in the fourth quarter.

Sales for the first nine months of 2026 reached KRW 3.32 trillion, up 49.1% year on year, so the annual target requires a large step-up in fourth-quarter sales. On orders, the company's 2026 guidance is about KRW 10.4 trillion, while year-to-date orders through the third quarter were about KRW 2.2 trillion.

Reaching the annual target would require KRW 8.23 trillion of orders in the fourth quarter alone. Domestically, a roughly KRW 3 trillion third-batch LAH production contract is being pursued. For Indonesia, negotiations cover an initial 16-aircraft KF-21 batch with a possibility of up to 48 aircraft.

Kiwoom Securities views the Indonesian 16-aircraft supply talks as nearing completion. Over the longer term, KAI submitted a ten-year revenue outlook to a National Assembly defense committee member and projects sales of KRW 10.4 trillion in 2030.

For the fourth quarter, Hanwha Investment & Securities estimated operating profit of KRW 167.1 billion, above market expectations. KAI expects the fourth quarter to be supported by KF-21 production deliveries, the resumption of LAH deliveries and FA-50M deliveries to Malaysia.

08

Bull factors

KF-21 production revenue begins

Two KF-21 fighters delivered to the Korean military generated about KRW 200 billion of revenue in the third quarter. Hana Securities expects domestic KF-21 production sales to rise step by step through 2028. Domestic business sales grew 83% in the third quarter, supporting this trend.

Export expansion and backlog base

Backlog stood at KRW 26.29 trillion at the end of the third quarter. Fixed-wing export sales rose 78% to KRW 291.4 billion. The Poland program is proceeding as planned and aircraft deliveries to Malaysia began in October. Kiwoom Securities views the Indonesian 16-aircraft KF-21 supply talks as nearing completion.

Recovery in export profitability

Operating margin rose from 6.1% in the first quarter and 4.1% in the second to 7.5% in the third quarter of 2026. Kiwoom Securities attributed part of this to export profitability recovery, including a gain of about KRW 40 billion from estimated cost changes. Civil aircraft structure sales rose 7%, broadening the business mix.

09

Bear factors

Gap to the order target

Cumulative orders through the third quarter stood at 21.2% of the 2026 order target. Reaching the target would require KRW 8.23 trillion of orders in the fourth quarter alone. If the Indonesian contract slips into next year, achieving the target becomes less likely, and order gaps affect visibility beyond 2028.

Declining profit and one-off items

Profit attributable to controlling shareholders fell from KRW 60.6 billion in the fourth quarter of 2025 to KRW 20.4 billion in the third quarter of 2026, declining for three straight quarters. About KRW 30 billion of provisions, including an Iraq bad debt, were booked as one-off losses in the third quarter.

Shinhan Investment Corp. estimated third-quarter operating profit at KRW 69.4 billion after excluding exchange effects and one-off costs, 8.7% below consensus.

Helicopter delivery disruption

The light armed helicopter (LAH) made no deliveries in the third quarter because of an engine supply problem, delaying expected revenue recognition. The company says deliveries are set to resume in the fourth quarter to normalize the program.

10

Risk factors

Timing of export contracts

A key contract for the Indonesian KF-21 order may slip into next year, which would weigh on this year's order target. Hana Securities noted that export orders are needed to improve earnings visibility beyond 2028, since domestic volume alone is limited. Contract delays would affect both the order target and medium-term earnings visibility.

FX, cost changes and one-off items

Third-quarter results included a gain of about KRW 40 billion from estimated cost changes on export programs after the won strengthened, and about KRW 30 billion of one-off provisions. When quarterly profit depends on exchange rates and cost estimates, the range of interpretation widens.

Balance sheet and cash flow

The debt ratio rose to 446.6% in 2025 from 364.7% in 2024. Total liabilities at the end of 2025 were KRW 8.47 trillion, well above total equity of KRW 1.9 trillion. Operating cash flow has been negative every year since 2023, reaching -KRW 903.3 billion in 2025. The pace at which orders convert into sales and cash will determine the financial burden.

12

Overall view

Korea Aerospace Industries entered a phase in the third quarter of 2026 in which sales and operating profit rose sharply from a year earlier, with KF-21 production and fixed-wing exports lifting results.

However, profit attributable to controlling shareholders has fallen for three consecutive quarters since the fourth-quarter 2025 peak, and third-quarter operating profit included both a cost-change gain and one-off provisions.

Year-to-date orders of KRW 2.21 trillion remain far below the annual target of about KRW 10.4 trillion, and the timing of the Indonesian KF-21 contract is the largest variable. On the balance sheet, the debt ratio rose to 446.6% at the end of 2025, and operating cash flow has been negative every year since 2023.

The valuation shows high multiples and a low dividend yield, and the pace of profit recovery will shape how that is read. Strengths such as KF-21 deliveries and export expansion and weaknesses such as the order gap and earnings volatility are weighed equally here.

The next checkpoints in sequence are the preliminary fourth-quarter 2026 results, the 2026 annual report and the outcome of the Indonesian contract. This report is provided for information only and does not include a buy or sell opinion.
