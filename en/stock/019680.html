02

Business structure

Daekyo is an education company whose core business is education services centered on home-study learning materials. An analysis of its 2024 annual report found that the education services segment made up 95.7% of total revenue, and that the segment had been flat for three consecutive years.

The education services are run by Daekyo itself together with its education subsidiaries. Because sales depend on membership-based learning, a shrinking school-age population directly affects member numbers and revenue. The overseas education business has stayed in the low-2% range of revenue for ten years.

The senior business is run by a senior-care subsidiary launched in 2022, which offers visiting care services by specialist instructors and day-care services at its centers.

The subsidiary generated KRW 1.3 billion in revenue in its first year, and a June 2024 report expected its revenue to exceed KRW 10 billion in that year.

In 2025 Daekyo acquired an online education group, renamed it as an education subsidiary, and brought it into the group; that unit runs a credit-bank lifelong education institute, and the report expected it to create synergies with the senior-care subsidiary.

In the same period Daekyo liquidated two overseas units, Eye Level Hub and the Indian subsidiary. Against larger home-study rivals that have raised non-education revenue shares to around 40%, a March 2025 report described Daekyo as highly dependent on education.

04

Earnings analysis

Annual revenue went from KRW 683.1 billion in 2022 to KRW 659.7 billion in 2023 (down 3.4% year on year), KRW 663.5 billion in 2024 (up 0.6%), and KRW 650 billion in 2025 (down 2.0%). Among 2022 to 2025, 2022 was the largest year and 2025 the smallest.

Operating loss went from KRW 50 billion in 2022 to KRW 27.8 billion in 2023 and KRW 1.56 billion in 2024, then widened to KRW 3.4 billion in 2025. The operating margin was -7.3%, -4.2%, -0.2% and -0.5% in those years, so the losses narrowed and then slightly widened again in 2025.

The company recorded operating losses in each of the four years from 2022 to 2025. An Edaily report from March 2026 said operating losses have continued since 2020.

Net income attributable to controlling shareholders swung to KRW 649 million in 2024 from a loss of KRW 93.8 billion in 2023, then returned to a loss of KRW 36.4 billion in 2025. Net income including non-controlling interests was KRW 4.04 billion in 2024 and a net loss of KRW 37.2 billion in 2025.

In 2024 the operating loss persisted even though net income attributable to controlling shareholders was positive. By quarter, the operating loss was KRW 30.76 million in 3Q 2025, then widened to KRW 4.33 billion in 4Q 2025, KRW 3.57 billion in 1Q 2026, KRW 3.72 billion in 2Q 2026, and KRW 1.37 billion in 3Q 2026.

Losses have run for five consecutive quarters from 3Q 2025 through 3Q 2026. The 3Q 2026 operating loss narrowed from the prior quarter but widened from the same quarter a year earlier.

Revenue bottomed at KRW 157.7 billion in 1Q 2026, then rose to KRW 158.6 billion in 2Q and KRW 163.3 billion in 3Q, two consecutive quarters of growth.

The net loss attributable to controlling shareholders shrank from KRW 33.5 billion in 4Q 2025 to KRW 3.92 billion in 1Q 2026 and KRW 1.16 billion in 2Q, then widened to KRW 9.61 billion in 3Q 2026.

For 1Q 2026, the company said membership-based education revenue fell as the school-age population declined, and that marketing spending for new service launches weighed on profitability (reported May 7, 2026).

Operating cash flow was KRW 45.3 billion in 2022, KRW 12 billion in 2023, KRW 6.56 billion in 2024 and KRW 38.4 billion in 2025. The debt ratio climbed from 64.9% in 2022 to 95.9% in 2023, 93.2% in 2024 and 108.2% in 2025. At the end of 2025, total liabilities were KRW 305.1 billion and total equity was KRW 282 billion.

05

Industry analysis

The shrinking school-age population is a structural pressure across the home-study learning industry. In its 1Q 2026 results commentary, the company itself said membership-based education revenue fell as the school-age population declined.

A March 2026 report pointed to the shift of the global education market from offline to online as a reason for weak overseas operations, and said failure to localize the Korean education model was also a factor.

A March 2025 report contrasted Daekyo, which raised home-study prices more than any of the three leading learning-materials groups, with weak results. The same report said Daekyo's education share was 97.5%, while two larger rivals had non-education revenue shares of around 40%.

Unlike rivals with non-education revenue, Daekyo's results depend more heavily on pricing and membership counts. The senior business targets new demand from an aging population, and in a June 2024 interview the company cited a growing base of senior citizens with spending power.

The learning-materials market has repeatedly seen membership attrition and share declines, and a 2011 securities research note also flagged Daekyo's membership losses and share decline. The 2026 disclosed results do not yet show a clear recovery signal for the industry.

Outlook

In a corporate value-up plan disclosed on March 27, 2026, the company said it would upgrade its premium education portfolio centered on AI education services and total senior care. The same plan said the company would continue reviewing shareholder return policies, including dividends, based on stable cash flow.

In a disclosure dated April 27, 2026, the company decided to dispose of 1,769,968 treasury shares over-the-counter to raise funds for new-business investment, with a holding company in the Daekyo group as the buyer.

In a May 7, 2026 disclosure, the company said it would focus on profitability improvement by upgrading its offline learning centers in 2Q, and would expand its senior visiting care service nationwide.

After liquidating overseas units in 2025, the company was reported to be seeking its path mainly through domestic senior care. The addition of the online education group was made alongside expectations of synergies with the senior-care subsidiary.

In 3Q 2026, revenue rose for two consecutive quarters, but the operating loss widened from the same quarter a year earlier. In the next annual report, whether the operating margin improves from its loss range is the checkpoint.

Treasury shares tied to the exchangeable bonds issued on October 2, 2025 become fixed for disposal only when the exchange right is exercised, so the timing of that supply also needs tracking.

Bull factors

Diversification Through Senior Care

The senior-care subsidiary has run visiting care and day-care services since 2022, expanding the senior business. The 2025 addition of the online education group added lifelong education and credit-bank programs.

In its March 2026 value-up plan, the company placed total senior care at the center of its portfolio upgrade. The senior revenue share remains small, but the direction toward non-education revenue is clear.

Margin Improvement and Revenue Rebound

The operating margin improved from -7.3% in 2022 to -0.2% in 2024, before slipping slightly to -0.5% in 2025. Net income attributable to controlling shareholders turned positive in 2024. Revenue rose for two consecutive quarters through 3Q 2026, and the operating loss narrowed from the prior quarter. The evidence for the improvement is in the disclosed figures.

Overseas Restructuring and Cash Flow Recovery

In 2025 the company liquidated Eye Level Hub and the Indian subsidiary as part of a portfolio overhaul of its overseas operations. Operating cash flow rose to KRW 38.4 billion in 2025 from KRW 6.56 billion in 2024. Total equity stood at KRW 282 billion at the end of 2025. The company said it would keep reviewing shareholder returns, including dividends, based on stable cash flow.

Bear factors

Declining School-Age Population and Concentration

The education services segment accounted for 95.7% of revenue in the 2024 annual report, and an analysis said it had been flat for three consecutive years. The company said membership-based education revenue declined in 1Q 2026 as the school-age population shrank.

Revenue fell from KRW 683.1 billion in 2022 to KRW 650 billion in 2025. The high dependence on education means demographic change hits the company directly.

Persistent Losses and Recent Widening

The company recorded operating losses in each year from 2022 to 2025, and the 2025 operating loss of KRW 3.4 billion was wider than the KRW 1.56 billion in 2024. The net loss attributable to controlling shareholders over the last four quarters totals KRW 48.2 billion.

The 3Q 2026 net loss attributable to controlling shareholders of KRW 9.61 billion was wider than a year earlier. The debt ratio also rose from 95.9% in 2023 to 108.2% in 2025.

Overseas Setbacks and Time to Monetize New Businesses

The overseas education business has stayed in the low-2% range of revenue for a decade, and two overseas units were liquidated in 2025. A March 2026 report said losses had continued for six years across the company.

Because the senior and new businesses are still at an early stage, analysts expected it would take time for them to reverse the overall results.

Risk factors

Demographics and Membership

The shrinking school-age population erodes the base for membership-based learning sales, a structural factor. In its 1Q 2026 commentary the company directly cited a decline in membership-based education revenue. Raising marketing spend to launch new services adds short-term costs.

Because the 3Q 2026 operating loss was wider than a year earlier, losses could persist longer if the membership base does not recover.

Funding and Share Supply

In an April 27, 2026 disclosure, the company decided to sell 1,769,968 treasury shares over the counter to fund new-business investment, with a holding company in the Daekyo group as the buyer.

Common treasury shares of 1,960,015 tied to the exchangeable bonds issued on October 2, 2025 will be fixed for disposal when the exchange right is exercised. These shares could affect future share supply.

Overall view

The share price sits well below book value, so the gap between asset value and earnings recovery is the central issue. The next checkpoints are the 4Q 2026 preliminary results, the operating margin in the 2026 annual report, and the 1Q 2027 quarterly report.
