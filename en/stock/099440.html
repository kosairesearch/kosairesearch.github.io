02

Business structure

Smec was founded in 1999 on the machine-tool business of Samsung Heavy Industries and is a specialist machine-tool maker. A securities report put machinery-segment sales at 97.3% of total company revenue in the first half of 2024, which points to a structure concentrated on the core machine-tool business.

Its main products include the high-performance STX2600 machining center and the PL 800GB and SL 1000GB semiconductor-process machines shown at trade fairs. Linking its in-house robotic automation solution, LAM (Load Assistant Machine), to its machines to run unmanned machining is another pillar of its competitiveness.

Its semiconductor-specific equipment is designed for processing semiconductor materials such as ceramics and quartz. In March 2026, a distributor, Sail MT, placed an order for about KRW 22 billion covering 110 high-end machining centers for a domestic semiconductor ceramics processor.

The distributor earns more than 90% of its revenue from this business line. The customer mix, once centered on auto parts, is reportedly shifting toward semiconductor equipment.

Overseas, the company is strengthening efforts in North America, Europe and China, and at IMTS 2026 in the United States in September 2026 it set a North American order target of USD 10 million.

Its acquisition of a 34.8% stake in the machine-tool unit spun off from an automotive-parts affiliate in 2025 widens its business scope.

04

Earnings analysis

On a disclosed annual basis, revenue rose from KRW 159.5 billion in 2022 to KRW 176.6 billion in 2023 (up 10.8% year on year) and KRW 201.3 billion in 2024 (up 14.0%), then fell to KRW 153.6 billion in 2025 (down 23.7%). Among the years from 2022 to 2025, 2024 was the largest.

The company attributed the 2025 revenue decline to a business slowdown caused by global tariff policies. Operating profit reached KRW 22 billion in 2023 (up 87.6%) and KRW 24 billion in 2024 (up 8.7%) before the company posted an operating loss of KRW 16.8 billion in 2025, a swing into the red.

The company also cited a one-time increase in fees tied to its sale of treasury shares and the acquisition of the machine-tool unit as factors that reduced 2025 earnings. The operating margin moved from 12.5% in 2023 and 11.9% in 2024 to -10.9% in 2025.

The net loss attributable to controlling shareholders for 2025 was KRW 21.5 billion, and the disclosed net loss including non-controlling interests was the same figure.

Quarterly data show that the fourth-quarter 2025 operating loss of KRW 19.7 billion was larger than the full-year operating loss, meaning the earlier quarters combined were in profit.

Revenue in the first quarter of 2026 was KRW 29.2 billion, down 27.4% from the previous quarter, with an operating loss of KRW 4.84 billion.

In the second quarter, revenue rose to KRW 47.8 billion (up 63.5% from the previous quarter), with operating profit of KRW 1.61 billion and net profit attributable to controlling shareholders of KRW 2.86 billion.

Third-quarter revenue was KRW 41.7 billion (down 12.7% from the previous quarter and up 15.1% year on year), but the company returned to an operating loss of KRW 3.79 billion and a net loss attributable to controlling shareholders of KRW 18.1 billion.

Operating cash flow was KRW 9.63 billion in 2022 and KRW 2.38 billion in 2023, then turned negative at -KRW 17.8 billion in 2024 and -KRW 1.43 billion in 2025.

05

Industry analysis

Industry accounts describe demand for machine tools as having been shaken across the board in 2025 by the US tariff burden and a slowdown in manufacturing. The company likewise pointed to tariffs and a business slump as the reasons for its 2025 underperformance.

In 2026, the company said exports weakened as the Middle East conflict and the prolonged Russia-Ukraine war slowed global manufacturing. Semiconductors, by contrast, are described as having entered a genuine recovery phase in 2026.

The company also said strong domestic semiconductor demand supported its third-quarter 2026 sales growth. Ceramic and quartz processing equipment falls in an area where process quality and yields depend on fine tolerances, so demand for specialized equipment with precision and stability is rising.

On competition, Smec differentiates itself by combining semiconductor-specific equipment with robotic automation rather than competing on general-purpose machine tools. Acquiring the stake in the machine-tool unit is seen as a step that enlarges its standing within the industry.

One market-data site describes the acquisition as setting up a move to second place in the sector, which is a forecast from a single source rather than a settled outcome. The key to judging the cycle is whether the recovery in export markets and semiconductor demand line up at the same time.

06

Outlook

In its second-quarter 2026 results announcement, the company said it expected results to keep improving in the second half, supported by expanding semiconductor-related equipment supply and steady growth in its core business.

In its first-quarter announcement, it had expressed hope for a turnaround in the first half and a stronger second half. The company said its backlog remained near KRW 70 billion as of May 2026, with deliveries concentrated after the first half.

The backlog at the end of the first quarter of 2026 was reported at KRW 70.9 billion, up 48% from a year earlier. A securities report issued in June 2026 projected that results would improve more in the second half as orders for specialized semiconductor equipment expanded.

However, the third-quarter operating loss means that projection has not yet been confirmed. At IMTS 2026 in September 2026, the company set a North American order target of USD 10 million and also exhibited at AMB 2026 in Germany, running sales efforts in North America and Europe in parallel.

Whether contract disclosures follow the trade-show period will determine whether the overseas order target is met. Another item to watch is how much the inventory-equipment discount sales and trade-show costs cited for the third quarter decline in the fourth quarter.

Bull factors

Orders for semiconductor-specific equipment accumulate

After a contract worth about KRW 22 billion covering 110 high-end machining centers in March 2026, a further contract for more than 60 semiconductor-specific machines worth about KRW 10.4 billion was disclosed in May. The company said its backlog remains near KRW 70 billion.

A securities report also put the end-of-first-quarter backlog up 48% from a year earlier. If order growth continues beyond one-off contracts, as the company describes, it would add visibility to future sales.

Return to profit in the second quarter of 2026

Second-quarter 2026 revenue reached KRW 47.8 billion, up 63.5% from the prior quarter, and the company posted operating profit of KRW 1.61 billion and net profit attributable to controlling shareholders of KRW 2.86 billion.

The company attributed the turnaround to a fuller ramp-up of semiconductor-specific equipment sales. Because it was the first quarter out of an operating loss, it can be read as evidence of a change in the business. Whether profit continues in the following quarters will determine how durable this strength is.

Wider business scope and overseas sales push

One market-data site described the 34.8% stake in the machine-tool unit as setting up a move to second place in the sector. The strategy also pairs the company's LAM robotic automation with its machines.

At IMTS 2026 in September 2026, it showed the STX2600 and semiconductor-specific equipment and set a North American order target of USD 10 million. If the acquisition benefits and overseas sales results are confirmed, the scope of top-line growth could widen.

Bear factors

Quarterly results alternating between loss and profit

After an operating loss of KRW 16.8 billion in 2025, losses continued with a first-quarter 2026 operating loss of KRW 4.84 billion and a third-quarter operating loss of KRW 3.79 billion. The second-quarter profit lasted only one quarter.

The fourth-quarter 2025 operating loss of KRW 19.7 billion was the quarter that drove the annual deficit. Over the last four quarters, net losses attributable to controlling shareholders totaled KRW 43 billion.

Heavier financial burden

The debt-to-equity ratio rose from 132.4% in 2024 to 209.1% in 2025. Total liabilities at the end of 2025 stood at KRW 204 billion, above total equity of KRW 97.6 billion. Operating cash flow was negative at -KRW 17.8 billion in 2024 and -KRW 1.43 billion in 2025. This structure could limit room for debt repayment and working-capital financing.

Export weakness and cost pressure

In the first quarter of 2026, exports lagged year on year as overseas manufacturing investment sentiment weakened, while rising raw-material prices and unstable supply of key parts added pressure.

In the third quarter, discounted sales of overseas inventory equipment, trade-show costs and an FX translation loss of about KRW 6.2 billion on export receivables weighed on profitability. If the slowdown in overseas manufacturing persists, a recovery in export results could be delayed.

Risk factors

Governance dispute

A February 2026 report said SNT Holdings, which had become the largest shareholder, demanded a replacement of the board and signaled a proxy contest at the shareholders' meeting.

One compilation as of October 1, 2026 shows the SNT Motiv group at 28.15%, while another as of September 14, 2026 lists SNT Motiv at 20.54%, a discrepancy between sources. The direction of governance could affect the continuity of management strategy.

Acquisition and one-off costs

The company said one-off fees linked to the 2025 acquisition of the machine-tool unit increased. How the integration of the acquired business and its cost structure show up in disclosed results still needs to be confirmed.

In the third quarter, a higher cost ratio at overseas subsidiaries was also cited as a burden on consolidated profitability. If acquisition benefits are delayed, the timing of a return to sustained profit could also slip.

Concentration in semiconductor customers and channels

Orders for semiconductor-specific equipment are concentrated among a small number of domestic ceramics processors and a distributor channel. The distributor is reported to earn more than 90% of its revenue from this business line. Such concentration can increase order volatility. If semiconductor capital spending slows, equipment orders could fall quickly.

Overall view

Smec is shifting its business structure by adding semiconductor-specific equipment and automation solutions to its core machine-tool business. Contracts worth about KRW 22 billion and about KRW 10.4 billion followed one another in 2026, and the company said its backlog stood near KRW 70 billion.

Results have alternated between a second-quarter profit and a third-quarter loss since the swing to an operating loss in 2025. The fourth-quarter 2025 operating loss of KRW 19.7 billion, which drove the annual deficit, is also confirmed in the disclosed figures.

Financially, the debt-to-equity ratio rose to 209.1%, and operating cash flow was negative in both 2024 and 2025. A governance dispute between the SNT group, the largest shareholder side, and management was a key issue in early 2026.

Strengths include the accumulation of semiconductor equipment orders and the second-quarter return to profit, while weaknesses include the third-quarter loss, the debt burden and the slowdown in overseas manufacturing.

The next checkpoints are the fourth-quarter operating result within the 2026 annual results and whether profit continues into the first quarter of 2027.
