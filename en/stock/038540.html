Report overview

Losses Narrowing and a Subsidiary Back in the Black; Valuation Gap Remains

Sangsangin posted losses for three straight years from 2023 to 2025, and although its operating loss has narrowed in 2026, the swing to profit at subsidiary SANGSANGIN INVESTMENT&SECURITIES has emerged as a key variable for group earnings recovery.

  4. 4

02

Business structure

Sangsangin was founded in 1989 and listed on KOSDAQ in 2000, and it currently operates in information and communications, finance, shipbuilding equipment and software development.

The IT business provides network design, construction and maintenance, and its network integration solutions were 99.90% domestic in December 2025.

The finance business consists of two savings bank subsidiaries, each 100% owned, and SANGSANGIN INVESTMENT&SECURITIES, 57% owned, which handles brokerage and investment banking. The shipbuilding subsidiary, 100% owned, mainly produces automatic welding panel lines for shipyard automation.

Software development and operation is handled by two group companies. As of December 2025, the group had 21 consolidated entities, many of them asset-securitization vehicles.

In the first nine months of 2026, SANGSANGIN INVESTMENT&SECURITIES ranked eighth in capital bond underwriting with KRW 2.03 trillion, a 4.43% market share, and eighth in card bond underwriting with KRW 970 billion, a 4.10% share. As of July 2026, related parties including the largest shareholder held 33.42% of the company.

04

Earnings analysis

On a disclosed annual basis, revenue rose from KRW 764.6 billion in 2022 to KRW 937 billion in 2023, a 22.5% increase, then fell 7.1% to KRW 870.3 billion in 2024 and 51.9% to KRW 418.6 billion in 2025.

Revenue has declined for two consecutive years since the 2023 peak, with 2023 the largest year and 2025 the smallest in the 2022 to 2025 window.

Operating profit of KRW 64.9 billion in 2022 became an operating loss of KRW 64.7 billion in 2023, widened to KRW 214.8 billion in 2024, and narrowed to KRW 48.2 billion in 2025, marking three consecutive years of losses. The operating margin moved from 8.5% in 2022 to -6.9% in 2023, -24.7% in 2024 and -11.5% in 2025.

Net income attributable to controlling shareholders went from KRW 47.4 billion in 2022 to net losses of KRW 64.3 billion in 2023, KRW 193.6 billion in 2024 and KRW 120.6 billion in 2025, also three years of losses.

Net income including non-controlling interests, a separate figure, was a net loss of KRW 123.1 billion in 2025.

Operating cash flow swung from KRW 62.6 billion in 2022 to -KRW 1.6 trillion in 2023 before recovering to KRW 688.3 billion in 2024 and KRW 404.6 billion in 2025; for a financial group these flows are sensitive to deposits, loans and trading positions.

The debt-to-equity ratio rose from 642.2% in 2022 to 867.2% in 2025, when total equity was KRW 467.1 billion, of which KRW 382.8 billion belonged to controlling shareholders, against total liabilities of KRW 4.05 trillion.

Quarterly, an operating loss of KRW 59.1 billion in Q3 2025 became an operating profit of KRW 22.5 billion in Q4 2025, then KRW 14.7 billion in Q1 2026 (down 34.4% from the prior quarter), before turning into an operating loss of KRW 4.52 billion in Q2 2026.

The Q3 2026 operating loss narrowed to KRW 3.07 billion, while revenue of KRW 97.7 billion was down 36.4% year on year. Net losses attributable to controlling shareholders over the last four quarters total KRW 68.5 billion.

05

Industry analysis

Conditions in the securities business were difficult in Q3 2026. Most brokerages reported lower third-quarter results than in the second quarter, and weak equity markets and higher market interest rates were cited as external pressures.

Against that backdrop, subsidiary SANGSANGIN INVESTMENT&SECURITIES said its Q3 2026 operating profit rose 59.2% from the prior quarter, according to its November 16, 2026 disclosure. The company attributed this to an expanded contribution from its newly launched equity trading unit and new IB departments.

Cumulative provisions for credit losses in the first three quarters of 2026 were KRW 38 million, down 93.2% from the same period of 2025, which the company linked to the disposal of legacy impaired assets. The net capital ratio rose from 278.4% at the end of 2025 to 292.5% at the end of Q3 2026.

Savings bank earnings depend on funding costs and delinquency trends, so interest rate conditions feed directly into group results. Demand for shipbuilding equipment depends on the order and construction schedules of domestic and overseas shipyards.

The group spans securities, savings banks, shipbuilding and IT, so it is hard to compare directly with large financial holding companies in a single sector. The company itself attributed its valuation discount to continued losses and delayed disposals of affiliates rather than to industry conditions.

06

Outlook

In its value-up plan disclosed in October 2026, the company set a medium-term target of a 0.70x price-to-book ratio for fiscal 2028.

On the P/E side, the plan lists timely disclosure and investor relations engagement to lower the cost of shareholder capital, and a review of the group's business structure to raise expected growth.

On shareholder returns, it lists a review of the need for cash dividends and further share buybacks and cancellations to raise the payout ratio. Strengthening overseas business is also part of the plan.

Subsidiary SANGSANGIN INVESTMENT&SECURITIES said it aims to stay profitable in Q4 2026 and reach a full-year profit, focusing on mezzanine investments, rights offerings and M&A advisory within corporate finance.

The parent's Q4 2026 results are not yet filed in a periodic report; they will appear in the 2026 annual report, due by the end of March 2027. The next checkpoint is therefore the full-year operating result in that report and whether the subsidiary achieves its full-year swing to profit.

Disposals of affiliate stakes are also a variable. A stake in Sangsangin Industry was reportedly set for sale in March 2025, with a call option allowing repurchase within three years included in the agreement.

08

Bull factors

Subsidiary Swing to Profit

Subsidiary SANGSANGIN INVESTMENT&SECURITIES reported cumulative net income of KRW 14.8 billion for the first three quarters of 2026, after a net loss of KRW 4.2 billion in the same period of 2025. Over the same period its IB unit posted a profit of KRW 8.7 billion and its retail unit KRW 3.2 billion. The company said it aims to stay profitable in Q4 and reach a full-year swing to profit.

Narrowing Quarterly Losses

The parent's operating result swung to an operating profit of KRW 22.5 billion in Q4 2025, then to operating losses of KRW 4.52 billion in Q2 2026 and KRW 3.07 billion in Q3 2026. The Q3 loss was smaller than the Q2 loss, continuing the narrowing trend.

The 2025 annual operating loss of KRW 48.2 billion was smaller than the KRW 214.8 billion loss recorded in 2024.

Shareholder Return Review and Treasury Stock

The company set a 0.70x price-to-book target for fiscal 2028 and disclosed a review of cash dividends and share buybacks and cancellations. Treasury stock rose from 1.09% in January 2025 to 5.22% in July 2026. If the review to raise the payout ratio is carried out, it could mark a change in shareholder return policy.

Bear factors

Consecutive Losses and Financial Burden

The company recorded three consecutive years of losses from 2023 to 2025. At the end of 2025, equity attributable to controlling shareholders was KRW 382.8 billion against total liabilities of KRW 4.05 trillion. The debt-to-equity ratio rose from 642.2% in 2022 to 867.2% in 2025.

Net losses attributable to controlling shareholders continued in Q2 and Q3 2026, bringing the last four quarters to KRW 68.5 billion.

Shrinking Revenue

Q3 2026 revenue of KRW 97.7 billion fell 22.5% from the prior quarter and 36.4% from a year earlier. Revenue has declined for two consecutive quarters since the Q1 2026 peak.

Delayed Affiliate Disposals and Governance

The company cited delayed affiliate disposals in its disclosure as a reason for its low price-to-book ratio. A stake in Sangsangin Industry was set for sale in March 2025, but the agreement includes a call option allowing repurchase within three years. Related parties including the largest shareholder held 33.42% as of July 2026, which limits the free float.

10

Risk factors

Equity Market and Rate Environment

The securities unit is strongly affected by trading activity and market interest rates. In Q3 2026, weak equity markets and higher rates weighed on results at most brokerages. Whether the subsidiary's profitability holds if market conditions worsen will show up in its Q4 and full-year results.

Savings Bank Asset Quality

Savings bank earnings move sharply with funding costs and delinquencies. The decline in provisions at the subsidiary reflected the disposal of legacy impaired assets, so whether new impairments emerge is an important point to watch.

Operating cash flow also swung from -KRW 1.6 trillion in 2023 to KRW 688.3 billion in 2024, so cash flow stability is a risk factor.

Group Restructuring and Governance

Restructuring of the group and disposals of affiliates have not yet been tied to specific dated disclosures. Some affiliate stake sales include repurchase rights, so the scale of actual disposals is hard to gauge until deal structures are clear.

A high share held by related parties makes the decision-making structure a factor to examine for minority shareholders.

Overall view

Sangsangin recorded three consecutive years of losses from 2023 to 2025, and 2025 revenue of KRW 418.6 billion was well below the 2023 peak. The operating loss narrowed from KRW 214.8 billion in 2024 to KRW 48.2 billion in 2025, and in 2026 it narrowed further to KRW 4.52 billion in Q2 and KRW 3.07 billion in Q3.

However, the last four quarters show net losses attributable to controlling shareholders of KRW 68.5 billion, and total liabilities of KRW 4.05 trillion at the end of 2025 far exceed total equity of KRW 467.1 billion, a highly leveraged structure.

Subsidiary SANGSANGIN INVESTMENT&SECURITIES swung to cumulative net income of KRW 14.8 billion in the first three quarters of 2026, and its chief executive said it aims for a full-year swing to profit.

The group has set a 0.70x price-to-book target for fiscal 2028 and disclosed a review of dividends, buybacks and cancellations, and business restructuring.

Strengths are the subsidiary's profit trend and shareholder return review, while weaknesses are consecutive losses, shrinking revenue and delayed affiliate disposals. The next checkpoint is the full-year operating result in the 2026 annual report and whether the subsidiary achieves its full-year swing to profit.
