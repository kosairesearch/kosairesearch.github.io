Milestone-driven ADC earnings, with wide quarterly swings

AimedBio turned profitable in 2025 on three ADC licensing deals, but most of its revenue depends on when milestones are recognized, so quarterly results swing widely.

02

Business structure

AimedBio was spun out of Samsung Medical Center in 2018 to develop ADC cancer drugs. Its core technology is P-ADC, an integrated ADC discovery platform that combines patient-derived cells and animal models with genomic and transcriptomic data. It has three main assets.

It signed deals for AMB302 with Biohaven (December 2024), for AMB303 with SK plasma (May 2025) and for ODS025 with Boehringer Ingelheim (October 2025). The Boehringer Ingelheim deal is worth up to KRW 1.4 trillion. Together, the licensing and co-development deals total more than KRW 3 trillion.

In practice, licensing is the only source of revenue. In the first half of 2026, KRW 24.8 billion of licensing revenue came from overseas and KRW 3.7 billion from Korea. The SK plasma deal works differently: AimedBio develops the asset with its partner and the two co-own the license.

The Samsung relationship is another pillar. AimedBio was chosen as the only co-development partner for Samsung Biologics' ADC toolbox program. The CEO has said the company does not sell platforms but builds high-value ADC assets that global drugmakers can acquire directly. That sets its model apart from LigaChem Biosciences, a Korean ADC peer that relies more on platform deals.

04

Earnings analysis

On a reported basis, revenue rose 302.0% from KRW 11.8 billion in 2024 to KRW 47.3 billion in 2025. The company swung from an operating loss of KRW 408 million in 2024 to an operating profit of KRW 20.8 billion in 2025, and the operating margin went from -3.5% to 43.9%.

Net income attributable to owners also turned positive, from a loss of KRW 3.3 billion to a profit of KRW 5.02 billion, but it was far smaller than operating profit. The 2025 income statement shows non-operating expenses of about KRW 27.2 billion, which produced a pre-tax loss that an income tax benefit offset.

Of 2025 revenue of KRW 47.3 billion, KRW 38 billion was booked in the fourth quarter. Fourth-quarter operating profit was KRW 25.5 billion and attributable net income was KRW 34.9 billion. That timing matches the October 2025 Boehringer Ingelheim deal.

In Q1 2026, revenue fell 77.6% quarter on quarter to KRW 8.5 billion, and the company swung to an operating loss of KRW 3.96 billion and an attributable net loss of KRW 2.01 billion.

In Q2 2026, revenue rose 137.7% quarter on quarter to KRW 20.2 billion, and the company returned to profit with operating profit of KRW 8.52 billion and attributable net income of KRW 10.9 billion. Q2 included a milestone payment tied to progress on ODS025 and co-development fees for AMB303.

Costs are rising too: R&D and SG&A expenses roughly doubled in 2025 to KRW 26.5 billion. Operating cash flow turned positive, from -KRW 4.87 billion in 2024 to KRW 17.9 billion in 2025. Third-quarter 2026 results have not yet been filed in a periodic report.

05

Industry analysis

According to company materials, the global ADC market is growing at more than 29% a year, and global ADC licensing deals topped USD 16.5 billion in 2023. The company says that as big pharma keeps signing large deals, finished ADC assets have come to command higher prices than platform companies.

On room for growth, the CEO has said ADCs make up less than 5% of the overall cancer drug market. Competition is shifting toward discovering new targets. The company focuses on clean targets that are barely expressed in normal tissue.

In the FGFR3 space, the benchmark for AMB302 is existing small-molecule FGFR inhibitors. Biohaven has said the main class toxicities known from FGFR inhibitors have not been seen so far.

In Korea, Samsung Biologics is building ADC contract development and manufacturing capacity, while LigaChem Biosciences and ABL Bio pitch to the same global drugmakers as licensing-focused biotechs.

AimedBio has licensed out all three of its early clinical-stage assets, so clinical data will decide its next competitive edge.

06

Outlook

The company has raised its 2026 outlook from the forecast it gave at listing. It cited clearer accounting standards for some contracts, which changed how revenue is recognized, and a revised exchange-rate assumption (from KRW 1,340 to KRW 1,450 per dollar).

Reported first-half 2026 revenue is the sum of KRW 8.5 billion and KRW 20.2 billion, so the second half needs larger recognition to reach the full-year guidance of KRW 56.3 billion. One second-half source of revenue is confirmed.

On October 26, 2026, the company received written notice from Boehringer Ingelheim that an ODS025 milestone had been reached. Press reports put that milestone at KRW 4.7 billion or more. AMB303 is next: the CEO has said the goal is to file and win approval for its clinical trial application within 2026.

The long-term strategy is also changing. The company plans to expand into central nervous system diseases such as brain disorders and to build 15 clinical-stage programs by 2030. It also plans to license more assets only after running its own clinical trials. Biohaven has started a combination study of AMB302 with Libtayo.

08

Bull factors

Milestones tied to clinical entry

All three contracts pay upfront fees plus stage-based milestones. ODS025 has triggered two milestones: FDA clearance in June 2026 and first-patient dosing in October. The June payment equaled at least 10% of 2025 consolidated revenue. More payments can follow as the programs advance through clinical stages.

Early clinical responses for AMB302

In Biohaven's Phase 1 trial, objective responses, including confirmed partial responses, were seen across several tumor types and patients. Dose escalation has not yet reached a dose-limiting toxicity.

Responses came in FGFR3-overexpressing tumors as well as FGFR3-mutant ones, so the company believes the eligible patient population could be broader than for existing inhibitors. It is also the first sign of efficacy in humans for a molecule the company discovered, which supports confidence in its platform.

Balance sheet to fund in-house trials

Cash and short-term financial instruments reached KRW 170.8 billion at end-2025, about six times the KRW 35.5 billion a year earlier. The debt-to-equity ratio fell from 1,299.8% in 2024 to 3.3% in 2025. The CEO has said net assets, including cash, now exceed KRW 200 billion. That lets the company build clinical data before negotiating, rather than rushing to license assets.

09

Bear factors

A single source of revenue

More than 99% of revenue comes from licensing, so quarters without milestones, such as Q1 2026, end in operating losses. The drop from KRW 38 billion of revenue in Q4 2025 to KRW 8.5 billion in Q1 2026 shows how this works.

Analysts have also noted that the timing of revenue recognition can make quarterly results highly volatile. With no recurring revenue, earnings are hard to forecast with confidence.

A growing cost base

All KRW 55.7 billion raised in the IPO is earmarked for R&D and platform building. The company expects R&D spending of KRW 13.2 billion to KRW 24 billion a year over five years. Running its own trials instead of licensing assets early means it carries far more cost per trial. If milestones fail to keep pace with rising costs, loss-making quarters could become more frequent.

Gap between net and operating profit

Operating profit was KRW 20.8 billion in 2025, but attributable net income was only KRW 5.02 billion. Non-operating expenses were large, and the company posted a pre-tax loss. Coming quarterly reports will show whether costs linked to capital-structure changes around the listing recur. With the stock at a wide premium to book value, it could react sharply to disappointing data.

10

Risk factors

Clinical and contract

The company has said its licensing contracts can be terminated if trials, approvals or commercialization do not go as planned. All three assets are at Phase 1 or earlier, the stage where failure rates are high. A partner could also halt development if its priorities change. If a contract ends, the remaining milestones go with it.

Limited disclosure

The terms of the Biohaven deal have not been disclosed. The target of ODS025 has not been revealed either. Most milestone amounts are known only as ranges, which makes earnings hard to forecast from the outside. Much of the information on development progress also depends on partner announcements.

Currency

Overseas deals generated the overwhelming share of licensing revenue in the first half of 2026, so the won-dollar exchange rate directly affects revenue in won. When it revised its 2026 outlook, the company raised its exchange-rate assumption to KRW 1,450 per dollar.

If the won strengthens, the same dollar milestone brings in less revenue in won. Filings do not show how much dollar-denominated R&D costs offset this exposure.

12

Overall view

AimedBio turned profitable in 2025, with revenue of KRW 47.3 billion and operating profit of KRW 20.8 billion, making it one of the few KOSDAQ biotechs to earn a profit from licensing alone. But because most revenue arrives on milestone timing, reported quarterly results have swung between profit and loss.

In the second half of 2026, a second milestone was confirmed when ODS025 dosed its first patient. Early clinical responses for AMB302 have also been disclosed, so the story is moving from deal-making to clinical data.

On the other side are rising costs from in-house trials, undisclosed deal terms and a wide premium to book value. The next things to watch are the third-quarter cost structure, the AMB303 trial application and whether full-year 2026 results meet guidance.
