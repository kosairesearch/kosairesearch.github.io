Report overview

Nuclear bets against a loss-making core

Konasol's core steel-roll business is still loss-making while neutron absorbers for nuclear plants and semiconductor sputtering targets are being built into new growth lines.

02

Business structure

Konasol is a materials company whose core lines are metal composites and nano-fusion products, and it currently runs steel, semiconductor, mobility, energy, and powder business units.

Of 2024 revenue of 18.9 billion won, steel accounted for 14.4 billion won, semiconductors for 3.0 billion won, and mobility for 1.5 billion won. The steel unit centers on rolls and guide rollers used in steel mill equipment, and its share of sales fell from over 93 percent in 2022 to 76.0 percent in 2024.

The semiconductor unit has production and quality approval from major domestic chipmakers and is centered on sputtering targets, with applications extending to batteries and PCBs.

The mobility unit consists mainly of lubricant additives and planar heating elements, and the nano-diamond lubricant additive is seen as a next-stage growth driver tied to nuclear power expansion. The energy unit's neutron absorbers and nuclear materials build on HIP high-temperature, high-pressure forming.

On competition, the company's in-house Alloy-TiC material has been described as offering better wear resistance than Fujico's KFC705 from Japan.

In the global roll market, Proterial (formerly Hitachi Metals) holds the HINEX brand, so Konasol is seeking to expand from domestic steelmakers into overseas mill equipment markets.

Domestic steel demand, however, faces downward pressure from lower auto and construction output and the overseas shift of appliance steel production. The large HIP unit installed in July 2024 became the base for domestically produced large monoblock rolls that were previously imported.

03

Recent trends

According to recent reports, 2025 revenue reached 19.9 billion won, up from the prior year, and operating loss narrowed to 2.3 billion won from 3.3 billion won in 2024 according to the 2024 business report.

Net profit turned to 18.0 billion won, but this was driven by a gain on disposal of tangible assets and is separate from core profitability. First-half 2025 revenue was 8.9 billion won, slightly lower year on year, which analysts attributed to a global slowdown and delayed export recovery.

Over the same period, operating loss was 2.3 billion won and the operating margin was -25.2 percent. Debt-to-equity was 281.8 percent, the current ratio 72.6 percent, and the equity ratio 26.2 percent at the end of the first half of 2025.

Total liabilities at the end of 2024 were 31.6 billion won, up about 23.0 percent year on year, while total equity fell to 13.2 billion won.

A May 29, 2025 agreement with US-listed Materion covers the sale of some tantalum target production equipment, and the company expects this to improve its financial structure and provide growth funding.

At the March 31, 2026 annual general meeting, amendments to the articles and a stock option grant were approved, and on March 27, 2026 a cancellation of part of the stock option grant reduced total options from 569,500 shares to 209,150 shares.

The share price closed at 3,345 won on February 13, 2026, well below the 52-week high of 8,200 won, and the 12-month change was -47.38 percent. The current price is 1,794 won, down 2.97 percent from the previous close, with trading value of 224,319 won.

04

Outlook

The nuclear unit rests on a 40 billion won framework supply agreement signed in 2022, and the current Orano TN Americas order of about 600 million won is the first volume contract. A production order was received in April 2026, with first shipments reported to start in August 2026.

How quickly the framework agreement converts into recognized revenue is the key variable for this unit. The company also plans to pursue domestic dry-storage projects as domestic demand for spent fuel storage grows.

In semiconductors, a letter of intent with Materion underpins joint development of new semiconductor materials and entry into shared global markets. In planar heaters, a 2023 report indicated orders totaling 15.4 billion won through 2027.

The company has set a 2030 target of 300 billion won in revenue and 2 trillion won in market value, which is a very wide gap from 2025 revenue of 19.9 billion won. A Korea Technology & Credit Rating report projected 2025 revenue of about 19.0 billion won, and actual revenue came in slightly above that.

The second HIP expansion is described as nearing completion, which would shift cash spending toward maintenance investment.

05

Bull factors

Entry into the global nuclear materials chain

Konasol signed a volume supply contract for neutron absorbers with Orano TN Americas, with roughly 600 million won in revenue expected from the deal. It builds on a framework agreement of about 40 billion won signed in early 2022, after the company completed nuclear quality assurance and production qualification.

Nuclear-grade quality assurance is a high barrier, so securing a global reference carries weight. The company is also seeking to join domestic dry-storage projects on that basis.

Semiconductor references and partnerships

Konasol has production and quality approval from major domestic chipmakers, and reports say it has been the only domestic supplier of high-purity tantalum sputtering targets, about 23,000 pieces over 14 years.

A letter of intent with Materion signed alongside the 2025 asset deal covers joint development of new semiconductor materials and global market entry. The semiconductor share of sales rose to 15.7 percent in 2024, and the unit is cited as a pillar of revenue diversification.

Large HIP capacity and differentiated materials

The large HIP unit installed in July 2024 was introduced as a domestically built system meeting 200 MPa pressure and 2,000 degrees Celsius operating conditions. The company says it enables production of large monoblock rolls that were previously imported.

Its in-house Alloy-TiC material has been assessed as offering better wear resistance than Fujico's KFC705. The same equipment can also process ceramics, which supports expansion into higher-value nuclear and defense products.

06

Bear factors

Losses in the core and fixed-cost burden

Operating losses continued at 2.6 billion won in 2023 and 3.3 billion won in 2024, and the first half of 2025 recorded a further 2.3 billion won loss. Early investment in new businesses and fixed costs are cited as the main drivers of margin pressure.

Cost of sales held at 60 to 70 percent of revenue, but rising selling and administrative costs such as labor weighed on profitability. The operating margin in the first half of 2025 was -25.2 percent, lower than a year earlier.

High leverage and a thin equity base

Debt-to-equity rose to 281.8 percent in the first half of 2025 from 239.5 percent at the end of 2024. The equity ratio was 26.2 percent and the current ratio 72.6 percent, leaving limited short-term liquidity headroom.

Total equity fell to 11.1 billion won, and borrowing growth was attributed to new business investment combined with working capital needs. A 5.0 billion won third-party rights offering was carried out in 2024, with proceeds earmarked for facilities and operations.

Weak steel demand and concentrated sales

Domestic steel demand faces downward pressure from lower auto and construction output and the overseas shift of appliance steel production. The steel unit still accounted for 76.0 percent of 2024 sales, so it remains the center of reported results. First-half 2025 revenue was 8.9 billion won, lower than a year earlier.

The share price stood well below its 52-week high as of February 2026, and as a KONEX small cap, trading value was only 224,319 won.

07

Risk factors

Macro

Steel demand depends heavily on auto and construction investment and on the shift of production overseas. A 2023 report noted that prices of key inputs, including metals, ceramics, and high-speed tool steel powders, have trended upward since 2020.

If these costs cannot be passed on to customers, cost of sales pressure would rise. The report also indicated that the currency effect has shrunk as the export share declined.

Regulatory and certification

Neutron absorbers can only be supplied after passing nuclear-grade quality assurance and demanding product evaluation. Qualification took about three and a half years, so certification timelines carry delay risk.

Changes in nuclear policy and the timing of domestic dry-storage project awards will also shape when new orders arrive. Semiconductor materials likewise go through lengthy customer-specific approval processes.

Financing

With debt-to-equity at 281.8 percent and a current ratio of 72.6 percent, the company is sensitive to additional borrowing conditions. The 2025 asset sale to Materion was expected to support the financial structure, and the use of proceeds will be reflected in later disclosures.

Stock option grants were also cancelled and restated, adding to changes in the capital structure. If new-business investment runs ahead of a return to core profit, funding pressure could persist.

08

Overall view

Konasol posted 2025 revenue of 19.9 billion won and an operating loss of 2.3 billion won, so core profitability has not yet recovered. The net profit turn reflects a gain on asset disposal and is hard to view as a repeatable result.

Neutron absorbers, semiconductor sputtering targets, and HIP-based large rolls are cited as strengths in fields with high qualification barriers.

On the other hand, debt-to-equity of 281.8 percent and an equity ratio of 26.2 percent in the first half of 2025 show a financial structure that is thin for absorbing new-business investment.

Weak steel demand and uncertain nuclear order volumes coexist, so the timing of any earnings recovery depends on whether nuclear and semiconductor sales exceed fixed costs. The 2030 target of 300 billion won in revenue sits far above current results, and its path remains to be confirmed in future disclosures. It is also a fact that the company is a KONEX small cap with trading value of 224,319 won.
