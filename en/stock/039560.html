02

Business structure

DASAN Networks is a network equipment maker founded in 1993 and listed on KOSDAQ in 2000. Its core products are access network equipment and wireless LAN devices for internet data communications.

KT, SK Telecom and LG Uplus are cited as its main customers among South Korea's three largest carriers, so carrier capital spending flows directly into revenue.

The business has been widening into automotive electronics, where it co-developed Ethernet software for CCU units with Hyundai Mobis and began mass production of TMS, TGU and ESU units with HD Hyundai Construction Equipment.

Its subsidiary DTS Inc. supplies air coolers to a US LNG developer and sits inside the LNG value chain. DTS was reported to have won Phase 2 work following Phase 1 on a Venture Global project. Overseas affiliate DZS Inc. filed for bankruptcy in March 2025, citing liquidity pressure.

Since then the mix has tilted toward domestic telecom equipment, automotive electronics and LNG. Segment revenue shares should be checked in the segment disclosures of the business report.

04

Earnings analysis

Annual revenue rose from KRW 80.7 billion in 2022 to KRW 92.3 billion in 2023 (up 14.3%), KRW 337.6 billion in 2024 (up 265.8%) and KRW 541.8 billion in 2025 (up 60.5%), growing for three consecutive years from 2023. Operating profit lagged the revenue expansion.

Operating losses of KRW 565 million in 2022, KRW 1.25 billion in 2023 and KRW 8.28 billion in 2024 widened the deficit before the company posted operating profit of KRW 38.5 billion in 2025. The operating margin moved from -0.7% in 2022, -1.4% in 2023 and -2.5% in 2024 to 7.1% in 2025.

Net income attributable to shareholders was KRW 195.9 billion in 2022, then turned to a loss of KRW 93.4 billion in 2023. The loss narrowed to KRW 24.9 billion in 2024 and KRW 7.17 billion in 2025.

Total net income including non-controlling interests was KRW 4.19 billion in 2025, which points in a different direction from the parent-level loss, so non-controlling interest results need separate review.

The 2022 parent-level profit exceeded that year's revenue by a wide margin, which suggests non-operating items should be examined. Operating cash flow shifted from an outflow of KRW 58.2 billion in 2022 to an inflow of KRW 19.4 billion in 2025.

The debt-to-equity ratio rose from 22.6% in 2022 to 92.0% in 2024 before easing to 79.1% in 2025.

Quarterly, revenue was KRW 136.1 billion with operating profit of KRW 12.6 billion in the third quarter of 2025, and in the fourth quarter revenue was KRW 156.4 billion with operating profit of KRW 5.95 billion, while the parent-level net loss was KRW 35.2 billion, a direction different from operating profit.

Revenue was KRW 120.4 billion in the first quarter of 2026, KRW 159.2 billion in the second and KRW 132.9 billion in the third, with third-quarter operating profit of KRW 8.31 billion and parent-level net income of KRW 19.4 billion. Parent-level net losses across the last four quarters total KRW 6.65 billion.

05

Industry analysis

Several major South Korean telecom equipment makers returned to a stronger earnings footing in 2025, and DASAN Networks is reported among those that swung from loss to profit. Over the same period, KMW and other wireless equipment makers only narrowed their losses, which makes DASAN's turnaround stand out somewhat.

Revenue in this segment depends on carrier investment cycles, and DASAN's heavy reliance on South Korea's three largest carriers means it is directly exposed to that cycle. The company is building automotive electronics and LNG projects to reduce that dependence.

In automotive electronics, its work with Hyundai Mobis and HD Hyundai Construction Equipment is widening the product lineup. In LNG, subsidiary DTS Inc. supplies a US project, so its customer base differs from carriers.

Against KMW, a wireless-focused peer, DASAN's 2025 return to profit ranks among the larger improvements in the group. Third-quarter 2026 revenue fell 2.3% year on year, so whether revenue growth carries across quarters remains to be confirmed.

How the domestic and automotive-focused structure settles after the overseas affiliate restructuring will shape the company's position within the industry.

06

Outlook

In its 2026 corporate value-up plan, disclosed on April 20, 2026, the company set out profit-focused management as its goal. Specific measures include strengthening cost competitiveness to expand the operating margin, growth investment and stabilizing the borrowing structure.

The plan also covers a cash dividend policy pursued in light of domestic and external conditions. It further states that setting a dividend record date after the dividend amount is fixed would improve predictability.

Second-quarter 2026 operating profit was KRW 16.3 billion, a relatively strong quarter, but third-quarter operating profit fell to KRW 8.31 billion, so how steady quarterly operating profit stays is the key thing to watch.

On the capital side, warrant exercises in May 2026 added 400,800 common shares to the listed count. On November 12, 2026 the board resolved to cancel 822,713 treasury shares, with the scheduled date on November 20, 2026. After cancellation, total issued shares would fall from 42,888,468 to 42,065,755.

The proposal to approve the listing of subsidiary DTS Inc. was tabled at the extraordinary shareholders meeting on June 19, 2026. The fourth-quarter 2026 results and full-year operating margin will be confirmed in the 2026 annual business report.

Bull factors

Shift to a profitable structure

Operating profit reached KRW 38.5 billion in 2025, a swing back to profit with a 7.1% operating margin. Operating cash flow also turned positive at KRW 19.4 billion. The debt-to-equity ratio eased from 92.0% in 2024 to 79.1% in 2025. On an annual basis, earnings strength is recovering.

Customer base widening into automotive and LNG

Joint development of CCU Ethernet software with Hyundai Mobis and the start of mass production of TMS, TGU and ESU units with HD Hyundai Construction Equipment are widening the automotive revenue base. Subsidiary DTS has joined the LNG value chain with Phase 1 and Phase 2 work on a Venture Global project. This is read as a move away from a carrier-centered customer base.

Shareholder returns and capital structure moves

Cancellation of 822,713 treasury shares scheduled for November 20, 2026 would reduce shares outstanding. The 2026 value-up plan includes expanding the operating margin, stabilizing the borrowing structure and pursuing a cash dividend policy. It is notable that the plan pairs capital structure goals with shareholder return items.

Bear factors

Volatility in quarterly earnings

The fourth-quarter 2025 parent-level net loss of KRW 35.2 billion ran against operating profit in the same quarter. Third-quarter 2026 operating profit of KRW 8.31 billion fell 34.2% year on year. A steady build-up of quarterly profit has not yet shown up in the figures.

Gap between revenue growth and consecutive losses

Parent-level net losses were recorded for three consecutive years from 2023 to 2025. Revenue grew from KRW 80.7 billion in 2022 to KRW 541.8 billion in 2025, yet losses persisted. Third-quarter 2026 revenue was KRW 132.9 billion, down 2.3% year on year. Closing the gap between revenue scale and earnings strength remains necessary.

Balance sheet and dilution factors

The debt-to-equity ratio reached 92.0% in 2024, and total liabilities of KRW 272 billion at end-2025 are similar in size to parent-level equity of KRW 261.2 billion. Warrant exercises in May 2026 added 400,800 common shares to the listed count. A correction to a fund loan decision disclosed in October 2026 showed an increased loan limit.

10

Risk factors

Customer concentration and carrier investment cycle

Three major carriers are the main customers, so their facility investment levels flow directly into revenue. Earnings took time to follow revenue growth in 2024 and 2025. The year-on-year decline in third-quarter 2026 revenue is one example of volatility arising from this structure. The pace at which automotive and LNG customers expand is the condition that would ease this risk.

Balance sheet and borrowing burden

The 92.0% debt-to-equity ratio in 2024 shows a period of heavy borrowing relative to equity. It eased to 79.1% in 2025, but total liabilities remain at KRW 272 billion. Listing borrowing structure stabilization as a plan item shows the company recognizes this burden. The correction that raised a fund loan limit is another reason to monitor cash flows closely.

Overseas affiliates and project dependence

Overseas affiliate DZS Inc. filed for bankruptcy in March 2025 citing liquidity pressure. The LNG business at subsidiary DTS relies on project awards from Venture Global, so its results can move with the pace of specific projects.

How the share of overseas projects in consolidated results changes after the group restructuring remains an open question.

12

Overall view

DASAN Networks swung back to profit in 2025 with revenue of KRW 541.8 billion and operating profit of KRW 38.5 billion, a sign that annual earnings strength is recovering. Operating cash flow turning positive and the debt-to-equity ratio easing to 79.1% are also improvements on the balance sheet.

On the other side, parent-level net losses have continued for three consecutive years from 2023, and third-quarter 2026 operating profit fell 34.2% year on year. With parent-level net losses over the last four quarters totaling KRW 6.65 billion, the stability of quarterly profit is still being tested.

The automotive electronics business and LNG projects at DTS are cited as ways to broaden the carrier-centered customer base, but how much they feed into revenue must be confirmed through future disclosures.

The bankruptcy of overseas affiliate DZS and the correction to the fund loan limit are points to monitor for financial risk. Treasury share cancellation and the value-up plan mark changes on the shareholder return side.

The next checkpoints are the fourth-quarter 2026 results in the annual business report and quarterly profit in the first quarter of 2027.
