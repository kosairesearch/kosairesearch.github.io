02

Business structure

MiraeN Edupartner focuses on private-participation computer classes and full after-school outsourcing in the elementary after-school learning market. The company was established in April 2009 and is listed on the KONEX market.

It began operating fully outsourced after-school programs in 2018 and adopted its current name in 2020. Its main shareholders are Mirae-N Co. and Yong-seop Choi.

Its competitive strengths include in-house animation-based hands-on computer education content, a textbook publishing system, and an instructor operation and training framework. The company is expanding IT content such as Python and metaverse programs, and is extending into teacher training and facility rental.

According to Saramin company data, education services account for 99.1% of its business mix. For 2025, consolidated revenue was KRW 22.2 billion and separate revenue was KRW 19.5 billion.

Outsourcing vendors are selected through school-level two-stage bidding on specification and price, and some education office guidelines set the instructor fee as the base amount for competitive bids.

In one Incheon elementary school, a different vendor won the full outsourcing contract, which shows that contracts are contested school by school.

03

Recent trends

In 2025, separate-basis revenue rose 17.8% to KRW 19.54 billion, while operating profit fell 72.2% to KRW 147 million. The operating margin was 0.75%. In 2024, separate revenue was KRW 16.59 billion and operating profit was KRW 530 million.

The company posted an operating loss of KRW 1.08 billion in 2022 and an operating profit of KRW 41 million in 2023, so it has moved out of loss territory. Net income for 2025 was roughly KRW 21.7 million, while pre-tax profit was slightly negative, so the company was close to breakeven before tax.

Total equity at the end of 2025 was KRW 3.96 billion. The market capitalization of KRW 3.6 billion on October 27, 2026 is below that equity figure. The debt ratio was 128.33% and borrowings dependence was 33.69%.

Investor-relations disclosures were filed on April 21, May 28 and September 21, 2026, but results for the first half of 2026 or later could not be confirmed through our searches. The share price was KRW 900, unchanged from the prior session, and traded value was zero, so price discovery is limited.

04

Outlook

From 2026, Neulbom school was renamed community-wide elementary care and education, and third graders also receive an after-school program voucher. The voucher is KRW 500,000 per year, and the Ministry of Education aims to raise third-grade after-school participation from 42% last year to 60%.

The ministry said it would analyze the voucher's results and review extending eligibility to fourth grade and above. The free two-hour daily customized program for first and second graders continues.

Because the voucher can be used on school after-school programs, the environment may support demand for computer classes, but the actual benefit depends on enrollment at each school. Some education office guidelines state that a program cannot open when demand surveys show no demand.

There are also rules requiring a new public tender once a contract with the same vendor passes one year, so continuity depends on each bidding round. NICE Information Service said in its 2025 technical analysis report that the expansion of the Neulbom policy was expected to provide a new growth driver.

On operating metrics, the 0.75% operating margin leaves considerable room for improvement, but the pace depends on instructor fees and the cost structure of textbooks and content.

05

Bull factors

Policy-linked demand base

Its main market is public elementary schools, which is viewed as more stable than the private tutoring market. Third graders now receive an after-school voucher, which broadens the user base of school after-school programs.

Computer and coding education is one of the programs that can be chosen with the voucher, so the company sits within the reach of the policy change.

Revenue recovery and continued profitability

Separate revenue rose from KRW 16.59 billion in 2024 to KRW 19.54 billion in 2025, exceeding the 2023 level. Operating profit was positive for three straight years from 2023 to 2025, after an operating loss in 2022. As the business scales, a lower fixed-cost burden could improve the operating margin.

Integrated content, textbook and instructor model

The company owns in-house content, a textbook publishing system, and an instructor operation and training framework. The largest shareholder changed to Mirae-N Co., placing the company within an education and publishing group. This structure can serve as a point of differentiation in outsourcing bid proposals.

Bear factors

Operating margin below 1%

The 2025 operating margin was 0.75%, down from 3.20% in 2024. Revenue rose 17.8% while operating profit fell 72.2%, so higher sales did not translate into profit. This implies a heavier cost and administrative burden, although we could not confirm the specific causes.

Bidding competition and short contract cycles

Vendors are chosen through school-level competitive bidding, and some guidelines use the instructor fee as the base amount. Contracts run for up to one year, and a contract with the same vendor must be re-tendered once it passes one year. Intensifying price competition could push profitability lower.

Illiquidity of a micro-cap KONEX stock

On October 27, 2026, market capitalization was KRW 3.6 billion and traded value was zero. The price stayed at KRW 900, unchanged from the prior session, so no new price was formed in the market. Orders may be hard to execute, which makes it difficult to judge whether the price reflects the company's value.

07

Risk factors

Policy

Neulbom school was reorganized into community-wide elementary care and education in 2026, and any expansion of voucher eligibility will be reviewed after results are analyzed. Recent media coverage also carried a proposal to shift after-school and care services to a public model led by metropolitan education offices. A change in policy direction could alter outsourcing demand and contract structures.

Recent reporting says that fundamental improvements to the outsourcing structure remain insufficient even after the Libaschool controversy. In Gyeonggi Province, criticism over instructor treatment and educational quality has grown as outsourced operation has increased. Stricter oversight of outsourcing vendors could raise operating costs.

At the end of 2025, the debt ratio was 128.33%, borrowings dependence was 33.69% and the interest coverage ratio was 0.76. Operating profit did not fully cover interest costs, so any weakness in profitability could increase financial strain. Low liquidity on KONEX and a small capital base also constrain fundraising and share transactions.

08

Overall view

MiraeN Edupartner is a small education services company that operates computer classes and full outsourcing in the elementary after-school market. Separate revenue grew to KRW 19.54 billion in 2025, but the operating margin was only 0.75%, so whether top-line growth converts into profit is the central issue.

The Neulbom reorganization and the third-grade voucher are favorable on the demand side, while school-level bidding, contracts of up to one year and criticism of the outsourcing model add uncertainty. The debt ratio is 128.33% and interest coverage is below 1, so financial headroom is not generous.

With a market capitalization of KRW 3.6 billion and zero traded value on October 27, 2026, it is hard to judge how well the market price reflects changes in the company's results.
