02

Business structure

MiraeN Edupartner is an after-school education service company founded in 2009, running after-school class programs for elementary schools and kindergartens.

Its core offerings are the after-school computer and coding class 'Edupartner Computer Class', the after-school English class 'JumpEnglish Class', and the 'Full After-school Program' that operates the entire after-school program for elementary schools.

According to company information published by Saramin, the education service segment accounts for 99.1% of business segment weight, making the business effectively single-segment.

Tuition fees are the main revenue source, and according to FnGuide, the tuition share was 79.34% in 2024 while non-tuition sales, mainly textbooks, were 20.66%.

The company develops and supplies IT education textbooks, creative convergence coding content, and teaching tools, and also supplies content to other businesses in the industry.

As of year-end 2024, eight companies were consolidated, including Jump Edu (after-school English outsourcing and educational publications), Haeram Books (publishing and books), Edupartner School, and five regional subsidiaries.

In 2020, MiraeN acquired a 44.1% stake through a third-party rights offering and became the largest shareholder, and its current stake is 88.45%. In the private after-school market, operators are diversifying solutions as policy changes, and competing to widen share.

Market share figures by competitor are not available for direct comparison, so qualitatively the opportunity set depends on school policy and outsourcing structures.

03

Recent trends

On the disclosed basis, 2025 revenue was KRW 19.54 billion, up from KRW 16.59 billion in 2024, and the NICE Credit Evaluation data shows revenue growth of 17.8%. Operating profit in the same year was KRW 147.44 million, down sharply from KRW 530.44 million in 2024, with an operating margin of only 0.8%.

Net profit was KRW 21.72 million, a steep decline from KRW 579.12 million in 2024. In 2023 the company posted operating profit of KRW 40.82 million but a net loss of KRW 510.48 million, and in 2022 it recorded an operating loss of KRW 1.08 billion and a net loss of KRW 1.22 billion.

On a consolidated basis (FnGuide), 2025 revenue was about KRW 22.2 billion and operating profit about KRW 300 million, with operating profit down 61.0% year on year. The cost of sales ratio rose from 76.53% in 2022 to 82.04% in 2025, increasing cost pressure.

Operating cash flow fell from roughly KRW 2.9 billion in 2023 to roughly KRW 1.3 billion in 2025. As of April 15, 2026, the share price returned -9.09% over one month and -22.18% over one year, with a 52-week range of KRW 751 to KRW 1,269 according to wisereport. The current price is KRW 900 and market capitalization is KRW 3.6 billion.

04

Outlook

The starting point for future results is the 2025 full-year performance. Revenue grew but profit remains thin, so room for improvement in business indicators depends mainly on the gross margin and the efficiency of selling and administrative expenses.

The consolidated cost of sales ratio rose into the low 82% range in 2025, so whether that ratio falls is the key to recovering the operating margin.

On policy, childcare for first and second graders expanded from 2025, and a program voucher of about KRW 500,000 for third graders was provided in 2026, with plans to extend it to sixth grade.

If this policy lifts school after-school demand, the revenue base could widen, but the voucher structure means the actual revenue impact will depend on parent choice and school operations. Whether the company can raise non-tuition income by selling more textbooks and content is also a point to monitor.

Financially, the interest coverage multiple around 0.76 means the key question is whether operating profit continues to fall short of covering interest expense. The next checkpoints are whether quarterly results from 2026 onward show a stable cost ratio and sustained operating profit.

The largest shareholder's 88.45% stake and the low free float ratio will remain constraints on trading liquidity.

05

Bull factors

Expanding after-school and childcare policy

Government after-school and childcare policy is strengthening private-sector cooperation and systematizing customized program operations. Childcare for first and second graders expanded from 2025, and a KRW 500,000 program voucher for third graders was introduced for 2026, with plans to extend coverage to sixth grade.

The 17.8% revenue growth in 2025 suggests this demand expansion has translated into top-line growth. However, how the policy affects enrollment and unit pricing cannot be separated from the disclosed figures.

Proprietary textbook and content base

The company develops and supplies its own IT education textbooks, creative convergence coding content, and teaching tools. It also supplies content to other businesses in the industry, which could broaden income sources beyond tuition.

The non-tuition share rose slightly to 20.66% in 2024 from 19.77% in 2023, according to FnGuide. Entry into the adult market and IT content development are cited as expansion directions. Its link to the education publishing capabilities of parent MiraeN is viewed as a content-related strength.

Financial buffer indicators

Based on 2025 figures, the equity ratio was 43.80% and the borrowing dependence ratio was 33.69%, both lower than in 2024. The reserve ratio of 109.55% indicates that retained earnings remain positive. The credit rating was rated good as of March 2026, according to Catch.

Total equity was KRW 3.96 billion in 2025, slightly up from 2024. Operating cash flow stayed positive in 2025, indicating a cash-generating base remains even in loss-making periods.

06

Bear factors

Shrinking profit margins

Operating profit in 2025 was KRW 147.44 million, down sharply year on year, and the operating margin was only 0.8%. Revenue grew 17.8%, but consolidated gross profit fell 3.8% year on year. The rise in the cost of sales ratio from 76.53% in 2022 to 82.04% in 2025 appears to be the main pressure.

The selling and administrative expense ratio eased from 17.60% in 2024 to 16.60% in 2025 but did not offset the higher cost ratio.

Interest burden and short-term liquidity

The 2025 debt ratio was 128.33%, lower than 131.62% in 2024 but still high. The interest coverage multiple of about 0.76 means operating profit does not fully cover interest expense.

Current liabilities in 2025 were in the KRW 4.3 billion range, exceeding current assets in the KRW 3.9 billion range, which leaves short-term repayment pressure. The quick ratio was 74.38%, below 100%.

Thin liquidity and concentrated ownership

The largest shareholder holds 88.45% and the free float ratio is 11.55%, leaving a small tradable float. Trading value is currently zero, indicating very low trading liquidity. Returns as of April 15, 2026 were -9.09% over one month and -22.18% over one year.

Liquidity constraints could widen given the trading characteristics of the KONEX market. The increase in the largest shareholder's stake from 44.1% after the 2020 rights offering to 88.45% is also a feature of the ownership structure.

07

Risk factors

Macro

Education demand depends on household income and parents' spending capacity. If rates or inflation reduce household education spending, tuition-based revenue would feel the impact directly. With tuition at 79.34% of sales in 2024, changes in household spending pass through quickly to revenue.

Policy

After-school and childcare operations change with government budgets and guidelines. If the voucher or outsourcing structure changes, or budgets shrink, contract volumes with schools could shift. Policy expansion is an opportunity, but it also carries regulatory risk from institutional changes.

Liquidity and governance

The largest shareholder holds 88.45% and the free float is 11.55%, so the number of tradable shares is small. With trading value at zero and market capitalization of KRW 3.6 billion, price formation faces constraints. Thin operating and net profit may also limit the company's own funding capacity.

08

Overall view

MiraeN Edupartner's top-line grew in 2025 amid expanding after-school and childcare policy, but operating and net profit remain very small. Bulls point to policy expansion, proprietary textbook and content capabilities, and financial buffers such as the 43.80% equity ratio.

Bears point to a lower profit margin from a rising cost of sales ratio, an interest burden with coverage around 0.76, and trading constraints from the 88.45% largest-shareholder stake and low free float. On business indicators, the gap between revenue growth and profit recovery is the central issue.

The next checkpoint on the results side is whether the cost ratio and operating margin recover from 2026 onward. On valuation, wisereport lists a PER of 24.07 and a PBR of 0.97 based on 2025, and interpreting these figures depends heavily on how fast earnings recover. This is a qualitative summary for information purposes only.
