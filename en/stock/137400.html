Report overview

Backlog Strong, Earnings Recovery Still to Be Shown

People & Technology's earnings peaked in 2024 and declined in 2025. Sales rose for two straight quarters through 3Q 2026, but the company swung to a net loss attributable to controlling shareholders. Dry-electrode equipment and the LFP new business are the next points to verify.

02

Business structure

People & Technology is centered on secondary-battery manufacturing equipment and is now building new businesses in battery cells and cathode active materials.

The core equipment line is dry-electrode process equipment, where a brokerage described the company as one of the few domestic players holding an independent patent related to the primary roll. Its customers are global battery makers, and expansion into new regions including India has been cited.

A brokerage estimate from February 2026 put the order backlog at about KRW 1.5 trillion. In the new business, the Gumi fourth plant has a 0.2 GWh battery cell line and annual cathode active material capacity of 5,500 tons, and the company has said full-scale production begins in the second half of 2026.

The line targets LFP batteries for energy storage systems. In its 3Q 2025 disclosure, the company said costs for new businesses at its subsidiaries were reflected in results.

The company emphasizes an integrated value chain spanning equipment and materials, together with its dry-process technology, as its differentiator.

04

Earnings analysis

On an annual basis, revenue rose from KRW 417.8 billion in 2022 to KRW 545.4 billion in 2023, up 30.5% year on year, and then to KRW 1.04 trillion in 2024, up 89.8%. It fell to KRW 744.9 billion in 2025, down 28.0%. Among 2022 to 2025, 2024 was the largest year.

Operating profit was KRW 77.8 billion in 2022, KRW 76.9 billion in 2023 (down 1.1%), KRW 163.2 billion in 2024 (up 112.2%), and KRW 95.6 billion in 2025 (down 41.4%). The operating margin was 18.6%, 14.1%, 15.8% and 12.8% for 2022 through 2025, so margins did not move in step with revenue.

Net income attributable to controlling shareholders was KRW 60.2 billion in 2022, KRW 69.8 billion in 2023, KRW 142 billion in 2024 (up 103.5%), and KRW 74.3 billion in 2025 (down 47.6%). Net income including non-controlling interests, which is a separate figure, was KRW 69.5 billion in 2025.

Operating cash flow swung from a KRW -39.9 billion outflow in 2023 to KRW 39.7 billion in 2024 and KRW 4.45 billion in 2025. Quarterly revenue reached KRW 372.1 billion in 4Q 2025, up 246.0% from the prior quarter, then dropped to KRW 101.4 billion in 1Q 2026, down 72.7%.

It then rose to KRW 117.6 billion in 2Q 2026 and KRW 121.1 billion in 3Q 2026, two consecutive quarterly increases. Operating profit peaked at KRW 45.2 billion in 4Q 2025 and then fell for three consecutive quarters, to KRW 8.79 billion, KRW 3.7 billion and KRW 1.07 billion.

Net income attributable to controlling shareholders over the latest four quarters totals KRW 39.3 billion, and 3Q 2026 recorded a net loss of KRW 29.8 billion. The sources used here do not pin down the drivers of the 4Q 2025 revenue jump, so no cause is assigned.

05

Industry analysis

People & Technology's results track the secondary-battery investment cycle closely. In its 3Q 2025 disclosure, the company cited a stall in global battery growth and a slowdown in EV demand as reasons for weaker results.

By contrast, analysts see medium- to long-term growth in ESS demand, supported by expanding global power infrastructure investment and a rising share of renewables. In this setting, battery makers' capex is shifting from EV-centered to ESS-centered.

In its 1Q 2026 release, the company said an adjustment in downstream investment cycles, the seasonal off-season and geopolitical uncertainty overlapped. The company itself describes the current stage as an adjustment phase in equipment orders.

On competition, a brokerage argued that dry-electrode technology carries high entry barriers during the process transition. However, order timing may vary with the pace of dry-process adoption and customers' equipment plans.

Unlike listed peers in the same machinery sector that are oriented toward robotics or construction equipment, the company's earnings are concentrated in battery equipment demand.

06

Outlook

The company has said it will bring the LFP battery cell line and cathode active material production at the Gumi fourth plant to full scale in the second half of 2026. In its 1Q 2026 release it stressed second-half LFP battery mass production and framed the fourth-plant preparation as part of mid- to long-term growth.

In its 3Q 2025 release it said it expected results from both equipment and new businesses to become gradually visible in 2026. Brokerage estimates differ. In a February 2026 report, Mirae Asset Securities projected 2026 revenue of KRW 948.6 billion, up 27% year on year, and operating profit of KRW 123.5 billion.

Reported quarterly revenue in 1Q through 3Q 2026, however, stayed in the KRW 100 billion range each quarter. Whether the gap closes depends on whether revenue concentrates late in the year.

On orders, the company reported a backlog above KRW 1.6 trillion in November 2025, and a brokerage expected new orders to stay firm in 2026. No confirmed figures are available for specific order announcements or new customer contracts after February 2026.

Next points to verify are whether 4Q 2026 revenue moves above the KRW 100 billion range and whether new-business sales offset the decline in equipment sales.

08

Bull factors

Dry-electrode technology barrier

A brokerage described People & Technology as the only domestic company holding an independent patent related to the primary roll in dry-electrode equipment. The view is that technology barriers will stand out during the shift to dry processes. This is the brokerage's assessment, and the pace of order conversion depends on customers' equipment plans.

Order backlog scale

In its 3Q 2025 release, the company said its order backlog remained stable at more than KRW 1.6 trillion. A brokerage put the backlog at about KRW 1.5 trillion as of February 2026. A large backlog is cited as a basis for higher visibility in equipment revenue.

Revenue recovery trend

Revenue rose to KRW 117.6 billion in 2Q 2026, up 15.9% from the prior quarter, and to KRW 121.1 billion in 3Q 2026, up 3.0% quarter on quarter and up 12.6% year on year. Those are two consecutive quarterly increases following the 1Q 2026 low. Operating profit fell over the same period, so profitability recovery remains a separate task.

Bear factors

Profit decline and swing to loss

Operating profit, which was KRW 45.2 billion in 4Q 2025, kept falling over the next three quarters to KRW 1.07 billion in 3Q 2026. Net income attributable to controlling shareholders turned into a net loss of KRW 29.8 billion in 3Q 2026. Quarterly swings are large relative to the KRW 39.3 billion total over the latest four quarters.

Gap versus brokerage estimates

In a February 2026 report, Mirae Asset Securities projected 2026 revenue of KRW 948.6 billion. Reported quarterly revenue from 1Q through 3Q 2026 stayed in the KRW 100 billion range. The estimate dates from February 2026 and may have been revised since, but the reported quarterly trend sits well below it.

Customer project delays and industry conditions

In its 3Q 2025 results the company attributed weaker performance to a stall in global battery growth and delays in some customer projects. In 1Q 2026 it again cited an adjustment in downstream investment cycles and the seasonal off-season. If equipment orders slip at individual customers, revenue recognition can slip with them.

Risk factors

Customer and industry risk

If the slowdown in EV demand and the stall in battery growth persist, equipment orders could slip further. Because revenue tracks the secondary-battery investment cycle, delays at a single customer can move quarterly results sharply.

Revenue rose to KRW 372.1 billion in 4Q 2025 and fell to KRW 101.4 billion in 1Q 2026, illustrating that volatility.

New business investment and ramp-up

The LFP line and cathode active material production at the Gumi fourth plant are scheduled for full-scale production in the second half of 2026. Early costs for the new businesses were already reflected in 3Q 2025 results. If ramp-up is delayed or yields fall short, the cost burden could offset any recovery in equipment earnings.

Balance sheet and cash flow

At end-2025, total liabilities of KRW 1.02 trillion exceeded total equity of KRW 698.2 billion. The debt-to-equity ratio fell to 146.1% in 2025, but liabilities still exceed equity. Operating cash flow swung from KRW -39.9 billion in 2023 to KRW 39.7 billion in 2024 and then fell to KRW 4.45 billion in 2025. If profits keep falling and working capital absorbs cash, financial room could narrow.

12

Overall view

People & Technology combines dry-electrode equipment for secondary batteries with an LFP new business. After peaking in 2024, revenue and profit declined in 2025.

Revenue rose for two consecutive quarters through 3Q 2026, but operating profit fell for three consecutive quarters and the company swung to a net loss attributable to controlling shareholders. The company reported an order backlog above KRW 1.6 trillion, which supports the visibility of equipment revenue.

On the other side, quarterly revenue stayed in the KRW 100 billion range from 1Q to 3Q 2026, and the gap to brokerage estimates is wide, so the timing of an earnings recovery is uncertain. On the financial side, the debt-to-equity ratio fell to 146.1% in 2025, but total liabilities still exceed total equity.

The points to verify next are the LFP ramp-up at the Gumi fourth plant, the 4Q 2026 provisional results, and the composition of non-operating items in the 2026 annual report.
