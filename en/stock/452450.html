02

Business structure

PIE, founded in 2018, is an AI-based manufacturing intelligence solutions company that listed on KOSDAQ in 2025. Its core products are AI machine-vision inspection software and inspection equipment used in battery cell production lines.

Its customers are battery makers, and revenue arises when they build new lines or change inspection conditions on existing ones. A 2024 industry report said the company supplies three major Korean battery makers and overseas leading cell makers, exclusively supplying line-specific solutions.

The company says that since 2025 it has moved its focus from EV battery inspection toward inspection systems for energy storage system (ESS) batteries. The company announced ESS-related supply contracts worth roughly KRW 9.3 billion in April 2026 and roughly KRW 9.5 billion in October 2026.

A separate battery vision-system development contract worth KRW 9.5 billion runs through October 31, 2027, equal to 14.68% of 2025 consolidated sales. The company also cites non-destructive inspection and AI semiconductor and glass-substrate inspection as new growth engines.

Commercialization timing for these businesses and customer order conversion still need to be followed through company disclosures.

04

Earnings analysis

On the basis of the disclosed consolidated results, sales in 2025 were KRW 65.1 billion, down 47.5% from KRW 124 billion in 2024. Operating profit fell from KRW 9.43 billion in 2024 to an operating loss of KRW 21.2 billion in 2025, a swing into losses, and the operating margin dropped from 7.6% to -32.6%.

Net income attributable to controlling shareholders moved from KRW 10.5 billion in 2024 to a net loss attributable to controlling shareholders of KRW 22.3 billion in 2025. Net income including non-controlling interests went from KRW 9.95 billion in 2024 to a net loss of KRW 24.6 billion in 2025.

The company attributed the sales decline to lower new orders from a weaker downstream market, and the operating loss to upfront investment for medium- to long-term growth. Operating cash flow fell from KRW 15.2 billion in 2024 to -KRW 6.88 billion in 2025.

According to a press report, investing cash flow was -KRW 57.94 billion and financing cash flow was KRW 64.63 billion in the same year, showing greater reliance on external funding.

At the end of 2025, total equity was KRW 56.3 billion, of which KRW 56.2 billion belonged to controlling shareholders, and total liabilities were KRW 93.9 billion, lifting the debt-to-equity ratio from 89.2% to 166.6%.

Quarterly sales rose to KRW 16.7 billion in the fourth quarter of 2025, then fell to KRW 9.84 billion in the first quarter of 2026, the low point.

Sales then recovered to KRW 11 billion in the second quarter and KRW 13 billion in the third quarter of 2026, rising for two straight quarters, although third-quarter 2026 sales were still down 12.1% from a year earlier.

The operating loss narrowed from KRW 6.88 billion in the third quarter of 2025 to KRW 1.44 billion in the third quarter of 2026, and the company has posted operating losses for five consecutive quarters from the third quarter of 2025 through the third quarter of 2026.

The net loss attributable to controlling shareholders widened to KRW 10.2 billion in the second quarter of 2026 before narrowing to KRW 2.03 billion in the third quarter, and the four-quarter total for the last four quarters is KRW 23.1 billion.

05

Industry analysis

PIE's core customers, the secondary battery industry, are in a phase of stagnant EV demand. The company itself described a temporary slowdown in the EV market as the reason for its portfolio shift.

Industry reports say battery makers add or expand production capacity while increasing orders for inspection equipment and solutions, so supplier revenue tends to follow customers' capex cycles.

The company's order flow now leans toward ESS battery lines rather than EV lines, with North American and other major global customers at the center.

In semiconductors, a press report in early 2025 said Samsung Electronics was discussing cooperation with materials, parts and equipment suppliers for glass substrates, and later reports said large domestic and overseas companies had raised inquiries about PIE's glass-substrate inspection technology.

Whether these inquiries and cooperation talks convert into reported sales has not yet appeared in the disclosed figures. Industry reports point to PIE's exclusive supply of line-specific solutions as a competitive feature.

Outlook

A company official said on November 12, 2026 that, given the orders now in final negotiation, a return to profit in the fourth quarter is achievable.

On the same day the company disclosed preliminary separate-basis figures of KRW 24.42 billion in cumulative sales for the first three quarters of 2026, up 89.8% year on year, and a cumulative operating loss of KRW 4.33 billion.

These figures use a different basis from the consolidated disclosures and should be read separately from the reported results. The company says it is also pursuing additional ESS-related orders. The KRW 9.5 billion vision-system contract signed in October 2026 runs through October 31, 2027.

The next checkpoint is preliminary fourth-quarter 2026 results, which are expected around February 2027 given that full-year 2025 results were disclosed on February 11, 2026. The 2026 annual report is due by the end of March 2027 and will provide confirmed full-year results, cash flow and debt structure.

The first-quarter 2027 quarterly report is due by May 15, 2027. Whether the company's profit target translates into actual results will be tested in these filings.

08

Bull factors

ESS pivot and orders

The company said it signed ESS-related supply contracts worth roughly KRW 9.3 billion in April 2026 and roughly KRW 9.5 billion in October 2026, and that it is pursuing additional orders. It described ESS battery inspection equipment supply as expanding mainly with North American and other major global customers. The company presents its shift away from a slowing EV market as the basis for the 2026 sales recovery.

Narrowing quarterly losses

Sales rose for two straight quarters, from KRW 11 billion in the second quarter of 2026 to KRW 13 billion in the third, and the operating loss narrowed each quarter from KRW 6.45 billion in the fourth quarter of 2025 to KRW 1.44 billion in the third quarter of 2026.

The third-quarter 2026 operating loss was also smaller than a year earlier. The company said that if the narrowing continues, a fourth-quarter return to profit is achievable.

Margin improvement analysis

A news report said the rising share of deep-learning-based inspection software and ESS-specialized inspection systems appears to have improved margins. The report cited this as a factor behind the sharp reduction in the operating loss in the first half of 2026 compared with a year earlier. This is the report's analysis, not a cause quantified by the company.

Bear factors

Sharp 2025 earnings decline

Sales in 2025 fell 47.5% from the prior year, and the operating margin dropped to -32.6%. The company cited lower new orders from a weaker downstream market as the cause. Third-quarter 2026 sales were still 12.1% below the year-earlier level.

Cash flow and debt burden

Press reports raised concerns about share dilution from a large convertible bond issuance. The four-quarter total of net losses attributable to controlling shareholders is KRW 23.1 billion.

Wider loss in Q2 2026

The net loss attributable to controlling shareholders widened to KRW 10.2 billion in the second quarter of 2026 from KRW 2.22 billion in the first quarter. It narrowed to KRW 2.03 billion in the third quarter, but the operating-loss streak of five consecutive quarters continued. The specific drivers of the second-quarter widening remain a point to verify in company filings.

10

Risk factors

Customer and industry concentration

Revenue is tied to battery makers' capacity additions, so a cut in customer investment plans could reduce orders. The company's explanation that the 2025 sales decline came from lower new orders illustrates this linkage. Even with the pivot to ESS, dependence on specific customers and markets may remain high.

Financial burden

Total liabilities at the end of 2025 were KRW 93.9 billion, and the debt-to-equity ratio was 166.6%. Reports said the company funded investment and financing needs while operating cash flow was negative. If the convertible bond issuance proceeds, existing shareholders' stakes could be diluted.

Order continuity and new-business commercialization

Because orders depend on large supply contracts, quarterly sales can swing widely depending on contract timing. The KRW 9.5 billion vision-system contract runs through October 31, 2027.

Commercialization timing for non-destructive inspection and glass-substrate inspection, and conversion into customer orders, must be verified through company filings.

12

Overall view

PIE's earnings base weakened sharply, with 2025 sales down 47.5% and an operating loss of KRW 21.2 billion, but the 2026 quarterly trend shows two straight quarters of sales growth and a narrowing operating loss.

On the business side, the recovery is attributed mainly to rising orders for ESS battery inspection equipment amid stagnant EV demand. The company has stated a fourth-quarter 2026 return to profit is achievable, but this is a management view, not a reported result.

On the other side, operating cash flow turned negative in 2025, the debt-to-equity ratio rose to 166.6%, and press reports flagged dilution concerns from a convertible bond issuance.

The four-quarter net loss attributable to controlling shareholders totals KRW 23.1 billion, and the widening in the second quarter of 2026 should be weighed as well. Bullish and bearish factors currently carry comparable weight.

The next checkpoints are preliminary fourth-quarter 2026 results, the cash flow and debt structure in the 2026 annual report, and whether additional ESS orders are disclosed.
