02

Business structure

Apact is a semiconductor back-end company founded in 2007 by a group of SK hynix supplier firms and listed on KOSDAQ in 2014.

The company began mainly as an outsourced memory test provider, then broadened its scope in 2022 by acquiring a packaging business, creating a turnkey model that covers both packaging and testing.

As of March 2026, packaging accounted for 84% of sales and testing for 16%, while a 2024 breakdown reported back-end at 61% and testing at 39%, so the mix depends on the date and classification used. Packaging sales reached KRW 85 billion in 2025, up 62.8% year on year, and led growth.

Its main customers are integrated device manufacturers such as SK hynix and Samsung Electronics; in 2024 SK hynix accounted for 53% of sales, Samsung Electronics 32%, and other fabless customers 15%. Combined, the two largest customers exceed 80% of sales, so their outsourcing schedules flow directly into results.

Fabless customers include LX Semicon, Silicon Mitus, Jeju Semiconductor and Dongsim Semiconductor. Core offerings are DDR5 packaging, SOCAMM2 module testing and burn-in testing for automotive chips, a niche the press has described as a domestic sole specialist.

The SOCAMM2 test work is reported as a project the company won alone. The largest shareholder changed from Mutual Growth to Dynamic Growth in June 2026, and the new holder owned 52.57% as of October 30, 2026.

04

Earnings analysis

Annual revenue rose from KRW 73.6 billion in 2022 to KRW 93.7 billion in 2023, up 27.3% year on year, fell to KRW 86.7 billion in 2024, down 7.5%, and recovered to KRW 111 billion in 2025, up 28.0%. Among 2022 to 2025, 2025 was the largest year and 2022 the smallest.

Operating profit went from KRW 4.59 billion in 2022 to an operating loss of KRW 22.9 billion in 2023, a swing into loss versus the prior year, and in 2024 the operating loss of KRW 21.5 billion persisted but narrowed.

In 2025 operating profit of KRW 5.27 billion marked a return to profit, with operating margins of 6.2% in 2022, -24.4% in 2023, -24.8% in 2024 and 4.7% in 2025.

Net profit attributable to controlling shareholders moved from a KRW 16.7 billion loss in 2023 and a KRW 50.7 billion loss in 2024 to a KRW 5.23 billion profit in 2025, a turn to profit.

In its 2025 DART filing the company attributed the revenue gain to higher outsourced volumes from key customers and the operating turn to profit to cost optimization that lowered costs.

Press reports credited the packaging segment, with KRW 85 billion of sales up 62.8%, for driving growth, and lower fixed-cost burdens were cited as a factor in the profit recovery. Quarterly, Q4 2025 revenue of KRW 32.6 billion rose 14.9% from the prior quarter and operating profit of KRW 4.71 billion rose 137.1%.

Q1 2026 revenue of KRW 30.5 billion fell 6.5% from the prior quarter and the company swung to an operating loss of KRW 1.91 billion, a result of burn-in volumes ending in February, a gap as module test mass production began in April, and depreciation and hiring costs tied to capex.

Q2 2026 revenue of KRW 45.2 billion rose 48.5%, and operating profit of KRW 1.11 billion and net profit attributable to controlling shareholders of KRW 4.39 billion both turned positive.

Q3 2026 revenue of KRW 58.8 billion rose 30.1% from the prior quarter and 107.4% from a year earlier, and operating profit of KRW 5.06 billion rose 154.8% year on year, while net profit attributable to controlling shareholders of KRW 3.6 billion fell 18.1% from the prior quarter, so the two measures moved in different directions. Net profit attributable to controlling shareholders over the last four quarters totals KRW 12.3 billion.

05

Industry analysis

Demand in the downstream market is concentrated in AI servers and high-value memory. Mirae Asset Securities analyzed that as SK hynix's process shifts toward HBM, outsourced back-end volumes for general-purpose DRAM and NAND should grow.

The company also said customer orders rose during the upcycle, and the memory supply tightness is seen as supportive for back-end providers. As NVIDIA adopted SOCAMM2 in earnest, Samsung Electronics, SK hynix and Micron have entered a contest for next-generation AI memory.

Apact is reported to hold the sole test mass-production order for that module, giving it a position in this test segment. In its Q3 2026 explanation the company cited higher DDR5 packaging and SOCAMM2 test volumes as key drivers of revenue.

Samsung Electronics also added DDR5 general-purpose products to work that had been mainly NAND packaging, which has been presented as customer diversification. In domestic burn-in testing the company positions itself as the sole specialist.

06

Outlook

In an August 19, 2026 fair disclosure, the company projected 2026 revenue of KRW 211.23 billion and operating profit of KRW 14.24 billion. The same disclosure put 2026 capex at KRW 103.2 billion and said it would expand production capacity by about 1.7 times.

The company expects the sales effect to take hold from Q4 once the investment is completed in Q3.

A May 2026 report on Q1 results said capex had been raised from a planned level of about KRW 20 billion at the start of the year to about KRW 70 billion, so the two capex figures differ and the August disclosure is the latest guidance.

Given cumulative sales through Q3, reaching the annual projection requires Q4 revenue to rise well above the KRW 58.8 billion of Q3. Management attributes growth to higher DDR5 and LPDDR5 outsourcing from key customers and mass production of SOCAMM2 module testing.

A February 2026 report said construction had started on an Indian joint venture targeting mass production in 2027. The next checkpoints are whether Q4 2026 results fall within the guided range and whether volumes from the new equipment convert into quarterly revenue.

08

Bull factors

Quarterly recovery under way

Revenue rose for two consecutive quarters from Q2 to Q3 2026, and operating profit and net profit attributable to controlling shareholders stayed positive after Q2. Q3 revenue of KRW 58.8 billion was up 107.4% year on year. Operating profit of KRW 5.06 billion rose 154.8%, a faster pace than revenue growth.

SOCAMM2 test position

SOCAMM2 is a next-generation DRAM module that NVIDIA has adopted in earnest, opening a contest among major memory makers. Apact is reported to have won the test mass-production project for the module alone. Its Q3 2026 explanation also cited rising SOCAMM2 module test volumes as a revenue driver.

Capacity expansion

In its August 2026 fair disclosure the company said 2026 capex of KRW 103.2 billion would lift production capacity by about 1.7 times. It said sales expansion should begin in earnest from Q4 once the investment is complete. Higher DDR5 and LPDDR5 outsourcing from customers was cited in the same context.

09

Bear factors

Q1 2026 loss and transition gap

The Q1 2026 operating loss of KRW 1.91 billion and net loss attributable to controlling shareholders of KRW 3.27 billion followed burn-in volume declines and a gap during the module ramp. Depreciation and hiring costs tied to capex were also booked in Q1. Profits in Q2 and Q3 have followed, but the Q1 loss shows how volatile quarterly results can be.

Customer concentration

In 2024 SK hynix accounted for 53% of sales and Samsung Electronics for 32%, so the two together exceed 80%. A change in either customer's outsourcing schedule could move revenue sharply. Whether new products broaden the customer base will show up in later quarterly results.

Capex burden and balance sheet

The KRW 103.2 billion capex planned for 2026 is very large compared with operating cash flow of KRW 5.64 billion in 2025. The debt-to-equity ratio fell from 194.2% in 2024 to 159.1% in 2025 but remains high. The funding mix will determine how much financial strain and dilution follow.

Risk factors

Ownership change

The largest shareholder changed in June 2026, and the new holder owned 52.57% as of October 30, 2026. An extraordinary shareholders meeting was convened in July 2026 and an independent director filing followed in August 2026. Changes to management direction and investment plans after the handover will appear in future filings.

Dilution risk

Bonds with warrants worth KRW 23.4 billion were issued in November 2025, and a convertible bond issuance decision was disclosed in June 2026. Several conversion-right exercise notices appeared between July and September 2026. Additional conversion shares would dilute per-share measures.

Cycle and outsourcing dependence

Back-end revenue is tied directly to the memory cycle and to key customers' outsourcing volumes. Higher outsourced volumes from key customers were a major factor behind the 2025 improvement.

If the memory cycle turns, utilization and operating margins could move quickly, and operating margins were negative in 2023 and 2024.

12

Overall view

Apact has shown a recovery since turning to operating profit in 2025, with revenue and profit both rising in Q2 and Q3 2026. Q3 2026 revenue of KRW 58.8 billion and operating profit of KRW 5.06 billion were both sharply higher than a year earlier.

On the other side, the Q1 2026 operating loss of KRW 1.91 billion, the cost burden of the capex push, and dependence on two customers remain variables. Reaching the annual projection published in August 2026 requires Q4 revenue to rise well above Q3.

The bullish factors are the SOCAMM2 test position, the capacity expansion and rising customer outsourcing, while the bearish factors are the loss-making quarter, the ownership change and possible dilution from convertible instruments.

Valuation reflects a small earnings base, with the last four quarters totaling KRW 12.3 billion of net profit attributable to controlling shareholders. The next checkpoints are the preliminary full-year 2026 results in early 2027 and the 2026 annual report.
