02

Business structure

Ilji Technology was founded in 1986 to manufacture and sell automotive parts and has been listed on KOSDAQ since 1992. Its core products are body panels, including dash and side reinforcement assemblies, covering dashboard, hood, door, side and floor panels.

Hyundai Motor is identified as its main customer, and Research Alarm said the company has directly taken part in developing body structure parts for models such as Genesis, IONIQ and Tucson. Research Alarm also judged that the company runs its own press, welding and assembly processes.

Research Alarm's 2025 estimate put the sales mix at 64% United States, 29% Korea and 6% China, with a North America-centered structure following the 2023 acquisition of a US subsidiary.

On technology, the company has upgraded hot stamping, dissimilar material joining and clad material processes and secured related patents. The business is widening into eco-friendly vehicle parts, with patents registered for battery module cases and battery cooling devices.

Research Alarm linked its lightweight electrical component technology to a possible extension into humanoid robot parts. Sungwoo Hitech and Myoung Shin Industry are cited as the main peers.

Because Hyundai Motor is presented as the single main customer, Hyundai Motor Group production plans flow directly into the company's results.

04

Earnings analysis

Annual sales rose from KRW 229.1 billion in 2022 to KRW 628.5 billion in 2023 (up 174.3% year on year), KRW 757.3 billion in 2024 (up 20.5%) and KRW 855.1 billion in 2025 (up 12.9%).

Sales have increased for three consecutive years from 2023 through 2025, with 2025 the largest year and 2022 the smallest in the 2022 to 2025 period. Operating profit jumped from KRW 616 million in 2022 to KRW 35.4 billion in 2023 (up 5,645.7%), a steep percentage gain that reflects a very low 2022 base.

Operating profit then fell 31.0% to KRW 24.4 billion in 2024 before rising 96.4% to KRW 48 billion in 2025, the largest year in the period. The operating margin moved from 0.3% in 2022 to 5.6% in 2023, 3.2% in 2024 and 5.6% in 2025, so 2025 matched the 2023 level.

Net profit attributable to controlling shareholders swung from a net loss of KRW 9.09 billion in 2022 to net income of KRW 32.3 billion in 2023, a turn to profit, then KRW 22.5 billion in 2024 (down 30.3%) and KRW 45.7 billion in 2025 (up 102.9%).

Net income including non-controlling interests is disclosed at the same amounts in each year. The company attributed the 2025 improvement to higher production at customers and currency effects.

Quarterly sales peaked at KRW 230 billion in the fourth quarter of 2025 (up 13.2% from the prior quarter), then fell to KRW 222.2 billion in the first quarter of 2026, KRW 207 billion in the second and KRW 190.8 billion in the third, three straight quarterly declines.

Quarterly operating profit fell from KRW 21.9 billion in the fourth quarter of 2025 to KRW 13.7 billion in the first quarter of 2026 and KRW 3.48 billion in the second, then rose 107.3% to KRW 7.21 billion in the third quarter, still 6.8% lower than a year earlier.

Net profit attributable to controlling shareholders over the last four quarters totaled KRW 47.5 billion. Operating cash flow was KRW 76.2 billion in 2022, KRW 46.5 billion in 2023, KRW 53.6 billion in 2024 and KRW 93.5 billion in 2025.

05

Industry analysis

Research Alarm cited a market research estimate that the global automotive lightweight materials market will grow from USD 78.89 billion in 2025 to USD 104.98 billion in 2030, a compound annual growth rate of 5.9%.

The same report expected the body segment, Ilji Technology's core business, to grow 6.1% a year, above the market average. Research Alarm's argument is that electrification and a rising share of SUVs and eco-friendly vehicles are lifting demand for larger, lighter body parts, which is positive for unit prices.

Auto parts makers are, however, sensitive to vehicle output and exchange rates, so the currency effect the company cited for 2025 could reverse and weigh on profit.

Among peers, Sungwoo Hitech and Myoung Shin Industry are the main comparisons, and the company's North America-centered sales make it exposed to US vehicle demand.

Research Alarm noted that Hyundai Motor Group plans to begin mass production of the 'Atlas' humanoid robot in 2028 and said the company's custom design and production capability warrants attention as a possible supplier.

That robot opportunity remains a possibility identified in the report, and the company's disclosures do not split out results by business segment.

On the cycle, quarterly sales peaked in the fourth quarter of 2025 and have fallen for three quarters of 2026, so whether body parts demand turns into a recovery will determine how this shows up in results.

Bull factors

Recovery in earnings power

Operating profit rose from KRW 616 million in 2022 to KRW 48 billion in 2025, and the operating margin climbed from 3.2% in 2024 to 5.6%. Operating cash flow increased from KRW 76.2 billion in 2022 to KRW 93.5 billion in 2025. The debt ratio fell from 309.3% in 2022 to 237.0% at the end of 2025.

North American sales base and new orders

Research Alarm estimated that 64% of 2025 sales were generated in the United States, a North America-centered mix since the 2023 US acquisition. The firm pointed to new vehicle orders at the US subsidiary as a growth driver.

It also expected existing body parts supply to increase with eco-friendly vehicle production at key customers in the US.

Expansion into electrification and lightweight parts

Patents for battery module cases and battery cooling devices extend the company into eco-friendly vehicle parts. Research Alarm judged its lightweight processing technology could extend into humanoid robot parts.

Hyundai Motor Group's plan to begin mass production of the 'Atlas' humanoid robot in 2028 is cited as a possible route to participation.

Bear factors

Three straight quarters of lower sales

Sales peaked at KRW 230 billion in the fourth quarter of 2025 and then fell to KRW 222.2 billion, KRW 207 billion and KRW 190.8 billion in the first three quarters of 2026. Third-quarter sales were down 6.0% year on year.

Net profit attributable to controlling shareholders of KRW 4.77 billion was 7.5% lower than a year earlier, so both sales and profit are declining.

Customer concentration and reliance on external factors

Hyundai Motor is presented as the main customer, so changes in Hyundai Motor Group output pass directly into sales. The company credited higher customer production and currency effects for the 2025 increase. Because both drivers of the profit gain come from outside the company's control, this is a weakness.

High debt load and volatile earnings

Total liabilities at the end of 2025 were KRW 454.6 billion, above total equity of KRW 191.8 billion. Operating profit has swung sharply, including a 31.0% decline in 2024.

Net profit attributable to controlling shareholders fell 90.8% from KRW 17.1 billion in the first quarter of 2026 to KRW 1.56 billion in the second.

Risk factors

Customer concentration risk

With Hyundai Motor as the main customer, changes in vehicle production plans flow directly into results. Growth in SUVs and eco-friendly vehicles at Hyundai Motor Group supports demand for lightweight body parts, but volume gaps can appear during model changeovers. The more reliant the company is on specific models, the larger the impact of such gaps.

Currency and North American exposure

Research Alarm estimated that 64% of 2025 sales came from the United States, so the dollar exchange rate and changes in US policy could affect profitability. The company also cited currency effects in its 2025 improvement. If the exchange rate moves the other way, the profit contribution from the same sales would shrink.

Financial structure and quarterly profit swings

The debt ratio was 237.0% at the end of 2025, and total liabilities exceed twice total equity. Under this structure, financial flexibility could be constrained when earnings decline.

Quarterly net profit attributable to controlling shareholders also swung sharply, from KRW 24.2 billion in the fourth quarter of 2025 to KRW 1.56 billion in the second quarter of 2026.

Overall view

Ilji Technology moved from a 2022 net loss to operating profit of KRW 48 billion and net income attributable to controlling shareholders of KRW 45.7 billion in 2025, building up earnings power as an automotive body parts maker.

Sales have risen for three consecutive years from 2023 through 2025, and operating cash flow was KRW 93.5 billion in 2025 compared with KRW 76.2 billion in 2022. However, sales fell in each of the three quarters of 2026, and third-quarter operating profit was 6.8% lower than a year earlier.

Growth factors include the North American sales mix and expansion into electrification and lightweight parts, while weaknesses include dependence on Hyundai Motor, external variables such as currency and production volume, and a high debt ratio.

On valuation, the price-to-book ratio sits well below book value per share, yet the earnings base has changed quickly, which widens the range of interpretation.

Research Alarm's 2026 operating profit forecast of KRW 60.6 billion assumes a sharp fourth-quarter recovery, so the 2026 annual results expected in early 2027 are the key checkpoint.
