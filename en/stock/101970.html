02

Business structure

Wooyang HC is a plant equipment maker founded in 1996 that designs and manufactures equipment for petrochemical, refining and LNG plants. The chemical plant segment accounts for the overwhelming share of revenue.

According to a domestic brokerage report, the mix in 1Q 2026 was 94.9% chemical plant, 0.88% energy plant and 4.25% other.

Its core products are process equipment built on experience with offshore plant systems (FPSO, FLNG and FPU), and it has recently expanded into carbon capture, utilization and storage (CCUS), energy-saving equipment and wastewater treatment systems.

Its main customers are large domestic and overseas EPC (engineering, procurement and construction) companies. After winning a project, the company designs to customer specifications and manufactures in its own facilities.

In energy, it was registered as a qualified supplier to the Korean state nuclear power operator in 2024, laying groundwork for nuclear plant and small modular reactor (SMR) markets. According to the same report, its largest shareholder is Solbrain Holdings with 38.7%, and a second major shareholder holds 18.2%.

Competitively, it supplies equipment to large EPC firms such as Samsung E&A rather than performing construction itself, which gives it a different business structure from construction contractors.

In January 2025, an industry source noted that the United States is its largest export market and that changes in U.S. energy policy affect its results.

04

Earnings analysis

Revenue rose from KRW 120.7 billion in 2022 to KRW 200.1 billion in 2023, up 65.8% year on year, and then declined 8.4% to KRW 183.2 billion in 2024 and 45.0% to KRW 100.9 billion in 2025, a two-year decline running from 2024 to 2025.

Operating profit went from KRW 5.23 billion in 2022 to KRW 25.3 billion in 2023 (up 383.5%) and KRW 31.1 billion in 2024 (up 23.0%), making 2024 the largest year in the 2022 to 2025 window.

Operating profit rose in 2024 even as revenue fell, and a Korean financial daily attributed this to cost of sales falling by more than revenue (reported May 2025). Operating profit in 2025 was KRW 706 million, down 97.7%, the smallest year in the window.

Operating margin was 4.3% in 2022, 12.6% in 2023, 17.0% in 2024 and 0.7% in 2025. Net profit attributable to controlling shareholders was KRW 2.14 billion in 2022, KRW 21.8 billion in 2023 and KRW 27.4 billion in 2024, then swung to a net loss of KRW 4.31 billion in 2025.

Total net profit including non-controlling interests was also a net loss of KRW 4.31 billion in 2025. By quarter, the company posted an operating loss of KRW 1.25 billion in 3Q 2025 and KRW 3.91 billion in 4Q 2025, before reaching operating profit of KRW 3.01 billion in 1Q 2026.

Operating profit was KRW 2.01 billion in 2Q 2026, down 33.1% from the prior quarter, and KRW 2.57 billion in 3Q 2026, up 27.7% from the prior quarter. However, the net loss attributable to controlling shareholders in 3Q 2026 was KRW 2.56 billion, so operating profit and bottom-line results diverged.

Net profit attributable to controlling shareholders over the last four quarters totaled KRW 2.34 billion. Operating cash flow was KRW 25.6 billion in 2022, KRW 1.65 billion in 2023, KRW 44.3 billion in 2024 and KRW 22.9 billion in 2025.

At the end of 2025, total equity was KRW 184.3 billion against total liabilities of KRW 66.4 billion, and the debt ratio fell from 105.6% in 2022 to 36.0% in 2025.

05

Industry analysis

Wooyang HC operates in the petrochemical, refining, LNG and power plant equipment market, where revenue moves sharply with the timing of orders.

A 2025 analysis of cumulative 3Q 2025 results said demand swings and delayed orders in the chemical plant market reduced sales of its core products, while order delays in the energy plant segment hurt results.

On the industry outlook, a domestic brokerage report from June 2026 pointed to expected growth in offshore and LNG plant investment driven by rising global energy demand, plus potential power plant investment and entry into the SMR market as key checkpoints.

The same report said the company expects new awards in several LNG plant projects in 2026, centered on North America. However, the energy plant segment was only 0.88% of revenue in 1Q 2026, so the energy transition business is still at an early stage with a small revenue contribution.

Competitively, the company is a supplier to large EPC contractors, so when projects cluster revenue can rise quickly, and when orders are delayed quarterly sales can swing sharply. Large construction companies in the same sector typically move with their construction awards, which is a different structure.

As the company expands into CCUS and wastewater treatment, a key point to watch is whether demand shifts from petrochemical investment cycles toward energy transition projects.

The same report said the company expects new awards in several LNG plant projects in 2026, centered on North America. In energy, the company has been preparing for entry into the SMR market after its 2024 registration as a qualified supplier to the Korean state nuclear power operator.

At the annual general meeting held on March 20, 2026, the separate financial statements for the 30th fiscal period were approved, and the auditor issued a clean opinion. Quarterly revenue moved unevenly, at KRW 31.2 billion in 1Q 2026, KRW 19.8 billion in 2Q and KRW 25.6 billion in 3Q.

The same brokerage report cited delays in revenue recovery from U.S. tariff effects and continued Middle East instability as risk factors. Risk factors

Order and timing volatility

Revenue moves with EPC customers' order schedules, so quarterly variation is large. The sharp 2025 decline also stemmed from delayed orders and timing adjustments. If one large project slips, quarterly results can swing widely.

U.S. tariffs and Middle East geopolitics

The United States is reported to be the company's largest export market, so U.S. tariffs and energy policy changes directly affect results. A domestic brokerage report cited delays in revenue recovery from U.S. tariff effects as a risk. Continued instability in the Middle East could also affect overseas project timing.

Accounting and governance history

An application for listing through a SPAC merger submitted in April 2024 was withdrawn in September 2024 due to audit issues. After strengthening internal audit systems and reapplying, the company completed its listing in March 2025.

Press reports said the share price fell sharply after a steep drop in 1Q 2025 results, so accounting reliability and earnings predictability remain items to monitor.
