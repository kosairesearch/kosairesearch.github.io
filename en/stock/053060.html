02

Business structure

SAE Dong manufactures and sells automotive parts, focusing on door belts, extruded parts, injection-molded parts and moldings. A July 2023 technical analysis report by NICE D&B identified GLASS protection door belts, general injection-molded door protection parts and moldings as the main drivers of total revenue.

The same report described two business segments, domestic and overseas operations, and listed domestic product lines including door belts, variable extrusions, general extrusions, general injection molding, gas injection molding and moldings.

The technical base is extrusion and injection processing, and the report framed lightweight body-part technology as the basis for competitiveness in the eco-friendly vehicle era. A November 4, 2026 news report described the company as a supplier of exterior and interior moldings and plastic parts for automakers.

Door belts and moldings move with vehicle production volumes, so customers' production plans feed directly into revenue. Peers include Hyundai Mobis, Sungwoo Hitech, Myoung Shin Industry, Seoyon E-Hwa, DN Automotive, SL and PIM Korea, which together form the competitive field for body and trim parts.

The most distinct feature of the business is a low profit margin relative to its revenue growth. In a October 6, 2026 news report, SAE Dong was cited among stocks that rose on the electric-vehicle parts theme.

04

Earnings analysis

Annual results show revenue and profit moving in opposite directions.

Revenue went from KRW 156.1 billion in 2022 to KRW 169.9 billion in 2023 (up 8.9% year on year), KRW 171.1 billion in 2024 (up 0.7%) and KRW 189.4 billion in 2025 (up 10.7%), rising for three straight years from 2023 through 2025, with 2025 the largest year of 2022–2025.

Operating profit peaked at KRW 12.6 billion in 2023 (up 0.9%), then fell to KRW 10.7 billion in 2024 (down 14.7%) and KRW 9.1 billion in 2025 (down 15.0%), declining for two straight years from 2024 to 2025.

The operating margin fell every year, from 8.0% in 2022 to 7.4%, 6.3% and 4.8% in 2025, and a Hankyung financial summary shows the gross margin trending lower after 2023.

Net income attributable to controlling shareholders was KRW 12.4 billion in 2022, KRW 9.8 billion in 2023 (down 20.8%), a net loss of KRW 1.15 billion in 2024 after turning from profit to loss, and KRW 5.88 billion in 2025 after turning back to profit, and total net income including non-controlling interests matched these figures in each year.

In 2024, non-operating expenses rose sharply and pushed pre-tax earnings down, which appears to be the main driver of the swing to a loss. Quarterly results show the same divergence.

Revenue hit KRW 56.1 billion in the fourth quarter of 2025 (up 24.3% from the prior quarter), which Thinkpool reported as the highest in three years, before easing to KRW 51.7 billion in the third quarter of 2026 (down 4.0% from the prior quarter).

Operating profit fell to KRW 1.51 billion in the fourth quarter of 2025, recovered to KRW 3.09 billion in the first quarter of 2026 (up 105.2%), then turned into an operating loss of KRW 17.94 million in the second quarter and KRW 321 million in the third.

In the first quarter of 2026, net income attributable to controlling shareholders of KRW 4.32 billion exceeded operating profit, suggesting non-operating items affected the bottom line.

Operating cash flow swung from KRW 19.9 billion in 2022 and KRW 13.9 billion in 2023 to KRW -1.46 billion in 2024 and KRW 21.4 billion in 2025. The debt ratio rose to 233.5% at the end of 2025 from 209.5% in 2024.

An October 12, 2026 report said several parts stocks, including SAE Dong, fell on concern about slowing automaker demand. A November 4, 2026 report said Hyundai Motor Group's expansion of overseas production bases could create more supply opportunities for domestic parts suppliers.

A November 16, 2026 report placed SAE Dong among stocks trading flat with a wait-and-see tone, while many traditional combustion-engine and body-part suppliers faced selling pressure from short-term profit-taking.

The sector broadly cites the expansion of electrification and autonomous driving as a shared growth driver, but analysts argue results will differ by company depending on actual revenue contribution and overseas customer wins.

SAE Dong's body and molding technology puts it alongside suppliers with materials and precision processing capabilities, including Sungwoo Hitech, Myoung Shin Industry and PIM Korea. Its position relative to peers is distinguished mainly by a low operating margin despite revenue growth.

Profit recovery in the first quarter of 2026

Whether the rebound is one-off or sustained can be judged from later quarters. Whether profit returns in the fourth quarter is the next point to watch.

Improving financial structure

Net income attributable to controlling shareholders of KRW 5.88 billion in 2025 reversed the 2024 loss. Risk factors

Door belts and moldings move with automaker production volumes. A cut in production plans or the end of production for a specific model would affect revenue directly. In October 2026, concern over weaker demand was cited as a reason for declines across parts stocks. Financial burden

Total liabilities at the end of 2025 stood at KRW 94.1 billion, above total equity of KRW 40.3 billion. The debt ratio rose again to 233.5% in 2025 from 209.5% in 2024. Operating cash flow was negative at KRW -1.46 billion in 2024. If losses continue, borrowing costs and repayment pressure could increase.

Delayed profitability recovery

After a profitable first quarter of 2026, operating losses returned in the second and third quarters, so the quarterly trend has not stabilized. If non-operating items drive reported profit, it becomes harder to judge whether core operations are recovering.

Quarterly revenue has stayed in the KRW 50 billion range since the fourth quarter of 2025. Without a margin recovery, revenue growth will not translate into higher profit.
