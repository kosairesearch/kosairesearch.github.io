02

Business structure

Sae Dong was founded in 1986 and listed on KOSDAQ in 2001 as a specialized manufacturer of exterior automotive parts. Its core products include metal extrusions, general extrusions, variable extrusions, general injection-molded parts, and gas-injection parts, with door belts and molding as key product groups.

Sae Dong develops and manufactures these products for supply to Hyundai and Kia, Korea's global automakers.

To respond to tightening environmental regulations and the spread of electrified vehicles, the company is focusing on next-generation lightweight and eco-friendly technologies such as ETFE coating, PVD-deposited door belts, aluminum roll forming, and triple extrusion.

Its production facility is located in Jangan-eup, Gijang-gun, Busan, with roughly 212 employees as of the end of June 2026. The customer structure reportedly covers all three major domestic automakers, meaning most revenue is tied to OEM order volumes.

In the competitive landscape, over 100 listed auto parts makers operate in Korea, and Sae Dong occupies a niche as a small-to-mid-sized Tier-1 supplier specializing in extrusion and injection molding technology. Its paid-in capital stands at KRW 8.82 billion, placing it in the small-cap category.

04

Earnings analysis

Sae Dong's revenue grew for four consecutive years, from KRW 156.1 billion in 2022 to KRW 169.9 billion in 2023, KRW 171.1 billion in 2024, and KRW 189.4 billion in 2025.

However, operating profit stagnated at KRW 12.4 billion in 2022 and KRW 12.6 billion in 2023, declined to KRW 10.7 billion in 2024, and fell further to KRW 9.1 billion in 2025, with operating margin narrowing from 8.0% in 2022 to 4.8% in 2025.

Net profit was volatile: KRW 12.4 billion in 2022 and KRW 9.8 billion in 2023, then swinging to a net loss of KRW 1.2 billion in 2024 before recovering to a net profit of KRW 5.9 billion in 2025.

On a quarterly basis, net profit reached KRW 3.2 billion in Q3 2025 but swung to a net loss of KRW 2.1 billion in Q4 2025, showing significant quarter-to-quarter volatility, before improving with revenue of KRW 52.7 billion, operating profit of KRW 3.1 billion, and net profit of KRW 4.3 billion in Q1 2026.

In Q2 2026, however, revenue rose modestly to KRW 53.8 billion, yet operating profit reverted to a loss of KRW 0.02 billion and net profit to a loss of KRW 0.16 billion.

This suggests cost pressures or one-off items weighed on profitability despite revenue growth, and net profit attributable to owners over the trailing four quarters (Q3 2025-Q2 2026) totaled roughly KRW 5.3 billion.

On the cash flow side, 2025 operating cash flow of KRW 21.4 billion significantly exceeded net profit, indicating stronger underlying cash generation than the income statement alone suggests. By contrast, 2024 operating cash flow was negative at KRW -1.5 billion, deteriorating alongside the net loss that year.

05

Industry analysis

The South Korean auto industry maintains a top-five global production position, with nine Korean parts makers ranking among the world's top 100 auto parts suppliers by revenue in 2024.

Korea's auto parts exports reached USD 35 billion in 2024, ranking sixth globally, and the auto industry accounts for 13.2% of total exports, underscoring its significance to the national economy.

The industry cycle is often framed as a chain running from OEM sales to production volume, utilization rates, parts order volume, fixed cost burden, and ultimately operating margin, meaning smaller exterior parts suppliers like Sae Dong have profitability tightly linked to automaker production schedules.

Upstream, the industry is undergoing a paradigm shift toward electrification, autonomous driving, and mobility services, with domestic suppliers seeking growth by integrating with adjacent sectors such as batteries and IT.

Competitors including Sungwoo Hitech and Hurim ATEC compete for Tier-1 status with Hyundai and Kia across exterior and interior parts, while Sae Dong operates in a niche built on extrusion and injection molding technology.

Fluctuations in aluminum raw material prices and the industry-wide push toward lightweight, eco-friendly materials driven by tightening environmental regulations remain common challenges across the sector.

Outlook

Sae Dong has not disclosed specific public revenue or profit guidance, meaning its future earnings path will largely depend on automaker production plans and order volumes.

The company continues developing lightweight and eco-friendly technologies such as ETFE coating, PVD-deposited door belts, aluminum roll forming, and triple extrusion, signaling efforts to address parts conversion demand driven by the spread of electrified vehicles.

The improvement seen in Q1 2026, with revenue up 26% and operating profit up 49% year-on-year, did not carry through to Q2, which reverted to an operating loss.

The company stated it would respond according to legal procedures regarding the September 2026 appeal over the extraordinary general meeting request, and the outcome of this litigation remains a variable that could affect future board composition and management direction.

The Q3 2026 quarterly report is due to be filed by mid-November 2026 under statutory deadlines, and will serve as the next data point to assess whether the Q2 loss was a temporary factor or a more sustained slowdown.

Raw material price movements and shifts in automaker production plans are cited as key external variables that could affect results for the remainder of 2026.

Response to Eco-Friendly, Lightweight Technology Trends

Sae Dong is focusing on next-generation lightweight and eco-friendly technologies including ETFE coating, PVD-deposited door belts, aluminum roll forming, and triple extrusion. This reflects efforts to respond to the industry shift driven by tightening environmental regulations and the spread of electrified vehicles.

Governance Dispute Litigation Risk

Depending on the litigation outcome, uncertainty around board composition and management decision-making could persist.

Elevated Debt Ratio and Small-Cap Characteristics
