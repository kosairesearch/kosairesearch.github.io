02

Business structure

Founded in 1986 and listed on KOSDAQ in 2001, SAE Dong is a specialized manufacturer of automotive exterior parts headquartered in Gijang, Busan.

Its core products are glass-protection door belt moldings and door frame moldings, produced using metal extrusion, general extrusion, variable extrusion, general injection molding, and gas injection molding technologies.

Its main customers are Hyundai Motor and KIA, and it is also certified as a Tier 1 supplier to Hyundai Mobis, GM Korea, and KG Mobility.

To support overseas expansion, the company operates a U.S. subsidiary, Saedong America, Inc., along with local entities in Brazil and China, building a supply chain aligned with its global OEM customers' local production bases.

SAE Dong previously established a logistics base in Brazil to supply Hyundai Motor's Brazilian operations, aimed at reducing logistics costs and improving local defect response. In terms of ownership, CEO Yoon Jeong-sang and related parties hold a 27.34% stake.

Competitively, SAE Dong is a small-to-mid-sized exterior parts maker specialized in a niche segment of door moldings, distinct from large module or electrification parts suppliers. Its earnings are directly tied to the production volumes and new-model cycles of its OEM customers.

04

Earnings analysis

SAE Dong's annual revenue rose for three consecutive years, from KRW 156.1 billion in 2022 to KRW 169.9 billion in 2023 (up 8.9%), KRW 171.1 billion in 2024 (up 0.7%), and KRW 189.4 billion in 2025 (up 10.7%), making 2025 the largest year among 2022-2025.

Operating profit, however, edged up from KRW 12.4 billion in 2022 to KRW 12.6 billion in 2023 before peaking, then fell for two straight years to KRW 10.7 billion in 2024 (down 14.7%) and KRW 9.1 billion in 2025 (down 15.0%), making 2025 the smallest year in the period.

The operating margin declined every year, from 8.0% in 2022 to 7.4% in 2023, 6.3% in 2024, and 4.8% in 2025, showing profitability eroding despite rising sales.

Net income attributable to controlling shareholders fell to KRW 9.8 billion in 2023 (down 20.8%), turned into a loss of KRW 1.15 billion in 2024, then swung back to a profit of KRW 5.88 billion in 2025; the company has previously attributed weaker 2024 results to one-off factors including a disposal loss from selling a joint-venture stake and foreign-exchange losses.

On a quarterly basis, revenue moved from KRW 46.2 billion in Q2 2025 to KRW 45.2 billion in Q3 2025 (down 2.2%), KRW 56.1 billion in Q4 2025 (up 24.3%), KRW 52.7 billion in Q1 2026 (down 6.2%), and KRW 53.8 billion in Q2 2026 (up 2.2% quarter-on-quarter and 16.5% year-on-year).

Operating profit declined from KRW 3.09 billion in Q2 2025 to KRW 2.43 billion in Q3 2025 (down 21.2%) and KRW 1.51 billion in Q4 2025 (down 38.1%), rebounded to KRW 3.09 billion in Q1 2026 (up 105.2%), then swung to an operating loss of KRW 17.94 million in Q2 2026, turning negative both quarter-on-quarter and year-on-year.

Net income attributable to controlling shareholders also alternated between profit and loss across quarters, from a loss of KRW 2.08 billion in Q4 2025 to a profit of KRW 4.32 billion in Q1 2026 and a loss of KRW 165 million in Q2 2026, with the trailing four quarters (Q3 2025-Q2 2026) summing to KRW 5.28 billion.

On the balance sheet, the debt-to-equity ratio fell from 285.0% in 2022 to 209.5% in 2024 before rising again to 233.5% in 2025, while operating cash flow improved markedly from negative KRW 1.46 billion in 2024 to KRW 21.4 billion in 2025.

Industry analysis

South Korea's auto industry saw uneven results in the first half of 2026, as Hyundai Motor's sales fell 4.9% year-on-year partly due to disruptions from a parts-supplier fire, while KIA posted its highest-ever first-half sales on strong hybrid and EV demand.

An industry outlook report projected 2026 domestic auto production at roughly 4.13 million units, a slight increase from the prior year, driven by the launch of eco-friendly-dedicated lines including Hyundai's new Ulsan plant and KIA's Gwangmyeong and Hwaseong EVO plants.

On the external front, the reduction of U.S. auto and auto-parts tariffs from 25% to 15% under the Korea-U.S. agreement has been cited as easing some export-related uncertainty.

However, auto parts exports in August 2026 declined year-on-year, partly reflecting fewer working days during the summer holiday period, underscoring notable month-to-month volatility.

Smaller exterior-parts makers like SAE Dong tend to be relatively sensitive to shifts in OEM production volumes as well as raw material and currency trends.

Notably, SAE Dong was once classified as having among the highest operating margins relative to peers in Q1 2022, but by Q4 2025 it ranked among the lowest in operating margin relative to peers, reflecting a shift in its relative profitability position within the sector.

Even as electrification proceeds, exterior parts such as door moldings remain commonly required across both internal-combustion and electric vehicles, providing a structural base of demand.

Outlook

New-model effects at its OEM customers in the second half of 2026 are likely to be a key variable. Hyundai Motor plans to follow up the new Grandeur with the launch of the all-new Avante, and one brokerage analysis suggested that the full-model-change Avante and Tucson launches would boost hybrid vehicle volumes.

KIA's continued strength in hybrid and EV sales could also support order volumes for exterior-parts partners such as SAE Dong.

Domestically, the ramp-up of Hyundai's new Ulsan plant and KIA's Gwangmyeong and Hwaseong EVO plants is expected to expand eco-friendly vehicle production capacity, which could broaden the demand base for exterior parts such as door moldings.

However, as shown by the swing to an operating loss in Q2 2026, periods in which raw material and labor cost burdens outpace revenue growth have recurred, so whether top-line expansion translates into a profitability recovery will need to be confirmed in coming quarterly results.

The operating status and earnings contribution of its overseas units in the U.S., Brazil, and China also remain items to monitor through future disclosures.

The company has not issued formal order guidance, and its annual report notes that order dates and delivery schedules are difficult to specify given the on-demand nature of its order intake, suggesting quarterly volatility could persist for some time.

Bull factors

Revenue Growth and Return to Profit

2025 revenue of KRW 189.4 billion was the largest among 2022-2025, and controlling-shareholder net income swung from a loss in 2024 to a profit of KRW 5.88 billion in 2025. Q1 2026 also showed revenue of KRW 52.7 billion and controlling-shareholder net income of KRW 4.32 billion, extending the earnings recovery. Alignment with OEM customers' new-model launch schedules could support continued top-line expansion.

Diversified Customer Base via Overseas Units

Through its Saedong America subsidiary and local entities in Brazil and China, SAE Dong has built a supply chain aligned with Hyundai Motor and KIA's overseas production bases. This could partly diversify revenue exposure to fluctuations in domestic OEM production.

However, the specific revenue contribution of each overseas unit is not separately disclosed in periodic reports.

Improved Cash Flow

Operating cash flow improved markedly from negative KRW 1.46 billion in 2024 to KRW 21.4 billion in 2025. This suggests the one-off loss factors seen in 2024 eased relatively in 2025. A more stable cash flow profile could also support management of the debt-to-equity ratio.

Bear factors

Structural Decline in Operating Margin

The operating margin declined every year from 8.0% in 2022 to 4.8% in 2025, with margins failing to improve even in years of revenue growth. As of Q4 2025, the company ranked among the lowest in operating margin relative to peers, indicating weakened relative profitability within the sector. Without an improvement in the cost structure, revenue growth may not sufficiently translate into higher profit.

Recurring Quarterly Volatility

Over the last five quarters, operating profit and controlling-shareholder net income alternated between profit and loss, with both an operating loss and a net loss occurring simultaneously in Q2 2026. This volatility makes it difficult to draw firm trend conclusions from any single quarter. Whether earnings stability returns will need to be confirmed in upcoming quarterly results.

Renewed Rise in Debt Ratio

The debt-to-equity ratio fell to 209.5% in 2024 before rising again to 233.5% in 2025. With the ratio holding in the mid-200% range, a decline in operating profit could relatively narrow the company's capacity to absorb interest expense. The level of financial leverage is a factor to watch alongside future earnings trends.

Risk factors

Customer Concentration

SAE Dong's revenue is concentrated among a small number of OEM groups, mainly Hyundai Motor and KIA, so changes in their production plans or demand could directly affect results. As seen in Hyundai's sales decline in H1 2026, supply-chain variables such as a parts-supplier fire can also affect OEM production. The pace of customer diversification will need to be tracked through future disclosures.

Raw Material and Currency Fluctuation

Fluctuations in the prices of steel and chemical materials used in metal extrusion and injection molding can directly affect costs. Given its overseas operations, foreign-exchange gains and losses tied to emerging-market currencies such as the Brazilian real have previously affected results. These factors have contributed to quarter-to-quarter earnings volatility.

Financial Leverage

Total liabilities stood at KRW 94.1 billion at the end of 2025, well above total equity of KRW 40.3 billion. If the debt ratio rises further during a period of declining operating profit, interest expense burdens could increase. Future cash flow and debt repayment capacity will need to be monitored together.
