Report overview

Digital Twin Pivot, Earnings Still in the Red

EGIS, the only Korean company with a proprietary Digital Earth platform, is shifting its business model from public-sector build contracts toward subscription SaaS and physical AI, but its 2025 consolidated operating and net income both swung into losses.

  1. 1

    2025 revenue fell to KRW 21.25 billion from KRW 30.28 billion in 2024, and the company swung to an operating loss of KRW 6.80 billion from an operating profit of KRW 2.39 billion a year earlier.

  2. 2

    Both the first and second quarters of 2026 continued to post operating and net losses, extending the loss trend based on disclosed filings.

  3. 3

    On August 31, 2026, the company signed a KRW 3 billion treasury stock trust agreement with Hana Securities, with plans to fully retire the acquired shares after the contract ends.

  4. 4

    The company is pursuing multiple new business lines including NVIDIA Omniverse integration, a Gaia-X-linked physical AI platform project with the German Aerospace Center (DLR), and a satellite data refinement solution.

  5. 5

    Since its December 2025 KOSDAQ technology-exception listing, the stock has shown significant volatility, with recent sharp moves driven by event-specific news such as the treasury stock buyback announcement.

02

Business structure

Founded in 2001, EGIS is a specialist in 3D GIS and digital twin technology that has developed its entire software stack in-house without relying on foreign software or open-source alternatives, securing a high degree of technological self-sufficiency.

Its core technology, 'Digital Earth,' digitizes real-world geospatial data with precision to collect, process, analyze and simulate physical spaces such as cities, infrastructure and the environment in real time; worldwide, only Google, ESRI, Cesium, Supermap and EGIS can implement such platforms, with EGIS being the sole Korean company among them.

As of 2024, revenue mix consisted of build/construction services at 89.5%, subscription services at 4.8%, maintenance services at 3.4% and other revenue at 2.3%, meaning individual public-sector project builds account for the overwhelming majority of sales.

The company became the first domestic digital twin firm to obtain the standard grade of Korea's Cloud Security Assurance Program (CSAP) certification in 2022, securing institutional credibility with government agencies and local governments, and counts the Seoul Metropolitan Government, Korea National Park Service and K-water among its clients.

It is now diversifying its revenue structure by adding a subscription (SaaS) model that allows customers to continuously subscribe to and use its digital twin platform in a cloud environment alongside its traditional individual project contracting approach.

More recently, the company completed NVIDIA Omniverse integration and is expanding into advanced sectors such as physical AI and aerospace, while running a platform-building project with the German Aerospace Center (DLR) linked to the European Union's Gaia-X data sovereignty standard framework.

Competitively, the company emphasizes price competitiveness relative to foreign solutions such as ESRI ArcGIS and Cesium GS along with its track record in the domestic public sector, though its global market share in the digital earth platform segment itself is still assessed as low.

04

Earnings analysis

On a consolidated basis, 2025 revenue fell sharply to KRW 21.25 billion from KRW 30.28 billion in 2024, and the company posted an operating loss of KRW 6.80 billion, reversing from an operating profit of KRW 2.39 billion the prior year.

The operating margin plunged from 7.9% in 2024 to negative 32.0% in 2025, reflecting a business structure where quarterly and annual results fluctuate widely depending on the timing of project wins and revenue recognition typical of construction-type contracts for public institutions.

Net income attributable to owners also swung from a profit of KRW 3.13 billion in 2024 to a net loss of KRW 4.81 billion in 2025, while operating cash flow reversed from an inflow of KRW 3.76 billion in 2024 to an outflow of KRW 9.32 billion in 2025, indicating weakened cash generation.

On the balance sheet side, total equity rose from KRW 31.70 billion in 2024 to KRW 48.93 billion in 2025, a change attributable to IPO proceeds from the December 2025 KOSDAQ listing.

The debt ratio improved from 70.4% in 2024 to 37.9% in 2025, showing an improvement in balance sheet stability metrics even as profitability deteriorated.

Losses continued into 2026, with first-quarter revenue of KRW 5.80 billion accompanied by an operating loss of KRW 2.22 billion and a net loss of KRW 1.70 billion, while second-quarter revenue of KRW 5.59 billion saw the loss widen further to an operating loss of KRW 2.42 billion and a net loss of KRW 2.93 billion.

This pattern suggests that upfront investment costs and personnel expenses are structurally constraining profitability, and that the subscription revenue base remains too small to offset fixed costs with recurring income.

05

Industry analysis

The digital twin and geospatial industry is growing on the back of expanding demand for digital transformation in urban, infrastructure and environmental management, with brokerage research citing a forecast compound annual growth rate of 58.7% for the global digital twin market from 2021 to 2027.

The domestic GIS market has likewise grown at roughly 20.0% annually since 2022 and is projected to reach approximately KRW 801.8 billion by 2027, indicating the underlying addressable market is in an expansion phase.

However, the domestic market centers on a B2G structure dependent on government and local government orders, linking results to budget cycles and public procurement schedules, which is a key driver of quarterly earnings volatility.

In the global competitive landscape, Google, ESRI (ArcGIS), Cesium and Supermap are cited as the few companies with Digital-Earth-level platforms, and EGIS is named alongside them as the sole domestic player of this kind.

More recently, application areas have expanded into NVIDIA Omniverse, physical AI and aerospace data, broadening demand beyond the traditional geospatial industry into manufacturing, robotics, drones and satellite data processing.

These new business areas remain at an early commercialization stage, however, and the timing and scale of any revenue contribution carries significant uncertainty.

06

Outlook

The company plans to formally launch its satellite data refinement solution in the second half of 2026 and intends to link it with its own physical AI platform to build an aerospace data ecosystem.

Its NVIDIA Omniverse-integrated solution is targeted for official registration within the year, and the Gaia-X-linked physical AI platform project with the German Aerospace Center (DLR) is stated to extend beyond platform construction into an ongoing operational role for the company.

The company has also outlined a longer-term plan to expand its proprietary 'Vision-X' platform into country-specific data hubs, aiming to build a recurring revenue model through platform operation, real-time data and API linkage, and industry-specific application services.

On shareholder returns, the company pursued a transfer of capital reserves to retained earnings and an articles-of-incorporation amendment at an extraordinary general meeting in July 2026, followed by a KRW 3 billion treasury stock trust agreement in August with stated consideration of future share cancellation.

However, no official timeline has been given for when these new business and shareholder-return initiatives will translate into actual revenue and profit, and losses persisted through the first and second quarters of 2026.

The company is also building an international reference base through MOUs with academic institutions in Vietnam and an applied science research organization in the Netherlands, though whether these evolve into commercial contracts remains a point to watch.

08

Bull factors

Sole Domestic Digital Earth Platform Position

Aerospace Technology is cited as one of only five companies worldwide capable of implementing a digital-earth-class platform, alongside Google, ESRI, Cesium, and Supermap, and is the only Korean company on this list.

The company has developed all of its solutions using proprietary technology, resulting in low dependence on foreign software, and it became the first domestic digital twin company to obtain the standard grade of Cloud Security Assurance Program (CSAP) certification, securing an institutional entry barrier in the public procurement market.

This technological self-sufficiency and certification base may serve as a differentiating factor compared to competitors.

Diversification into New Application Areas

The company is expanding its business scope into physical AI and aerospace fields, having completed integration with NVIDIA Omniverse, pursued a Gaia-X linkage project with the German Aerospace Center, and developed a satellite data refinement solution.

This represents an attempt to secure new application areas such as manufacturing, robotics, drones, and satellites, moving away from its existing revenue structure centered on public sector construction projects, and could contribute to revenue diversification if successful.

However, these businesses remain closer to the testing and verification stage, and the timing of actual revenue contribution is uncertain.

Strengthening Shareholder Return Policy

Through an extraordinary general meeting of shareholders in July 2026, the company pursued the transfer of capital reserves into retained earnings, and in August it signed a treasury stock acquisition trust agreement worth KRW 3 billion, stating that it would also consider cancellation of the shares in the future.

This represents a case in which the company has expressed its intention to enhance shareholder value through disclosure amid significant share price volatility since listing. If the treasury stock cancellation is actually implemented, it could result in a reduction in the number of outstanding shares.

09

Bear factors

Consecutive Operating and Net Losses

In 2025, the company recorded a consolidated operating loss of KRW 6,798,870 thousand and a net loss of KRW 4,807,680 thousand, and operating losses continued in both the first and second quarters of 2026, with the loss scale actually widening in the second quarter.

Operating cash flow also turned to an outflow of KRW 9,322,250 thousand in 2025, accompanied by a weakening in cash-generating capacity. If the loss-making trend becomes prolonged, the need for additional fundraising may become more pronounced.

Earnings Volatility from Public Procurement Dependence

A significant portion of revenue is generated from individual project construction services for public institutions and local governments, with construction service revenue accounting for 89.5% of total revenue as of 2024.

This business structure is characterized by large quarterly performance variations depending on the timing of budget execution and project order schedules, and since the proportion of subscription-based revenue remains low, a stable base of recurring revenue has not yet been sufficiently established.

Uncertainty Over Commercialization Timing of New Businesses

New businesses such as physical AI and aerospace data remain at the stage of successful testing and completed integration, and the timing of official launch and revenue generation has not yet been confirmed even based on the company's own announcements.

Overseas MOUs signed are also at the joint research and demonstration stage, and it remains unclear whether these will transition into commercial contracts.

Amid ongoing initial investment costs and labor expense burdens, there is a risk that losses could widen further if new businesses fail to contribute to profitability in a timely manner.

Risk factors

Earnings Risk

Operating losses and net losses have continued since 2025, and operating cash flow has also turned into an outflow. If losses become further prolonged, this could place a burden on financial capacity. Quarterly performance shows significant volatility, requiring caution in judging short-term trends.

Business Structure Risk

Since revenue is heavily dependent on individual project orders from public institutions, performance may be affected by changes in government budget formulation and execution schedules as well as public procurement policy. If the transition to subscription-based revenue does not proceed as planned, revenue volatility may persist.

New Business Execution Risk

New business areas such as physical AI and aerospace data remain at the testing and verification stage, and there is uncertainty regarding the timing of official commercialization and revenue generation.

The company may be at a disadvantage in terms of resources and scale compared to global competitors (Google, ESRI, Cesium, Supermap), creating a risk that securing market share in new business competition may not proceed as planned.

12

Overall view

EGIS, as the only Korean company with a proprietary Digital Earth platform, holds technological differentiation and certification-based credibility in the public procurement market, but profitability has been clearly weak since 2025, with consolidated operating and net losses persisting.

Its business structure centered on public-sector construction services amplifies quarter-to-quarter earnings volatility, and new initiatives such as the shift to subscription revenue and expansion into physical AI and aerospace remain at a testing or early stage, leaving the timing of their revenue contribution uncertain.

The company is simultaneously pursuing commercialization of new businesses and strengthening shareholder returns through treasury stock acquisition and potential cancellation during the second half of 2026, making the actual pace of execution of these initiatives a key point to watch going forward.

On the balance sheet, total equity and the debt ratio improved due to IPO proceeds, but continued operating cash outflows warrant attention to whether cash generation can recover.

Investors should weigh earnings volatility tied to public procurement schedules, the possibility of delays in new business commercialization, and the potential need for further fundraising. This report is for informational purposes only and does not include a buy or sell recommendation.
