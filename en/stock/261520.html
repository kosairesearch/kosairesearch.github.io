02

Business structure

Egis is a software company whose core business is a digital twin platform that combines heterogeneous data such as real estate, weather and transport on top of 3D geographic information system (GIS) technology.

The company was founded in 2001 and listed on KOSDAQ under the technology special listing route in December 2025. Based on the pre-listing investment document, build-out services made up 89.5% of 2024 revenue, subscription services 4.8%, maintenance services 3.4% and other revenue 2.3%.

Because build-out services dominate, results move sharply with the timing and size of project orders. Its main customers are public institutions, and its core offering is digital twin platform construction for them. The company says it runs its self-developed XDCloud Digital Earth platform on 100% in-house technology.

At the 2026 Industrial AI Expo in October 2026, it unveiled its next-generation Digital Earth platform and XDRoadMap, a solution for building precision road maps. It is pursuing a shift toward a subscription SaaS model and cites its CSAP standard-grade certification as a basis for reliability.

Within the IT and software sector, large SI companies such as Samsung SDS, Hyundai Autoever and LG CNS operate, but Egis focuses on geospatial digital twin platforms.

Through a board decision, Park Kwang-mok, a deputy president, was named the new CEO, moving the company from founder-led management to professional management.

04

Earnings analysis

On the disclosed annual basis, revenue in 2025 was KRW 21.3 billion, down 29.8% from KRW 30.3 billion in 2024. Over the same period, operating profit of KRW 2.39 billion turned into an operating loss of KRW 6.8 billion, and the operating margin moved from 7.9% to -32.0%.

Net income attributable to controlling shareholders of KRW 3.13 billion turned into a net loss of KRW 4.81 billion, and net income including non-controlling interests moved to a net loss of KRW 4.81 billion over the same period.

At the end of 2025, total equity was KRW 48.9 billion, all of it attributable to controlling shareholders, and total liabilities were KRW 18.5 billion. The debt ratio fell from 70.4% in 2024 to 37.9% in 2025, but operating cash flow turned from KRW 3.76 billion in 2024 to -KRW 9.32 billion in 2025.

The company explained the 2025 revenue decline as temporary order delays caused by the economic slowdown and external factors, and said the decline widened the operating and net losses; this explanation was reported in February 2026 for the standalone disclosure.

Because the disclosed quarterly table contains no figures for the third and fourth quarters of 2025, the quarterly trend is read from the first quarter of 2026.

Revenue was KRW 5.8 billion in the first quarter of 2026, then KRW 5.59 billion in the second quarter (down 3.6% from the prior quarter) and KRW 5.48 billion in the third (down 1.8%), declining for two consecutive quarters.

The operating loss was KRW 2.22 billion in the first quarter, KRW 2.42 billion in the second and KRW 2.53 billion in the third, so losses kept widening.

Net loss attributable to controlling shareholders was KRW 1.7 billion in the first quarter and KRW 2.93 billion in the second, then narrowed to KRW 2.47 billion in the third. The third quarter therefore showed a mixed pattern, with a smaller net loss but a larger operating loss.

The sum of net losses attributable to controlling shareholders over the last four quarters (first quarter to fourth quarter of 2025) is KRW 4.81 billion.

05

Industry analysis

Egis's end market is public-sector digital transformation and demand for geospatial data. The company says it is extending its digital twin platform into water management and urban infrastructure management for local governments and public institutions.

At EGU 2026, Europe's largest earth science conference, held in May 2026, it presented digital twin technology applying aerospace and physical AI, and proposed the concept of a Cognitive Digital Twin that combines large language models with digital twins.

Its push into future mobility such as autonomous driving connects with demand for precision road maps and high-precision 3D data. Because the 2025 revenue decline was attributed to order delays from the economic slowdown and external conditions, results appear exposed to the public project order cycle.

Compared with large SI companies, Egis is smaller in scale, but its geospatial-focused platform and CSAP certification are presented as points of differentiation. Overseas, it is pursuing data-space-based cooperation with the German Aerospace Center and the Netherlands Organisation for Applied Scientific Research.

Reliance on domestic public orders and the speed of securing overseas references are the two axes that will shape its position within the industry.

Outlook

At the 2026 Industrial AI Expo in October 2026, the company unveiled its Digital Earth platform and said it discussed local application and business cooperation with more than 10 overseas companies and institutions.

Company officials said they will continue follow-up talks to secure new results, so the current stage is one of negotiation and partner development. After its participation in EGU 2026 in May 2026, the company said it would fully launch its push into global markets, including Europe.

Since 2025, it has taken part in a digital twin-based three-dimensional cadastre construction pilot run by Indonesia's National Land Agency. The company is restructuring its business model around subscription-based solutions, and how this shift affects the revenue mix and operating margin is the next point to confirm.

Its plan to widen opportunities in future mobility such as autonomous driving was presented alongside the XDRoadMap launch. At the regular general meeting on March 27, 2026, amendments to the articles of incorporation and a proposal to grant stock options were approved.

On August 31, 2026, a decision to enter into a share buyback trust contract was disclosed, and the progress of that contract is a point to monitor for shareholder returns.

Whether overseas talks turn into contracts and revenue, and how the subscription share changes from 4.8% in 2024, are the medium-term points to watch.

Bull factors

Room for subscription shift

Subscription services were only 4.8% of 2024 revenue, but the company is pursuing a shift to a subscription SaaS model. If build-out-driven revenue becomes more recurring, there is room for the operating margin to improve.

The CSAP standard-grade certification is presented as a basis of trust in public-sector sales during this transition.

Wider overseas references

At EGU 2026 in May 2026, the company presented the Cognitive Digital Twin concept and is running cooperation with the German Aerospace Center and the Netherlands Organisation for Applied Scientific Research.

At the October 2026 Industrial AI Expo, it discussed local application with more than 10 overseas companies and institutions. If these talks turn into contracts, revenue could become less dependent on domestic public projects.

Balance sheet and shareholder return tools

The debt ratio at the end of 2025 was 37.9%, down from 70.4% in 2024, and total liabilities were KRW 18.5 billion. A decision to enter into a share buyback trust contract was disclosed on August 31, 2026. A shareholder return tool is in place while the capital base remains intact.

Bear factors

Operating losses widening

From the first quarter through the third quarter of 2026, the operating loss widened from KRW 2.22 billion to KRW 2.42 billion and then KRW 2.53 billion. The operating margin fell to -32.0% in 2025.

Although the net loss attributable to controlling shareholders narrowed in the third quarter, the operating-level loss did not improve.

Revenue decline and reliance on orders

Revenue fell 3.6% and 1.8% from the prior quarter in the second and third quarters of 2026, respectively. Revenue dropped 29.8% in 2025, and the company cited temporary order delays. Because build-out services account for most revenue, a prolonged gap in orders would increase earnings volatility.

Weaker cash flow

Operating cash flow turned from KRW 3.76 billion in 2024 to -KRW 9.32 billion in 2025. The sum of net losses attributable to controlling shareholders over the last four quarters is KRW 4.81 billion. While losses continue, the core business's ability to generate cash has weakened, which is a point on the bear side.

Risk factors

New business execution risk

The company presented a plan to widen its business into future mobility such as autonomous driving alongside the launch of XDRoadMap. This area connects with demand for precision road maps, but it is not yet visible as revenue in disclosed results.

If commercialization is delayed or cooperation does not take shape, research and development spending could weigh on results before any revenue appears.

Execution risk in the transition

The subscription shift and overseas expansion are not yet visible in the revenue mix. If staffing and development costs rise before the transition pays off, the loss period could lengthen. If overseas talks do not become contracts, the company could be left with cost burdens alone.

Major shareholder stake and free float

The stake held by the largest shareholder and related parties is 60.62% as of the last change date of March 27, 2026. Because the number of freely tradable shares is limited, trading can be thin. Share price volatility after listing is another factor to monitor.
