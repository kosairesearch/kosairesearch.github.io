Revenue Shrinks, Losses Widen as Egis Bets on SaaS Shift

Egis saw 2025 revenue fall 29.8% year on year and swing to an operating loss, and posted consecutive operating losses in the first and second quarters of 2026, while the company pursues a recovery in profitability through subscription-based (SaaS) digital twin supply to public institutions and new business expansion.

02

Business structure

Egis is a specialist in 3D GIS and digital twin technology founded in 2001, and it listed on KOSDAQ in December 2025 under the technology special listing track.

Its core technology, Digital Earth, is a platform that digitizes measured spatial data to analyze and simulate cities, infrastructure, and the environment in real time, developed in-house to achieve a high degree of technological self-sufficiency.

The company's flagship product is 'XDWORLD,' a digital-twin-based 3D GIS platform, through which it provides spatial information publishing and information services alongside software development and systems integration.

At the time of listing, the disclosed 2024 revenue mix showed construction services accounting for the vast majority at 89.5%, with subscription services at 4.8%, maintenance services at 3.4%, and other revenue at 2.3%.

The customer base is centered on the public sector, including government agencies and local governments, and the company has previously won a contract to build a digital twin water management platform for the Ministry of Environment's Han River Flood Control Office.

The company focuses on building digital twin platforms for public institutions, a structure in which profitability remains constrained by upfront investment costs and personnel expenses.

More recently, Egis has been pursuing a shift away from its traditional system-build business model toward a cloud-based SaaS platform in an effort to improve profitability.

In terms of competitive positioning, a number of specialized domestic startups operate in the spatial information and digital twin space, while globally only a handful of companies have commercialized similar source technology.

04

Earnings analysis

Egis's 2025 revenue came to KRW 21.3 billion, down 29.8% from KRW 30.3 billion in 2024.

Over the same period, operating profit swung from KRW 2.39 billion in 2024 to an operating loss of KRW 6.8 billion in 2025, and controlling-interest net profit likewise swung from KRW 3.13 billion in 2024 to a controlling-interest net loss of KRW 4.81 billion in 2025.

Net income including non-controlling interests moved in the same direction, from KRW 3.13 billion in 2024 to a net loss of KRW 4.81 billion in 2025. As a result, the operating margin deteriorated sharply from 7.9% in 2024 to -32.0% in 2025, reflecting the combined effect of declining revenue and rising cost burdens.

Moving into 2026, quarterly revenue fell 3.6% from KRW 5.8 billion in Q1 to KRW 5.59 billion in Q2, while the operating loss widened from KRW 2.22 billion in Q1 to KRW 2.42 billion in Q2, marking two consecutive quarters of operating losses.

The controlling-interest net loss also widened from KRW 1.7 billion in Q1 to KRW 2.93 billion in Q2, likewise two consecutive quarters in the red.

On a non-consolidated basis, however, Q1 2026 revenue rose 42.2% year on year, indicating that top-line growth continued in some periods even as consolidated profitability worsened.

On the balance sheet, total equity stood at KRW 48.9 billion at the end of 2025 (with controlling interest equity also at KRW 48.9 billion) against total liabilities of KRW 18.5 billion, while operating cash flow swung from KRW 3.76 billion in 2024 to -KRW 9.32 billion in 2025, showing the profit deterioration flowing through to cash generation as well.

05

Industry analysis

The digital twin and spatial information industry is seeing expanding demand centered on public-sector applications such as smart cities, disaster safety, environmental forecasting, and infrastructure maintenance.

Eugene Investment & Securities projected in a November 2025 report that the global digital twin market would grow at an average annual rate of 58.7% between 2021 and 2027.

Egis's platform has obtained the SaaS standard grade under South Korea's Cloud Security Assurance Program (CSAP), described as the only case in Korea of a spatial information service original provider receiving this certification.

The company has stated that it is extending digital-twin source technology, commercialized by only four companies worldwide including Google and Unity, into advanced sectors such as autonomous driving and aerospace.

A number of specialized domestic digital-twin and spatial-information startups are active, so competition persists around winning public-sector projects.

Because a substantial portion of revenue is generated from construction services for public institutions, quarterly revenue tends to be volatile depending on the timing of government and local government budget execution and project orders.

Separate from expectations for industry-wide growth, individual companies' profitability is likely to diverge depending on how quickly subscription-based revenue expands as a share of the total.

06

Outlook

In July 2026, Egis signed a contract with Jeju Special Self-Governing Province to build a next-generation digital twin platform, supplied in a SaaS format that, unlike traditional system-build projects, is more conducive to additional service integration and expansion.

At the end of September 2026, the company took part in a related workshop hosted by Jeju Province and unveiled interim results of the platform build under way since July, saying it was integrating administrative data on water, transportation, environment, and safety into a single platform and upgrading it into an AI transformation (AX) platform.

The company has been expanding overseas cooperation, including partnerships with the German Aerospace Center and a Dutch applied science research institute, as well as memoranda of understanding in Vietnam covering maritime logistics and transportation applications.

On the new business front, Egis has completed integration with NVIDIA Omniverse to implement physical AI and is pursuing official registration of the solution, while in the aerospace area it has finished satellite data conversion testing and plans to launch a satellite data refinement solution in the second half of 2026.

The company also said it is pursuing smart glasses applications following certification from the Telecommunications Technology Association (TTA).

On shareholder returns, Egis entered a KRW 3 billion treasury stock trust agreement with Hana Securities running from August 31, 2026 to March 2, 2027, stating that shares acquired under the agreement would be fully cancelled once the contract ends.

The company's strategy is oriented toward reducing reliance on construction-type revenue and raising the share of subscription-based SaaS revenue to improve profitability, though the specific timing of revenue contribution from new businesses and the SaaS transition has not yet been confirmed.

08

Bull factors

Revenue Diversification Through SaaS and New Business Expansion

The SaaS-based digital twin platform contract with Jeju Special Self-Governing Province is a concrete example of the effort to shift the revenue mix from construction-type to subscription-type revenue.

New businesses, including a physical AI solution via NVIDIA Omniverse integration and a satellite data refinement solution for aerospace use, are being pursued in parallel.

Expanding partnerships with overseas research institutions and companies could help diversify the business away from its current concentration on domestic public-sector projects.

Shareholder Return Intent via Buyback and Cancellation

Egis entered a KRW 3 billion treasury stock trust agreement with Hana Securities starting August 31, 2026, stating that the shares acquired would be fully cancelled once the contract ends. The planned acquisition equates to roughly 6.9% of outstanding shares, a meaningful size.

It is notable that the company has taken a capital-policy-driven shareholder return measure even while posting losses.

Improved Debt Ratio and Low Capital Impairment Concern

The debt-to-equity ratio fell from 70.4% in 2024 to 37.9% in 2025, easing the relative burden on the balance sheet. Total equity of KRW 48.9 billion at the end of 2025 reflects the effect of capital raised through the listing.

Despite continued operating losses, no extreme financial risk signal such as capital impairment has been observed to date.

09

Bear factors

Continued Revenue Decline and Deteriorating Profitability

2025 revenue fell 29.8% year on year, and operating profit swung from KRW 2.39 billion to an operating loss of KRW 6.8 billion. Operating losses continued into Q1 and Q2 of 2026, and the loss size actually widened from KRW 2.22 billion to KRW 2.42 billion.

With revenue decline and widening losses occurring simultaneously, a near-term reversal in profitability cannot be assumed.

Deteriorating Cash Flow

Operating cash flow swung from an inflow of KRW 3.76 billion in 2024 to an outflow of KRW 9.32 billion in 2025. This suggests that actual cash generation weakened alongside the loss recorded on the income statement.

If this trend continues, attention is likely to grow around how the company funds future investment and operations.

Revenue Volatility from Public-Sector Project Dependence

As of 2024, construction services accounted for 89.5% of revenue, reflecting a heavy reliance on project-based revenue from public institutions. Under this structure, quarterly results can swing significantly depending on the timing of government and local government budget execution and project orders. This volatility could persist for some time until the SaaS transition gains more traction.

Risk factors

Financial and Cash Flow Risk

Operating cash flow turned to an outflow of -KRW 9.32 billion in 2025, while the controlling-interest net loss for the same year reached KRW 4.81 billion. Against this backdrop, executing a KRW 3 billion treasury stock purchase adds a further cash outflow factor. If losses continue to accumulate, scrutiny of the company's capital buffer could become necessary.

Customer and Revenue Structure Risk

Revenue is heavily concentrated in construction services for public institutions, exposing the company to changes in a specific customer segment or budget policy. The timing and scale of government and local government projects can vary from year to year, which can reduce revenue predictability. This structural risk is likely to persist until SaaS-based revenue accumulates sufficiently.

New Business Commercialization Risk

New businesses such as physical AI and the satellite data solution for aerospace use appear to still be in the testing or formal registration stage. The timing and scale at which these businesses will translate into actual revenue have not yet been specifically confirmed.

There is also a possibility that investment costs for new businesses precede monetization, which could be delayed.

12

Overall view

Egis faces a profitability recovery challenge, having posted a 29.8% revenue decline and a swing to an operating loss in 2025, followed by consecutive operating losses in Q1 and Q2 of 2026.

At the same time, the company is attempting changes on both the business and capital fronts, including the SaaS-based digital twin contract with Jeju Province, new businesses in physical AI and aerospace, and a KRW 3 billion treasury stock acquisition and planned cancellation.

While the debt ratio has declined, operating cash flow turned to an outflow, producing diverging signals between balance-sheet structure and cash generation capacity.

Given the revenue structure's heavy reliance on construction services for public institutions, quarterly volatility may persist, and the actual results of the SaaS transition have not yet been confirmed in the numbers.

For readers, it appears important to sequentially track the upcoming quarterly earnings releases, whether new businesses translate into revenue, and the outcome of the treasury stock trust agreement upon its expiration. This report is intended to convey disclosed factual information.
