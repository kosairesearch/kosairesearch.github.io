Revenue Rebounds, but Losses Persist at Raontech

02

Business structure

Founded in October 2009, Raontech is a fabless company that designs and manufactures microdisplay and SoC (system-on-chip) semiconductors. Its former name was Raontech, and it changed to the current name as of December 2025. The company listed on KOSDAQ in 2023 as a technology growth company under ticker 418420.

Its main applications are smart glasses for XR devices, automotive head-up displays (HUD), and optical communication equipment. The company says its very low power consumption and small size have led to adoption by global big tech firms.

Its core focus is building LCoS display mass-production capacity, and it is expanding into the XR and AI-AR market in line with the outlook for XR device launches in 2026. A KRW 15 billion convertible bond issued in August 2025 was the company's first external fundraising since its 2023 KOSDAQ listing.

The conversion request period for this bond runs from August 26, 2026 to July 26, 2030, and shares issued upon conversion equal 9.18% of total shares issued. The company has stated that commercializing micro LEDs will strengthen its competitiveness in the AI glasses market.

The largest shareholder and related parties hold 28.40% as of the last change date of April 30, 2026. On competition, the company's stated differentiators are power efficiency and miniaturization.

04

Earnings analysis

On a separate-financials basis, annual revenue fell from KRW 10.9 billion in 2022 to KRW 10.7 billion in 2023, KRW 8.9 billion in 2024, and KRW 8.75 billion in 2025, down three consecutive years from 2023 through 2025.

The steepest decline was 17.1% year on year in 2024, and 2022 was the largest year and 2025 the smallest within 2022 to 2025.

Operating income was KRW 322 million in 2022, but turned into an operating loss of KRW 2.15 billion in 2023 and remained in deficit with losses of KRW 6.99 billion in 2024 and KRW 5.81 billion in 2025, three straight loss years.

The operating margin improved from -78.5% in 2024 to -66.4% in 2025 but remains deeply negative. According to a Thinkpool report, research and development expenses rose sharply from KRW 6.5 billion in 2023 to KRW 9.8 billion in 2024, which was cited as a major burden on the loss.

Net income attributable to controlling shareholders swung from KRW 585 million in 2022 to a net loss of KRW 7.08 billion in 2023, followed by net losses of KRW 6.48 billion in 2024 and KRW 6.55 billion in 2025. Net income including non-controlling interests was reported at the same amounts in those years.

Operating cash flow went from KRW 404 million in 2022 to negative KRW 3.15 billion in 2023, negative KRW 3.9 billion in 2024, and negative KRW 5.1 billion in 2025, with the cash burn widening.

The debt ratio jumped from 74.5% in 2024 to 150.8% in 2025, when total liabilities of KRW 25.1 billion exceeded total equity of KRW 16.7 billion.

Quarterly revenue moved from KRW 1.78 billion in the third quarter of 2025 to KRW 2.02 billion in the fourth quarter (up 13.3% from the prior quarter), fell to KRW 918 million in the first quarter of 2026 (down 54.5%), then rose to KRW 1.46 billion in the second quarter and KRW 2.57 billion in the third quarter, marking two straight quarters of growth.

Operating losses over the same stretch were KRW 1.5 billion, KRW 2.01 billion, KRW 3.32 billion, KRW 2.97 billion, and KRW 1.73 billion, a five-quarter losing streak. The third-quarter loss narrowed from the prior quarter but widened from a year earlier.

Shareholder net losses for the last four quarters total KRW 11.6 billion, and the size of quarterly losses shifts sharply with revenue.

05

Industry analysis

Raontech operates in microdisplays used in XR devices and AI glasses. A Thinkpool report described the AR glasses industry as having a positive medium- to long-term growth outlook, while the same report flagged uncertainty over when the company reaches breakeven as a risk.

The company is pursuing rapid product development and technological differentiation in line with the outlook for XR device launches in 2026. The timing of when adoption of glasses-type devices scales up will shape Raontech's revenue level.

Among 89 companies in the display and components sector tracked by AlphaSquare, Raontech ranks 88th on operating margin, near the bottom, while the sector average operating margin is slightly negative. Its debt ratio of 150.76% is above the sector average of 114.96%.

Improving the earnings structure therefore remains the central task. The link between semiconductor capital expenditure cycles and display component demand is not quantified in the company's disclosures, so it should be read qualitatively.

Demand for small, low-power displays is tied to XR device makers' design requirements, and whether customers adopt the technology will determine its position in the cycle.

Bull factors

Quarterly revenue rebound for a second straight quarter

Third-quarter 2026 revenue of KRW 2.57 billion rose 75.5% from the prior quarter and was the highest of the last five quarters. It has grown for two consecutive quarters since the first-quarter 2026 low and is up 44.0% from a year earlier. Continuation of this recovery is cited as the strongest bull argument.

XR and AI-AR growth axis with mass-production capacity

The company is pursuing product development and technological differentiation in line with the outlook for XR device launches in 2026. It has LCoS mass-production capacity in place, and a Thinkpool report described the AR glasses industry as having a positive medium- to long-term growth outlook. Its reported adoption by global big tech firms is another factor supporting its position in this market.

Narrowing loss trend

The operating loss narrowed from KRW 6.99 billion in 2024 to KRW 5.81 billion in 2025, and the third-quarter 2026 operating loss of KRW 1.73 billion was below the second-quarter figure of KRW 2.97 billion. The operating margin improved from -78.5% in 2024 to -66.4% in 2025. Whether losses keep shrinking as revenue grows is the test for this bull case.

Bear factors

Prolonged losses and accumulated deficits

Operating losses have continued for three consecutive years from 2023 through 2025, and net losses attributable to controlling shareholders have also persisted over the same period. Shareholder net losses for the last four quarters total KRW 11.6 billion. The operating cash outflow widened from negative KRW 3.15 billion in 2023 to negative KRW 5.1 billion in 2025.

Rising financial leverage

The debt ratio rose from 74.5% in 2024 to 150.8% in 2025, when total liabilities of KRW 25.1 billion exceeded total equity of KRW 16.7 billion at year-end. This is above the sector average debt ratio. Additional fundraising would add to the financial burden.

Convertible bond dilution and R&D burden

The KRW 15 billion convertible bond issued in August 2025 equals 9.18% of total shares issued if fully converted. Research and development expenses rose from KRW 6.5 billion in 2023 to KRW 9.8 billion in 2024 and were cited as a burden on earnings improvement. A Thinkpool report pointed to uncertainty over when breakeven is reached as a risk.

Risk factors

Earnings volatility

Quarterly revenue fell 54.5% from KRW 2.02 billion in the fourth quarter of 2025 to KRW 918 million in the first quarter of 2026 before recovering to KRW 2.57 billion in the third quarter. Quarterly operating losses ranged from KRW 1.5 billion to KRW 3.32 billion over the same period. A sharp revenue drop in any single quarter could change the loss scale significantly.

Fundraising and share dilution

Conversion requests for the convertible bond have been possible since August 26, 2026, and shares issued upon conversion equal 9.18% of total shares issued. The largest shareholder and related parties hold 28.40%, so new share issuance would dilute existing stakes. Further fundraising under the current financial structure could add to the burden.

Customer adoption and launch timing

If XR device launches and new customer adoption are delayed, the conversion of LCoS mass-production capacity into revenue could slip. Launch timing depends on external device makers, which the company cannot fully control. Because capacity expansion requires yield stability on new lines, yield issues are also a risk.
