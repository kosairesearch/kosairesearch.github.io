Thumbage: New Titles Face a Shrinking Revenue Base

Thumbage is preparing self-developed titles for 2026, but its disclosed results show shrinking sales, four straight years of losses, and a planned change of largest shareholder.

02

Business structure

Thumbage is a mobile game developer and publisher founded in 2013 that listed on KOSDAQ in 2016 through a merger with KB 6th SPAC. Its largest shareholder, Nesi Samsipsambun, distributed the mobile game Hero, whose success first brought the company public attention.

The company says later titles underperformed, pushing its results into a downtrend. Its main titles include God Raid, a PvP battle game that went global in early 2024, and a puzzle game based on the Boxing Star IP. Its subsidiary Unsained Studio launched Hero Raising, based on the Hero IP.

The company attributes the 2025 sales decline mainly to lower revenue from its existing live games. Its 2026 launch targets are Palladog Invenrise, a Palladog-IP defense puzzle RPG, and Project RB, a social-play casual action game aimed at global markets.

Both titles are described as casual games designed for broad mobile audiences. Medium-term titles disclosed include Project AR (strategy simulation), Project EH (idle RPG), and Project FPS (extraction shooter). The company plans to expand its IP business model using IP inherited from Nesi Samsipsambun. It holds three consolidated subsidiaries, including the development unit Champion Studio.

04

Earnings analysis

Annual revenue was KRW 12.5 billion in 2022, KRW 11.6 billion in 2023 (down 7.7% year on year), KRW 14 billion in 2024 (up 20.9%), and KRW 6.02 billion in 2025 (down 56.9%). Of 2022–2025, 2024 was the largest year and 2025 the smallest.

Operating losses were KRW 16.5 billion, KRW 12.8 billion, KRW 10.2 billion and KRW 7.88 billion across 2022–2025, with the loss narrowing each year from 2023. Operating margin moved from -131.6% to -110.9%, then -72.8%, then -130.9%.

In 2025 the operating loss shrank versus 2024, but revenue fell 56.9%, so the margin deteriorated again. Net loss attributable to controlling shareholders narrowed from KRW 21.5 billion in 2022 to KRW 6.89 billion in 2024, then widened to KRW 9.27 billion in 2025.

In 2024 the net loss was smaller than the operating loss, meaning non-operating items added to earnings; in 2025 the net loss exceeded the operating loss, so non-operating items turned into a drag.

Quarterly revenue was KRW 1.28 billion in Q3 2025, KRW 995 million in Q4 2025 (down 22.1% from the prior quarter), KRW 1.01 billion in Q1 2026, KRW 1.01 billion in Q2 2026, and KRW 963 million in Q3 2026 (down 24.6% year on year).

Operating loss fell from KRW 1.53 billion in Q3 2025 to KRW 981 million in Q1 2026, then widened to KRW 1.2 billion in Q2 and KRW 1.23 billion in Q3 2026.

Net loss attributable to controlling shareholders was largest in Q4 2025 at KRW 3.41 billion, followed by KRW 1.09 billion, KRW 1.36 billion and KRW 1.27 billion in the three 2026 quarters. The disclosed figures alone do not pin down the cause of the Q4 2025 loss increase.

At end-2025 total equity was KRW 12.9 billion, all attributable to controlling shareholders, against total liabilities of KRW 1.32 billion and a debt-to-equity ratio of 10.2%.

Operating cash flow was negative in all four years: -KRW 11.9 billion in 2022, -KRW 8.69 billion in 2023, -KRW 3.96 billion in 2024 and -KRW 6.69 billion in 2025.

05

Industry analysis

According to company commentary cited by a financial data provider, competition in mobile gaming has intensified, and the life cycles of existing live-service titles have matured, reducing revenue. The company says it narrowed its operating loss through cost cuts and operational efficiency.

Its casual-focused slate means entry barriers are relatively low but competition is intense, so post-launch performance will be the key test. A 2024 media report argued that PvP battle games face a long-term disadvantage against MMORPGs. The same report said God Raid carries the burden of serving as a cash cow.

The company describes the extraction shooter genre as attracting strong interest in global markets and is widening its development scope into it. Project H, an extraction shooter built on Unreal Engine 5, is reported to target a global release on PC and consoles. Developers include staff with FPS and TPS experience.

A 2024 report described Thumbage as having the smallest market capitalization among listed game companies at that time. The company disclosed a headcount of 13, so its development and marketing bandwidth is likely narrower than that of larger game publishers.

06

Outlook

The company says its 2026 titles are self-developed and will be served directly rather than through publishers. Disclosed launch targets were August 2026 for Palladog Invenrise and November 2026 for Project RB, and the company has said timing may shift with development quality, testing and market conditions.

Project AR was slated for March 2027 and Project EH for September 2027. The extraction shooter Project FPS was reported for March 2028. On financing, a rights offering plan announced in April 2026 was amended to raise KRW 6.74 billion, with all proceeds allocated to operating funds.

The company has stated that its market capitalization has been below KRW 20 billion since June 25, 2026.

A shareholder agreement signed on November 18, 2026 provides for a KRW 11.5 billion third-party share issuance payable on December 30, 2026, which would bring in new investors and new registered directors and change control.

The filing states the deal does not involve transfer of shares held by the existing largest shareholder. The key checkpoint is whether the new titles translate into a recovery in quarterly revenue. The next regular report, the 2026 annual business report, will show the full-year revenue trend and operating margin.

08

Bull factors

Expanding title pipeline

The company disclosed preparation of five new titles, with two set for 2026 and later releases planned from 2027. Self-development means the company avoids royalty payments to outside developers. If the new titles lift revenue, the operating margin has room to improve.

Narrowing losses and low leverage

Operating loss narrowed from KRW 16.5 billion in 2022 to KRW 7.88 billion in 2025. Total liabilities at end-2025 were KRW 1.32 billion, with a debt-to-equity ratio of 10.2%. The light debt load leaves more room for financing choices that fund new-title development.

Proven IP and inherited franchises

The Hero IP brought the company its earlier public profile, and the subsidiary has released Hero Raising on that franchise. The company plans follow-on projects built on IP inherited from its largest shareholder. It argues that reinterpreting IPs with an existing fanbase could help attract early users.

09

Bear factors

Shrinking revenue base

Annual revenue for 2025 was KRW 6.02 billion, down 56.9% from the prior year. Q3 2026 revenue of KRW 963 million fell 24.6% year on year. The company attributes the decline mainly to existing games, so the revenue base stays thin until new titles offset that drop.

Persistent losses and cash outflow

Operating losses ran for four straight years from 2022 through 2025, and operating cash flow was negative in every one of those years. Net loss attributable to controlling shareholders widened to KRW 9.27 billion in 2025. The last four quarters totaled KRW 7.13 billion in net losses, with Q4 2025 the largest at KRW 3.41 billion.

Repeated financing and control change

The company said its market capitalization has been below KRW 20 billion since June 25, 2026. A 2026 rights offering was revised upward to KRW 6.74 billion through a corrective filing. A KRW 11.5 billion third-party issuance and a change of largest shareholder are now under way.

Repeated financing means business plans could be affected while the shareholder base and management direction change.

Risk factors

Launch timing and title performance

Disclosed targets were August and November 2026, and the company has said timing may change with development quality, testing and market conditions. Competition in casual titles is intense, and earlier titles were reported to have underperformed. Delays or weak results could push back the follow-on slate planned from 2027.

Governance and control change

Under the November 18, 2026 shareholder agreement, new investors will join a third-party issuance and new registered directors will be appointed. Management direction and development priorities could change in this process.

Whether payment and registration are completed around the December 30, 2026 payment date is the item to confirm.

Listing requirements and financing

Under KOSDAQ listing rules, a market capitalization below KRW 20 billion for 30 consecutive days leads to administrative issue designation. The company said it could withdraw a rights offering if the market-cap requirement is not met.

Current market capitalization is below that threshold, and listing requirements and financing are tied together.

Overall view

Thumbage has narrowed its operating loss since 2022, but revenue fell to KRW 6.02 billion in 2025 and the operating margin worsened to -130.9% from -72.8% the year before.

Net loss attributable to controlling shareholders totaled KRW 7.13 billion over the last four quarters, and operating cash flow was negative in all four years. On the other side, the debt-to-equity ratio is a low 10.2% and total equity at end-2025 was KRW 12.9 billion.

The disclosed slate of two 2026 titles and follow-on projects from 2027 is the basis for a business recovery case, but the company itself says launch timing may change.

The November 18, 2026 shareholder agreement and the KRW 11.5 billion third-party issuance are variables that will reshape control and the capital structure. The market capitalization below the KRW 20 billion threshold is also a listing-requirement fact to weigh.

The next checkpoints are payment completion on December 30, 2026, revenue and operating margin in the 2026 annual business report, and the Project AR launch.
