G2Power: Nuclear and ESS Expansion Amid Sharp Quarterly Swings

G2Power has expanded from its leading position in publicly procured switchgear into nuclear-grade switchgear and liquid-cooled ESS, driving a clear earnings recovery through 2025, even as 2026 has shown a seasonal pullback following its fourth-quarter peak.

02

Business structure

G2Power was founded in 2010 and listed on KOSDAQ in April 2022 as a smart-grid solutions company built around its condition-monitoring-and-diagnosis (CMD) technology.

Its core products are switchgear, solar power systems (including EPC work), energy storage systems (ESS), and power conditioning systems (PCS), each fitted with CMD sensors that detect partial-discharge and other failure or fire signs in advance.

According to a brokerage report, as of 2024 the revenue mix was about 63% switchgear and 26% solar, with more than 70% of sales coming from public-sector procurement. The switchgear business in particular is reported to have held the top market share in the public-tender channel for several years.

Its customer base is centered on central and local government bodies and state enterprises, while the company has recently been expanding into private-sector and overseas channels.

One new growth pillar is nuclear-grade switchgear; according to Korea Investment & Securities, backed by KEPIC certification and Korea Hydro & Nuclear Power's Q-Class supplier qualification, G2Power won a KRW 43 billion high-voltage switchgear order for the Shin-Hanul 3&4 nuclear plant in February 2025.

A second pillar is a new AI-based liquid-immersion-cooled ESS product, developed by 2025 after the company was selected to lead a government-backed R&D project in 2024.

The public-tender switchgear market features many small and mid-sized competitors, with Public Procurement Service certification acting as a barrier to entry, placing the company in a niche distinct from the large-scale power equipment market.

Through solar subsidiaries such as G2Energy, the company has also expanded into commercial plant operation and asset management.

04

Earnings analysis

Annual revenue rose for three straight years, from KRW 37.4 billion in 2022 to KRW 49.4 billion in 2023 (+32.2%), KRW 55.4 billion in 2024 (+12.2%), and KRW 77.4 billion in 2025 (+39.6%).

Operating profit swung from a KRW 204 million profit in 2022 to a KRW 945 million loss in 2023, returned to profit at KRW 3.67 billion in 2024, and rose 139.9% to KRW 8.81 billion in 2025. The operating margin improved markedly as well, from 0.5% in 2022 and -1.9% in 2023 to 6.6% in 2024 and 11.4% in 2025.

A brokerage attributed the 2025 margin improvement to operating leverage from revenue growth as well as a reversal of inventory valuation losses. Net profit attributable to owners bottomed at KRW 671 million in 2023 before rising for two straight years to KRW 4.21 billion in 2024 and KRW 5.55 billion in 2025.

Operating cash flow improved steadily, from -KRW 2.81 billion in 2022 to KRW 6.73 billion in 2023, KRW 6.91 billion in 2024, and KRW 18 billion in 2025.

On a quarterly basis, however, after peaking in Q4 2025 (revenue KRW 37.5 billion, operating profit KRW 6.3 billion, net profit attributable to owners KRW 3.3 billion), results declined for two consecutive quarters in Q1 2026 (revenue KRW 19.2 billion, operating profit KRW 2.27 billion, net profit KRW 1.89 billion) and Q2 2026 (revenue KRW 10.4 billion, operating profit KRW 413 million, net profit KRW 349 million).

Q2 2026 also fell year-over-year by 26.8% in revenue, 29.9% in operating profit, and 43.2% in net profit attributable to owners, leaving the trailing four-quarter sum (Q3 2025-Q2 2026) of net profit attributable to owners at KRW 7.48 billion.

The debt-to-equity ratio climbed from 45.3% in 2022 to 113.3% in 2025, indicating that financial leverage has grown alongside the company's top-line expansion.

05

Industry analysis

The power-equipment and smart-grid industry G2Power operates in is benefiting from South Korea's Distributed Energy Activation Act, effective June 2024, which has legalized regional distributed-power operation and virtual power plant (VPP) business models, broadening demand for ESS, switchgear, and inverters.

Policy-wise, the Smart Grid Expansion Roadmap 2030 is expanding smart-distribution-grid upgrades and VPP pilot projects centered on local governments and industrial complexes.

The public-procurement market is structured so that Public Procurement Service certifications (new-technology and excellent-product designations) act as entry barriers, leaving a small number of players dominating the top tier, and G2Power is reported to have held the top switchgear share in this channel for years.

In the nuclear segment, following the Shin-Hanul 3&4 order, a bid for the Hanbit 3&4 replacement project is underway; Kyobo Securities noted in a February 2026 report that the project is valued at over KRW 70 billion and that participation in overseas projects via a KHNP-led consortium is also possible.

Externally, the late-September 2026 US-Korea investment framework, which included plans for up to eight large nuclear reactors in the United States, drew attention to potential benefits across Korea's nuclear supply chain.

However, the Ministry of Trade, Industry and Energy stated in a September 2026 explainer that the nuclear portion remains at a framework stage subject to feasibility validation in Q4 2026, with specifics not yet determined, meaning further confirmation is needed before this translates into orders.

In the ESS market, rising demand linked to data centers and renewable-energy integration is increasing interest in high-efficiency, high-reliability products such as liquid-immersion cooling, though G2Power's production scale remains relatively small compared with larger global ESS players.

06

Outlook

Through a corporate value-up plan resolved by its board on September 30, 2026, G2Power set a short-term target of 20%-plus annual revenue growth for 2026-2028 and a mid-to-long-term target of KRW 300 billion in revenue and KRW 33 billion in operating profit by 2030.

This followed an initial value-up plan disclosed in March 2026 that included stronger market communication, expanded shareholder returns, cost innovation and selective order-taking, and expansion into private and overseas markets.

On dividends, the company has disclosed that it qualifies as a high-dividend company under the Restriction of Special Taxation Act, with a 2025 payout ratio of 33.7%.

On order execution, Daishin Securities has reported that the Shin-Hanul 3&4 switchgear project is scheduled to be recognized in revenue in stages over three years starting in 2027, and the outcome of the Hanbit 3&4 replacement bid and other follow-on tenders could add further to the order backlog once confirmed.

For the new liquid-immersion-cooled ESS product, Kyobo Securities noted in a February 2026 report that order-taking could resume once KC certification is completed, while adding that actual contract timing would depend on market demand.

Korea Investment & Securities assessed in a report covering the second half of 2026 that, on an annual basis, switchgear is expected to remain the largest revenue contributor given the seasonality of second-half-weighted switchgear sales.

Whether the potential inclusion in supply chains for projects such as the US eight-reactor nuclear plan materializes will likely hinge on the outcome of the Q4 2026 feasibility validation.

Bull factors

Entry into the nuclear supply chain

Backed by KEPIC certification and KHNP Q-Class qualification, G2Power won a roughly KRW 43 billion high-voltage switchgear order for Shin-Hanul 3&4 in February 2025 and is also participating in follow-on bids such as the Hanbit 3&4 replacement project.

Participation in overseas projects via a KHNP-led consortium has also been discussed. The US-Korea investment framework announced in September 2026 includes plans for up to eight large nuclear reactors in the US, drawing wider attention to Korea's nuclear supply chain.

Multi-year improvement in earnings quality

Revenue rose for three straight years from 2022 to 2025, and operating profit swung from a loss in 2023 to profit in 2024 and rose again in 2025, lifting the operating margin to 11.4%. Operating cash flow also improved steadily, from a deficit in 2022 to KRW 18 billion in 2025.

A combination of operating leverage and a reversal of inventory valuation losses contributed to the 2025 profit expansion.

Simultaneous expansion of new businesses and shareholder returns

The company is broadening its new-business portfolio across liquid-cooled ESS, building-integrated photovoltaics (BIPV), and virtual power plants (VPP), with policy changes such as the Distributed Energy Activation Act underpinning demand for these segments.

Its 2026 value-up plan set a target of 20%-plus annual revenue growth for 2026-2028, and its 2025 payout ratio of 33.7% qualifies it as a high-dividend company under the Restriction of Special Taxation Act.

Bear factors

First-half slowdown following the Q4 peak

After revenue, operating profit, and net profit attributable to owners all peaked in Q4 2025, results declined for two consecutive quarters in Q1 and Q2 2026. Q2 2026 also fell year-over-year across all three metrics.

Korea Investment & Securities has pointed to seasonality concentrated in second-half switchgear sales, meaning quarter-to-quarter volatility is structurally large given the heavy weighting toward public procurement.

Rising financial leverage

The debt-to-equity ratio rose from 45.3% in 2022 to 113.3% in 2025. Simultaneous top-line growth and new-business investment appear to have increased working-capital needs. While operating cash flow has improved, the pace of change in the balance sheet itself warrants continued monitoring.

Overseas nuclear upside still at the framework stage

The US eight-reactor nuclear project was included in the September 2026 US-Korea investment framework, but the Ministry of Trade, Industry and Energy stated in a September explainer that specifics remain undetermined and will undergo feasibility validation in Q4 2026.

Multiple variables, including final site selection and actual order execution by Korean firms, remain before this translates into confirmed contracts.

10

Risk factors

Dependence on public procurement and sanction risk

With more than 70% of revenue coming from public procurement, according to a report by The Bell, the Public Procurement Service reissued a sanction—at a reduced duration—on a matter the company had previously won in court, and the company has signaled it will pursue legal action.

If a procurement-eligibility restriction actually takes effect, it could directly affect the company's core sales channel. The company has stated it intends to seek an injunction to limit any operational disruption.

Quarterly volatility from seasonality

After revenue and profit were concentrated in Q4 2025, results declined in both Q1 and Q2 2026, producing large swings between quarters. Given that public-sector budget execution tends to concentrate near year-end, this pattern could recur. The structure makes it difficult to draw trend conclusions from any single quarter's results.

Ownership and M&A-related uncertainty

According to a report by The Bell, G2Power's management-control sale negotiations were discontinued for a second straight year, following a similar outcome at the end of 2024. Over the same period, disclosures related to the discontinuation of trade with certain customers were amended multiple times.

Such governance-related uncertainty is a factor that can affect confidence in business stability independent of the share price.

12

Overall view

G2Power has built on its leading position in publicly procured switchgear by expanding into nuclear-grade switchgear and liquid-cooled ESS, delivering a turnaround in which revenue and profit improved together from 2022 to 2025.

However, results slowed for two consecutive quarters in Q1 and Q2 2026 after peaking in Q4 2025, re-highlighting the seasonality inherent in a business model weighted toward public procurement.

The Shin-Hanul 3&4 order, the follow-on Hanbit 3&4 and other bids, and discussions around the US eight-reactor project are cited as medium-to-long-term growth drivers, but many remain at the framework or bidding stage, requiring further confirmation before they show up in revenue.

A renewed Public Procurement Service sanction and two consecutive failed attempts to sell management control remain sources of uncertainty for both the core sales channel and the ownership structure. The rise in the debt-to-equity ratio to 113.3% is another point to watch alongside the company's top-line growth.

The next quarterly report and the outcome of the feasibility validation for the US nuclear project are likely to provide important clues for assessing the path ahead.
