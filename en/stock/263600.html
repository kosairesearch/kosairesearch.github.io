02

Business structure

Derkwoo Electronics, founded in 1992, is a specialist in precision press-formed mechanical parts. Its core products are stiffeners and brackets that protect smartphone camera modules, and mobile remains the center of revenue.

Mobile segment revenue in the first half of 2026 was KRW 83.6 billion, up 28.1% year on year, according to a August 2026 press report citing the Financial Supervisory Service's electronic disclosure system.

The automotive electronics segment, which includes automotive and secondary-battery parts, rose to KRW 8 billion from KRW 0.7 billion a year earlier. Its customers are global smartphone makers, and the mobile volume is concentrated among North American strategic customers.

Production sites are the mobile division in Gumi, Korea, the Vietnam subsidiary for mobile parts, the Mexico subsidiary for secondary-battery parts serving North American customers, and the Gimcheon plant for secondary-battery parts.

Its secondary-battery business supplies cap assemblies for 46-series cylindrical batteries made for a large domestic battery maker, with mass production at the Gimcheon plant since late 2025. The company says this business alone holds a backlog of about KRW 400 billion over the next five years.

It cites ultra-precision press processing at the 10-micrometer level as a strength, and says it has widened its gap with Chinese competitors on appearance standards and yield response despite their price pressure. Its automotive lineup includes ABS and EPS motors and battery management system parts.

A semiconductor-equipment subsidiary is developing 'E-Curtain', a foreign-matter control device for EUV lithography.

04

Earnings analysis

On a disclosed annual basis, revenue went from KRW 209 billion in 2022 to KRW 165.1 billion in 2023, down 21.0% year on year, and to KRW 161 billion in 2024, down 2.5%, before recovering to KRW 176.9 billion in 2025, up 9.9%. The largest year among 2022 to 2025 was 2022, and the smallest was 2024.

Profitability moved more sharply than sales. Operating profit fell from KRW 18.9 billion in 2022 to an operating loss of KRW 9.93 billion in 2023, a swing into deficit, and the operating loss continued in 2024 at KRW 992 million with a smaller shortfall.

Operating profit returned to KRW 1.58 billion in 2025, a swing back to profit, with an operating margin of 0.9%. Net profit attributable to controlling shareholders went from KRW 13.4 billion in 2022 to a net loss of KRW 8.55 billion in 2023, and then turned to a profit of KRW 6.91 billion in 2025.

Net profit including non-controlling interests was KRW 6.49 billion in 2025, a different figure from profit attributable to controlling shareholders. Operating cash flow was KRW 15.4 billion in 2025, up from KRW 3.42 billion in 2024.

Meanwhile the debt ratio rose from 84.3% in 2022 to 116.1% in 2025, so financial burden increased.

On a quarterly basis, first-quarter 2026 revenue was KRW 51.9 billion and operating profit was KRW 1.47 billion, while net profit attributable to controlling shareholders of KRW 17.4 billion in that quarter included a gain on the sale of an office building.

The company said that quarter's operating profit absorbed upfront costs for expanded second-half volume and R&D expenses for a semiconductor-process sensor at a subsidiary.

Second-quarter 2026 revenue was KRW 41.3 billion, down 20.4% from the prior quarter, with an operating loss of KRW 666 million, a swing into deficit.

The KRW 93.4 billion revenue and KRW 800 million operating profit reported in an August 2026 release as second-quarter figures match the first-half cumulative totals of KRW 93.1 billion and KRW 800 million, so they read as first-half numbers.

Third-quarter 2026 revenue was KRW 67.5 billion, up 25.8% year on year, but operating profit of KRW 1.37 billion fell 45.8% year on year. Net losses attributable to controlling shareholders were KRW 1.85 billion in the second quarter and KRW 3.21 billion in the third, two consecutive quarters of losses.

05

Industry analysis

The core end market is smartphone cameras. An earnings commentary on a company-monitoring site attributes rising demand for stiffeners and brackets to wider adoption of multi-camera setups and more high-pixel and OIS (optical image stabilization) models.

The global smartphone market was reported as recovering in 2026, and the company expects its second-half peak season to begin with new product launches at key customers. Smartphone parts are highly seasonal, though, and the 20.4% quarter-on-quarter drop in second-quarter 2026 revenue shows how sharp the swings can be.

On competition, the company acknowledged price pressure from Chinese rivals but cites stricter appearance standards and yield response as its differentiators.

In camera-module parts, its customer base overlaps with large listed players such as LG Innotek and Samsung Electro-Mechanics, but Derkwoo is a small to mid-sized specialist in press-formed mechanical parts, so its position differs.

In secondary batteries, the business was affected by slower electric-vehicle growth: a Gimcheon plant built in 2022 saw mass production delayed, and the company later shifted to a new form-factor product that began supplying key domestic customers, according to press reports. Reducing dependence on mobile through automotive and battery parts is the company's stated strategy.

Outlook

The company's stated schedule is as follows. Follow-on model supply of secondary-battery parts to overseas factories of key domestic and international customers is scheduled for late 2026.

The Mexico subsidiary's expansion budget is to rise from KRW 15 billion to about KRW 18 billion, with mass production of secondary-battery parts targeted for the first quarter of 2027. The Vietnam subsidiary plans to expand output to roughly three times its 2025 level.

The semiconductor venture E-Curtain is targeting simultaneous commercialization with new applications in 2027, after real-environment verification in 2026. The CEO has cited a 2026 revenue target of KRW 200 billion or more and KRW 400 billion by 2030, in a July 2026 interview with a technology daily.

Adding up the disclosed quarterly revenue through the third quarter of 2026 gives roughly KRW 160 billion, so reaching the KRW 200 billion target would require fourth-quarter revenue of roughly KRW 40 billion or more, by our arithmetic.

In August 2026 the company guided that third-quarter revenue and operating profit would both exceed the prior-year quarter. Revenue rose 25.8% year on year, consistent with that guidance, but operating profit fell 45.8%, so the guidance was only partly borne out.

The next checkpoints are therefore fourth-quarter 2026 revenue and operating margin, and how far secondary-battery sales can offset the seasonal swings in mobile.

08

Bull factors

Recovery in mobile volume

Mobile segment revenue in the first half of 2026 rose 28.1% year on year to KRW 83.6 billion. The company said it secured volume up 20 to 30% from the prior year for 2026, and plans to expand Vietnam output to about three times the 2025 level.

Strong iPhone shipments and demand from North American strategic customers were cited as supporting first-quarter 2026 sales.

Secondary-battery parts with a contracted base

The company has mass-produced cap assemblies for 46-series cylindrical batteries at its Gimcheon plant since late 2025. It says the battery-parts business alone holds a backlog of about KRW 400 billion over the next five years. The Mexico subsidiary is in customer approval for mass production targeted for the first quarter of 2027.

Turn to profit and better operating cash

Operating profit in 2025 was KRW 1.58 billion, a swing to profit, and profit attributable to controlling shareholders was KRW 6.91 billion, also a turn to profit. Operating cash flow rose to KRW 15.4 billion in 2025 from KRW 3.42 billion in 2024.

Operating profit of KRW 1.47 billion in the first quarter of 2026 was positive, so the direction of earnings improvement is visible.

09

Bear factors

Volatility in quarterly results

Losses that continued through the second and third quarters of 2026 widened the swings in quarterly results. Third-quarter operating profit of KRW 1.37 billion fell 45.8% year on year, at odds with the company's August 2026 outlook for growth over the prior-year quarter.

First-quarter net profit of KRW 17.4 billion included a property-sale gain, so quarterly earnings quality should be read net of one-off items.

Rising financial burden

The debt ratio rose from 84.3% in 2022 to 116.1% in 2025. Total liabilities at the end of 2025 of KRW 126.8 billion exceeded total equity of KRW 109.2 billion.

The company said sale proceeds would go to balance-sheet improvement and capital spending, but with the Mexico expansion and R&D spending continuing, cash-flow management has become more important.

Thin operating margin and price competition

The operating margin was 0.9% in 2025, after -6.0% in 2023 and -0.6% in 2024. The company itself acknowledged price pressure from Chinese rivals. Because mobile volume is concentrated among North American customers, pricing leverage could be limited, which is a bearish factor.

Risk factors

Customer concentration and seasonality

Mobile parts are tightly linked to the product cycles of a few North American strategic customers and global smartphone makers. Second-quarter 2026 revenue fell 20.4% from the prior quarter, and in the third quarter volume rose but operating profit fell. Changes in customer orders can move quarterly results quickly.

Timing of new-business mass production and validation

A Gimcheon plant built in 2022 saw mass production delayed when electric-vehicle growth slowed. The Mexico secondary-battery mass production is targeted for the first quarter of 2027, and E-Curtain is targeting commercialization in 2027. Any slippage would delay the point at which the contracted backlog turns into revenue.

Financial burden and reliance on one-off gains

First-quarter 2026 net profit included a gain on the sale of an office building, and press reports gave differing figures of about KRW 24 billion for the gain and about KRW 21 billion for the full-year disposal gain. With the debt ratio at 116.1%, financial headroom is limited unless earnings durability is confirmed. Earnings-based analysis is possible only after stripping out such one-off items.

12

Overall view

Derkwoo Electronics is in the process of expanding from mobile camera parts into secondary-battery parts and semiconductor-equipment parts. On a disclosed basis, operating profit in 2025 was KRW 1.58 billion, a turn to profit after the losses of 2023 and 2024, and operating cash flow rose to KRW 15.4 billion.

However, net losses attributable to controlling shareholders continued in the second and third quarters of 2026, and third-quarter operating profit fell 45.8% year on year. First-quarter 2026 net profit included a property-sale gain, so the last four quarters' total is hard to read directly as operating strength.

Bullish factors are higher first-half mobile sales, mass production of 46-series cap assemblies with a backlog the company puts at about KRW 400 billion, and the Mexico expansion. Bearish factors are a thin operating margin, a debt ratio that rose to 116.1%, and price competition from Chinese rivals.

The next checkpoints are fourth-quarter 2026 revenue and operating profit, follow-on battery supply to overseas factories in late 2026, and mass production of battery parts in Mexico in the first quarter of 2027. The 2026 annual business report is expected around the end of March 2027.
