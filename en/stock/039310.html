Sejoong: Losses Widen Despite the KRW 33 Billion Maintenance Contract

02

Business structure

Sejoong is a B2B business services company that provides IT services and travel services to corporate clients. By segment, the S&C business accounted for 58%, the travel business for 33%, and the IT business division for 9% of second-quarter 2026 revenue.

Within the S&C business, a KRW 33 billion maintenance contract with a domestic IT company was signed on February 10, 2026, running through December 31, 2028. The contract amount equals roughly 88.46% of 2024 revenue of KRW 37.3 billion.

The travel business is known as a travel agency focused on corporate clients, so its demand structure differs from large consumer-focused travel firms such as Hanatour Service and Modetour Network.

The IT business division accounts for 9% of revenue, and company profile materials describe the company as operating its own IDC facility. The PLM business is summarized as continuing to grow even as its market goes through a transition period.

Competitive dynamics differ by segment: maintenance work overlaps with large IT service providers, while travel overlaps with agencies that handle corporate travel. The revenue mix therefore leans on a large single contract and corporate demand.

04

Earnings analysis

On an annual basis, 2022 was the largest year of 2022 to 2025 with revenue of KRW 42.1 billion, and 2025 was the smallest at KRW 35.6 billion. Revenue fell 13.8% in 2023 to KRW 36.3 billion, rose 2.8% in 2024 to KRW 37.3 billion, and fell 4.5% in 2025.

Operating results moved from an operating loss of KRW 2.62 billion in 2022 to an operating profit of KRW 1.18 billion in 2023, a swing to profit, before falling 64.6% to KRW 418 million in 2024 and turning into an operating loss of KRW 1.03 billion in 2025.

The operating margin ran at -6.2% in 2022, 3.3% in 2023, 1.1% in 2024 and -2.9% in 2025. Net income attributable to controlling shareholders rose 929.2% to KRW 13.8 billion in 2023, with total net income of the same amount, which suggests a small noncontrolling interest effect.

That profit was far larger than the KRW 1.18 billion operating profit, so non-operating items deserve separate attention. In 2024, controlling-shareholder net income fell 86.8% to KRW 1.81 billion, and in 2025 the company recorded a net loss attributable to controlling shareholders of KRW 2.75 billion.

Operating cash flow went from negative KRW 7.91 billion in 2023 and negative KRW 1 billion in 2024 to positive KRW 2.06 billion in 2025. On a quarterly basis, the company posted an operating loss of KRW 405 million in the third quarter of 2025 and KRW 650 million in the fourth quarter.

The first quarter of 2026 revenue of KRW 24.1 billion, up 264.6% from the prior quarter, came with a small operating profit of KRW 33.88 million, a swing to profit, and its timing overlaps with the start of the February 2026 contract.

Second-quarter 2026 revenue of KRW 7.39 billion, down 69.3% quarter on quarter, produced an operating loss of KRW 28.46 million, and third-quarter revenue of KRW 7.25 billion came with an operating loss of KRW 1.7 billion. Controlling-shareholder net losses over the last four quarters total KRW 4.76 billion.

05

Industry analysis

Sejoong's business draws on three demand pools: corporate IT maintenance, corporate travel and PLM-related work. The PLM market is described as continuing to grow even while it goes through a transition phase. The PLM growth does not appear large enough to offset the overall revenue decline seen in recent quarters.

Corporate travel demand tends to track business trip and event budgets, so it is sensitive to economic conditions and corporate spending plans. In IT maintenance, large system integrators tend to hold long-term contracts, so a mid-sized player winning a large deal is relatively unusual.

Sejoong strengthened its presence in this market with a single KRW 33 billion contract signed in February 2026. Heavy dependence on that one contract, however, could increase quarterly revenue volatility if new orders are not secured after it.

The travel business depends more on repeat corporate clients than on individual travelers, which differs from large consumer-focused agencies such as Hanatour Service. On the cycle, the company appears to be in a phase where it must rebuild its order flow after a large contract. Relative to peers, the differences show up more in contract concentration and profitability than in scale.

Outlook

The clearest base is the KRW 33 billion maintenance contract signed on February 10, 2026, which runs through December 31, 2028. Because the contract's start overlaps with the first-quarter 2026 revenue spike, how evenly contract revenue is recognized across quarters will shape the trajectory ahead.

With revenue back in the KRW 7 billion range in the second and third quarters of 2026, the key question is how much revenue outside the contract can hold at that level. Since the third-quarter operating loss widened to KRW 1.7 billion, the behavior of fixed costs in the cost structure needs to be tracked.

The S&C business generates most of revenue, so its new order disclosures are a central checkpoint. In travel, the question is whether corporate demand recovers, while the IT business division contributes a small share of total revenue.

The company's annual reports and timely disclosures will show the direction of its business. Operating cash flow turned positive in 2025, so whether cash generation continues through the 2026 quarters is another point to follow.

Revenue declines and four quarters of net losses

Revenue fell in the second and third quarters of 2026, two consecutive quarterly declines. Annual revenue in 2025 was KRW 35.6 billion, down 4.5% year on year. Controlling-shareholder net losses over the last four quarters total KRW 4.76 billion, which weighs on equity.

Reliance on one contract and inconsistent earnings

Quarterly revenue dropped sharply after the first-quarter 2026 peak, showing how volatile contract-driven revenue can be. On an annual basis, profit moved from a 2023 profit to a 2024 decline and a 2025 loss, so earnings have not built up steadily.

Risk factors

Corporate travel demand risk

The travel business depends on corporate demand tied to business trip and event budgets. If companies cut their spending plans, travel revenue could decline in step.

The travel business accounted for 33% of second-quarter 2026 revenue, so a pullback in corporate demand could also affect the company's overall revenue trend. Because the segment relies on repeat transactions with corporate clients, any reduction in trade with major client companies should also be monitored.

PLM growth slowdown risk

The PLM business is summarized as continuing to grow even while its market goes through a transition period. If that transition lengthens, the pace of growth could slow and PLM revenue may not offset the declining revenue elsewhere. PLM-related growth does not appear large enough to offset the overall revenue decline.

The timing of PLM demand recovery and the flow of new orders may therefore become key variables for improving the earnings structure.

Cash flow risk

Operating cash flow was negative KRW 7.91 billion in 2023 and negative KRW 1 billion in 2024, two consecutive negative years. It turned positive at KRW 2.06 billion in 2025, but whether that continues is still to be seen. If cash generation weakens while losses persist, financial flexibility would shrink.
