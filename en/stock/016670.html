02

Business structure

Dmoa's core business is distributing software to corporate and education customers in Korea. The company was founded in December 1982 as Samsan Computer Service and listed on KOSDAQ in April 1993. It acts as a distributor for Microsoft Korea, Adobe and AhnLab, handling software sales and technical support.

In 2022 it discontinued its logistics and brand businesses and concentrated on the software segment. Software distribution is now the center of the business, sitting between overseas software vendors and Korean corporate and education buyers.

According to FnGuide, standalone revenue for the first nine months of 2025 rose 18.4% year on year and operating profit rose 130.0%. The company attributed the improvement to higher sales of key products, a wider partner network and stronger demand for remote work and education.

A distributor's competitiveness depends on the breadth of its vendor lineup and its technical support capability, and the Korean market has both large distributors and smaller resellers competing.

Because revenue depends on vendor contract terms, changes in a product line or a vendor relationship can pass through to sales quickly. With the logistics and brand businesses gone, software distribution is the only remaining business, which keeps the structure simple.

04

Earnings analysis

On a reported annual basis, revenue fell from KRW 36.7 billion in 2022 to KRW 31.4 billion in 2023 (down 14.6%), KRW 26.6 billion in 2024 (down 15.1%) and KRW 23.1 billion in 2025 (down 13.4%).

Revenue declined for three consecutive years from 2023 to 2025, with 2022 the largest year and 2025 the smallest in the 2022 to 2025 period. Profitability improved while revenue shrank.

Operating profit rose from KRW 1.71 billion in 2022 to KRW 2.91 billion in 2023 (up 70.0%), fell to KRW 2.14 billion in 2024 (down 26.5%), and then reached KRW 4.05 billion in 2025 (up 89.4%), the largest year in the period. The operating margin moved from 4.7% in 2022 to 9.3% in 2023, 8.0% in 2024 and 17.6% in 2025.

Profit attributable to controlling shareholders narrowed from a net loss of KRW 18.9 billion in 2022 to a net loss of KRW 2.5 billion in 2023, turned to a profit of KRW 3.47 billion in 2024, and rose to KRW 11.1 billion in 2025, up 220.6%.

Net income including non-controlling interests was also KRW 11.1 billion in 2025. A net profit larger than operating profit can reflect non-operating items, and the line-item amounts can be checked in the 2026 annual report.

On a quarterly basis, revenue was KRW 4.49 billion in the third quarter of 2025, KRW 5.93 billion in the fourth quarter (up 32.1% from the prior quarter), and KRW 8.17 billion in the first quarter of 2026 (up 37.8%), before falling to KRW 6.72 billion in the second quarter (down 17.7%) and rising slightly to KRW 6.78 billion in the third quarter (up 0.8%).

Operating profit rose from KRW 98.14 million in the third quarter of 2025 to KRW 3.74 billion in the first quarter of 2026, then fell to KRW 1.79 billion in the second quarter (down 52.1%) and recovered to KRW 2.13 billion in the third quarter (up 18.9%).

Profit attributable to controlling shareholders was KRW 4.69 billion in the fourth quarter of 2025, KRW 3.25 billion in the first quarter of 2026, KRW 862 million in the second quarter and KRW 4.6 billion in the third quarter, totaling KRW 13.4 billion over the last four quarters.

Because quarterly profit is concentrated in a few quarters, one quarter alone is not enough to define the trend.

05

Industry analysis

Software distribution in Korea is the intermediate step that sells overseas vendors' products to domestic corporations and institutions and adds technical support. Demand moves with corporate IT spending, the shift to cloud, the spread of remote work and digital transformation in education.

Dmoa's revenue declined for three consecutive years from 2023 to 2025, while its operating margin moved from 9.3% and 8.0% to 17.6% in 2025. This can be read as a change in profit structure after the company narrowed its business to software distribution, rather than a result of scale.

A single year of 2025 data is not enough to confirm a trend, and the decline in quarterly revenue from KRW 8.17 billion in the first quarter of 2026 to KRW 6.78 billion in the third quarter also needs to be weighed. On competition, a smaller distributor may have limited bargaining power in vendor contract terms.

A smaller organization, however, can manage relationships with specific vendors more closely. Education-related demand can shift with school calendars and budget execution timing, so separating structural from temporary causes of quarterly swings matters.

Industry indicators alone do not settle where the cycle stands, and the company's revenue path and vendor relationships need to be read together.

Outlook

The event most directly tied to the company's outlook is the capital-raising attempt in the first half of 2026. On April 24, 2026, following a board resolution, Dmoa filed a registration statement for a rights-based offering with a public offering of unsubscribed shares, aimed at funding working capital.

The Financial Supervisory Service then requested a corrected filing, and the company said this would create a considerable gap between the planned payment date and the planned use of funds. Dmoa withdrew the offering by disclosure on June 25, 2026.

In March 2026, Asis No. 1 Investment Partnership, a major shareholder, acquired 2.6 million common shares on March 13, and disclosed the transaction on March 20. Following that acquisition, the total number of issued shares was reported as 10,194,234.

On the business side, the key question is whether the third-quarter 2026 revenue of KRW 6.78 billion and operating profit of KRW 2.13 billion carry into the fourth quarter.

The first quarter of 2026, with operating profit of KRW 3.74 billion, was the strongest quarter in the recent series, so any change from that level is worth tracking. Whether the withdrawn capital raise is revived will be visible in future registration statements and disclosures. The full-year 2026 results will be confirmed in the annual report, the next periodic filing.

Margin gains while revenue shrank point to the effect of narrowing the business.

Earnings base from business focus

Demand from remote work and education was also presented as a support factor. With the logistics and brand businesses gone, the remaining operation is simpler to manage.

All of the equity at year-end 2025 belonged to controlling shareholders, which keeps the capital structure simple.

Risk factors

Vendor contract and partner dependence

Dmoa's revenue depends on distribution agreements with vendors including Microsoft Korea, Adobe and AhnLab. Changes in contract terms or product lineups could affect both sales and margins. If a few vendors account for a large share, a change in those relationships could move overall results.

Because the partner network was cited as a driver of the improvement, whether this structure holds is important.

Earnings volatility and revenue decline

Revenue has fallen for three consecutive years from 2023, and it dropped 17.7% in the second quarter of 2026. Operating profit has swung sharply from quarter to quarter, rising from KRW 98.14 million in the third quarter of 2025 to KRW 3.74 billion in the first quarter of 2026 before declining.

If profit is concentrated in a few quarters, one-off effects in a single quarter could drive the annual figures. Whether profit capacity can be maintained on a smaller sales base needs to be confirmed.

Funding and shareholding structure

With the working-capital rights offering withdrawn, the way the company secures funds remains open. A disclosure that a major shareholder acquired 2.6 million common shares in March 2026 shows the shareholding structure is shifting.

For a small-cap stock, shareholding changes can affect trading and the share price, so the disclosures need to be read together. If the capital raise is revived, the terms and timing of new share issuance would directly affect shareholder value.

Overall view

Revenue, however, fell from KRW 36.7 billion in 2022 to KRW 23.1 billion in 2025 and declined for three consecutive years.
