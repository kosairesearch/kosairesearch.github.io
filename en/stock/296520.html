02

Business structure

Gaia is a specialist brand distributor in premium baby products, toys and kidult goods. According to an FnGuide summary, it runs four divisions: premium baby goods, Stokke, kidult and toys.

The baby-goods division acts as a master distributor of premium products, the Stokke division operates consigned department-store counters, the kidult division diversifies revenue through direct-to-consumer sales, and the toy division expands channels through global brand partnerships.

The company website cites a network of more than 70 stores nationwide. No disclosed revenue split by division could be confirmed, so the mix is described only qualitatively; the company's own profile identifies baby products as the core business.

Sales run through wholesale and retail across online, discount stores, offline retailers and department stores. The company listed on the KONEX market on June 20, 2023. In May 2025 it joined the group of the Kosdaq-listed Ggumbi, and it states a goal of transferring to the Kosdaq market.

With its acquisition of a Kosdaq-listed children's footwear company, Ggumbi said it secured 134 offline channels in total, combining 52 from Gaia with 82 from the acquired company.

In the competitive landscape, large retailers such as Lotte Shopping, Shinsegae and E-Mart are both venue owners and sales-route counterparties, so Gaia's role is closer to a niche brand distributor and consignment operator. Its Philippine subsidiary suspended operations in December 2024, leaving overseas activity scaled back.

Recent trends

Per the company's IR page, revenue was 37.65149 billion won in 2022, 31.51996 billion won in 2023, 38.00733 billion won in 2024 and 41.97243 billion won in 2025. In the filed results, 2025 brought an operating loss of 3.536123 billion won, against an operating profit of 743.892 million won in 2024.

The 2025 net loss was 3.952451 billion won, and gross profit fell to 14.596431 billion won from 16.159583 billion won in 2024. Because revenue rose while gross profit fell, the deterioration appears to have shown up first in cost or sales mix.

At the end of 2025, total equity was 2.949 billion won, the equity ratio was 23.73% and the debt ratio was 321.34%. The quick ratio of 50.04% also points to limited short-term liquidity.

A report citing a Ggumbi Group press release put first-quarter 2026 revenue at 11.5 billion won and net income at 870 million won. Results for the second quarter onward could not be confirmed at the time of writing.

The share price moved from 311 won on March 31, 2026 to 580 won on April 8, and an exchange notice on June 15 listed the stock, then under an investment warning designation, for a possible trading halt. A similar notice followed on June 24.

FnGuide showed a 52-week high of 1,848 won and a low of 256 won as of June 9, 2026. As of October 27, 2026 the price is 261 won, down 13.00% from the previous close, with a market capitalization of 1.2 billion won and trading value of 47,871 won.

04

Outlook

The key near-term question is whether the 2025 operating loss was one-off or reflects a structural margin decline. First-quarter 2026 produced net income on a reported basis, but a full-year return to profit cannot be confirmed before the annual report.

The Hasbro contract lets the company sell Transformers, Play-Doh and Nerf through discount stores, specialty toy stores and an online brand store. New brand contracts, however, typically require inventory and marketing outlays first, so the timing of any profit contribution is uncertain.

Ggumbi said it will step by step expand department-store and offline channels in collaboration with Gaia and streamline purchasing, logistics and marketing.

On April 8, 2026 the company granted stock options on 138,280 common shares to 32 directors and employees at an exercise price of 500 won. This links management pay to performance, though exercise would also increase the share count.

A co-CEO structure under Won-seok Na and Bo-seon Park was put in place on March 4, 2026. A Kosdaq transfer is a stated company goal, but its timing and eligibility could not be confirmed.

Baby-goods demand faces a structural constraint from declining births, so a higher share of premium products and a wider age range through toys and kidult goods are the main variables for revenue stability.

On the balance sheet, equity of about 2.949 billion won is thin, and further losses could raise the need for new capital.

05

Bull factors

Combination with group distribution

The company has been part of the Kosdaq-listed Ggumbi Group since May 2025. Ggumbi said it will step by step strengthen online sales and expand department-store and offline channels. The acquisition of the children's footwear company brings the group's offline channels to 134.

Sharing the parent's purchasing, logistics and marketing capabilities could leave room for better selling-cost efficiency.

Hasbro contract and toy portfolio

An official Korean distributorship with Hasbro adds Transformers, Play-Doh and Nerf to the lineup. The company has kept widening its domestic and overseas brand portfolio since joining the Ggumbi Group.

Global toy brands can address both kidult and children's demand, partly offsetting the reliance of a pure baby-goods business on birth numbers.

Revenue growth and first-quarter profit

Revenue in 2025 was 41.97243 billion won, up 10.4% from the prior year. Revenue in 2024 had also grown by double digits over 2023, based on the IR figures. A report citing a Ggumbi Group press release put first-quarter 2026 revenue at 11.5 billion won and net income at 870 million won.

If top-line growth and quarterly profit are confirmed, there is room to discuss an improvement in operating margin.

Bear factors

2025 loss and balance-sheet strain

The 2025 operating loss was 3.536123 billion won and the net loss was 3.952451 billion won. The debt ratio rose to 321.34% from 129.15% in 2024. Total equity shrank to 2.949 billion won, leaving little cushion against further losses. The interest coverage ratio is also in negative territory.

Margin pressure

Gross profit in 2025 fell to 14.596431 billion won from 16.159583 billion won in 2024. A decline in gross profit in a year of rising revenue suggests pricing pressure or a cost-structure issue. The operating margin was -8.42% in 2025, down from 1.96% in 2024. Start-up costs for new brands could delay a recovery in near-term profitability.

Extreme illiquidity and price volatility

Market capitalization is 1.2 billion won and trading value on October 27, 2026 was only 47,871 won. In June 2026 the stock was listed for a possible trading halt while under an investment warning designation. The price fell 13.00% in a single session, showing how small trades can move it sharply. In such conditions, gaps in the order book can make it hard to transact at a desired price.

Risk factors

Macro

Baby-goods demand is exposed to the structural decline in births. The FnGuide summary notes that the premium segment is gaining share despite low birth rates, but that also means demand for higher-priced items could weaken first if consumer sentiment softens.

Reliance on department-store channels means shifts in retail conditions and the consumption cycle can feed directly into revenue.

Market

The KONEX market has a limited set of participants, and there can be days with no trades. In June 2026 the stock faced an investment warning designation and a trading-halt notice. Small orders can widen quotes sharply, which lowers the reliability of price signals. A Kosdaq transfer is a goal only, and its timing and eligibility are unconfirmed.

Business and financial

The largest shareholder changed from Won-seok Na to Ggumbi in May 2025, so group strategy and funding support strongly shape the company's direction. Overseas brand contracts may change in scope with expiry or revised terms.

With thin equity, further losses could require fresh capital or more borrowing, and the exercise of stock options would add to the share count.

08

Overall view

As the distribution affiliate of the Ggumbi Group, Gaia is widening its portfolio of toy and baby-goods brands and its offline channels. In the filed results, 2025 revenue rose but the company recorded an operating loss and a net loss, and its debt ratio increased.

The reported first-quarter 2026 profit is a positive signal, but whether profitability recovers for the full year must be confirmed by later filings. The Hasbro contract and group synergies are business-side improvement factors, while cost burdens and margin pressure are offsetting considerations.

A market capitalization of 1.2 billion won and trading value of 47,871 won show that the market's price signal is very weak. This report is for information purposes only, and judgment is left to the reader.
