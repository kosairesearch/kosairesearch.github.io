Earnings Recovery Amid Robot-Defense Pivot

02

Business structure

Woorim Power Train Solution started in 1998 as a sole proprietorship called Woorim Machinery, converted to a corporation in 2000, listed on KOSDAQ in April 2009, and changed its name to its current form in September 2021.

Headquartered in Changwon, Gyeongsangnam-do, it is a gearbox (reducer) specialist that handles design, development, production, testing and sales in-house.

Its core products are large industrial gearboxes and reducers for steel-mill facilities and transmissions for construction equipment, and it has secured a large number of domestic and overseas customers including Hyundai Steel and POSCO.

Based on second-quarter 2026 revenue, steel-facility and industrial gearboxes account for 53% of sales while construction-equipment, robot and defense gearboxes account for 47%.

As new growth engines, the company is building out robotic precision reducers and ultra-precision gearboxes for defense and aerospace, and it was selected by the Ministry of SMEs and Startups as one of the 'Hidden Champions 100' in materials, parts and equipment.

It also has a track record of participating with Samsung Heavy Industries and others in a government-backed project to verify the performance and reliability of core drive components (servo-motor reducers) for manufacturing robots.

A company official has said it continues to supply ultra-precision robot reducers to Boston Dynamics in the United States, though it has not yet reached the stage of large-scale mass-production supply.

The domestic gear and power-transmission manufacturing industry is dominated by small and medium-sized firms, which limits the capacity for R&D investment across the sector.

04

Earnings analysis

Annual revenue fell 16.1% from KRW 68.3 billion in 2022 to KRW 57.3 billion in 2023, rose 25.1% to KRW 71.8 billion in 2024, then fell 20.4% again to KRW 57.1 billion in 2025, the smallest figure in the 2022-2025 span.

Operating profit dropped 54.0% from KRW 5.38 billion in 2022 to KRW 2.47 billion in 2023, swung to an operating loss of KRW 1.6 billion in 2024, and then turned positive again at KRW 1.19 billion in 2025.

Net income attributable to controlling shareholders likewise swung from a loss of KRW 7.77 billion in 2024 to a profit of KRW 2.39 billion in 2025. The company has cited weak demand stemming from a downturn in upstream industries such as steelmaking as the background for the weak 2024-2025 results.

By quarter, operating losses of KRW 1.29 billion in the second quarter of 2025 turned into an operating profit of KRW 3.54 billion in the third quarter, only to swing back to an operating loss of KRW 1.26 billion in the fourth quarter, underscoring large quarter-to-quarter swings.

An operating loss of KRW 369 million continued into the first quarter of 2026, though the loss narrowed, and the second quarter turned positive on both a sequential and year-on-year basis with revenue of KRW 14.2 billion and operating profit of KRW 960 million.

The sum of controlling-shareholder net income over the most recent four quarters (third quarter 2025 through second quarter 2026) reached KRW 5.63 billion, exceeding the full-year 2025 figure and suggesting some improvement in underlying earnings power since mid-2025.

The operating margin fell from 7.9% in 2022 to 4.3% in 2023 and -2.2% in 2024 before recovering to 2.1% in 2025, still below its earlier levels.

05

Industry analysis

The company's end markets span steel-plant equipment, construction machinery, robotics, and defense/aerospace, each with its own capex and demand cycle.

The traditional steel-facility and construction-equipment gearbox business is tied to global steel and construction conditions, so revenue swings widen whenever those sectors slow.

By contrast, the newer robot precision-reducer segment has drawn attention amid expectations for humanoid robot commercialization, and interest in the related supply chain increased after Hyundai Motor Group's Boston Dynamics demonstrated a physical electric Atlas unit at CES 2026 in the United States in January 2026.

According to reports, Hyundai Motor Group has finalized plans to deploy the robot from 2028 at its Metaplant America factory in the United States. Samsung Electronics and LG Electronics are also each stepping up their humanoid robot businesses, broadening interest in the domestic robot-parts supply chain.

Existing motion-control component makers such as SPG also compete in the robot precision-reducer segment, making the pace of entry into mass production and quality verification key differentiators among suppliers.

The defense and aerospace segment is seen as one where rising overseas orders for domestic defense firms and the localization of defense components are increasing opportunities for new entrants among parts suppliers.

Bull factors

A company official has said it is supplying precision reducers to Boston Dynamics, giving it a foothold in the robot value chain. Reports indicate Hyundai Motor Group has finalized plans to deploy Atlas in mass production from 2028, leaving open the possibility that related demand could materialize.

Operating cash flow jumped to KRW 18.3 billion in 2024 and remained positive at KRW 3.39 billion in 2025, preserving cash-generation capacity. This financial flexibility could support both new-business capex and dividend growth at the same time.

Earnings Recovery Trend

After operating profit turned positive at KRW 3.54 billion in the third quarter of 2025, the company posted another profit of KRW 960 million in the second quarter of 2026, with profitable quarters becoming more frequent.

The sum of controlling-shareholder net income over the most recent four quarters reached KRW 5.63 billion, exceeding the full-year 2025 figure and pointing to improving underlying earnings power since mid-2025. The company also unveiled a value-up plan in April 2026 targeting a payout ratio of at least 25%.

Risk factors

Industry-Cycle Risk

The traditional steel-facility and construction-equipment gearbox business is sensitive to end-market conditions, as shown by the 20.4% revenue decline in 2025, leaving it exposed to demand fluctuations.

The company itself has cited the downturn in upstream industries such as steelmaking as a background factor for weak results. A further slowdown in global steel or construction activity could again weigh on the revenue base of the existing business.

Customer and Business Concentration Risk

Robot precision-reducer revenue is still at an early stage dependent on a small number of customers such as Boston Dynamics, making it sensitive to changes in that customer's mass-production schedule or procurement strategy.

The defense and aerospace segment also remains limited in scale, so the delay or cancellation of a specific project could have a relatively large impact on results.

Share-Price and Supply-Demand Risk

A sharp share-price surge from December 2025 to January 2026 led the Korea Exchange to issue an advance notice of investment-warning designation, raising the possibility that theme-driven price volatility could recur. As a small-cap stock with low foreign and institutional ownership, liquidity may also be limited.

Overall view

After an operating loss and net loss in 2024, Woorim Power Train Solution turned profitable in 2025 with operating profit of KRW 1.19 billion and controlling-shareholder net income of KRW 2.39 billion, and in the second quarter of 2026 both revenue and operating profit improved versus the prior quarter and the year-earlier period.

Still, 2025 revenue fell 20.4% year on year to KRW 57.1 billion, and operating losses reappeared in the fourth quarter of 2025 and first quarter of 2026, so quarter-to-quarter volatility remains significant.

Robot precision reducers and defense/aerospace gearboxes are drawing growth expectations through cases such as the Boston Dynamics supply relationship, but by the company's own account these remain at a pre-mass-production stage.

On the balance sheet, the debt-to-equity ratio of 12.3% and total equity of KRW 82.9 billion at the end of 2025 look stable, and in April 2026 the company disclosed a value-up plan targeting a payout ratio of at least 25%.

From late 2025 into early 2026, sharp price swings tied to the robotics theme led to an advance notice of investment-warning designation, making supply-demand volatility another factor to watch.

Overall, with the cyclicality of the legacy business coexisting with the early-stage nature of new businesses, upcoming quarterly results and any new-business order disclosures are likely to be the next points of reference.
