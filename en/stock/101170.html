02

Business structure

Woorim Power Train Solution makes large industrial gearboxes and construction-equipment transmissions, and has recently expanded into ultra-precision gearboxes for robots, defense and aviation. Its 2023 revenue mix was 42.4% industrial reducers for steel-mill equipment and 57.6% construction-equipment transmissions.

The company runs two plants: Plant 1 makes large gearboxes for energy and plant customers, while Plant 2 handles construction-equipment products and reducers for aviation, defense and robots.

One brokerage, Hana Securities, estimated that construction equipment plus defense and robots accounted for about 65% of revenue in Q3 2025, with steel-mill equipment and industrial gearboxes at about 35%.

A corporate data provider instead tallied Q2 2026 revenue at 53% for steel-mill equipment and industrial gearboxes and 47% for construction-equipment, robot and defense gearboxes. The two figures use different base periods and segment definitions, so they are hard to compare directly.

Customers named in coverage include John Deere, SMS, HD Hyundai, Hanwha Aerospace, Hyundai Rotem and Doosan Enerbility. In defense, the company supplies reducers for self-propelled ferry equipment and unmanned reconnaissance vehicles to defense makers including Hanwha Aerospace.

In robotics, it supplies ultra-precision reducers to Boston Dynamics, and a company official said in November 2025 that this was not yet at mass-production volume.

04

Earnings analysis

Annual revenue went from KRW 68.3 billion in 2022 to KRW 57.3 billion in 2023 (down 16.1% year on year), rose to KRW 71.8 billion in 2024 (up 25.1%) and fell to KRW 57.1 billion in 2025 (down 20.4%). 2024 was the largest year and 2025 the smallest in the 2022 to 2025 period.

Operating margin slid from 7.9% in 2022 to 4.3% in 2023, then came in at -2.2% in 2024, when operating loss was KRW 1.6 billion. In 2025 operating profit of KRW 1.19 billion turned the result positive, with an operating margin of 2.1%.

Coverage attributed the 2025 revenue decline to weaker demand from steel and other upstream industries. Profit attributable to controlling shareholders was KRW 5.09 billion in 2022 and KRW 5.04 billion in 2023 (down 1.1%), swung to a net loss of KRW 7.77 billion in 2024, and recovered to KRW 2.39 billion in 2025.

At end-2025, total equity was KRW 82.9 billion, total liabilities KRW 10.2 billion, and the debt ratio 12.3%. Operating cash flow fell from KRW 18.3 billion in 2024 to KRW 3.39 billion in 2025.

On a quarterly basis, Q4 2025 posted an operating loss of KRW 1.26 billion, turning the quarter negative, and Q1 2026 kept an operating loss of KRW 369 million, with the loss narrowing.

Q2 2026 operating profit of KRW 960 million turned positive, and Q3 2026 operating profit of KRW 1.56 billion rose 63.0% from the prior quarter but fell 55.8% year on year. Q3 2026 revenue of KRW 13.8 billion was down 13.0% year on year.

Profit attributable to controlling shareholders over the last four quarters (Q4 2025 to Q3 2026) totaled KRW 305 million... specifically KRW 3.05 billion.

05

Industry analysis

Steel and construction equipment, the company's core markets, are both upstream industries whose demand swings sharply with capex and the economic cycle.

Hana Securities said in a February 2026 report that traditional upstream industries such as construction equipment were facing a slowdown in business conditions and weaker demand. The same report noted that defense spending comes in government-led project contracts, which could support medium-term growth.

It also said robot precision reducers face high technical barriers because of the accuracy they demand. The report argued that supply volumes could rise sharply if the timing of mass production by robot makers lines up.

In the robot-parts theme, Rainbow Robotics is cited as the sector's bellwether, and Woorim Power Train Solution, with its smaller market capitalization, tends to react more strongly to the same catalysts.

On defense and aviation demand, a company official said in October 2025 that growth had continued through the first half and was expected to rise over the full year. Localization of defense materials is also cited as a supportive backdrop.

Still, defense and robot revenue remains a limited share of total sales, so the steel and construction-equipment cycle still weighs heavily. Steel-mill equipment investment rises and falls with the steel cycle, so the timing of upstream recovery will set the pace of earnings recovery.

06

Outlook

A coverage report from fall 2025 said the company planned to finish expanding capacity to 300 aviation gearbox sets and 20,000 precision gearbox sets by year-end. A April 2026 report gave the completion date as end-2026, so the timelines in different reports do not match.

The largest variable for robotics is the size of a volume contract with Boston Dynamics, and related reports point to the moment such a contract is disclosed in figures as the turning point. Hyundai Motor Group has reportedly planned to deploy the Atlas robot at its US plant from 2028.

Given that timeline, meaningful robot revenue may take time to show up in the books. In its April 2026 value-up plan, the company said it would keep a dividend payout ratio of at least 25% and keep increasing dividends.

The same plan named a higher share of precision reducers for robots and high value-added products for the energy sector as a route to better profitability. Expanding overseas market entry was also presented as a medium-term priority.

The next checkpoint is the 2026 annual report, which will show how far full-year operating margin moved from 2.1% in 2025 and how the robot and defense revenue share changed.

08

Bull factors

Profit Run Continues

Operating profit for 2025 was KRW 1.19 billion, turning positive, and Q2 and Q3 2026 operating profit of KRW 960 million and KRW 1.56 billion stayed in the black. Q3 2026 operating profit rose 63.0% from the prior quarter. Earnings strength has recovered from the KRW 1.6 billion operating loss in 2024.

Robot and Defense Mix Rising

Hana Securities estimated that construction equipment and defense and robot segments made up about 65% of revenue in Q3 2025. Defense projects are generated steadily as government-led programs, which supports the medium-term growth case. The company also says it continues to supply robot reducers to Boston Dynamics.

Shareholder Return Policy Codified

In its April 2026 value-up plan, the company said it would keep a payout ratio of at least 25% and keep dividends growing. It described a high-dividend policy of raising dividends by at least 10% each year. Total dividends for 2025 rose from the prior year.

Bear factors

Core Upstream Demand Weak

Revenue fell 20.4% year on year to KRW 57.1 billion in 2025, and Q3 2026 revenue was down 13.0% year on year. Coverage attributed weaker steel-related demand to a downturn in upstream industries. A brokerage also described construction-equipment conditions as slowing.

Limited Robot Revenue Visibility

A company official said in November 2025 that supply to Boston Dynamics was not yet at mass-production volume. With the Atlas plant deployment planned for 2028, large robot revenue may take time to appear. Until robot demand shows up in results, recovery in the core business's profitability remains the key.

Small and Volatile Earnings Base

Profit attributable to controlling shareholders totaled KRW 3.05 billion over the last four quarters, a small base. Q3 2026 profit attributable to controlling shareholders of KRW 837 million fell 75.5% year on year. The company posted a net loss attributable to controlling shareholders of KRW 7.77 billion in 2024.

Risk factors

Share Price Volatility and Flows

The share price has reacted strongly to theme catalysts such as the robot demonstration. A high share of retail trading means flows can swing the price widely. A short correction followed a surge in early January 2026. When earnings and theme catalysts diverge, price swings can widen.

Delay in Volume Contracts

Large robot revenue may only show up in results after a volume contract with Boston Dynamics is disclosed. If the contract timing slips, the time for robot expectations to turn into results also lengthens. Robot revenue is currently small relative to steel and construction-equipment revenue.

Governance and Voting

At the March 2026 annual shareholder meeting, the director compensation limit was left undecided, with voting restrictions applied to special interested parties. The largest shareholder and related parties hold 56.30%. The voting structure and the controlling stake's influence are points to watch for minority holders.

12

Overall view

Woorim Power Train Solution swung from a loss to a KRW 1.19 billion operating profit in 2025 and stayed profitable in Q2 and Q3 2026. However, revenue fell both for full-year 2025 and in Q3 2026, so the profit recovery looks more like better profitability than revenue growth.

The business mix is shifting from steel and construction equipment toward defense and robots. Estimates of that mix still conflict, with about 65% in Q3 2025 in one analysis and 47% in Q2 2026 in another, so the trend is hard to pin down.

Robot reducer supply to Boston Dynamics continues but is not yet at mass-production volume, and the Atlas plant deployment is planned for 2028. Profit attributable to controlling shareholders over the last four quarters totaled KRW 3.05 billion, so earnings metrics can move sharply on a single quarter.

The next checkpoints are the operating margin in the 2026 annual report, completion of the capacity expansion, and whether a volume contract with Boston Dynamics is disclosed.
