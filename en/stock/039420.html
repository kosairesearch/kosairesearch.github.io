02

Business structure

KL-Net is a logistics IT company that combines e-logistics services built on electronic data interchange (EDI) with system integration (SI), solution sales and IT outsourcing. The company is the dedicated operator of the national Comprehensive Logistics Information Network and cites 29 years of experience in the field.

Revenue is split into four segments: value-added communications, SM, SI and other. In 2025 the mix was 62.67% value-added communications, 27.22% SM, 8.99% SI and 1.12% other, and the value-added communications share had risen from 48.67% in 2022 to 58.45% in 2024.

Value-added communications is reported to depend on cargo volumes, freight rate movements and express parcel volumes. SM is less sensitive to business cycles but declined during an economic slowdown, according to a company earnings commentary.

Its services serve government e-filing and business-to-business e-commerce users. In 2024 the company was designated the first port logistics information relay operator and registered as an electronic payment agent. R&D spending equaled 2.29% of revenue in 2024, and the company reported 32 patents acquired. It is expanding services such as PLISM and LogisBill to widen new revenue.

04

Earnings analysis

On the filed figures, revenue rose from KRW 50.3 billion in 2022 to KRW 50.9 billion in 2023, up 1.2% year on year, and then fell to KRW 50.2 billion in 2024, down 1.5%, and to KRW 47.6 billion in 2025, down 5.2%.

Operating profit rose from KRW 7.93 billion in 2022 to KRW 8.31 billion in 2023, KRW 9.97 billion in 2024 (up 20.0%) and KRW 10.5 billion in 2025, a three-year run of increases. The operating margin moved from 15.8% in 2022 to 16.3%, 19.9% and 22.0%.

Margins widened even as sales shrank, which points to a better cost structure, and the cost of sales ratio in the company data fell from 69.75% in 2022 to 63.65% in 2025.

Profit attributable to controlling shareholders rose from KRW 6.86 billion in 2022 to KRW 9.29 billion in 2025, while net profit including non-controlling interests reached KRW 10.1 billion in 2025.

Quarterly revenue went from KRW 11.5 billion in the third quarter of 2025 to KRW 12 billion in the fourth quarter, then KRW 11.4 billion in the first quarter of 2026, KRW 10.7 billion in the second quarter and KRW 12.5 billion in the third quarter.

Operating profit bottomed at KRW 2.32 billion in the first quarter of 2026 and then rose to KRW 2.55 billion and KRW 2.79 billion, two straight quarters of growth, with the third quarter up 15.2% year on year.

Profit attributable to controlling shareholders in the same quarter was KRW 2.81 billion, down 1.5% year on year, so the operating gain did not carry through to that line. Non-operating items may explain the gap, which can be checked in the income statement detail of the quarterly report.

A company earnings commentary on the first half of 2026 described lower revenue and operating profit compared with a year earlier.

05

Industry analysis

Value-added communications, KL-Net's largest segment, is tied to export and import cargo volumes and freight rate movements. A company earnings commentary on the first half of 2026 attributed the segment's results to changes in cargo volumes, freight rates and express parcel volumes.

Revenue fell in the first and second quarters of 2026, a period that overlaps with these pressures. The rebound to KRW 12.5 billion in the third quarter could signal a volume recovery, but one quarter is too short to pin down where the cycle stands.

SM depends on corporate system-building demand and declined during an economic slowdown. Computing equipment, a key input, has been getting cheaper as domestic and foreign competition and technology progress push prices down, which reporting described as favorable for costs.

A news report from late 2024 said the company was developing autonomous vessel monitoring and failure prediction systems ahead of a law taking effect, which sets up future R&D-linked opportunities.

On competition, the company describes its position in the EDI market as stable, and media coverage has noted its ongoing government projects. These points rest on company descriptions and press reports rather than market share data.

Outlook

The company is pursuing a broader revenue base through new services such as PLISM and LogisBill. Whether these services lift quarterly revenue will show in sales from the fourth quarter of 2026 onward.

Operating profit in the third quarter of 2026 rose 15.2% year on year, which reads as a quarter in which revenue recovery and cost efficiency arrived together. However, profit attributable to controlling shareholders fell 1.5% in the same quarter, so the operating gain did not fully reach net profit.

R&D equaled 2.29% of revenue in 2024, and a late 2024 report described work on autonomous vessel and smart port technology projects. Operating cash flow rising to KRW 11.5 billion in 2025 shows capacity for dividends and investment. Treasury shares equal 11.8% of outstanding shares, based on a March 2026 disclosure.

The next checkpoints are whether fourth-quarter 2026 revenue holds near the third-quarter level and how revenue by segment appears in the 2026 annual report, which is due by the end of March 2027.

08

Bull factors

Three Years of Operating Profit Growth and a Higher Margin

Operating profit rose three years in a row, from KRW 7.93 billion in 2022 to KRW 10.5 billion in 2025, and the operating margin climbed from 15.8% to 22.0%. Profit attributable to controlling shareholders also grew from 2023 to 2025, reaching KRW 9.29 billion. The margin rise despite shrinking sales is consistent with a better cost structure.

A Light Balance Sheet

At the end of 2025 the debt ratio was 13.6%, total liabilities were KRW 9.13 billion and total equity was KRW 67.2 billion. Operating cash flow rose from KRW 8.39 billion in 2022 to KRW 11.5 billion in 2025. Low reliance on borrowing leaves relatively more room to absorb swings in the business.

Revenue and Profit Recovered Together in Q3 2026

Third-quarter 2026 revenue reached KRW 12.5 billion, up 17.7% from the prior quarter and 9.4% year on year. Operating profit rose for a second straight quarter to KRW 2.79 billion, up 15.2% year on year. Two consecutive quarters of operating profit growth since the first-quarter 2026 low support the recovery case.

Bear factors

Two Years of Falling Revenue and a Weak First Half of 2026

Revenue fell 1.5% in 2024 and 5.2% in 2025, a two-year decline, and 2025 was the smallest year of 2022 to 2025. Revenue then dropped to KRW 11.4 billion in the first quarter of 2026 and KRW 10.7 billion in the second, and a first-half earnings commentary described lower revenue and operating profit. Until sales recover, the sustainability of profit growth on this base remains an open question.

Large Swings in Quarterly Net Profit

Profit attributable to controlling shareholders fell to KRW 1.68 billion in the fourth quarter of 2025, down 41.1% quarter on quarter, and to KRW 1.64 billion in the second quarter of 2026, down 29.7%, before rebounding to KRW 2.81 billion in the third quarter.

In that third quarter operating profit grew 15.2% year on year while this profit line slipped 1.5%. The last four quarters total KRW 8.45 billion.

Sensitivity of the Core Segment to Cargo Cycles

In 2025 value-added communications accounted for 62.67% of revenue, making it the most exposed to cargo volumes and freight rates. Its share rose from 48.67% in 2022, concentrating the business more in one segment.

SM has also declined during an economic slowdown, so a simultaneous slowdown in both core segments would make defending revenue harder.

10

Risk factors

Cargo Volume and Freight Rate Cycle Risk

Value-added communications results swing with cargo volumes, freight rates and express parcel volumes. Revenue declining in the first and second quarters of 2026 shows these effects reaching reported sales. If volumes and freight rates weaken together, the margin improvement trend could reverse.

Dependence on Government Projects and Regulation

The company relies on public-sector anchors, including e-filing for government, operation of the national logistics network and designation as a port logistics information relay operator. Changes in these projects' terms or budgets would directly affect revenue. If new services stay tied to government projects, the pace of expansion may be limited.

Earnings Volatility and Share Float Risk

Quarterly profit attributable to controlling shareholders dropped sharply in the fourth quarter of 2025 and the second quarter of 2026, and in some quarters operating profit and net profit did not move together.

Treasury shares equal 11.8% of outstanding shares, which can limit the free float and raise trading volatility. Earnings consistency and share structure both remain variables to watch.
