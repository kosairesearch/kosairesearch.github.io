02

Business structure

Daegu Department Store began in 1944 as 'Daegu Sanghoe' and grew into the city's leading department store after opening its Dongseong-ro head office in 1969, making it a local retail company with deep regional roots.

Lotte Shopping entered Daegu in 2002, Hyundai Department Store in 2011, and Shinsegae in 2016, and the company's position was reported to have weakened afterward. After the Dongseong-ro head office closed in July 2021, Daegu Plaza is reported to be the only store still in operation.

The former outlet site is leased for ten years to Hyundai Department Store and operates as Hyundai City Outlet, while the logistics center in Dong-gu is leased to a logistics firm affiliated with a large conglomerate, so leased assets form one pillar of the business.

At the March 2026 shareholder meeting, the chairman stressed asset restructuring and a reshaping of the business structure. The company said asset sales would secure financial stability, while MD changes, renovation and service upgrades aim to raise customer value.

In the first half of 2024, Shinsegae Daegu ranked first among Daegu department stores by sales, followed by The Hyundai Daegu and Lotte Daegu. A 2026 first-half company monitor comment cited intensifying regional competition, a domestic slowdown and new competing stores as drags on results.

Concern also exists that Lotte's Times Villas Suseong and Shinsegae Simon's premium outlet, if opened in Daegu within five years, could weaken the competitiveness of the leased outlet. Losing rental income could also accelerate deterioration in the financial structure, according to some observers.

04

Earnings analysis

Based on DART-filed annual results, sales fell from KRW 75.9 billion in 2022 to KRW 67.6 billion in 2023 (down 10.9% year on year), KRW 59.9 billion in 2024 (down 11.5%), and KRW 53.1 billion in 2025 (down 11.3%).

Sales declined for three consecutive years from 2023 through 2025, with 2022 the largest year and 2025 the smallest in the 2022–2025 window.

The operating loss was KRW 16.3 billion in 2022, KRW 15.1 billion in 2023, and KRW 15 billion in both 2024 and 2025, a four-year run of losses in which the loss narrowed slightly after 2023.

Because sales fell while losses barely moved, the operating margin deteriorated from -21.5% in 2022 to -22.4%, -25.0% and -28.2% in 2025.

The net loss attributable to controlling shareholders widened from KRW 18.6 billion in 2022 to KRW 29.7 billion in 2023, KRW 31.3 billion in 2024 and KRW 32.5 billion in 2025, while the net loss including non-controlling interests was reported at the same levels in each year.

A first-half 2024 report pointed to financial costs as the factor that widened the net loss relative to the operating loss. Operating cash flow was negative in every year, at KRW -14.6 billion in 2022, KRW -26.2 billion in 2023, KRW -28.4 billion in 2024 and KRW -26.8 billion in 2025.

The debt ratio climbed from 103.2% in 2022 to 136.7%, 160.3% and 206.9% in 2025, when total equity was KRW 137.1 billion and total liabilities KRW 283.7 billion.

In quarterly terms, sales were KRW 13.4 billion in Q3 2025, KRW 13 billion in Q4 2025, KRW 12.6 billion in Q1 2026, KRW 11.6 billion in Q2 2026 and KRW 12.4 billion in Q3 2026, which was up 6.6% from the prior quarter but down 7.3% year on year.

The operating loss moved from KRW 4.03 billion in Q3 2025 to KRW 4.67 billion in Q3 2026, five straight loss-making quarters, with the latest quarter's loss wider than a year earlier.

As a one-off item, a 2024 report described the full sale of a Hyundai Home Shopping stake for KRW 17.8 billion, and its profit impact should be read separately from the filed results.

05

Industry analysis

The regional department store market has seen repeated entries by large department store operators into Daegu since the 2000s, so competition has accumulated over time.

In its business report, the company attributed its deteriorating results to intensifying competition among regional department stores and a sluggish domestic economy. In the first half of 2024, Shinsegae Daegu, The Hyundai Daegu and Lotte Daegu ranked as the top three department stores in Daegu by sales.

Listed peers Hyundai Department Store, Shinsegae and Lotte Shopping also operate stores in Daegu, and Daegu Department Store's store scale and operating footprint differ considerably from them.

A prolonged slump in the local property market and a long downturn in offline retail were cited as obstacles to selling the head office and real estate.

Vacancy rates in the Dongseong-ro commercial district are well above the Daegu average, and the decline of the surrounding area since the head office closed has drawn criticism. The Daegu city government is pursuing the 'Dongseong-ro Renaissance Project' to revive the tourist district.

The company's results appear to be shaped more by store competition and changes in its asset base within the region than by broad industry conditions.

06

Outlook

The company has repeatedly stated that asset sales will secure financial stability. On October 13, 2026, its board resolved to transfer 18 land lots and the buildings on the Dongseong-ro head-office site for KRW 130 billion to a Daegu-based development company, according to a disclosure.

The balance is due in December 2027, with a reduction of up to KRW 10 billion for early payment, and the contract is automatically cancelled if the deadline is missed. The company said the transaction would improve its financial structure and stabilize its operating conditions.

How the head-office building will be used has not yet been decided. A share purchase agreement signed on July 15, 2026 covered a 25.82% stake held by the chairman and related parties, which was sold to two buyers, with closing originally scheduled for late August 2026.

A DART filing dated November 18, 2026 shows that shares held by the largest shareholder and related parties rose to 3,600,920 shares (33.26%), reflecting open-market purchases of 68,857 shares by a related entity. The business direction is expected to become clearer once the new governance structure is settled.

Expectations are also low that the lease on Hyundai City Outlet will be extended. The next checkpoints are the 2026 annual business report, the Q1 2027 quarterly report, and the December 2027 deadline for the head-office balance.

08

Bull factors

Expected financial improvement from head-office proceeds

In October 2026 the board resolved to transfer the head-office land and buildings for KRW 130 billion, which the company presented as a step toward a stronger financial structure and more stable operations. The balance is due by December 2027, with up to KRW 10 billion of reduction for early payment. If the head-office book value of about KRW 110 billion is reflected, completion could ease the debt burden.

Possible business reshaping after the ownership change

A share purchase agreement signed in July 2026 covers a 25.82% stake held by the chairman and related parties, sold to two buyers.

Because the founding family's management control is changing, attention centers on whether the department store business will be kept or the company will shift toward real estate development and asset management. The company has said reshaping its business structure is aimed at raising shareholder value.

Asset base and a rise in related-party holdings

The company still holds real estate including the head-office site, the outlet site and a logistics center, leaving room for further asset disposals. As of a November 18, 2026 filing, shares held by the largest shareholder and related parties rose to 33.26%.

The increase reflects open-market purchases by a related entity, and the filing does not disclose the reason for the purchases.

09

Bear factors

Three straight years of sales decline and store competition

Sales in 2025 were KRW 53.1 billion, down 11.3% from the prior year, and they have declined three years running since 2023. Consolidated sales in the first half of 2026 were reported as down 9.0% year on year.

New department store openings and a softer regional consumer market overlap, and no factor supporting a sales recovery has yet appeared in the filings.

Continued operating losses and negative operating cash flow

The operating loss in Q3 2026 was KRW 4.67 billion, five consecutive loss-making quarters, and wider than a year earlier. Operating cash flow was negative every year from 2022 through 2025.

The operating loss narrowed from KRW 16.3 billion in 2022 to KRW 15 billion in 2025, but the pattern of cash outflows has not changed.

Financial burden and a history of failed sales

The debt ratio was 206.9% at end-2025, with total liabilities of KRW 283.7 billion exceeding total equity of KRW 137.1 billion. A 2022 head-office sale agreement worth KRW 212.5 billion collapsed when the balance went unpaid, and talks with a biotech group in 2023 also broke down. Completion of the balance payment is central to the current transfer agreement.

Risk factors

Sale execution

The head-office transfer requires the balance in December 2027, and the contract is automatically cancelled if the deadline is missed. A 2022 head-office sale agreement also collapsed after the balance went unpaid.

A failed transaction would reverse the expected financial improvement and leave the site's use undetermined again.

Financial and liquidity

Total liabilities of KRW 283.7 billion exceed total equity of KRW 137.1 billion, and operating cash flow was negative each year from 2022 through 2025. Expectations are low that the Hyundai City Outlet lease will be extended, which could reduce rental income. While operating losses persist, reliance on asset sale proceeds may grow.

Competition and local commercial district

Concern exists that if Lotte's Times Villas Suseong and Shinsegae Simon's premium outlet open in Daegu within five years, the competitiveness of Hyundai City Outlet could decline. Vacancy in the Dongseong-ro commercial district is well above the Daegu average. The area's slump has continued since the head office closed.

Overall view

Daegu Department Store's sales fell from KRW 75.9 billion in 2022 to KRW 53.1 billion in 2025, while operating and net losses attributable to controlling shareholders persisted over the same period. The debt ratio rose to 206.9% at end-2025, and operating cash flow was negative every year.

Losses have continued for five straight quarters through Q3 2026, and the net loss attributable to controlling shareholders over the last four quarters totaled KRW 33.5 billion.

Meanwhile, a share purchase agreement covering a 25.82% stake was signed in July 2026, and in October 2026 the board resolved to transfer the head-office site for KRW 130 billion.

Completion would be expected to improve the financial structure, but the balance deadline, early-payment terms and automatic-cancellation clause remain conditions.

On one side, asset disposal room and an ownership change support the case for reshaping; on the other, falling sales, persistent losses and financial strain weigh against it. PER cannot be calculated, and the price-to-book ratio sits well below 1x.

The next checkpoints are the 2026 annual business report, the Q1 2027 quarterly report and the December 2027 deadline for the head-office balance.
