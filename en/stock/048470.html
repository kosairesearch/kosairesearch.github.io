02

Business structure

DAE Dong Steel was founded in 1973 and started as a steel distributor, then was designated a POSCO hot-rolled product distributor in 1975. In 1989 it converted into a comprehensive steel coil service center with cutting lines, and it now runs five coil service facilities in Pohang and Incheon.

Its core products are primary steel materials and surface-treated steel. The Pohang plant sits next to POSCO's works, which helps reduce logistics costs, while the Incheon plant lowers transport costs for imported products, according to company-linked data.

Construction and manufacturing form the core end markets, so the business cycle of domestic steel demand passes straight through to results. The company's 2025 results commentary repeatedly pointed to weak construction and manufacturing activity.

In response, the company is pushing exports, productivity gains from larger and more automated equipment, quality improvements and a wider sales network.

Large steelmakers such as Hyundai Steel Company, Dongkuk Steel Mill and SeAH Steel are listed in the same sector, but DAE Dong Steel is a smaller coil service and processing operator.

Because its margin depends on the spread between purchase and selling prices, changes in raw-material costs and selling prices feed directly into its profit.

04

Earnings analysis

Annual sales moved from KRW 182.5 billion in 2022 to KRW 137.6 billion in 2023, down 24.6% year on year, then rose 1.5% to KRW 139.7 billion in 2024 before falling 14.7% to KRW 119.2 billion in 2025. The largest year among 2022 to 2025 was 2022, and the smallest was 2025.

The operating loss narrowed from KRW 6.08 billion in 2022 to KRW 2.42 billion in 2023, KRW 1.95 billion in 2024 and KRW 1.17 billion in 2025, and the operating margin improved from -3.3% to -1.0%. The company recorded an operating loss in each of the four years from 2022 to 2025.

Net income attributable to controlling shareholders was a loss of KRW 4.59 billion in 2022 and KRW 2.19 billion in 2023, turned positive at KRW 449 million in 2024, and fell 38.4% to KRW 276 million in 2025.

Net income including non-controlling interests, which is a different figure, was KRW 449 million in 2024 and KRW 276 million in 2025.

For 2025, the company said cost of sales fell and gross profit rose, and that cost reductions narrowed the operating loss despite higher selling and administrative expenses, including bad-debt provisions.

Operating cash flow fell from KRW 17.9 billion in 2022 to KRW 1.29 billion in 2023 and KRW 4.54 billion in 2024, then turned to -KRW 5.65 billion in 2025.

Quarterly results began with sales of KRW 31.9 billion, an operating loss of KRW 728 million and a net loss attributable to controlling shareholders of KRW 173 million in the third quarter of 2025.

The fourth quarter of 2025 showed an operating loss of KRW 344 million and a positive net income attributable to controlling shareholders of KRW 70.03 million.

In the first quarter of 2026, sales were KRW 31.8 billion, up 10.3% quarter on quarter, with operating profit of KRW 100 million and net income attributable to controlling shareholders of KRW 413 million.

The second quarter of 2026 produced operating profit of KRW 2.11 billion and net income attributable to controlling shareholders of KRW 2.78 billion, the highest of the last five quarters.

The third quarter of 2026 recorded sales of KRW 29.5 billion, operating profit of KRW 990 million and net income attributable to controlling shareholders of KRW 1.3 billion.

Of the KRW 4.56 billion in net income attributable to controlling shareholders over the last four quarters, more than half came from the second quarter of 2026, so the durability of the profit recovery depends on the next few quarters.

05

Industry analysis

A company profile compiled around 2025 described global steel demand as declining for three consecutive years. The same profile pointed to China's property slump and the inflation and high-interest-rate shock in advanced economies as factors delaying a demand recovery.

Domestically, weak construction and manufacturing activity has been cited across the company's results commentary as the cause of lower domestic steel demand.

Within this setting, DAE Dong Steel benefits from its location next to POSCO's works and its hot-rolled distribution channel, but when volumes fall, both shipment volumes and selling prices come under pressure. The company attributed part of the 2025 sales decline to lower selling prices.

Export expansion is presented as a way to partly offset weaker domestic demand, but how much exports can offset the decline is something that can only be judged once figures are disclosed. Sales fell in the second and third quarters of 2026, so the recovery in demand has not yet appeared in the top-line numbers.

Large steelmakers in the same sector operate with different production scale and raw-material structures, which makes direct comparison with DAE Dong Steel difficult.

DAE Dong Steel operates as a coil service and processing business, so its margins depend on managing the spread between purchase and selling prices, and the pace of any industry recovery will shape the pace of earnings recovery.

Outlook

In its 2026 corporate value-up plan, disclosed on March 27, 2026, DAE Dong Steel said it would pursue quantitative growth through higher sales alongside qualitative growth through better profitability. The plan includes efficiency improvements in its cost-reduction process.

The dividend policy is to be set within distributable profit, taking into account earnings scale, investment funding needs and financial soundness. The board approved the year-end dividend for the 2025 fiscal year on February 24, 2026, and the payout ratio for that year was presented at 100.9%.

A cancellation of 350,000 common shares was carried out on February 20, 2026, adjusting the total listed shares to 9,650,000. In late May 2026, the company's joint representative director structure changed to a single representative director structure led by Lim Ju-hee.

The plan has been disclosed only in directional terms, so the regular reports will be the benchmark for judging subsequent results. The next regular report is the 2026 annual report, which under the 90-day filing rule is due by the end of March 2027.

The fourth-quarter and full-year 2026 results will be disclosed with it. Because sales declined through the third quarter of 2026, the annual report will show whether the top-line decline has stopped and whether the operating margin stayed in positive territory. The first-quarter 2027 quarterly report will then show whether sales and operating profit have held up.

Bull factors

Steady narrowing of losses and a profit turn

The operating loss narrowed from KRW 6.08 billion in 2022 to KRW 1.17 billion in 2025, and net income attributable to controlling shareholders turned positive in 2024. Operating profit continued through the first three quarters of 2026, with cumulative operating profit of KRW 3.2 billion for January to September. The move from losses toward profit is the core strength in this view.

Cost structure and location advantages

According to company-linked data, the Pohang plant's location next to POSCO's works reduces logistics costs, and the Incheon plant cuts transport costs on imported products. The 2025 narrowing of the operating loss came from lower cost of sales, which lifted gross profit. Efficiency gains in the cost-reduction process are the basis for expecting this structure to strengthen.

Financial capacity and shareholder return measures

The debt ratio fell to 30.9% at the end of 2025 from 33.6% in 2022, and total liabilities were KRW 21.9 billion. A cancellation of 350,000 common shares was carried out in February 2026. The company also disclosed a dividend policy that ties payouts to distributable profit, earnings and financial soundness.

Bear factors

Continued sales decline

Sales fell from KRW 182.5 billion in 2022 to KRW 119.2 billion in 2025. Sales declined in the second and third quarters of 2026, and third-quarter sales of KRW 29.5 billion were down 7.6% year on year.

Profit that holds up with a shrinking top line depends on a recovery in shipment volumes, which is the core bearish point.

Weaker cash generation

Operating cash flow fell from KRW 17.9 billion in 2022 to -KRW 5.65 billion in 2025. The company's 2025 commentary also pointed to higher bad-debt provisions within selling and administrative expenses. Profit on the books has not yet translated into cash inflows, which is a bearish point.

Thin earnings base and quarterly concentration

Net income attributable to controlling shareholders totaled KRW 4.56 billion over the last four quarters, and more than half of that came from the second quarter of 2026, at KRW 2.78 billion. Operating profit of KRW 990 million in the third quarter of 2026 was down 53.1% from the prior quarter.

The small earnings base and concentration in a few quarters leave the durability of profit uncertain, which is a bearish point.

Risk factors

Domestic demand and selling price risk

Weak construction and manufacturing activity is reducing domestic steel demand, and lower selling prices were cited as a cause of the 2025 sales decline. Slower global steel demand and China's property slump are also factors delaying a recovery. If volumes and selling prices come under pressure together, the effect of cost reductions could be offset.

Share count and trading volatility

After the February 2026 cancellation, total listed shares were adjusted to 9,650,000. Commentary has noted that a smaller share count reduces tradable supply and can raise trading volatility. Given the small market capitalization, price movements can be large when trading thins.

Management structure and execution risk

In late May 2026, the company's representative director structure changed to a single representative director led by Lim Ju-hee. The corporate value-up plan sets out directions for higher sales, better profitability and cost reduction, but no quantitative targets have been disclosed.

How fully the plan is executed, along with the burden of bad-debt provisions, could affect the pace of earnings recovery.
