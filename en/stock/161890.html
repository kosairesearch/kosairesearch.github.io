02

Business structure

Kolmar Korea is a consolidated manufacturer with four segments: cosmetics, pharmaceuticals, packaging and food. In 2025, cosmetics revenue was KRW 1.44 trillion (53% share), pharmaceuticals KRW 985.4 billion (36%), packaging KRW 222.9 billion (8%) and food KRW 73.2 billion (3%).

Cosmetics operating profit in 2025 was KRW 129.2 billion, up 26% from the prior year, and accounted for 54% of operating profit. The cosmetics business centers on ODM, designing and manufacturing products for client brands.

In the preliminary third-quarter 2026 release, the company said its revenue mix was 48% skincare, 32% sun care and 15% makeup. The top ten clients account for 57% of revenue, a high degree of client concentration.

By region, Korean sales in 2025 were KRW 2.23 trillion, or 82% of the total, and China sales were KRW 168.8 billion (6%). The US and Canadian units stayed loss-making in the preliminary third-quarter 2026 results. In domestic cosmetics ODM, Cosmax is a principal competitor.

Amorepacific and LG H&H are large cosmetics companies centered on their own brands, so their business models differ.

04

Earnings analysis

On the basis of the disclosed annual figures, revenue rose from KRW 1.87 trillion in 2022 to KRW 2.16 trillion in 2023 (up 15.5%), KRW 2.45 trillion in 2024 (up 13.7%) and KRW 2.72 trillion in 2025 (up 11.0%), growing for three consecutive years from 2023 through 2025. 2025 was the largest year of the 2022 to 2025 period.

Operating profit climbed from KRW 73.3 billion in 2022 to KRW 136.1 billion in 2023, KRW 193.9 billion in 2024 and KRW 239.6 billion in 2025, and the operating margin rose from 3.9% to 8.8%. Operating profit grew faster than revenue in every year, even as revenue growth slowed from 15.5% to 11.0%.

In 2025, cosmetics operating profit rose 26% and drove the group's profit gain. Parent-company net profit swung from a loss of KRW 22 billion in 2022 to KRW 5.19 billion in 2023, the first profit, then rose to KRW 90.1 billion in 2024 and KRW 125.1 billion in 2025, with growth in both of the last two years.

Net profit including non-controlling interests, a different measure, was KRW 168.2 billion in 2025. Operating cash flow increased from KRW 90.8 billion in 2022 to KRW 291.4 billion in 2025.

Quarterly, revenue in the fourth quarter of 2025 was KRW 655.5 billion, down 4.0% from the prior quarter, and operating profit of KRW 47.8 billion fell 18.0%. Revenue then rose to KRW 728 billion in the first quarter of 2026 and KRW 861.3 billion in the second quarter, when operating profit reached KRW 110.3 billion.

The third quarter of 2026 brought revenue of KRW 828.2 billion, up 21.3% from a year earlier, and operating profit of KRW 81.9 billion, down 25.7% from the second quarter.

In the preliminary third-quarter release, the company cited sun care and skincare growth, new products from global clients, and improved results at its container subsidiary.

Hanwha Investment & Securities noted that the third quarter has fewer working days because of summer holidays and the Chuseok break, which can shift production. Parent-company net profit over the latest four quarters totals KRW 193.6 billion.

05

Industry analysis

Demand for domestic cosmetics ODM is being supported by clients' export growth. In a June 2026 report, Hanwha Investment & Securities said rising exports by domestic clients and higher production for global MNC luxury brands were appearing at the same time, diversifying the order base beyond a few clients.

The same report noted that second-half revenue at the Korean unit averaged 10% below first-half revenue in 2022 to 2024, but is expected to sit about 2% below on average in 2025 to 2027. The firm presented this as a sign that the traditional off-season effect is easing.

A February 2026 securities report said capacity expanded to serve growing indie brand demand would translate into volume growth. In an October 2026 report, LS Securities said domestic client orders remain solid, but capacity limits and fewer working days could leave revenue growth slightly below market expectations.

On the demand side, the cycle is read as an upswing where exports and indie brands expand together. On the supply side, capacity constraints are read as a ceiling on near-term growth.

A February 2026 securities report described the company as holding a leading position in sun care and said its margins were strong relative to domestic peers on a standalone basis. Cosmax is the main domestic ODM rival.

06

Outlook

Kolmar Korea's full-year 2026 results will be confirmed in the fourth-quarter preliminary release and the 2026 annual report.

Preliminary cumulative revenue for the first three quarters of 2026 was KRW 2.42 trillion and operating profit was KRW 271.2 billion, up 17% and 41.4% from a year earlier, as announced on November 6, 2026. Securities estimates vary widely.

In a June 2026 report, Hanwha Investment & Securities projected 2026 revenue of KRW 3.10 trillion and operating profit of KRW 312.0 billion.

In an October 2026 report, LS Securities projected 2026 revenue of KRW 3.25 trillion and operating profit of KRW 354.0 billion, and for 2027 revenue of KRW 3.62 trillion and operating profit of KRW 412.0 billion.

Because the gap between the two operating profit estimates is large, the fourth-quarter 2026 result will show which estimate is closer. Overseas, the second US plant is a key variable.

In the June 2026 report, Hanwha said new-client talks at the second plant center on existing Korean clients, which gives visibility on utilization. The same report expected North American operating losses to narrow from KRW 19.0 billion in 2025 to KRW 18.0 billion in 2026. The next checkpoints are new client wins at the US second plant and the actual trend in North American losses.

08

Bull factors

Diversifying domestic order base

In a June 2026 report, Hanwha Investment & Securities said orders are broadening from a few clients to more clients and wider SKU ranges.

In the preliminary third-quarter 2026 release, the domestic unit reported revenue of KRW 433.7 billion and operating profit of KRW 62.9 billion, and the company said domestic revenue was a record for a third quarter. The domestic operating margin of 14.5% was described as the highest for any third quarter. The bull case rests on whether these orders prove recurring rather than one-off.

Improved profitability structure

The operating margin rose from 3.9% in 2022 to 8.8% in 2025, and operating profit grew from KRW 73.3 billion to KRW 239.6 billion over the same period. Operating cash flow also increased, from KRW 90.8 billion in 2022 to KRW 291.4 billion in 2025, showing stronger cash generation alongside profit.

Hanwha Investment & Securities estimated in its June 2026 report that the operating margin would rise to 10.1% in 2026. The bull case is that continued margin gains would allow profit to keep growing alongside revenue.

Possible easing of seasonality

Hanwha Investment & Securities expected the gap between first-half and second-half revenue at the Korean unit to narrow from an average of 10% in 2022 to 2024 to about 2% in 2025 to 2027, according to its June 2026 report. That would mean smaller swings in results across halves than in the past.

Quarterly revenue was KRW 861.3 billion in the second quarter of 2026 and KRW 828.2 billion in the third, both above KRW 800 billion, which indicates the higher revenue level is holding. The bull case includes the possibility that steadier quarterly results change how the market values the company.

Bear factors

Persistent losses at overseas units

In the preliminary third-quarter 2026 results, the China, US and Canada units remained in the red.

Hanwha Investment & Securities expected North American operating losses to narrow from KRW 19.0 billion in 2025 to KRW 18.0 billion in 2026, while still expecting the US business to stay loss-making in its June 2026 report. The pace at which the US plant's utilization recovers was flagged as a variable to confirm. If overseas losses persist, strong domestic results may leave limited room for consolidated profit improvement.

Capacity limits and client concentration

In an October 2026 report, LS Securities expected domestic client orders to stay solid but revenue growth to fall slightly short of market expectations because of capacity limits and fewer working days.

In the preliminary third-quarter 2026 release, the top ten clients accounted for 57% of revenue, so changes in orders from a few clients can have a large effect on results. The bear case is that revenue growth may be capped if supply cannot keep pace with orders.

Quarterly profit volatility

Operating profit of KRW 47.8 billion in the fourth quarter of 2025 fell 18.0% from the prior quarter, and KRW 81.9 billion in the third quarter of 2026 fell 25.7% from the second quarter. Profit rose from KRW 78.9 billion in the first quarter of 2026 to KRW 110.3 billion in the second before declining again.

Revenue grew 21.3% year on year in the third quarter, but quarterly profit has not moved as steadily as revenue. The debt-to-equity ratio stood at 107.4% at the end of 2025, which can make financial burdens more visible when profit weakens.

Risk factors

Demand and order risk

Hanwha Investment & Securities listed a slowdown in global cosmetics demand, weaker export growth at domestic clients and delays in new global brand orders as the main risks to its earnings outlook in its June 2026 report.

If export growth at domestic clients slows, the benefit from a broader order base would also weaken. Cosmetics made up 53% of 2025 revenue, so a demand slowdown would feed directly into reported results.

Delay in US normalization

The US unit remained loss-making in the preliminary third-quarter 2026 results, and the key question is whether new clients for the second US plant translate into actual production. Hanwha Investment & Securities flagged the pace of utilization recovery at the US plant as a variable to confirm in its June 2026 report.

Press coverage noted that the market is focused on when the US unit could move into profit. A slower turn to profit could reset expectations for consolidated earnings gains.

Leverage and client concentration

Total liabilities were KRW 1.79 trillion at the end of 2025, and the debt-to-equity ratio based on total equity of KRW 1.67 trillion was 107.4%. Equity attributable to parent shareholders was KRW 910.4 billion. The top ten clients account for 57% of revenue.

An order cut or production shift at one client could show up strongly in quarterly results, and with a sizable debt load, profit swings can become financial pressure.

12

Overall view

Kolmar Korea has posted consecutive parent-company profit gains in 2024 and 2025 after returning to profit in 2023, and its operating margin rose from 3.9% in 2022 to 8.8% in 2025.

In the preliminary third-quarter 2026 results, the domestic unit's operating margin was 14.5%, and domestic ODM orders are leading earnings. Securities firms expect profit growth in 2026 and 2027, citing a broader order base and easing seasonality.

On the other side, the US, China and Canada units remain loss-making, and operating profit in the third quarter of 2026 fell 25.7% from the prior quarter. Capacity limits and a top-ten client share of 57% show both the ceiling on growth and the volatility in results.

With a debt-to-equity ratio of 107.4%, swings in quarterly profit could also become financial pressure. The next checkpoints are the fourth-quarter 2026 preliminary results, the 2026 annual report, and the pace of new client wins at the second US plant and the trend in North American losses.
