Report overview

Separators Heading Into a Merger: Utilization Is the Test

SK ie technology has posted an operating loss in each of the five quarters from the third quarter of 2025 through the third quarter of 2026, and its separator business is set to move into parent SK Innovation through a merger, so separator utilization and demand recovery are the points to watch.

02

Business structure

SK ie technology is a lithium-ion battery separator maker that was spun off from SK Innovation's materials business in April 2019 and listed on the KOSPI in May 2021. Its core application is separators for electric vehicles, and the company plans to widen its reach into separators for energy storage systems.

Production is spread across the Jeungpyeong plant in Chungbuk, the Jiangsu plant in China, and plants in Poland.

Media reports in May 2026 said the Jeungpyeong plant generated about KRW 117.7 billion of sales in 2025, equal to 45% of company revenue, and the plant's full production lines will stop from November 30, 2026. The Jiangsu plant is being sold to a local Chinese company for KRW 88.8 billion.

Captive demand from affiliated battery makers is a large share of sales, so changes in those customers' shipments flow directly into separator volumes. A long-term LFP separator supply contract signed in February 2025 for KRW 291.4 billion was shortened in March 2026 and cut to KRW 2.4 billion.

Price competition from Chinese producers is the largest external variable, and brokers typically group Asahi Kasei, Toray Industries and Yunnan ENM as peers.

04

Earnings analysis

On a disclosed basis, revenue was KRW 585.8 billion in 2022 and KRW 649.6 billion in 2023, up 10.9%, which made 2023 the largest year of the four, before falling 66.5% to KRW 217.9 billion in 2024, the smallest year. Revenue recovered 20.2% to KRW 261.9 billion in 2025.

Operating results moved from an operating loss of KRW 52.3 billion in 2022 to an operating profit of KRW 32 billion in 2023, a swing back into the black, then to an operating loss of KRW 291 billion in 2024 that turned the year into a loss, and to an operating loss of KRW 246.4 billion in 2025, a continued loss with a smaller deficit.

The operating margin was 4.9% in 2023, -133.6% in 2024 and -94.1% in 2025.

Attributable net income went from KRW 82.2 billion in 2023 to an attributable net loss of KRW 246.6 billion in 2024 and KRW 211.4 billion in 2025, while net income including non-controlling interests was reported as a net loss of KRW 246.6 billion and KRW 211.4 billion in those years.

Operating cash flow was KRW 125.3 billion in 2023, then negative at -KRW 87.2 billion in 2024 and -KRW 33.4 billion in 2025.

At the end of 2025, total equity was KRW 2.6 trillion, all of it attributable to controlling shareholders, total liabilities were KRW 1.79 trillion, and the debt ratio was 68.8%, down from 78.5% in 2024.

On a quarterly basis, revenue fell from KRW 79.1 billion in the third quarter of 2025 to KRW 41.8 billion in the fourth quarter and KRW 35.9 billion in the first quarter of 2026, then rose to KRW 39.5 billion and KRW 41.4 billion in the second and third quarters.

Operating losses narrowed from KRW 75.8 billion in the fourth quarter of 2025 to KRW 73.2 billion, KRW 63.4 billion and KRW 36.8 billion through the third quarter of 2026, but the streak of losses runs five quarters.

The attributable net loss widened to KRW 1.33 trillion in the second quarter of 2026 and was KRW 109.6 billion in the third quarter. The attributable net loss over the last four quarters totals KRW 1.65 trillion.

In the third quarter the operating loss was smaller than a year earlier while the attributable net loss grew from KRW 40.1 billion to KRW 109.6 billion, which points to non-operating items, whose amounts should be checked in the periodic report notes.

05

Industry analysis

The electric vehicle market, the core demand driver for separators, has slowed. U.S. EV purchase subsidies ended on September 30, 2025, and KB Securities estimated that U.S. EV sales would fall sharply in 2026. Canceled and delayed launches by North American automakers have also weighed on separator demand.

SK Innovation itself cited intensifying price competition from Chinese entrants in announcing the merger. According to SK Innovation, the plant utilization rate of SK ie technology was about 20% in the first quarter of 2026, and it said improvement would be hard without structural change.

On the cycle, the business looks to be in a trough waiting for demand to recover, but the timing of any rebound is not yet visible.

Compared with peers, Asahi Kasei and Toray Industries are diversified materials companies where separators are one part of the business, while SK ie technology is centered on separators and therefore feels changes in EV demand more directly.

Price competition from players such as Yunnan ENM also pressures selling prices. The company is pursuing energy storage system separators to reduce its dependence on EVs.

06

Outlook

The confirmed schedule centers on the merger. SK Innovation and SK ie technology plan to receive board and shareholder approval on November 24, 2026, complete the merger on January 1, 2027, and list the new SK Innovation shares issued for it on January 18, 2027.

The companies say the merger will cut duplicate and financing costs and combine research and development capabilities to respond to new demand such as energy storage separators.

Production restructuring is already under way: the Jeungpyeong plant's lines stop from November 30, 2026, and the Jiangsu plant is being sold for KRW 88.8 billion. Poland's second plant is scheduled to start operating by the end of 2026, and expansion of the third and fourth plants is in progress.

The company had projected that second-quarter 2026 volumes would rise more than 10% from the first quarter, and disclosed second-quarter revenue rose 10.2% from the prior quarter.

However, the reduction of a KRW 291.4 billion LFP supply contract to KRW 2.4 billion shows that customer investment changes can cut contracted volumes, so order recovery is the key question.

Whether a periodic report under the company's own name is filed after the merger, and in what scope, should be confirmed through disclosures once the merger process is complete.

08

Bull factors

Footprint consolidation could ease fixed costs

Halting the Jeungpyeong plant and selling the Jiangsu plant concentrates production in Poland. The company says it is focusing investment on European plants after the domestic and Chinese plants proved less productive.

If Poland's second plant starts by the end of 2026 and the third and fourth expansions proceed as planned, the cost structure per unit of output could change. This is the utilization-rate headroom to track.

Quarterly losses and revenue have begun to shift direction

The third-quarter 2026 operating loss was KRW 36.8 billion, narrower than KRW 47.2 billion a year earlier. Revenue rose in the second and third quarters of 2026 after the first-quarter low, to KRW 39.5 billion and KRW 41.4 billion.

The operating loss shrank from KRW 75.8 billion in the fourth quarter of 2025 to KRW 36.8 billion, which shows the direction of the deficit is changing. Five straight quarters of losses mean no turn to profit has yet appeared in the disclosures.

The merger aims to cut duplicate costs and financing burden

SK Innovation says the merger will reduce duplicate and financing costs in the separator business and secure financial stability. The plan also combines SK Innovation's research capabilities with SK ie technology's product development.

Bringing the unit under the parent addresses the limits of its standalone fundraising. Whether the approval and effective date proceed as scheduled is the next check.

09

Bear factors

EV demand slowdown is capping utilization

After U.S. EV purchase subsidies ended on September 30, 2025, North American EV sales fell, and automakers canceled or delayed launches. SK Innovation said the plant utilization rate was about 20% in the first quarter of 2026. Low utilization makes it hard to absorb fixed costs, which can prolong operating losses. If demand recovery is delayed further, the loss structure could last longer.

Contract cuts and dependence on affiliated customers

A KRW 291.4 billion LFP separator supply contract signed in February 2025 was ended early in March 2026 and cut to KRW 2.4 billion. The customer adjusted its investment plans in response to changes in the downstream market.

With demand depending heavily on affiliated battery makers, a drop in their shipments feeds directly into lower separator sales. Whether other customers can fill the gap is the key question.

Chinese price competition and accumulated net losses

SK Innovation said intensified price competition followed Chinese producers' entry into global markets. The attributable net loss widened to KRW 1.33 trillion in the second quarter of 2026, with media reports tying it to an accounting loss from winding down the China business. Repeated losses could weaken both investment capacity and the ability to defend selling prices.

10

Risk factors

Demand and policy risk

Changes in EV subsidy policy and North American automakers' launch schedules drive separator demand. If the projected fall in U.S. EV sales in 2026 materializes, the timing of any utilization recovery could slip further. The shift toward energy storage separators has not yet been shown to lift revenue meaningfully. Continued losses from demand and policy changes are the largest risk.

Customer and contract risk

Changes in a major customer's investment plans translate quickly into lower volumes, as the KRW 291.4 billion contract reduction showed. High dependence on affiliated customers means their shipment declines reduce separator sales. If new orders come late, plant operating plans may need another revision. The Jeungpyeong shutdown and Poland expansion timing are tied to customer volumes.

Merger and shareholder risk

The exchange ratio sets one SK ie technology common share at about 0.11 of an SK Innovation common share. SK Innovation's side of the merger is a small-scale procedure that skips the share purchase claim process.

After the merger SK ie technology will cease to exist and its shares will be exchanged for SK Innovation shares. The business and shareholder structure will change, so disclosures before and after completion should be read together.

Overall view

On a disclosed basis, SK ie technology posted losses in 2024 and 2025, and it has recorded an operating loss in each of the five quarters from the third quarter of 2025 through the third quarter of 2026.

The operating loss narrowed from KRW 73.2 billion in the first quarter of 2026 to KRW 36.8 billion in the third quarter, and revenue rose for two consecutive quarters. The attributable net loss, however, widened to KRW 1.33 trillion in the second quarter of 2026, and its four-quarter total is KRW 1.65 trillion.

On the business side, the Jeungpyeong shutdown and the shift of production to Poland are under way, while contract cuts and reliance on affiliated customers add volume volatility. The external drivers of slower EV demand and Chinese price competition sit alongside the company's statement that utilization was about 20%.

Earnings-based multiples cannot be calculated, and the price-to-book ratio sits slightly above 1 times.

The merger path runs through approval on November 24, 2026 to a planned effective date of January 1, 2027, and the key checkpoint is the 2026 annual business report on the annual operating loss trend and the reporting structure after the merger.
