02

Business structure

Guyoung Technology was founded in November 1989 and listed on KOSDAQ in February 2002 as an automotive parts manufacturer. The company emphasizes its in-house design and manufacturing of progressive dies for its stamping process as a core competitive edge, and it reports automotive parts as a single operating segment.

Its products include brackets, seat parts, hinges, roller arms and oil pans. In the cumulative period through the third quarter of 2024, brackets accounted for 44.1% of revenue and seat parts for 29.8%, while hinges were 5.1%, oil pans 4.6%, roller arms 4.4% and other products 12.0%.

A JobKorea breakdown as of March 2026 showed brackets at 38%, body parts at 24% and seat parts at 16%, but the classification differs, so the two sets of shares are not directly comparable.

By region, the United States made up 70.0% of revenue, Korea 29.9% and China 0.1% in the cumulative period through the third quarter of 2024. Its main customers are Hyundai Motor and KIA, so volumes depend heavily on those two automakers.

Car Tech, LLC in Alabama and Guyoung Tech USA, INC serve as overseas production and sales bases, and both are wholly owned by the company. At its March 2026 annual shareholders' meeting, the company added the manufacture of secondary batteries to its stated business purposes.

Because it supplies many part types, a change in one item rarely drives total revenue, but volumes move with automaker output.

04

Earnings analysis

Annual revenue rose from KRW 274.2 billion in 2022 to KRW 357.4 billion in 2023 (up 30.4% year on year), KRW 377.4 billion in 2024 (up 5.6%) and KRW 426.2 billion in 2025 (up 12.9%), rising for three consecutive years from 2023 through 2025.

Operating profit jumped from KRW 7.7 billion in 2022 to KRW 29 billion in 2023, up 276.2%, making 2023 the largest year in the 2022–2025 span. It then declined in 2024 to KRW 28.9 billion (down 0.3%) and in 2025 to KRW 24.3 billion (down 15.7%), falling for two consecutive years from 2024 through 2025.

The operating margin was 2.8% in 2022, 8.1% in 2023, 7.6% in 2024 and 5.7% in 2025, so margins narrowed in 2025 even as revenue grew. A cumulative analysis through the third quarter of 2025 pointed to continued production disruptions and weak market conditions, with operating profit down 30.4% year on year.

Operating cash flow was KRW 22.1 billion in 2022, KRW 46 billion in 2023, KRW 33.4 billion in 2024 and KRW 26.4 billion in 2025.

Profit attributable to controlling shareholders went from KRW 3.98 billion in 2022 to KRW 18.6 billion in 2023 (up 369.0%), KRW 18.2 billion in 2024 (down 2.3%) and KRW 22.6 billion in 2025 (up 23.9%), with 2025 the largest year.

In 2025, operating profit fell while profit attributable to controlling shareholders rose, so non-operating items and taxes need to be checked in the detailed disclosures.

Quarterly operating profit bottomed at KRW 3.9 billion in the third quarter of 2025, then rose to KRW 7.85 billion in the fourth quarter (up 101.5% from the prior quarter), KRW 8.44 billion in the first quarter of 2026, KRW 9.17 billion in the second quarter and KRW 9.93 billion in the third quarter, a four-quarter run of gains.

The third-quarter 2026 operating margin of 7.1% was 3.1 percentage points above the year-earlier level. Revenue peaked at KRW 147.6 billion in the first quarter of 2026 and then declined for two quarters, to KRW 141.3 billion and KRW 139.1 billion, although third-quarter revenue was up 43.6% year on year.

Profit attributable to controlling shareholders, however, fell to KRW 1.96 billion in the third quarter of 2026, down 71.6% from the prior quarter and 74.7% from a year earlier, so it moved opposite to operating profit.

05

Industry analysis

Guyoung Technology's demand base centers on Hyundai Motor and KIA, so production and sales at those two automakers are the starting point for its earnings.

A corporate analysis report published in December 2024 attributed the automakers' sales growth to a higher mix of premium models, stronger sales in North America and Europe, a weaker won and expanding demand for eco-friendly vehicles.

The same report said U.S. revenue reached KRW 249.2 billion in 2023, up 34.1% year on year, and drove overall revenue growth. With the United States at 70.0% of revenue in the cumulative period through the third quarter of 2024, local sales and exchange-rate moves feed directly into revenue.

One media report said industry observers expect revenue and profitability to rise gradually as the company mass-produces higher-value EV and hybrid battery parts such as upper cases and end plates for module cases.

The shift to electrified vehicles weighs on demand for combustion-engine parts while creating new demand for battery-related components, so the product mix matters.

Among listed automotive parts peers are Hyundai Mobis, Hanon Systems, HL Mando, Hyundai WIA, Sungwoo Hitech, Myoung Shin Industry and Seoyon E-Hwa, and Guyoung Technology points to its in-house die design and stamping capability as a differentiator.

The most direct gauge of where the cycle stands is automaker output and the sales trends at Hyundai Motor and KIA. Exposure to the U.S. market and to won-dollar exchange rates means the company is sensitive to external variables.

In April 2026, subsidiary Car Tech, LLC decided on a rights offering worth about KRW 29.6 billion to fund facilities and debt repayment.

A January 2026 disclosure showed a guarantee of KRW 43.26 billion on Car Tech, LLC's borrowing from the Export-Import Bank of Korea, with the guarantee period running to January 6, 2027.

An October 2026 corrected filing extended the guarantee period for USD 7 million of borrowings by Guyoung Tech USA to October 21, 2027, and the disclosed guarantee amount equals 6.10% of equity.

On governance, a July 2026 report said the chairman planned to gift most of his shares, about 3.5 million, to his eldest son, a vice president. To qualify for the family-business inheritance tax relief, the recipient must become CEO within three years of the gift.

At the March 2026 annual shareholders' meeting, shareholders approved transferring KRW 5.1 billion of capital surplus into retained earnings, and the company said this was aimed at a reduction-type dividend that is not subject to dividend income tax.

On the positive side, whether operating profit keeps rising after four straight quarterly gains is the key test. On the negative side, the question is how long the drop in profit attributable to controlling shareholders persists.

The next checkpoint is the 2026 annual report, where fourth-quarter 2026 results and the gap between operating profit and controlling-shareholder profit will be reflected together.

Risk factors

Customer concentration and automaker output

Hyundai Motor and KIA are the main customers, so their production volumes and new-model schedules largely determine revenue. Production disruption was cited in the cumulative results through the third quarter of 2025, so schedule changes can show up quickly in quarterly results.

Model discontinuations or volume adjustments at a specific automaker affect revenue for the related parts directly.

Exchange rates and U.S. exposure

The United States accounted for 70.0% of revenue in the cumulative period through the third quarter of 2024. A weaker won can lift won-denominated revenue, but dollar borrowings and guarantees grow more burdensome at the same time, so exchange-rate moves cut both ways. Local utilization rates and trade conditions are also variables that shape the overseas subsidiaries' results.

Financial structure and succession

A debt-to-equity ratio of 361.5% and total liabilities of KRW 556.5 billion at the end of 2025 raise sensitivity to changes in interest rates and borrowing terms. The drop in profit attributable to controlling shareholders in the third quarter of 2026 shows how volatile earnings power has been.

The gift of major shareholder stakes is tied to the inheritance-tax requirement that the recipient become CEO within three years, which links the timing to management succession.

Overall view

Guyoung Technology is a growing company on a revenue basis, with sales rising from KRW 274.2 billion in 2022 to KRW 426.2 billion in 2025.

Operating profit peaked at KRW 29 billion in 2023 and fell to KRW 24.3 billion in 2025, but quarterly operating profit has risen for four straight quarters through the third quarter of 2026.

Profit attributable to controlling shareholders, however, dropped to KRW 1.96 billion in the third quarter of 2026, down 71.6% from the prior quarter, so the operating-level improvement has not carried to the bottom line.

On the financial side, the debt-to-equity ratio rose to 361.5% at the end of 2025 as overseas investment and borrowing guarantees increased. Ownership and the second-generation succession are moving forward, as reported in July 2026, and they are tied to the conditions of the inheritance tax relief.

The price-to-book ratio sits well below 1x, and the year-end 2025 dividend has been confirmed. Both an asset-value and dividend reading and a concern over earnings volatility and leverage can be sustained from these facts.

The next checkpoint is the 2026 annual report, where how the gap between operating profit and controlling-shareholder profit is explained will be central.
