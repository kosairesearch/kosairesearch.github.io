Legacy-equipment distributor returns to quarterly operating profit; annual recovery still open

02

Business structure

SurplusGLOBAL is centered on the resale and refurbishment of used semiconductor equipment. The company said it has supplied more than 60,000 used units to about 6,000 companies, mainly legacy, or mature-node, tools and parts.

A 2025 press report said it handles more than 20,000 equipment types and over 1 million parts, with a network of over 150,000 customers worldwide. The same report said 70–80% of revenue comes from overseas, and that about 300 domestic refurbishers work with the company.

The business has two arms: equipment distribution, and parts through an online parts marketplace and an offline parts mall in Yongin. Parts were 1–2% of sales in 2025 according to press reports, with a medium-to-long-term goal of 50%.

Overseas subsidiaries operate in the United States, China, Taiwan, Singapore and Japan, and a German subsidiary was reported as confirmed for the second half of 2024.

Competitively, management argues that equipment makers' focus on advanced tools has reduced legacy equipment supply, and that the company is targeting a niche that combines legacy equipment and parts. The claim of a number-one share among legacy equipment suppliers is the company's own statement. As of October 8, 2026, the largest shareholder group held 56.71% and treasury shares accounted for 6.81%.

04

Earnings analysis

On a reported basis, annual revenue went from KRW 234.9 billion in 2022 to KRW 166 billion in 2023, a 29.3% decline, then recovered to KRW 251.6 billion in 2024, up 51.5%. Revenue fell 17.2% to KRW 208.4 billion in 2025. Among 2022–2025, 2024 was the largest year and 2023 the smallest.

Operating profit dropped from KRW 31.9 billion in 2022 to KRW 13 billion in 2023, a 59.2% decline, and rose 8.6% to KRW 14.2 billion in 2024. Even with higher 2024 revenue, the operating margin of 5.6% was below the 13.6% of 2022.

The company posted an operating loss of KRW 11.7 billion in 2025, with an operating margin of -5.6%. According to FnGuide financial data, gross profit fell from KRW 43.5 billion in 2024 to KRW 22.9 billion in 2025, while selling and administrative expenses rose from KRW 29.3 billion to KRW 34.6 billion.

Operating cash flow was -KRW 23.9 billion in 2025. By quarter, revenue rose to KRW 67.5 billion in the fourth quarter of 2025 from KRW 37.3 billion in the third, and the operating loss narrowed to KRW 4.79 billion.

First-quarter 2026 revenue fell 45.7% quarter on quarter to KRW 36.6 billion, the low point, and the net loss attributable to controlling shareholders widened to KRW 7.2 billion. The second-quarter operating loss narrowed to KRW 359 million.

Third-quarter revenue rose to KRW 55.2 billion, up 48.2% year on year, and operating profit of KRW 4.52 billion marked a turn to profit. According to WiseReport's compilation, the first-half 2026 operating loss widened 162.7% year on year. Whether the third-quarter profit is one-off or sustained will show up in the next quarters' operating margins.

05

Industry analysis

The semiconductor equipment market tends to lag the chip industry cycle, which makes results volatile, according to WiseReport's company commentary. The same source notes that used-equipment prices move sharply with global supply and demand.

According to FnGuide, equipment investment was delayed by the chip downturn in 2023, and sales rose in 2024 as the equipment market recovered. The company's CEO has said that mature-node fabs face shortages of equipment and parts.

He attributes this to equipment makers concentrating on advanced tools and some discontinuing legacy production. He also describes the equipment market as an oligopoly with intellectual property issues, and says the company is targeting a niche that integrates legacy equipment and parts.

A 2025 press report carried his remark that tighter export procedures to China are pushing customers toward suppliers they trust. A 2024 report cited the company's view that chip fab investments in Germany could create new demand. The claim of a number-one share in legacy equipment should be read as the company's own assertion.

06

Outlook

The company cited a 2026 revenue target of KRW 250–260 billion and also set a goal of returning to profit that year. Disclosed revenue for the first three quarters of 2026 totals KRW 130.6 billion, just over half of the low end of that target.

Ahead of Semicon Korea 2026, the CEO said a new building, B-block, was due for completion in July 2026, and that the parts mall now occupying one floor of Block A would move into 3,000 pyeong of B-block space. A 2025 press report described B-block as a KRW 50 billion investment.

Whether completion and relocation happened as planned is not yet reflected in disclosed figures and should appear in the next annual report's capex details.

The online marketplace opened in beta in June 2025, and press reports gave different timing for the shift to an open marketplace, with October 2025 seller onboarding in one report and a seller-led open format from December in another.

In 2026 the company unveiled its marketplace bidding service at Semicon China 2026, and press headlines reported more than 40 participants in online bidding. A 2025 press report carried the CEO's comment that the company is pursuing a large order from a US fab seeking legacy equipment solutions.

The next scheduled disclosure is the 2026 annual report, where the key test is whether the operating margin improves from -5.6% in 2025.

08

Bull factors

Quarterly return to profit

Operating profit turned to KRW 4.52 billion in the third quarter of 2026, with revenue up 42.4% from the prior quarter to KRW 55.2 billion. Revenue has risen for two consecutive quarters since the first-quarter 2026 low. The shift from narrowing losses to a quarterly profit can be read as an early sign of earnings recovery.

Legacy supply gap

The CEO says mature-node fabs face shortages of equipment and parts. The company said it has supplied more than 60,000 used units to about 6,000 firms. Its claim of a number-one legacy-equipment share is the company's own statement, not an independently verified figure. Even so, demand from aging fab supply chains is central to the business model.

Room to grow in parts

Parts were 1–2% of sales in 2025 according to press reports, against a medium-to-long-term goal of 50%. The marketplace unveiled a bidding service in 2026, and press headlines reported more than 40 participants in online bidding.

Together with the planned move of the parts mall into B-block, the parts mix has room to improve on a business-metric basis.

Bear factors

Annual loss and cash outflow

The company posted an operating loss of KRW 11.7 billion and a net loss attributable to controlling shareholders of KRW 14.5 billion in 2025, and operating cash flow was -KRW 23.9 billion that year.

According to WiseReport's compilation, first-half 2026 revenue fell 27.3% year on year and the operating loss widened 162.7%. One quarter of profit is thin evidence that this trend has reversed.

Balance sheet pressure

The debt ratio rose to 136.6% in 2025 from 97.5% in 2022, and total liabilities at the end of 2025 were KRW 245.9 billion. The planned KRW 50 billion B-block build-out could add to financial pressure as spending proceeds.

Cycle lag and price volatility

The equipment market lags the chip cycle, so results are volatile. Used-equipment prices move with global supply and demand, which affects inventory valuation and margins. Revenue fell 29.3% in 2023 during a downturn, showing how sharply results can move in a weak cycle. Selling and administrative expenses rose to KRW 34.6 billion in 2025, adding to cost pressure.

10

Risk factors

Durability of profit

It is not yet clear whether the third-quarter profit reflects one-off factors or a durable recovery in revenue. Revenue was KRW 36.6 billion in the first quarter of 2026, the low point, and the KRW 55.2 billion of the third quarter remains below the KRW 67.5 billion of the fourth quarter of 2025. With quarterly swings this large, the profit could prove short-lived.

Cost of expansion

The B-block build-out, the parts-mall move, IT spending on the marketplace and overseas subsidiary expansion are proceeding at once. These outlays raise labor and fixed selling and administrative costs, which could weigh on profit until revenue recovers. The CEO has said in earlier interviews that investment and labor costs would rise.

Concentrated ownership

With a high share of closely held stock, the float is limited and the share price can be sensitive to supply and demand. Decision-making is also concentrated in a single shareholder group, which is a governance point to watch.

Overall view

SurplusGLOBAL began as a used legacy semiconductor equipment distributor and is widening into parts platforms. Operating profit fell from KRW 31.9 billion in 2022 to an operating loss of KRW 11.7 billion in 2025, and operating cash flow was -KRW 23.9 billion that year.

In 2026, revenue rose in the second and third quarters after the first-quarter low, and the third quarter returned to operating profit of KRW 4.52 billion. Bullish factors include the quarterly profit turn, the company's account of legacy supply gaps, and its parts expansion plans.

Bearish factors include the annual loss, a 136.6% debt ratio, the market's lag to the chip cycle and volatile used-equipment prices. Net losses over the last four quarters totaled KRW 12.2 billion, and first-half 2026 revenue fell 27.3% year on year.

With parts at 1–2% of sales in 2025 press reports, the earnings effect of the expansion is still limited. The next checks are the operating margin in the 2026 annual report and whether profit persists in the first-quarter 2027 report.
