02

Business structure

Kolon Industries operates three segments: chemicals, industrial materials and fashion. The industrial materials segment generated consolidated revenue of KRW 2,302.2 billion in 2025, or 47.2% of the total, according to an October 2026 ZDNet Korea report.

Its core product is PET tire cord, a reinforcing material for passenger car tires, and tire manufacturers are its main customers. Aramid, a high-strength fiber used in optical cable reinforcement, ballistic materials and power cables, is the segment's other pillar.

The chemicals segment sells petroleum resins and engineering plastics, along with phenolic and epoxy resins, and it also includes colorless polyimide (CPI) film for foldable displays.

The fashion segment runs outdoor and golf brands including Kolon Sport, and growth at its Chinese subsidiary has supported consolidated results. On the supply side, a February 2026 Yuanta Securities report said PET chips are purchased from Lotte Chemical.

Stated capacities are 40,000 tons per year of PET tire cord in Korea and 36,000 tons per year in Vietnam.

In 2024 the company separated its film business into a joint venture to exit a loss-making unit, and in July 2026 it selected IMM PE as the preferred buyer for its functional materials and imaging materials businesses. The company says it is concentrating resources on core materials such as tire cord, aramid and chemicals.

04

Earnings analysis

Annual revenue fell from KRW 5.37 trillion in 2022 to KRW 5.06 trillion in 2023 (down 5.7% year on year) and KRW 4.84 trillion in 2024 (down 4.3%), then rose slightly to KRW 4.87 trillion in 2025 (up 0.6%). The largest revenue year in the 2022–2025 period was 2022, and the smallest was 2024.

Operating profit dropped from KRW 242.5 billion in 2022 to KRW 157.6 billion in 2023 (down 35.0%), was roughly flat at KRW 158.7 billion in 2024 (up 0.7%), and then fell to KRW 108.9 billion in 2025 (down 31.4%). The operating margin narrowed from 4.5% in 2022 to 3.1% in 2023, 3.3% in 2024 and 2.2% in 2025.

Controlling shareholder net income fell from KRW 179.8 billion in 2022 to KRW 42.8 billion in 2023 (down 76.2%), recovered to KRW 98.6 billion in 2024 (up 130.6%), and reached KRW 38.3 billion in 2025 (down 61.2%).

Total net income for 2025 was KRW 51.6 billion, which includes non-controlling interests and should not be read as the same figure as controlling shareholder net income.

The debt-to-equity ratio improved from 119.7% in 2022 to 89.0% at the end of 2025, when total equity was KRW 4.07 trillion, of which KRW 3.93 trillion belonged to controlling shareholders. Operating cash flow rose from KRW 30.4 billion in 2022 to KRW 355 billion in 2025.

On a quarterly basis, operating profit in 4Q 2025 was KRW 9.76 billion, down 63.7% from the prior quarter, before recovering to KRW 61.9 billion in 1Q 2026 and KRW 98.7 billion in 2Q 2026. In 3Q 2026 operating profit was KRW 36.2 billion, down 63.3% from the prior quarter but up 34.6% year on year.

Controlling shareholder net income turned from a net loss of KRW 9.52 billion in 4Q 2025 into a profit of KRW 74.2 billion in 1Q 2026, and then declined for two consecutive quarters to KRW 64.9 billion in 2Q and KRW 2.83 billion in 3Q.

Over the last four quarters, controlling shareholder net income totaled KRW 132.5 billion. The company attributed the 2Q 2026 improvement to higher sales of airbag materials, tire cord and aramid, along with a narrower aramid loss.

Brokers attributed the 3Q decline to higher raw material prices and a lower exchange rate, which hit profitability with a lag. In 2Q 2024, one-off costs of about KRW 10 billion, including voluntary retirement payments tied to the film joint venture, were recorded.

05

Industry analysis

Tire cord is supplied under annual contracts with tire makers, so volumes respond to replacement demand and customer inventory cycles. In July 2026, a Yuanta Securities analyst estimated that tire cord margins had recovered to around 5–6%.

The same report said aramid began full operation for the first time in two years after its expansion and was approaching breakeven. An August 2026 company statement said a higher share of hybrid tire cord products had improved profitability.

Aramid demand is widening into optical cable, defense and aerospace materials and electric vehicle tires. However, KB Securities noted in a November 2026 report that the recovery in aramid market conditions was being delayed.

The chemicals segment is supported by demand for petroleum resins and higher-value products, and growing downstream demand for petroleum resins contributed to 2Q 2026 results. When raw material prices and oil move quickly, product pricing adjusts with a lag, which makes short-term margins volatile.

The mPPO material used as an insulating layer in AI server copper-clad laminates and CPI film for foldable displays are the new demand areas the company has highlighted.

06

Outlook

In its August 2026 earnings announcement, the company said it expected to run its CPI film production lines at maximum capacity from the third quarter.

For mPPO, the company aimed to complete a production facility at its Gimcheon No. 2 plant in 2Q 2026 and plans to add new mass-production equipment in the second half, according to a July 2026 report.

Supply is being expanded mainly with three Taiwanese copper-clad laminate makers, and deliveries to domestic customers are expected to widen by year-end.

Vietnam tire cord equipment relocation is scheduled to be completed in 4Q 2026, with full-scale production and sales starting in 2027, according to an October 2026 ETNews report. Hana Securities projected operating profit of KRW 235.3 billion for 2026 and KRW 260.7 billion for 2027 in a November 2026 report.

The same report cited CPI shipment improvement, full mPPO operation, a narrower aramid loss and the Vietnam relocation as 2027 drivers. Hana expects the new aramid process to reach full operation in early 2027 after a stabilization period.

The report also warned that a lag in passing through lower oil prices could add pressure to short-term earnings. The company is in the process of selling its functional and imaging materials businesses, and the definitive agreement and its earnings impact will become clear through future disclosures. The next checkpoints are the 4Q 2026 provisional results and the 2026 annual report.

Bull factors

Earnings recovery in the first half of 2026

Operating profit rose to KRW 61.9 billion in 1Q 2026 and KRW 98.7 billion in 2Q 2026. The company said higher sales of airbag materials, tire cord and aramid, along with a narrower aramid loss, drove 2Q. Controlling shareholder net income also moved from a net loss in 4Q 2025 to a profit in 1Q 2026. This recovery covers only the first half, so it should be read together with the 3Q decline.

AI and foldable materials ramp-up

mPPO is used as an insulating material in copper-clad laminates for AI servers, and the company plans to add new mass-production equipment in the second half. CPI film is a material for foldable displays, and the company expected its production lines to run at maximum capacity from the third quarter.

Reports say supply is expanding mainly with three Taiwanese laminate makers. Deliveries to domestic customers are expected to grow by year-end.

Portfolio reshaping and balance sheet improvement

The company separated its film business into a joint venture, exiting a unit that had posted losses of several hundred billion won each year. In July 2026 it selected IMM PE as the preferred buyer for its functional and imaging materials businesses.

The debt-to-equity ratio improved from 119.7% in 2022 to 89.0% in 2025. Controlling shareholders' equity stood at KRW 3.93 trillion at the end of 2025.

Bear factors

Sharp drop in 3Q 2026 profit

Operating profit fell to KRW 36.2 billion in 3Q 2026, down 63.3% from the prior quarter. Controlling shareholder net income dropped to KRW 2.83 billion, down 95.6% from the prior quarter. Hana Securities attributed the decline to higher raw material prices and a lower exchange rate. The same analysis said the industrial materials segment swung to an operating loss of KRW 4.8 billion.

Pressure on aramid profitability

Hana Securities said aramid's loss widened despite higher production and sales, due to rising prices of its raw materials PPD and TPC. The same report said an improved product mix could help profitability. KB Securities noted in a November 2026 report that the recovery in aramid market conditions was being delayed. Views that aramid is near breakeven coexist with analysis that its losses widened.

Lower target prices and short-term lag risk

In a November 2026 report, Hana Securities said it lowered its target price from KRW 110,000 to KRW 60,000. KB Securities presented a target of KRW 55,000 in a November 2026 report, down from its prior level. Hana Securities said the lag in passing through lower oil prices could add pressure to short-term earnings. Declining peer valuation multiples were also cited as a reason for cutting estimates.

Risk factors

Raw materials, FX and oil prices

Higher raw material prices and a lower exchange rate were cited as the main causes of weaker recent profitability. Cost increases were reflected with a lag, which reduced third-quarter earnings. When oil prices fall, a backward-lagging effect can appear as materials bought at higher costs flow through later. The pace of raw material procurement and price pass-through will shape margins in the coming quarters.

Portfolio restructuring and one-off costs

In 2Q 2024, one-off costs of about KRW 10 billion, including voluntary retirement payments tied to the film joint venture, weighed on results. An MOU for the sale of the functional and imaging materials businesses was signed in July 2026.

The definitive agreement, timing of the sale and its earnings effect will become clear in future disclosures. How restructuring costs and gains are reflected in quarterly results is a variable to track.

Delays in ramping up new capacity

Vietnam tire cord equipment is scheduled for relocation completion in 4Q 2026, with full production planned from 2027. Hana Securities expects the new mSomething process for mPPO and aramid to reach full operation in early 2027 after stabilization.

Delays in ramp-up or weaker selling prices could push back the timing of earnings improvement. Utilization rates and selling prices can be tracked in quarterly results.
