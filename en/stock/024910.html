KyungchangIndustrial: Profit Recovery Remains the Key Question

KyungchangIndustrial posted its largest sales year of the 2022–2025 span at KRW 705.3 billion in 2025, yet it swung to a net loss attributable to controlling shareholders of KRW 20.8 billion and recorded an operating loss of KRW 5.16 billion in the third quarter of 2026, so profitability recovery is the key variable.

02

Business structure

KyungchangIndustrial was founded in 1961 and listed on KOSDAQ in 1994 as an automotive parts maker. Valueline reports that the company is headquartered in Dalseong-gu, Daegu and operates nine production plants. Its core product is Auto T/M (automatic transmission) parts, which Valueline says account for 58% of sales.

A WiseReport breakdown of 2025 sales by product shows Auto T/M parts at 65.55%, actuators at 8.80%, levers at 6.90% and pedals at 6.84%, so the two sources differ on the mix. In the same breakdown, the 2025 export share was 58.52% for Auto T/M parts and 67.72% for actuators, indicating a large overseas contribution.

A 2024 brokerage analysis said an electric motor parts assembly business, run on customer production lines and booked under other sales, was estimated at roughly 10% of total sales. The same report said sales of this business fell in the first half of 2024 as EV demand slowed.

On the customer side, sales linked to the Hyundai Mobis drive module rose as the market shifted toward eco-friendly vehicles, and the company is broadening its base with orders from PSA, Chrysler and BorgWarner. A 2025 report said the company supplies parts to BYD, which points to a wider EV customer base.

A first-half 2024 brokerage analysis, however, flagged higher outsourcing, depreciation and power costs as pressure on margins. Competition with other listed auto parts makers is largely framed by supply structures tied to OEM sourcing decisions.

04

Earnings analysis

Annual sales rose from KRW 615.6 billion in 2022 to KRW 667.3 billion in 2023 (up 8.4% year on year), fell to KRW 638 billion in 2024 (down 4.4%), and recovered to KRW 705.3 billion in 2025 (up 10.5%), the largest year in the 2022–2025 span.

Operating profit was KRW 19.2 billion in 2022 and peaked at KRW 21 billion in 2023 (up 9.7%). It then fell to KRW 9.64 billion in 2024 (down 54.2%) and to KRW 7.34 billion in 2025 (down 23.8%), declining for two consecutive years from 2024 through 2025.

Sales grew in 2025 while the operating margin slid from 3.1% in 2022 and 3.2% in 2023 to 1.5% in 2024 and 1.0% in 2025, so top-line and profit moved in opposite directions.

A FnGuide summary of 2025 results said sales from the Hyundai Mobis drive module and a reducer rose with the shift to eco-friendly vehicles, but higher one-off costs limited profitability.

Net income attributable to controlling shareholders fell from KRW 20.6 billion in 2022 to KRW 9.13 billion in 2023 (down 55.7%) and KRW 2.82 billion in 2024 (down 69.2%), then turned to a net loss of KRW 20.8 billion in 2025.

Net loss including non-controlling interests was KRW 21.5 billion in 2025, a separate figure from the controlling-shareholder line. At end-2025, total equity was KRW 118.9 billion, equity attributable to controlling shareholders was KRW 110.1 billion, and total liabilities were KRW 372.5 billion.

Quarterly, an operating loss of KRW 2.47 billion in the third quarter of 2025 was followed by operating profit of KRW 4.27 billion in the fourth quarter, then KRW 4.45 billion in the first quarter of 2026 and KRW 8.4 billion in the second quarter.

The third quarter of 2026 brought sales of KRW 165.7 billion (down 11.2% from the prior quarter) and an operating loss of KRW 5.16 billion, widening the loss versus a year earlier. Net loss attributable to controlling shareholders over the last four quarters totaled KRW 20.1 billion.

Operating cash flow was KRW 41.6 billion in 2022 and KRW 74.1 billion in 2023, turned to -KRW 3.17 billion in 2024, and recovered to KRW 23.4 billion in 2025.

05

Industry analysis

The auto parts sector is in the middle of a structural shift toward electrification. A 2024 brokerage analysis pointed to slowing EV demand and said that a recovery could follow as customers increase EV production over the medium term.

A 2025 report also described the EV chasm, a stalled phase during the transition to growth, as a drag on operating profit. This means demand for internal-combustion-oriented components such as automatic transmission parts and demand for electric drivetrain parts are overlapping for longer.

Within the eco-friendly shift, KyungchangIndustrial's sales linked to the Hyundai Mobis drive module increased, and sales tied to reducers also rose. Competition is intense among larger listed parts makers in the same sector, including Hyundai Mobis, Hyundai WIA, Seoyon E-Hwa, SL and HL Mando.

Expansion of overseas customers is the factor most likely to separate the company's position from peers. Automatic transmission parts, which carry a high export share, are affected by overseas demand and exchange rates together.

Overall, the sector appears to be in a transition phase where order structures shift with the pace of the EV transition.

Outlook

A 2025 report said monthly output of the North American eight-speed mid-to-large transmission rose to 40,000 units, stabilizing production yields. The report said the facility, with an annual capacity of 400,000 units, would improve profits once fully operational.

The same report described plans to strengthen sales of BYD-related parts as a growth driver. A FnGuide summary outlined plans to expand exports and secure global customers. Orders from PSA, Chrysler and BorgWarner were cited as evidence of customer diversification.

However, operating profit of KRW 8.4 billion in the second quarter of 2026 was followed by an operating loss of KRW 5.16 billion in the third quarter, and third-quarter sales fell 4.5% from a year earlier. Whether the North American capacity shows up in reported results is therefore the most direct checkpoint.

In the 2026 annual report, the next regular filing, the full-year operating margin versus 1.0% in 2025 will be the main gauge of direction. The next filing will also show how far overseas orders translate into sales. The outlook depends on how quickly EV demand recovers and how customer production schedules are adjusted.

A 2025 report said the company supplies parts to BYD, which points to a wider EV customer base.

Weaker earnings base

Operating profit fell from KRW 21 billion in 2023 to KRW 7.34 billion in 2025, and the operating margin dropped to 1.0%. In the third quarter of 2026, sales fell 11.2% from the prior quarter and the company returned to an operating loss of KRW 5.16 billion, with the loss widening from a year earlier.

A KRW 20.1 billion net loss attributable to controlling shareholders over the last four quarters raises doubts about the durability of the recovery.

Financial burden and cash flow volatility

The debt ratio climbed from 270.5% in 2022 to 313.2% in 2025, with total liabilities of KRW 372.5 billion at end-2025. Total equity was KRW 118.9 billion, of which equity attributable to controlling shareholders was KRW 110.1 billion. Operating cash flow of -KRW 3.17 billion in 2024 shows how volatile cash generation has been.

EV exposure and cost pressure

A 2025 report said operating profit was held back by the EV chasm. A brokerage analysis said sales of electric motor parts fell in the first half of 2024 as EV demand slowed.

Higher outsourcing, depreciation and power costs compressed margins in the first half of 2024, and a FnGuide summary said one-off cost increases limited profitability in 2025.

Risk factors

Customer concentration and OEM demand

Results are tied to a small number of customers, including Hyundai Mobis drive module programs and BYD, and to OEM production volumes. A reduction in customer orders or changes to EV production plans could affect sales and utilization together. Third-quarter 2026 sales fell 4.5% from a year earlier, an early sign of demand volatility.

Balance sheet and cash flow

The debt ratio of 313.2% and total liabilities of KRW 372.5 billion are well above total equity of KRW 118.9 billion. Continued losses attributable to controlling shareholders would shrink equity further and raise leverage.

Operating cash flow turned negative in 2024, so the variability of debt-servicing capacity also needs monitoring.

Currency, cost and one-off expenses

Automatic transmission parts carry a 58.52% export share, so exchange rates and overseas demand affect results. Rising outsourcing and power costs compressed the operating margin in 2024. Larger one-off expenses could widen losses independently of the core business trend.
