02

Business structure

Showbox, founded in June 1999 and listed on KOSDAQ, is a film and drama distributor whose parent is the Orion group. Its business is shifting from film-centric revenue toward drama and content planning and production.

Press reports based on company data show film accounting for 98% of revenue in 2024, falling to 29.1% in 2025 while drama rose to 70.45%. The same reporting put 2025 theatrical screening and audience settlement income at KRW 5.9 billion (9.48%) and content planning and production income at KRW 44.1 billion (70.41%).

Management says investment distribution earns large profits on hits but carries high loss risk, while planning and production recognizes revenue by progress and provides steadier cash flow.

In H1 2026, hits such as The King's Man, If We Were, Salmokji and Gunche lifted Showbox to first place in distributor revenue share at 48.6% (KRW 281.4 billion). On an audience basis, a report says its four H1 films drew 28.37 million viewers, a 75.9% share cited in the semi-annual report.

A April 2026 report, however, put its film distribution share at 1.8%, behind a major US studio's local unit and CJ ENM, so the share figure varies widely by methodology.

In September 2025 Showbox signed a three-year deal with kt Studio Genie to co-invest in and produce 10 commercial films, with Showbox handling domestic distribution and marketing.

04

Earnings analysis

Annual revenue fell from KRW 56.7 billion in 2022 to KRW 40.2 billion in 2023 (down 29.1% year on year), then rose to KRW 93.1 billion in 2024 (up 131.8%), the largest year in the 2022 to 2025 span. Revenue fell again to KRW 62.7 billion in 2025 (down 32.6%).

Operating margin was -5.6% in 2022 and -70.4% in 2023, when losses widened, before turning to 26.3% in 2024 and back to -18.6% in 2025. Net profit attributable to controlling shareholders swung from a loss of KRW 30.3 billion in 2023 to a profit of KRW 27.4 billion in 2024 and a loss of KRW 15 billion in 2025.

Net income including non-controlling interests was KRW 27.4 billion in 2024 and a loss of KRW 15 billion in 2025. Press coverage attributed the 2025 downturn to rising production costs, fewer theater visits and OTT growth eroding the film industry's momentum.

Operating cash flow was -KRW 19.3 billion in 2025, down from KRW 29.3 billion in 2024. Quarterly results peaked in Q1 2026 with revenue of KRW 78.9 billion, operating profit of KRW 21.7 billion and controlling-shareholder net profit of KRW 17.9 billion.

Korean Film Council data show Showbox's Q1 distribution revenue at KRW 176.3 billion (55.4% of the market), of which the film The King's Man contributed KRW 151.8 billion; that council measure differs from the consolidated revenue of KRW 78.9 billion.

Q2 2026 posted revenue of KRW 67.6 billion, operating profit of KRW 12.5 billion and controlling-shareholder net profit of KRW 9.83 billion, and Q3 posted revenue of KRW 16.4 billion, operating profit of KRW 4.39 billion and controlling-shareholder net profit of KRW 3.76 billion, with revenue declining for two straight quarters.

Controlling-shareholder net profit for the last four quarters (Q4 2025 to Q3 2026) totals KRW 25.9 billion. Q3 2026 operating profit turned positive year on year, but revenue fell 4.8% year on year, showing how quarterly results track the box office of individual titles.

05

Industry analysis

The H1 2026 box office rebounded, with market revenue of KRW 579.0 billion (up 41.9% year on year) and 57.05 million admissions (up 34.2%). According to the Korean Film Council's H1 report, foreign film admissions fell 6.9% in the same period, so Korean titles drove the recovery.

Premium screen revenue rose 56.3% to KRW 45.7 billion, 7.9% of the total, and the average ticket price rose to KRW 10,150. The council said the Q1 rebound depended on the performance of a single film amid fewer releases. Whether moviegoers return in Q2 and Q3 remains uncertain.

On the consumption side, domestic OTT usage is close to 89% and paid subscription usage is 54.2%, making platform competition a structural variable. Rising production costs are also cited as a burden.

On investment, filmmakers say few distributors are committing to new films as the industry contracts, and new projects have concentrated at Showbox and a rival distributor, intensifying competition. Industry-wide, quarterly results have tended to swing sharply on a few large titles.

06

Outlook

The next checkpoint is the release of 'Snowfall', scheduled for November 18, 2026. The film stars Kim Yun-seok and Koo Kyo-hwan and is distributed by Showbox, which has also run promotions parodying posters of its recent releases.

Showbox continues to build its lineup on recent titles including If We Were, The King's Man, Salmokji and Gunche. Industry commentary largely holds that audience size and recovery of production costs on upcoming titles will determine earnings durability.

Some observers also say drama, short-form and other IP revenue must continue after hits. The kt Studio Genie agreement covers 10 commercial films over three years, with 50% co-investment in production costs each, while Showbox handles domestic distribution and marketing.

Under that deal, kt Studio Genie handles production efficiency, including AI-based pre-visualization. The partners planned to start the first film within 2025, so the start and scheduling should be verified through filings and press coverage.

Drama accounted for a large share of 2025 revenue, so scheduling and title count are variables for the earnings path. Management has acknowledged loss risk in investment distribution, so upcoming title performance remains the factor that sets the direction of quarterly results. The 2026 annual report will confirm full-year operating margin and the revenue mix by segment.

Bull factors

Earnings recovery on hit titles

After a 2024 turn to operating profit of KRW 24.5 billion, Q1 2026 operating profit peaked at KRW 21.7 billion. That year, The King's Man recorded box-office revenue of KRW 163.1 billion, the top all-time figure. Operating profit stayed positive from Q1 to Q3 2026, which supports the case for earnings capacity.

Growing planning and production mix

Content planning and production income reached KRW 44.1 billion (70.41%) in 2025. Management says planning and production recognizes revenue by progress and strengthens the base business. The revenue mix is spreading away from film box office toward drama.

Distribution capability and market position

Showbox ranked first in H1 2026 distributor revenue share at 48.6%, and its semi-annual report cites a 75.9% audience share. Filmmakers credit its aggressive preview screenings for building word of mouth. Without a large theater chain, it has kept investing in new films, which the industry has noted.

Bear factors

Earnings volatility and loss history

Operating losses of KRW 28.3 billion in 2023 and KRW 11.7 billion in 2025 show repeated loss years. Operating margin fell to -70.4% in 2023 and was -18.6% in 2025. Operating cash flow turned to a -KRW 19.3 billion outflow in 2025.

Downward quarterly revenue trend

Revenue peaked at KRW 78.9 billion in Q1 2026, then fell to KRW 67.6 billion in Q2 and KRW 16.4 billion in Q3. Q3 operating profit of KRW 4.39 billion was well below Q2's KRW 12.5 billion. The Korean Film Council noted the Q1 rebound was concentrated in specific titles, leaving Q2 and Q3 attendance uncertain.

Platform shift and production cost pressure

Domestic OTT usage is close to 89%, with paid subscriptions at 54.2%. Rising production costs and fewer theater visitors were cited as reasons for the 2025 downturn. An April 2026 report put its distribution share at 1.8%, which raises questions about lineup scale against larger distributors.

Risk factors

Concentration on individual titles

Korean Film Council analysis shows the gap between the Q1 2026 top and second films exceeded 13 million admissions. The market is not recovering evenly and depends on individual titles. If upcoming releases underperform, few mechanisms exist to fill the quarterly gap.

Investment and production cost losses

Management says investment distribution earns large profits on hits but carries high loss risk. Rising production costs were cited as a factor behind the 2025 loss. If new titles fail to reach break-even, quarterly results would bear the direct impact.

Trading liquidity and share price volatility

Thinly traded stocks can see outsized moves on small order flow. A 14.73% one-day rise was recorded in an October 8, 2026 report. As a small-cap, the stock can move sharply on a single news item.

12

Overall view

Showbox runs film distribution alongside planning and production as a mid-sized content company, and it has posted quarterly operating profit from the 2024 turn through Q3 2026.

On filed figures, losses persisted in 2022 and 2023, widened in 2023, and after a 2024 profit the company returned to a loss in 2025, so earnings volatility is high.

Hit films in 2026 lifted Showbox to first place in H1 distributor revenue share at 48.6%, but revenue has fallen for two straight quarters since the Q1 peak. Strengths include the growing planning and production mix, distribution capability and a run of quarterly profits in 2026.

Weaknesses include reliance on individual titles, rising production costs and a consumption shift toward OTT. Controlling-shareholder net profit for the last four quarters totals KRW 25.9 billion, so earnings-based comparisons are now possible.

The share price sits below net asset value, but that does not mean business risk has eased. The next checkpoints are the box-office run of the late-November release, FY2026 Q4 preliminary results and the 2026 annual report.
