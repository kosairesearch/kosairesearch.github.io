02

Business structure

Daesung Industrial is an energy company built around petroleum and gas, power generation, energy, and machinery, and it also runs construction and retail-related businesses.

By segment, petroleum and gas accounted for 51% of revenue in the second quarter of 2026, followed by power generation at 29%, energy at 10%, machinery at 5%, and other businesses at 4%, according to the data aggregator Butler.

The petroleum and gas unit centers on fuel and gas sales, and press coverage tied the 2025 revenue increase mainly to higher sales in that unit. The company's profile describes the energy business as mainly oil and gas distribution and cogeneration, with the power unit part of that core.

The machinery unit handles hydraulic and pneumatic equipment, reduction gears, and industrial robot parts, according to company information. Boiler manufacturing sits with a subsidiary, whose 2025 standalone revenue of KRW 125.0 billion and net loss of KRW 46.7 billion were reported in the press.

The construction side pursues mixed-use development, exemplified by the D-Cube City complex, which combines retail, hotel, and cultural functions. Overseas, the company has expanded into China, the United States, and Russia in energy and distribution.

The largest shareholder, Daesung Holdings, held 48.64% as of March 31, 2026, or 51.74% including related parties. Among listed companies in the same consumer and distribution sector, POSCO International, LX International, and SKNetworks have different business mixes, which limits direct comparison.

04

Earnings analysis

Annual revenue went from KRW 1.87 trillion in 2022 to KRW 1.61 trillion in 2023 (down 14.1% year on year) and KRW 1.48 trillion in 2024 (down 8.2%), before rising to KRW 1.57 trillion in 2025 (up 6.0%). Across 2022 to 2025, the largest revenue year was 2022 and the smallest was 2024.

Operating profit fell from KRW 48.3 billion in 2022 to KRW 29.4 billion in 2023 (down 39.0%), KRW 21.8 billion in 2024 (down 26.0%), and KRW 10.8 billion in 2025 (down 50.3%), a three-year consecutive decline from 2023 to 2025.

Operating margin narrowed from 2.6% in 2022 to 1.8% in 2023, 1.5% in 2024, and 0.7% in 2025, so margins fell even in 2025 when revenue grew.

Net income attributable to controlling shareholders moved from a KRW 11.9 billion loss in 2022 to a KRW 6.33 billion loss in 2023 (losses continued but narrowed), a KRW 6.46 billion profit in 2024 (a swing to profit), and a KRW 60.9 billion loss in 2025 (a swing back to loss).

Total net income including non-controlling interests turned from an KRW 8.41 billion profit in 2024 to a KRW 61.7 billion loss in 2025, and the gap between the two measures comes from non-controlling interests.

The company attributed the 2025 decline to impairment charges on investments and intangible assets, reflecting weaker results at a subsidiary, despite higher petroleum-unit sales. Press coverage linked the impairment to the subsidiary's deterioration and described the 2025 net loss as the largest since 2019.

Operating cash flow was KRW 41.9 billion in 2022, KRW 66.9 billion in 2023, KRW 51.1 billion in 2024, and KRW 37.8 billion in 2025, the lowest of the four years.

On a quarterly basis, operating profit of KRW 3.61 billion in the third quarter of 2025 turned into an operating loss of KRW 6.48 billion in the fourth quarter, then returned to a KRW 21.8 billion profit in the first quarter of 2026, before falling to KRW 2.86 billion in the second quarter (down 86.9% from the prior quarter) and rising to KRW 11.7 billion in the third quarter (up 309.6% from the prior quarter and up 224.5% year on year).

The controlling-shareholder loss narrowed to KRW 0.55 million in the third quarter of 2026, but the company has posted losses in two consecutive quarters from the second to the third quarter of 2026, and the controlling-shareholder net loss over the last four quarters totals KRW 39.9 billion.

05

Industry analysis

Daesung Industrial spans petroleum distribution, district energy, plant engineering, and construction, which follow different demand cycles, so a single industry indicator does not explain the group well.

The petroleum and gas unit's revenue moves with international oil prices and sales volumes, and higher petroleum-unit sales were the main reason for the 2025 revenue increase. The power generation unit is presented as relying on demand for district energy, including cogeneration.

In plant work, the company is pursuing more petrochemical EPC orders in the Middle East and Southeast Asia, and it is developing green hydrogen and CCS facilities as new projects, according to an investment analysis profile.

The construction side pursues housing, civil engineering, building, and urban development projects, while the distribution side connects to mixed-use facilities such as D-Cube City.

Among listed companies in the same consumer and distribution sector, LX International, POSCO International, and SKNetworks have larger trading and resource businesses, so their business mix differs from Daesung Industrial's.

The boiler business at the subsidiary depends on heating equipment demand, and that subsidiary's losses were cited as a background to the 2025 impairment.

On energy transition, the company lists hydrogen, ammonia, and renewable energy plants as long-term growth targets, so the shift in weight away from petroleum is a key point to track. The position of the industry cycle can be gauged from petroleum and gas sales trends and from plant order intake.

If the move away from the fourth-quarter 2025 operating loss continues, there is room for operating margin improvement. Whether the recovery is durable will be shown in upcoming periodic reports.

Share price low relative to book value

Price-to-book is well below 1x, and controlling-shareholder equity stood at KRW 625.5 billion at the end of 2025. The share price relative to asset value is a factual comparison point. How quickly that asset value turns into earnings depends on how the subsidiary issues are resolved.

Shareholder return policy and asset efficiency

The value-up plan disclosed in April 2026 includes an earnings-linked dividend, use of treasury shares, and efficiency measures for low-return assets. The largest shareholder, Daesung Holdings, holds 48.64%, a concentrated ownership structure. Implementation of the policy will be confirmed through later filings.

Bear factors

Swing to loss in 2025

Operating profit fell 50.3% to KRW 10.8 billion in 2025, and the controlling-shareholder net loss swung to KRW 60.9 billion. The company cited impairments tied to weaker subsidiary results. The bear case rests on whether such impairments recur, since that determines the quality of annual earnings.

Weakness at the boiler subsidiary

According to press reports, the boiler subsidiary posted a standalone net loss of KRW 46.7 billion in 2025, and its capital impairment ratio reached 97.5%. The parent contributed KRW 29.0 billion to a rights offering in December 2025. If the subsidiary's recovery is delayed, the parent could face repeated impairment burdens.

Declining operating margin

Operating margin declined from 2.6% in 2022 to 0.7% in 2025. Revenue rose 6.0% in 2025, yet operating profit fell 50.3%, so top-line growth did not carry through to profit. The drop to KRW 2.86 billion in operating profit in the second quarter of 2026 raises questions about how durable the recovery is.

Risk factors

Subsidiary impairment and capital injections

Subsidiary impairments pulled 2025 net income sharply lower, and further impairments or capital injections could follow if the subsidiary's capital deficit persists. The September 2026 capital reduction and October 2026 rights offering filings at consolidated subsidiaries relate to this pattern. The scale of impact can be assessed from the amounts and purposes stated in the original filings.

Oil price and energy demand swings

The petroleum and gas unit accounted for 51% of second-quarter 2026 revenue, so it is highly exposed to swings in international oil prices and sales volumes. The power generation unit depends on district energy demand, including cogeneration. Each unit's contribution to profit should be confirmed from disclosed figures.

Balance sheet burden and cash flow

Total liabilities stood at KRW 1.09 trillion at the end of 2025, with a debt ratio of 169.0%. Operating cash flow of KRW 37.8 billion in 2025 was the lowest of the four years shown. If the earnings recovery does not translate into cash generation, borrowing burden could come into focus.
