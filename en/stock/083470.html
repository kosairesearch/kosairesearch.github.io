Report overview

Heavy-water and solid-state bets face a test as OLED sales shrink

EMnI posted losses in each quarter from the first quarter through the third quarter of 2026 as its core OLED materials sales shrank, and heavy-water processing and all-solid-state battery materials are the routes the company has put forward for a turnaround.

02

Business structure

EMnI was founded in 1999 and listed on KOSDAQ in 2008. In 2020 it merged with an OLED materials specialist to cover the full OLED materials chain.

According to Alphasquare's company profile, OLED materials development and production account for about 89% of sales, while precision molds and parts, grouped as process solutions, make up about 11%.

WiseReport describes a fabless system in which the company keeps core technology in-house and outsources other processes to hold down costs. Its main customers are Chinese OLED makers, and the company is focusing on expanding sales and marketing to them.

Its heavy-water processing unit supplies processed heavy-water products for semiconductor and display uses, and the company says India's heavy water board granted formal export approval in February 2026.

It brought in 3 tonnes of heavy water that month and confirmed an additional 5 tonnes in October 2026, for 8 tonnes in total.

The all-solid-state battery materials business runs through a subsidiary, which opened an energy materials center in June 2026 and is moving oxide-based solid electrolyte production equipment in. On batteries, WiseReport notes a supply agreement with an Indian company for lithium-ion cells used in drones.

The disclosed financials are not split by segment, so the contributions of OLED materials and heavy water cannot be separated from the reported figures. The company points to its core materials technology and in-house process control as its competitive edge.

04

Earnings analysis

Annual revenue fell from KRW 34.5 billion in 2022 to KRW 26.3 billion in 2023 (down 23.7% year on year), recovered to KRW 29.7 billion in 2024 (up 12.9% year on year), and then dropped to KRW 18.4 billion in 2025 (down 38.1% year on year), its smallest level. 2022 was the largest year of the 2022-2025 period.

Operating profit of KRW 0.69 billion in 2022 swung to an operating loss of KRW 1.29 billion in 2023, returned to an operating profit of KRW 0.34 billion in 2024, and then swung back to an operating loss of KRW 1.93 billion in 2025.

The operating margin was 2.0% in 2022, -4.9% in 2023, 1.1% in 2024 and -10.5% in 2025, with 2025 the lowest.

Net income attributable to controlling shareholders was a net loss of KRW 0.12 billion in 2022 and a net loss of KRW 0.39 billion in 2023, so losses continued and widened; it turned to a net profit of KRW 1.49 billion in 2024 and then swung back to a net loss of KRW 4.62 billion in 2025.

Over the same period, net income including non-controlling interests was a net loss of KRW 4.84 billion in 2025, larger than the loss attributable to controlling shareholders. In 2024, revenue grew 12.9%, and both operating profit and net income attributable to controlling shareholders turned positive together.

The debt-to-equity ratio was 57.0% in 2022, 58.0% in 2023 and 58.0% in 2024, then fell to 31.7% in 2025; at the end of 2025, total liabilities were KRW 3.22 billion and total equity was KRW 10.2 billion, of which the equity attributable to controlling shareholders was KRW 10.3 billion.

Operating cash flow was KRW 2.05 billion in 2022, -KRW 1.39 billion in 2023, KRW 0.94 billion in 2024 and -KRW 0.47 billion in 2025, changing direction every year.

On a quarterly basis, the company started from revenue of KRW 3.0 billion and an operating loss of KRW 0.48 billion in the third quarter of 2025, went through revenue of KRW 1.73 billion and an operating loss of KRW 1.27 billion in the first quarter of 2026, and then in the second quarter posted revenue of KRW 4.41 billion (up 154.8% quarter on quarter) and an operating loss of KRW 0.27 billion, a narrower loss.

In the third quarter of 2026, revenue was KRW 1.43 billion (down 67.7% quarter on quarter and down 52.4% year on year) and the operating loss was KRW 1.09 billion, so the deficit widened again.

The net loss attributable to controlling shareholders was KRW 0.96 billion in the first quarter of 2026, KRW 0.55 billion in the second quarter and KRW 1.61 billion in the third quarter. The fourth quarter of 2025 is missing from this trend because quarterly data are not included in the disclosed results.

In a preliminary explanation released on May 15, 2026, the company described the first quarter as the trough in earnings and cited one-off factors such as weak revenue from its OLED supply model, legal costs related to the management control dispute, and R&D costs for a solid-state battery at a subsidiary.

WiseReport summarized, as of September 14, 2026, that consolidated revenue in the first half of 2026 fell 50.0% year on year and the operating loss increased 461.1%.

05

Industry analysis

Demand for OLED materials moves with the operating rates and investment of Chinese OLED panel makers, and EMnI's core business is directly tied to that cycle.

The company attributed its weak first quarter of 2026 to lower sales under its supply model for core OLED materials, and it expects supply to existing customers to recover from the second quarter.

The spread of deuterated materials is a positive, yet WiseReport notes that operating losses hurt profitability even as deuterated materials gained adoption. Heavy-water supply depends on a single source, the Indian heavy water board.

The CEO said the company is not considering another import source for now and is prioritizing internalizing its partnership with Indian counterparts to secure supply.

The all-solid-state battery materials business is still at the production-base stage, with targets of at least 100 kilograms of solid electrolyte in 2026 and 600 kilograms in 2027. This unit carries R&D costs at its subsidiary, which weigh on near-term profit.

The disclosed industry classification is chemicals, but most sales come from display materials, so the company straddles display and secondary-battery materials cycles. Unlike large chemical makers that scale through big plants and commodity products, EMnI operates a fabless model focused on core technology.

06

Outlook

According to what the company said in an interview with Seoul Economic Daily around July 2026, heavy water processing revenue in 2026 is expected to be about KRW 14.0 billion with operating profit of about KRW 2.5 billion, and in 2027 the company targets heavy water processing revenue of more than KRW 20 billion.

Cumulative revenue from the same business in 2026 was reported in October 2026 as about KRW 3.5 billion. The gap between the annual target and the cumulative results is the first checkpoint for the heavy water business.

The supply base totals 8 tonnes cumulatively, consisting of 3 tonnes in February 2026 and an additional 5 tonnes confirmed in October 2026, and the CEO explained that it takes about 2 months from raw material import to finished product production and delivery to the customer.

The company said it is considering a staged investment strategy based on the heavy water supply business.

An idea was also presented that, once the joint venture's spent heavy water reprocessing plant is completed, waste purification and a tritium extraction and sales business would become possible, but there is no confirmed material on the completion schedule.

For solid-state battery materials, the company targets production of at least 100 kilograms in 2026 and 600 kilograms in 2027, after equipment installation and trial operation following the opening of the EM Energy Materials Center.

In the battery business, a supply contract for lithium-ion cells for drones with an Indian company is presented as the revenue touchpoint.

In its core OLED business, the company cut monthly fixed costs by 17% from the previous level, and it said it expects the effects of lower fixed costs and the expansion of new businesses to be reflected in turn.

On governance, a disclosure of a share transfer agreement involving a change of the largest shareholder and a notice convening an extraordinary general meeting of shareholders were issued on October 15, 2026, so it needs to be checked whether the closing of the transaction and the outcome of the shareholders' meeting could change who drives the business. The next checkpoints are the 2026 annual report and the first-quarter 2027 earnings announcement.

08

Bull factors

Heavy-water processing revenue is materializing

Reports from October 2026 put cumulative heavy-water revenue at about KRW 3.5 billion, and the company has secured 8 tonnes of Indian heavy water in total. The company says the cycle from raw material import to customer delivery takes about two months.

Second-quarter 2026 revenue of KRW 4.41 billion, up 154.8% from the prior quarter, is consistent with that expansion.

A narrower operating loss in the second quarter

The operating loss narrowed to KRW 274 million in the second quarter of 2026 from KRW 1.27 billion in the first. The company says it cut monthly fixed costs by 17%, which it expects to convert revenue growth into profit improvement. It has also described the first quarter of 2026 as the low point.

Building production capacity for solid-state battery materials

The company opened its energy materials center in June 2026 and began bringing oxide-based solid electrolyte production equipment in July. Its targets are at least 100 kilograms in 2026 and 600 kilograms in 2027.

A supply agreement with an Indian company for drone lithium-ion cells is presented as a revenue point for the battery business.

09

Bear factors

Third-quarter revenue fell again and the loss widened

Third-quarter 2026 revenue was KRW 1.43 billion, down 67.7% from the prior quarter, and the operating loss widened to KRW 1.09 billion. The net loss attributable to controlling shareholders of KRW 1.61 billion was the largest of the three 2026 quarters.

Over the last four quarters that loss totaled KRW 6.21 billion, so the second-quarter rebound did not carry through.

Contraction in the core OLED business

WiseReport summarized first-half 2026 consolidated revenue as down 50.0% year on year, attributing the decline mainly to lower core OLED materials sales. Annual revenue of KRW 18.4 billion in 2025 is below KRW 34.5 billion in 2022.

The company is pushing sales to Chinese OLED makers, but the disclosed figures do not yet show a recovery in the core business.

Ownership changes and deal uncertainty

A report from August 2026 said the sale of management control had been completed, while on October 15, 2026, a share transfer agreement involving a change in the largest shareholder and a notice of an extraordinary shareholder meeting were disclosed.

In October 2026 the company also disclosed a response, still unconfirmed, to an exchange inquiry about a significant market movement. Dependence on a single Indian supply source for heavy water is another burden.

Risk factors

Single-source raw material supply

Heavy-water feedstock depends on approval from India's heavy water board, and the CEO has said no alternative import source is being considered for now. Delays in export or import permits could disrupt supply. Any interruption would directly affect the heavy-water processing revenue targets.

Ownership structure and change in the largest shareholder

As of October 30, 2026, the largest shareholder group, DSP Corporation and one other party, held 27.66%, while another investor group held 26.66%.

Depending on whether the change-of-control agreement disclosed on October 15, 2026 closes and what the extraordinary meeting decides, management and the direction of the business could shift. Changed deal terms could also affect continuity in the heavy-water and battery projects.

Continued losses and cash flow

The company has reported losses in each quarter from the first quarter through the third quarter of 2026, and operating cash flow was negative KRW 470 million in 2025. The subsidiary's solid-state battery R&D costs may be recurring rather than one-off, unlike items the company has described as non-recurring.

If losses continue against total equity of KRW 10.2 billion, the change in financial capacity becomes the key variable.

Overall view

EMnI's revenue fell from KRW 34.5 billion in 2022 to KRW 18.4 billion in 2025, and the company moved into operating and net losses attributable to controlling shareholders in 2025.

Revenue rebounded in the second quarter of 2026, but third-quarter revenue fell to KRW 1.43 billion and the operating loss widened, leaving losses in each of the three quarters from the first through the third.

Heavy-water processing and solid-state battery materials are the axes the company has put forward for a turnaround, yet cumulative heavy-water revenue of about KRW 3.5 billion as of October 2026 shows a gap to the full-year target remains.

A change in the largest shareholder and a pending extraordinary meeting are also in progress. Valuation in this period reflects a company with no earnings-based multiple and a price above book value.

The next checkpoints are closing of the ownership deal and meeting outcome, the 2026 annual business report, and first-quarter 2027 results.
