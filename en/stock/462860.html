Flat Sales and Slower Profit Amid FX and Digital Banking Growth

02

Business structure

Dozn was founded in 2017 and listed on KOSDAQ in March 2025 as a B2B digital financial solutions company.

Its core products fall into three groups: digital banking solutions (firm banking and virtual accounts), FX solutions (cross-border fund management and self-service currency exchange kiosks), and data solutions (The Apartment ERP and messaging services).

Its customers are service operators such as online shopping malls, fintech platforms, card companies and insurers, and it automates transfers and collections by connecting directly to bank networks. Customers cited in company materials include Samsung Pay, Naver Pay, Korbit and Gmarket.

Revenue is recognized net, counting only the portion of bank fees attributable to Dozn, which the company says keeps variable cost burden limited.

Firm banking functions as near-essential infrastructure for platforms offering remittance and payment features, and constant connectivity to many bank networks and experience in handling outages are cited as barriers to entry.

First-half 2026 digital banking service revenue of KRW 12.1 billion and FX solution revenue of KRW 7.4 billion together total KRW 19.5 billion, a large share of consolidated first-half revenue of KRW 30.7 billion. Revenue sizes for the remaining data solutions are a matter for separate segment detail.

As of October 30, 2026, the largest shareholder group, including related parties, held 51.60%, and Kakao Pay held 8.28%.

04

Earnings analysis

Annual sales in 2025 were KRW 60 billion, down 4.3% from KRW 62.7 billion in 2024, but operating profit rose 36.0% to KRW 13.6 billion. Operating margin climbed from 16.0% in 2024 to 22.7% in 2025, so profitability improved even as revenue shrank.

Net profit attributable to controlling shareholders rose 9.9% to KRW 11 billion in 2025 from KRW 10 billion in 2024, while net profit including non-controlling interests was KRW 10.7 billion in 2025 against KRW 9.92 billion in 2024.

The 2024 profit decline was linked in press coverage to one-off items such as IPO-related costs and restructuring, and to the loss of major overseas clients in the cross-border business.

Operating cash flow increased from KRW 20.9 billion in 2024 to KRW 62.4 billion in 2025, and the debt ratio fell from 179.6% to 162.4%. At year-end 2025, total equity was KRW 73.6 billion, of which KRW 73.1 billion belonged to controlling shareholders, and total liabilities were KRW 119.5 billion.

On a quarterly basis, operating profit was KRW 3.97 billion in the third quarter of 2025 and KRW 3.94 billion in the fourth quarter, before the first quarter of 2026 reached KRW 3.9 billion, the company's highest first-quarter result on record.

Sales then fell to KRW 15 billion in the second quarter of 2026 and to KRW 14.8 billion in the third quarter, two consecutive quarterly declines.

Operating profit has fallen for four consecutive quarters, from KRW 3.94 billion in the fourth quarter of 2025 to KRW 2.56 billion in the third quarter of 2026, and was down 35.5% year on year in that quarter.

The company attributed the third-quarter decline to a temporary effect from trading adjustments and a weaker won.

Net profit attributable to controlling shareholders still rose 4.7% to KRW 3.73 billion in the same quarter, while pre-tax profit of KRW 4.19 billion exceeded operating profit, suggesting non-operating items contributed to the gap. Net profit attributable to controlling shareholders over the last four quarters totaled KRW 11.9 billion.

05

Industry analysis

Dozn's business is tied to both domestic electronic payment demand and overseas payment and currency exchange demand. The company attributes growth in digital banking demand to the expansion of domestic e-commerce and the spread of simple payment and remittance services.

The FX segment is growing on more cross-border trading partners, a rise in foreign tourists and greater use of self-service currency exchange kiosks. Exchange rates cut both ways, and the company said a weaker won and trading adjustments contributed to the third-quarter decline.

In firm banking, constant connectivity to many bank networks and experience handling outages are cited as entry barriers that limit new competitors. Dozn is classified in the telecom sector, alongside SK Telecom, KT and LG Uplus, but its revenue model differs from mobile carriers, so direct comparisons are limited.

Some information services classify Dozn as software, so the peer group changes with the classification used. The most useful cycle indicators are domestic payment volumes and cross-border transaction values, and whether these move in line with quarterly results.

06

Outlook

In its first-half 2026 announcement, the company said it would accelerate growth in the second half by expanding new domestic clients and fully commercializing overseas business. Broadening the revenue contribution of overseas subsidiaries was also presented as a second-half growth path.

For the first quarter of 2026, the company said it secured new large clients in its core solutions and improved operating margin by 7.9 percentage points year on year.

For the first nine months of 2026, FX solution revenue reached KRW 10.8 billion, up 14.9%, which the company linked to more cross-border trading partners and more foreign tourists.

Press coverage reported plans for cross-border payment and settlement services based on stablecoins, and whether this cooperation leads to an operating service and business results is seen as the key to securing a new growth driver.

On shareholder returns, the company decided in the first quarter of 2026 to buy back about KRW 3 billion of its own shares and disclosed the cancellation of 139,003 treasury shares. An interim dividend record date was announced in June 2026.

The 2026 annual business report, the next regular filing, will show full-year operating margin and segment revenue on a disclosed basis.

The next checkpoints are whether the effect of new domestic clients halts the string of quarterly sales declines, and whether overseas business contributions appear in reported figures.

Bear factors

Four consecutive quarterly declines in operating profit

Operating profit fell from KRW 3.94 billion in the fourth quarter of 2025 to KRW 2.56 billion in the third quarter of 2026, down 29.7% from the prior quarter and 35.5% year on year in the latest quarter. Sales also declined in the second and third quarters of 2026. Third-quarter 2026 revenue of KRW 14.8 billion was down 2.1% year on year.

Limited top-line growth

Sales in 2025 were KRW 60 billion, down 4.3% from KRW 62.7 billion in 2024. Cumulative sales for the first three quarters of 2026 were KRW 47.5 billion, up only 0.8% year on year. With top-line growth this limited, profit improvement depends on changes in operating margin.

Volatility from exchange rates and client concentration

The company attributed the third-quarter 2026 decline to trading adjustments and a weaker won. Press coverage reported that the loss of major overseas clients in the cross-border business weighed on 2024 profit.

Because new large clients have driven growth, swings in individual client volumes could have a sizable effect on results.

Risk factors

Earnings composition and one-off items

Operating profit declined in the third quarter of 2026, yet pre-tax profit of KRW 4.19 billion exceeded it. How far non-operating items supported controlling-shareholder profit changes how durable the earnings are.

The company described the third-quarter decline as temporary, while press coverage linked 2024 profit to one-off items such as IPO costs and restructuring.

Exchange rates and overseas execution

A weaker won and trading adjustments were cited as reasons for the third-quarter decline. The company said it would fully commercialize overseas business in the second half, but sales declines continued through the third quarter of 2026.

The stablecoin-based payment business is still at the service-build stage, so its results may take time to become visible.

Ownership concentration and liquidity

As of October 30, 2026, the largest shareholder group including related parties held 51.60%, and Kakao Pay held 8.28%. The free float ratio is 47.07%, limiting the number of shares available for trading. Trading concentrated in a small float can amplify price swings.

Overall view

Dozn's 2025 was a year of improved earnings structure, with operating profit up 36.0% and operating margin reaching 22.7%. However, quarterly sales have fallen for two consecutive quarters in 2026, and operating profit has declined for four consecutive quarters since the fourth quarter of 2025.

Net profit attributable to controlling shareholders rose year on year in the third quarter of 2026, and the composition of non-operating items accounts for that gap.

Digital banking and FX solutions both grew double digits in the first half of 2026, and new domestic clients and commercialization of overseas business form the growth path the company has set out.
