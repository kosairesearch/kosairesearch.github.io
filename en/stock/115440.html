Report overview

Leading PTN Supplier Still Verifying Earnings Recovery

02

Business structure

Woori-Net is a telecom equipment maker whose core product is packet transport network (PTN) equipment for optical networks. A February 2025 Hanaw Investment & Securities report showed the product mix as transport equipment 56.5%, access gateways 6.2%, cellular IoT 7.6%, services 11.4% and other merchandise 9.2%.

A different classification of second-quarter 2026 sales shows telecom equipment and IoT modules at 72%, installation and maintenance at 16%, and merchandise at 12%, so the two splits cannot be compared directly.

The company has exited its content business and is pursuing entry into the defense IT market, with plans to tailor its tera-class POTN and quantum cryptography technology to defense networks. Its key customers are KT and defense procurement agencies.

A KRW 79.9 billion next-generation defense broadband network equipment contract with KT, signed in July 2023, formed a large part of its earnings base. A ten-year maintenance contract worth KRW 43.6 billion was added in August 2024.

The company also plans to widen its customer base to financial institutions and public agencies. Media reports said it held the number one share of the domestic PTN market.

Compared with the three major telecom operators its revenue scale is small, and because it combines equipment supply with maintenance and services, quarterly results swing with project timing.

04

Earnings analysis

Annual results started from revenue of KRW 62.3 billion and an operating loss of KRW 6.95 billion in 2022. In 2023 revenue rose to KRW 119.1 billion, up 91.2%, and operating profit of KRW 8.48 billion marked a turn to profit.

In 2024 revenue reached KRW 131.6 billion, up 10.5%, and operating profit reached KRW 20.1 billion, up 136.6%, making 2024 the largest year of 2022 to 2025. The operating margin moved from -11.2% in 2022 to 7.1% in 2023 and 15.2% in 2024.

In 2025 revenue fell 42.6% to KRW 75.5 billion, operating profit fell 90.3% to KRW 1.95 billion, and attributable net income fell 82.0% to KRW 3.67 billion, leaving an operating margin of 2.6%. The 2024 result rested on the defense network project contracted in 2023.

The 2025 decline reflected a base effect from a defense ministry project of over KRW 70 billion won in 2024, as Hanaw noted in July 2025. Operating cash flow went from KRW -10.5 billion in 2022 and KRW -14.8 billion in 2023 to KRW 43 billion in 2024 and KRW 16.6 billion in 2025.

Quarterly, operating profit of KRW 473 million in the third quarter of 2025 turned into an operating loss of KRW 314 million in the fourth quarter. The first quarter of 2026 posted an operating loss of KRW 3.99 billion and an attributable net loss of KRW 3.33 billion.

The second quarter returned to operating profit of KRW 505 million on revenue of KRW 22.4 billion, but the third quarter revenue of KRW 19.9 billion produced operating profit of KRW 38.78 million. Third-quarter attributable net loss of KRW 102 million marked a return to a loss.

The debt ratio fell from 88.0% in 2022 to 35.0% at the end of 2025, and total equity was KRW 99.9 billion. Quarterly profit has swung sharply, so the share of one-off project gains should be checked in the next periodic report.

05

Industry analysis

The domestic transport network equipment market moves with telecom carriers' 5G investment and traffic growth. Hanaw Investment & Securities said in a March 2026 report that linked 5G SA investment and indoor base station rollouts should lift demand for transport equipment.

The same report cited a government statement that backbone traffic could rise several times over if physical AI spreads. Indoor base station construction also adds fronthaul transport demand. The brokerage view is that the leading domestic PTN share should concentrate this incremental demand on Woori-Net.

Replacement and new build demand, however, depends on carriers' investment schedules and does not translate into near-term earnings automatically. In 2025 revenue fell on a defense project base effect, and in the first half of 2026 both product and service revenue declined.

Within the telecom sector, Woori-Net sits as a supplier to large carrier capex programs, so both carrier investment plans and defense orders drive its results. A March 2026 report also described a view that wired equipment stocks tend to follow wireless equipment rallies.

Outlook

In a March 2026 report, Hanaw Investment & Securities estimated 2026 revenue at KRW 115.8 billion and operating profit at KRW 8.5 billion. First-half 2026 revenue declined 15.1% year on year and operating results turned to a loss, so the trajectory has diverged sharply from that estimate.

The company has exited its content business and plans to enter the defense IT market, with its tera-class POTN and quantum cryptography technology aimed at defense networks. The size and timing of defense IT orders are not yet visible in disclosed figures.

A ten-year maintenance contract worth KRW 43.6 billion signed in August 2024 is cited as a support for the revenue floor. The company also plans to expand orders from financial institutions and public agencies.

Transport network demand tied to 5G SA is linked to spectrum allocation requirements, so the timing of earnings recognition depends on carriers' investment schedules.

Physical AI traffic growth is presented as a medium-term demand driver, and its link to specific company orders will be confirmed through individual disclosures.

The next checkpoints are the 2026 provisional annual results, expected around early February 2027 based on the announcement on February 9, 2026, and the 2026 annual business report. The key question then is whether the operating margin shows a recovery trend toward the 2024 level.

Defense IT and financial or public sector order disclosures can arrive at any time, so their timing is hard to pin down.

Bull factors

Transport network replacement and 5G SA demand

Hanaw Investment & Securities argued in a March 2026 report that 5G standalone investment and indoor base station rollouts will translate into transport equipment demand. The brokerage also expected that growth in transport equipment demand would concentrate on Woori-Net as the leading domestic share holder. This thesis reaches reported earnings only when carrier investment timing and company orders connect.

Defense IT and quantum cryptography expansion plan

The company has exited its content business and is pursuing defense IT, aiming to tailor its tera-class POTN and quantum cryptography technology to defense networks.

Its experience in defense network work is a relevant asset, since the 2023 KRW 79.9 billion defense broadband network equipment contract with KT formed a basis for earnings.

Improved financial strength

Total equity at the end of 2025 was KRW 99.9 billion, and the debt ratio fell to 35.0% from 88.0% in 2022. Operating cash flow was positive at KRW 43 billion in 2024 and KRW 16.6 billion in 2025, after negative readings in 2022 and 2023. This shift is one basis for reading an improvement in the business structure.

Bear factors

First-half 2026 decline and swing to losses

Consolidated revenue for the first half of 2026 fell 15.1% year on year, and operating results turned to a loss. The KRW 3.99 billion operating loss in the first quarter was partly offset by KRW 505 million of operating profit in the second quarter, but third-quarter operating profit fell to KRW 38.78 million. Both revenue and profit are trending weaker, which forms the core of the bear case.

Cumulative losses over the last four quarters

Attributable net losses have accumulated over the last four quarters. The third-quarter 2026 attributable result was a loss of KRW 102 million, reversing the prior quarter's profit. Because earnings-based multiples cannot be applied while losses persist, the timing of the earnings recovery remains uncertain.

Gap between forecasts and actual results

Hanaw Investment & Securities estimated 2026 revenue at KRW 115.8 billion and operating profit at KRW 8.5 billion in its March 2026 report. Those figures assumed a large recovery from 2025 revenue of KRW 75.5 billion and operating profit of KRW 1.95 billion.

First-half 2026 revenue fell year on year and operating results turned to a loss, so the estimates are diverging from reported results.

Risk factors

Customer and project concentration

Revenue depends heavily on KT and defense procurement projects. A defense ministry project above KRW 70 billion in 2024 produced record earnings, and the base effect afterward reduced both revenue and profit. A project completion or delayed order can move quarterly results sharply.

Execution of business transition

The company has exited content and is transitioning toward defense IT and quantum cryptography. The timing and size of new market orders have not yet appeared in disclosures, so transition costs could hit earnings first.

Whether the shift can offset the decline in core transport equipment revenue will show up in future quarterly results.

Carrier investment timing risk

Transport demand is linked to 5G SA investment, indoor base station rollouts and spectrum allocation requirements. If carrier investment slows or compliance timelines slip, the earnings impact of replacement demand will also be delayed. Whether the industry recovery thesis turns into actual orders is the most direct variable.

Overall view

Woori-Net is a leading domestic transport equipment supplier, and 5G SA investment and physical AI traffic growth are cited as grounds for transport network replacement demand.

However, operating profit fell from KRW 20.1 billion in 2024 to KRW 1.95 billion in 2025, and in the first half of 2026 revenue declined and operating results turned to a loss. The attributable net loss over the last four quarters totals KRW 2.09 billion.
