Credit Costs Push The Leadcorp into a Q3 2026 Operating Loss

The Leadcorp swung to an operating loss of KRW 1.25 billion in Q3 2026, and credit costs are the key variable against its shareholder-return and overseas expansion plans.

  1. 1

    Q3 2026 revenue rose 20.2% year on year to KRW 125.9 billion, but the company posted an operating loss of KRW 1.25 billion.

  2. 2

    Net income attributable to controlling shareholders bottomed at KRW 8.97 billion in 2023 and rose for two straight years, reaching KRW 13.3 billion in 2025.

  3. 3

    The company raised its year-end dividend for fiscal 2025, met the high-dividend company criteria, and stated it will keep the payout ratio at 30% or above.

  4. 4

    The debt-to-equity ratio rose to 139.6% at the end of 2025 from 114.3% in 2024, and operating cash flow turned to a net outflow of -KRW 50.6 billion.

  5. 5

02

Business structure

The Leadcorp was founded in 1977 for petroleum business and now runs three main businesses: petroleum wholesale and retail, expressway service areas, and consumer lending (licensed moneylending).

According to a Sisaweek report, most operating revenue comes from petroleum wholesale and consumer lending, and a large part of operating profit has depended on the lending business. The petroleum unit operates a fuel station network under a general dealership agreement with S-Oil.

The consumer lending unit is a second-tier moneylender that was the first in its industry to offer online loans. According to the Korea Financial News, the company has designed and built its own alternative credit scoring model to upgrade underwriting.

The service area business operates expressway rest stops and forms one arm of the group. On the subsidiary side, the company decided to acquire 47.3% of the common shares of Mason Capital for KRW 61.6 billion and take part in management.

Its Kazakhstan subsidiary LC LOMBARD serves as the base for overseas lending in Central Asia. Segment revenue shares should be taken from the business segment information in the annual report.

04

Earnings analysis

Annual revenue fell from 6,478 hundred million won in 2022 to 4,906 hundred million won in 2023 (down 24.3% year over year), 4,205 hundred million won in 2024 (down 14.3% year over year), and 3,877 hundred million won in 2025 (down 7.8% year over year), declining for three straight years from 2023 to 2025.

Operating profit went from 593 hundred million won in 2022 to an operating loss of 135 hundred million won in 2023, then returned to profit at 216 hundred million won in 2024. Operating profit in 2025 was 109 hundred million won, down 49.2% from the previous year.

The operating margin moved from 9.2% in 2022 to -2.8% in 2023, 5.1% in 2024, and 2.8% in 2025.

Net profit attributable to controlling shareholders dropped from 320 hundred million won in 2022 to 89.7 hundred million won in 2023 (down 72.0% year over year), then rose for two straight years, to 96.4 hundred million won in 2024 (up 7.5% year over year) and 133 hundred million won in 2025 (up 38.4% year over year).

Net profit for 2025, in the same year, was 117 hundred million won, which is smaller than the net profit attributable to controlling shareholders.

According to a Korea Financial News report, the company explained that it raised profitability through more advanced credit screening in its consumer finance segment, where business conditions are difficult.

However, net profit attributable to non-controlling shareholders for full-year 2025 came to minus 16 hundred million won (based on FnGuide consolidated financials).

Operating cash flow fell from 1,472 hundred million won in 2022, 2,545 hundred million won in 2023, and 131 hundred million won in 2024 to -506 hundred million won in 2025. The debt ratio declined from 183.5% in 2022 to 118.9% in 2023 and 114.3% in 2024, then rose to 139.6% in 2025.

Looking at the quarterly trend, operating profit was 3.4 hundred million won in Q3 2025, 45.6 hundred million won in Q4, 18.4 hundred million won in Q1 2026, and 24.5 hundred million won in Q2, followed by a swing to an operating loss of 12.5 hundred million won in Q3.

According to a Vegan News report, based on the Q3 2026 quarterly report, bad debt expense was 228.1 hundred million won, up 88.9% from the same period a year earlier, and operating expenses exceeded operating revenue. Net loss attributable to controlling shareholders for the same period was 40.2 hundred million won.

The sum of net profit attributable to controlling shareholders over the last four quarters is 62.1 hundred million won, and the Q3 loss is included in this total.

05

Industry analysis

Licensed moneylending in Korea has faced structural pressure since the statutory maximum interest rate was lowered, with some lenders cutting new lending or exiting the business, according to Sisaweek. The Leadcorp has responded by expanding equity investments in recent years to find new growth engines.

Government inclusive-finance policy is another variable: in an October 2026 announcement, the company said it expanded lending to low-credit and thin-file borrowers. This direction can raise both pricing pressure and credit cost burden, which makes asset-quality management more important.

Its business mix of petroleum distribution and expressway service areas alongside lending is distinctive, but the lending unit competes with the broader second-tier financial sector.

Overseas, the Kazakhstan unit is building a collateral-backed microfinance business, and the Korea Financial News reported that collateral loans as the main product can lower default risk.

Central Asia and Southeast Asia are presented as medium- to long-term growth drivers, but the overseas unit is still small relative to domestic consumer lending. Domestic industry conditions and policy changes will continue to weigh heavily on results.

06

Outlook

The company has set a target of raising consumer lending assets above KRW 1 trillion within 2026 while keeping delinquency ratios stable, as reported by Money Today and Dealsite. Whether that target was met should be confirmed against the annual report.

The company also plans to upgrade its risk management framework, including risk monitoring and stress testing, and to secure liquidity. On shareholder returns, the company stated it will keep the dividend payout ratio at 30% or above.

Total dividends rose from KRW 2.56 billion for 2024 to KRW 3.84 billion for 2025, and the payout ratio rose to 32.69%.

Treasury shares were retired in February 2026, and in late July 2026 the board decided to retire the remaining 200,000 shares, completing the disposal or retirement of treasury shares under its value-up plan.

Overseas, the company injected about KRW 5 billion into its Kazakhstan unit LC LOMBARD in April 2026 to open new branches. Domestically, it plans company-wide AI transformation to improve efficiency and risk management.

The Mason Capital acquisition is seen by Sisaweek as a platform for expanding the credit finance business. These targets depend on credit costs and delinquencies stabilizing, so whether the sharp rise in Q3 2026 provisions carries into later quarters is the next point to check.

Bull factors

Expanded shareholder returns and completed treasury share retirement

The company raised its fiscal 2025 dividend, met the high-dividend criteria, and said it will keep the payout ratio at 30% or above. Treasury shares were retired in February 2026, and the remaining shares were retired after a July 2026 board decision. Total dividends rose from KRW 2.56 billion to KRW 3.84 billion.

Earnings recovery and two years of growth through 2025

Net income attributable to controlling shareholders rose for two years after the 2023 low, reaching KRW 13.3 billion in 2025. Operating profit returned to a surplus in 2024 and stayed positive at KRW 10.9 billion in 2025. Revenue declined, but profitability recovered directionally.

Policy tailwinds and overseas and acquisition growth

In October 2026 the company said it expanded lending to low-credit and thin-file borrowers in line with inclusive-finance policy. In April 2026 it injected about KRW 5 billion into LC LOMBARD in Kazakhstan. It also acquired 47.3% of Mason Capital's common shares to broaden its credit finance business.

Bear factors

Sharp rise in credit costs and a Q3 2026 operating loss

The company posted an operating loss of KRW 1.25 billion in Q3 2026 after a profit in the prior quarter. Provisions for credit losses rose 88.9% year on year to KRW 22.81 billion.

Lending regulation and profitability pressure in moneylending

Sisaweek reported that profitability in moneylending has worsened since the statutory maximum interest rate was lowered, and some lenders have cut new lending or exited. Because consumer lending is the profit core, rate and regulatory changes directly affect results. Operating profit fell from KRW 59.3 billion in 2022 to KRW 10.9 billion in 2025.

Higher leverage and weaker cash flow

The debt-to-equity ratio rose from 114.3% in 2024 to 139.6% in 2025, with total liabilities at KRW 643.4 billion. Operating cash flow shifted from KRW 13.1 billion in 2024 to a net outflow of -KRW 50.6 billion in 2025. Net income attributable to controlling shareholders over the last four quarters totals only KRW 6.21 billion.

10

Risk factors

Credit risk

As lending assets grow, delinquency and credit cost burdens can increase. The 88.9% year-on-year rise in Q3 2026 provisions illustrates this. The company has said it will strengthen risk monitoring and stress testing, and the effect will show in later quarters' credit costs.

Regulatory and policy risk

The statutory maximum interest rate and inclusive-finance policy directly change the economics of moneylending. Expanding lending to low-credit borrowers can raise both pricing constraints and loss burden. A regulatory change would force adjustments to loan terms and volumes.

Overseas expansion and execution risk

The Kazakhstan unit is at the stage of opening new branches after a capital injection of about KRW 5 billion, and its overseas lending remains small. The Mason Capital acquisition also needs to deliver synergies after closing. If overseas and new businesses are slow to contribute, dependence on domestic consumer lending will remain.

Overall view

The Leadcorp runs petroleum distribution, expressway service areas, and consumer lending, and its profit core sits in consumer lending. Annual operating profit moved from KRW 59.3 billion in 2022 to an operating loss of KRW 13.5 billion in 2023, then KRW 21.6 billion in 2024 and KRW 10.9 billion in 2025.

Net income attributable to controlling shareholders grew for two years through 2025, but Q3 2026 posted an operating loss of KRW 1.25 billion and a controlling-shareholder net loss of KRW 4.02 billion as provisions rose.

On shareholder returns, the company met the high-dividend criteria, has said it will keep the payout at 30% or above, and completed treasury share retirement. On the other side, the debt-to-equity ratio rose to 139.6% and operating cash flow turned to a net outflow of -KRW 50.6 billion.

The share price sits well below book value, but a thin earnings base means that level does not by itself signal an earnings recovery. The next points to check are the annual credit cost trend in the 2026 annual report and operating profit in the Q1 2027 quarterly report.
