02

Business structure

Q.S.ICo was founded in 2000 to manufacture and sell semiconductor laser and optoelectronic products and listed on KOSDAQ in 2006. Its core structure is a total-solution model built on laser diode source technology, producing customer-specific products.

Its main product lines are sensors and laser beam printer (LBP) models, with supply also reaching power tools, barcode scanners, factory automation, medical devices, 3D depth sensors and optical communications. The company has one consolidated subsidiary.

For the 2025 sales increase, the company cited higher sales in its foundry segment. In the first quarter of 2026, sales growth was attributed to rising demand for core product lines such as sensors and LBP models and expanded supply to global customers.

In February 2026 it began an indium phosphide (InP)-based RF foundry service and is raising spending on R&D for micro LEDs and RF semiconductors. Press coverage in February 2026 noted that these new businesses had not yet translated into reported results.

The largest shareholder and its related parties rose to a 32.69% stake after a 2025 treasury share disposal, keeping control above 30%. Competition is likely shaped by laser light source technology and the speed of customer qualification. Segment sales shares are best confirmed in the segment note of the annual report.

04

Earnings analysis

Annual sales were KRW 22.7 billion in 2022, KRW 18.7 billion in 2023 (down 17.7% year on year), KRW 21.4 billion in 2024 (up 14.3%) and KRW 23.3 billion in 2025 (up 9.0%). Sales rose in 2024 and 2025 after the 2023 low, and 2025 was the largest year of 2022–2025.

Operating profit of KRW 1.85 billion in 2022 turned into an operating loss of KRW 4.24 billion in 2023, then returned to profit at KRW 1.18 billion in 2024. Operating profit fell 75.2% to KRW 294 million in 2025, and the operating margin went from 8.1% to -22.7%, then 5.5%, then 1.3%.

The 2025 decline was attributed in the company's disclosure to higher raw material costs and higher R&D expenses.

Net profit attributable to controlling shareholders was KRW 7.01 billion in 2022, the largest year, then KRW 2.71 billion in 2023 (down 61.3%), KRW 3.25 billion in 2024 (up 20.0%) and KRW 2.52 billion in 2025 (down 22.7%). Net profit including non-controlling interests was reported at the same amounts in each year.

Total equity at the end of 2025 was KRW 87.4 billion, and the debt ratio stayed between 5.5% and 6.4% over 2022–2025. Operating cash flow was KRW 5.28 billion in 2022, KRW 5.47 billion in 2023, KRW 3.23 billion in 2024 and KRW 4.75 billion in 2025.

Quarterly sales of KRW 7.7 billion in the second quarter of 2026 were the highest of the last five quarters, yet that quarter posted an operating loss of KRW 12.53 million. Operating profit in the third quarter of 2026 was KRW 752 million, a swing to profit, up 248.0% year on year.

However, net loss attributable to controlling shareholders was KRW 1.03 billion in that quarter, a swing into deficit. Net profit attributable to controlling shareholders over the last four quarters totaled KRW 4.81 billion.

Because operating profit and controlling-shareholder earnings moved in opposite directions, the size of non-operating items is the next point to check.

05

Industry analysis

Q.S.ICo is a component maker built on laser diodes and optoelectronic devices. Unlike equipment-focused HANMI Semiconductor or laser equipment maker EO Technics, its business centers on supplying light-source components.

Its core demand comes from sensors, laser beam printers and industrial applications, so it is affected more by diversification of end markets than by a single semiconductor cycle. In 2025, higher foundry-segment sales were cited as the driver of top-line growth.

RF semiconductors and micro LEDs have been presented in the press as areas of expected demand, but their sales have not yet been separated in the disclosed figures. Quantum computing applications have been cited as a backdrop to theme-driven share price moves.

On 9 November 2026 the share price rose 16.72% in a single day, yet reports noted that no new in-house business announcement or results disclosure appeared that day.

Press coverage stressed that R&D news should be distinguished from mass-production revenue, and that customer qualification, yield and order size need to be confirmed for each product.

Compared with large-cap listed companies, its smaller scale means the share price is more exposed to outside factors and tends to swing widely.

Outlook

The company's next test is converting new businesses into revenue. The February 2026 launch of the InP-based RF foundry service was presented as aimed at demand for high-frequency chips for 6G and satellite communications.

R&D for RF semiconductors and micro LEDs is expensed first, which could slow the pace of margin recovery. Second-quarter 2026 sales of KRW 7.7 billion were the highest of the last five quarters, yet that quarter recorded an operating loss, showing that higher sales did not immediately lift profit.

Third-quarter 2026 operating profit was KRW 752 million, but net loss attributable to controlling shareholders continued. At the regular shareholders' meeting disclosed on 24 March 2026, the financial statements were approved and a cash dividend was passed, continuing a practice in place since 2017.

The next checkpoint is how far the annual operating margin recovers from 1.3% in 2025 in the 2026 annual report. The timing at which customer qualification, yield and order size for new products become public will mark the turning point for revenue conversion. Overall view

Operating profit turned positive in the first and third quarters of 2026, and second-quarter sales of KRW 7.7 billion were the highest on a disclosed basis. However, the third-quarter net loss attributable to controlling shareholders of KRW 1.03 billion shows that non-operating items need review.
