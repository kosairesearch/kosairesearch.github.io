02

Business structure

Founded in 2000 and listed on KOSDAQ in 2006, Jinbiotech specializes in functional feed additives for livestock, developing, producing, and selling probiotics, enzyme preparations, and immune enhancers based on proprietary microbial fermentation technology.

In 2011 the company expanded into veterinary medicine by bringing Dawon Chemical, an animal drug manufacturer, under its consolidated structure. The additives segment supplies fermented protein and probiotic products such as Pepsoigen, Anerbens, and Lactocare to compound feed plants and livestock farms.

A key technical strength is cost competitiveness achieved through shortened production cycles using solid-state fermentation and by skipping purification/separation steps in enzyme manufacturing.

The portfolio also includes disinfectant products such as Speed Kill, aimed at avian influenza and African swine fever prevention.

More recently, the company has invested in new growth areas including functional additives and exclusive premix supply for the companion animal market, as well as cultured meat research and development.

Its product lineup is segmented by livestock type, covering swine, cattle, and poultry, with domestic compound feed makers and livestock farms as its main customer base.

The feed additive and veterinary medicine market features competition among numerous small and mid-sized players, where technological capability and cost competitiveness largely determine market position.

04

Earnings analysis

Consolidated 2025 revenue came in at KRW 90.79 billion, up from KRW 82.96 billion in 2024, with operating profit of KRW 3.92 billion representing an operating margin of 4.3%.

Notably, the operating margin edged down slightly from 5.0% in 2023 and 4.6% in 2024, indicating revenue growth did not fully translate into margin expansion.

Net income attributable to owners rose sharply to KRW 3.79 billion in 2025 from KRW 2.87 billion in 2024, a jump that outpaced the modest increase in operating profit, suggesting non-operating factors played some role.

Looking at the trailing four quarters (Q3 2025 through Q2 2026), revenue moved from KRW 24.26 billion to KRW 20.54 billion to KRW 21.50 billion to KRW 23.02 billion, showing seasonal fluctuation, while operating profit swung from KRW 1.36 billion to KRW 0.92 billion to KRW 0.76 billion to KRW 1.50 billion, underscoring notable quarter-to-quarter volatility.

In particular, net income attributable to owners in Q2 2025 (KRW 1.21 billion) exceeded that quarter's operating profit (KRW 0.90 billion), and a similar pattern of net income (KRW 1.29 billion) approaching operating profit (KRW 1.50 billion) recurred in Q2 2026, indicating non-operating items are influencing quarterly results.

The margin trajectory, having fallen to 2.0% in 2022 before recovering to a 3-5% range in 2023-2025, can be read as a sign of cost structure stabilization or product mix improvement.

On the cash generation side, operating cash flow swung between negative territory in 2022 (KRW -3.68 billion) and 2024 (KRW -0.46 billion) and strongly positive levels in 2023 (KRW 7.37 billion) and 2025 (KRW 7.61 billion), a sign-flipping pattern that suggests working capital changes such as inventory and receivables have a considerable impact on reported results.

05

Industry analysis

The animal feed additive and veterinary medicine industry is a moderately growing market underpinned by rising demand for livestock productivity improvement and antibiotic replacement both domestically and globally.

As regulations on antibiotic use tighten, structural demand for eco-friendly alternatives such as probiotics and enzyme preparations is being supported.

Seasonally, demand for disinfectant products tends to concentrate in autumn and winter when livestock diseases such as avian influenza and African swine fever are more likely to occur.

The competitive landscape is a mix of numerous small and mid-sized feed additive companies and the in-house R&D arms of large feed manufacturers, with patented technology and cost competitiveness determining individual companies' market standing.

Jinbiotech is regarded as having secured technical competitiveness through production cost reduction via solid-state fermentation technology and a number of registered patents.

That said, results are linked to upstream cycles such as raw material and grain prices and fluctuations in domestic livestock headcount, meaning individual companies' cost management and new product development capabilities tend to matter more than overall industry growth.

Outlook

However, continued expansion of production infrastructure and R&D output has been confirmed, including the 2025 completion of a logistics warehouse in the Nam-Gongju Industrial Complex and a patent registration related to a feed additive for reducing rumen greenhouse gas emissions.

Ahead of the winter disease-prevention season, demand for disinfectant products such as Speed Kill could fluctuate depending on the incidence of avian influenza and African swine fever, a seasonal variable to watch.

Separately, the shareholder proposal submitted to the board in late January 2026 by second-largest shareholder Kim Sung-ho centers on full cancellation of treasury shares, establishment of internal M&A rules, introduction of an interim dividend, and enhanced disclosure; whether these items are actually placed on the agenda and pass at a general shareholders meeting is flagged as a key variable for the future direction of shareholder return policy.

Bull factors

Continued Earnings Recovery

Operating margin has improved from a low of 2.0% in 2022 to a 3-5% range in 2023-2025, and net income attributable to owners has clearly risen from KRW 1.52 billion in 2022 to KRW 3.79 billion in 2025.

Q2 2026 also showed improvement from the prior quarter with operating profit of KRW 1.50 billion and net income of KRW 1.29 billion. Continuation of this earnings recovery could be read as a positive signal for financial stability.

Shareholder Return Demands Creating Institutional Pressure

The second-largest shareholder has submitted a proposal to the board covering full cancellation of treasury shares, introduction of an interim dividend, and establishment of M&A internal rules, creating pressure for a change in shareholder return policy.

Indeed, the most recent disclosure confirms cash dividends per share have resumed, indicating some shift in dividend policy is already underway. With the debt ratio stably managed in the 36-41% range, the company appears to have financial capacity to support dividend expansion or share buybacks.

Structural Growth in Disease Prevention and Antibiotic Replacement Demand

As antibiotic use regulations tighten, structural demand for eco-friendly alternatives such as probiotics and enzyme preparations is being supported.

The company's disease-prevention product portfolio, including the disinfectant Speed Kill for avian influenza and African swine fever, offers potential for demand increases during seasonal disease outbreaks. Cost competitiveness based on solid-state fermentation technology is also cited as a technical strength.

Bear factors

Governance Dispute Uncertainty

The second-largest shareholder Kim Sung-ho has raised his stake to around 13% and formalized his intent to influence management, while the gap with largest shareholder Lee Chan-ho (roughly 28% including related parties) is not large, making the outcome of any vote difficult to predict.

There have been instances where a planned extraordinary general meeting was announced but not actually convened, raising the possibility the dispute could drag on. A prolonged governance dispute raises concerns about potential inconsistency in management decision-making.

Quarterly Earnings Volatility

Both revenue and operating profit over the trailing four quarters have repeatedly fluctuated, and in Q1 2026 in particular operating profit fell to KRW 0.76 billion, showing the earnings improvement trend has not been consistently sustained every quarter.

Operating cash flow was also negative in both 2022 and 2024, indicating persistent volatility in cash generation tied to working capital changes. This raises uncertainty in short-term earnings forecasting.

Dual-Edged Nature of the Renew Medical Backdoor Listing Scenario

There are reports that Renew Medical, led by the second-largest shareholder, is targeting a KOSDAQ listing in the first half of 2027, and market speculation exists that the outcome of the governance dispute could lead to discussions of a backdoor listing.

However, there is also a risk that market expectations could reverse if such a scenario fails to materialize or if the synergy with the existing business proves limited. Backdoor listing-related issues could function as an event-driven variable separate from the core business fundamentals.

Risk factors

Governance Risk

With no large gap in ownership between the largest and second-largest shareholders, the ongoing dispute over management control carries the risk of escalating into board reshuffling or legal proceedings such as lawsuits.

Because minority shareholder sentiment appears likely to determine the dispute's outcome, competition over proxy voting could intensify.

Upstream Industry and Cost Risk

Fluctuations in domestic livestock headcount and grain/raw material prices directly affect revenue and cost structure. Demand for disease-prevention products can shift rapidly depending on the occurrence of livestock diseases such as avian influenza and African swine fever, adding volatility to earnings forecasts.

Small-Cap Liquidity Risk

As a small-cap KOSDAQ stock, limited trading volume can amplify price volatility in response to news or disclosures. The possibility of sharp short-term supply-demand shifts driven by governance dispute-related news flow cannot be ruled out.

Overall view

Jinbiotech has shown a recovery in operating margin and net income since 2022 on the back of its stable core business in feed additives and veterinary medicine, while maintaining a relatively stable financial structure with the debt ratio managed within a 36-41% range.

However, both revenue and profit have continued to fluctuate on a quarterly basis, meaning the earnings improvement has not progressed smoothly every quarter.

At the same time, second-largest shareholder Kim Sung-ho has raised his stake to around 13% while formalizing his intent to exert influence over management, and has submitted a shareholder proposal calling for treasury share cancellation and an interim dividend, among other measures, suggesting governance-related uncertainty could persist for some time.

Recent confirmation that dividends have resumed points to some sign of change in shareholder return policy, but whether this evolves into a sustained and expanded policy depends on decisions at shareholder meetings and the board.

The backdoor listing scenario tied to Renew Medical's potential listing remains an event-driven variable separate from business fundamentals. Investors will want to watch both the sustainability of the core business's earnings recovery and how the governance dispute unfolds.
