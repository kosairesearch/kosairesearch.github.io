02

Business structure

Sebo Manufacturing Engineering makes and installs piping and ducts for semiconductor and display production facilities as a construction mechanical equipment company.

The business is split between equipment and plant segments, and based on Q2 2026 sales compiled by Butler the equipment share is 88% and the plant share is 12%. In Q1 2023 the split was 95.7% equipment and 3.0% plant, so the plant share has risen since then.

Its main clients are EPC contractors such as Samsung C&T and Samsung E&A, along with semiconductor fab owners.

The large contracts disclosed in 2026 were a Pyeongtaek piping project (Phase 2) worth KRW 40.0 billion with Samsung E&A, a Giheung facility mechanical contract worth KRW 97.017 billion with Samsung C&T, and an LNG pipeline job of about KRW 24.0 billion for the Yongin district energy project with an SK group engineering unit.

In fab construction, MEP work is tendered intensively after the shell stage, which makes the company a direct beneficiary at that stage. The Yongin cluster is also set to proceed in stages through civil shell, utilities, MEP, piping and air-conditioning work.

Samsung C&T and Samsung E&A act as prime EPC contractors for fab construction and utility infrastructure, while the company specializes in MEP, piping and air-conditioning. It also has energy piping capability covering gas and fluid transfer and district heating. One industry report grouped the company with Hanyang ENG as fab in-facility infrastructure names.

04

Earnings analysis

Annual sales were KRW 1.03 trillion in 2022, KRW 871.7 billion in 2023 (down 15.5% year on year), KRW 788.8 billion in 2024 (down 9.5%) and KRW 715.8 billion in 2025 (down 9.3%), a decline for three straight years from 2023 through 2025. Across 2022 to 2025, sales were largest in 2022 and smallest in 2025.

Operating profit moved from KRW 33.1 billion in 2022 to KRW 37.6 billion in 2023 (up 13.6%), KRW 28.6 billion in 2024 (down 23.8%) and KRW 40.4 billion in 2025 (up 41.1%), making 2025 the largest year and 2024 the smallest.

Operating margin rose from 3.6% in 2024 to 5.6% in 2025 even as sales fell, which is why operating profit grew despite the revenue decline.

Net income attributable to controlling shareholders was KRW 23.6 billion in 2022, KRW 30.2 billion in 2023 (up 27.6%), KRW 28.1 billion in 2024 (down 6.9%) and KRW 35.6 billion in 2025 (up 26.7%). Total net income including non-controlling interests is reported at the same amounts in each of these years.

On a quarterly basis, sales were KRW 163.8 billion in Q3 2025, KRW 199.2 billion in Q4 2025, KRW 245.9 billion in Q1 2026, KRW 289.8 billion in Q2 2026 and KRW 353.3 billion in Q3 2026, rising for four straight quarters from Q4 2025.

Operating profit went from KRW 7.65 billion in Q3 2025 to KRW 20.5 billion in Q3 2026, and net income attributable to controlling shareholders over the last four quarters totaled KRW 57.2 billion.

A July 2026 article summarizing a securities firm's research said a shorter build period lets orders and revenue recognition run at the same time. In 2023 the outlook pointed to shrinking sales on lower chip investment, which shows how closely revenue has tracked the semiconductor capex cycle.

Operating cash flow went from negative KRW 14.5 billion in 2022 to KRW 69.4 billion in 2023, KRW 98.7 billion in 2024 and KRW 69.8 billion in 2025. The debt ratio fell from 141.0% in 2022 to 68.4% in 2025, with year-end 2025 total equity of KRW 271.2 billion and total liabilities of KRW 185.6 billion.

05

Industry analysis

The Semiconductor Equipment and Materials International (SEMI) projected in its June 2026 outlook that global 300mm memory fab equipment investment would rise 29% year on year to USD 52.0 billion in 2026 and reach USD 57.0 billion in 2027.

Expanding AI infrastructure spending and demand for high-performance memory are widening order opportunities for in-fab infrastructure contractors. A 2023 revision to the enforcement decree let designated industrial complexes raise floor-area ratios from 350% to as much as 490% for the general industrial zone standard.

Equipment orders depend on cleanroom scale, tool layout, and gas and water specifications.

Memory capacity additions and cleanroom expansions widen opportunities for piping and duct contractors, but analysts note that fabrication and construction capability and cost control will determine results as orders convert into sales and profit.

The Pyeongtaek P5 Fab 1 shell work was reported to be resuming, and an earlier start for Fab 2 was flagged as possible, which would support recurring MEP and cleanroom work after the shell stage.

Average factory utilization in the first half of 2026 was 46.7%, which one industry report read as production capacity ready to meet rising orders. Reports from 2023 expected investment to recover from 2024 onward, and memory fab equipment spending has since been forecast to keep rising.

The industry position reads as an investment expansion phase, but the pace at which backlog turns into revenue should be tracked through construction progress.

These are estimates, and their gap with the disclosed results through Q3 2026 will be tested against the full-year figures in the 2026 annual report.

The 2026 contract disclosures ran from a January LNG pipeline job for the Yongin project, to a May Pyeongtaek piping project (Phase 2), to an October Giheung facility mechanical contract.

The Giheung contract runs from October 14, 2026 to March 28, 2028, and the Pyeongtaek Phase 2 piping contract runs from May 26, 2026 to December 31, 2027.

The reported resumption of Pyeongtaek P5 Fab 1 shell work and the possibility of an earlier Fab 2 start are cited as a base for MEP and cleanroom work after the shell stage. Work at the Yongin SK hynix cluster is also expected to proceed in stages, so whether further tenders follow is an open item.

The Yongin LNG pipeline contract ends on July 31, 2027 and the Pyeongtaek Phase 2 piping contract on December 31, 2027, so whether revenue recognized in the quarters around those dates matches expectations is a checkpoint. Revenue from 2027 onward depends on how many orders are added after the second half of 2026.

Whether 2026 contract values are large relative to annual sales will be checked by comparing each disclosed amount with past sales.

Bear factors

Three years of sales declines

Sales were KRW 871.7 billion in 2023, KRW 788.8 billion in 2024 and KRW 715.8 billion in 2025, a decline for three straight years from 2023. Outlooks at the time pointed to a shrinking top line during the 2023 chip investment slowdown.

Quarterly sales rose quickly in 2026, but the evidence on how many more quarters that growth will last is still limited.

Share price moves driven by contract news

According to the ORGO tally, the stock rose 17.8% on June 29, 11.1% on July 31 and 13.5% on August 28, 2026. The June 29 move was tagged as an HBM-related contract disclosure and the August 28 move as a contract disclosure.

Because price reactions to disclosures have been large, gaps between announced contract values and the pace of revenue recognition could add to volatility.

Reliance on Samsung-affiliated clients and utilization

Two of the three large contracts confirmed in 2026 are with Samsung-affiliated companies, Samsung E&A and Samsung C&T. Average factory utilization of 46.7% in the first half of 2026 suggests spare capacity, which can be read as room for more orders.

How much sales growth depends on a single group's orders still needs to be checked against client-level sales disclosures in the annual report.

Risk factors

Client concentration and the capex cycle

Because sales have tracked fab investment schedules, any delay in client spending could slow both orders and revenue. A 2023 slowdown in chip investment led to a forecast of shrinking sales. A high share of Samsung-affiliated projects also means exposure to decisions made at the group level.

Construction timelines and revenue recognition

The Yongin LNG pipeline contract runs to July 31, 2027, the Pyeongtaek Phase 2 piping contract to December 31, 2027, and the Giheung mechanical contract to March 28, 2028. Process delays or material price swings during construction could shift the timing of revenue and profit recognition. Contract values and annual revenue recognized may differ, which requires checking against the periodic reports.

Share price volatility and disclosure dependence

Single-day moves above 10% around contract disclosures appeared several times in 2026. Swings driven by disclosure events can diverge from the earnings trend. As a small-cap KOSDAQ stock, trading depth can also amplify price volatility.

Overall view

Sales fell for three straight years from 2023 through 2025, but the operating margin reached 5.6% in 2025 and the debt ratio dropped from 141.0% in 2022 to 68.4% in 2025.

In 2026, large contracts with Samsung C&T and Samsung E&A and an LNG pipeline job for an SK group project were disclosed, and quarterly sales and operating profit rose for four straight quarters.

Strengths include expanding contract disclosures, forecasts for higher memory fab equipment spending, and a stronger balance sheet. Weaknesses include the sales decline from 2023 through 2025, reliance on Samsung-affiliated clients, and share price volatility around contract disclosures.

Shinhan Securities' 2026 sales projection of KRW 980.1 billion is an estimate and should be read apart from disclosed results. Whether the 2026 quarterly trend carries into full-year results will be confirmed in the 2026 annual report.
