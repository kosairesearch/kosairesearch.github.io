An ink maker shifting toward materials, with profits still being tested

02

Business structure

Inktec was founded in 1992 and listed on KOSDAQ in 2002, specializing in inkjet application technology and printed electronics. The business runs in three segments: image printing, electronic materials and components, and printing systems.

A company profile summary from Wisereport shows a sales mix of 48.07% image printing, 31.72% electronic materials, and 17.57% printing systems.

A filing summary covering January through September 2026 shows 47.0% image printing, 34% electronic materials and components, 16.2% printing systems, and 2.8% rental income, so the electronic materials share edged up.

Image printing supplies inks optimized for each printer to OEM customers, and the company says its sales network covers more than 90 countries and about 120 customers.

The electronic materials segment produces paste inks, insulating films, EMI shielding films, and inductors based on its transparent electronic ink nanotechnology.

According to press release headlines, the company supplies shielding films for the Galaxy Watch and a Motorola camera module, and it has also announced domestic localization of an EMI shielding film material previously monopolized by a Japanese supplier.

The printing systems segment centers on the UV-curable inkjet printer JETRIX and offers ink and equipment together. As of December 2025, exports accounted for 73.29% of office ink sales and 60.68% of JETRIX sales.

Production is split between Ansan (printers and ink) and Seosan (electronic materials), and the overseas sales unit InkTec Europe Ltd is a 100%-owned subsidiary.

In competition, image printing overlaps with printer consumables makers, while electronic materials overlap with larger listed chemical and materials companies such as LG Chem and SKC.

04

Earnings analysis

On a reported annual basis, sales were KRW 61.5 billion in 2022, KRW 62.8 billion in 2023 (up 2.1% year on year), KRW 73.9 billion in 2024 (up 17.7%), and KRW 77.5 billion in 2025 (up 4.9%). Sales rose for three consecutive years from 2023 through 2025, and 2025 was the largest year of the 2022 to 2025 period.

Operating profit over the same span was KRW 5.82 billion in 2022, KRW 3.85 billion in 2023, KRW 2.71 billion in 2024, and KRW 802 million in 2025, declining for three consecutive years. Operating profit in 2025 fell 70.4% from the prior year, making it the smallest year of the period, so sales grew while profit shrank.

The operating margin moved from 9.5% to 6.1%, 3.7%, and 1.0%. Net income attributable to controlling shareholders fell for three consecutive years, from KRW 3.96 billion in 2022 to KRW 2.51 billion in 2025, and total net income including non-controlling interests was reported at the same values.

The debt ratio rose from 59.9% in 2022 to 109.3% in 2024 before easing to 102.0% in 2025. At the end of 2025, total equity was KRW 78.2 billion and total liabilities were KRW 79.8 billion. Operating cash flow fell from KRW 6.8 billion in 2022 and KRW 6.29 billion in 2024 to KRW 683 million in 2025.

Quarterly results moved from an operating loss of KRW 1.82 billion and an attributable net loss of KRW 2.15 billion in the third quarter of 2025 to an operating profit of KRW 2.21 billion and attributable net income of KRW 5.78 billion in the fourth quarter.

Operating profit was KRW 1.99 billion in the first quarter of 2026 and KRW 2.38 billion in the second, up 19.8% from the prior quarter. A Wisereport company summary put first-quarter 2026 operating profit up 336.7% year on year as of June 9, 2026.

Operating profit then fell to KRW 99.08 million in the third quarter, down 95.8% from the prior quarter, and the company posted an attributable net loss of KRW 277 million. Third-quarter sales of KRW 22.2 billion were up 14.0% year on year, so the top line grew while the margin stayed below 1%. Across the quarters, profitability recovered in the first half of 2026 and weakened again in the third quarter.

05

Industry analysis

Demand for the industrial ink business is tied to digital transformation. A report by the credit rating agency NICE Information Service says ink demand is steadily rising in advertising, packaging, and industrial printing, and describes the ink business as a low-volatility base for generating cash.

For electronic materials, the demand driver is the expansion of AI data centers and energy storage systems.

Wisereport summarized that the electronic materials segment improved profitability on higher component demand from these two markets, and that demand for MLCCs for AI servers and high-efficiency materials is also growing.

The same report argues that high-function materials for displays, semiconductors, and electronic components have high technical barriers and demanding customer qualification processes, so transactions tend to stabilize after entry.

That is the view of a rating agency, however, and the actual scale of revenue conversion has to be confirmed through reported results. The printing business depends heavily on exports, so it is exposed to overseas demand and exchange rate movements.

In electronic materials, the company competes in part with larger listed chemical and materials companies such as LG Chem, SKC, and Hanwha Solutions, but Inktec's sales base is far smaller than theirs.

To gauge where the business sits in its cycle, the key is whether spending on AI servers and energy storage translates into electronic materials sales for the company, which can be tracked quarter by quarter.

06

Outlook

The direction the company has described publicly is expansion in electronic materials.

According to the company profile, it is pursuing technology development for ultra-thin, high-reliability electrode materials and functional process materials using its Seosan plant, and aims to expand volume sales in global component materials markets such as AI data centers and energy storage systems.

The Seosan plant broke ground in July 2023, and the facility that moved from Pyeongtaek to Seosan was completed on December 27, 2024. Press reports also say the company decided to acquire land and buildings in Seosan for KRW 14.8 billion, which points to a wider production base.

Drivers cited for first-quarter 2026 profit improvement included high-value inks in image printing, share gains in Southeast and South Asia, and diversification into DTF solutions.

In the second quarter of 2026, sales were KRW 23.3 billion and operating profit was KRW 2.38 billion, the highest operating profit among the last five reported quarters.

In the third quarter of 2026, sales rose 14.0% year on year, but the operating margin stayed below 1%, leaving margin recovery as the main open question.

The core question for the outlook is whether electronic materials volumes from the Seosan plant show up in the sales mix and lift the margin back toward the second-quarter level.

Demand direction in electronic materials

According to press release headlines, the company supplies shielding films for the Galaxy Watch and a Motorola camera module, and it has announced localization of a material previously monopolized by a Japanese supplier.

The Seosan production base and technology focus

Bear factors

Three straight years of lower profit

Net income attributable to controlling shareholders fell from KRW 3.96 billion in 2022 to KRW 2.51 billion in 2025, declining for three consecutive years. The operating margin dropped from 9.5% to 1.0% over the same period.

Operating profit of KRW 802 million in 2025 fell 70.4% from the prior year, so profit declined even in a year when sales grew.

A sharp third-quarter drop and a loss

Operating profit in the third quarter of 2026 was KRW 99.08 million, down 95.8% from the prior quarter. The attributable net loss of KRW 277 million was a continuing loss, though smaller than a year earlier. Sales of KRW 22.2 billion were up 14.0% year on year, but the profit margin fell sharply.

Financial burden and cash flow

Total liabilities at the end of 2025 were KRW 79.8 billion, close to total equity of KRW 78.2 billion, and the debt ratio stood at 102.0%. That is down from 109.3% in 2024 but above 59.9% in 2022.

Operating cash flow fell from KRW 6.29 billion in 2024 to KRW 683 million in 2025, so the cash generated by operations has weakened alongside the debt burden.

Risk factors

Earnings volatility

Quarterly operating profit swung sharply, from KRW 2.38 billion in the second quarter of 2026 to KRW 99.08 million in the third. Losses in the third quarter of 2025 and profits in the fourth sit side by side, making annual trends hard to read from quarterly results alone.

Within the last four quarters' total of KRW 9.3 billion, the KRW 5.78 billion from the fourth quarter of 2025 is a large share, so the figure is sensitive to a single quarter.

Overseas demand and exchange rates

As of December 2025, exports accounted for 73.29% of office ink sales and 60.68% of JETRIX sales, indicating high reliance on overseas markets. Changes in overseas printer demand or exchange rates can flow directly into the results of image printing and printing systems. Electronic materials require customer qualification, so new sales take time to build.

Investment burden and financial capacity

Press reports on a decision to acquire Seosan land and buildings for KRW 14.8 billion point to a rising investment burden from expanding production. With a debt ratio of 102.0% and operating cash flow of KRW 683 million in 2025, how the investment is financed has become important. Whether electronic materials sales grow enough to offset this burden is the key variable.

Overall view

Inktec is in the middle of shifting its weight from industrial ink toward electronic and functional materials. Annual sales rose from KRW 61.5 billion in 2022 to KRW 77.5 billion in 2025, but operating profit fell from KRW 5.82 billion to KRW 802 million, so profitability weakened.

The first half of 2026 showed a recovery in operating profit, but in the third quarter operating profit dropped to KRW 99.08 million and the company posted an attributable net loss of KRW 277 million. On the financial side, the debt ratio was 102.0% at the end of 2025, and operating cash flow fell to KRW 683 million.

Supporting factors include electronic materials demand tied to AI data centers and energy storage, the Seosan production base, and the return to quarterly profit. Opposing factors include three years of falling profit, an operating margin of about 1%, the third-quarter loss, and financial burden.

Which side prevails will likely become clearer from the operating margin and operating cash flow reported in the 2026 annual report.
