02

Business structure

In the F operates a portfolio of fashion brands, including women's wear Joinus, men's suits Trugen, select store BIND, and fashion jewelry Mosbani, along with other labels.

Based on brand-level domestic sales ratios for 2024, most major brands generated more than 98% of their sales in Korea, so revenue is concentrated in domestic distribution. The company has adjusted its store base by replacing or closing inefficient stores.

It has also pursued channel diversification, including directly operated galleries, and broader supplier relationships to reduce costs and improve productivity. The company attributed the 2025 operating turnaround to lower cost of sales from closing inefficient stores and managing discount rates.

Because the business is centered on apparel, changes in consumer spending flow directly into revenue. In competition, new launches by large domestic brands and the entry of global brands into Korea have widened consumer choice, which makes it harder for existing brands to grow sales.

Listed peers in the same sector include F&F, LF, Handsome, Shinsegae International, Hansae and BYC, which allows comparison of brand operations and channel mix.

Earnings analysis

Annual results show revenue shrinking while profitability improved. Revenue went from KRW 135.3 billion in 2022 to KRW 126.5 billion in 2023 (down 6.5% year on year), KRW 114.7 billion in 2024 (down 9.3%) and KRW 104.6 billion in 2025 (down 8.8%), declining for three consecutive years from 2023 to 2025.

Among the years from 2022 to 2025, 2022 was the largest by revenue and 2025 the smallest. Operating loss of KRW 13 billion in 2022 narrowed to KRW 2.7 billion in 2023 and KRW 827 million in 2024, while the company remained in the red, before turning to an operating profit of KRW 1.19 billion in 2025.

The operating margin moved from -9.6% in 2022 to -2.1% in 2023, -0.7% in 2024 and 1.1% in 2025.

Net loss attributable to controlling shareholders was KRW 10.2 billion in 2022, KRW 1.65 billion in 2023, KRW 1.36 billion in 2024 and KRW 107 million in 2025, a loss in each of the four years from 2022 to 2025 with the loss shrinking every year.

Net loss including non-controlling interests was KRW 10.2 billion, KRW 1.65 billion, KRW 1.36 billion and KRW 107 million in the same years. Quarterly results moved sharply.

From an operating loss of KRW 2.39 billion and a controlling-shareholder net loss of KRW 3.14 billion in the third quarter of 2025, the fourth quarter of 2025 delivered revenue of KRW 30.4 billion (up 38.3% from the prior quarter) and an operating profit of KRW 2.86 billion.

The first quarter of 2026 brought revenue of KRW 24.3 billion (down 20.0% from the prior quarter), an operating profit of KRW 496 million (down 82.6%) and a controlling-shareholder net loss of KRW 282 million.

The second quarter of 2026 recovered to revenue of KRW 28.3 billion, an operating profit of KRW 2.28 billion (up 360.2% from the prior quarter) and controlling-shareholder net profit of KRW 1.51 billion.

The third quarter of 2026 returned to an operating loss of KRW 1.79 billion and a controlling-shareholder net loss of KRW 1.91 billion, on revenue of KRW 20.3 billion (down 28.3% from the prior quarter and down 7.6% year on year).

Net profit attributable to controlling shareholders totaled KRW 2.78 billion over the last four quarters.

Balance sheet and cash flow: at the end of 2025, total equity was KRW 37.9 billion, all attributable to controlling shareholders, and total liabilities were KRW 77.9 billion, giving a debt-to-equity ratio of 205.5%, down from 215.6% in 2022 but up from 191.7% in 2023 and 220.5% in 2024.

Operating cash flow was KRW 3.45 billion in 2022, KRW 8.62 billion in 2023, KRW 3.24 billion in 2024 and KRW 8.87 billion in 2025, the highest of the four years.

Industry analysis

The apparel sector is facing weak consumer sentiment and intensifying competition. The company's earnings commentary attributed the revenue decline to a prolonged slowdown in consumer spending amid the global economic downturn.

The same commentary pointed to new brand launches and the entry of global brands into Korea as factors that have intensified competition. In cycle terms, revenue has declined for three consecutive years from 2023 to 2025, so a recovery in top-line trends has not yet appeared in annual results.

The second quarter of 2026, however, showed improved quarterly profitability on revenue of KRW 28.3 billion and an operating profit of KRW 2.28 billion, so demand trends are worth tracking quarter by quarter.

Listed peers in the same textile, apparel and lifestyle sector include Youngone, F&F, LF, Handsome, Shinsegae International and Hansae. In the F runs a multi-brand portfolio, so profit variation across individual brands flows directly into consolidated results.

Under this structure, a weaker contribution from a single brand tends to show up as a decline in total revenue. On the channel side, the company is shifting weight from department stores and large retail toward directly operated galleries and a broader supplier base. A comparison with peers' scale requires placing revenue and operating profit on the same basis.

Because the 2025 turnaround came from this direction, cost of sales will remain a key variable for profit after 2026. Revenue in the third quarter of 2026 declined from a year earlier, so whether recovery in sales or the cleanup of inefficient stores has the larger effect on profit is the next point to watch.

Quarterly results have alternated between losses and profits, with a loss in the first quarter of 2026, a recovery in the second and a loss again in the third, so the direction is hard to read from a single quarter.

Because the swings in quarterly profit are large, fourth-quarter results should be read alongside seasonal patterns and inventory and discount policies. Bull factors

From losses to profit

Compared with the KRW 13 billion operating loss in 2022, the earnings structure has changed materially. The second quarter of 2026 added an operating profit of KRW 2.28 billion, another profitable quarter.

Cost and store structure improvements

The company attributed the 2025 operating improvement to lower cost of sales from closing inefficient stores and managing discounts. Directly operated galleries and a broader supplier base are being pursued to widen the distribution structure. If this approach continues, the company has room to protect margins even as sales shrink.

Cash generation and balance sheet trends

Operating cash flow reached KRW 8.87 billion in 2025, compared with KRW 3.45 billion in 2022. The debt-to-equity ratio declined from 215.6% in 2022 to 205.5% in 2025. The ratio rose to 220.5% in 2024 before easing, so the trend has been gradual.

Bear factors

Revenue still shrinking

Revenue declined each year, from KRW 126.5 billion in 2023 to KRW 114.7 billion in 2024 and KRW 104.6 billion in 2025. The 2025 decline of 8.8% was smaller than the 9.3% drop in 2024, but the contraction has not stopped. Third-quarter 2026 revenue of KRW 20.3 billion fell 7.6% from a year earlier.

Unstable quarterly profit

Operating profit fell 82.6% quarter on quarter to KRW 496 million in the first quarter of 2026, and the third quarter swung back to an operating loss of KRW 1.79 billion. Net profit attributable to controlling shareholders moved from KRW 1.51 billion in the second quarter to a KRW 1.91 billion loss in the third. The lack of consecutive profitable quarters is a weakness.

Competition and consumer conditions

The company's earnings commentary pointed to weaker consumer sentiment from the global slowdown and to intensified competition from large domestic brands and global entrants. In this setting, cost cuts alone may not offset falling revenue. Whether cost improvements can outweigh the sales decline is the central question for the bear case.

Risk factors

Cost ratio and discounting

The 2025 turnaround came from lower cost of sales, driven by closing inefficient stores and managing discount rates. Cost of sales will remain a key variable for profit after 2026. If discounts are widened to support sales, the cost gains could be offset and the operating margin could fall again.

With quarterly results alternating between profit and loss, discount policy could shift the direction of earnings.

Costs of directly operated galleries

The company is diversifying its distribution channels, including directly operated galleries. Directly operated stores carry their own operating costs, so fixed-cost pressure could rise if sales do not grow.

If these costs exceed the savings from lower cost of sales, the profit base built in 2025 would be harder to maintain. Changes in the cost structure from channel shifts could determine the direction of the operating margin.

Financial base and share price volatility

Total liabilities of KRW 77.9 billion at the end of 2025 exceeded total equity of KRW 37.9 billion, a debt-to-equity ratio of 205.5%. Net profit attributable to controlling shareholders totaled only KRW 2.78 billion over the last four quarters, which makes it hard to build the equity base quickly through earnings. With a small market capitalization, the share price moved nearly 30% in a single day in early September 2026.
