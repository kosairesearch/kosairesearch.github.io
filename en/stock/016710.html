02

Business structure

Daesung Holdings was founded in 1983 as a holding company centered on city-gas operations. Its core subsidiary, Daesung Energy, supplies city gas across Daegu Metropolitan City and parts of Gyeongbuk Province.

The group has broadened its portfolio into renewable energy through a Gyeongbuk city-gas subsidiary, into venture investing through Daesung Private Equity, and into IT services.

As of December 31, 2025, the company held more than 50% stakes in domestic subsidiaries including Daesung Energy, and investments in subsidiaries and affiliates made up about 61.3% of its standalone total assets.

The company also holds a 48.64% stake in Daesung Private Equity and a 13.20% stake in a Seoul city-gas utility. Its own business segments are small: in the Q1 2026 quarterly report, the management services segment accounted for 1.91% of consolidated sales and the IT segment for 0.31%.

City gas is treated as a stable cash generator because it operates under exclusive regional franchises. A 2021 credit rating report called city gas the group's core business but said non-city-gas subsidiaries contributed limited earnings. Competition is organized by regional franchise rather than nationwide.

Earnings analysis

Annual sales were KRW 1.25 trillion in 2022, KRW 1.22 trillion in 2023 (down 2.9% year on year), KRW 1.21 trillion in 2024 (down 0.4%), and KRW 1.22 trillion in 2025 (up 0.8%), moving within a narrow band.

Operating profit went from KRW 6.98 billion in 2022 to KRW 9.96 billion in 2023 (up 42.7%), KRW 23.3 billion in 2024 (up 133.8%), and KRW 28.1 billion in 2025 (up 20.5%), rising three consecutive years from 2023, with 2025 the largest year of 2022 to 2025.

The operating margin rose every year from 0.6% in 2022 to 2.3% in 2025, but the level remains thin, which is consistent with the cost structure of city-gas supply.

Net income attributable to controlling shareholders jumped from KRW 3.62 billion in 2022 to KRW 153.8 billion in 2023 (up 4,148.3%), then fell to KRW 32.1 billion in 2024 (down 79.1%) and KRW 17.2 billion in 2025 (down 46.6%), declining two years running.

In 2023 that figure far exceeded operating profit of KRW 9.96 billion, which points to a large contribution from non-operating items such as investment-related gains; the specific cause should be traced in the annual report notes.

Net income including non-controlling interests was KRW 159.4 billion in 2023, KRW 44.3 billion in 2024, and KRW 22.3 billion in 2025, of which KRW 17.2 billion belonged to controlling shareholders.

Operating cash flow was KRW 74.6 billion in 2022, -KRW 1.56 billion in 2023, KRW 55.8 billion in 2024, and KRW 55.6 billion in 2025, with 2023 the only negative year.

Quarterly sales went from KRW 164.8 billion in Q3 2025 to KRW 349 billion in Q4 2025 (up 111.8% quarter on quarter) and KRW 459.3 billion in Q1 2026 (up 31.6%), peaked there, then fell to KRW 203.3 billion in Q2 2026 (down 55.7%) and KRW 178.5 billion in Q3 2026 (down 12.2%), declining two quarters in a row; Q3 2026 sales still rose 8.3% from a year earlier.

Quarterly operating results moved from an operating loss of KRW 8.28 billion in Q3 2025 to operating profit of KRW 6.67 billion in Q4 2025, a swing to profit, then KRW 27.9 billion in Q1 2026 (up 317.3%), KRW 2.72 billion in Q2 2026 (down 90.2%), and an operating loss of KRW 6.25 billion in Q3 2026, which was a turn to loss; versus a year earlier the Q3 2026 loss continued but narrowed.

Net income attributable to controlling shareholders was a net loss of KRW 2.38 billion in Q3 2025, KRW 4.01 billion in Q4 2025, KRW 18 billion in Q1 2026, KRW 5.13 billion in Q2 2026, and a net loss of KRW 4.31 billion in Q3 2026, where the loss continued and widened from a year earlier. The last four quarters combined total KRW 22.9 billion.

05

Industry analysis

Daesung Holdings' consolidated results center on city gas, which operates under exclusive regional franchises. One source describes Daesung Energy as a top-seven city-gas supplier nationwide by supply volume as of 2020.

City-gas tariffs and raw-material cost structures are shaped by policy and cost swings, so they follow different drivers than the demand and inventory cycles of general manufacturing.

Quarterly sales concentrated in Q4 2025 (KRW 349 billion) and Q1 2026 (KRW 459.3 billion), which coincides with the winter heating season. Compared with fellow holding companies SK Square and Hanjinkal, Daesung Holdings' consolidated earnings rest on a single city-gas subsidiary cluster.

Results at its venture subsidiary Daesung Private Equity depend on exit markets such as IPOs and M&A, which drive valuation and realization gains. The Q3 2026 operating loss came during a lower-demand period, so it should be read together with Q4 results given seasonality.

In short, the industry variables are city-gas tariffs and costs on one side and the venture investment market on the other, and both feed into the value of the holding company's stakes and its dividend flow.

The bull case reads this as an improvement in subsidiary profitability, while whether it reflects structural change should be confirmed in the segment disclosures of the annual report.

Improving financial structure

The debt-to-equity ratio fell from 130.0% in 2022 to 103.1% in 2025, and controlling shareholders' equity stood at KRW 459.5 billion at year-end 2025. Operating cash flow held in the KRW 500 billion range at KRW 55.8 billion in 2024 and KRW 55.6 billion in 2025. The bull case sees this as evidence of reduced borrowing burden and room for dividends.

Trading below book value of equity

Market capitalization sits below the year-end 2025 book value of controlling shareholders' equity.

About 61.3% of standalone total assets are investments in subsidiaries and affiliates, including Daesung Private Equity, so the bull argument is that if those stakes are worth more than book value, the discount to net asset value could narrow.

Risk factors

Regulation and cost structure

City-gas tariffs linked to raw-material costs and government regulation can move subsidiary earnings. Exclusive regional franchises provide stability, but if tariff adjustments lag cost increases, profit pressure follows. The next periodic reports should show how costs and tariffs relate.

Valuation swings in investment stakes

About 61.3% of standalone total assets are investments in subsidiaries and affiliates, so valuation and disposal gains or losses on these stakes can move net income attributable to controlling shareholders significantly. In 2023 that effect was large enough to exceed operating profit by a wide margin. Changes in stake values can push holding-company profit in a direction different from operating trends.

Earnings volatility and venture cycle

Net income attributable to controlling shareholders fell sharply from KRW 153.8 billion in 2023 to KRW 17.2 billion in 2025, and quarterly results alternated between profit and loss. Results at the venture subsidiary depend on exit markets. Because earnings swing widely, one or two years of figures are not enough to judge the trend.

Overall view

Daesung Holdings is a holding company centered on city-gas subsidiaries, and its operating profit rose three consecutive years from 2023 through 2025 to KRW 28.1 billion on a reported basis.

Over the same span, net income attributable to controlling shareholders peaked at KRW 153.8 billion in 2023 and then fell two years running, showing that holding-company profit depends on both subsidiary operations and investment-stake results.

The debt-to-equity ratio improved from 130.0% in 2022 to 103.1% in 2025, and controlling shareholders' equity stood at KRW 459.5 billion at year-end 2025.

The 2026 quarterly picture is mixed: the Q4 2025 swing to profit and Q1 2026 operating profit of KRW 27.9 billion are positive signals, while declining sales after Q1, the Q3 2026 operating loss of KRW 6.25 billion, and the net loss of KRW 4.31 billion attributable to controlling shareholders are negatives.

Net income attributable to controlling shareholders totaled KRW 22.9 billion over the last four quarters.

The bull case rests on the operating profit recovery, the improved financial structure and the discount to net asset value, while the bear case rests on quarterly losses, two years of lower net income and a thin operating margin.
