02

Business structure

Speco was founded in 1979 to manufacture special-purpose construction machinery and steel structures and listed on KOSDAQ in 1997.

Its core business is construction equipment such as asphalt plants and concrete batching plants, alongside naval machinery including naval stabilizer systems and wind tower production for wind power.

Key products include asphalt mixing plants, concrete batching plants, naval stabilizer systems, active roll-reduction steering gear and wind towers.

Based on second-quarter 2026 sales, the mix was 75% naval machinery, 19% construction equipment and 6% wind power, but other data sets classify plant equipment at 55.27%, naval stabilizers and related items at 41.80% and wind towers at 2.93%, so the split depends on the classification used.

The wind tower business is run by the Mexican subsidiary SPECO WINDPOWER, while the Vietnamese subsidiary SPECO VIETNAM handles construction equipment plant sales. Main revenue comes from domestic and overseas local markets, and in Korea the company is expanding parts sales under an MRO model.

Plant equipment is a project business whose timing depends on client investment schedules, raw material prices and exchange rates. In defense, Speco supplies equipment for naval vessels. Customers include overseas road authorities, and the March 2026 Ethiopian Roads Administration contract is a representative example.

04

Earnings analysis

Annual revenue fell from KRW 31.1 billion in 2022 to KRW 23 billion in 2023, a 26.0% decline, and then rose to KRW 27.2 billion in 2024 (up 18.3%) and KRW 38 billion in 2025 (up 39.6%), a two-year increase. 2025 was the largest year in the 2022 to 2025 period.

Operating losses were KRW 3.77 billion in 2022, KRW 4.15 billion in 2023 and KRW 5.69 billion in 2024, a widening loss, before narrowing to KRW 461 million in 2025.

The operating margin moved from -12.1% in 2022, -18.0% in 2023 and -20.9% in 2024 to -1.2% in 2025, and the company has recorded losses for four consecutive years from 2022 through 2025.

Net income attributable to controlling shareholders went from KRW 611 million in 2022 to a net loss of KRW 2.06 billion in 2023, a turn into losses, then a net loss of KRW 1.71 billion in 2024 and KRW 308 million in 2025, with losses narrowing.

Net income including non-controlling interests shows the same figures for each year. Operating cash flow fell from KRW 8.57 billion in 2022 to KRW 2.95 billion in 2023, then turned negative at -KRW 1.97 billion in 2024 and -KRW 1.78 billion in 2025.

The debt-to-equity ratio declined from 69.1% in 2022, 79.7% in 2023 and 87.5% in 2024 to 58.4% in 2025, when total equity was KRW 45.6 billion, equity attributable to controlling shareholders was KRW 44.3 billion and total liabilities were KRW 26.6 billion.

On a quarterly basis, revenue reached KRW 13.5 billion in the fourth quarter of 2025, up 45.5% from the prior quarter, and operating profit of KRW 1.47 billion marked a turn to profit.

Revenue then fell to KRW 6.29 billion in the first quarter of 2026, down 53.3% from the prior quarter, with an operating loss of KRW 58.41 million.

The second quarter brought revenue of KRW 4.11 billion and an operating loss of KRW 1.29 billion, and the third quarter brought revenue of KRW 4.17 billion with an operating loss of KRW 502 million. The third-quarter operating loss was smaller than a year earlier, but revenue was down 54.9% year on year.

A first-half 2026 summary by a financial data service showed revenue down 31.8% and a larger operating loss, while net income turned positive.

In the first quarter of 2026 net income attributable to controlling shareholders was positive despite the operating loss, and non-operating income and tax items appear to account for the gap.

05

Industry analysis

Speco's earnings are sensitive to the construction cycle and to plant tender timing. One data service describes the construction equipment segment as hit by a construction slowdown and trade tensions, which weigh on overseas sales.

At the same time, the same data points to a recovery in plant order volume as domestic and overseas plant markets normalize. The sharp rise in revenue in the first half of 2025 is also linked to that normalization.

In naval machinery, the company is pursuing recycled asphalt plant projects to serve a growing eco-friendly market, a product group tied to tighter environmental rules. The wind tower business has relied on its production base in Mexico, where demand depends on local policy and power generation plans.

Naval equipment supply to defense customers appears to be a smaller share, and the exact weight should be checked in the company's filings.

Compared with construction equipment makers in the same sector classification, such as HD Construction Equipment and Doosan Bobcat, Speco is smaller and has a higher weight in plant equipment and naval machinery.

On balance, the plant order recovery is a positive factor, while the exposure of overseas sales to trade conditions and client investment decisions is a negative one.

The key question is how quickly order backlog converts into revenue, given that first-half 2026 revenue declined. The defense business has reported naval equipment supply, but the contract size and timing need to be confirmed in disclosures. No specific full-year revenue guidance was found in the material reviewed.

Because revenue fell and losses continued through the third quarter of 2026, the direction of full-year results will depend on how much revenue is recognized in the fourth quarter of 2026.

Risk factors

Order and revenue volatility

Plant equipment is a project business affected by client investment schedules, raw material prices and exchange rates. As quarterly revenue fell from KRW 6.29 billion to KRW 4.11 billion, revenue can drop sharply during gaps in orders. Heavier reliance on single large contracts would widen quarterly swings.

Cash flow and financial capacity

Operating cash flow was negative at -KRW 1.97 billion in 2024 and -KRW 1.78 billion in 2025. Total equity of KRW 45.6 billion at the end of 2025 is a buffer, but without cash generation, working capital needs could become heavier. The debt-to-equity ratio fell to 58.4%, yet continued losses would reduce financial room.

Overseas operations and policy risk

The wind tower business depends on the Mexican subsidiary's production and is affected by local policy and power plans. The Vietnamese subsidiary and overseas plant sales are also exposed to exchange rates and trade terms. A prolonged trade dispute could weigh on overseas orders and export profitability at the same time.

Overall view

Speco recorded losses for four consecutive years from 2022 through 2025, but in 2025 revenue rose 39.6% to KRW 38 billion and the operating loss narrowed to KRW 461 million, with the debt-to-equity ratio falling to 58.4% at year-end.

However, quarterly revenue through the third quarter of 2026 dropped to around KRW 4 billion, and operating losses have run for three straight quarters.

Net income attributable to controlling shareholders has been negative for two straight quarters since the second quarter of 2026, with a four-quarter total of KRW 240 million. On the positive side are the recovery in plant orders, the Ethiopian contract, and the expansion of recycled asphalt plants and MRO parts sales.

On the negative side are exposure of overseas sales to trade conditions, two consecutive years of negative operating cash flow, and the possibility that orders convert to revenue slowly.

On valuation, the price-to-book ratio has moved below book value compared with the at-or-above-1x year-end levels of 2022 to 2024, while earnings-based multiples offer little guidance during loss years.
