Sales Slide and Operating Loss Keep Shinwha Intertek's Recovery in Question

02

Business structure

Shinwha Intertek, founded in 1988, is a display and mobile materials company and a subsidiary of its parent, Hyosung Group. Its core product is optical film used in backlight units and panels for LCD and OLED displays.

The company supplies customized products to customers' specifications on a B2B basis and is expanding into higher-value films such as OLED encapsulation materials. According to its 2025 quarterly report, the main business divisions are the optical film division and the mobile OLED tape division.

The optical film lineup includes general composite films, quantum dot (QD) films, DBEF composite films and ND films. Production runs through two Chinese subsidiaries: the Dongguan unit makes mobile OLED tape and the Suzhou unit makes optical film.

The tape business started with adhesive tape for IT devices such as smartphones, and it extended into battery materials after mass production of external insulation tape for secondary batteries began in January 2024. A 2024 press report estimated the domestic battery tape market at about KRW 400 billion.

The company is also developing a 3D XR HUD optical module for automotive use, and it disclosed that it completed A2 sample validation with a global customer in July 2024 and is negotiating detailed specifications. Revenue shares by division are not presented as figures in this report.

04

Earnings analysis

Annual sales fell from KRW 201.2 billion in 2022 to KRW 177.5 billion in 2023, a decline of 11.8%, then rose 39.4% to KRW 247.4 billion in 2024 before falling 7.4% to KRW 229.1 billion in 2025. Of the 2022 to 2025 period, 2024 was the largest year and 2023 was the smallest.

Profitability moved from losses toward recovery. Operating losses widened from KRW 2.77 billion in 2022 to KRW 15.5 billion in 2023, and the company returned to operating profit of KRW 5.81 billion in 2024. Operating profit rose 56.6% to KRW 9.1 billion in 2025, and the operating margin improved from 2.3% to 4.0%.

Net profit attributable to controlling shareholders turned positive at KRW 845 million in 2024 and rose 297.8% to KRW 3.36 billion in 2025. Net profit including non-controlling interests was the same in both years.

Operating cash flow moved from KRW 1.13 billion in 2022 and -KRW 456 million in 2023 to -KRW 4.8 billion in 2024 and KRW 23.3 billion in 2025. The debt ratio fell from 100.7% in 2024 to 72.8% in 2025. At year-end 2025, total equity was KRW 97.7 billion and total liabilities were KRW 71.1 billion.

On a quarterly basis, Q3 2025 sales of KRW 55.3 billion and operating profit of KRW 1.84 billion were followed by Q4 2025 sales of KRW 53.1 billion and an operating loss of KRW 1.28 billion. Q1 2026 sales were KRW 49.3 billion with operating profit of KRW 579 million.

Q2 2026 sales were KRW 44.3 billion, and operating profit rose 382.2% quarter on quarter to KRW 2.79 billion. Q3 2026 sales were KRW 48.8 billion, up 10.3% from the prior quarter but down 11.7% from a year earlier, and the company posted an operating loss of KRW 2.01 billion, a switch to loss from a year earlier.

Net loss attributable to controlling shareholders was KRW 4.09 billion in Q3 2026, and the cumulative figure over the last four quarters is KRW 5.38 billion.

The filings do not break out cost of sales ratios or selling and administrative expenses, so the drivers of the Q3 swing to an operating loss cannot be separated by item, and one-off items cannot be identified from the disclosed figures. The next periodic report is where these details should be checked.

05

Industry analysis

Shinwha Intertek's results are tied to display panel investment and changes in mobile device specifications. Optical films are in a transition as LCD demand gives way to OLED and higher-value films, so product mix and customer composition drive sales and margins.

Tape for mobile OLED devices also varies with design changes in smartphone panels. Battery insulation tape sits in a market that could benefit from EV and ESS demand growth, but a 2024 press estimate put the domestic market at about KRW 400 billion, and share competition is likely to be intense.

The reliance on a small number of large customers is another factor to watch. Within the electronics components sector, Shinwha Intertek is a small materials company compared with large component makers such as Samsung Electro-Mechanics and LG Innotek.

Those groups supply a wide range of components, while Shinwha Intertek concentrates on optical films and tapes, so cycles in its specific products feed more directly into results. Its overseas production base in China is also a factor for cost and currency exposure.

Sales declined or stalled quarter to quarter through 2026, which may reflect demand adjustments in its existing optical film business, but the specific causes cannot be confirmed from the disclosed figures.

Outlook

Future results depend on how quickly volumes recover in the existing optical film and OLED tape businesses and how much the new businesses contribute to sales.

Battery external insulation tape began mass production in January 2024 and is supplied to large domestic companies, and the company has said it plans to expand into internal tape, module and pack tape, and tape for electric vehicles.

The 3D XR HUD optical module for automotive use completed A2 sample validation with a global customer in July 2024, and detailed specification talks are ongoing.

The disclosed materials do not show mass production timing or the size of customer orders after sample validation, so when this business will reflect in results is uncertain.

Operating profit rose to KRW 2.79 billion in Q2 2026, and whether that was a temporary recovery or a trend should be judged from the following quarters, since Q3 2026 returned to an operating loss of KRW 2.01 billion.

From a financial standpoint, the next annual report will show whether the operating margin holds at the 2025 level and whether the debt ratio stays around the 70% range. Operating cash flow was positive at KRW 23.3 billion in 2025, so whether 2026 keeps the same direction should also be checked.

For the new businesses to affect results, customer expansion must be confirmed through disclosures or investor relations, so ad hoc disclosures matter.

Bull factors

Earnings recovery and cash flow improvement in 2025

Operating cash flow swung to KRW 23.3 billion from negative readings in 2023 and 2024.

Improved financial structure

The debt ratio fell from 100.7% in 2024 to 72.8% in 2025. At year-end 2025, total liabilities were KRW 71.1 billion against total equity of KRW 97.7 billion. A capital base larger than liabilities can act as a buffer when industry conditions weaken.

New businesses in battery tape and AR HUD

Battery external insulation tape entered mass production in January 2024 and is supplied to large domestic companies. The 3D XR HUD optical module completed A2 sample validation with a global customer in July 2024. The company has said it will broaden its tape lineup to electric vehicles.

Cumulative net losses over the last four quarters

Risk factors

Customer concentration and display cycle

Optical film and OLED tape depend heavily on specific panel makers and mobile device specifications. If panel investment slows or customers change designs, volumes can fall quickly. The decline in quarterly sales through 2026 may reflect this risk, although the cause is not confirmed by the filings.

Timing of new business monetization

The battery tape market is expected to grow but could face intense competition. For the AR HUD optical module, mass production timing and order size have not been confirmed through disclosures after sample validation. If new businesses take time to convert into sales, weakness in the existing businesses could persist.

China production base and cash flow volatility

Production is concentrated in two Chinese subsidiaries, so the company is exposed to currency movements, local regulation and cost changes. Operating cash flow was KRW 1.13 billion in 2022, negative in 2023 and 2024, and turned to KRW 23.3 billion only in 2025. One year of positive cash flow is not enough to call it stable.
