Report overview

Hanjinkal: Shareholder Contest Over the Korean Air Stake

Hanjinkal holds 26.05% of Korean Air. After the largest-shareholder change disclosed on November 4, 2026, the Cho side's planned share purchase and the December 16, 2026 merger date have become the key variables for the year-end.

  1. 1

    Control: The November 4, 2026 disclosure named the Hoban side as largest shareholder, and the Cho side plans to add shares through an off-market block trade from early December.

  2. 2

    Earnings: The company swung to an operating loss of KRW 7.52 billion in 2025, while net income attributable to controlling shareholders turned to KRW 275.1 billion in the third quarter of 2026.

  3. 3

    Dividend structure: About 83.7% of parent-only dividend income in the first half of 2026 came from Korean Air, tying the holding company's cash flow to its main subsidiary.

  4. 4

  5. 5

02

Business structure

Hanjinkal is the holding company of the Hanjin Group, and its core asset is a 26.05% stake in Korean Air. Its income relies heavily on dividends from subsidiaries.

About 83.7% of parent-only dividend income in the first half of 2026, roughly KRW 72.2 billion out of about KRW 86.3 billion, came from Korean Air. iM Securities estimated the value of Hanjinkal's Korean Air stake at about KRW 2.8 trillion.

The Cho Won-tae side's related-party stake was 20.56% at the end of 2025 according to the annual report. Including Delta Air Lines at 14.90%, KDB at 10.58% and LX Pantos at 3.83%, the friendly stake totals 49.88%.

The Hoban Group became a second-largest shareholder after buying a stake from a private equity fund in 2022 and raised its holding to 20.15% in July 2026. The Hanjin side says the friendly stakes give it no concern over control. Japan Airlines has also acquired Hanjinkal shares, widening the group of friendly holders.

Because the stake structure ties control to Korean Air, the holding company's business is concentrated in one main subsidiary.

04

Earnings analysis

Based on the annual reports, revenue rose from KRW 200.3 billion in 2022 to KRW 298.4 billion in 2025, increasing for three consecutive years from 2023 through 2025. Growth slowed from 37.6% in 2023 to 6.0% in 2024 and 2.1% in 2025.

Operating profit went from KRW 14.5 billion in 2022 to KRW 42.8 billion in 2023 (up 195.7%) and KRW 49.2 billion in 2024 (up 14.8%), then swung to an operating loss of KRW 7.52 billion in 2025. The operating margin was 15.5% in 2023 and 16.8% in 2024, and fell to -2.5% in 2025.

The third-quarter 2025 operating loss of KRW 36.6 billion appears to be the segment that most affected the annual loss. Net income attributable to controlling shareholders moved from KRW 683.8 billion in 2022 to KRW 385.1 billion in 2023, KRW 497 billion in 2024 and KRW 155 billion in 2025.

Net income including non-controlling interests was KRW 159.2 billion in 2025, a different figure. Quarterly revenue rose for two consecutive quarters in 2026, from KRW 69.6 billion in the first quarter to KRW 71.5 billion in the second and KRW 80.2 billion in the third.

Operating profit was KRW 13 billion, KRW 16.8 billion and KRW 11.9 billion in the first three quarters of 2026, falling 29.3% from the second to the third quarter.

Net income attributable to controlling shareholders swung from a loss of KRW 87 billion in the second quarter to KRW 275.1 billion in the third, a gain that far exceeds operating profit and warrants a look at non-operating items in the filings.

Net income attributable to controlling shareholders over the latest four quarters totals KRW 321.3 billion. The debt ratio fell from 52.0% in 2022 to 20.9% in 2025, with total equity of KRW 3.42 trillion at year-end 2025, of which KRW 3.34 trillion belonged to controlling shareholders. Operating cash flow improved from negative KRW 3.56 billion in 2022 to KRW 122.2 billion in 2025.

05

Industry analysis

On the aviation outlook, Eugene Investment & Securities said in a November 2026 report that solid passenger demand, strength in AI and semiconductor cargo, and a weaker won-dollar rate are favorable, and it kept Korean Air as its top sector pick.

By contrast, Mirae Asset Securities said in a January 2026 report that confirming a peak in the exchange rate and a bottom in the cargo market should come first. The two views agree on the sector but differ on the cargo cycle and the direction of the exchange rate.

The Korean Air and Asiana Airlines integration is approaching launch of the combined Korean Air at year-end, with work underway to combine organization, routes, reservation systems and mileage programs.

Some analysts see Korean Air's room to raise dividends after the integration as dependent on aircraft purchase burdens and post-merger cash flow. For the holding company, Hanjinkal has been described as lagging while domestic group holding companies benefited from strength in semiconductors and defense.

Among 10 listed Hanjin Group affiliates over the period from early June 2025 to mid-May 2026, only three, Korean Air, Korean Air preferred and Hanjin Korea Airport, saw market capitalization rise.

Over the same period, holding-company discount and governance issues appear to have weighed on the share price alongside the aviation cycle.

06

Outlook

The nearest checkpoint within the year is the re-filing of the largest-shareholder change on December 1, 2026. Cho Won-tae plans to buy 325,000 common shares, worth about KRW 37.05 billion, through an off-market block trade from December 3, 2026 to January 1, 2027.

If the purchase is completed, the Cho side's stake would rise into the 21% range and move ahead of the Hoban side again. The Korean Air and Asiana Airlines merger date has been set for December 16, 2026.

The record date for voting rights at the regular shareholders meeting is December 31, and the stake competition may continue until then. KDB reportedly plans to sell its Hanjinkal stake after the integration is complete.

Reports differ on KDB's 2020 investment size, at KRW 500 billion in one report and KRW 800 billion in another, so the size of potential supply needs to be confirmed. Korean Air's current shareholder-return policy ends with the 2026 fiscal year, and whether it is strengthened has drawn attention.

At the regular shareholders meeting in March 2027, three independent directors' terms expire, and the Hoban side could try to enter the board. The 2026 annual report is the next regular filing, and it will show full-year 2026 results and the dividend plan.

08

Bull factors

Possible expansion of Korean Air shareholder returns

Brokers see a possibility that Korean Air strengthens its shareholder returns as the largest-shareholder position stabilizes. The current policy ends with the 2026 fiscal year, so the next policy could change Hanjinkal's dividend income.

Each KRW 100 rise in Korean Air's per-share dividend adds roughly KRW 9.6 billion to Hanjinkal's dividend income.

Aviation demand and post-merger bargaining power

Eugene Investment & Securities viewed solid passenger demand, AI and semiconductor cargo strength and a weaker exchange rate as favorable. Mirae Asset Securities projected stronger bargaining power after the merger over the long term.

Because Korean Air is integrating organization, routes and reservation systems, whether integration results appear will decide how the bullish case holds up.

Control premium and value of the held assets

Common shares carry part of a control premium arising from the stake contest with the Hoban side. Adding the Cho side and friendly holders gives 49.88%, which some see as sufficient for control defense.

Bear factors

Holding company discount and market-cap outflow

Reports say the holding company discount persists. Over the period from June 2025 to May 2026, Hanjinkal's market capitalization fell while Korean Air's rose. Hanjinkal's preferred shares fell 34.1% over the same period and did not benefit from the governance issue.

2025 loss swing and volatile quarterly profit

Operating profit swung to a loss of KRW 7.52 billion in 2025, and net income attributable to controlling shareholders fell 68.8% to KRW 155 billion.

In the second quarter of 2026, a KRW 87 billion loss attributable to controlling shareholders came despite KRW 16.8 billion in operating profit, so the two measures moved in opposite directions. Third-quarter operating profit fell 29.3% from the prior quarter, so quarterly direction has not settled.

Governance uncertainty and the KDB stake

The Hoban side states a simple-investment purpose, but some analysts consider a control purpose more likely given the size of the investment and the stake gap. The National Pension Service holds 5.46% and voted against Cho Won-tae's reappointment as an inside director at the March 2026 shareholders meeting. How KDB disposes of its 10.58% stake remains an open variable.

Risk factors

Governance dispute

The stake competition between the Hoban side and the Cho side may continue through year-end. Depending on whether the block purchase is completed, the largest-shareholder position could change again, so control uncertainty persists. Some analysts still consider a near-term change of control limited.

KDB share supply after the merger

Once the integration is complete, KDB's 10.58% stake is expected to reach the market. Some observers expect a full control dispute if the Hoban side acquires the stake. Because reports differ on KDB's 2020 investment size, the actual share volume needs further confirmation.

Dependence on Korean Air dividends

Most of Hanjinkal's dividend income comes from Korean Air, so the holding company's cash flow depends on Korean Air's dividend capacity. That capacity depends on aircraft acquisition burdens and post-integration cash flow.

If the aviation cycle or the exchange rate weakens, both Korean Air's dividends and the holding company's income would be affected.

Overall view

Hanjinkal is a holding company with a 26.05% stake in Korean Air. Its earnings swung widely, from an operating loss in 2025 to net income attributable to controlling shareholders turning positive in the third quarter of 2026.

Most of its dividend income comes from Korean Air, so its results are tied to Korean Air's dividends and cash flow. On governance, the largest-shareholder position moved to the Hoban side on November 4, 2026, and the Cho side plans a December block purchase to regain it.

Bullish points include a possible strengthening of Korean Air's shareholder returns and favorable views on the aviation outlook. Bearish points include the holding company discount, market-cap outflow, large swings in quarterly profit and supply pressure from KDB's stake disposal.

On valuation, the share price trades at a large premium to net asset value, and the current market capitalization is well above iM Securities' estimate of appropriate value.

The next checkpoints are the December 16, 2026 merger date, completion of the December block purchase and the March 2027 regular shareholders meeting.
