JR Global REIT: Debt Restructuring Is the Key Variable

02

Business structure

JR Global REIT is an overseas real estate REIT managed by JR Investment Management and listed in August 2020. It operates as a parent REIT that invests in child REITs, which in turn hold overseas properties or local entities whose underlying assets are overseas real estate.

The two child REITs are JR No. 26 and JR No. 28. JR No. 26 holds the Finance Tower Complex, a landmark building in Brussels, Belgium, while JR No. 28 holds the office building at 498 Seventh Avenue in Manhattan, New York.

According to the company website, the Belgian asset is 100% leased to the Belgian Federal Buildings Agency, and the New York asset is 93.2% leased with the Service Employees International Union (SEIU), the largest US healthcare workers union, as its main tenant.

A new lease and early extension for the New York asset disclosed in July 2024 meant no tenant lease expires until 2030. With only two assets, the lease situation of individual tenants has an outsized effect on total rental income.

The balance sheet is debt-heavy: at June 2026 total liabilities were KRW 1.7 trillion and total equity was KRW 1.06 trillion, with a debt-to-equity ratio of 160.0%. Borrowings tied to the Belgian asset are the main burden.

Because the company directly holds overseas assets, exchange rates and overseas leasing conditions directly affect earnings.

04

Earnings analysis

Revenue rose from KRW 60.5 billion in the fiscal year ended June 2023 to KRW 62.6 billion in the fiscal year ended June 2024 (up 3.5% year on year), KRW 72.2 billion in the fiscal year ended June 2025 (up 15.3%), and KRW 87.5 billion in the fiscal year ended June 2026 (up 21.3%), marking three consecutive years of growth from the fiscal year ended June 2024 through the fiscal year ended June 2026.

Operating profit fell from KRW 32.6 billion in the fiscal year ended June 2023 to KRW 31 billion in the fiscal year ended June 2024 (down 4.9%), then rose to KRW 54.5 billion in the fiscal year ended June 2025 (up 76.0%) and KRW 69.8 billion in the fiscal year ended June 2026 (up 27.9%), marking two consecutive years of growth from the fiscal year ended June 2025 through the fiscal year ended June 2026.

Within the fiscal years ended June 2023 through June 2026, the largest operating profit was in the fiscal year ended June 2026 and the smallest in the fiscal year ended June 2024.

The operating margin moved from 53.9% and 49.5% in the fiscal years ended June 2023 and June 2024 to 75.6% and 79.7% in the fiscal years ended June 2025 and June 2026, reflecting operating profit growth that outpaced revenue growth.

Net income attributable to controlling shareholders was KRW 23.2 billion in the fiscal year ended June 2023, KRW 20.7 billion in the fiscal year ended June 2024 (down 10.9%), and KRW 16.2 billion in the fiscal year ended June 2025 (down 21.6%), before the company swung to a net loss of KRW 100.7 billion in the fiscal year ended June 2026.

Net income including non-controlling interests was reported as a net loss of KRW 100.7 billion in the same year.

The gap of more than KRW 170 billion between operating profit of KRW 69.8 billion and that net loss points to large costs below the operating line, and the composition of those items should be checked in the periodic report.

Operating cash flow was KRW 40.4 billion, KRW 34.4 billion, negative KRW 24.6 billion and KRW 21 billion in the fiscal years ended June 2023 through June 2026, turning positive again in the most recent year.

The net loss attributable to controlling shareholders over the last four quarters (from the third quarter of 2025 to the second quarter of 2026) also totaled KRW 100.7 billion, the same as the annual loss for the fiscal year ended June 2026.

A news report dated November 17, 2026 put consolidated operating revenue for the 15th half-year period (July to September 2026) at KRW 44.2 billion and operating profit at KRW 31.3 billion; these are preliminary figures, not audited periodic-report results, and they show that operating profit remained positive.

The debt-to-equity ratio was 139.4% in the fiscal year ended June 2023, 166.3% in June 2024, 131.4% in June 2025 and 160.0% in June 2026, staying above 100%. Operating profit alone does not guarantee the ability to repay debt or the company's continuity as a going concern.

05

Industry analysis

JR Global REIT's position within the REIT sector is mixed across scale and profit indicators. In a comparison of 27 real estate sector companies on an annual basis compiled by AlphaSquare, the company ranks 4th in revenue, 2nd in operating profit and 27th in net income.

The gap between a high operating profit ranking and a low net income ranking suggests that costs below the operating line are heavy. Funding conditions also add cost.

The 4th bond issuance listed in March 2025 carried a 6.5% coupon and traded at a yield 2.7 percentage points above the market yield of around 3.8% for the same credit rating. Of the KRW 50 billion borrowing disclosed in February 2026, the issuance in that tranche was KRW 16 billion at a 6% coupon.

As an overseas real estate REIT, exchange rates and overseas leasing markets directly affect distributable earnings. The company's investor relations list includes a notice that part of the currency hedge transactions for JR No. 26 and JR No. 28 was terminated early.

With only two assets, the concentration of tenant risk is high compared with other listed REITs in the same sector. Unlike larger sector peers such as Macquarie Korea Infrastructure Fund, SK REIT and Lotte REIT, the company has limited room to widen its portfolio through asset rotation or acquisitions. Its sector position is mixed between earnings scale and financial stability.

06

Outlook

The nearest checkpoint for the company is debt restructuring. The company is restructuring its domestic debt and filed for commencement of rehabilitation proceedings with the Seoul Rehabilitation Court in April 2026. The court has deferred its decision on whether to commence proceedings until December 13, 2026.

Before that, the company plans to hold a bondholders meeting on December 2, 2026 to pursue changes to debt terms. The company aims to complete debt repayment by the end of 2028 and has suspended dividends until repayment of the target claims is finished.

After repayment is complete, whether to resume dividends and their size will be decided in light of asset management performance and business results. In October 2026, the lender group sent a letter declaring a loss of the benefit of term and reserving its right to enforce security interests.

The company says no event triggering loss of the benefit of term has occurred. If the loss of the benefit of term is actually declared, the pledge over shares and mortgage on the Belgian Finance Tower property could be enforced. According to the company, no tenant lease in the New York asset expires until 2030.

On the listing side, the Korea Exchange granted a remedy period until April 14, 2027, and trading has been suspended since April 28, 2026.

Whether the remedy period is cleared and the audit opinion is resolved are the next checkpoints, and the audit opinion on the annual report for the fiscal year ending June 2027 will frame the interpretation of later results.

Bull factors

Operating cash flow turned from negative KRW 24.6 billion in the fiscal year ended June 2025 to KRW 21 billion in the fiscal year ended June 2026. Stable leasing structure

According to the company website, the Belgian asset is 100% leased to the Belgian Federal Buildings Agency. The New York asset is 93.2% leased, with SEIU as its main tenant, and the company says no tenant lease expires until 2030.

Debt repayment plan and company position

The company has set a target of completing debt repayment by the end of 2028 and suspended dividends until repayment of the target claims is finished. If changes to debt terms are pursued at the December 2, 2026 bondholders meeting, the repayment burden could be spread out.

Bear factors

Repeated disclaimers of opinion

Auditor Hanul Accounting Corp. issued disclaimers on both the 14th annual audit report and the 15th half-year financial statements, citing going-concern uncertainty. A disclaimer does not establish accounting fraud, but it signals that the auditor could not issue an unqualified opinion. The Korea Exchange granted a remedy period until April 14, 2027 in connection with the delisting trigger.

Gap between profit and repayment capacity

Net loss attributable to controlling shareholders was KRW 100.7 billion in the fiscal year ended June 2026, and the cumulative net loss over the last four quarters is the same amount. Total liabilities were KRW 1.7 trillion, with a debt-to-equity ratio of 160.0%.

A news report notes that positive operating profit does not guarantee repayment capacity or the company's continuity as a going concern.

Possible enforcement of security and extended dividend suspension

In October 2026 the lender group sent a letter reserving its right to enforce security. Whether the lender group declares the loss of the benefit of term determines whether the pledge over shares and mortgage tied to the Belgian Finance Tower could be enforced.

Dividends are suspended until the end of 2028, and whether and how much they will resume after repayment has not been decided.

10

Risk factors

Listing risk

Trading has been suspended since April 28, 2026, and the remedy period for the delisting trigger caused by the disclaimer of opinion runs until April 14, 2027. If the audit opinion is not resolved within that period, whether the listing can be maintained becomes uncertain. In that case, shareholder liquidity would be the first variable to surface alongside the listing decision.

Collateral and debt structure

Loans secured by the Belgian asset are the core burden. The lender group has reserved its right to enforce security while withholding a declaration of loss of the benefit of term.

In February 2026 the company borrowed KRW 50 billion to repay short-term bonds and fund CAPEX on the Belgian asset, and the issuance in that tranche carried a 6% coupon.

Overseas asset management risk

Because the two child REITs invest in overseas assets, exchange rate movements directly affect earnings and distribution capacity. Part of the currency hedge transactions for JR No. 26 and JR No. 28 was terminated early, so the degree to which exchange rate movements affect earnings may differ from before.

With only two assets, changes in the leasing situation or valuation of an individual asset have an outsized effect on total results.

Overall view

In the fiscal year ended June 2026, JR Global REIT recorded operating profit of KRW 69.8 billion and an operating margin of 79.7%, the largest operating profit in the 2023 to 2026 period.

However, the company swung to a controlling-shareholder net loss of KRW 100.7 billion in the same year, and with total liabilities of KRW 1.7 trillion and a debt-to-equity ratio of 160.0%, operating profit alone does not guarantee debt repayment capacity.

The auditor issued a disclaimer of opinion on the 15th half-year financial statements for July to September 2026, citing going-concern uncertainty, after a similar disclaimer on the 14th annual audit report. On the listing side, trading remains suspended and the remedy period runs until April 14, 2027.

On the debt side, the bondholders meeting on December 2, 2026 and the rehabilitation decision by December 13, 2026 are the nearest events. Bullish factors include continued operating profitability, no tenant lease expiries in the New York asset until 2030, and a 100% leased Belgian asset.

Bearish factors include repeated disclaimers of opinion, the possibility of enforcement on security interests, and dividend suspension through the end of 2028. The most important variables now are the outcome of debt restructuring and whether the audit opinion is resolved.
