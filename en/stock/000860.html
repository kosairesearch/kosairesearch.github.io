Report overview

Paint Losses Weigh as Resin Subsidiaries Carry Profit

Subsidiaries in resins and composite materials continue to offset losses in Kangnam Jevisco's core paint business, while 2026 quarterly results have swung sharply, with a large profit rebound in the second quarter followed by a steep slowdown in the third.

02

Business structure

Kangnam Jevisco operates three business segments: paints, resins, and composite molding materials, with production plants in China and Vietnam. Based on the 2024 first-quarter report, segment revenue shares were 52.5% for paints, 36.0% for resins, and 11.5% for composite molding materials and others.

The paint segment centers on architectural, marine, automotive, and steel-pipe coatings, sold under the Jevisco-brand name, and press coverage describes the company as the third-largest domestic paint maker.

Based on December 2025 data, domestic sales accounted for 87.64% of paint product revenue and exports for 12.36%. Per a March 2026 snapshot, the company's paint-segment market share was 8.20%.

The resin segment, run through a subsidiary group, produces industrial and construction-material resins, and press reports put its 2024 operating profit at KRW 24.7 billion. The composite materials segment serves automotive parts and office supplies and posted KRW 5.3 billion in operating profit in 2024.

Press reports tied the 2024 consolidated operating profit of KRW 20.5 billion to a paint-segment operating loss of KRW 7.4 billion, with KRW 2.0 billion of intercompany transactions excluded.

On inputs, the paint segment relies largely on imported pigments, resins, and solvents, so it is exposed to global raw material prices and exchange rates. Resin feedstock in the 2024 first-quarter report was 42.3% phenol and 57.7% urethane by purchase value.

04

Earnings analysis

Annual revenue fell from KRW 673.2 billion in 2022 to KRW 638.8 billion in 2023 (down 5.1% year on year), rose to KRW 643.1 billion in 2024 (up 0.7%), and dropped to KRW 598.6 billion in 2025 (down 6.9%). Among 2022–2025, 2022 was the largest year by revenue and 2025 the smallest.

Operating profit rose from KRW 4.41 billion in 2022 to KRW 23.2 billion in 2023 (up 425.9% year on year).

The company attributed the 2023 gain to lower raw-material costs and profitability efforts, and noted that a goodwill-related CGU impairment recorded the prior year did not recur, which helped swing pre-tax segment profit to positive.

Operating profit then fell to KRW 20.5 billion in 2024 (down 11.4%) and to KRW 8.29 billion in 2025 (down 59.6%), declining for two consecutive years from 2024 through 2025. The operating margin moved from 0.7% in 2022 to 3.6% in 2023, 3.2% in 2024, and 1.4% in 2025.

Net profit attributable to controlling shareholders was KRW 6.19 billion in 2022, KRW 16.5 billion in 2023 (up 166.9%), and KRW 13.3 billion in 2024 (down 19.8%), before turning into a net loss of KRW 2.69 billion in 2025.

Total net profit including non-controlling interests was KRW 926 million in 2025, a gap that reflects a sizable minority share of subsidiary earnings.

Quarterly results ran as follows: operating profit of KRW 4.38 billion in the third quarter of 2025, an operating loss of KRW 3.23 billion in the fourth quarter, KRW 862 million in the first quarter of 2026, KRW 12.4 billion in the second quarter (up 1,335.2% from the prior quarter), and KRW 1.21 billion in the third quarter (down 90.2% from the prior quarter and down 72.3% year on year).

Second-quarter revenue rose 21.5% to KRW 178.5 billion, yet the profit swing was far larger than the revenue change.

Press coverage tallied cumulative paint-segment operating losses of KRW 62.1 billion from 2019 through 2024, but the press gave conflicting readings of whether paint losses widened or narrowed in specific years because of differences in how the losses were measured.

Operating cash flow was KRW 14.4 billion in 2022, KRW 65.8 billion in 2023, KRW 47.2 billion in 2024, and KRW 38.6 billion in 2025, declining after 2024.

05

Industry analysis

Paint demand tracks construction and capital spending in end markets such as buildings, shipbuilding, automobiles, and steel pipes.

A 2025 first-quarter press analysis cited the escalation of US-China trade tensions, high interest rates that curbed investment and consumption, and tighter environmental rules as headwinds.

Paint margins are sensitive to the cost cycle because pigments, resins, and solvents are priced globally and exposed to exchange rates, and the company itself has credited lower raw-material costs for its 2023 profit gain.

Among domestic paint makers the company is ranked near the top, but its profile is closer to a mid-sized, paint- and resin-focused player than to large chemical groups such as LG Chem or KCC.

Large petrochemical producers such as Lotte Chemical, Kumho Petro Chemical, and Hanwha Solutions operate at larger scale, while the company's resin operations buy phenol and urethane from outside suppliers, so feedstock price moves drive its margins.

The composite materials business is linked to automotive parts demand and follows vehicle production trends. Overseas, plants in China and Vietnam produce paints, and press reports put overseas sales at 17.53% of revenue in the first half of 2025, a limited share compared with export-heavy chemical peers.

06

Outlook

According to the Corporate Monitor compilation as of August 31, 2026, first-half 2026 consolidated revenue rose 7.5%, operating profit rose 85.0%, and net profit rose 8.9% year on year. The same compilation credited resin-segment technology leadership and overseas expansion for the improvement.

The company has told the press it continues to pursue market diversification while revenue growth has stalled. New business areas include secondary-battery materials and composite molding materials. Based on December 2025 data, exports were 12.36% of paint product sales and 24.00% of the other resin-related sales.

Whether overseas sales keep expanding will be visible in upcoming results. The Pyeongtaek plant was completed in October 2022 and went through a normalization period.

However, third-quarter 2026 operating profit dropped to KRW 1.21 billion and the net loss attributable to controlling shareholders widened to KRW 4.72 billion, so the durability of the first-half improvement depends on the second-half profit trend.

The next confirmation point is the 2026 annual business report, which will show how far resin profits offset paint losses and whether the operating margin recovered from 1.4% in 2025.

Bull factors

Resin subsidiaries supply the profit

Press reports say the resin segment earned KRW 24.7 billion in operating profit in 2024, and the composite materials segment added KRW 5.3 billion. Those two segments offset a KRW 7.4 billion paint-segment operating loss to support consolidated operating profit of KRW 20.5 billion. The offset pattern has persisted for several years and is cited as the base of earnings resilience.

First-half 2026 profit improvement

According to the Corporate Monitor compilation dated August 31, 2026, consolidated operating profit rose 85.0% in the first half of 2026. The same compilation credited resin-segment technology and overseas expansion.

Second-quarter 2026 operating profit of KRW 12.4 billion is the largest single-quarter figure within the filed quarterly results.

Asset value and cash returns

Earlier press coverage described the company as an asset-heavy stock, valuing its fixed assets in the several-hundred-billion-won range. At the March 2026 shareholder meeting, a common cash dividend for fiscal 2025 was approved.

With the price-to-book ratio well below 1x, how much of that asset value is reflected in the share price remains an open question.

Bear factors

Cumulative paint-segment losses

Press tallies put cumulative paint-segment operating losses at KRW 62.1 billion from 2019 through 2024, and the parent-only cumulative operating loss over the same period at KRW 85.5 billion.

Consolidated profit has relied on subsidiaries to cover these losses, so the core business's structural deficit keeps dependence on subsidiaries high unless it narrows.

Sharp profit decline in Q3 2026

Third-quarter 2026 operating profit fell to KRW 1.21 billion, down 90.2% from the prior quarter and down 72.3% year on year. The net loss attributable to controlling shareholders widened to KRW 4.72 billion.

The second-quarter swing to profit reversed within a single quarter, which points to weak stability in quarterly earnings.

Shrinking revenue and cost exposure

Annual revenue declined from KRW 673.2 billion in 2022 to KRW 598.6 billion in 2025, with a 6.9% drop in 2025 alone. Because most paint raw materials are imported, swings in input prices and exchange rates can squeeze margins. The operating margin fell to 1.4% in 2025 from 3.6% in 2023, so both top-line and profitability weakened.

10

Risk factors

Raw materials and FX

Pigments, resins, and solvents used in paint are largely imported, leaving the company directly exposed to global raw-material prices and exchange rates. Phenol and urethane feedstock for the resin business are also bought externally, so cost increases can compress margins.

Because lower raw-material costs drove the 2023 profit gain, a reversal would likely affect earnings in a similar way.

Subsidiary dependence and ownership

Consolidated earnings lean heavily on the resin and composite materials subsidiaries, so a downturn there could weigh on the whole group. A filing in late December 2025 disclosed that a major shareholder's stake declined through inheritance. Changes in the ownership structure make management succession a key item to monitor.

Demand conditions and regulation

A 2025 first-quarter press analysis pointed to escalating US-China trade tensions and high interest rates that curbed investment and consumption, along with tighter environmental rules. Paint demand is tied to end markets such as construction and automobiles, so an economic slowdown can quickly reduce orders. Environmental rules that change raw-material composition could also raise product conversion costs.

Overall view

Kangnam Jevisco's core paint business has posted losses since 2019, and its resin and composite materials subsidiaries have supported consolidated profit.

Annual operating profit peaked at KRW 23.2 billion in 2023 and declined in both 2024 and 2025, when the company swung to a net loss attributable to controlling shareholders.

Operating profit rose to KRW 12.4 billion in the second quarter of 2026, and the first-half compilation showed improvement, but third-quarter operating profit fell to KRW 1.21 billion and the net loss attributable to controlling shareholders widened to KRW 4.72 billion.

On the balance sheet, the debt ratio was 37.4% at the end of 2025 and total equity stood at KRW 708.8 billion, and the price-to-book ratio sits well below 1x.

Against that, the price-to-earnings ratio cannot be calculated given four consecutive quarters of net losses, and exposure to raw materials, exchange rates, and shifts in major-shareholder holdings remain open variables.

The next checkpoint is the 2026 annual business report, where the key questions are whether the operating margin recovers from 1.4% in 2025 and whether paint-segment results improve.
