02

Business structure

DTS designs air-cooled heat exchangers and thermal management equipment to order and supplies them to global EPC (engineering, procurement and construction) contractors in the Middle East, Europe and the United States. AFC and ACC products accounted for 93.6% of 2025 revenue, and overseas sales represented 89%.

Its core equipment is used in LNG facility and power infrastructure projects, so revenue shifts from period to period depending on the timing of large individual projects.

According to the company's listing briefing, Middle East sales reached KRW 42.7 billion in the first half of 2026, or 54% of total revenue, which the company attributed to large UAE projects clustering in that period rather than to a regional sales focus.

Main customers are reported to be EPC contractors in Europe, Japan and the United States. New orders rose from KRW 41.0 billion in 2023 to KRW 114.6 billion in 2025, and KRW 57.8 billion of new orders were secured in the first half of 2026.

The company is pursuing two new businesses, AI data-center dry coolers and ORC (organic Rankine cycle) waste-heat power generation. It signed a commercial supply contract for a 1.5MW ORC system in July 2026 and plans to expand into 3–5MW product lines and enter Europe.

The company says few players operate in the ORC market and that it expects to compete well on price, though this is management's own view.

The company is classified under IT and software, but its operations are closer to plant equipment manufacturing, so direct comparison with listed software and IT services companies is limited.

03

Recent trends

Cumulative third-quarter 2026 revenue was KRW 33.2 billion, down 15.0% year on year. Operating profit came in at KRW 3.82 billion, down 55.2%, and net income at KRW 2.76 billion, down 54.9%. Profit fell far faster than revenue, which points to margin pressure.

The cost line items behind the decline cannot be pinpointed from the disclosed figures alone.

Press reports quoted company figures for the first half of 2026 of KRW 79.1 billion in revenue and KRW 15.0 billion in operating profit, up 26.2% and 42.9% year on year; these do not align in scale or direction with the cumulative disclosed figures, so this analysis relies on the disclosed numbers.

According to press reports, 2025 revenue was KRW 142.7 billion and operating profit KRW 25.1 billion, with operating profit up from KRW 13.3 billion in 2023.

The stock traded at 10,600 won on the morning of November 14, 2026, down 2.30% from the prior session, with a market capitalization of KRW 157.7 billion and trading value of KRW 2.7 billion. Against the company's indicated offering range of 17,000 to 18,500 won, the current price sits below that range.

Immediately after listing, 2,250,787 shares, or 15.15% of listed shares, were tradable. The largest shareholder, the listed parent holding a 32.03% stake, and a related party holding 12.20% are subject to a 30-month lock-up.

04

Outlook

After listing, the company plans to expand orders for its core business in line with LNG facility expansion and power infrastructure investment, while widening cooperation with local EPC firms from its Middle East base.

It targets about KRW 90 billion of new orders in the fourth quarter of 2026 alone, which would lift the year-end backlog to roughly KRW 140 billion, according to the company.

For regional diversification, it is pursuing 11 projects worth KRW 77 billion in total across eight regions, including the United States, Brazil and Qatar, from September to November 2026, of which U.S. projects account for KRW 45.5 billion, or 59%.

Offering proceeds are allocated to project response at KRW 12.0 billion, new-field facility investment at KRW 5.86 billion, and technology development at KRW 4.44 billion.

The company places first revenue from ORC in 2027 and from AI data-center cooling in 2029, so AFC and ACC are expected to remain the main earnings base through 2027.

The CEO said actual contracts for data-center cooling are expected in early 2027 or 2028, and that the business is currently closer to the planning and sales stage.

The CFO said the listed parent committed to a dividend payout ratio of about 30% through 2029 with the exchange, and that DTS would deliver a similar level of shareholder returns after listing. The company has said it currently has no plan for share buybacks after listing.

05

Bull factors

Order backlog and order growth

According to press reports, new orders rose from KRW 41.0 billion in 2023 to KRW 114.6 billion in 2025, and the backlog was KRW 129.3 billion at the end of the first half of 2026. A larger backlog improves visibility on future revenue.

The company aims to add about KRW 90 billion of orders in the fourth quarter, lifting the year-end backlog to roughly KRW 140 billion. If that target is met, the revenue base for 2027 and beyond would widen.

Commercial reference in ORC waste-heat power

DTS signed a commercial supply contract for a 1.5MW ORC waste-heat recovery power system in July 2026. The company said this reference should help ORC orders gain momentum. It is also pursuing 3–5MW product lines and overseas markets including Europe.

Using its existing EPC customer network to pursue follow-on CCUS and hydrogen projects is cited as a source of expansion potential.

Limited float and shareholder-return commitments

Immediately after listing, 2,250,787 shares, or 15.15% of listed shares, were tradable. The largest shareholder, the listed parent with a 32.03% stake, and a related party holding 12.20% are subject to 30-month holding requirements, and part of the financial investors' holdings is locked up for 6 to 12 months.

The parent committed with the exchange to a payout ratio of about 30% through 2029, and DTS said it would deliver similar shareholder returns. These factors bear on both float and shareholder returns.

06

Bear factors

Profit decline through the third quarter

Cumulative third-quarter 2026 revenue declined 15.0%, while operating profit and net income fell 55.2% and 54.9%, respectively. Profit fell much faster than revenue, which suggests weaker profitability is the main driver of the decline.

Whether this trend carries into full-year results can only be judged once fourth-quarter figures are available. The gap between company-cited first-half growth figures in the press and the disclosed numbers is also a variable in interpreting results.

Concentration in the Middle East and large projects

According to the company's listing materials, Middle East sales reached KRW 42.7 billion in the first half of 2026, or 54% of total revenue. The company attributed this to large UAE projects clustering in that period.

Because revenue moves sharply with the execution schedules of large projects, quarterly results are volatile. With overseas sales at 89% of 2025 revenue, the business is highly exposed to the timing of overseas projects.

New-business revenue begins only from 2027

The company has indicated that ORC revenue will begin in 2027 and AI data-center cooling revenue in 2029. The data-center cooling business is currently closer to the planning and sales stage than to signed contracts.

Until new-business revenue scales, results depend on the AFC and ACC structure, which made up 93.6% of 2025 revenue. Roughly KRW 6.0 billion of offering proceeds is allocated to new businesses.

07

Risk factors

Macro

Orders depend heavily on the energy investment cycle, particularly LNG facility expansion and power infrastructure spending. A slowdown in energy plant orders could affect progress toward new-order and backlog targets. With heavy exposure to Middle East and U.S. projects, regional investment decisions flow directly into results.

Governance

DTS listed as a subsidiary of a KOSDAQ-listed parent, a dual listing that was controversial during the process. The transaction followed the exchange's guidelines for dual listings, but it drew scrutiny during the listing.

Given the parent's large stake, the market may continue to assess how minority shareholder interests are balanced.

Supply and demand

Only 15.15% of listed shares were immediately tradable, so price formation takes place on a thin float. Some financial investors' shares are scheduled to be released from lock-up in stages over 6 to 12 months, leaving the timing of share supply as a variable in market flows. Trading value on the morning of November 14, 2026 was KRW 2.7 billion.

08

Overall view

DTS is trading on KOSDAQ after cumulative third-quarter 2026 revenue and profit both declined. Revenue and operating profit rising from 2023 through 2025, along with a backlog reported at KRW 129.3 billion, point to an established business base.

On the other side, the 55.2% decline in cumulative operating profit through the third quarter is the most visible weakness. High dependence on the Middle East and on project timing increases quarterly volatility.

New businesses such as ORC and AI data-center cooling are slated to produce revenue from 2027 and 2029, so their near-term contribution to results is limited. The limited float and the parent's dividend commitment are factors on the supply and shareholder-return side.

The current share price of 10,600 won is below the indicated offering range. This view is a qualitative summary provided for information only.
