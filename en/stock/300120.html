Report overview

Sales Recover and Losses Narrow; Balance Sheet Still Strained

LaonPeople's sales have grown for two straight years since 2023 and its quarterly operating losses narrowed through Q3 2026, but a 345.8% debt ratio and four straight years of net losses attributable to controlling shareholders remain the key checkpoints.

  1. 1

    Sales recovery: 2025 revenue was KRW 223.8 billion, up 1.6% year on year, and has risen for two consecutive years from 2024 to 2025.

  2. 2

    Profitability: 2025 operating loss was KRW 16.2 billion (operating margin -7.2%), with operating losses for three consecutive years from 2023 and net losses attributable to controlling shareholders for four consecutive years from 2022.

  3. 3

    Quarterly trend: operating profit of KRW 2.41 billion in Q4 2025 turned positive, but Q1 to Q3 2026 were three consecutive quarters of operating losses, narrowing to KRW 495 million in Q2 and KRW 171 million in Q3 2026.

  4. 4

    Growth drivers: golf sensor revenue for the first nine months of 2026 reached KRW 4.18 billion, up about 380% year on year, while generative AI platform revenue also rose sharply.

  5. 5

    Balance sheet: the debt ratio at end-2025 was 345.8%, with total liabilities of KRW 152.2 billion exceeding total equity of KRW 44 billion. A KRW 19.7 billion rights offering in February 2026 funded the repayment of the second convertible bond.

02

Business structure

LaonPeople is an AI vision solutions specialist founded in 2010, centered on AI machine vision and smart vision solutions for quality inspection in manufacturing. The company acquired a stake in TDG in 2023 and now holds three subsidiaries.

Its cloud business is handled by subsidiary TDG, which accounts for more than 90% of total revenue according to a March 2026 report.

The parent company supplies the generative AI platforms HI FENN and ODIN AI to public institutions and enterprises, and the company said revenue from both platforms rose 816% and 920% respectively year on year in the first nine months of 2026.

Golf sensor revenue for the same period reached KRW 4.18 billion, up about 380% year on year, driven by overseas supply in North America and Australia. Government projects include a KRW 70.2 billion physical AI foundation model R&D program and the K-Display AX demonstration industrial complex project.

Vision inspection customers have expanded to secondary battery, mobile device and semiconductor manufacturers. Golf sensors are exported mainly to the Americas and Europe through official resellers.

Compared with large IT services players such as Samsung SDS and Hyundai Autoever, LaonPeople is a much smaller specialist.

04

Earnings analysis

Annual revenue fell 57.6% from KRW 26.5 billion in 2022 to KRW 11.2 billion in 2023, then rose to KRW 220.2 billion in 2024, an increase of 1,858.5%. 2025 revenue was KRW 223.8 billion, up 1.6%, marking two consecutive years of growth from the 2023 low, and 2025 was the largest year in the 2022 to 2025 period.

The consolidation of subsidiary TDG into group financial statements is cited as a factor behind the scale increase. Operating profit of KRW 2.39 billion in 2022 turned into an operating loss of KRW 11.1 billion in 2023, followed by losses of KRW 16.2 billion in both 2024 and 2025.

The 2025 operating loss was about 0.4% smaller than in 2024 on a consolidated basis, so the loss size was largely unchanged. Operating margin moved from 9.0% in 2022 to -99.0% in 2023, -7.4% in 2024 and -7.2% in 2025.

Net loss attributable to controlling shareholders was KRW 450 million in 2022, KRW 8.18 billion in 2023, KRW 12.3 billion in 2024 and KRW 17.3 billion in 2025, four consecutive years of losses.

Net loss including non-controlling interests was KRW 20.4 billion in 2025, a gap that a March 2026 report attributed to derivative valuation losses tied to a shareholder agreement.

Operating cash flow was KRW 2.08 billion in 2022, negative KRW 7.35 billion in 2023, KRW 378 million in 2024 and negative KRW 10.6 billion in 2025.

Quarterly revenue went from KRW 19.9 billion in Q3 2025 to KRW 95 billion in Q4 2025, up 377.5% quarter on quarter, with operating profit of KRW 2.41 billion in Q4 after an operating loss of KRW 6.22 billion in Q3 2025.

A company official attributed the Q4 2025 profit partly to about KRW 2 billion in personnel cost subsidies from government projects.

Q1 2026 revenue was KRW 39.9 billion with an operating loss of KRW 2.69 billion, Q2 revenue was KRW 64.1 billion with an operating loss of KRW 495 million, and Q3 revenue was KRW 29.4 billion with an operating loss of KRW 171 million.

Net loss attributable to controlling shareholders over the last four quarters totaled KRW 1.44 billion. A separate-basis preliminary Q3 2026 operating profit of KRW 1.18 billion was announced on October 21, 2026, which differs from the consolidated figures and should be read separately.

05

Industry analysis

Demand in the AI vision market LaonPeople serves splits into manufacturing inspection and video surveillance. Vision inspection uses AI to detect defects, an area where the company says it has long shown strength.

Beyond object-recognition technology, vision language models and AI agents have drawn attention, and the company says it already holds VLM and AI agent technology. Expanded government AX investment is leading to national R&D project awards.

The company reported in July 2026 that first-half new orders were KRW 10 billion, up about 85% from KRW 5.4 billion a year earlier. Golf sensor demand is growing in North America and Australia, but hardware demand can shift with distribution channels and overseas economic conditions.

Growth in the generative AI platform business will depend on how many public and enterprise deployment references accumulate. Among peers, large IT services companies such as Samsung SDS and Hyundai Autoever lead in revenue scale and cloud infrastructure capability.

LaonPeople emphasizes its vision AI specialization and government project track record. TDG's cloud MSP business competes with large cloud providers and is worth reading separately for profitability. Capital spending trends in secondary battery and semiconductor equipment are a variable for vision inspection orders.

06

Outlook

In its March 2026 annual shareholder meeting, the company said golf sensor volume through official resellers would start in earnest in the second quarter and could grow more than fivefold year on year. When it reported Q3 2026 results, it said it aimed to keep the profit trend going in Q4.

In March 2026 it said its ODIN AI product achieved GS Certification Class 1, and in the same month it announced golf sensor supply agreements in North America and Canada. In February 2026 it announced a contract to build an AI agent platform for global semiconductor company PSK.

New orders have increased since Q1 2026, and the KRW 10 billion in first-half new orders can be read as a leading indicator for second-half revenue. In June 2026 the CTO and CFO bought 94,000 shares on the open market, according to the company.

On the financial side, the KRW 19.7 billion rights offering in February 2026 funded a partial repayment of convertible bonds, reducing debt. Debt maturities and cash flow remain items to watch.

The 2026 annual business report, the next regular filing, will fix full-year sales, operating results and the debt ratio, and is due by the end of March 2027. The Q4 2026 results have not yet been filed in a regular report.

Whether golf sensor supply in North America and Australia and revenue from national R&D projects continue is the key operational checkpoint.

08

Bull factors

Narrowing quarterly losses

Operating profit of KRW 2.41 billion in Q4 2025 turned positive, and net profit attributable to controlling shareholders was KRW 520 million in Q2 2026. The Q3 2026 operating loss of KRW 171 million was smaller than the KRW 495 million loss in Q2. Whether the quarterly loss narrowing continues is the core of the bullish case.

High growth in golf sensors and generative AI

Golf sensor revenue for the first nine months of 2026 reached KRW 4.18 billion, up about 380% year on year. The company said revenue from the HI FENN and ODIN AI generative AI platforms rose 816% and 920% year on year. A larger share from these businesses could change the overall earnings structure.

Government project track record

The company won a KRW 70.2 billion national physical AI foundation model R&D project, which reduces R&D cost burden while advancing its technology. References from projects such as the K-Display AX demonstration complex and AI coastal control systems are also accumulating. First-half 2026 new orders reached KRW 10 billion, up about 85% year on year.

09

Bear factors

Prolonged losses and continued quarterly operating losses

The operating margin in 2025 was -7.2%, with an operating loss of KRW 16.2 billion. Net losses attributable to controlling shareholders have continued for four years from 2022 to 2025.

Operating losses have run for three consecutive quarters from Q1 to Q3 2026, so profitability has only been confirmed on a quarterly basis.

Debt burden and cash outflow

The debt ratio rose from 60.8% in 2022 to 345.8% in 2025. Total liabilities at end-2025 were KRW 152.2 billion, well above total equity of KRW 44 billion. Operating cash flow in 2025 was negative KRW 10.6 billion, and although the February 2026 rights offering repaid convertible bonds, borrowing maturities remain.

Weaker control and reliance on a subsidiary

The combined stake of the CEO and related parties fell from 49.11% to 31.47% through the rights offering. The cloud business, which accounts for more than 90% of revenue, depends on subsidiary TDG, so its performance drives the group. The Q4 2025 profit was partly supported by government project personnel subsidies.

10

Risk factors

Financial and liquidity

The debt ratio of 345.8% is high, and operating cash flow turned negative in 2025. Overlapping borrowing maturities and convertible bond repayments could require additional funding. Further rights offerings could dilute existing shareholders, making the financing method a key checkpoint.

Listing maintenance requirements

A March 2026 report said the KOSDAQ market capitalization delisting threshold rose from KRW 15 billion to KRW 20 billion on July 1, 2026, and is scheduled to rise to KRW 30 billion in January 2027. Current market capitalization of KRW 25 billion is below the January 2027 threshold. Compliance will depend on share price and changes in shares outstanding.

Business concentration and overseas dependence

Golf sensors depend on exports to the Americas and Europe and on official reseller channels. Generative AI platform revenue depends on winning public and enterprise customers, and government project revenue depends on project schedules. Headcount reductions made during cost cutting could also strain capacity to deliver new projects.
