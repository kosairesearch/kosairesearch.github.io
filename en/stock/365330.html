02

Business structure

SY Steel Tech was founded in 2015 and listed on KOSDAQ in 2023.

Its core business is deck plates used for floor slabs in apartments, logistics centers and knowledge industry centers, supplied as TG DECK (standard), GREEN DECK (removable form type) and HEAT DECK (insulation-integrated), with revenue recorded from products, construction works and merchandise.

The company promotes its long-span DEEP DECK system as a way to reduce floor height and construction cost, and says it has built a track record in knowledge industry centers, data centers and basement parking lots of large apartment complexes.

In April 2024 it signed an investment agreement with a floor-impact soundproofing maker, acquiring a stake and taking exclusive supply and installation rights, and soundproofing revenue was KRW 1.1 billion in 2024.

Solar power is being expanded into EPC (engineering, procurement and construction) business through a subsidiary, leveraging the technology of its parent company. Customers include LH housing projects and private builders, with cooperation cited with several major construction firms.

Segment revenue shares are not given as figures, so the picture is that deck plates remain the core business while soundproofing and solar are in an early stage.

Recent orders include a deck-plate contract for a new building at The Hyundai Gwangju running from July 1, 2026 to May 21, 2029 (KRW 12.96 billion), and a subcontract for the Seosomun Building redevelopment with Samsung C&T running from November 9, 2026 to October 31, 2029 (KRW 9.47 billion).

Both are long projects spanning several years, so revenue is likely to be recognized across multiple quarters. On competition, deck-plate bidding has reportedly intensified during the construction slowdown, so whether order pricing translates into profit is the key question.

04

Earnings analysis

Annual revenue rose from KRW 100.1 billion in 2022 to KRW 119.8 billion in 2023, the largest year in the 2022 to 2025 period, and then declined for two consecutive years to KRW 117 billion in 2024 (down 2.3%) and KRW 97.4 billion in 2025 (down 16.8%), the smallest year in the period.

Operating profit was KRW 8.33 billion in 2022 and KRW 14.6 billion in 2023 (up 75.2%), then fell to KRW 8.1 billion in 2024 (down 44.5%) before the company posted an operating loss of KRW 2.67 billion in 2025. The operating margin moved from 8.3% in 2022 to 12.2% in 2023, 6.9% in 2024 and -2.7% in 2025.

Net profit attributable to controlling shareholders followed the same path, reaching KRW 10.8 billion in 2023 (up 82.4%) and KRW 6.09 billion in 2024 (down 43.7%), before turning into a net loss of KRW 2.1 billion in 2025. Net income, which includes non-controlling interests, was a net loss of KRW 2.1 billion in 2025.

The company attributed the 2025 deterioration to intensifying competition and lower selling prices, while a brokerage analyst cited falling deck-plate prices and rising construction wages as the cause of the 2024 margin squeeze.

Operating cash flow went from KRW 7.22 billion in 2022 and KRW 11.8 billion in 2023 to KRW -221 million in 2024 and KRW -9.89 billion in 2025.

On a quarterly basis, revenue of KRW 18.9 billion and an operating loss of KRW 218 million in Q3 2025 were followed by revenue of KRW 17.5 billion and an operating loss of KRW 589 million in Q1 2026.

Revenue then jumped to KRW 37.6 billion in Q2 2026 (up 115.6% from the prior quarter), but the operating loss widened to KRW 2.65 billion.

In Q3 2026 revenue reached KRW 39.6 billion (up 109.0% year on year), the operating loss narrowed to KRW 638 million, and net profit attributable to controlling shareholders turned positive at KRW 1.82 billion.

The quarterly table has no Q4 2025 entry, so the cumulative net loss attributable to controlling shareholders over the last four quarters, KRW 4.22 billion, is the reference point.

05

Industry analysis

Demand for deck plates is directly tied to domestic construction investment. Construction investment in Korea has trended down steadily since 2022, and construction payments in Q1 2025 fell 20.7% year on year, while construction orders fell 7.7% over the same period.

During this slowdown, bidding for deck-plate orders intensified, and a rise in low-priced contracts pushed down project-level margins, according to explanations reported at the time. The pattern matches the revenue decline and the swing to an annual loss in 2025.

In an August 2025 report, the company said it expected construction activity to recover from 2026. Large long-term orders have continued into 2026, but whether they signal a turn in the construction cycle can only be judged once quarterly revenue and the order backlog build up together.

On competition, SY Steel Tech positions itself as a specialist that bundles design, supply and installation at the site level. In soundproofing, the company says it holds all of the LH certified grades 1 to 3 for floor impact noise, which it presents as a qualification for public housing projects.

Market size and competitor share for soundproofing materials are not given in figures, so comparison remains at the level of business structure. Solar relies on energy demand drivers that are separate from construction activity.

06

Outlook

In its July 2025 investor briefing, the company said it plans to introduce dedicated equipment for its long-span deck at its Chungju No. 2 plant in 2026 and set up a dedicated team for the product.

It also plans to expand beam deck applications from floor slabs to structures such as columns, and to raise production capacity for its core deck-plate lines by expanding factories.

Of the KRW 37 billion facility funds from the 2025 rights offering, KRW 28 billion was earmarked for deck-plate and soundproofing capacity and KRW 9 billion for solar power equipment.

However, the 2024 investment plan of about KRW 18.2 billion was executed at only about KRW 1.9 billion, and about KRW 4.3 billion of offering proceeds remained at mid-2025. A second factory has been put on hold as the construction recovery was delayed.

In Gwangju, the company plans to set up a local subsidiary wholly owned by itself and then transfer the contract position of The Hyundai Gwangju project to it, with a correction disclosure to follow once client consultations and construction licensing are complete.

The Samsung C&T contract may also change in amount and period depending on project progress and negotiations, with correction disclosures required if so. Segment figures for the new businesses are not separated in the disclosed results, so how much solar and soundproofing will contribute to earnings is not yet visible.

The next checkpoints are therefore whether the Chungju No. 2 long-span equipment comes online by the end of 2026, and the operating margin in the 2026 annual report.

Bull factors

Long-term project orders secured

Disclosed orders include a deck-plate contract for The Hyundai Gwangju (KRW 12.96 billion) and a subcontract for the Seosomun Building redevelopment with Samsung C&T (KRW 9.47 billion). Both contract periods run into 2029, so revenue could be spread over several years. The Samsung C&T contract was voluntarily disclosed at 9.73% of consolidated revenue.

Revenue up for two straight quarters in 2026

Q2 2026 revenue of KRW 37.6 billion rose 115.6% from the prior quarter, and Q3 2026 revenue of KRW 39.6 billion rose a further 5.1%. Q3 revenue was up 109.0% year on year. Net profit attributable to controlling shareholders turned positive at KRW 1.82 billion in the same quarter. The operating loss narrowed to KRW 638 million from KRW 2.65 billion in Q2.

New product pipeline in soundproofing and long-span deck

The company says it holds all of the LH certified grades 1 to 3 for floor impact soundproofing products. Under its July 2025 investor briefing, long-span deck equipment is planned for the Chungju No. 2 plant in 2026.

Beam deck applications are planned to extend to column structures, and solar EPC work is being pursued through a subsidiary, drawing on the parent company's power generation experience.

Bear factors

Persistent operating losses

Operating losses ran for three straight quarters, at KRW 589 million in Q1 2026, KRW 2.65 billion in Q2 and KRW 638 million in Q3. The Q2 loss widened even though revenue rose to KRW 37.6 billion. The cumulative net loss attributable to controlling shareholders over the last four quarters is KRW 4.22 billion. Operating cash flow also turned negative at KRW -9.89 billion in 2025.

Construction slowdown and weaker demand

Domestic construction investment has trended lower since 2022, and construction payments fell 20.7% year on year in Q1 2025. Revenue peaked at KRW 119.8 billion in 2023 and then declined for two consecutive years, falling 16.8% in 2025. The company itself pointed to intensifying competition and lower selling prices as causes of the deterioration.

Pace of new-business investment

Against an investment plan of about KRW 18.2 billion for 2024, actual spending was about KRW 1.9 billion, and the second factory was put on hold. The 2025 capital raise of KRW 65.6 billion secured funding, but new businesses take time to generate profit. Soundproofing and solar contributions are not yet visible in the reported figures.

10

Risk factors

Construction cycle risk

Lower domestic construction investment and project delays can reduce deck-plate shipments. The company also cites seasonality, with winter and rainy-season delays in structural works reducing shipments. A decline in design and construction volume at sites would weaken demand itself.

Order pricing and margin risk

Reports cite low-priced contracts as pushing down project-level margins. Both the Samsung C&T and The Hyundai Gwangju contracts were disclosed as subject to change in amount and period depending on project progress and client negotiations. Even if contract revenue grows, rising costs could keep operating losses in place.

Financing and execution risk

Operating cash flow was negative in both 2024 at KRW -221 million and 2025 at KRW -9.89 billion. Capacity spending alongside solar and soundproofing projects could strain cash management. Analysts have also flagged the risk that margins could worsen if construction recovery lags behind capacity expansion.

Overall view

SY Steel Tech's reported results show revenue falling for two consecutive years after peaking in 2023, and a swing to an operating loss of KRW 2.67 billion and a net loss attributable to controlling shareholders of KRW 2.1 billion in 2025.

Revenue then rose in 2026 from KRW 17.5 billion in Q1 to KRW 37.6 billion in Q2 and KRW 39.6 billion in Q3, a sign of top-line recovery.

Over the same period, however, operating losses persisted for three straight quarters and widened in Q2, so whether higher revenue has translated into better profitability is not yet shown in the numbers.

The Q3 2026 swing to a KRW 1.82 billion net profit attributable to controlling shareholders is a positive change, but the last four quarters still total a net loss. Factors on the positive side include long-term orders from large projects, LH-certified soundproofing products and planned long-span deck equipment.

Factors on the negative side include the construction slowdown, low-priced contracts, the pace of new-business spending and negative operating cash flow. With no calculable P/E, the speed of earnings recovery is central to reading valuation.

The next checkpoints are the 2026 full-year preliminary results, the operating margin in the 2026 annual report and whether the Chungju No. 2 equipment comes online.
