02

Business structure

STX Engine runs three divisions: civilian engines for ships and power generation, special-purpose engines for K1 tanks and K9 self-propelled howitzers, and electronics and communications, including sonar systems.

As of the end of the third quarter of 2025, order backlog was KRW 495.1 billion in civilian, KRW 774.9 billion in special-purpose and KRW 212.8 billion in electronics and communications, according to press reports.

The special-purpose unit has localized engines for the K9 howitzer, and an August 2025 report described a Vietnamese government export contract for self-propelled howitzers as a foothold for overseas sales.

The electronics unit won sonar work for KDDX in 2020, and in November 2025 it signed a supply contract worth KRW 36.81 billion with Hanwha Ocean for towed-array and torpedo countermeasure sonar systems, running through December 31, 2030.

Civilian sales lean heavily on the HD Hyundai group, which accounted for about KRW 172 billion of supply contracts in 2025. Large-vessel engines are dominated by the HD Hyundai and Hanwha groups, so STX Engine has focused on mid-size vessels and special-purpose engines.

According to Meritz Securities, its naval-grade core product is a medium-speed diesel engine built under a German MTU license. Larger shipbuilders are moving to bring key equipment in-house, which raises questions about how long civilian orders will last.

In July 2026 the company signed a contract with the Chinese NTS shipyard covering engines for 19,000-TEU container ships, eight firm and six optional, which press reports described as its first large direct entry into overseas ship-engine business.

04

Earnings analysis

Annual sales rose from KRW 544 billion in 2022 to KRW 630.4 billion in 2023, KRW 724.6 billion in 2024 and KRW 789.3 billion in 2025, growing three years in a row from 2023 through 2025.

Growth slowed from 15.9% in 2023 and 14.9% in 2024 to 8.9% in 2025, and 2025 was the largest year of 2022 through 2025 while 2022 was the smallest.

Operating profit swung from an operating loss of KRW 8.82 billion in 2022 to KRW 18.9 billion in 2023, then rose to KRW 42.2 billion in 2024 (up 123.9%) and KRW 69.6 billion in 2025 (up 64.8%), a two-year run of increases.

Operating margin climbed from -1.6% in 2022 to 3.0%, 5.8% and 8.8% in 2025, so margin gains outpaced sales growth.

Net income attributable to controlling shareholders moved from a net loss of KRW 26.7 billion in 2022 to KRW 5.68 billion in 2023, then KRW 27.2 billion in 2024 and KRW 68.9 billion in 2025, also rising two years in a row.

Net income including non-controlling interests was reported as a net loss of KRW 26.7 billion in 2022 and KRW 68.9 billion in 2025.

The debt-to-equity ratio was 227.9% in 2022, 246.3% in 2023, 250.3% in 2024 and 237.5% in 2025, with total equity of KRW 343 billion and total liabilities of KRW 814.6 billion at the end of 2025.

Operating cash flow was KRW 26.6 billion in 2022, -KRW 13.3 billion in 2023, KRW 36.7 billion in 2024 and KRW 72.9 billion in 2025, exceeding operating profit in 2025.

Quarterly sales went from KRW 192.1 billion with operating profit of KRW 15.4 billion in the third quarter of 2025 to KRW 244.5 billion in the fourth quarter, while operating profit fell to KRW 5.37 billion.

In the first quarter of 2026 sales were KRW 189 billion and operating profit KRW 17.5 billion, and in the second quarter sales were KRW 213 billion and operating profit KRW 25 billion, the highest of the last five quarters on the disclosed basis.

Third-quarter 2026 sales were KRW 211.8 billion, down 0.6% from the prior quarter and up 10.3% year on year, but operating profit fell to KRW 13.9 billion, down 44.5% quarter on quarter and 10.2% year on year.

Third-quarter net income attributable to controlling shareholders was KRW 12.4 billion, down 11.9% year on year, and the last four quarters total KRW 65.8 billion.

05

Industry analysis

In a March 2026 report, Meritz Securities said that because orders lead sales by about two years, the large order wave at Korean shipyards in 2026 and 2027 is likely to feed into naval engine sales.

The same report estimated naval engine sales would roughly double from KRW 275.8 billion in 2026 to KRW 531.4 billion in 2028, with company-wide sales expanding from KRW 976.6 billion to KRW 1.3156 trillion.

Cited growth drivers include global demand for Korean naval vessels and artillery modernization projects in Spain and Middle Eastern countries. On the demand side, an April 2026 report said US data center power needs and a shortage of gas turbines had pushed medium-speed marine engines into view as an alternative.

Korea Investment & Securities said at the time that capacity expansion in medium-speed engines could raise its 2028 earnings estimates. In competition, HD Hyundai and Hanwha dominate large commercial-vessel engines, while STX Engine holds its own niche in naval engines and land defense special-purpose engines.

In civilian markets, in-house production by large shipbuilders is a variable, and some observers note that auxiliary engines have lower technical barriers and are easier for group companies to internalize.

Order momentum is now entering the phase of conversion to sales, but the civilian backlog of KRW 495.1 billion at the end of the third quarter of 2025 was smaller than the special-purpose backlog of KRW 774.9 billion, so the business mix leans toward defense.

06

Outlook

Reports put the order backlog at KRW 1.9269 trillion at the end of June 2026, up 27.4% from KRW 1.5125 trillion a year earlier.

A March 25, 2026 disclosure covered two supply contracts with Hanwha Aerospace, for export engines for defense projects and high-altitude engine assemblies for export to India, totaling about KRW 318 billion.

The NTS contract signed on July 30, 2026 comprises eight firm ships and six options, so whether the options are exercised will change the scale of ship-engine sales.

On defense, after the August 2025 Vietnamese self-propelled howitzer export contract, reports said supply of diesel engines for Australian Redback armored vehicles would proceed in stages through 2027, including a technology transfer package.

The electronics unit's sonar contract with Hanwha Ocean runs through December 31, 2030, which underpins medium-term sales. On capacity, Korea Investment & Securities said that medium-speed engine expansion could shape its 2028 earnings estimates.

If Meritz's estimate of naval engine sales reaching KRW 531.4 billion in 2028 is realized, naval engines would represent a larger share of company sales. Civilian sales are expected to grow only modestly by most estimates.

The company has described its main civilian and defense engines as delivered two to three years after orders, so there is a lag between order intake and reported revenue.

The near-term question is whether the third-quarter 2026 pattern of higher sales and lower operating profit persists into the fourth quarter and beyond.

Bull factors

Naval engine sales expected to expand

Meritz Securities estimated in a March 2026 report that naval engine sales could roughly double from KRW 275.8 billion in 2026 to KRW 531.4 billion in 2028. The same report put company-wide sales at KRW 976.6 billion in 2026 and KRW 1.3156 trillion in 2028. The firm classified the company as its top pick among shipbuilding equipment suppliers.

Backlog and overseas contracts widen

The order backlog is larger than a year earlier, which means more volume waiting to be booked as revenue, and the pace of recognition depends on delivery schedules. In March 2026, two export engine contracts with Hanwha Aerospace totaling about KRW 318 billion were disclosed. The July 2026 contract with the NTS shipyard was described as a first major win in overseas ship engines.

Operating margin improvement trend

Operating margin rose from -1.6% in 2022 to 8.8% in 2025. Analysts have credited growing defense exports, which are not subject to margin caps, with helping lift profitability. Net income attributable to controlling shareholders reached KRW 68.9 billion in 2025, up 153.3% year on year.

09

Bear factors

Third-quarter operating profit decline

Third-quarter 2026 sales rose 10.3% year on year to KRW 211.8 billion, but operating profit fell 10.2% to KRW 13.9 billion. Net income attributable to controlling shareholders was KRW 12.4 billion, down 11.9% year on year. Quarter-to-quarter swings in operating profit are wide, so more quarters are needed to confirm the trend.

Largest shareholder stake and possible sale

The largest shareholder, a turnaround PEF, held 61.68% of common shares as of October 2026. It has sold stakes over time through off-market block trades, and additional block sales were discussed during a sharp share price rise. No timetable for a full sale has been announced, so float and supply remain variables.

Financial and legal burdens

The debt-to-equity ratio was 237.5% at the end of 2025, with total liabilities of KRW 814.6 billion. The company lost its court challenge to a two-year public bidding restriction imposed by KHNP, so it cannot bid for public-sector contracts during that period.

A Chinese affiliate is in a court-supervised restructuring process, and STX Engine guaranteed a KRW 292.3 billion loan taken out in 2009.

10

Risk factors

Ownership and supply

The largest shareholder's high stake leaves a limited free float. Block trades would add tradable shares and could affect institutional flows. A full sale of control could change the business direction depending on the buyer.

Legal and guarantees

KHNP's bidding restriction bars public-sector bids for two years and could limit public contract wins. The Chinese affiliate's restructuring and the guarantee exposure are part of the balance sheet review. Court and restructuring developments can be tracked through disclosures.

Dependence on civilian orders

A large share of civilian orders comes from the HD Hyundai group, which signed about KRW 172 billion of contracts in 2025. If large shipbuilders expand in-house equipment production, the continuity of civilian orders could weaken. Civilian engine orders are cited as the key to further earnings growth.

Overall view

STX Engine has moved from an operating loss in 2022 to KRW 69.6 billion of operating profit in 2025. Operating margin rose from -1.6% to 8.8%, and net income attributable to controlling shareholders grew in both 2024 and 2025.

Naval engines, defense exports and an order backlog of KRW 1.9269 trillion at the end of June 2026 are cited as sources of medium-term sales visibility.

On the other side, third-quarter 2026 sales rose but operating profit fell year on year, and civilian operations face dependence on the HD Hyundai group and in-house production by shipbuilders.

The debt-to-equity ratio of 237.5%, guarantees tied to a Chinese affiliate and the KHNP bidding restriction remain items to monitor on the financial and legal side. The largest shareholder's 61.68% stake and the possibility of a sale are variables for float and supply.

The next checkpoints are the full-year 2026 operating margin in the annual report and first-quarter 2027 results, with the key question being whether naval engine sales projections translate into reported revenue.
