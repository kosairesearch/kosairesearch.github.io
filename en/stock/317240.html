Channel Reset Lifts Profit; New Businesses Still Unproven

TS Trillion's quarterly operating profit recovered in 2026 after a channel reshuffle, but revenue has fallen for three straight years, shareholder net losses persist, and its AI data center project has not yet broken ground.

02

Business structure

TS Trillion's core business is hair-care products, including shampoos marketed for relief of hair-loss symptoms. Home-shopping and military-supply channels, long its main outlets, shrank 31.4% and 27.5% year on year in the first half of 2026.

General retail accounts, including Olive Young (listed in November 2025) and Daiso (listed in January 2026), grew 71.7%, while its own mall, Smartstore, and overseas sales rose 27.4%, 67.0%, and 21.0%, filling the gap left by the declines.

The company says its teen shampoo line is now about 25% of domestic sales and has a repurchase rate of roughly 95%, above the 78 to 80% for its traditional hair-loss shampoos. The company reported that its Amazon Prime Day sales in the United States rose about 500% year on year in 2025.

In China, the subsidiary stopped operating directly and shifted to a local distributor model. Its subsidiary TS CORE is pursuing AI data center and energy infrastructure projects. Demand tailwinds include wider awareness of hair-loss prevention and a shift toward online and multi-brand retail.

Compared with large cosmetics groups such as Amorepacific and LG H&H, the company trails meaningfully in product breadth and distribution bargaining power.

04

Earnings analysis

Annual revenue fell from KRW 62.9 billion in 2022 to KRW 40 billion in 2023 (down 36.5% year on year), KRW 30.8 billion in 2024 (down 23.1%), and KRW 26 billion in 2025 (down 15.4%), a decline for three consecutive years; 2022 was the largest year in the period and 2025 the smallest.

Operating profit moved from an operating loss of KRW 5.81 billion in 2022 to operating profit of KRW 1.23 billion in 2023, a turnaround to profit, then fell to KRW 381 million in 2024 (down 68.9%) and KRW 25.67 million in 2025 (down 93.3%), a decline for two consecutive years.

For 2025, the company said in its filing that the wind-down of its Chinese subsidiary's direct operations and the move to a distributor model affected consolidated sales and profit.

Net loss attributable to controlling shareholders was KRW 9.98 billion in 2022, KRW 1.59 billion in 2023, KRW 4.24 billion in 2024, and KRW 2.01 billion in 2025, a loss in each of four consecutive years.

The 2024 net loss including non-controlling interests (KRW 4.99 billion) was larger than the controlling-shareholder loss (KRW 4.24 billion), so the non-controlling share of losses was also reflected. Operating margin went from -9.2% in 2022 to 3.1% in 2023, then 1.2% in 2024 and 0.1% in 2025, near break-even.

Operating cash flow was -KRW 3.54 billion in 2022, KRW 1.04 billion in 2023, -KRW 1.2 billion in 2024, and -KRW 485 million in 2025.

The debt ratio fell from 291.9% in 2022 to 92.3% in 2025, with total liabilities of KRW 26.8 billion and total equity of KRW 29 billion at year-end 2025, all attributable to controlling shareholders.

Quarterly revenue was KRW 7.3 billion in the third quarter of 2025, KRW 6.06 billion in the fourth quarter, KRW 10.1 billion in the first quarter of 2026, KRW 7.9 billion in the second quarter, and KRW 8.79 billion in the third quarter.

Operating profit was KRW 184 million, KRW 125 million, KRW 1.11 billion, KRW 290 million, and KRW 1.07 billion over the same quarters.

Net income attributable to controlling shareholders was a loss of KRW 246 million, a loss of KRW 467 million, a profit of KRW 922 million, a loss of KRW 131 million, and a loss of KRW 205 million.

Losses have occurred in two consecutive quarters since the second quarter of 2026, and the sum of the last four quarters is KRW 119 million.

Preliminary first-half 2026 figures (standalone basis, announced August 4, 2026) show revenue of KRW 1.514 billion (up 18%), operating profit of KRW 1.18 billion, and net income of KRW 636 million. These preliminary figures are on a standalone basis and are not summed with the consolidated quarterly figures.

05

Industry analysis

Cosmetics is generally treated as an essential consumer category with relatively low demand volatility, but prolonged economic weakness has pressured sector earnings.

Consumer-goods listed companies broadly face weak domestic spending, and industry observers viewed TS Trillion's first-half 2026 gains as the result of structural change rather than a one-off rebound.

Wider awareness of hair-loss prevention and the growth of online and multi-brand retail channels expand the demand base. On the distribution side, the company says wider presence at Olive Young, Daiso, and Emart Traders drove general-account sales higher.

Reliance on a few large retail partners, however, ties the company's bargaining power to those relationships. In China, the company restructured its local operating model and appointed a new global distribution agent to revive sales.

Its product strategy is to move from a hair-loss shampoo base toward teen and women's lines. A single-category shampoo model has less product breadth and marketing scale than diversified groups such as Amorepacific and LG H&H.

An industry official said that if the first-half profit surge carries into full-year results, overseas expansion and new businesses in the second half could add momentum.

06

Outlook

The Cheonan AI data center joint development is reported at a total project cost of KRW 2.3 trillion, of which KRW 2 trillion is to be funded through project financing (PF) and KRW 300 billion from equity.

The site has received construction and development permits, and a 80 MW electricity supply contract has been signed. The project unit is advancing design, construction structure, and PF procedures for a first phase targeting groundbreaking on a 40 MW data center.

The company intends to diversify its portfolio into AI data center development and infrastructure around this project. TS CORE's results were described in May 2026 reporting as still in an early stage, with only part of its contribution reflected.

On the retail side, the company is preparing for a Walmart listing in Canada and has completed product development and approval for a hair color product. The company expects second-half 2026 global expansion and new business momentum to add to its results.

Timing for groundbreaking and PF closing has not been confirmed in filings. Whether the subsidiary records revenue from the project will be checked in upcoming periodic reports.

08

Bull factors

Operating profit recovery after channel reshuffle

Operating profit reached KRW 1.11 billion in the first quarter of 2026 and KRW 1.07 billion in the third quarter, based on filed figures. Preliminary first-half 2026 operating profit was KRW 1.18 billion, up 1,511% year on year, according to the company's August 4, 2026 announcement.

The company attributes the gain to shifting away from high-fee home-shopping and military-supply channels toward online and general retail accounts.

Repeat purchase trend in teen shampoo

According to the company, the repurchase rate for its teen shampoo is about 95%, above the 78 to 80% for its traditional hair-loss shampoos. Teen shampoo accounts for roughly 25% of domestic sales. Moving beyond a hair-loss core could reduce dependence on one product group. The repurchase figure is a company-compiled number and has not been independently verified.

Balance sheet improvement and a platform for new businesses

The debt ratio fell from 291.9% in 2022 to 92.3% in 2025. In 2025 the company said all litigation tied to its control dispute had been withdrawn and that it raised KRW 6 billion through a third-party rights offering. The proceeds were earmarked for new businesses and marketing.

09

Bear factors

Shrinking revenue and four years of losses

Revenue fell from KRW 62.9 billion in 2022 to KRW 26 billion in 2025, a decline for three consecutive years. Net losses attributable to controlling shareholders persisted for four consecutive years from 2022 through 2025. Losses returned in the second and third quarters of 2026. Despite the operating profit recovery, bottom-line profit has not followed.

Thin earnings base and quarterly volatility

Shareholder net income over the last four quarters totals only KRW 119 million. After a KRW 922 million profit in the first quarter of 2026, net losses of KRW 131 million and KRW 205 million followed in the second and third quarters.

Operating profit improved in some quarters without flowing through to shareholder net income, which suggests the earnings base is not yet stable.

Governance and unproven new businesses

In 2025 the majority shareholder changed to a newly formed company, which a news outlet reported was effectively a shell company. The company had also explored electric-vehicle battery businesses that did not materialize. The execution capacity of the AI data center plan has therefore not yet been demonstrated.

10

Risk factors

Funding risk for the business pivot

The Cheonan data center depends on roughly KRW 2 trillion of project financing out of a KRW 2.3 trillion total cost. The KRW 300 billion equity portion is far larger than year-end 2025 total equity of KRW 29 billion. Failure to close external financing could delay the schedule.

Channel concentration and weak consumer demand

Growth in general-retail sales relies on listings at a small number of large retailers such as Olive Young and Daiso. New channels have filled the gap left by home shopping and military supply, so slower sales at those partners could affect total revenue. The risk grows if domestic consumer weakness persists.

Overseas and new product launch delay

The Walmart listing in Canada is still ahead, and the hair color product has completed development and approval, but its launch timing is not fixed. Because the China distribution structure has been reorganized and a new global agent appointed, revitalizing sales may take longer.

If the listing and launch slip or do not convert into actual sales, expectations for second-half global expansion may not show up in results.

Overall view

TS Trillion's revenue fell for three consecutive years from 2022 through 2025, and shareholder net losses persisted for four years. In 2026 the company entered a turnaround phase in which channel reshuffling has lifted operating profit.

Full-year 2025 operating profit was only KRW 25.67 million, while filed operating profit was KRW 1.11 billion in the first quarter of 2026 and KRW 1.07 billion in the third quarter.

However, shareholder net losses continued in the second and third quarters of 2026, so whether operating gains carry through to net income is not yet clear. The debt ratio fell to 92.3% at year-end 2025 from 2022, but operating cash flow was still negative in 2025.

The AI data center plan is reported at KRW 2.3 trillion but still awaits PF financing and groundbreaking, and the change in majority shareholder and past business history remain items to verify.

Bullish factors are the earnings mix shift from channel reshuffling and the repurchase trend in teen shampoo; bearish factors are shrinking revenue, a thin earnings base, and the funding structure of the new business.

The next checkpoints are the preliminary full-year 2026 results (around early February 2027) and the 2026 annual business report (filing deadline March 31, 2027). This report does not present an investment opinion, target price, or buy or sell view.
