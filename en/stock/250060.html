Report overview

Fusion-Tech Promise Against Four Straight Loss Years

Mobiis holds rare domestic technology in the ITER and accelerator control fields, but it has posted operating losses for four straight years from 2022 through 2025, and a turn to profitability has not yet been confirmed.

02

Business structure

Mobiis was founded in 2000, centered on researchers from Samsung Electronics' network division and multimedia laboratory, and listed on KOSDAQ in 2017.

Its business is organized around three pillars: EPICS-based ultra-precision control systems for accelerators and fusion reactors, smart factory solutions, and a machine-learning platform.

The smart factory segment supplies a turnkey control package called 'QRP,' an all-in-one MES built on ISO/TS16949 and IATF standards, and has built a reference base of roughly 450 client companies including suppliers affiliated with POSCO and the Hyundai Motor Group.

The accelerator and fusion segment, built on domestically unique EPICS-based ultra-precision control design technology, supplied LLRF systems, undulator precision control systems and BPMs to the Pohang Accelerator Laboratory's fourth-generation X-ray free-electron laser (PAL-XFEL).

In the fusion field, the company participates in the ITER project, supplying EPICS-based central control, interlock control, and power-supply precision control systems, and has been described as involved in five of the project's six control-system sections.

It has also previously won a contract, through a consortium with the Korea Institute of Fusion Energy (KFE), to design and build high-voltage signal-processing systems for ITER superconducting-coil quench detection.

As of the third quarter of 2025, revenue mix was reported at 63.7% smart factory and 36.3% fusion and accelerators, making smart factory the larger pillar.

Because the business model recognizes revenue on a project basis tied to contract execution, inspection and cash collection timing, quarterly results are structurally volatile.

In December 2025 the company disclosed a contract under which an asset management firm would acquire controlling shareholder Kim Ji-heon's stake, but the contract was later cancelled and the disclosure reversed, exposing uncertainty around control of the company.

04

Earnings analysis

Annual revenue fell sharply from KRW 19.7 billion in 2022 to KRW 19.1 billion in 2023 (down 3.0% year on year) and KRW 5.03 billion in 2024 (down 73.7% year on year), before edging up to KRW 5.1 billion in 2025 (up 1.4% year on year).

Operating losses moved from KRW 4.01 billion in 2022 to KRW 6.52 billion in 2023 (losses continued and widened), then narrowed to KRW 2.39 billion in 2024 (losses continued but narrowed), before widening again to KRW 2.94 billion in 2025 (losses continued and widened).

The company posted operating losses for four consecutive years from 2022 through 2025, and the operating margin deteriorated steadily from -20.4% in 2022 to -57.6% in 2025.

Net profit attributable to controlling shareholders went from a loss of KRW 5.76 billion in 2022 to a loss of KRW 385 million in 2023 (losses continued but narrowed), then swung to a profit of KRW 9.22 billion in 2024, before reverting to a loss of KRW 1.05 billion in 2025 (a swing back to loss).

The 2024 swing to profit occurred even as revenue collapsed 73.7%, suggesting non-operating factors played a significant role.

On a quarterly basis, the company logged operating losses for five consecutive quarters from Q2 2025 through Q2 2026, while revenue peaked at KRW 1.26 billion in Q4 2025 before declining for two straight quarters, in Q1 2026 (down 17.6%) and Q2 2026 (down 61.5%).

Net profit attributable to controlling shareholders briefly turned positive at KRW 1.01 billion in Q3 2025, but reverted to losses for three straight quarters from Q4 2025 through Q2 2026, with the trailing four quarters (Q3 2025 through Q2 2026) showing a combined net loss attributable to controlling shareholders of KRW 1.61 billion.

According to WiseReport data, Q1 2026 standalone revenue fell 16.3% year on year while the operating loss narrowed 9.7% year on year, indicating that while the loss size itself shrank versus prior periods, the revenue base was also thinning out at the same time.

05

Industry analysis

The accelerator and fusion control-system market in which Mobiis operates is a specialized market dependent on large national research facilities and internationally coordinated projects, characterized by order-taking on a project-by-project basis rather than the recurring-revenue pattern of general software markets.

ITER, a fusion experimental reactor being jointly built by seven countries including Korea, is a project in which Mobiis has been described as involved in five of six control-system sections.

Domestically, the company's core equipment, including LLRF systems, has had its performance verified through Pohang Accelerator Laboratory's fourth-generation X-ray free-electron laser (PAL-XFEL), giving it a relatively scarce supply-chain position in this niche.

However, the commercialization of fusion power remains a distant prospect, and even when experimental-reactor-stage project orders translate directly into revenue, the absolute scale remains limited.

The smart-factory MES market is comparatively more competitive, and while Mobiis has built a position on roughly 450 client references including suppliers affiliated with POSCO and the Hyundai Motor Group, its structure leans toward filling niches with solutions specialized to particular industrial processes rather than competing head-on with large IT services firms.

Within the KOSDAQ IT services category, most peer companies post operating profits, while Mobiis remains unable to generate a calculable price-to-earnings ratio due to its net losses, placing it in a different profitability phase from the sector average.

Taken together, Mobiis shows clear technological scarcity, but in terms of revenue scale and earnings stability it still appears to be at an early stage of its industry cycle.

06

Outlook

In July 2026, Mobiis disclosed a contract to build a manufacturing AI system for an autonomous smart factory with BorgWarner's Changnyeong limited liability entity, valued at KRW 420 million, equivalent to about 8.2% of the company's recent revenue of KRW 5.1 billion.

The contract runs for roughly nine months, from July 20, 2026 to April 30, 2027, suggesting revenue recognition will likely be spread across the second half of 2026 and the first half of 2027 if executed as planned.

The company has stated it has built a foundation for overseas expansion of its smart factory solutions, making the pace of supply expansion abroad, built on its domestic reference base, a key factor for future revenue diversification.

In the fusion and accelerator segment, participation in the ITER project continues, but given the nature of experimental-reactor-stage projects, the timing and scale of new orders remain difficult to predict.

On governance, after the December 2025 controlling-shareholder change contract was reversed, the controlling shareholder as of late January 2026 was confirmed again as Kim Ji-heon and five related parties with a 32.81% stake, and related unfaithful-disclosure designation procedures took place, making it worth continuing to watch for further changes in ownership or control-related disclosures.

While the pace of revenue decline in Q1 2026 narrowed somewhat on a year-on-year basis, revenue fell sharply again quarter on quarter in Q2 2026, so whether a trend reversal is underway remains unclear.

Whether the company secures new large-scale projects that could mark an inflection point in annual results, along with the execution rate of existing contracts, stands out as the key variable for upcoming quarterly results.

08

Bull factors

Scarce Technology Position in ITER and Accelerators

Mobiis holds the only domestically developed EPICS-based ultra-precision control design technology and has been described as participating in five of six ITER control-system sections.

Its track record of having core equipment such as LLRF systems verified at the fourth-generation PAL-XFEL accelerator gives it a supply-chain position in a field with few comparable domestic players.

If fusion power demonstration and commercialization progress, this design and verification experience could serve as a basis for winning new projects.

Broad Reference Base in Smart Factory Solutions

The smart factory solution 'QRP' has a reference base of roughly 450 client companies, including suppliers affiliated with POSCO and the Hyundai Motor Group, and was the largest business segment at 63.7% of total revenue as of Q3 2025.

Its standardized all-in-one MES package built on ISO/TS16949 and IATF standards is cited as a factor that could lower certification-related barriers when expanding overseas. In July 2026 the company secured an additional manufacturing AI system contract with BorgWarner's Changnyeong plant.

Past Profit Swings Illustrate Earnings Variability

In 2024, net profit attributable to controlling shareholders swung to a gain of KRW 9.22 billion even as revenue collapsed 73.7% year on year, and in Q3 2025 a single quarter briefly showed a net profit attributable to controlling shareholders of KRW 1.01 billion.

This illustrates how, under a project-based order-and-inspection structure, specific contract payment collection or one-off factors can swing earnings sharply over a short period.

If the inspection and revenue-recognition timing of new contracts aligns favorably, a renewed improvement in quarterly earnings cannot be ruled out.

Bear factors

Four Straight Years of Operating Losses and Worsening Margins

Mobiis posted operating losses for four consecutive years from 2022 through 2025, and its operating margin deteriorated steadily from -20.4% in 2022 to -57.6% in 2025. Operating cash flow was also negative every year from 2022 through 2025, reflecting a continued inability to generate cash from operations.

Operating losses have now extended to five consecutive quarters through Q2 2026, and no clear sign of a profitability turnaround has yet been confirmed.

The Revenue Base Itself Has Shrunk Significantly

Annual revenue fell 73.7% from KRW 19.7 billion in 2022 to KRW 5.03 billion in 2024, and remained at only around KRW 5.1 billion in 2025, a significantly reduced revenue base versus prior years.

Q2 2026 revenue of KRW 401 million fell 61.5% quarter on quarter and 76.3% year on year, showing extremely high revenue volatility. With a cost structure that includes fixed-cost elements, a thinning revenue base can make it harder to reach breakeven.

Uncertainty Surrounding Corporate Governance

A December 2025 disclosure stated that an asset management firm would acquire controlling shareholder Kim Ji-heon's stake, but the contract was subsequently cancelled, and the resulting disclosure reversal led to a notice of designation as an unfaithful disclosure company.

As of late January 2026, the controlling shareholder was confirmed again as Kim Ji-heon and five related parties with a 32.81% stake, suggesting the change of control was called off for now, though whether the related dispute has fully concluded requires further confirmation. This history of disclosure reversal itself can weigh on investor confidence.

Risk factors

Earnings and Cash Flow Risk

Four consecutive years of operating losses and negative operating cash flow every year raise questions about the company's ability to generate cash internally. With the revenue base sharply reduced from 2022 levels and fixed-cost burdens persisting, the need for additional external financing could grow. With the scale and timing of new orders uncertain, it is difficult to gauge when breakeven might be reached.

Governance and Disclosure Risk

A controlling-shareholder change contract disclosed in December 2025 was later reversed, leading to a notice of designation as an unfaithful disclosure company.

Under KOSDAQ disclosure rules, penalty points above a certain threshold can lead to a trading halt or a review for listing eligibility, so a recurrence of similar disclosure reversals could affect trading. Any further changes to the controlling shareholder's stake also warrant continued monitoring.

Order Concentration and Volatility Risk

Because Mobiis's revenue depends on a project-order structure tied to the execution, inspection and payment collection timing of individual contracts, a delay or reduction in a specific contract can cause a sharp drop in quarterly revenue.

Indeed, revenue declined for two consecutive quarters in Q1 and Q2 2026 after peaking in Q4 2025. The accelerator and fusion segment is also dependent on national research facility budgets and international project schedules, creating the potential for order timing to shift due to external factors.

Overall view

Mobiis combines a rare domestic technology position in the ITER and accelerator control field with a smart factory business built on a reference base of roughly 450 client companies, yet it has not yet shown a structural improvement in profitability, having posted operating losses for four consecutive years from 2022 through 2025. 2025 revenue rose slightly year on year, but the operating loss actually widened, and heading into 2026 quarterly revenue declined for two consecutive quarters, leaving no clear sign of a trend reversal.

The governance dispute that began with a December 2025 controlling-shareholder change contract, later reversed and followed by a notice of designation as an unfaithful disclosure company, appears to have settled for now with the controlling shareholder confirmed unchanged as of late January 2026, though whether the related uncertainty has been fully resolved requires further confirmation.

Small new orders, such as the July 2026 contract with BorgWarner's Changnyeong plant, continue to come in, and the direction of future quarterly results will depend on the revenue-recognition timing of new contracts and the pace of inspection and payment collection on existing projects.

Investors will want to continue monitoring upcoming quarterly and annual reports for signs of revenue recovery, trends in the operating margin, and any improvement in cash flow.
