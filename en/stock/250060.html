ITER Orders Pile Up, Losses Persist

02

Business structure

Mobiis was founded in 2000, centered on researchers from Samsung Electronics' network division and multimedia research institute, and listed on KOSDAQ in 2017.

Its core business spans three pillars: ultra-precision EPICS-based control systems for particle accelerators and fusion reactors, smart factory solutions, and machine learning platforms.

The company describes itself as holding the only domestic ultra-precision special control and system design technology based on EPICS, and has been expanding the industrial applications of its machine learning platform.

Its smart factory solution, QRP, is an all-in-one MES package based on ISO/TS16949 and IATF standards supplied to domestic and global clients, serving as a foothold for overseas expansion.

Key customers include large scientific project owners such as the France-based ITER Organization and the Korea Institute of Fusion Energy (KFE), while recent contracts with automotive parts makers such as BorgWarner Changnyeong and Neotech reflect growing smart-factory and manufacturing-AI business.

Because revenue is recognized on a project-by-project basis, a single contract can account for anywhere from 8% to 50% of annual revenue, producing significant quarter-to-quarter volatility.

The company's revenue scale is very small even within the KOSDAQ IT services and software sector, and its competitive position is closer to that of one of a handful of niche suppliers in the accelerator and fusion control market rather than a general-purpose control systems vendor.

04

Earnings analysis

Consolidated 2025 revenue of KRW 5.098 billion was little changed from KRW 5.026 billion in 2024, but both years mark a sharp structural decline from KRW 19.08 billion in 2023 and KRW 19.67 billion in 2022.

The operating loss widened from KRW 4.015 billion in 2022 to KRW 6.516 billion in 2023, narrowed to KRW 2.394 billion in 2024, then widened again to KRW 2.935 billion in 2025. The operating margin worsened for four consecutive years, from -20.4% in 2022 to -34.1% in 2023, -47.6% in 2024 and -57.6% in 2025.

Owner net income moved from -KRW 5.759 billion in 2022 and -KRW 0.385 billion in 2023 to +KRW 9.221 billion in 2024 and -KRW 1.053 billion in 2025; the large 2024 profit despite an operating loss appears linked to a one-off item tied to a change in capital structure, including the disappearance of non-controlling interests.

On a quarterly basis, revenue fell from KRW 1.692 billion in Q2 2025 to KRW 0.896 billion in Q3 2025 and further to KRW 0.401 billion in Q2 2026, with operating losses recorded every quarter and widening sharply to -KRW 1.251 billion in Q4 2025.

In Q3 2025, despite an operating loss of -KRW 0.535 billion, owner net income swung to +KRW 1.009 billion, which again reflects a one-off gain rather than core operations.

Summed over the most recent four quarters (Q3 2025 through Q2 2026), revenue totaled roughly KRW 3.6 billion with a combined owner net loss of about KRW 1.613 billion, showing that quarterly volatility carries directly into the annual results.

Operating cash flow was negative in all four years examined—-KRW 2.1 billion in 2022, -KRW 4.8 billion in 2023, -KRW 1.0 billion in 2024 and -KRW 2.6 billion in 2025—indicating persistent cash consumption from operations.

05

Industry analysis

Mobiis's business is heavily dependent on demand for control systems in large-scale national and international scientific projects such as ITER and heavy-ion accelerators.

These projects carry large budgets and span long timeframes, but complex ordering, design-change and approval processes create a significant lag between contract signing and revenue recognition.

For example, the ITER superconducting coil power supply main controller upgrade contract signed in February 2026 runs from February 2026 through June 2033, with milestone-based payments spread over many years following an initial down payment.

In the smart factory and manufacturing AI segment, domestic auto parts makers are key customers, with recently disclosed contracts signed with BorgWarner Changnyeong and Neotech.

In terms of competitive positioning, the EPICS-based ultra-precision control systems market is considered a high-barrier niche, though the company's own revenue scale is markedly smaller than the KOSDAQ IT services and software sector average, making its relative industry standing difficult to gauge in size terms.

The accelerator and fusion segment is reportedly seeing medium- to long-term budget growth tied to energy-transition and advanced-research investment in regions such as the United States, Europe and Japan, though order visibility at the individual company level remains limited.

Given the order-driven nature of the business, where revenue concentrates in specific quarters, results tend to be governed by individual project progress rather than a clear seasonal pattern.

06

Outlook

Mobiis has continued to win ITER-related orders into 2026.

In February 2026 it signed a KRW 2.5 billion contract (49.7% of recent revenue) to upgrade the main controller for the ITER superconducting coil power supply system, and in September 2026 it signed, as consortium leader alongside VERSE EUROPA, a fusion Disruption Mitigation System instrumentation and control supply contract with the ITER Organization worth a combined KRW 1.439 billion (firm KRW 1.413 billion plus conditional KRW 0.026 billion, equal to 28.22% of revenue), to be performed through September 2028.

Both contracts are self-produced and carry no down payment or advance, with payments made in stages tied to project progress, meaning actual cash inflow is linked to execution milestones.

In the smart factory segment, contracts with BorgWarner Changnyeong (KRW 0.42 billion, 8.2% of revenue) in July 2026 and Neotech (KRW 0.416 billion, 8.2% of revenue) in August 2026 were disclosed in succession, pointing to a broadening base of manufacturing-AI and smart-factory demand.

No formal annual revenue or profit guidance has been confirmed, however, and the next regular filing—the Q3 2026 quarterly report—is due by November 16, 2026.

On the balance sheet, the debt ratio fell sharply from 73.6% in 2022 to 18.9% in 2025, an apparent improvement that appears driven more by capital transactions such as convertible bond retirement than by improved operating cash flow.

Future results are likely to hinge both on the pace of revenue recognition from the multi-year contracts already booked and on how the governance dispute over the controlling shareholder's share transfer is ultimately resolved.

08

Bull factors

Niche Technology and Global References

Mobiis describes itself as the only domestic holder of EPICS-based ultra-precision special control technology, and has built a multi-year track record with large international projects including the ITER Organization and the Korea Institute of Fusion Energy.

In 2026 alone, two ITER-related contracts were signed (KRW 2.5 billion in February and KRW 1.439 billion in September), extending this reference base. Such specialized references can act as a barrier to entry for new competitors.

Expansion into Smart Factory and Manufacturing AI

The smart factory and manufacturing-AI contracts signed in succession with BorgWarner Changnyeong and Neotech in July and August 2026 show expansion into the automotive parts manufacturing customer base.

QRP, an all-in-one MES package based on ISO/TS16949 and IATF standards, is described as providing a foothold for overseas expansion as well. This can be read as an attempt to diversify revenue sources beyond fusion and accelerator work.

Reduced Debt Ratio and Balance Sheet Changes

The debt ratio fell sharply from 73.6% in 2022 to 18.9% in 2025. Disclosures also show moves to retire convertible bonds, partially addressing potential dilution. However, it should be noted that this change has not been accompanied by an improvement in operating cash flow.

Bear factors

Shrinking Revenue Base and Persistent Operating Losses

Revenue fell sharply from KRW 19.08 billion in 2023 to the KRW 5 billion range in 2024-2025 and has since stagnated. The operating margin worsened for four consecutive years, from -20.4% in 2022 to -57.6% in 2025. The 2025 operating loss of KRW 2.935 billion is well over half the size of annual revenue.

Continuous Operating Cash Outflow

Operating cash flow was negative in all four years from 2022 to 2025 (-KRW 2.1 billion, -KRW 4.8 billion, -KRW 1.0 billion and -KRW 2.6 billion). This steady cash drain from operations can increase reliance on external financing. The repeated issuance and retirement of convertible bonds is consistent with this pattern.

Project Concentration and Earnings Volatility

A single contract can represent 8-50% of annual revenue, producing large quarter-to-quarter swings. Revenue fell from KRW 1.692 billion in Q2 2025 to KRW 0.401 billion in Q2 2026. One-off swings are also evident, such as Q3 2025 when net income briefly turned positive despite an ongoing operating loss.

10

Risk factors

Governance and Control Risk

Controlling shareholder Kim Ji-heon signed an agreement to transfer a 26.02% stake to Hyuksin Asset Management, and a lawsuit was filed seeking an injunction to block the related extraordinary shareholders' meeting.

The plaintiff alleged the buyer intended to fund the acquisition through stock-collateral loans after artificially inflating the share price, while the company countered that the suit was an attempt to obstruct normal procedures. If this dispute remains unresolved, uncertainty over management stability could persist.

Accounting and Disclosure Reliability Risk

Some media reports have noted that the company's audit report contains keywords indicating accounting estimate uncertainty, such as impairment, suggesting room for management judgment in asset valuation and revenue recognition.

Corrections to single sales/supply contract disclosures have also recurred over the past year. The accuracy and consistency of disclosures can be an important variable for investment decisions.

Order Concentration and Foreign Exchange Risk

A significant portion of revenue is concentrated in a small number of large overseas projects such as ITER, so delays or changes in contract terms can materially affect results. The ITER contracts are euro-denominated and converted into won, exposing the company to currency fluctuations. Contract disclosures themselves note that terms such as duration and amount may change as projects progress.

12

Overall view

Mobiis continues to accumulate orders for large international scientific projects such as ITER based on its niche EPICS-based ultra-precision control technology, and new contracts in both fusion and smart factory segments continued to emerge in 2026.

However, its revenue base remains stagnant at a level far below 2023, the operating margin has worsened for four consecutive years, and operating losses alongside negative operating cash flow have recurred.

The swing to net profit in 2024 appears to have been driven by a one-off item, and the company returned to net losses in 2025 and through the first half of 2026.

In addition, a legal dispute over the controlling shareholder's share transfer and reports of accounting estimate uncertainty in the audit report add governance and disclosure-reliability uncertainty.

Investors should weigh the positive aspects of new order news alongside the actual timing of revenue recognition, the trajectory of cash flow, and the resolution of the governance dispute. The next quarterly report (due by November 16, 2026) and developments in the control dispute are near-term variables to watch.
