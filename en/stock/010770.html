02

Business structure

Pyung Hwa Holdings traces its roots to Pyung Hwa Rubber Industrial, founded in 1950, and in May 2006 became a pure holding company through a spin-off that left the investment business in the surviving entity. Its operating revenue consists of IT and other service fees charged to subsidiaries plus dividend income.

Manufacturing sits with the subsidiaries; as of the end of September 2024 the company had 12 consolidated subsidiaries, seven domestic and five overseas.

The core unit, Pyung HWA Industrial, makes vibration-damping and hose parts and sells mainly to domestic automakers including Hyundai, Kia, GM Korea and SsangYong, and it has been reported to hold the top technology rating from Hyundai and Kia.

Pyung Hwa Holdings held 54.27% of Pyung HWA Industrial as of October 30, 2026, and 63.88% including related parties. Other subsidiaries cover oil seals and O-rings, press and machined parts, rubber compounds, and molds and jigs, spreading the group across several stages of the auto-parts supply chain.

Standalone revenue of the holding company for the 2025 fiscal year was KRW 18.76 billion, against KRW 883.7 billion on a consolidated basis, so most revenue is generated by subsidiaries. Some subsidiaries are loss-making: one reported a net loss of KRW 7.88 billion in a March 2026 disclosure.

Because the portfolio is concentrated in auto parts, consolidated results track vehicle production and customer orders. The revenue split by segment and product-level profitability can be checked in the detailed items of the 2026 annual report.

04

Earnings analysis

Consolidated revenue rose from KRW 740.2 billion in 2022 to KRW 797.1 billion in 2023 (up 7.7% year on year), KRW 835.2 billion in 2024 (up 4.8%) and KRW 883.7 billion in 2025 (up 5.8%), so revenue has grown for three consecutive years from 2023 through 2025.

Operating profit went from KRW 3.6 billion in 2022 to KRW 16.8 billion in 2023 (up 365.3%), KRW 28.8 billion in 2024 (up 71.8%) and KRW 34.9 billion in 2025 (up 21.1%). The 2023 improvement was attributed mainly to the easing of semiconductor supply bottlenecks and a return to normal auto production.

In 2025 the company said higher subsidiary revenue lifted operating profit and lower non-operating expenses helped net income.

Net income attributable to controlling shareholders swung from a loss of KRW 14.1 billion in 2022 to KRW 8.18 billion in 2023, a turn to profit, then fell to KRW 1.56 billion in 2024 (down 80.9%) before recovering to KRW 34.7 billion in 2025 (up 2,125.4%).

In 2024 operating profit rose to KRW 28.8 billion while controlling-shareholder income fell to KRW 1.56 billion, which shows that items below operating profit drove the bottom line.

The gap between 2025 total net income of KRW 45.2 billion and controlling-shareholder net income of KRW 34.7 billion reflects the non-controlling share. The debt-to-equity ratio fell from 355.8% in 2022 to 243.4% in 2025, and operating cash flow rose from KRW 19.4 billion in 2024 to KRW 45.7 billion in 2025.

Quarterly revenue climbed from KRW 223 billion in the third quarter of 2025 to KRW 237.2 billion in the second quarter of 2026, then slipped 3.6% to KRW 228.7 billion in the third quarter of 2026.

Operating profit peaked at KRW 12.3 billion in the first quarter of 2026, then fell to KRW 11.6 billion and KRW 6.45 billion, with the third quarter down 15.1% year on year.

Controlling-shareholder net income peaked at KRW 15.3 billion in the fourth quarter of 2025 and fell to KRW 2.22 billion in the third quarter of 2026, down 72.3% year on year, a steeper drop than operating profit.

Revenue has grown while profit has shrunk, so the cost, selling and non-operating items in the quarterly reports need to be examined.

05

Industry analysis

Pyung Hwa Holdings' results track domestic and overseas vehicle production and the ordering patterns of automakers. Its key subsidiary, Pyung HWA Industrial, supplies vibration-damping and hose parts mainly to Hyundai and Kia, so customer concentration is high.

In 2023 the company said the easing of chip-shortage bottlenecks brought auto output back to pre-pandemic levels. An analysis published in early 2025 expected limited top-line growth for the holding group because of a slowdown in global economic growth and intense competition in the auto market.

The same analysis expected sales to stay healthy on the strength of the subsidiaries' competitiveness. Electric-vehicle parts are cited as a growth area, with securing EV-related technology and expanding overseas markets presented as the key tasks.

Overseas subsidiaries in the United States, China and India expose the group to local vehicle demand and exchange rates. In a holding structure, subsidiary profit reaches the parent only in proportion to its stake, so subsidiary profitability and ownership structure jointly shape results.

On competition, the group has a portfolio focused on specific part families such as vibration dampers and hoses, while OEM supply competition among auto-parts holding companies is intense. Industry figures should be checked against vehicle production statistics in the coming quarterly results.

Outlook

Growth directions cited for Pyung Hwa Holdings include electric-vehicle parts development and new investments in areas such as smart factories and eco-friendly energy. The company profile describes a plan to strengthen the expertise of each subsidiary under the holding structure and to pursue new businesses.

That guarantee equals 8.5% of equity, and the total outstanding guarantee balance was reported at KRW 160.3 billion in the same disclosure.

At the March 24, 2026 annual shareholders' meeting, the 52nd-term financial statements were approved and a year-end cash dividend for fiscal 2025 was resolved.

On October 28, 2026, related party Kim Joo-young bought 22,420 shares of Pyung HWA Industrial on the exchange, lifting the group's stake in that subsidiary from 63.84% to 63.88%.

With operating profit and controlling-shareholder net income both down year on year in the third quarter of 2026, the next check is whether this trend continues in the 2026 annual report. The February 19, 2027 maturity will reveal whether the guarantee is extended and on what terms.

Risk factors

Customer concentration and auto cycle

Revenue at the main subsidiary is tied to production and orders from domestic automakers such as Hyundai and Kia. As in 2023, easing supply-chain constraints can lift results quickly, but production cuts pass through to subsidiary profit at once. An analysis cited the possibility that a global slowdown and competition could limit top-line growth.

Leverage and guarantee maturity

With total liabilities exceeding twice total equity and a sizable guarantee balance, leverage is a structural risk. The guarantee on Pyung HWA Industrial runs to February 19, 2027, so whether it is extended is the next check on financial strain. Liquidity management and financial stabilization have been named as key tasks.

Governance concentration and share-price volatility

As of January 2025, chairman Kim Jong-suk held 27.07% as the largest shareholder, and reports said the controlling group together holds a majority. The float available for trading may be relatively small. Reports have described sharp short-term share-price jumps linked to political themes, so volatility can be high.

Overall view

Pyung Hwa Holdings swung from a loss attributable to controlling shareholders in 2022 to a profit of KRW 34.7 billion in 2025, restoring its earnings capacity. Operating profit rose from KRW 3.6 billion in 2022 to KRW 34.9 billion in 2025, and the debt-to-equity ratio fell from 355.8% to 243.4%.
