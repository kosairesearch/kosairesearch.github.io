Silla: Earnings Swings and Ownership Concentration in Focus

02

Business structure

Founded in 1967, Silla is the parent company of the Silla group, starting from distant-water fishing with tuna longline and purse seine operations and later expanding into seafood and agricultural distribution.

Its fishing operations center on the central and western Pacific, with stated priorities of securing global marine resources and stabilizing supply chains.

In 2019 it acquired Donghwa Cheonggwa, a fruit and vegetable distributor, entering agricultural distribution and brokerage, and it has since built a structure spanning seafood and farm produce distribution.

A third-party aggregator puts the mix as of Q2 2026 revenue at seafood distribution 48%, distant-water fishing 40%, agricultural distribution 7%, and other 5%, so this breakdown should be read as an outside estimate.

Around 2025 the company wound down its steel business and sold its subsidiary Naeluxurious Company, which had started the Popeyes business in Korea after its founding in 2021.

In 2022 its marine products export record was USD 64.83 million, about a second-place level domestically, against USD 176 million for the domestic leader Dongwon Industries. The largest shareholder holds 40.18%, and including related parties the stake is 64.33%. It is a top-tier domestic player in distant-water fishing, but well behind the leader.

04

Earnings analysis

Annual revenue rose from KRW 432.6 billion in 2022 to KRW 434.2 billion in 2023, up 0.4%, and reached KRW 496.1 billion in 2024, up 14.3%. It fell 8.3% to KRW 454.7 billion in 2025, and 2024 was the largest year and 2022 the smallest in the 2022 to 2025 period.

Operating profit went from KRW 14.5 billion in 2022 to KRW 13.9 billion in 2023, down 3.7%, then recovered to KRW 16.4 billion in 2024, up 18.0%. In 2025 the company posted an operating loss of KRW 1.57 billion, a swing from profit, and the operating margin fell from 3.3% in 2024 to -0.3% in 2025.

Net income attributable to controlling shareholders peaked at KRW 39.6 billion in 2024, up 130.7%, before dropping to KRW 4.64 billion in 2025, down 88.3%. Net income including non-controlling interests was KRW 40.2 billion in 2024 and KRW 5.31 billion in 2025.

In 2024 net income was well above operating profit, which suggests non-operating items contributed meaningfully, and 2025 also kept net income positive despite an operating loss.

Operating cash flow rose from KRW 11.1 billion in 2022 to KRW 42.6 billion in 2023 and KRW 66.8 billion in 2024, then turned to -KRW 13.1 billion in 2025.

The debt ratio fell from 28.1% in 2022 to 19.8% in 2025, with year-end 2025 total equity of KRW 603.1 billion, controlling shareholders' equity of KRW 601.5 billion, and total liabilities of KRW 119.4 billion.

Quarterly, an operating loss of KRW 3.16 billion in Q3 2025 was followed by an operating profit of KRW 778 million in Q4 2025, then KRW 4.47 billion in Q1 2026 and KRW 506 million in Q2 2026, before a KRW 3.38 billion operating loss in Q3 2026.

The Q3 2026 net loss attributable to controlling shareholders of KRW 37.2 billion, against a KRW 20.2 billion profit in Q2, is a sharp swing, and the reason it is far larger than the operating loss needs to be checked in the non-operating items in the quarterly report.

The last four quarters together show controlling-shareholder net income of only KRW 7.27 billion, a thin earnings base.

05

Industry analysis

Distant-water fishing is highly sensitive to tuna prices and the exchange rate. Coverage of 2023 results pointed to higher processing-tuna output and rising currency and fish prices as drivers of revenue growth. Coverage in Q1 2024 said that pricing in dollar terms meant a higher exchange rate lifted sales.

Conversely, the steel business suffered a swing to losses from lower selling prices and weaker demand, and it was later wound down. In competition, the marine products export record in 2022 was USD 64.83 million for Silla against USD 176 million for the domestic leader, a clear gap.

Coverage expected Silla to focus on narrowing that gap. Combining seafood and farm produce distribution is cited as a buffer against swings in the fishing business.

Overall, tuna prices and the exchange rate are the main industry variables, while the base rests on distant-water fishing combined with distribution channels.

Whether the Q3 2026 loss widening stems from industry conditions or company-specific factors can be separated using segment sales and cost lines in the quarterly report.

Quarterly profit recovery

In 2024 operating profit was KRW 16.4 billion and controlling-shareholder net income KRW 39.6 billion, so the earnings base has not disappeared. However, Q2 2026 operating profit fell to KRW 506 million, so quarterly variance remains a challenge.

Lower financial leverage

The debt ratio fell from 28.1% in 2022 to 19.8% in 2025. Year-end 2025 total equity was KRW 603.1 billion against total liabilities of KRW 119.4 billion, a relatively light debt burden. This equity base widens room to fund acquisitions or investment.

Diversification through acquisition

The company completed the purchase of all 30,000 issued shares of the target company for KRW 82.3 billion in October 2026 and stated diversification as the purpose. It follows a pattern similar to the 2019 acquisition of a fruit and vegetable distributor. If the acquired business adds to earnings alongside existing operations, it would be a supporting factor.

Bear factors

Swing to losses and wider losses

An operating loss of KRW 1.57 billion in 2025 marked a swing from profit, and Q3 2026 showed an operating loss of KRW 3.38 billion. The Q3 2026 net loss attributable to controlling shareholders of KRW 37.2 billion is a swing into loss year on year.

Controlling-shareholder net income of KRW 7.27 billion over the last four quarters limits the scale of recovery.

Weaker cash generation

Operating cash flow fell from KRW 66.8 billion in 2024 to -KRW 13.1 billion in 2025. Revenue fell 8.3% to KRW 454.7 billion in the same year. If weaker cash generation persists, funding acquisitions and shareholder payouts becomes a challenge.

Concentrated stakes and a pledge

The largest shareholder holds 40.18%, and including related parties the stake is 64.33%. It signed a pledge agreement with a bank on March 16, 2026, covering 6,429,287 shares. With related-party stakes this large, the float available to the market may be limited, which is a bearish factor.

Risk factors

Industry and FX

Tuna prices and the exchange rate are the main variables for revenue and selling prices. Coverage in Q1 2024 cited dollar-based pricing as a driver of sales. A fall in fish prices or a reversal in the exchange rate could widen quarterly earnings swings again.

Governance and pledge

Combined with related parties, the largest shareholder's stake totals 64.33%, so ownership is concentrated. Changes in the status of the 6,429,287 pledged shares are disclosed publicly. Shifts in the controlling stake could affect the number of freely tradable shares and supply and demand.

Post-acquisition integration

Separate-basis total assets rose by KRW 70 billion at the 2026 half-year end after the acquisition. How the target's earnings fit with the existing fishing and distribution businesses is the central question. If the Q3 2026 losses persist, the benefit of the acquisition could be delayed.
