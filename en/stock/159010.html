02

Business structure

Asflow was founded in 2001 and listed on KOSDAQ in 2021, and it manufactures high-cleanliness piping parts for semiconductor process gas lines, including tubes, valves, fittings, filters and regulators.

According to an external corporate data service, tubes and pipes accounted for 57% of sales in the first half of 2026. The same source notes that module sales, a assembled-unit supply, newly appeared in 2026.

R&D spending in the first half of 2026 equaled 1.60% of revenue, and the company runs government-funded projects including ones from the Ministry of Trade, Industry and Energy.

Its customer base is weighted toward domestic chipmakers such as Samsung Electronics and SK hynix, and the same source puts domestic sales at about 66% and exports at about 34% for 2025.

The main export channel is OEM parts supply to Applied Materials (AMAT), where Asflow has been registered as an OEM parts supplier since 2018.

Product approval for Intel has been completed, while approvals for Micron and Lam Research are in progress, with roughly 1,000 weldment items reportedly under Lam Research qualification.

04

Earnings analysis

Based on the DART-filed annual figures, revenue fell from KRW 88.7 billion in 2022 to KRW 86.9 billion in 2023 (down 2.0% year on year), KRW 83.6 billion in 2024 (down 3.8%) and KRW 67.9 billion in 2025 (down 18.8%).

Revenue declined for three straight years from 2023 through 2025, with 2022 the largest year and 2025 the smallest in the 2022–2025 period.

Operating profit fell from KRW 10.5 billion in 2022 to KRW 6.35 billion in 2023 (down 39.5%), swung to an operating loss of KRW 1.86 billion in 2024, and widened to an operating loss of KRW 7.01 billion in 2025. The operating margin moved from 11.8% in 2022 to 7.3% in 2023, -2.2% in 2024 and -10.3% in 2025.

Net income attributable to controlling shareholders dropped from KRW 9.05 billion in 2022 to KRW 3.57 billion in 2023 (down 60.5%), then recorded losses of KRW 2.37 billion in 2024 and KRW 9.94 billion in 2025.

The company cited delayed overseas projects with customers in China and Taiwan, development costs for next-generation products booked into cost of sales, and delays in equipment-maker qualification for its module business as drivers of the 2025 deterioration.

The debt-to-equity ratio rose from 106.5% in 2022 to 155.4% in 2025, and at end-2025 total equity was KRW 46.4 billion and total liabilities KRW 72.1 billion. Operating cash flow swung from an inflow of KRW 5.47 billion in 2024 to an outflow of KRW 1.78 billion in 2025.

Quarterly revenue rose from KRW 13.4 billion in Q3 2025 and KRW 13.6 billion in Q4 2025 to KRW 28.3 billion in Q1 2026, KRW 31.7 billion in Q2 and KRW 32.6 billion in Q3. Q1 2026 operating profit of KRW 1.38 billion was a swing to profit, and Q2 operating profit of KRW 4.42 billion rose 219.5% from the prior quarter.

Q3 2026 operating profit of KRW 4.15 billion was down 6.0% from Q2 but a swing to profit from a year earlier. Q3 2026 nonetheless posted a controlling-shareholder net loss of KRW 492 million despite positive operating profit, so the items between operating profit and net income need to be checked. Net income attributable to controlling shareholders over the last four quarters totaled KRW 7.13 billion.

Industry analysis

Asflow's semiconductor process-gas piping parts move with the investment cycles of chip fabs and equipment makers. An external data source attributes the 2025 deterioration to a downcycle in which lower utilization and volumes raised the burden of fixed costs.

In the first half of 2026, semiconductor materials and parts stocks broadly strengthened, and Asflow's shares rose more than 200% over one year on a closing-price basis before being designated as an investment-warning stock on June 15, 2026.

Whether the 2026 quarterly sales recovery reflects a restart in fab investment or the effect of new items such as modules can be separated by looking at the product breakdown in quarterly results.

Because Asflow supplies after qualification by global equipment makers, qualification status acts both as an entry barrier and as a variable that sets the pace of revenue. Intel approval is complete, while approvals for Micron and Lam Research are in progress, so widening qualification is the next step.

Within the same machinery and equipment sector, listed equipment and parts makers such as SFA Engineering, People & Technology, Protec and Sunic System are commonly cited for comparison. Peer comparisons are typically made by reading revenue growth and operating margin trends side by side.

Outlook

The largest investment the company has disclosed is a semiconductor parts plant in China. According to a report dated November 3, 2026, the total investment is USD 150 million, with a first site of about 7,000 pyeong (8,400 pyeong of floor area) and an additional 6,000 pyeong secured for expansion.

The target completion date is January 2027. The same report notes that the Chinese side provided large-scale land to attract the semiconductor parts industry. Whether the overseas customer projects in China and Taiwan, cited as a cause of the 2025 deterioration, resume is the first gate for an overseas sales recovery.

Because module sales newly appeared in 2026, the module qualification schedule remains a variable for revenue expansion. The Micron product approval was targeted for completion within Q3 2026, and whether that approval has been completed should be checked in the next regular filing.

Qualification for roughly 1,000 Lam Research items is in progress. Whether the 2026 quarterly profits carry through on a full-year basis will be checked against the operating margin in the 2026 annual report.

The China plant's capacity effect will feed into earnings depending on when it starts operating after completion.

Bull factors

Quarterly turnaround in 2026

Q1 2026 revenue of KRW 28.3 billion rose 108.6% from the prior quarter, and operating profit of KRW 1.38 billion marked a swing to profit. Q2 operating profit of KRW 4.42 billion rose 219.5% from Q1, and Q3 operating profit of KRW 4.15 billion kept the profit streak going.

Revenue rose for four consecutive quarters from Q4 2025 through Q3 2026. Q3 2026 revenue of KRW 32.6 billion was up 143.0% from a year earlier.

Module sales and qualification progress

According to an external corporate data source, module sales newly appeared in 2026, extending supply from single parts to assembled units. This is read as a broadening of supply scope. Intel product approval has been completed, and approval processes for Micron and Lam Research are underway. More global equipment-maker qualifications could widen the range of items and customers.

China plant investment

The company is building a semiconductor parts plant in China with a reported total investment of USD 150 million. According to a November 3, 2026 report, about 7,000 pyeong of first-phase land and 6,000 pyeong for expansion have been secured. The completion target is January 2027.

A larger overseas production base would give the company more capacity to respond if overseas customer projects resume.

Bear factors

Two straight annual losses

The 2025 operating loss was KRW 7.01 billion and the net loss attributable to controlling shareholders was KRW 9.94 billion, a second consecutive year of losses. The operating margin fell from 11.8% in 2022 to -10.3% in 2025. Revenue of KRW 67.9 billion in 2025 was down 18.8% year on year.

The company pointed to delayed overseas customer projects, development costs booked into cost of sales, and delayed module qualification as causes of the losses.

Quarterly net income swings

Q3 2026 operating profit of KRW 4.15 billion was positive, but the net loss attributable to controlling shareholders of KRW 492 million turned the bottom line negative. Compared with the KRW 8.82 billion controlling-shareholder profit in Q2, quarterly net results moved sharply.

Net income attributable to controlling shareholders over the last four quarters totaled KRW 7.13 billion, a modest cumulative profit. The items behind the gap between operating profit and net income should be checked in the next regular report.

Higher debt and negative operating cash flow

The debt-to-equity ratio rose from 106.5% in 2022 to 155.4% in 2025, with total liabilities of KRW 72.1 billion and total equity of KRW 46.4 billion at end-2025. Operating cash flow moved from an inflow of KRW 5.47 billion in 2024 to an outflow of KRW 1.78 billion in 2025.

With domestic sales at about 66% of the total, results are tied directly to domestic chip investment. A slowdown in domestic investment could weigh on both volumes and cash generation.

Risk factors

Customer concentration and chip capex cycle

With a high domestic share, results are directly tied to domestic fab investment at companies such as Samsung Electronics and SK hynix. In the 2025 downcycle, lower utilization and volumes raised the burden of fixed costs and pulled down results. If chip investment slows, the durability of the 2026 quarterly recovery could be tested.

Overseas project and qualification delays

Delayed projects with customers in China and Taiwan were cited as the cause of the 2025 overseas sales shortfall. Micron and Lam Research product approvals and module qualification timing remain variables for results. Whether construction of the China plant keeps to the January 2027 target is also a point to watch.

Balance sheet and share price volatility

A debt-to-equity ratio of 155.4% and a 2025 operating cash flow deficit remain balance-sheet burdens. The share price rose more than 200% over one year on a closing-price basis and was designated as an investment-warning stock on June 15, 2026. If short-term overheating continues after such a designation, trading restrictions can apply.

Overall view

Asflow makes semiconductor process-gas piping parts, and sales and profits declined through 2025, producing two consecutive years of losses. In 2026, quarterly revenue rose for four straight quarters, and operating profit has stayed positive since Q1.

However, Q3 2026 turned to a controlling-shareholder net loss, and net income attributable to controlling shareholders over the last four quarters totaled KRW 7.13 billion. On the balance sheet, the debt-to-equity ratio rose to 155.4% in 2025 and operating cash flow was negative.

The China plant investment, module sales and qualification expansion are cited as growth drivers, while customer concentration and overseas project delays remain risk factors.

The share price swung widely amid strength in semiconductor materials stocks and an investment-warning designation, and the valuation read depends on whether the earnings recovery lasts.

The next checkpoints are the operating margin in the 2026 annual report, completion of the China plant in January 2027, and quarterly results for Q1 2027.
