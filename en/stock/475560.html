02

Business structure

The BORN Korea runs mainly as a restaurant franchisor, with distribution, hotel and regional development businesses alongside, and operates 25 in-house restaurant brands. In 2025, the franchise segment generated KRW 323.1 billion in sales, or 89.4% of the total, and recorded an operating loss of KRW 20.3 billion.

The distribution segment's 2025 sales were KRW 30.5 billion, down 47.6% year on year, and it swung to an operating loss of KRW 3.7 billion. Franchise sales are concentrated in a few brands: in the 2024 securities registration statement, the low-priced coffee brand Paikdabang accounted for 37.3% of sales.

The company says it keeps launching new brands to reduce dependence on any single one. One press report noted that 13 of the 25 brands saw franchise store counts decline in 2025.

Beyond franchising, the company's shift toward a comprehensive food business rests on ready-to-eat restaurant meal (RMR) products and B2B and B2C distribution built on its TBK sauce.

Overseas sales were only about 2% of the total in 2023, and the company is building its overseas base across North America, Southeast Asia, China, Japan and Europe.

Its hotel business is seeing a recovery in Jeju tourism demand, but profitability improvement has been limited, according to a first-quarter 2026 company profile.

04

Earnings analysis

On a disclosed basis, annual sales rose from KRW 410.7 billion in 2023 to KRW 464.2 billion in 2024, up 13.0%, making 2024 the largest year in the 2023-2025 window. Sales then fell to KRW 361.2 billion in 2025, down 22.2%, the smallest year in that window.

Operating profit went from KRW 25.6 billion in 2023 to KRW 36 billion in 2024, up 40.8%, before the company swung to an operating loss of KRW 23.7 billion in 2025.

Controlling-shareholder net income moved from KRW 20.9 billion in 2023 to KRW 31 billion in 2024, up 48.2%, then turned into a controlling-shareholder net loss of KRW 17.4 billion in 2025. Net income including non-controlling interests showed the same pattern, with a net loss of KRW 17.4 billion in 2025.

Press analysis of the 2025 annual report shows the cost of sales ratio rose to 75.2% from 66.7%, and the selling and administrative expense ratio climbed to 31.4%.

The main driver was franchisee support: about KRW 33.2 billion of promotional costs was deducted from revenue in 2025, against roughly KRW 5.3 billion in 2024. Operating margin fell from 6.2% in 2023 and 7.8% in 2024 to -6.6% in 2025.

Operating cash flow went from KRW 34.4 billion in 2023 and KRW 45.9 billion in 2024 to -KRW 29 billion in 2025. The debt-to-equity ratio fell from 57.0% in 2023 to 31.8% in 2024 and 23.3% in 2025, with total equity of KRW 246 billion and total liabilities of KRW 57.3 billion at year-end 2025.

Quarterly sales went from KRW 87.4 billion in the third quarter of 2025 to KRW 89 billion in the fourth quarter, bottomed at KRW 79.6 billion in the first quarter of 2026, then rose to KRW 83.2 billion and KRW 85 billion in the second and third quarters.

Operating losses have run for five consecutive quarters, from KRW 4.36 billion in the third quarter of 2025 to KRW 3.73 billion in the third quarter of 2026. A press report says the third-quarter 2026 cost of sales ratio fell to 70.5% from 73.1% a year earlier.

Cumulative operating loss for January to September 2026 was KRW 13.5 billion, down about 35% from the prior year, according to press reports.

05

Industry analysis

The restaurant franchise sector is being pressured by high prices, a weaker won and softer consumer sentiment amid global political uncertainty. A first-quarter 2026 company profile links the weakness in franchise and distribution results to these conditions.

In 2025, the company ran large-scale franchisee support promotions to counter the restaurant downturn, and management has said these one-off costs weighed on results. Public disclosures do not yet show when restaurant demand will recover.

On the competitive side, a press report noted that the number of low-priced coffee franchise stores reached 5,285 at the end of 2023, up 37.3% from a year earlier, raising concerns about price-led competition.

Because the franchise model relies on royalties and supply margins, operating margin moves sharply with store-support policy and input costs. Compared with manufacturing-led listed peers in the food sector, The BORN Korea's profit margins are more volatile.

At the time of its listing, the company's valuation was debated after comprehensive food companies were used as comparables instead of franchise operators.

06

Outlook

The company says it is keeping normal operating activity and focusing on profit improvement. Management's basic approach this year is to manage profitability without the large franchisee support spending seen in 2025.

A press headline from late June 2026 reported 128 improvements in one year of the franchisee committee's activity, and the CEO said the company would help store owners further. Over the medium term, the company aims to broaden its franchise-centered model into a comprehensive food business.

To do so, it plans to expand its RMR product line, grow global B2B and B2C sales built on TBK sauce, and enter markets in North America, Southeast Asia, China, Japan and Europe. Content intellectual property and new domestic businesses have also been mentioned for review.

The company plans to keep investing in its overseas and distribution foundations and expects some overseas subsidiary costs to weigh on consolidated results for the time being. No public timetable has been given for when the new businesses will turn profitable.

The next checkpoints are therefore the size of overseas subsidiary costs and the growth of RMR and distribution sales. The most direct check is how the operating margin moves from -6.6% in 2025 in the 2026 annual report, the next regular filing.

Bull factors

Narrowing operating losses

The third-quarter 2026 operating loss of KRW 3.73 billion narrowed from both the prior year and the prior quarter. A press report says the cost of sales ratio fell to 70.5% from 73.1%, lifting the gross margin to 29.5%.

Unlike 2025, when large franchisee support was spent, operations are running on a more normal basis, and the profit trend is moving in the improving direction.

Stronger balance sheet

The debt-to-equity ratio fell from 57.0% in 2023 to 31.8% in 2024 and 23.3% in 2025. Total equity at year-end 2025 was KRW 246 billion, against total liabilities of KRW 57.3 billion.

The fourth quarter of 2025, which returned to a controlling-shareholder net profit of KRW 2.74 billion, is one example of earnings recovering within a quarter.

Room to grow overseas and in distribution

Overseas sales were only about 2% of the total in 2023, which is the starting point for the company's strategy to expand there. TBK sauce-based B2B and B2C distribution and a wider RMR product line are presented as ways to diversify revenue.

The distribution segment's sales fell 47.6% in 2025, so room for improvement in its business indicators remains.

Bear factors

Slow sales recovery

First-quarter 2026 sales fell 28.1% year on year, and cumulative January-to-September 2026 sales were KRW 247.8 billion, down 9.0%. Third-quarter 2026 sales of KRW 85 billion were also 2.7% below the prior year. A second-quarter flash report showed a 12% gain, but a full-year sales recovery has not yet been shown.

Continued losses and wider net loss

Operating losses have continued for five consecutive quarters from the third quarter of 2025 through the third quarter of 2026.

Controlling-shareholder net losses have run for three consecutive quarters since the first quarter of 2026, and the third-quarter 2026 net loss of KRW 4.3 billion widened from KRW 2.7 billion a year earlier. Operating cash flow also turned negative, at -KRW 29 billion in 2025.

Dependence on franchise and a few brands

The franchise segment accounted for 89.4% of 2025 sales, so the parent's results depend heavily on store conditions. Paikdabang's share of sales was 37.3% in the 2024 securities registration statement, and in 2025, 13 of the 25 brands saw store counts decline.

If store closures and owner-related controversy continue together, both franchise recruitment and the pace of sales recovery could slow.

Risk factors

Owner and reputation risk

Press coverage in 2025 described continuing owner-related risk, citing product controversies and an alleged violation of farmland law. Trust with franchisees is directly linked to franchise recruitment and store retention.

Even with stronger store-owner support policies, reputation-related costs remain a source of swings in consolidated results.

Restaurant demand and cost risk

High prices, a weaker won and softer consumer sentiment are pressing franchise and distribution results at the same time. The cost of sales ratio rose to 75.2% in 2025 from 66.7% the year before, and the selling and administrative expense ratio rose to 31.4%.

Cost ratio improvement appeared in the third quarter of 2026, but a renewed swing in restaurant demand or input prices could delay margin recovery.

Overseas and distribution investment cost risk

The company is continuing investment in overseas and distribution businesses and has said costs at some overseas subsidiaries will weigh on consolidated results for the time being. The new businesses have not yet shown a clear profit contribution. If franchisee support expands again, sales deductions and profit damage similar to 2025 could recur.

Overall view

The BORN Korea's annual sales and profit both fell in 2025 as franchisee support costs were booked as sales deductions. In 2026, operating losses have narrowed each quarter, alongside an improving cost of sales ratio.

Sales bottomed in the first quarter of 2026 and recovered in the second and third quarters, but have not yet returned to prior-year levels. Controlling-shareholder net losses have run for three consecutive quarters, with the last four quarters totaling KRW 8.24 billion.

The balance sheet is comparatively sturdy, with a debt-to-equity ratio of 23.3% and total equity of KRW 246 billion, but earnings recovery has not yet been confirmed. The franchise segment accounted for 89.4% of 2025 sales, which leaves results sensitive to store trends and a few key brands.

Overseas and distribution businesses offer room to grow, while the company has said their costs will weigh for some time. The next checkpoints are the operating margin in the 2026 annual report and the sales trend in the first quarter of 2027.
