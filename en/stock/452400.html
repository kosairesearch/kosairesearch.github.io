02

Business structure

Inics was founded in 1984 and has focused since 2015 on developing materials and parts that improve the safety of EV secondary batteries.

It currently supplies EV safety solutions such as battery cell pads and fire-resistant barriers to global customers, and the company states it was the first to develop and exclusively supply both products.

Its lineup also includes tapes and interior and exterior materials, which the company says carry a meaningful share of sales. Its customer base spans battery makers and the automaker supply chain.

As of a 2024 press report, major customers were Hyundai Mobis, Hyundai Green Power and SK On, together accounting for roughly 80% to 85%. Backlog stood at about KRW 344.4 billion at the end of 2023, roughly three times that year's annual revenue.

Overseas, its base centers on a new plant in Auburn, Alabama and a Czech subsidiary, and its US unit Inics Battery Solution is wholly owned.

In December 2025 the company won an order to supply fire-resistant pressure pads for Hyundai Motor and Kia's Porter 2 and PBV models, with actual delivery routed through Hyundai Mobis. To reduce its dependence on EVs, the company is also developing fire-resistant materials for energy storage systems (ESS).

04

Earnings analysis

On a disclosed basis, annual revenue rose from KRW 114.3 billion in 2022 to KRW 118.9 billion in 2023 (up 4.0% year on year), fell to KRW 104.5 billion in 2024 (down 12.1%), and recovered to KRW 122.6 billion in 2025 (up 17.3%). Among 2022 to 2025, 2025 was the largest year and 2024 the smallest.

Operating profit peaked at KRW 10.8 billion in 2023 (up 15.1% year on year), then turned into an operating loss of KRW 1.54 billion in 2024; in 2025 the operating loss of KRW 1 billion continued but narrowed. Operating margin moved from 8.2% in 2022 and 9.1% in 2023 to -1.5% in 2024 and -0.8% in 2025.

Net income attributable to controlling shareholders peaked at KRW 11 billion in 2023 (up 2.0%), then fell to KRW 1.02 billion in 2024 (down 90.8%) and KRW 247 million in 2025 (down 75.7%), a two-year decline.

Net income including non-controlling interests, which is a different measure, was KRW 232 million in 2025, and both measures have declined since 2023. Operating cash flow went from KRW 6.54 billion in 2022 and KRW 13.9 billion in 2023 to KRW 1.38 billion in 2024 and KRW -2.32 billion in 2025.

On a quarterly basis, third-quarter 2025 revenue was KRW 34.1 billion with operating profit of KRW 29.98 million; the fourth quarter then bottomed at KRW 26.4 billion in revenue (down 22.6% quarter on quarter) and an operating loss of KRW 1.24 billion.

First-quarter 2026 revenue was KRW 26.6 billion with an operating loss of KRW 563 million, and second-quarter revenue of KRW 29.4 billion produced operating profit of KRW 98.19 million, a swing back to profit.

Third-quarter 2026 revenue was KRW 29.4 billion (up 0.2% quarter on quarter, down 13.8% year on year) with operating profit of KRW 377 million, yet net loss attributable to controlling shareholders reached KRW 1.17 billion, a swing into loss.

A press report on the first quarter of 2026 said falling output of the main product cut both sales and utilization, compounded by losses at an overseas subsidiary.

05

Industry analysis

Demand for EV battery safety parts is rising as attention to EV fires grows. Amid tightening EV safety standards, observers say Inics' fire-resistant technology has gained recognition.

However, a press report links a slowdown in EV demand to falling output of the main product, so the pace of demand recovery is a key driver of results. In the US, after the Inflation Reduction Act took effect, automakers and battery makers expanded local production, increasing pressure to localize parts supply.

Inics says its US plant and other overseas bases let it respond to changes in tariff policy. On competition, material technology and automaker qualification are cited as the main barriers to entry.

Adoption of its fire-resistant pressure pad in mass-production models for Hyundai Motor and Kia is viewed as a foundation for registration as a Tier 1 supplier. In June 2026, Samsung Securities addressed weak demand from EV customers in a research report.

Compared with larger listed auto parts makers in the same sector, Inics sits closer to a small or mid-sized supplier with high customer concentration.

06

Outlook

The most concrete near-term event is mass production of the fire-resistant pressure pad for Hyundai Motor and Kia's Porter 2 and PBV models. Production is scheduled to start in June 2027 and be applied in stages over about 12 years.

The company says the order size could reach a considerable level over the medium to long term. It planned capital expenditure of about KRW 10 billion tied to the order to stabilize its production lines.

When the Busan No. 2 plant expansion was announced in December 2025, completion was planned for year-end, and whether it is operating can be checked in the business report's facilities disclosure.

The company said it aims to expand fire-resistant cell-to-cell pad applications across more vehicle models and to improve material technology, and it expects profitability to improve over the medium to long term.

A 2025 press report said the US unit produces fire-resistant barriers and second-generation battery cell pads and plans to expand capacity. The fire-resistant battery cell pad for Ford EVs was then in performance testing.

A press report also says whether fire-resistant materials for ESS convert into actual orders will be a turning point for earnings recovery. The next checkpoints are therefore execution of the mass-production schedule, capital expenditure, and whether ESS materials convert into orders.

Bull factors

Mass-production reference at automakers

The fire-resistant pressure pad was adopted in Hyundai Motor and Kia mass-production models for the first time, which is highlighted as a key milestone. The order is viewed as a foundation for registration as a Tier 1 supplier to automakers.

Application is planned in stages over about 12 years after mass production starts in June 2027, which the company says could support medium- to long-term revenue.

US local production and tariff/IRA positioning

The company completed a new plant in Auburn, Alabama and built a local production setup. A press report notes that after the IRA took effect, automaker and battery investment in local production rose, lifting demand for localized parts. The company has said its overseas production bases let it respond to tariff policy changes.

Tighter EV safety rules and ESS materials

Observers say Inics' fire-resistant technology has gained recognition amid tightening EV safety standards. The company is developing fire-resistant materials for energy storage systems to reduce its dependence on EVs. If these materials convert into orders, the business could extend to demand sources beyond EVs.

Bear factors

EV demand slowdown and lower utilization

A press report links an EV demand slowdown to falling output of the main product. Sales and utilization fell together as a result. Third-quarter 2026 revenue of KRW 29.4 billion was down 13.8% year on year, so the pace of recovery remains limited.

Weaker earnings power and cash flow

Net income attributable to controlling shareholders fell for two consecutive years from 2024 to 2025, down 75.7% in 2025. Net loss attributable to controlling shareholders totaled KRW 924 million over the last four quarters. Operating cash flow turned negative at KRW -2.32 billion in 2025, signaling weaker earnings capacity.

Customer concentration and overseas losses

As of a 2024 press report, major customers including Hyundai Mobis, Hyundai Green Power and SK On accounted for roughly 80% to 85%. High customer concentration means production swings at a particular automaker or battery maker flow directly into results.

A press report also notes losses at an overseas subsidiary, so the profitability of overseas expansion needs confirmation.

Risk factors

Customer and end-market concentration

Major customers are concentrated among a small number of automakers and battery makers. If EV demand slows, their orders fall and revenue is directly affected. Shifts in order timing within the same customer group can also widen quarterly earnings swings.

Capex and mass-production timing

The company planned capital expenditure of about KRW 10 billion tied to the order. Mass production is scheduled to start in June 2027, so any delay would push back the timing of investment recovery. Operation of the Busan No. 2 plant and stabilization of production lines are needed to handle larger volumes.

Financial capacity and cash generation

Total equity was KRW 120.9 billion at the end of 2025 and the debt-to-equity ratio was 16.8%, so debt burden appears limited. However, operating cash flow turned negative in 2025, weakening the company's ability to generate cash from operations. If losses continue, funding capex will depend more on equity and external financing.

Overall view

Inics' disclosed results show revenue peaked in 2023 before recovering in 2025, while operating profit and net income attributable to controlling shareholders have declined and turned to losses since 2024.

Operating profit moved back toward profit from the second quarter of 2026, but the third-quarter 2026 net loss attributable to controlling shareholders of KRW 1.17 billion leaves the direction of earnings recovery mixed.

Supportive factors include automaker mass-production adoption, US local production and tighter EV safety standards, while weaker factors include lower utilization from the EV demand slowdown, customer concentration and weaker cash flow.

On valuation, an earnings-based multiple cannot be calculated, the price-to-book ratio sits well below 1x, and dividend payouts are close to nil.

The next checkpoints are the 2026 business report and annual results, the first-quarter 2027 quarterly report, and the start of fire-resistant pad mass production in June 2027. The key observation at these points is whether operating profit stays positive and net income attributable to controlling shareholders follows.
