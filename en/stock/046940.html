02

Business structure

Woowon Development was founded in 1998 and listed on KOSDAQ in 2002 as a civil engineering specialist contractor.

It performs earthworks across expressways, road works, bridges and tunnels, port and land development, and environmental works, and pursues urban development through its subsidiary Daejeon-style integrated development vehicle.

According to the company profile, it has completed more than 70 projects, including the Jungbu Expressway, covering over 320 km of construction length, and has filed a patent for a slip-form method. Its project list is centered on rail subgrade and urban transit earthworks and tunnels.

Sites listed on its website include the Sinansan line private-investment section, the Chuncheon-Sokcho rail subgrade section 7, the Seoul Subway Line 7 Cheongna extension, and the Goyang Changneung station works.

The prime contractors shown in its project records are large builders such as Hyundai Engineering & Construction, so Woowon appears to work under large prime contractors.

Two orders disclosed in 2026, a KRW 56.2 billion rail subgrade package on the Metropolitan Express GTX-B line disclosed on February 26 and a KRW 68.3 billion package on the Daejang-Hongik transit line disclosed on May 6, were both joint contracts with Hyundai Engineering & Construction and others.

A single-sales contract disclosed on August 25, 2026 covered the Seoul Subway Line 7 Cheongna extension additional station construction.

Listed peers in the same construction and building materials sector include Hyundai Engineering & Construction, Daewoo Engineering & Construction, GS Engineering & Construction and DL E&C, all large general contractors, while Woowon concentrates on earthworks and tunnels in specialty trades.

04

Earnings analysis

Annual revenue went from KRW 196.3 billion in 2022 to KRW 252.5 billion in 2023 (up 28.6%), KRW 302.8 billion in 2024 (up 19.9%) and KRW 376.3 billion in 2025 (up 24.3%), rising for three consecutive years from 2023 through 2025.

Operating profit moved from KRW 578 million in 2022 to KRW 1.36 billion in 2023 (up 135.0%) and KRW 2.83 billion in 2024 (up 108.1%), before reaching KRW 67.2 billion in 2025 (up 2,277.3%). Operating margin was 0.3% in 2022, 0.5% in 2023 and 0.9% in 2024, then rose to 17.9% in 2025.

The company attributed the 2025 improvement to higher construction orders that expanded revenue, and to cost savings from shortened work schedules that lifted profit.

Profit attributable to owners of the parent was KRW 3.74 billion in 2022, KRW 1.48 billion in 2023 (down 60.5%) and KRW 736 million in 2024 (down 50.1%), before reaching KRW 48 billion in 2025 (up 6,416.7%). Net profit including non-controlling interests matched the parent figure in each year from 2022 through 2025.

The debt ratio rose from 59.0% in 2022 to 85.5% in 2023 and 120.1% in 2024, then fell to 68.5% in 2025. At the end of 2025, total equity was KRW 156.7 billion, all attributable to owners of the parent, and total liabilities were KRW 107.4 billion.

Operating cash flow was KRW 2.84 billion in 2022, negative KRW 13.9 billion in 2023, then KRW 17 billion in 2024 and KRW 53 billion in 2025.

Quarterly revenue peaked at KRW 93.8 billion in the third quarter of 2025 with operating profit of KRW 26.6 billion, then KRW 93 billion in the fourth quarter (down 0.8% quarter on quarter) with operating profit of KRW 27.4 billion (up 3.0%).

Revenue fell to KRW 60.9 billion in the first quarter of 2026 (down 34.6%) and operating profit to KRW 1.57 billion (down 94.3%). It recovered to KRW 67.4 billion in the second quarter (up 10.7%) with operating profit of KRW 4.03 billion (up 157.7%).

The third quarter of 2026 recorded revenue of KRW 65.9 billion (down 2.2% quarter on quarter and down 29.7% year on year) and an operating loss of KRW 432 million, a swing into loss. Profit attributable to owners of the parent was KRW 175 million, down 99.1% year on year.

Over the last four quarters, from the fourth quarter of 2025 through the third quarter of 2026, profit attributable to owners of the parent totaled KRW 22.9 billion, with KRW 17.4 billion of that in the fourth quarter of 2025.

05

Industry analysis

The company credits its rise in earthworks orders to expanded government investment in social overhead capital, transit investment, and activation of wide-area economic zones. It also cites the government's push to expand social infrastructure, which has increased private capital participation.

Order flow in this structure tracks the procurement timing of rail, wide-area transit and urban subway projects.

Earthworks and tunneling revenue is recognized as work progresses, so quarterly revenue can swing widely, and the move from KRW 60.9 billion in the first quarter of 2026 to KRW 67.4 billion in the second quarter fits that pattern.

On the cycle, rail and road subgrade orders were still being disclosed in 2026, which shows that the order flow that began in 2025 has not stopped.

Listed peers in the same construction and building materials sector include Hyundai Engineering & Construction, Daewoo Engineering & Construction, GS Engineering & Construction, DL E&C and Samsung C&T, all large general contractors.

Woowon, unlike these full-scope builders, focuses on specialty earthworks and tunnels and works under large prime contractors. Its track record includes port breakwater caisson works and river works. Weaknesses include dependence on prime contractors and a structure tied to specific procurement schedules.

Changes in infrastructure budgeting or private-investment timing would affect the order pipeline across the sector.
