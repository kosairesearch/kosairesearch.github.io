Copper Strength Drives a Rebound and a Transformer Push

02

Business structure

Founded in 1987 and listed on KOSDAQ in 1994, KBI Metal is a nonferrous metals and electrical-component manufacturer organized around three segments: metal, electrical components, and wire.

The metal segment is the only domestic company operating both an SCR line using electrolytic copper and a JCR line using copper scrap, supplying copper rod used in wire manufacturing to small and mid-sized wire producers.

The metal segment is understood to hold roughly a 40% share of the JCR market with a stable sales network.

The electrical-components segment produces motor and magnetic cores, BLDC motors for passenger cars, and alternators for medium and heavy commercial vehicles, supplying automotive, appliance, and electronics customers.

The wire segment manufactures and sells power and communication cables through its Vietnamese subsidiary KBI Cosmolink Vina and domestic affiliate KBI Cosmolink, securing long-term order volumes from overseas production facilities.

In 2026, the company acquired transformer maker Wonyoung Hitech, which had gone through court receivership, renamed it KBI Electric, and folded it in as a wholly owned subsidiary, expanding into electrical equipment.

This gives the group a vertically integrated structure spanning copper materials (metal), wire (KBI Cosmolink), and transformers (KBI Electric). The largest shareholder is KBI Tech and related parties, with a combined stake understood to be 31.32% as of May 2026.

04

Earnings analysis

Annual revenue bottomed at KRW 685 billion in 2023 (down 3.6% from KRW 710.5 billion in 2022) before rising for two straight years to KRW 702.7 billion in 2024 (up 2.6%) and KRW 758.6 billion in 2025 (up 8.0%).

Operating profit climbed for three consecutive years, from KRW 5.36 billion in 2022 to KRW 7.82 billion in 2023 (up 45.9%), KRW 17.5 billion in 2024 (up 123.7%), and KRW 24.3 billion in 2025 (up 39.2%), with the operating margin steadily improving from 0.8% to 3.2%.

Controlling-interest net income, however, fell sharply from KRW 1.66 billion in 2022 to KRW 62.63 million in 2023 (down 96.2%), recovered to KRW 810 million in 2024 (up 1,192.5%), then swung to a net loss of KRW 44.22 million in 2025.

Total net income including non-controlling interests stayed positive at KRW 1.38 billion that year, underscoring a gap between the controlling-interest figure and consolidated profit given the weight of subsidiary performance.

By quarter, revenue slid from KRW 201.2 billion in Q2 2025 to KRW 176.3 billion in Q4 2025, then rebounded for two straight quarters to KRW 210.6 billion in Q1 2026 (up 19.4%) and KRW 279.9 billion in Q2 2026 (up 32.9% quarter on quarter and 39.1% year on year).

Operating profit swung from a loss of KRW 1.99 billion in Q2 2025 to a profit of KRW 3.15 billion in Q3, peaked at KRW 18.1 billion in Q4 2025 (up 473.6%), then eased for two straight quarters to KRW 15.5 billion in Q1 2026 (down 14.0%) and KRW 12.9 billion in Q2 2026 (down 17.2% quarter on quarter, swinging to profit year on year).

Controlling-interest net income rose for two straight quarters from KRW 3.55 billion in Q4 2025 to KRW 7.55 billion in Q1 2026 (up 112.6%) and KRW 9.79 billion in Q2 2026 (up 29.7% quarter on quarter and 1,089.0% year on year), bringing the trailing four-quarter total (Q3 2025-Q2 2026) to KRW 16.6 billion.

Operating cash flow was negative KRW 21.1 billion in 2025, following negative KRW 7.12 billion in 2024, marking a second straight year of negative cash flow that highlights a widening gap between reported profit improvement and cash generation.

05

Industry analysis

Copper, KBI Metal's core raw material, has trended sharply higher since mid-2025, exceeding $6.80 per pound in September 2026 to hit record levels.

Citigroup projected the average 2026 LME copper price at $10,500 per ton, rising above $12,000 by year-end, while Goldman Sachs raised its end-2026 copper price forecast to $13,735 per ton.

By contrast, BofA forecast that China's copper demand would grow just 0.5% annually in 2026, the slowest pace since 1988, highlighting demand-side risks.

Copper price forecasts thus reflect a tug-of-war between supply disruptions and AI data center or power grid demand on the bullish side, and a Chinese slowdown or prolonged high interest rates on the bearish side.

Because wire and copper-processing businesses price products in line with copper benchmarks, revenue tends to expand mechanically when raw material prices rise, and this pricing effect appears to account for a meaningful share of KBI Metal's 2025-2026 revenue growth.

Separately, the global transformer market is projected by Fortune Business Insights to grow at roughly 10% annually from 2025 to 2032, driven by aging grid replacement in the United States and Europe and data center buildouts.

Competitively, KBI Metal has built a position in wire-material supply chains on the strength of being the only domestic operator with both SCR and JCR lines, but in the power equipment and transformer space it is a new entrant that will face established manufacturers.

06

Outlook

On August 13, 2026, KBI Metal disclosed that consolidated operating profit for the first half (Q1-Q2) reached KRW 28.3 billion, up 813% year on year, calling it the best first-half result since listing-a figure broadly consistent with the already-disclosed quarterly results (KRW 15.5 billion in Q1 plus KRW 12.9 billion in Q2).

The company attributed the improvement to the sharp rise in international copper prices and to the performance of subsidiaries KBI Cosmolink Vina, in which it holds a 64% stake, and KBI Cosmolink, in which it holds a 50% stake.

On the new-business front, the company acquired transformer maker Wonyoung Hitech for KRW 10.3 billion in April 2026, renamed it KBI Electric, and folded it in as a wholly owned subsidiary, and later issued KRW 40 billion in convertible bonds, part of which is earmarked for building a new KBI Electric plant.

The company has said it expects KBI Electric's revenue to grow from KRW 16.4 billion in 2026 to KRW 95.5 billion by 2028, driven by AI data center expansion and aging grid replacement demand in North America.

KBI Electric is pursuing Korea Electric Power Corporation vendor registration and UL certification for the U.S. market within 2026, and the pace of actual order intake and revenue recognition will likely hinge on when those certifications are secured.

KBI Group Chairman Park Han-sang said the company aims to strengthen its competitiveness in the global power infrastructure market through vertical integration spanning copper materials, wire, and transformers.

At the same time, this expansion plan warrants monitoring for its potential to further strain the balance sheet, given a debt ratio of 175.6% at end-2025, and cash flow.

08

Bull factors

Revenue Leverage from Strong Copper Prices

With copper prices climbing to record levels in 2026, the metal segment, which processes electrolytic copper and scrap, is seeing a structural tailwind to its revenue scale.

Major investment banks including Goldman Sachs and Citigroup have flagged the potential for further copper price increases in 2026, leaving room for processing margins and revenue to expand together. Revenue growth for two straight quarters in Q1-Q2 2026 coincided with this raw-material price upcycle.

Vertical Integration Across the Power-Grid Value Chain

The addition of KBI Electric (formerly Wonyoung Hitech) completes vertical integration from copper materials to wire to transformers, providing a foothold in the power infrastructure market.

The company has said it expects the transformer business to scale up given rising AI data center demand and aging grid replacement in North America. A KRW 40 billion convertible bond issuance to fund a new plant also underpins this expansion strategy.

Consecutive Quarterly Profit Rebound

Controlling-interest net income rose for two consecutive quarters in Q1 and Q2 2026 following a trough in Q4 2025, showing a recovery trend. Q2 2026 controlling-interest net income reached KRW 9.79 billion, up 1,089.0% year on year, a marked improvement from the weak year-ago period. The trailing four-quarter total of KRW 16.6 billion also marks a move away from the 2025 full-year net loss.

09

Bear factors

Sharply Higher Debt Ratio and Negative Cash Flow

The debt ratio jumped from 97.7% in 2024 to 175.6% in 2025, raising financial leverage quickly. Operating cash flow was negative for a second straight year, at negative KRW 21.1 billion in 2025 following negative KRW 7.12 billion in 2024, pointing to a gap between book profit improvement and actual cash generation.

Combined with expansion moves such as the transformer acquisition and capital spending, this could increase the need for additional funding.

High Volatility in Controlling-Interest Earnings

Controlling-interest net income swung between profit and loss from 2022 to 2025 without a clear trend, posting a full-year net loss of KRW 44.22 million in 2025.

A wide gap between total net income including non-controlling interests and the controlling-interest figure means consolidated improvement may not fully translate into the controlling shareholders' share. Quarterly controlling-interest net income also swung between losses and profits, showing high volatility.

Margin Compression Risk if Raw Material Prices Fall

Because revenue and profit are closely tied to rising copper prices, a price correction could shrink both scale and margins simultaneously. BofA has said it expects China's 2026 copper demand growth to be the slowest since 1988, raising the possibility of demand-side softness.

The quarterly volatility seen in the Q3 2025 controlling-interest net loss of KRW 4.33 billion could also recur.

10

Risk factors

Raw Material Price Volatility

A substantial portion of metal segment revenue is tied to copper prices, so a sharp drop or heightened volatility in LME copper prices could directly affect revenue and margins.

Major investment banks' 2026 copper price forecasts also range widely, from the low-$10,000s to the high-$13,000s per ton, leaving uncertainty in place.

Balance Sheet and Liquidity

The 175.6% debt ratio at end-2025 and two consecutive years of negative operating cash flow could increase the need for further borrowing or capital raises. With fundraising such as the KRW 40 billion convertible bond issuance continuing, future interest expense burden and potential dilution also warrant attention.

New Business Execution Risk

If KBI Electric's Korea Electric Power Corporation vendor registration and U.S. UL certification do not proceed as planned, the timeline for expanding transformer business revenue could be delayed.

Integrating a company that went through court receivership also carries the possibility of additional time and cost needed to stabilize operations and production.

12

Overall view

KBI Metal has shown a profit recovery supported by rising copper prices and strong subsidiary performance, evidenced by three consecutive years of operating profit growth through 2024-2025 and a quarterly rebound in the first half of 2026.

At the same time, controlling-interest net income swung to a loss in 2025 and showed high volatility, while the debt ratio rose to 175.6% and operating cash flow stayed negative for a second straight year, adding to financial pressure.

The strategy of vertically integrating the power-grid value chain through the addition of transformer subsidiary KBI Electric could become a growth driver, but execution items such as Korea Electric Power Corporation vendor registration and U.S. UL certification remain in progress.

Copper price forecasts diverge widely even among major investment banks, suggesting raw material price swings are likely to remain a key variable for future results.

Investors will want to watch the upcoming Q3 earnings release, whether KBI Electric secures its certifications, and the trajectory of balance sheet improvement.
