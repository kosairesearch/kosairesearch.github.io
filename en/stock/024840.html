Copper-Linked Earnings Rebound, Grid Expansion Is the Test

02

Business structure

KBI Metal is a wire materials and automotive electronic components maker within the KBI Group, with its Metal division supplying copper materials and its Electronics division supplying parts for automotive, home appliance and electronics applications.

The company says its Metal division holds a stable sales network with about a 40% share of the JCR market. Some products are priced in linkage to copper market prices, so raw material price swings pass directly into revenue.

The Electronics division is growing alongside the automotive, home appliance and electronics industries, and the company is pushing equipment automation to raise productivity and cut costs.

The subsidiary KBI Cosmolink produces electric wire, with KBI Metal holding a 50% stake, and the overseas subsidiary KBI Cosmolink Vina is 64% owned by KBI Metal.

In April 2026 the company took part in a rights offering by Wonyoung Hitech, a domestic transformer maker, acquiring a 100% stake for KRW 10.3 billion and renaming it KBI Electric. The company describes this as completing a grid value chain running from copper materials to electric wire and then to transformers.

On a consolidated basis, the larger contribution from subsidiaries such as KBI Cosmolink is cited as a main reason for the 2026 improvement.

Peers in copper processing and cables include Poongsan and Kiswire, and the KBI Group chairman stressed a positioning as a system solution supplier rather than a single-product maker.

The business has widened from materials and parts toward finished power infrastructure equipment, which is the starting point for reading this company.

04

Earnings analysis

KBI Metal's consolidated revenue went from KRW 710.5 billion in 2022 to KRW 685 billion in 2023, a 3.6% decrease, and then rose to KRW 702.7 billion in 2024, up 2.6%, and KRW 758.6 billion in 2025, up 8.0%.

Revenue increased for two consecutive years from 2024 to 2025, and 2025 was the largest year in the 2022 to 2025 period. Operating profit went from KRW 5.36 billion in 2022 to KRW 7.82 billion in 2023, up 45.9%, KRW 17.5 billion in 2024, up 123.7%, and KRW 24.3 billion in 2025, up 39.2%.

Operating profit increased for three consecutive years from 2023 to 2025, and 2022 was the smallest year. The operating margin moved from 0.8% to 1.1%, 2.5% and 3.2% over the same period, so profitability improved alongside revenue growth.

By contrast, net income attributable to controlling shareholders went from KRW 1.66 billion in 2022 to KRW 62.63 million in 2023, down 96.2%, and KRW 810 million in 2024, up 1,192.5%, before turning into a net loss of KRW 44.22 million in 2025.

Net income for 2025 of KRW 1.38 billion includes non-controlling interests, so it differs from the net loss attributable to controlling shareholders.

The debt-to-equity ratio at the end of 2025 was 175.6%, up from 97.7% at the end of 2024, with total liabilities of KRW 248.1 billion and total equity of KRW 141.2 billion, of which KRW 121.4 billion belongs to controlling shareholders.

Operating cash flow was KRW 8.34 billion in 2022 and KRW 15.6 billion in 2023, but turned negative with an outflow of KRW 7.12 billion in 2024 and KRW 21.1 billion in 2025.

On a quarterly basis, operating profit rose from KRW 3.15 billion in the third quarter of 2025 to KRW 18.1 billion in the fourth quarter, up 473.6% from the prior quarter, and then declined for three consecutive quarters to KRW 15.5 billion in the first quarter of 2026, KRW 12.9 billion in the second and KRW 2.43 billion in the third.

Revenue bottomed at KRW 176.3 billion in the fourth quarter of 2025 and then rose for three consecutive quarters to KRW 210.6 billion, KRW 279.9 billion and KRW 288.3 billion in the third quarter of 2026, so revenue and operating profit moved in opposite directions.

Third-quarter 2026 revenue was up 46.6% from a year earlier, while operating profit fell 22.6% over the same period. The company attributes its 2026 improvement to the pass-through of higher copper prices into selling prices and to stronger results at subsidiaries.

The four most recent quarters total KRW 28.8 billion in net income attributable to controlling shareholders, and the second quarter of 2026 at KRW 9.79 billion was the highest quarterly figure.

05

Industry analysis

Copper is a raw material directly tied to demand for power grids and electric wire. According to the company, the average LME copper cash settlement price in the first half of 2026 was US$13,088 per ton, about 38% above the US$9,432 average for the same period of 2025.

As sales of products priced in linkage to copper rose, the price increase passed quickly into revenue and operating profit. On the power infrastructure side, the spread of AI data centers and the replacement of aging grids are cited by both the company and the press as the demand backdrop.

In May 2026, reports said the wire theme stocks moved strongly from the early session, with expectations for expanded power infrastructure investment reflected across related shares. The company's description of about a 40% share of the JCR market supports the stability of its share-based sales.

However, when copper prices fall, linked selling prices fall too, which can shrink revenue, so the business is strongly exposed to the raw material cycle. Competitors frequently mentioned include Poongsan in copper processing and Kiswire in electric wire.

The company stresses its positioning as a system solution supplier rather than a single-product maker, and says it aims to differentiate by offering copper materials through transformers as one package.

Readers can separate the fact that copper prices rose sharply from the year-earlier level from the longer-term outlook for power demand.

06

Outlook

The direction the company has set out is to expand production capacity through capital expenditure and to strengthen technology and quality competitiveness. A company official said it will work to achieve record annual results based on the grid value chain.

In its first-half announcement, the company said it would strengthen its push into overseas markets.

Since the Wonyoung Hitech acquisition, the business has been in a restructuring process that has now concluded, and capital spending is under way, so how the capacity expansion at KBI Electric feeds into consolidated results is the next checkpoint.

Kyobo Securities estimated 2026 consolidated operating profit at KRW 27.7 billion in a report carried in May 2026. Cumulative operating profit of KRW 30.8 billion for the first three quarters of 2026 is above that estimate, and such estimates can change with later results.

Because third-quarter operating profit fell sharply from the prior quarter, how selling price pass-through and cost trends evolve from the fourth quarter onward matters. Whether copper prices stay at elevated levels also feeds directly into selling prices and is a variable for the revenue outlook.

The annual record-profit goal the company has cited will be tested against the actual figures in the 2026 annual report, which will be the first regular report to show full-year results alongside the quarterly trend.

08

Bull factors

Earnings leverage from copper-linked pricing

The average LME copper cash settlement price in the first half of 2026 was about 38% above the year-earlier level, and the company attributes its profit recovery mainly to pass-through of higher selling prices.

Cumulative operating profit for January to September 2026 reached KRW 30.8 billion, above the full-year 2025 operating profit of KRW 24.3 billion. The company says the cumulative operating margin rose to about 4% from 1% a year earlier.

Vertical integration across the grid value chain

The company says it has assembled a chain running from copper materials in its Metal division, to electric wire at KBI Cosmolink, to transformers at KBI Electric.

It entered the transformer business by acquiring Wonyoung Hitech for KRW 10.3 billion in April 2026, and it cites AI data center expansion and grid replacement demand as the basis for growth. KBI Group chairman Park Han-sang said the group aims to secure a differentiated position as a system solution supplier.

A turn in earnings capacity

Net income attributable to controlling shareholders swung to a net loss of KRW 44.22 million for full-year 2025, while the four most recent quarters total KRW 28.8 billion. The second quarter of 2026 delivered KRW 9.79 billion, the largest quarterly figure in that span. The company says stronger results at subsidiaries such as KBI Cosmolink contributed to consolidated profit.

Bear factors

Sharp swings in quarterly profitability

Third-quarter 2026 revenue rose 3.0% from the prior quarter to KRW 288.3 billion, but operating profit fell 81.1% to KRW 2.43 billion. Operating profit has declined for three consecutive quarters since peaking at KRW 18.1 billion in the fourth quarter of 2025.

Because profit did not follow revenue higher in this stretch, the stability of quarterly profitability is cited as a bearish factor.

Balance sheet strain and a gap in cash flow

The debt-to-equity ratio rose to 175.6% at the end of 2025 from 97.7% at the end of 2024, with total liabilities of KRW 248.1 billion. Operating cash flow was negative, with an outflow of KRW 7.12 billion in 2024 and KRW 21.1 billion in 2025. The gap between reported profit and operating cash flow is a bearish factor.

Copper price exposure and a lag in capital spending

Some products are priced in linkage to copper market prices, so a fall in copper prices would reduce both revenue and profit. KBI Electric, acquired in 2026, is still carrying out capital spending after its restructuring process concluded, so there is a lag before capacity expansion shows up in operating results. Until that spending is complete, the benefit of the expansion is unlikely to appear fully in annual figures.

10

Risk factors

Raw material prices

Because revenue and selling prices are linked to copper prices, moves in copper directly affect results. Just as the 2026 price rise lifted earnings, a decline would work the other way and could reduce revenue and operating profit. The timing gap between raw material costs and selling price pass-through can also drive quarterly volatility.

Balance sheet and cash flow

At the end of 2025 the debt-to-equity ratio was 175.6%, with total liabilities of KRW 248.1 billion against total equity of KRW 141.2 billion. Operating cash flow was negative in both 2024 and 2025.

If debt repayment needs and working capital growth coincide, financial flexibility could be constrained even as profits rise.

Execution risk in expansion

The company entered transformers by acquiring Wonyoung Hitech for KRW 10.3 billion in April 2026 and is now carrying out capital spending after the restructuring process concluded. Synergies across the grid chain depend on completed capacity and order wins, so delays in execution would push back the expected benefits.

Running copper, wire and transformers together also leaves the challenge of managing profitability in each segment.

Overall view

KBI Metal's cumulative operating profit for January to September 2026 exceeded its full-year 2025 operating profit as copper prices rose and subsidiaries performed well, a clear increase in earnings scale.

Net income attributable to controlling shareholders moved from a full-year 2025 loss to KRW 28.8 billion across the four most recent quarters, which changes the earnings base.

However, third-quarter operating profit fell to KRW 2.43 billion and revenue growth did not carry through to profit, which shows quarterly profitability can swing sharply with selling price pass-through and cost trends.

On the financial side, the debt-to-equity ratio rose to 175.6% at the end of 2025, and operating cash flow was negative in 2024 and 2025, leaving converting profit into cash as an open issue.

The grid value chain is a growth axis aimed at AI data center and grid replacement demand, but the effect of capital spending has not yet appeared in full-year regular report figures.

Bullish factors are copper-linked selling prices, share-based sales and vertical integration, while bearish factors are quarterly profit volatility, debt burden and negative cash flow. The next checkpoint is how fourth-quarter operating profit and cash flow appear in the 2026 annual report.
