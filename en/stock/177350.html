Business structure

Vessel runs its business through two divisions. Its Materials division makes high-performance films, while its System division supplies equipment used in LCD, OLED and touch-panel manufacturing.

In the Materials business, tighter environmental regulation has lifted demand for eco-solvent media, and the company says its global partnership with 3M is widening its export markets, according to its cumulative results summary for the third quarter of 2025.

The System business, by contrast, saw sales decline as investment in downstream display industries contracted, and the company continues to focus on high-performance film development.

In the first-quarter 2026 results, the film materials segment accounted for most of total revenue, and within it exports were a larger share than domestic sales. The display segment added modest revenue from parts sales and retrofits, along with rental income.

At the regular shareholders' meeting on March 31, 2026, five inside directors and one auditor were elected, and three of the inside directors were newly appointed. Films are used across advertising and printing applications, so the customer base of the Materials business is not confined to display makers.

System equipment, on the other hand, moves directly with the capital spending of display panel makers. The fact that the two businesses are driven by different demand sources is the starting point for reading this company's structure.

Earnings analysis

Annual revenue fell from KRW 44.1 billion in 2022 to KRW 26.1 billion in 2023, a 40.8% decline year on year, then to KRW 25.7 billion in 2024 (down 1.6%) and KRW 16.6 billion in 2025 (down 35.5%).

Revenue has declined for three consecutive years from 2023 through 2025, with 2022 the largest year and 2025 the smallest in the 2022–2025 window. The operating loss widened from KRW 6.58 billion in 2022 to KRW 13.1 billion in 2023, then narrowed to KRW 11.7 billion in 2024 and KRW 2.61 billion in 2025.

Operating margins were -14.9% in 2022, -50.2% in 2023, -45.6% in 2024 and -15.8% in 2025, and the company has been loss-making for four consecutive years from 2022 through 2025. The steepest loss came in 2023, when revenue was falling sharply, which points to a fixed-cost burden.

Net loss attributable to controlling shareholders was KRW 12.1 billion in 2022, KRW 24 billion in 2023 and KRW 10.5 billion in 2024, before turning to a net profit of KRW 17.7 billion in 2025.

Because the 2025 operating loss was only KRW 2.61 billion, the gap to that profit came from non-operating items, and whether those items were one-off or recurring is a point to verify in the notes to the next regular report.

Net income including non-controlling interests was also KRW 17.7 billion in 2025, the same figure as net income attributable to controlling shareholders.

Operating cash flow moved from negative KRW 5.55 billion in 2022, negative KRW 6.65 billion in 2023 and negative KRW 7.96 billion in 2024 to positive KRW 752 million in 2025.

The debt ratio fell from 175.6% in 2022 to 41.3% in 2025, with total liabilities of KRW 17.1 billion and total equity of KRW 41.3 billion at the end of 2025.

On a quarterly basis, revenue was KRW 3.54 billion in the third quarter of 2025, KRW 4.22 billion in the fourth quarter (up 19.4% from the prior quarter), KRW 3.88 billion in the first quarter of 2026 (down 8.0%), KRW 5.01 billion in the second quarter (up 29.1%) and KRW 4.16 billion in the third quarter (down 17.0%).

The second-quarter 2026 figure is the highest of the last five quarters on the disclosed basis.

Operating results ran from a loss of KRW 121 million in the third quarter of 2025, to a loss of KRW 573 million in the fourth quarter, a loss of KRW 136 million in the first quarter of 2026 and a loss of KRW 617 million in the second quarter, before an operating profit of KRW 887 million in the third quarter of 2026, a turn to profit that came with revenue up 17.6% year on year.

Net loss attributable to controlling shareholders was KRW 1.19 billion in the first quarter of 2026, KRW 1.83 billion in the second quarter and KRW 571 million in the third quarter, three consecutive loss quarters.

Operating profit and a controlling-shareholder loss appearing together in the same quarter means costs and taxes below the operating line will be a key item to check in the next regular report.

Industry analysis

Vessel's two businesses are exposed to different demand drivers, one tied to capital spending by downstream display makers and the other to environmental regulation.

The System business's revenue moves with equipment investment in LCD, OLED and touch-panel production, and the cumulative summary for the third quarter of 2025 cited contracting downstream investment as a cause of lower sales.

Demand for display equipment tends to cluster around panel makers' capacity expansions and generation transitions, so judging where the cycle stands requires tracking panel makers' capital spending plans.

In the Materials business, stricter environmental rules have increased demand for eco-solvent media, a supportive factor. Whether that demand converts into higher revenue depends on how fast export markets and overseas partnerships expand.

Export share in film materials was higher than domestic share in the first quarter of 2026, which shows a heavy reliance on overseas demand. When the two business lines move at different times, the degree to which one offsets weakness in the other shapes quarterly volatility.

Revenue rising to KRW 5.01 billion in the second quarter of 2026 and then easing to KRW 4.16 billion in the third quarter can be read as a reflection of this mixed structure. The most important industry question is whether regulation-driven demand in Materials and capex-driven demand in System recover together.

Outlook

Vessel's near-term challenges combine a recovery in earnings with meeting the market-cap threshold. Korea Exchange designated its common stock as a managed stock on October 20, 2026 over a market-cap shortfall.

The exchange explained that the designation is lifted the next day if the market cap stays at or above the threshold for 45 trading days within 90 trading days after designation.

In the same notice, the exchange said delisting procedures are deferred in light of a court ruling on October 2, 2026 in a provisional injunction case related to market-cap-based delisting.

On October 21, 2026 the stock was also briefly designated as an investment-caution stock for one day after meeting closing-price volatility criteria.

The company carried out a share consolidation in July 2026, changing the par value from KRW 500 to KRW 1,000, with the new shares effective July 28, 2026 and listing scheduled for August 18, 2026. The company cited stabilizing the share price and enhancing corporate value as the purpose.

A treasury-share acquisition trust contract was signed on June 23, 2026 and terminated on September 9, 2026. On the convertible-bond side, the company acquired KRW 1 billion of face value of the 10th series before maturity on November 3, 2026, reducing the outstanding face value of that series to KRW 7 billion.

The next checkpoint is whether the operating profit trend continues on an annual basis in the 2026 annual report, and whether net income attributable to controlling shareholders turns positive.
