From Printer Parts to Optoelectronics: Restructuring Amid Losses

Pakers returned to a net profit attributable to controlling shareholders in 2025 while its operating loss widened, and it has recorded five consecutive quarters of operating losses through the third quarter of 2026, so whether its business restructuring shows up in earnings is the key point to track.

02

Business structure

Pakers is a manufacturer of printer parts and lighting equipment. Its printer business has been reorganized around laser printer and multifunction device components, and in an August 27, 2026 announcement the company set out a plan to shift from simple assembly toward a solutions business.

The LED business is expanding into IoT lighting, LED light sources and medical devices. On the cosmetics side, its K-beauty subsidiary is pushing into global markets and plans to widen its offline reach with a pop-up store in Los Angeles, moving beyond an online-centered channel.

The optoelectronics business began with the acquisition of a 58.53% stake in an optical-semiconductor subsidiary, into which a total of KRW 5.0 billion was invested including asset transfers and cash.

The subsidiary holds 51 patents in Korea and abroad and is widening its portfolio from optical semiconductor devices to packaging, sensors and application products.

The company intends to combine its LED manufacturing capabilities with this subsidiary's technology to link product development, mass production and market supply. Printer-part demand tracks the shipment flow of finished printers.

Although classified under chemicals, the company differs from large petrochemical groups in being a small and mid-sized manufacturer centered on parts and application products.

04

Earnings analysis

Based on the disclosed annual figures, revenue moved from KRW 73.1 billion in 2022 to KRW 63.6 billion in 2023 (down 13.0% year on year), KRW 63.7 billion in 2024 (up 0.1%), and KRW 53.5 billion in 2025 (down 16.0%). Within 2022 to 2025, 2022 was the largest year and 2025 the smallest.

The operating loss narrowed from KRW 15.2 billion in 2022 to KRW 9.54 billion in 2023 and KRW 1.43 billion in 2024, then widened again to KRW 12.7 billion in 2025. The company has posted operating losses in each of the four years from 2022 through 2025.

The operating margin was -20.8% in 2022, -15.0% in 2023, -2.2% in 2024 and -23.8% in 2025, so it deteriorated sharply in 2025 after the 2024 improvement.

Net loss attributable to controlling shareholders of KRW 9.6 billion in 2022 and KRW 12.6 billion in 2023 narrowed to KRW 2.16 billion in 2024 before turning into a KRW 6.7 billion profit in 2025.

Net income including non-controlling interests was KRW 4.9 billion in 2025, and a profit alongside an operating loss of KRW 12.7 billion means items outside operating profit changed the direction of net earnings. The specific items can be checked in the notes to the annual report.

Quarterly, the operating loss widened from KRW 919 million in the third quarter of 2025 to KRW 2.92 billion in the fourth quarter, narrowed to KRW 87.66 million in the first quarter of 2026, and then widened again to KRW 644 million in the second quarter and KRW 1.37 billion in the third.

Revenue fell 10.4% quarter on quarter to KRW 11.7 billion in the first quarter of 2026 and then rose to KRW 13.3 billion and KRW 15.4 billion, marking two straight quarters of growth.

Per a Vegan News report, cumulative operating losses for January to September 2026 shrank sharply from the prior-year period, but on a quarterly basis revenue growth has not translated into better profit.

Net income attributable to controlling shareholders was KRW 1.25 billion in the first quarter of 2026 and KRW 2.19 billion in the second, before a net loss of KRW 4.1 billion in the third.

05

Industry analysis

The printer-parts business is shifting toward a solutions business as demand for printers declines. Weak printer demand and cost pressure were cited as reasons for poor results in the first half of 2025.

In optoelectronics, the company plans to expand from UVC-LED into next-generation displays, optical sensors and optical communications. Its optical-semiconductor subsidiary has named automotive and high-end lighting, AR and VR smart glasses, next-generation headlamps and CPO for AI data centers as expansion targets.

In K-beauty, a Edaily report described the US beauty market as a KRW 143 trillion market and noted that the US has emerged as the largest export market for Korean beauty products. In LED and optical semiconductors the company competes with established LED and optical-semiconductor makers.

Within the chemicals classification, large petrochemical and materials groups move with raw-material and price cycles, whereas Pakers is centered on printer parts and application products with a different demand cycle.

Its results are therefore better read against printer demand and the launch timing of optoelectronic products than against sector averages.

06

Outlook

The company said on August 27, 2026 that printer solutions, a K-beauty platform and an optoelectronics platform are its three growth drivers.

In printer solutions it aims to enter higher-value markets, and a company official said it will strengthen competitiveness in the new growth pillars of K-beauty and optoelectronics.

In optoelectronics, the subsidiary launched a UVC-LED product in 2026 and plans to roll out micro LED, optical sensor and other optical-semiconductor products in sequence from 2027.

Over the medium to long term, the plan extends to automotive and high-end lighting, AR and VR smart glasses, next-generation headlamps and CPO for AI data centers. The K-beauty subsidiary has announced a pop-up store in Los Angeles to widen offline customer contact in the United States.

On the earnings side, the operating loss widened in the second and third quarters even as revenue grew, which is the point to watch. Whether the revenue from the new businesses will feed into quarterly profit has not yet been shown in the disclosed results. The operating margin in the next regular report is the next checkpoint.

08

Bull factors

Three-pillar restructuring

The company said on August 27, 2026 that it had reorganized its portfolio around three growth engines: printer solutions, a K-beauty platform and optoelectronics. In printers it plans to move from simple assembly toward solutions, and its K-beauty subsidiary is pushing into the US market. Reusing printer and LED capabilities in new fields is cited as the main bullish argument.

Optical-semiconductor technology and product roadmap

After acquiring a 58.53% stake and consolidating an optical-semiconductor subsidiary, the company has moved its optoelectronics business forward. The subsidiary holds 51 patents in Korea and abroad, launched a UVC-LED product in 2026, and plans to roll out micro LED and optical sensor products in sequence from 2027.

Pairing this with the existing LED manufacturing capability, from design through mass production, is cited as a strength.

Revenue recovery and a smaller cumulative loss

Revenue bottomed at KRW 11.7 billion in the first quarter of 2026 and then rose to KRW 13.3 billion and KRW 15.4 billion, two straight quarters of growth, with third-quarter revenue up 22.4% year on year. Net income attributable to controlling shareholders was KRW 2.19 billion in the second quarter of 2026.

Vegan News reported that cumulative operating losses for January to September 2026 shrank sharply from the prior-year period.

Bear factors

Five consecutive quarters of operating losses

The company has posted five consecutive quarters of operating losses from the third quarter of 2025 through the third quarter of 2026. In the third quarter of 2026 the operating loss widened to KRW 1.37 billion from the prior-year period even as revenue rose. Revenue growth not yet translating into profit improvement is the main bearish point.

2025 revenue decline and wider operating loss

Revenue for 2025 fell 16.0% year on year to KRW 53.5 billion, and the operating loss widened to KRW 12.7 billion. The operating margin deteriorated to -23.8% from -2.2% in 2024.

Because the 2025 profit attributable to controlling shareholders reflects items outside operating profit, it is difficult to read as a recovery in the core business.

Weak printer demand and new-business spending

Weak printer demand and cost pressure were cited for poor results in the first half of 2025. A total of KRW 5.0 billion was invested in the optical-semiconductor subsidiary, including asset transfers and cash.

The net loss attributable to controlling shareholders reached KRW 4.1 billion in the third quarter of 2026, wider than a year earlier, so the new businesses may take time to show up in profit.

Risk factors

Demand concentration risk

The printer-parts business faces declining demand, and the company is responding by shifting toward solutions. If that shift does not move the business from simple assembly into higher-value markets, revenue declines could continue.

How much the 16.0% revenue drop in 2025 relates to these demand conditions is a point the business description in the annual report should clarify.

Execution and integration risk in new businesses

The optoelectronics business depends on product launch and mass-production schedules after the subsidiary was consolidated. Micro LED and optical sensor products are scheduled to roll out in sequence from 2027, so any delay would postpone their contribution to earnings.

Changes in the consolidated scope also alter the profit structure and need to be considered when interpreting results.

Continued losses and cash generation risk

Net losses attributable to controlling shareholders over the last four quarters total KRW 2.58 billion, and a prolonged loss streak could erode financial capacity.

Total equity at the end of 2025 was KRW 50.6 billion and the debt ratio fell to 60.7%, but operating cash flow was negative in three of the four years from 2022 to 2025. Whether the core business generates cash is the next point to check.

12

Overall view

Pakers is restructuring its business, moving weight from printer parts and LED toward optoelectronics and K-beauty, with steps taken in August and September 2026.

The 2025 profit attributable to controlling shareholders of KRW 6.7 billion marked a turn to profit, but the operating loss widened to KRW 12.7 billion, and five consecutive quarters of operating losses followed through the third quarter of 2026.

Third-quarter 2026 revenue rose 22.4% year on year to KRW 15.4 billion, yet the operating loss widened to KRW 1.37 billion. Bullish factors include the revenue recovery, the lower debt ratio, and the patent base and product roadmap in optical semiconductors.

Bearish factors include continued operating losses, weakening printer demand, and uncertainty over commercialization timing for the new businesses. The weight of these factors is not yet visible in the disclosed results.

The next checkpoints are the operating margin in the 2026 annual report and the contribution from the subsidiary. The success of the restructuring can be judged when the 2027 product launch schedule and the direction of quarterly profit line up.
