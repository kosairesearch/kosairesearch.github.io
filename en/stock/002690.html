02

Business structure

Dong IL Steel MFG was founded in 1959 as a wire specialist and is headquartered in Anseong, Gyeonggi Province.

Its main products are PC steel wire, PC strand, hard steel wire, piano wire, galvanized steel wire and bar wire, supplied to domestic and overseas customers as core components for construction, civil engineering and machinery.

The business is split into a steel wire and bar segment and an aluminum lightweight materials segment, with the steel side centered on construction wire and bar. Commentary on first-half 2026 results attributed the improved profitability of the bar wire unit to higher volumes of high-value items and a larger market.

The aluminum rod unit posted higher sales and turned operating profit positive in the first half of 2026 on rising demand from aluminum forging. The aluminum business targets demand for automotive lightweighting.

The company says it secures the high-price segment with high-quality products and aims to take market leadership in high value-added items. In wire, its market share was 14.4% in 2023 according to its annual report, down from 16.2% in 2021 and 14.8% in 2022. Kiswire held a higher 29.8% share in the same table.

Its customers are construction, civil engineering and machinery buyers, so construction activity drives the direction of wire demand.

04

Earnings analysis

Annual revenue went from KRW 196.8 billion in 2022 to KRW 180.8 billion in 2023 (down 8.1% year on year), KRW 151.1 billion in 2024 (down 16.4%), and KRW 147.3 billion in 2025 (down 2.5%).

Revenue fell for three consecutive years from 2023 through 2025, with 2022 the largest year and 2025 the smallest in the 2022-2025 period.

Operating profit of KRW 2.46 billion in 2022 became an operating loss of KRW 2.91 billion in 2023, a turn to deficit from the prior year, and the loss widened to KRW 5.8 billion in 2024.

The 2025 operating loss of KRW 3.51 billion was narrower, but operating losses have now run for three consecutive years from 2023 through 2025. The operating margin moved from 1.2% in 2022 to -1.6% in 2023, -3.8% in 2024 and -2.4% in 2025.

Net profit attributable to controlling shareholders fell from KRW 335 million in 2022 to a net loss of KRW 3.16 billion in 2023, a net loss of KRW 484 million in 2024, and then returned to a profit of KRW 1.35 billion in 2025.

Pre-tax income of KRW 1.59 billion in 2025 exceeded the operating loss, which suggests non-operating items offset the operating deficit. The company attributed 2025 changes mainly to lower sales volumes from weak steel demand, intensified price competition and higher cost burdens.

By quarter, the third quarter of 2025 showed revenue of KRW 36.9 billion, an operating loss of KRW 441 million and profit attributable to controlling shareholders of KRW 5.2 billion.

The fourth quarter showed revenue of KRW 31.2 billion (down 15.5% from the prior quarter), an operating loss of KRW 2.77 billion and a net loss attributable to controlling shareholders of KRW 5.76 billion, so the full-year 2025 profit leaned heavily on the third quarter.

In 2026, revenue was KRW 38.6 billion with an operating profit of KRW 1.44 million in the first quarter, KRW 41.9 billion with KRW 394 million in the second quarter, and KRW 39 billion with an operating loss of KRW 317 million in the third quarter, so the operating loss narrowed.

Net profit attributable to controlling shareholders was KRW 663 million, KRW 686 million and KRW 329 million in the three quarters, with the third quarter down 93.7% year on year.

The last four quarters, from the fourth quarter of 2025 through the third quarter of 2026, add up to a net loss attributable to controlling shareholders of KRW 4.08 billion.

Operating cash flow moved from negative KRW 6.36 billion in 2022 to KRW 12.9 billion in 2023, KRW 3.08 billion in 2024 and KRW 728 million in 2025, staying positive since 2023 but on a declining trend.

05

Industry analysis

Domestic steel demand has weakened as the construction downturn has dragged on. According to the Korea Iron and Steel Association, domestic rebar demand in 2025 was 6.754 million tons, down 13.9% from the prior year, and demand in January through September 2026 was 4.8 million tons, down 5.1% year on year.

Rebar is not a core product for Dong IL Steel MFG, but it is a useful indicator of construction-related steel demand. Compared with the 11.25 million tons peak in 2021, demand has fallen by roughly 4.5 million tons.

Hyundai Steel Company moved in early 2026 to close a small rolling line at its Incheon plant, and Dongkuk Steel Mill has reduced operating days at its Incheon plant to adjust supply. Daehan Steel disclosed that it will halt the rolling line at its Sinpyeong plant on December 31, 2026.

Kisco has also reportedly joined the production cuts. In wire, Kiswire held a 29.8% share in 2023, above Dong IL Steel MFG's 14.4%.

A 2025 report noted that Dong IL Steel MFG's export share is not large, which may limit the direct impact of Chinese oversupply, but said it would be hard to avoid the effects of heightened domestic and external uncertainty.

Automotive lightweighting demand, tied to the expansion of electric vehicles, is cited as a growth driver for the aluminum rod business.

Aluminum forging demand for the rod business

The aluminum rod unit posted higher revenue and turned operating profit positive in the first half of 2026 on rising demand from aluminum forging, according to results commentary. Automotive lightweighting linked to electric vehicle growth is cited as a growth driver.

Whether this lightweight materials business can support results while rebar and wire demand remains weak is the point to watch.

Low leverage and positive operating cash flow

Risk factors

Construction demand and production cuts

Domestic rebar demand fell 13.9% in 2025 and another 5.1% in January through September 2026, while Hyundai Steel Company, Dongkuk Steel Mill and Daehan Steel moved to cut output. Wire is tied to construction and civil engineering demand, so a prolonged downturn could delay a revenue recovery. Production cuts across mid-sized mills also point to a broader utilization problem.

Cost pressure and price competition

The company cited lower sales volumes from weak steel demand, intensifying price competition and higher cost burdens as the drivers of 2025 results. If cost pressure stays high, operating margin improvement could be limited even if revenue grows. The 2025 operating margin of -2.4% remains negative.

Weakening position in wire

According to its 2023 annual report, the company's wire market share was 14.4%, down from 16.2% in 2021 and 14.8% in 2022. Kiswire recorded 29.8% in the same table. If the share keeps falling, the company could face disadvantages in scale and in price negotiations.
