02

Business structure

Egtronics is a KOSDAQ-listed maker of electric motors, generators and electric conversion, supply and control equipment, and its business falls into three lines. The first is DC/DC converters for electric and hydrogen buses, where the company says it holds the top share of the domestic market.

The second is the E-Fast EV charger series (11kW to 400kW), for which the company has completed UL-standard design work and is pursuing North American market entry. The third is defense power supplies, such as AC/DC converters for small tactical vehicles built to military-standard harsh-environment specifications.

In defense, the company previously supplied converters for unmanned vehicles to Estonia's Milrem Robotics, and it describes military vehicle electrification as a segment with long-term volumes secured through 2030. Listed customers include Hyundai Motor, SK Telecom and Milrem Robotics.

Overseas, the US subsidiary EG TRONICS INC in Garland, Texas handles North American production and sales, and the company has joint ventures in China and India. The Indian joint venture is 51% held by the Motherson Group and 49% by Egtronics, with the company contributing KRW 2.7 billion.

In the first half of 2025, the KRW 13.8 billion investment in the Chinese joint venture and the cost of building new plants weighed on results. The company says a subsidiary sells electric commercial trucks.

Its stated strengths are its domestic bus DC/DC share and more than ten years of defense quality verification for its defense products.

04

Earnings analysis

On reported results, revenue rose from KRW 21.6 billion in 2022 to KRW 35.2 billion in 2023 (up 62.9%) and KRW 36.5 billion in 2024 (up 3.7%), making 2024 the largest year in the period. Revenue then fell to KRW 13.2 billion in 2025 (down 63.7%), the smallest year.

Over the same span the operating loss went from KRW 2.98 billion in 2022 to KRW 3.96 billion in 2023, KRW 4.02 billion in 2024, and KRW 18.6 billion in 2025. The operating margin moved from -13.8% in 2022 and -11.3% in 2023 to -11.0% in 2024 and -140.7% in 2025.

The controlling-shareholder net loss grew from KRW 2.31 billion in 2022 to KRW 12.4 billion in 2025, and the net loss including non-controlling interests reached KRW 15.6 billion in 2025.

The company attributed the 2025 decline to lower demand and a weaker market in its downstream industries, along with equity-method losses from its overseas subsidiary investments. Quarterly, the third quarter of 2025 showed revenue of KRW 3.22 billion and an operating loss of KRW 3.14 billion.

The fourth quarter then reached KRW 4.52 billion in revenue (up 40.3% quarter on quarter) with a KRW 2.42 billion operating loss. The first quarter of 2026 was the low point, with KRW 1.42 billion in revenue (down 68.5% quarter on quarter) and a KRW 3.46 billion operating loss.

Revenue then rose in the second quarter to KRW 3.48 billion (up 144.9%) and in the third quarter to KRW 6.39 billion, with the operating loss narrowing to KRW 1.24 billion. Operating cash flow was KRW 10.5 billion positive in 2024 but turned to an outflow of KRW 17.1 billion in 2025. The debt ratio rose from 15.3% in 2022 to 46.1% in 2025 as losses accumulated.

05

Industry analysis

Downstream demand comes from electric and hydrogen buses, EV charging, and electrification of defense vehicles.

The company cited lower demand and a weaker market in its downstream industries as reasons for the 2025 revenue decline, and it also pointed to lower sales of rectifiers for telecom equipment and uncertainty in the EV market. This suggests EV-related demand remains in a volatile phase.

In August 2025 a company official said the Chinese subsidiary's EV parts project would enter mass production from 2026, and that profitability could improve more if Chinese EV demand expands and the local supply chain stabilizes. In defense, military vehicle electrification is cited as the basis for medium-term demand.

In July 2026 a report said the company would supply power conversion systems to a domestic autonomous driving company. In August and September 2026, articles on used-battery reuse mentioned the company, but these are market-theme articles rather than company disclosures.

Its competitive position rests on the top share of the domestic bus DC/DC market and its defense quality verification record. EV charging is at the North American entry stage, so its share of results appears small for now.

Bear factors

Long record of losses and widening deficits

The company has posted losses for four consecutive years from 2022 to 2025. The 2025 operating loss was KRW 18.6 billion, with an operating margin of -140.7%. Losses have continued for five consecutive quarters through the third quarter of 2026.

The combined controlling-shareholder net loss for the last four quarters is KRW 8.62 billion. The bear case rests on the fact that no annual profit has been recorded since listing.

Financial strain and weaker cash flow

The debt ratio rose from 15.3% in 2022 to 46.1% in 2025. Total liabilities at the end of 2025 were KRW 18.9 billion. Operating cash flow turned to an outflow of KRW 17.1 billion in 2025.

Reports also noted that cash and equivalents at the end of the second quarter of 2025 had fallen to about half of the prior quarter's level.

Revenue volatility and overseas investment burden

Revenue fell 63.7% in 2025 and then dropped 68.5% quarter on quarter in the first quarter of 2026. The company invested KRW 13.8 billion in the Chinese joint venture and contributed KRW 2.7 billion to the Indian one. Equity-method losses from overseas investments were cited as a drag on 2025 results. With a thin revenue base, the overseas investment burden is a weakness.

Risk factors

Demand and industry cycle

The company cited lower demand and a weaker market downstream as causes of the 2025 revenue decline. Lower rectifier sales for telecom equipment and EV market uncertainty were also mentioned. If demand for EV and bus power conversion does not recover, the revenue base could weaken again. Quarterly swings are large, as seen in the first quarter of 2026, when revenue fell 68.5% quarter on quarter.

Overseas investment and equity-method results

The KRW 13.8 billion Chinese joint venture investment and the costs of new plant construction and operations weighed heavily on first-half 2025 results. Equity-method losses from overseas investments were cited among the causes of the 2025 decline.

If the China and India businesses do not convert into sales as expected, losses could recur. A delay in when overseas operations show up in profit is also a risk.

Management change and funding capacity

After an extraordinary shareholders' meeting in June 2026 and a CEO change, Min-kyung Kang leads the company. Research head Chan-ho Kang disclosed open-market purchases from March to April 2026, and his stake was 39.75% as of April 30, 2026. Total equity at the end of 2025 was KRW 41.1 billion.

If operating cash outflows require new funding while the company is loss-making, dilution or financing strain could follow.

Overall view

Egtronics' reported results show operating losses in each year from 2022 through 2025, and 2025 was the weakest year with revenue of KRW 13.2 billion and an operating loss of KRW 18.6 billion.

In 2026, third-quarter revenue rose 98.3% year on year to KRW 6.39 billion and the operating loss narrowed to KRW 1.24 billion, but the company has posted losses for five consecutive quarters.

Positive factors include preparation for defense power converter orders, reports of mass-supply of unmanned vehicle converters, and the start of local vehicle production at the Chinese subsidiary.

Negative factors are the long loss record, the rise in the debt ratio to 46.1% in 2025, and continued operating cash outflows. Year-end 2025 total equity of KRW 41.1 billion and controlling-shareholder equity of KRW 38.2 billion provide some buffer, but that buffer narrows unless earnings turn.

Because the company is small by market value, its share price has moved sharply on limited volume, as in the October 2026 session. The next checkpoints are defense order disclosures, operating profit and cash flow in the 2026 annual report, and whether losses continue in the first-quarter 2027 report.
