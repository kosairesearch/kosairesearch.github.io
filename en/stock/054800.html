02

Business structure

IDIS Holdings, founded in September 1997, is a holding company that conducts video equipment development, manufacturing and sales, electronic components and industrial monitor businesses through its subsidiaries.

Its core subsidiaries are IDIS, which makes DVR and NVR video recorders; Kortek, an industrial monitor maker; Bixolon, which makes mini and POS printers; and IDP, a card printer maker, while a separate affiliate runs public frequency radio communications.

A company-information portal classifies the holding company's standalone income as 77.33% rental income, 21.82% service income and 0.85% other, a split that differs from the consolidated view. Because consolidated sales include these subsidiaries, group revenue tracks subsidiary demand.

IDIS reported 2025 consolidated sales of KRW 327.6 billion, operating profit of KRW 21.2 billion and net income of KRW 23.3 billion. IDIS Holdings is the de facto controlling shareholder of IDIS, holding 48.63% directly as of October 29, 2026, or 60.09% including related parties.

Between September 14 and October 27, 2026, it made 28 open-market purchases of IDIS shares totaling KRW 638 million. In March 2026 IDP exited the IDP Japan group, and in May 2026 IDIS established a Japanese unit, IDIS Japan Co., Ltd. In June 2024 Bixolon set up a Mexican subsidiary, widening its overseas footprint. The video security equipment market is contested by global suppliers on both price and technology.

04

Earnings analysis

Revenue fell 14.4% from KRW 874.1 billion in 2022 to KRW 748.6 billion in 2023, then rose 4.4% to KRW 781.4 billion in 2024 and 16.2% to KRW 908.1 billion in 2025, which is the largest year in the period.

Operating profit dropped 52.6% from KRW 94.3 billion in 2022 to KRW 44.7 billion in 2023, then rose 41.2% to KRW 63.1 billion in 2024 and 42.1% to KRW 89.7 billion in 2025. Operating margin moved from 10.8% in 2022 to 6.0% in 2023, then recovered to 8.1% in 2024 and 9.9% in 2025.

Net income attributable to controlling shareholders was KRW 38 billion in 2022, KRW 20.9 billion in 2023 (down 45.0%), KRW 28.5 billion in 2024 and KRW 39.1 billion in 2025, the largest year in the period. Total net income in 2025 was KRW 78 billion, so the gap reflects the share belonging to non-controlling interests.

In the fourth quarter of 2025, revenue reached KRW 260.5 billion (up 15.4% from the prior quarter), operating profit KRW 30.7 billion (up 59.5%) and controlling-shareholder net income KRW 17.5 billion (up 105.9%), the peak of the recent period.

Revenue then came in at KRW 244.6 billion, KRW 268.3 billion and KRW 230.9 billion across the first three quarters of 2026, with the third quarter up 2.3% year on year. Operating profit was KRW 27.7 billion, KRW 38.6 billion and KRW 25.8 billion over the same quarters, with the third quarter up 33.9% year on year.

Controlling-shareholder net income was KRW 14.5 billion, KRW 11.6 billion and KRW 2.23 billion, declining for three consecutive quarters, with the third quarter down 73.7% year on year. Net income attributable to controlling shareholders totaled KRW 45.9 billion over the last four quarters.

At the end of 2025, total equity was KRW 890.4 billion, of which KRW 371.8 billion belonged to controlling shareholders, and total liabilities were KRW 328.4 billion. Operating cash flow rose to KRW 147.7 billion in 2023 but declined to KRW 82.2 billion in 2024 and KRW 43.1 billion in 2025.

Whether the third-quarter gap between operating profit and controlling-shareholder net income stems from non-operating items, taxes or minority allocation is a point to confirm in the quarterly report notes.

05

Industry analysis

IDIS Holdings' earnings depend heavily on demand for video security equipment, led by its subsidiary IDIS. Demand splits between new installations and replacement of aging equipment, and order timing shifts with investment cycles at public, industrial and commercial facilities.

Technical requirements for video recorders are moving toward storage capacity and video analytics as high-resolution cameras spread. In this shift, the ability of hardware-focused suppliers to add software and networking functions is a key competitive factor.

Among affiliates, Kortek makes industrial monitors, Bixolon makes mini and POS printers and IDP makes card printers, so their end markets differ from video security. Because the group's end markets are split, a slowdown in one market is partly diluted at the group level.

Bixolon's Mexican subsidiary, set up in June 2024, extends the printer business's footprint in Latin America. The video security equipment market is contested by global suppliers competing hard on price, so defending margins is a challenge.

Component costs and exchange rates feed directly into equipment costs and therefore affect operating margin. Group operating profit of KRW 89.7 billion includes IDIS's standalone operating profit of KRW 21.2 billion together with other affiliates, so profitability should be read by subsidiary.

The next periodic report is the 2026 annual report, which will fix full-year operating profit and controlling-shareholder net income for 2026. The revenue contribution of the Japanese unit and the structure of non-operating items are also points to check.

Bull factors

Recovering operating profit

Operating profit fell to KRW 44.7 billion in 2023 before rising to KRW 63.1 billion in 2024 and KRW 89.7 billion in 2025. Operating margin also climbed from 6.0% in 2023 to 8.1% in 2024 and 9.9% in 2025. The disclosed figures show profitability recovering at the subsidiary level, which is the core bullish argument.

Easing financial burden

The debt ratio declined from 45.7% in 2022 to 36.9% in 2025. Total equity at year-end 2025 was KRW 890.4 billion and total liabilities were KRW 328.4 billion. With borrowing reliance falling, the group can be read as having relatively more financial cushion if subsidiary demand weakens.

Expanding control over the subsidiary

IDIS Holdings made 28 open-market purchases of IDIS shares between September 14 and October 27, 2026, totaling KRW 638 million. As of October 29, 2026, the holding company and related parties held 60.09%. This reflects a strengthening of control, with the stake structure of the subsidiary changing incrementally.

Bear factors

Consecutive quarterly declines in net income

Net income attributable to controlling shareholders was KRW 2.23 billion in the third quarter of 2026, down 73.7% year on year. It has declined for three consecutive quarters since the first quarter of 2026 and sits well below the KRW 17.5 billion of the fourth quarter of 2025. The last four quarters total KRW 45.9 billion.

Shrinking operating cash flow

Operating cash flow fell from KRW 147.7 billion in 2023 to KRW 82.2 billion in 2024 and KRW 43.1 billion in 2025. Cash flow did not rise alongside the operating profit recovery, which suggests lower conversion of earnings into cash. Detailed drivers, such as working capital movements, are items to confirm in the annual report notes.

Revenue volatility and minority leakage

Third-quarter 2026 revenue fell 13.9% from the prior quarter, and revenue also dropped 14.4% in 2023, showing sensitivity to the cycle. Total net income in 2025 was KRW 78 billion, but only KRW 39.1 billion belonged to controlling shareholders, so a large share of profit goes to non-controlling interests. That gap reduces the earnings that holding company shareholders directly receive.

Risk factors

Subsidiary demand and price competition

Video security equipment, the base of group revenue, can see orders swing with installation budgets. Intense price competition with global suppliers could compress margins and weaken the operating margin recovery. Operating margin fell to 6.0% in 2023, so a similar shock cannot be ruled out.

Ownership concentration and free float

The holding company and related parties hold 60.09% of IDIS, which can limit the number of shares trading in the market. A higher controlling-stake share reduces the free float, so price movements may depend more on trading volume.

Any change in the ownership structure could also shift the interests of minority shareholders at the subsidiary.

Exchange rates and overseas units

Overseas units in the United States, Europe, Japan and Mexico mean exchange-rate moves can affect export profitability and translation gains or losses. The new Japanese unit could raise the overseas share further.

Overseas unit profits and translation effects are items to separate in the consolidated notes of quarterly reports.

Overall view

IDIS Holdings is a holding company whose group includes IDIS, Kortek, Bixolon and IDP. On reported figures, 2025 was the largest year in the period, with revenue of KRW 908.1 billion and operating profit of KRW 89.7 billion.

Since the 2023 low, operating profit and operating margin recovered for two consecutive years through 2025, and the debt ratio fell to 36.9%.

On the other side, controlling-shareholder net income has declined for three consecutive quarters since the first quarter of 2026, and in the third quarter operating profit rose year on year while controlling-shareholder net income fell to KRW 2.23 billion.

Operating cash flow has also declined since 2023, so the conversion of profit into cash is a point to confirm together. With related parties holding 60.09%, open-market purchases between September 14 and October 27, 2026 point to a continued push to strengthen control.

Valuation sits in a low range against the industry average, but it should be read together with controlling-shareholder earnings and the stake structure.
