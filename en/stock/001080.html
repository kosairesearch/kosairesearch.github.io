Report overview

Back in the Black Amid a Permanent-Magnet Pivot and Buybacks

MH Materials (Manho Rope & Wire) swung to an operating profit and a net profit owed to controlling shareholders for the fiscal year ended June 2026, but revenue has fallen for three straight fiscal years as the company shifts toward permanent magnets.

  1. 1

    The fiscal year ended June 2026 posted an operating profit of KRW 227 million and a net profit attributable to owners of KRW 1.19 billion, reversing the operating loss of KRW 12 billion and net loss of KRW 3.54 billion in the year ended June 2025.

  2. 2

    Revenue for the fiscal year ended June 2026 was KRW 149.6 billion, down 2.2% from the prior year, and the third consecutive annual decline since the year ended June 2024.

  3. 3

    Operating profit for the quarter ended March 2026 was KRW 1.26 billion, a swing to profit, after an operating loss of KRW 3.7 billion in the quarter ended December 2025.

  4. 4

    The company cancelled 6,385,040 treasury shares in July 2026, cutting total shares issued by about 15.4%, and on November 5, 2026 it decided to buy back KRW 3 billion of its own shares through February 5, 2027.

  5. 5

    After its September 2026 renaming, the company is positioning robotics and advanced materials, including permanent magnets, as new growth pillars. Its first development target is ultra-small, high-coercivity motor magnets for robot actuators, and no disclosed revenue contribution from these businesses has been confirmed yet.

02

Business structure

MH Materials (Manho Rope & Wire) was renamed in September 2026, and its core legacy business remains steel wire and wire rope. The company says it aims to strengthen profitability and competitiveness in that business as a stable earnings base.

On top of it, robotics and advanced materials have been set up as new growth pillars. The robotics push centers on affiliates that are expanding a platform linking materials, hardware, and software and control.

In advanced materials, the company announced on October 6, 2026 that it would pivot toward a business centered on permanent magnets.

Permanent magnets are used in motors and drive units for robots, electric vehicles, and defense equipment, and the company plans to develop high-performance magnets based on hot deformation manufacturing technology.

Its first target is ultra-small, high-coercivity motor magnets for robot actuators, to be validated alongside an affiliate's actuator products. Revenue split by segment, revenue by product, and customer mix were not available as public figures in this review, so they are not included.

A domestic competitive share comparison was also not confirmed, so the competitive position is described qualitatively only. The headquarters is moving from Busan to Changwon, and the head office employs about 30 people.

Because the portfolio is being rebuilt around materials and robotics, the earnings contribution of the legacy and new businesses is not yet separable from available data.

04

Earnings analysis

On a filed basis, annual revenue went from KRW 213.3 billion in the fiscal year ended June 2023 to KRW 179.9 billion in the year ended June 2024, KRW 153 billion in the year ended June 2025, and KRW 149.6 billion in the year ended June 2026.

Revenue has declined for three consecutive fiscal years from the year ended June 2024 through the year ended June 2026, and the largest year in the 2023 to 2026 window is the fiscal year ended June 2023. Trends before the fiscal year ended June 2023 were not confirmed in this review.

Operating results moved from an operating loss of KRW 7.44 billion in the year ended June 2023 to a wider operating loss of KRW 30.8 billion in the year ended June 2024, then a narrower operating loss of KRW 12 billion in the year ended June 2025.

The year ended June 2026 posted an operating profit of KRW 227 million, an operating margin of 0.2%.

Net profit attributable to owners went from KRW 8.99 billion in the year ended June 2023 to a net loss of KRW 44.4 billion in the year ended June 2024, a net loss of KRW 3.54 billion in the year ended June 2025, and a profit of KRW 1.19 billion in the year ended June 2026.

Net profit including non-controlling interests was reported at the same amounts. Operating cash flow was weakest in the year ended June 2024 at negative KRW 33.9 billion, turned positive at KRW 6.78 billion in the year ended June 2025, and was negative KRW 1.36 billion in the year ended June 2026.

At the end of June 2026, total equity was KRW 361.4 billion, total liabilities were KRW 101.5 billion, and the debt ratio rose from 12.0% in the year ended June 2025 to 28.1% in the year ended June 2026.

On a quarterly basis, operating losses of KRW 2.83 billion, KRW 567 million, KRW 604 million and KRW 3.7 billion ran from the quarter ended March 2025 through the quarter ended December 2025, followed by an operating profit of KRW 1.26 billion in the quarter ended March 2026.

Net profit attributable to owners rose to KRW 7.63 billion in the quarter ended September 2025, a large increase from a small base, but whether that quarter had one-off factors was not confirmed in this review.

Net profit attributable to owners over the last four quarters, from the quarter ended June 2025 through the quarter ended March 2026, totaled KRW 3.03 billion.

05

Industry analysis

MH Materials faces two distinct demand streams. One is conventional steel demand for steel wire and wire rope, and the other is permanent-magnet demand for motors used in robots, electric vehicles and defense equipment.

The company says the permanent-magnet market is growing and that strengthening critical-mineral supply chains is adding momentum. It also cites national industrial policy, including rare-earth reduction technology and robotics competitiveness, as a background for the pivot.

In robot actuators, the company says demand is rising for smaller size and higher performance. The legacy steel backdrop is weaker.

Headlines in a steel trade publication's weekly market coverage point to weak wire-rod product prices amid construction slowdown and surging imports, and the same outlet's weekly coverage of special steel bars reported deteriorating conditions from weaker raw material prices and rising imports.

These trends bear directly on demand for steel wire and wire rope. However, product-level market share and profitability relative to domestic peers were not confirmed as figures in this review.

The permanent-magnet business is still at the development stage, and the company plans to build domestic production capacity step by step, starting with products that have demand. The cycle position should therefore be read separately for the legacy steel segment and the new materials segment.

06

Outlook

Three points are confirmed in the company's announcements. First, the KRW 3 billion share buyback decided on November 5, 2026 is being executed on the open market from November 6, 2026 through February 5, 2027, and the company plans to cancel all acquired shares after a separate board resolution.

Second, following the cancellation of 6,385,040 shares in July 2026, further cancellations would reduce shares outstanding again, and the company expects the lower share count to support per-share value while continuing shareholder returns in light of its financial condition and growth investment needs.

Third, the permanent-magnet business has ultra-small, high-coercivity motor magnets for robot actuators as its first target, with validation planned alongside affiliate products, and the company says it will start with products that have demand to build domestic production capacity.

On the headquarters move, a press report says construction in Changwon is expected soon with completion around July to August 2027. Quantitative profitability targets for the legacy business and revenue guidance for the new businesses were not found in announcements or reports.

The next checkpoints are therefore whether the legacy business keeps its profitability in results after the quarter ended December 2026, and how much of the new-business development cost shows up in those results.

Bull factors

Expanding shareholder returns

The July 2026 cancellation of treasury shares reduced the number of shares among which earnings are divided. A KRW 3 billion buyback decided on November 5, 2026 runs through February 5, 2027, with plans to cancel all acquired shares. The company expects the lower share count to support per-share value.

Turnaround from losses to profits

Net profit attributable to owners moved from a KRW 44.4 billion net loss in the fiscal year ended June 2024 to a KRW 1.19 billion profit in the year ended June 2026. Operating profit also turned positive at KRW 227 million, and the quarter ended March 2026 posted an operating profit of KRW 1.26 billion.

Demand backdrop for the permanent-magnet business

Permanent magnets are used in motors for robots, electric vehicles and defense equipment. The company is targeting ultra-small, high-coercivity magnets for robot actuators using hot deformation manufacturing technology.

The company says linking these magnets to affiliate actuator products would create a path from materials to finished products.

Bear factors

Revenue has shrunk for three years

Revenue has declined for three consecutive fiscal years from the year ended June 2024 through the year ended June 2026. Revenue in the year ended June 2026 was KRW 149.6 billion, below KRW 213.3 billion in the year ended June 2023.

A thin earnings base and volatility

The operating margin for the year ended June 2026 was 0.2%. Net profit attributable to owners over the last four quarters totals only KRW 3.03 billion. Quarterly results have been volatile, with a net loss attributable to owners of KRW 5.09 billion in the quarter ended December 2025.

Timing of new-business revenue and rising leverage

The permanent-magnet business is still at the first development target, and no disclosed revenue contribution has been confirmed. The debt ratio rose from 12.0% in the year ended June 2025 to 28.1% in the year ended June 2026.

New-business investment and shareholder returns are proceeding at the same time, making the allocation of financial capacity a key issue.

Risk factors

Legacy steel market conditions

Headlines in a steel trade publication's weekly coverage point to weak wire-rod prices amid construction slowdown and surging imports. The same outlet reported weaker special steel bar conditions from lower raw material prices and rising imports. Steel wire and wire rope sit close to these trends, so margin pressure could persist.

Execution and funding of new businesses

The permanent-magnet business still requires development, validation and domestic production build-out. The company plans to start with products that have demand, but production scale and customer timelines have not been disclosed. Higher development costs on top of a rising debt ratio could add financial strain.

Simultaneous restructuring and shareholder returns

The renaming, business restructuring, headquarters move, and treasury share buyback and cancellation are all proceeding at the same time. The company says it will continue shareholder returns in light of financial condition and growth investment needs. If these goals conflict, the scale of shareholder returns could be adjusted.

Overall view

MH Materials swung to a KRW 227 million operating profit and a KRW 1.19 billion net profit attributable to owners for the fiscal year ended June 2026, and the quarter ended March 2026 also posted an operating profit of KRW 1.26 billion.

Revenue, however, has declined for three consecutive fiscal years from the year ended June 2024, and the 0.2% operating margin leaves a thin earnings base. Reported conditions in the legacy steel wire and wire rope business are weak, with construction slowdown and rising imports.

The permanent-magnet and robotics efforts remain at the first development target, and no revenue contribution has been confirmed in disclosures.

Shareholder returns have been strengthened by the July 2026 cancellation and the KRW 3 billion buyback decided in November 2026, while the debt ratio rose from 12.0% in the year ended June 2025 to 28.1% in the year ended June 2026.

The profit turnaround and shareholder returns sit alongside the shrinking revenue, weak steel conditions and uncertain timing of new-business revenue, with each carrying comparable weight.

The next points to track are how the profit run and new-business costs appear in the third-quarter quarterly report for 2026 and the fourth-quarter report for 2026.
