02

Business structure

MH Materials (formerly Manho Rope & Wire) is the company that was known as Manho Rope & Wire and produced steel wire and wire rope before changing its name in September 2026. Shareholders approved the name change at the 74th annual general meeting held on September 18, 2026.

The company says it will keep the existing steel business as its foundation while building a robot business that links materials, hardware, and software and control. Subsidiary MH Systems, which handles robot electronics and control, recorded revenue of about KRW 19 billion in 2025.

Subsidiary MH Dynamics covers precision machining and robot hardware. The company plans to combine the two subsidiaries to supply integrated robot core components and control technology rather than individual parts.

In October 2026 it announced a pivot toward magnet-centered advanced materials, with the first target being ultra-small, high-coercivity motor magnets for robot actuators. These magnets will be developed alongside actuators under development at MH Dynamics and verified in actual products.

The company says it will apply manufacturing capability built in its existing business to magnet development. Revenue shares by segment and key customers are not presented in figures in the available sources, so the wire rope and steel business still appears to form the base of the company's results.

04

Earnings analysis

On an annual basis, revenue declined while profitability improved.

Revenue went from KRW 213.3 billion in the fiscal year ended June 2023 to KRW 179.9 billion in the fiscal year ended June 2024 (down 15.7% year on year), KRW 153 billion in the fiscal year ended June 2025 (down 15.0%), and KRW 149.6 billion in the fiscal year ended June 2026 (down 2.2%).

Operating results moved from an operating loss of KRW 7.44 billion in the fiscal year ended June 2023 to an operating loss of KRW 30.8 billion in the fiscal year ended June 2024, narrowed to an operating loss of KRW 12 billion in the fiscal year ended June 2025, and turned into operating profit of KRW 227 million in the fiscal year ended June 2026.

The operating margin went from -3.5% and -17.1% to -7.8% and then 0.2%.

Net income attributable to controlling shareholders swung from KRW 8.99 billion in the fiscal year ended June 2023 to a net loss of KRW 44.4 billion in the fiscal year ended June 2024, a smaller net loss of KRW 3.54 billion in the fiscal year ended June 2025, and a net profit of KRW 1.19 billion in the fiscal year ended June 2026.

Net income including non-controlling interests is filed with the same values as net income attributable to controlling shareholders. Operating cash flow was negative at KRW -1.36 billion in the fiscal year ended June 2026, after an inflow of KRW 6.78 billion in the fiscal year ended June 2025.

The debt ratio rose from 12.0% to 28.1% over the same period.

Quarterly revenue rose from KRW 37.1 billion in the first quarter of 2025 to KRW 39.6 billion in the second quarter, then fell to KRW 36.7 billion in the third and KRW 36.1 billion in the fourth quarter, before reaching KRW 37.7 billion in the first quarter of 2026.

Operating results were operating losses of KRW 2.83 billion, KRW 567 million, KRW 604 million and KRW 3.7 billion across the four quarters of 2025, followed by operating profit of KRW 1.26 billion in the first quarter of 2026.

In the third quarter of 2025 an operating loss of KRW 604 million coexisted with net income attributable to controlling shareholders of KRW 7.63 billion, which points to a large non-operating contribution. The fourth quarter of 2025 showed a net loss attributable to controlling shareholders of KRW 5.09 billion.

Net income attributable to controlling shareholders over the last four quarters (second quarter of 2025 to first quarter of 2026) totals KRW 3.03 billion.

05

Industry analysis

Permanent magnets are described as a core material for motors and drive units in robots, electric vehicles, and defense equipment. The company says demand is rising for ultra-small, high-coercivity products as robot actuators require smaller size and higher performance.

On the policy side, stabilizing critical mineral supply chains, developing rare earth reduction technology, and strengthening the robot industry were cited as reasons for the new business. Because robot demand is the starting point, the end-market is tied to investment cycles in the robot industry.

Electric vehicles and defense are presented as later expansion areas after the first product is verified. The existing steel wire and wire rope business is a traditional materials business whose results move with construction and industrial equipment investment.

Kiswire is a listed company in the same steel wire and wire rope business. The magnet business is in an early stage of building domestic production capacity, so the competitive landscape on price and market share is not yet clearly visible.

06

Outlook

The company said both operating profit and net profit turned positive for fiscal 2026, and on that basis confirmed a year-end dividend for the first time since fiscal 2022, as described at the September 2026 shareholder meeting.

The company aims to keep stable profitability in its existing business while growing the robot business as a new growth engine. Subsidiary MH Systems recorded revenue of about KRW 19 billion in 2025, and the company expects revenue growth to continue this year.

In robotics, the company is pursuing a structure that combines MH Systems' electronics and control capabilities with MH Dynamics' precision machining and hardware capabilities.

The magnet business is at the stage of demonstrating its first target, robot actuator magnets, together with MH Dynamics products, and the company plans to widen applications to electric vehicles and defense depending on development results and market demand.

On the shareholder return side, the company retired 6,385,040 treasury shares in July 2026, which reduced total issued shares by about 15.4%. It also plans to buy back KRW 3 billion worth of shares on the open market from November 6, 2026 to February 5, 2027, and retire all of them. The planned purchase is 776,196 shares, equal to about 2.21% of total issued shares.

Bull factors

Profit turnaround

Operating profit of KRW 227 million in the fiscal year ended June 2026 compares with an operating loss of KRW 12 billion a year earlier. Net income attributable to controlling shareholders also turned to a KRW 1.19 billion profit.

The operating profit was confined to the first quarter, so whether it continues in the following quarter is the next point to check. The operating margin rose to 0.2%, which marks the starting point of the turnaround.

Stronger shareholder returns

In July 2026 the company retired 6,385,040 treasury shares, reducing total issued shares by about 15.4%. In November 2026 it decided to buy back KRW 3 billion worth of shares and said it plans to retire all of them. The fiscal 2026 year-end dividend was confirmed for the first time since fiscal 2022. Shareholder return measures are continuing along several lines.

Robotics and magnet businesses

Subsidiary MH Systems recorded revenue of about KRW 19 billion in 2025, and the company expects growth to continue this year. The first target for the magnet business is ultra-small, high-coercivity magnets for robot actuators. The company plans to demonstrate them together with MH Dynamics actuators.

The company presents its manufacturing capability in the existing steel wire business as an advantage for the new business.

Bear factors

Revenue down three straight years

Revenue was KRW 149.6 billion in the fiscal year ended June 2026, down 2.2% year on year. It has declined for three consecutive years from the fiscal year ended June 2024, and is well below KRW 213.3 billion in the fiscal year ended June 2023.

The return to profit came from a better cost and loss structure rather than from revenue growth. An operating margin of 0.2% remains thin.

Volatile quarterly results

The fourth quarter of 2025 showed an operating loss of KRW 3.7 billion and a net loss attributable to controlling shareholders of KRW 5.09 billion.

The third quarter of 2025 showed an operating loss of KRW 604 million but net income attributable to controlling shareholders of KRW 7.63 billion, which shows a large dependence on non-operating items. Net income attributable to controlling shareholders over the last four quarters totals only KRW 3.03 billion. Because quarterly swings are large, whether profit can be sustained needs confirmation in further quarters.

Execution risk and financial burden

The magnet business is at an early stage of building domestic production capacity in stages. Product demonstration and the timing of commercialization have not been fixed. Operating cash flow was negative at KRW -1.36 billion in the fiscal year ended June 2026. The debt ratio rose from 12.0% to 28.1%, and total liabilities stand at KRW 101.5 billion.

10

Risk factors

Market overheating and caution designation

On November 6, 2026, the Korea Exchange designated the stock as an investment caution issue due to concentrated trading in a small number of accounts. Shares rose 16.55% over the three trading days ending November 5, and the top ten accounts accounted for 54.05% of buying.

The exchange's filing notes that alerts may be escalated in stages. Because price swings have widened, price volatility risk is elevated.

Execution of the business pivot

The magnet and robot businesses are still at the stage of product demonstration and building production capacity. The existing steel business and subsidiaries may also compete for resources with the new businesses. If new business results are delayed, the existing business will continue to drive earnings. The pace of the pivot and the scale of investment need to be watched for their effect on results.

Raw materials and policy environment

Permanent magnets depend on rare earth and critical mineral supply. The company cites rare earth reduction technology as a rationale for the business, but the cost and availability of raw materials directly affect profitability.

Government supply chain policy can create a favorable environment for the new business, but its impact could change if policy direction shifts. Raw material sourcing and policy changes both need to be followed.

Overall view

Revenue, however, has declined for three consecutive years from the fiscal year ended June 2024 to KRW 149.6 billion, and the profit base is still small.

Quarterly results returned to operating profit in the first quarter of 2026, but the fourth quarter of 2025 posted an operating loss of KRW 3.7 billion, so volatility remains high.

On shareholder returns, retiring treasury shares in July 2026 cut total issued shares by about 15.4%, and a further KRW 3 billion buyback was decided in November 2026.

On the business side, robotics and permanent magnets are presented as new pillars, and results from the first magnet product have not yet been published.

On valuation, market capitalization is below book equity, while earnings-based measures are hard to compare with the past because profit only recently turned positive. The November 6, 2026 investment caution designation shows that price volatility has increased.

The next checkpoints are the third quarter 2026 quarterly report, completion of the buyback, and the magnet demonstration results.
