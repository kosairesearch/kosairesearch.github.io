Report overview

Nuclear maintenance timing weighs on earnings; overseas orders are the next variable

KEPCO Plant Service & Engineering's operating profit fell 75.4% year on year in 3Q 2026 as nuclear overhaul timing slipped, leaving overseas nuclear maintenance orders as the next checkpoint.

  1. 1

    Annual revenue rose for three consecutive years from 2023 through 2025, but operating profit fell 33.1% to KRW 140.1 billion in 2025, pushing the operating margin down to 8.9%.

  2. 2

02

Business structure

KEPCO Plant Service & Engineering is a subsidiary of Korea Electric Power that handles maintenance, operation and technical support for power plant equipment.

Its core business is planned preventive maintenance at thermal and nuclear plants, supplemented by transmission and substation inspection and overseas nuclear maintenance. In 3Q 2026, thermal segment revenue rose to KRW 125.5 billion, while nuclear segment revenue fell 26.3% year on year to KRW 130.5 billion.

Transmission and substation revenue was KRW 29.0 billion, down 1.7%. Overseas revenue fell 42.9% to KRW 26.2 billion and other external revenue fell 42.4% to KRW 19.3 billion, reflecting the completion of major projects such as the UAE contract.

The four segments together total KRW 330.5 billion, matching the reported 3Q revenue. Customers are mainly power generators, with Korea Electric Power as the largest shareholder and a 2026 overhead line inspection contract worth KRW 96.3 billion.

A 2026 contract with Korea Hydro & Nuclear Power for Hanbit Unit 2 steam generator maintenance is worth KRW 83.6 billion. Analysts note that about 70 percent of the order backlog renews every year. Nuclear contracts carry terms that settle work differently depending on whether continued operation is approved.

04

Earnings analysis

Annual revenue grew from KRW 1.43 trillion in 2022 to KRW 1.58 trillion in 2025, but growth slowed from 7.3% in 2023 to 1.5% in 2024 and 1.2% in 2025.

Operating profit rose from KRW 130.6 billion in 2022 to KRW 199.4 billion in 2023 (up 52.7%) and KRW 209.5 billion in 2024 (up 5.1%), before falling 33.1% to KRW 140.1 billion in 2025. The operating margin moved from 9.1% in 2022 and 13.0% in 2023 to 13.5% in 2024 and 8.9% in 2025.

Operating profit was largest in 2024 and smallest in 2022 across the four years. Net income attributable to controlling shareholders was KRW 100.2 billion in 2022, KRW 162.7 billion in 2023 (up 62.4%), KRW 172.4 billion in 2024 (up 6.0%), and KRW 124.2 billion in 2025 (down 28.0%).

Net income including non-controlling interests was KRW 124.3 billion in 2025, a different figure. A February 2026 broker report attributed the 2025 profit decline to structural margin pressure from core cost items.

Operating cash flow was KRW 81.3 billion in 2022, KRW -38.1 billion in 2023, KRW 540.5 billion in 2024 and KRW 135.7 billion in 2025. The debt ratio stayed between 25.5% and 28.5% over 2022 to 2025.

Quarterly, 4Q 2025 revenue was KRW 440.8 billion with operating profit of KRW 19.3 billion, down 59.4% from the prior quarter. 1Q 2026 operating profit recovered to KRW 37 billion, up 92.3%, which one broker linked to overhaul work delayed from 4Q 2025 moving into early 2026. 2Q 2026 revenue was KRW 437.9 billion and operating profit KRW 43.6 billion. 3Q 2026 revenue was KRW 330.5 billion, operating profit KRW 11.7 billion and net income attributable to controlling shareholders KRW 5.73 billion. Net income attributable to controlling shareholders over the latest four quarters totals KRW 92.9 billion.

05

Industry analysis

Demand depends on the number of units scheduled for planned preventive maintenance. For 2026, plans called for thermal units to rise from 87 to 101 and nuclear units from 13 to 20 compared with 2025. In 3Q 2026, however, only three nuclear units were under planned overhaul, half the six a year earlier.

Brokers attribute the gap mainly to longer work and delayed revenue recognition rather than to weaker demand. Continued operation approval for aging reactors is a key variable for nuclear maintenance volume, and Korea Investment & Securities flagged significant uncertainty around it.

Domestic coal phase-out policy is a concern for thermal volumes. On the other side, the Team Korea win of the Dukovany project in the Czech Republic in December 2025, valued at about KRW 26 trillion, is cited as a basis for overseas maintenance growth.

One broker estimated the maintenance value of 17 overseas nuclear projects at about KRW 877 billion, while noting that actual revenue from these projects may begin only in 2027 or later. The market is structured around utility-led procurement, so relationships with large clients shape order flow.

Outlook

In March 2026 the company disclosed a value-up plan that set a 2026 revenue target of KRW 1.9 trillion. Cumulative 1Q to 3Q 2026 revenue, summing the disclosed quarters, is KRW 1.12 trillion, so reaching the target would require a large 4Q. After the 3Q results, brokers lowered their 2026 operating profit estimates.

Analysts point to recovery in nuclear maintenance volume and progress of nuclear-related construction as the items to watch in 4Q.

For overseas work, Daishin Securities expected pre-work revenue on the Cernavoda Unit 1 pressure tube replacement project to begin in 4Q 2026, a project with total contract value of KRW 485 billion.

Reports said Dukovany Units 5 and 6 maintenance awards were scheduled for announcement in the second half of 2026, and analysts expect KEPCO Plant Service to follow Korean reactor exports.

On the domestic side, the target date for continued operation approval at Hanbit Unit 2 is December 2026, and the outcome will shape settlement under that contract. Beyond 2027, the number of planned overhauls completed and overseas maintenance revenue will likely determine the earnings direction. Analysts also caution that timing of overhaul schedules makes the timing of a recovery hard to predict.

Bull factors

Overseas nuclear maintenance pipeline

The Cernavoda Unit 1 pressure tube project carries total contract value of KRW 485 billion, and Daishin Securities expected pre-work revenue to start in 4Q 2026. The Dukovany project, valued at about KRW 26 trillion, could bring follow-on maintenance if Korean reactor exports advance. One broker estimated the maintenance value of 17 overseas projects at about KRW 877 billion.

Quarterly earnings stabilizing signs

Operating profit was KRW 37 billion in 1Q 2026 and KRW 43.6 billion in 2Q 2026, both above KRW 30 billion. The 1Q recovery was linked to overhaul work pushed from 4Q 2025. The 3Q decline is tied to fewer overhauls and overseas base effects rather than a broad-based deterioration.

Domestic maintenance volume and new contracts

Planned overhauls for 2026 were set to rise to 101 thermal units and 20 nuclear units. In October 2026 the company disclosed contracts worth KRW 96.3 billion with Korea Electric Power and KRW 83.6 billion with Korea Hydro & Nuclear Power. Analysts note that about 70 percent of the backlog renews each year.

Bear factors

3Q results far below expectations

3Q 2026 operating profit of KRW 11.7 billion fell well short of consensus, with the operating margin at 3.5%. Net income attributable to controlling shareholders fell 86.6% to KRW 5.73 billion. Brokers cut targets in response, and the stock fell more than 7% intraday the day after the release.

Overhaul timing and continued operation uncertainty

Only three nuclear units were under planned overhaul in 3Q 2026, half the six a year earlier. Longer contracts delayed revenue recognition, showing how directly overhaul timing reaches earnings. Analysts also flagged significant uncertainty around continued operation approvals.

Overseas and external segments declining

Overseas revenue fell 42.9% to KRW 26.2 billion and external revenue fell 42.4% to KRW 19.3 billion in 3Q 2026, mainly because major projects such as the UAE maintenance contract have ended. With the operating margin at 8.9% in 2025, a slower rebuild of overseas revenue could delay margin recovery.

Risk factors

Coal phase-out and thermal volume

Domestic coal phase-out policy could reduce the thermal plant overhaul volume. The 2026 plan set thermal units for planned maintenance at 101, up from 87 in 2025, but if policy cuts coal generation, the number of units scheduled for overhaul could be adjusted. Thermal revenue rose in 3Q, yet quarterly volume could depend directly on such policy-driven changes.

Earnings volatility

Power plant maintenance earnings vary widely by quarter and season. Operating profit was KRW 19.3 billion in 4Q 2025, KRW 37 billion in 1Q 2026 and KRW 11.7 billion in 3Q 2026. Quarterly figures can change after external audit review.

Client concentration

Korea Electric Power is the largest shareholder and a counterparty to major contracts, and maintenance volume moves with generators' operating plans and procurement methods. A 2026 overhead line inspection contract was also signed with Korea Electric Power.

If contracts concentrate with a single utility, changes to a generator's overhaul plan or contract terms could reach earnings directly. Because the market relies on utility-led procurement, maintaining relationships with large clients is a precondition for winning orders.

Overall view

KEPCO Plant Service & Engineering's revenue has risen steadily since 2022, but operating profit peaked in 2024 and fell 33.1% in 2025.

In 3Q 2026, fewer nuclear units under planned overhaul and delayed revenue recognition cut operating profit to KRW 11.7 billion, and net income attributable to controlling shareholders fell sharply. In 1Q and 2Q 2026, however, operating profit recovered to KRW 37 billion and KRW 43.6 billion.

Overseas, the KRW 485 billion Cernavoda project and the Dukovany project are cited as grounds for medium-term maintenance volume. Domestically, continued operation approval and coal phase-out policy are the variables for volume.

Brokers cut target prices after 3Q, and some view meaningful overseas revenue as arriving only from 2027. The next points to confirm are the December 2026 continued operation outcome, whether 4Q overseas pre-work revenue appears, and the operating margin in the 2026 annual report due by March 2027.
