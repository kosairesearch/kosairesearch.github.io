02

Business structure

Rifa was founded in 1950 as Korea's first dye manufacturer and listed on the Korea Stock Exchange in 1994. It no longer manufactures and now runs wholesale and retail trade in dyes and cosmetics-related products.

The company's own description lists dyes for textile dyeing, food additives and cosmetics-related products as its main items. Segment shares differ by data provider. One service puts Q2 2026 revenue at 49% distribution, 28% dye business, 23% rental and 1% transport.

Another, based on March 2026 data, shows cosmetics goods at 35% and property rental at 27%. Both show distribution as the largest block, with rental at roughly a quarter of sales. Rental income comes from owned property and is less exposed to raw material prices than the trading businesses.

The dye business follows textile dyeing demand, while cosmetics distribution tracks consumer spending and sales channels. Against large retailers such as Lotte Shopping, Shinsegae and E-Mart, Rifa is a small operator. Its profit therefore depends on the combination of rental income and trading margins.

04

Earnings analysis

On a DART-filed annual basis, revenue went from KRW 58.6 billion in 2022 to KRW 47.2 billion in 2023 (down 19.5% year on year), rose to KRW 49.5 billion in 2024 (up 4.9%), and fell to KRW 46.3 billion in 2025 (down 6.5%). 2022 was the largest year and 2025 the smallest in the 2022–2025 period.

Operating loss was KRW 884 million in 2022 and KRW 1.81 billion in 2023, a widening deficit, before an operating profit of KRW 772 million in 2024 marked a turn to profit. In 2025 the company returned to an operating loss of KRW 164 million.

Operating margin was -1.5%, -3.8%, 1.6% and -0.4% from 2022 to 2025, so 2024 was the only profitable year.

Profit attributable to controlling shareholders was KRW 766 million in 2022, then a loss of KRW 2.08 billion in 2023, KRW 629 million in 2024 (a smaller loss) and KRW 1.79 billion in 2025 (a larger loss), making 2023–2025 three consecutive loss years.

The 2025 net loss including non-controlling interests was KRW 2.14 billion, larger than the controlling-shareholder loss, so non-controlling interests also absorbed losses. Operating cash flow was -KRW 4.98 billion in 2022, then KRW 2.36 billion, KRW 897 million and KRW 3.55 billion in 2023–2025.

The debt-to-equity ratio (total liabilities divided by total equity) rose from 27.9% in 2022 to 31.6% in 2025, with total equity of KRW 204.9 billion, of which KRW 134 billion is controlling shareholders' equity.

Quarterly, an operating loss of KRW 539 million in Q3 2025 and KRW 1.12 billion in Q4 2025 gave way to an operating profit of KRW 786 million in Q1 2026 and KRW 1.13 billion in Q2 2026. Q2 2026 revenue was KRW 13.4 billion, up 21.5% from the prior quarter.

Q3 2026 revenue fell 12.4% from the prior quarter to KRW 11.8 billion, and the company posted an operating loss of KRW 356 million. Over the last four quarters, the cumulative controlling-shareholder net loss was KRW 855 million.

The drivers of the quarterly swings cannot be pinned down from the filed figures alone, so the cost and SG&A structure is an item for the next periodic report.

05

Industry analysis

The dye business sells textile dyeing products, so its demand depends on how much fabric is produced and traded. Textile supply chains are sensitive to raw material costs and to production shifting overseas, which can move both price and volume.

Cosmetics-related distribution tracks consumer spending and the buying policies of large retail channels, where pricing competition sets the margin. Property rental depends on tenant demand and lease terms for owned buildings, and its revenue moves less with the business cycle than trading does.

Distribution and dyes are therefore the more cyclical parts, while rental is the steadier source. Against large distributors such as Lotte Shopping, Shinsegae and E-Mart, Rifa is a much smaller operator with KRW 46.3 billion in 2025 revenue, which can limit its sourcing and pricing power.

The cycle position should be read alongside the revenue decline from KRW 58.6 billion in 2022 to KRW 46.3 billion in 2025. Relative position is best judged from the revenue trend and the segment mix.

Outlook

Quarterly results in 2026 have been mixed: an operating profit of KRW 786 million in Q1, KRW 1.13 billion in Q2 and an operating loss of KRW 356 million in Q3. Q2 revenue reached KRW 13.4 billion, but Q3 revenue fell to KRW 11.8 billion and profit turned to a loss.

Whether full-year 2026 operating results close in profit depends on the size of the Q4 result. The Q4 2026 figures have not yet been filed in a periodic report, so they will first be confirmed as final numbers in the 2026 annual report.

Rental makes up roughly a quarter of sales and partly cushions swings in the trading and dye profits, though its share ranges from 23% to 27% depending on the data provider. Operating margin was positive only in 2024, at 1.6%, and returned to -0.4% in 2025.

Given how often quarterly profit has changed sign, the next checkpoint is the sign and size of the Q4 2026 operating result.

Bull factors

Asset base and modest leverage

Total equity at end-2025 was KRW 204.9 billion, with KRW 134 billion attributable to controlling shareholders. Total liabilities were KRW 64.7 billion, and the debt-to-equity ratio of 31.6% indicates a relatively light debt burden.

Rental accounts for about 23% of sales in one data set, which can partly offset operating losses in the trading business. The weight of this argument depends on how closely book value of the owned assets matches their market value.

Quarterly profit recovery and positive operating cash flow

After turning to an operating profit of KRW 786 million in Q1 2026, the company posted KRW 1.13 billion in Q2 2026. Q2 revenue of KRW 13.4 billion, up 21.5% from the prior quarter, may signal a recovery in sales volume.

Operating cash flow stayed positive from 2023 through 2025, which narrows the gap between reported profit and cash.

Price below net asset value and narrowing losses

One stock diagnosis site reports the price-to-book ratio below net asset value, which is a factor cited in favor of the stock. Operating margin reached 1.6% in 2024, showing the company has recorded profit recently. The operating loss narrowed from KRW 1.12 billion in Q4 2025 to KRW 356 million in Q3 2026.

Profit attributable to controlling shareholders has been negative for three consecutive years, 2023 to 2025. The 2025 loss of KRW 1.79 billion was larger than the KRW 629 million loss in 2024.

The 2025 operating loss of KRW 164 million means the 2024 turn to profit did not carry through the full year, a point the 2026 annual report will confirm.

Shrinking revenue and a Q3 decline

Revenue fell from KRW 58.6 billion in 2022 to KRW 46.3 billion in 2025, with growth only in 2024. The 2025 revenue decline was 6.5% year on year. Q3 2026 revenue fell 12.4% from the prior quarter to KRW 11.8 billion, reversing part of the Q2 gain.

Renewed quarterly loss and cumulative deficit

The company returned to an operating loss of KRW 356 million in Q3 2026. Continued net losses attributable to controlling shareholders have enlarged the cumulative deficit. Operating margin was positive in only one of the four years from 2022 to 2025, so the profitability base is not yet established.

Risk factors

Governance and shareholding changes

A company information site recorded two changes in the largest shareholder over the 180 days to September 2026. Major-shareholder and large-holding reports were also filed in July 2026. Shifts in ownership can create uncertainty over management direction and business strategy.

Core profitability and textile demand

Operating margin was -1.5% in 2022, -3.8% in 2023 and -0.4% in 2025, positive only in 2024. Dye demand moves with textile output, so higher raw material costs combined with weaker demand could compress margins further.

Because quarterly operating results alternate between profit and loss, sustaining a full-year profit is the central risk.

Asset valuation and borrowing costs

The gap between book value and market value of rental property cannot be settled from filed figures alone. Whether the asset-based argument turns into cash flow depends on the stability of rental income.

Total liabilities of KRW 64.7 billion mean that changes in the interest rate environment could raise financing costs.
