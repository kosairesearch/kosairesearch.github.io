Yeonghwa Metal: Profit Recovery Meets a 2026 Third-Quarter Net Loss

02

Business structure

Yeonghwa Metal was founded in 1977 and listed on KOSPI in 1990. The company makes automotive parts and is headquartered in Jinhae District, Changwon, South Gyeongsang Province.

It started as a gray iron foundry focused on pipe fittings and valves and widened into automotive components, and in the 2000s it obtained GM and HMC Group certifications and was designated a Tier 1 supplier to BMW.

As of March 2026, automotive parts accounted for 98% of revenue, products for 1% and other businesses for 1%. Its core lines are internal-combustion-engine components such as turbochargers and cylinder blocks, and it also produces brake discs and drums.

It added gray iron casting and machining plants in 2002 and 2015, and a stainless steel casting, machining and assembly plant in 2017 that extended it into steel casting.

The company runs four plants, and management has credited the Hamyang plant's profitability and the operating contribution of its No. 2 plant for the improved results.

Exports go mainly to the United States, the Netherlands, Poland, Germany and Japan, with turbochargers as the main item and cylinder blocks shipped to India.

Export volumes were USD 92 million in 2023, USD 96 million in 2024 and USD 131 million in 2025, and the company received a USD 100 million export tower award on that basis.

On competition, the company is strengthening export customer relationships to respond to rivalry in global domestic markets, and it is also upgrading equipment on its main production lines for efficiency.

04

Earnings analysis

On an annual basis, revenue rose from KRW 284.9 billion in 2022 to KRW 292.3 billion in 2023 (up 2.6% year on year), KRW 301.7 billion in 2024 (up 3.2%) and KRW 351.4 billion in 2025 (up 16.5%), growing for three consecutive years from 2023 to 2025. 2025 was the largest year of 2022 to 2025, and 2022 was the smallest.

Operating profit moved from an operating loss of KRW 1.12 billion in 2022 to operating profit of KRW 9.4 billion in 2023, which marked a turn to profit, and then rose to KRW 9.88 billion in 2024 (up 5.2%) and KRW 15.2 billion in 2025 (up 54.1%), growing for two consecutive years from 2024 to 2025.

The operating margin improved from -0.4% in 2022 to 3.2% in 2023, 3.3% in 2024 and 4.3% in 2025. The company attributed the 2025 operating profit increase to higher revenue and efforts to improve its profit structure.

Profit attributable to owners of the parent rose from KRW 469 million in 2022 to KRW 7.54 billion in 2023 (up 1,505.4%), KRW 8.65 billion in 2024 (up 14.8%) and KRW 12.2 billion in 2025 (up 40.5%), growing for three consecutive years from 2023 to 2025.

The 2023 jump reflects a move from an operating loss to profit, after which the growth rate slowed. Operating cash flow went from KRW 5.32 billion in 2022 to KRW 11.1 billion in 2023 and KRW 14.8 billion in 2024, then eased to KRW 14.2 billion in 2025.

At the end of 2025, total equity was KRW 81.5 billion, all of it attributable to owners of the parent, and total liabilities were KRW 154.5 billion.

Quarterly results show revenue of KRW 85.4 billion and operating profit of KRW 3.17 billion in the third quarter of 2025, followed by fourth-quarter revenue of KRW 84.7 billion (down 0.8% quarter on quarter) and operating profit of KRW 2.16 billion (down 31.7%).

In the first quarter of 2026, revenue reached KRW 90.1 billion and operating profit KRW 3.1 billion (up 43.4%), and in the second quarter revenue was KRW 92.2 billion and operating profit KRW 4.21 billion (up 35.9%).

In the third quarter of 2026, revenue was KRW 89.2 billion (down 3.2% quarter on quarter, up 4.4% year on year) and operating profit was KRW 3.83 billion (down 9.0% quarter on quarter, up 21.0% year on year).

Profit attributable to owners of the parent was KRW 3.59 billion in the second quarter, but in the third quarter it became a net loss of KRW 1.39 billion, a swing into loss.

A report on the third-quarter preliminary release said net income swung to a loss even though operating profit rose, and that pre-tax income was also in loss. Over the last four quarters, from the fourth quarter of 2025 to the third quarter of 2026, profit attributable to owners of the parent totaled KRW 8.97 billion.

05

Industry analysis

The automotive parts industry is directly affected by the operating conditions of vehicle makers. As car ownership matures, industry growth is described as gradual.

On the other side, expanding new-model launches and a growing vehicle parc are expected to lift demand for aftermarket parts, along with overseas export demand.

Turbochargers, the company's core item, have been reported to be used more often in both diesel and gasoline vehicles as demand for fuel-efficient cars rose ahead of the shift to electric vehicles.

The recovery in results since 2022 has coincided with a rebound in the auto industry and higher output from vehicle makers.

In a past domestic auto downturn that spread to parts suppliers, the company's operating profit fell to about a tenth of its level within two years, which shows a high sensitivity to the vehicle cycle.

On electrification, the company said it is internally reviewing an expansion into electric-vehicle-related business beyond internal-combustion parts. On competition, the company is strengthening export customer relationships to respond to rivalry in global domestic markets.

Because it works in both gray iron and steel casting, vehicle output and export customer wins are the variables that will shape its position against listed peers.

The next checkpoint is fourth-quarter 2026 results, and since the 2025 full-year preliminary figures were disclosed on February 9, 2026, the 2026 full-year preliminary figures could come out in early 2027.

Electric-vehicle business has been reported as being at the internal review stage, and no specific plan or amount has been disclosed. Whether vehicle output keeps recovering and whether the third-quarter loss continues will be the biggest drivers of future results.

Bear factors

Third-quarter swing to a loss

Third-quarter 2026 revenue of KRW 89.2 billion rose 4.4% year on year and operating profit rose 21.0%, but the company recorded a parent-attributable net loss of KRW 1.39 billion. The fact that operating gains did not carry through to net income is the key point to watch. Profit attributable to owners over the last four quarters totaled KRW 8.97 billion.

Concentration in automotive parts

Automotive parts make up 98% of revenue, so results depend heavily on domestic and overseas auto cycles. A drop in vehicle output would quickly affect volumes. With an operating margin of 4.3% in 2025, the cushion to absorb a downturn is limited.

Reliance on combustion-engine parts

Turbochargers and cylinder blocks are the core lines, so faster electrification would require a change in product mix. The company has described an expansion into electric-vehicle business as an internal review. The timing for any such business to reach revenue has not been disclosed.

Risk factors

Customer concentration and cycle

Most revenue is tied to vehicle production, so results are sensitive to a single customer or a fall in output. The recovery since 2022 relies heavily on higher vehicle production. An operating margin of 4.3% in 2025 leaves limited room to absorb a cyclical shock.

Financial leverage

Total liabilities of KRW 154.5 billion exceed total equity of KRW 81.5 billion, so changes in interest rates and borrowing terms could weigh on earnings. Operating cash flow fell to KRW 14.2 billion in 2025 from KRW 14.8 billion in 2024. If the net loss persists, the financial burden could increase.

Market-caution disclosure

A market-caution notice was disclosed on August 5, 2026, and the results of an extraordinary shareholders' meeting were disclosed on July 29, 2026. A caution notice of this kind can affect trading sentiment. The specific reasons are set out in the original disclosure.

Overall view

Yeonghwa Metal is an internal-combustion-parts maker whose automotive parts account for 98% of revenue, and it has recovered from an operating loss in 2022 to operating profit of KRW 15.2 billion and a 4.3% operating margin in 2025.

Revenue grew for three consecutive years from 2023 to 2025, and profit attributable to owners also grew for three consecutive years over the same period.

The debt-to-equity ratio fell from 252.0% to 189.5%, but total liabilities of KRW 154.5 billion still exceed total equity of KRW 81.5 billion, so financial leverage remains high.

Operating profit rose from the prior quarter in the first and second quarters of 2026, but in the third quarter it fell from the prior quarter and the company posted a parent-attributable net loss of KRW 1.39 billion.

Strengths include the earnings recovery, the improved balance sheet, export growth and aftermarket demand, while weaknesses include dependence on vehicle output, the shift toward electrification for combustion-engine parts, and the third-quarter swing to a loss.

The next checkpoints are fourth-quarter 2026 results and the operating margin and debt-to-equity ratio in the 2026 annual business report, and the first-quarter 2027 report will show whether the third-quarter loss persists.
