02

Business structure

Virnect was founded in 2016 as an industrial extended reality (XR) solutions company, led by CEO Ha Tae-jin, a KAIST graduate.

Built on its in-house computer vision engine Track, the company's main products are the remote collaboration solution VIRNECT Remote, the no-code content creation tool VIRNECT Make, the content augmentation solution VIRNECT View, and the digital twin solution VIRNECT Twin.

Its solutions are applied in manufacturing sectors such as secondary batteries, petrochemicals, information and communications, semiconductors and displays, for remote collaboration, content creation and digital twins.

In a 2022 report, the company said it supplied solutions to 39 large corporations and affiliates and 27 public corporations. Its customers fall into two groups, large corporate affiliates and government and public agencies, and the company has pursued both at once.

In a Chungbuk disaster-safety program for 2024, Virnect was tasked with building an integrated 3D metaverse-based monitoring platform, a two-year project running through the end of 2025.

The company said it became the first AR, VR, metaverse and digital twin firm named to the third cohort of the Defense Innovation 100 program. After its 2023 KOSDAQ listing, the company stated it would use proceeds for new businesses, overseas expansion and platform building.

An acquisition completed on November 20, 2025 added a new variable to the business structure, and how the acquired company's results feed into consolidated earnings in 2026 is the point to watch.

04

Earnings analysis

Annual revenue rose from KRW 5.06 billion in 2022 to KRW 6.5 billion in 2023, a 28.6% increase that marked the peak, before falling 29.3% to KRW 4.6 billion in 2024 and 32.7% to KRW 3.1 billion in 2025.

Operating losses were KRW 14.1 billion in 2022, KRW 11.3 billion in 2023, KRW 12.7 billion in 2024 and KRW 8.49 billion in 2025, a run of four consecutive loss years, and the 2025 loss was the smallest of the four.

Operating margins were -279.7% in 2022, -173.9% in 2023, -276.1% in 2024 and -274.2% in 2025, so apart from 2023 all three other years sat below -270%.

In 2024 revenue fell 29.3% while the operating loss widened from KRW 11.3 billion to KRW 12.7 billion, which suggests cost adjustments did not keep pace with the sales decline.

Net loss attributable to controlling shareholders grew from KRW 8.48 billion in 2022 to KRW 10.4 billion in 2023 and KRW 12.6 billion in 2024, then narrowed to KRW 5.03 billion in 2025, and the net loss including non-controlling interests followed the same path.

Operating cash flow was negative in every year, at -KRW 11.2 billion in 2022, -KRW 11.9 billion in 2023, -KRW 10.9 billion in 2024 and -KRW 8.26 billion in 2025.

The debt ratio moved from 61.9% in 2022 to 26.7% in 2023, 34.2% in 2024 and 53.4% in 2025, and at year-end 2025 total equity of KRW 3.02 billion was entirely attributable to controlling shareholders against total liabilities of KRW 1.61 billion.

Quarterly revenue went from KRW 684 million in the third quarter of 2025 to KRW 903 million in the fourth quarter (up 32.0% from the prior quarter) and KRW 3.8 billion in the first quarter of 2026 (up 320.3%), then slipped to KRW 3.46 billion in the second quarter (down 8.9%) and KRW 3.31 billion in the third quarter (down 4.2%), marking two straight quarterly declines.

Third-quarter 2026 revenue was up 384.2% from a year earlier, but the trend since the first-quarter peak has been downward.

The operating loss narrowed from KRW 2.39 billion in the third quarter of 2025 to KRW 901 million in the third quarter of 2026, yet it widened to KRW 1.47 billion in the first quarter and KRW 1.84 billion in the second quarter of 2026.

Net income attributable to controlling shareholders turned positive at KRW 1.87 billion in the fourth quarter of 2025, then returned to losses of KRW 1.71 billion, KRW 2.09 billion and KRW 1.19 billion in the first three quarters of 2026.

Over the last four quarters the combined net loss attributable to controlling shareholders was KRW 3.12 billion. In coverage of the third-quarter 2026 disclosure, the company attributed the results to a cost offset from a research project award and ongoing profitability efforts.

05

Industry analysis

The industrial XR market is one where global big tech companies, including Apple, Meta, Google, Microsoft and Samsung Electronics, are competing to make XR and spatial computing a next-generation AI platform.

With that much investment flowing in, specialized XR software firms face a dual challenge of differentiating technology while winning customers.

Virnect centers its business on software solutions such as remote collaboration, content creation and digital twins, aimed at demand from manufacturing and public-sector sites.

In a 2022 interview, the CEO said that after the Serious Accidents Punishment Act took effect, companies began weighing XR adoption for safety management as well as efficiency. Since then, the company's demand base has broadened into defense and disaster-safety projects.

However, demand for industrial XR is tied to customers securing budgets, and the company has said in its annual report that delays or cuts in customer budget execution translated directly into revenue shortfalls.

A news report on the backlog at the end of the third quarter of 2025, at KRW 1.1 billion, illustrates the same structure. Against larger platform companies, Virnect is better positioned as a custom solutions provider for manufacturing and public sites than as a direct rival.

Comparing its revenue trend and operating margin side by side with listed peers in the same sector would sharpen the picture of its position within the industry.

Overall view

On disclosed figures, Virnect's revenue peaked at KRW 6.5 billion in 2023 and then fell for two straight years, and quarterly revenue that jumped to KRW 3.8 billion in the first quarter of 2026 declined in each of the next two quarters.

The 2025 operating loss of KRW 8.49 billion was the smallest since 2022, and the third-quarter 2026 operating loss of KRW 901 million was narrower than a year earlier, but losses have run for four straight years and operating cash flow has been negative in each of those years.

Net loss attributable to controlling shareholders over the last four quarters totaled KRW 3.12 billion, with only the fourth quarter of 2025 in profit. Total equity of KRW 3.02 billion at the end of 2025 is entirely attributable to controlling shareholders, and the price-to-book ratio sits below 1x.

Factors on the positive side include a higher quarterly revenue floor, narrower losses, and an equity base alongside access to defense and government programs. Factors on the negative side include an order structure tied to customer budgets, a delayed payoff from the acquisition, and continued cash outflows.

The company's explanation of research project award effects and profitability efforts can be checked against future quarterly numbers. The next checkpoints are the 2026 annual report and the first-quarter 2027 quarterly report.
