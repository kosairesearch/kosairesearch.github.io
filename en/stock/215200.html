02

Business structure

MegastudyEdu listed on KOSDAQ in May 2015 and is classified under general tutoring academies. Its core customers are students repeating the college entrance exam, N-su students and students who are retaking the exam to switch majors.

Its academy network recruited for N-su classes at nine repeat-year academies in the Seoul and Gyeonggi regions in a 2025 recruitment round. The N-su program uses a student-level analysis and consultation to let students choose only the lessons or weak subjects they need.

The repeat-year comprehensive courses are also run under the Russell brand, and the company's website says mandatory classes were cut by up to 50 percent compared with conventional courses. Mock exams are another pillar, including nationwide large-scale mock tests, premium mock exams and Alpha mock exams.

In late 2024 the company sold its loss-making civil-service exam business for KRW 11.4 billion, which changed its business mix. Because of this change, some commentators say profitability before and after the sale is hard to compare on a like-for-like basis.

The Q3 2026 preliminary disclosure did not include revenue or cost breakdowns by business, so segment profitability cannot be confirmed from that filing.

Student choice depends on published exam results, study management systems and access to online lectures, and offline academies and online lectures compete for the same demand.

04

Earnings analysis

Annual revenue rose from KRW 836 billion in 2022 to KRW 935.2 billion in 2023 (up 11.9% year on year) and KRW 942.2 billion in 2024 (up 0.8%), then fell to KRW 884.9 billion in 2025 (down 6.1%). Among 2022 to 2025, 2024 was the largest year and 2022 the smallest.

Operating profit went from KRW 135.4 billion in 2022 to KRW 127.4 billion in 2023 (down 5.9%), KRW 123.6 billion in 2024 (down 3.0%) and KRW 121 billion in 2025 (down 2.1%), a decline in each of the three years from 2023 to 2025.

The operating margin fell from 16.2% in 2022 to 13.6% in 2023 and 13.1% in 2024, then edged up to 13.7% in 2025. Even though revenue declined in 2025, the operating margin was higher than in 2024.

Net profit attributable to controlling shareholders fell from KRW 103.6 billion in 2022 to KRW 94.6 billion in 2023 (down 8.7%) and KRW 46.2 billion in 2024 (down 51.2%), then recovered to KRW 85.3 billion in 2025 (up 84.7%).

Net profit including non-controlling interests was KRW 50.2 billion in 2024 and KRW 92.9 billion in 2025, and the gap from controlling-shareholder profit reflects non-controlling interests. The late-2024 sale of the civil-service exam business for KRW 11.4 billion is also a structural factor to weigh.

On a quarterly basis, operating profit in Q4 2025 was KRW 8.98 billion, down 83.1% from the prior quarter, which marked the low point. It then rose to KRW 26 billion in Q1 2026 (up 189.5%), KRW 28.4 billion in Q2 (up 9.3%) and KRW 53.7 billion in Q3 (up 89.0%), three consecutive quarterly increases.

The filings alone do not pin down the cause of the Q4 2025 drop. Q3 2026 revenue of KRW 260.1 billion and operating profit of KRW 53.7 billion rose 4.2% and 0.9% year on year, respectively. The debt-to-equity ratio fell from 108.6% in 2022 to 72.2% in 2025, and operating cash flow in 2025 was KRW 153.9 billion.

05

Industry analysis

For a test-preparation company like MegastudyEdu, results depend heavily on the college entrance system and on demand from repeat-year and N-su students.

LS Securities explained that from 2027, when current second-year high school students sit the exam, both integrated science and integrated social studies will fall within the exploration section.

LS Securities argued that exploration-subject preparation is therefore more likely to shift toward online lectures than offline academies. Press reports put third-quarter revenue at about KRW 261 billion in 2023, after declines in 2024 and 2025, before it returned to the KRW 260 billion range in 2026.

Over the same span, operating profit of about KRW 59 billion in Q3 2023 compared with KRW 53.7 billion in Q3 2026, so profit recovery has lagged the revenue recovery. Asia Economae reported that third-quarter results exceeded market and company estimates.

In competition, the repeat-year market turns on how courses are structured and how mock exams and other content differ. Because the company's business mix differs from other listed companies in the same sector, simple metric comparisons are limited.

Demand sits where entrance-system changes meet the spread of online lectures, so which channel gains share will shape the industry's direction.

Outlook

The next regular filing is the 2026 annual report. Q4 2026 results will not be filed separately and will be included in that report, so the recovery from the KRW 8.98 billion Q4 2025 operating profit will be reflected in full-year figures.

Cumulative revenue for January to September 2026 rose 1.9%, but cumulative operating profit fell 3.5%, and an industry source argued that full-year profitability recovery depends on Q4 earnings. The company's stated directions center on repeat-year comprehensive courses, N-su classes and expanded mock-exam offerings.

LS Securities viewed structural demand for online lectures as likely to grow once the exploration section broadens from 2027. That outlook is one brokerage's analysis, not a demand metric disclosed by the company.

On the business side, the late-2024 sale of the civil-service exam business raises the question of what structure future earnings will come from. Quarterly earnings are volatile, so whether Q4 2026 operating profit extends the three-quarter rising streak will be central to judging the full-year trend. The Q1 2027 quarterly report will allow comparison with Q1 2026 operating profit of KRW 26 billion.

08

Bull factors

Third-quarter revenue recovery and beat on estimates

Q3 2026 revenue of KRW 260.1 billion rose 4.2% year on year, and operating profit of KRW 53.7 billion rose 0.9%. Asia Economae reported that the quarter exceeded market and company estimates. Revenue returning to the KRW 260 billion range after three years is cited as evidence of top-line recovery.

Forecast of wider online-lecture demand from entrance reform

LS Securities analyzed that a broader exploration section under the 2028 entrance reform would likely shift preparation toward online lectures rather than offline academies. Brokerage target prices differ widely. This is one brokerage's outlook, not a demand metric confirmed by the company.

Improved balance sheet and steady cash generation

The debt-to-equity ratio fell from 108.6% in 2022 to 72.2% in 2025. Total equity at the end of 2025 was KRW 520.6 billion, of which KRW 497.1 billion belonged to controlling shareholders. Operating cash flow stayed at or above KRW 150 billion a year from 2022 to 2025.

09

Bear factors

Cumulative operating profit still declining

Cumulative revenue for January to September 2026 rose 1.9% to about KRW 701 billion, but cumulative operating profit fell 3.5% to about KRW 108 billion. Digital Daily reported that the cumulative operating margin declined from 16.28% to 15.41%.

An industry source said that the third-quarter improvement alone does not show full-year profitability has recovered.

Three years of falling operating profit and lower margins

Operating profit fell to KRW 127.4 billion in 2023, KRW 123.6 billion in 2024 and KRW 121 billion in 2025, a decline in each of those three years. The operating margin of 13.7% in 2025 remained below the 16.2% of 2022. Revenue also fell 6.1% in 2025 to KRW 884.9 billion.

Quarterly volatility and limited comparability

Net profit attributable to controlling shareholders was KRW 2.44 billion in Q4 2025, down 93.7% from the prior quarter, before rebounding to KRW 22.6 billion in Q1 2026. Quarterly swings this large make the trend hard to read.

The late-2024 sale of the civil-service exam business also makes profitability before and after the change hard to compare directly.

10

Risk factors

Pace of entrance-system change

Whether the 2028 entrance reform applies on schedule and how far the exploration section expands cannot yet be confirmed. Whether online demand actually grows and whether offline comprehensive courses keep their share remain brokerage-level expectations. A change in the system could require the company to restructure its product lineup.

Durability of the profit recovery

Operating profit has risen for three straight quarters since Q1 2026, but cumulative profit for January to September 2026 is still down 3.5%. The KRW 85.1 billion total for the last four quarters depends on quarterly swings, such as the KRW 38.5 billion booked in Q3 2025. Whether Q4 2026 keeps the recovery going is the key question.

Limited disclosure and changing business mix

The Q3 2026 preliminary disclosure did not include revenue or cost changes by business. The late-2024 sale of the civil-service exam business changed the business mix, so comparisons with earlier figures are not straightforward. Segment profit contribution has to be checked in the regular reports.

12

Overall view

Third-quarter 2026 revenue of KRW 260.1 billion (up 4.2% year on year) and operating profit of KRW 53.7 billion (up 0.9%) both rose.

However, operating profit fell for three consecutive years from 2023 to 2025, and cumulative operating profit for January to September 2026 fell 3.5%, so the full-year profitability recovery is not yet settled.

Operating profit rising for three straight quarters from Q1 to Q3 2026 is a positive sign, but quarterly profit swings are large, so judging the trend from one or two quarters is premature. The debt-to-equity ratio improved, falling from 108.6% in 2022 to 72.2% in 2025.

On the business side, LS Securities' view that online-lecture demand could grow after the 2028 reform coexists with reports that the business-mix change makes comparisons with past profitability difficult.

The KRW 60,000 target price that LS Securities presented in its November 2026 report reflects one brokerage's view. The next checkpoints are the operating margin in the 2026 annual report, against 13.7% in 2025, and the Q1 2027 quarterly report.
