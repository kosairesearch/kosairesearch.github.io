02

Business structure

HD Hyundai Heavy Industries operates in three areas: shipbuilding, which includes commercial vessels and naval ships; engine and machinery; and offshore plant.

In the third quarter of 2026, shipbuilding sales were KRW 4.40 trillion, down 11.6% from the prior quarter, with operating profit of KRW 646.3 billion and an operating margin of 14.7%. Within shipbuilding, commercial vessel operating profit was KRW 607.7 billion and naval vessel operating profit was KRW 38.6 billion.

The engine and machinery segment posted sales of KRW 951.1 billion and operating profit of KRW 229.9 billion, keeping its margin at 24.2%. The offshore plant segment recorded sales of KRW 379.9 billion and operating profit of KRW 126.5 billion, a 33.3% margin, supported by progress on the Ruya project.

After its 2025 merger with an affiliated shipbuilder, the company planned to convert part of the combined dock capacity to naval construction. Naval pipeline targets include follow-on units for two frigates for the Philippines and one for Thailand, two offshore patrol vessels for Estonia, and one submarine for Peru.

Europe has led the naval market, but interest in Korean yards is spreading. Domestic rivals Hanwha Ocean and Samsung Heavy Industries compete with the company for commercial and naval orders.

04

Earnings analysis

Annual revenue rose from KRW 9.05 trillion in 2022 to KRW 12 trillion in 2023 (up 32.3%), KRW 14.5 trillion in 2024 (up 21.1%), and KRW 17.6 trillion in 2025 (up 21.4%), with three consecutive years of growth from 2023 through 2025. 2025 was the largest year in the 2022–2025 period.

Operating profit moved from an operating loss of KRW 289.2 billion in 2022 to KRW 178.6 billion in 2023, a turn to profit from the prior year, then KRW 705.2 billion in 2024 (up 294.8%) and KRW 2.04 trillion in 2025 (up 188.9%), with two consecutive years of growth from 2024 through 2025.

The operating margin went from -3.2% in 2022 to 1.5% in 2023, 4.9% in 2024, and 11.6% in 2025. Profit attributable to controlling shareholders moved from a net loss of KRW 352.1 billion in 2022 to KRW 24.7 billion in 2023, a turn to profit, then KRW 621.5 billion in 2024 and KRW 1.42 trillion in 2025.

Net profit including non-controlling interests was also reported at KRW 1.42 trillion for 2025. The debt-to-equity ratio at the end of 2025 was 180.1%, down from 239.9% in 2024, with total equity of KRW 9.34 trillion and total liabilities of KRW 16.8 trillion.

Operating cash flow grew from KRW 71.2 billion in 2022 to KRW 3.51 trillion in 2025.

Quarterly operating profit ran KRW 557.3 billion in the third quarter of 2025, KRW 575 billion in the fourth quarter, KRW 905.4 billion in the first quarter of 2026 (up 57.4% from the prior quarter), and KRW 1.04 trillion in the second quarter (up 14.9%), before falling to KRW 899.2 billion in the third quarter (down 13.5%).

For the third-quarter decline, the conference call of HD Korea Shipbuilding & Offshore Engineering cited 47 working days, 14 fewer than the prior quarter, with four more lost days costing about KRW 50 billion in operating profit.

The average quarterly exchange rate decline was cited as a negative of about KRW 70 billion, and additional labor-agreement costs were reported.

Foreign-exchange-related losses of KRW 400.6 billion were reflected in non-operating results, pushing third-quarter net profit down 56.9% from the prior quarter to KRW 354.3 billion.

Profit attributable to controlling shareholders over the last four quarters (fourth quarter of 2025 to third quarter of 2026) totaled KRW 2.44 trillion.

05

Industry analysis

Reports indicate that rising demand for LPG and ammonia shipping has kept gas carrier newbuilding prices at high levels. The VLGC contract with a Middle Eastern owner in October 2026 was reported at roughly KRW 156.4 billion per vessel (about USD 116 million).

Middle Eastern producers and shipowners are expanding LPG and ammonia transport capacity, and orders are reportedly concentrating at Korean yards. In the naval market, rising security tensions and stronger maritime defense policies are lifting demand for naval power upgrades across several countries.

The company said naval order inquiries, once limited to Southeast Asia and South America, have broadened globally. Icebreakers have long been dominated by European yards. The parent, HD Korea Shipbuilding & Offshore Engineering, reported third-quarter 2026 operating profit of KRW 1,447.2 billion, up 37.3% year on year.

Domestic shipbuilders are competing for high-value commercial and naval orders at the same time. Naval earnings are still small relative to commercial shipbuilding, so the company's position in the cycle depends on two drivers: gas carrier pricing in commercial vessels and execution of naval contracts.

06

Outlook

The 2026 order target was set at USD 20.42 billion, of which shipbuilding accounts for USD 14.5 billion, split into USD 11.5 billion for commercial vessels and USD 3 billion for naval ships.

A January 2026 report put the offshore and plant target at USD 3.26 billion, while another securities report cited USD 3.6 billion, so the figures differ by source. Cumulative orders were USD 611.9 million in the first quarter of 2026 and USD 1.85 billion through September (provisional).

The 2026 revenue target of KRW 24.4 trillion was reported in January 2026, compared with KRW 18.0 trillion in nine-month 2026 sales. Naval revenue targets of KRW 7 trillion for 2030 and KRW 10 trillion for 2035 were presented at the merger briefing.

Naval pipeline items include follow-on frigates for the Philippines and Thailand, two offshore patrol vessels for Estonia, and a submarine for Peru. Part of the combined dock capacity is planned for naval construction.

The company also won a first EPC contract for a 500MW offshore substation project in Taean after a main contract was signed. The next-generation Aegis destroyer has been launched, with delivery to the Navy scheduled for December 2027.

The gap between the annual order target and actual intake will become clear through fourth-quarter contract disclosures.

08

Bull factors

Expanding order intake

Cumulative orders through September 2026 were USD 1.85 billion on a provisional basis, up 98.34% year on year, against USD 932 million in the same period of 2025. That places most of the USD 20.42 billion annual target already booked within nine months, indicating a larger order pipeline in 2026.

High-value vessel orders

The VLGC contract with a Middle Eastern owner in October 2026 is worth KRW 625.8 billion, with deliveries spread through the mid-2030s. The LPGC contract with an Oceania owner, dated November 13, 2026, is worth KRW 272.1 billion.

Gas carrier orders continue amid LPG and ammonia transport demand and high newbuilding prices, which is cited as a profitability tailwind.

Structural profit improvement

Operating profit moved from an operating loss of KRW 289.2 billion in 2022 to KRW 2.04 trillion in 2025, with a margin of 11.6%. Cumulative operating profit through the third quarter of 2026 reached KRW 2.84 trillion, up 94.5% year on year.

Engine and machinery, at a 24.2% margin, and offshore plant, at 33.3%, supported group profitability in the third quarter.

09

Bear factors

Quarterly profit softening

Third-quarter 2026 operating profit fell 13.5% from the prior quarter, and sales fell 9.1%. Shipbuilding sales declining 11.6% from the prior quarter was reported as the main factor. Quarterly operating profit has come down from KRW 1.04 trillion in the second quarter of 2026.

Foreign-exchange drag

In the third quarter, foreign-exchange-related losses of KRW 400.6 billion were reflected in non-operating results, bringing non-operating losses to KRW 424.3 billion. As a result, net profit fell 17.8% year on year even as operating profit rose. The lower average exchange rate was reported to have cut operating profit by about KRW 70 billion.

Naval contribution still early

Naval operating profit in the third quarter was KRW 38.6 billion, against KRW 607.7 billion for commercial vessels. The naval sales target of KRW 7 trillion for 2030 is far ahead of the current contribution to earnings.

Turning the USD 3 billion naval order target into reported profit will take time through execution and delivery.

10

Risk factors

Currency and FX-related losses

Foreign-exchange-related losses of KRW 400.6 billion were booked in non-operating results in the third quarter of 2026, weighing on net profit. The exchange rate decline was reported to have cut operating profit by about KRW 70 billion. With orders largely denominated in dollars, currency swings remain a source of quarterly earnings volatility.

Working days and production disruption

Third-quarter working days were 47, 14 fewer than the prior quarter, with four further lost days costing about KRW 50 billion in operating profit. Seasonal factors combined with stoppages can move quarterly earnings sharply. Additional labor-agreement costs were also reflected in third-quarter results.

Order concentration and newbuilding price cycle

Gas carrier orders in 2026 are concentrated in high-priced vessels, so a pullback in newbuilding prices could narrow margin gains. Deliveries on large orders run into the mid-2030s, and production delays would affect delivery schedules and revenue recognition. Naval projects carry execution risk that grows with the scale of the order target.

Overall view

Cumulative orders through September 2026 were USD 1.85 billion on a provisional basis, up 98.34% year on year, covering most of the USD 20.42 billion annual target.

On earnings, operating profit moved from a loss of KRW 289.2 billion in 2022 to KRW 2.04 trillion in 2025, and the debt-to-equity ratio fell to 180.1% at the end of 2025.

Third-quarter 2026 operating profit of KRW 899.2 billion was up 61.3% year on year but down 13.5% from the prior quarter, and profit attributable to controlling shareholders fell 56.9% from the prior quarter to KRW 354.3 billion after foreign-exchange-related losses of KRW 400.6 billion.

Strengths include high-value gas carrier orders, a larger naval target, and high-margin engine and offshore plant segments. Weaknesses include sensitivity of quarterly profit to exchange rates and working days, swings in foreign-exchange items, and the still-limited earnings contribution from naval work.
