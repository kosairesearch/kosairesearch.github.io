02

Business structure

HL Genomics is a basic pharmaceutical substance manufacturer whose main products are synthetic APIs. Founded in 2000, it produces APIs, the key raw materials of finished drugs.

Built on high-purity crystallization and impurity-control technology, its portfolio centers on chronic-disease treatments in the cardiovascular, respiratory, musculoskeletal and nervous-system areas.

Main products include pitavastatin calcium for hyperlipidemia, an esamlodipine salt for hypertension and bepotastine besilate, an antihistamine ingredient. In 2025, cardiovascular products made up 45.2% of sales, respiratory 27.6%, musculoskeletal 7.9% and nervous system 7.3%. According to the company website, it also supplies intermediates and reference standards alongside APIs.

Its ability to reliably produce domestically made, high-quality APIs in a market dominated by low-priced Chinese and Indian suppliers is cited as a strength. An IR Qudus report noted that a new customer switching suppliers must go through process validation and approval changes, which creates high entry barriers.

The company says about 90% of its sales came from Hanlim Pharm in its early years. That share fell to about 54.5% in 2025 as external customers were added. The HL in the name derives from Hanlim and has no connection to genomic analysis.

New initiatives include contract development and manufacturing (CMO/CDMO) and vaccine microneedle manufacturing.

03

Recent trends

Per DART filings, cumulative first-half 2026 revenue was KRW 6.61 billion, up 6.2% year on year. Operating profit rose 6.7% to KRW 2.12 billion, and the cumulative operating margin of roughly 32% was in line with the annual pattern. Net income was KRW 1.79 billion, up only 0.2% and trailing operating profit growth.

Press reports from the offering stage cited first-quarter 2026 revenue of KRW 8.4 billion, which exceeds the cumulative half-year filing figure, so the two should not be compared directly until the basis of each is confirmed.

On a consolidated basis, revenue rose from KRW 24.8 billion in 2023 to KRW 28.2 billion in 2024 and KRW 28.9 billion in 2025, while operating profit was KRW 7.7 billion, KRW 9.0 billion and KRW 9.3 billion. Revenue growth slowed from 13.52% in 2024 to 2.52% in 2025 and 2.35% in the first quarter of 2026.

On its KOSDAQ debut on July 24, 2026, the stock closed at KRW 14,790, down 31.21% from the KRW 21,500 offer price. Early in the session it had risen as high as KRW 25,550, or 1.2 times the offer price. That day, surging oil prices sent the KOSPI down 5.72% and the KOSDAQ down 5.32%.

Analysts also pointed to the fact that 98.1% of institutional participation was not covered by lock-up commitments. On October 27, 2026, the stock closed at KRW 7,160 (-0.56% from the prior day), with a market capitalization of KRW 55.6 billion and trading value of KRW 0.2 billion. At the confirmed offer price, the post-listing market capitalization had been estimated at KRW 167.3 billion.

04

Outlook

The company expects the second plant to cost KRW 75.9 billion, said KRW 12.1 billion had been spent before the listing, and reported roughly KRW 38 billion in cash-equivalent deposits. The second plant is planned to have a floor area about 4.4 times that of the first, with one large multi-line unit and two small lines.

The company expects per-product capacity to expand 1.5 to 2 times and manufacturing costs to improve by 10% to 30%. Under the plan, the building is completed in the fourth quarter of 2027, approvals and qualification finish by the end of 2028, and commercial production starts in 2029.

The earnings contribution from the expansion therefore lies some time ahead, and until then the first plant's remaining capacity and demand from the parent largely drive results.

In microneedles, press reports said technology fees that the partner receives from global pharmaceutical R&D collaboration could begin as early as the second half of 2026 through 2027. Revenue from the company directly manufacturing and supplying microneedles is expected as early as 2029.

The investment in a microneedle manufacturing site will be decided after progress is checked in 2028. The indicators to watch are whether operating margins stay above 30% after the expansion, whether the external customer share actually rises, and whether CDMO orders materialize. The direction of these indicators will be tested in future disclosures and periodic reports.

05

Bull factors

Sustained high profitability and low leverage

The company has posted an operating profit for 19 consecutive years, with a 2023-2025 average operating margin of 31.7%. At the end of 2025, equity was KRW 83.6 billion, total liabilities KRW 5.5 billion and the debt ratio 6.6%.

In the filed first half of 2026, both revenue and operating profit also rose from a year earlier. This earnings base helps the company fund its expansion from internal cash and offering proceeds.

Entry barriers and demand for domestic ingredients

A finished-drug maker that wants to change its ingredient supplier must go through process validation and approval changes. This structure favors suppliers that already have products in the supply chain.

The ability to reliably make high-quality domestic APIs in a market led by low-priced Chinese and Indian producers is viewed as a fundamental strength. The chronic-disease focus relies on recurring prescription demand, which keeps revenue volatility relatively low.

Expansion, new businesses and governance safeguards

The second plant, with EU GMP-grade facilities, could provide a base for CDMO work and direct exports. Hanlim Pharm committed to a 2.5-year lock-up on its remaining stake after the secondary sale, to use the secondary-sale proceeds only for facility investment, and to forgo dividends for five years.

The company also pledged to bar the parent's controlling shareholder from direct or indirect management involvement and to block improper support among affiliates after listing. These measures partly ease concerns about overhang and conflicts of interest involving the parent.

06

Bear factors

Slowing growth and delayed expansion benefit

Slower revenue growth is explained by the first plant running at full capacity, leaving little room for extra output. First-plant utilization was 124% in 2024, 118% in 2025 and 108% in the first quarter of 2026. Filed first-half 2026 revenue growth was 6.2%, but net income growth was only 0.2%. With the second plant contributing to sales only from 2029, top-line growth could be limited in the interim.

Dependence on parent Hanlim Pharm

Sales to Hanlim Pharm were reported at KRW 12.8 billion in 2023, KRW 11.3 billion in 2024 and KRW 15.7 billion in 2025, above 50% of revenue. Commentators noted that the weight of related-party sales may act as a discount factor in valuation.

The fact that CDMO development cases so far center on collaboration with the parent is also pointed to as a limitation. If external customer growth falls short of plan, changes in the parent's orders could directly affect results.

Supply-demand pressure and thin liquidity

98.1% of institutional participation had no lock-up commitment, and institutions without long-term holding pledges were analyzed to have released shares from the first day. The stock closed at KRW 7,160 on October 27, 2026, well below the KRW 21,500 offer price.

Trading value that day was only KRW 0.2 billion, so liquidity is not deep. Thinly traded stocks can see sizable price moves even on small orders.

07

Risk factors

Macro

On the listing day, investor sentiment weakened as geopolitical tension between the U.S. and Iran pushed oil prices sharply higher and U.S. stocks fell. Geopolitical variables and oil prices of this kind raise volatility across KOSDAQ and affect small-cap share prices.

Swings in exchange rates and raw-material prices could also affect the cost structure of synthetic ingredient manufacturing. The specific sensitivity has not been confirmed in filings.

Industry

The API market competes with low-priced supply from Chinese and Indian producers, so pricing pressure persists. Changes in pricing or approval conditions for customers' finished drugs could also affect demand for ingredients.

The company said the second plant will prepare it for CMO/CDMO work and for highly regulated markets such as the EU. However, winning CDMO customers and developing overseas markets are assessed to be at an early stage.

Company-specific

The second plant targets groundbreaking in 2026, building completion at the end of 2027 and full set-up by the end of 2028. Construction delays or cost overruns could push back the payback period.

One report also assessed that funding capacity is tight for carrying both the second plant and a dedicated microneedle plant at the same time. The microneedle business depends on its partner's success in commercializing the technology.

08

Overall view

HL Genomics is a small manufacturer focused on chronic-disease APIs, with operating margins above 30% for three straight years and a low debt ratio.

Per DART filings, cumulative first-half 2026 revenue was KRW 6.61 billion, operating profit KRW 2.12 billion and net income KRW 1.79 billion, all higher than a year earlier. Net income growth was only 0.2%, and revenue growth has slowed as the first plant runs at saturated utilization.

The second plant targets commercial production from 2029, so the benefit of the expansion will take time to verify. Hanlim Pharm's 54.5% share of 2025 revenue can be read both as a stable demand base and as a customer-concentration burden.

After listing, the stock trades at KRW 7,160, well below the KRW 21,500 offer price, on thin trading value. Whether margins hold, external customers grow and the expansion schedule is met can be tracked through future filings, and the judgment is left to each reader.
