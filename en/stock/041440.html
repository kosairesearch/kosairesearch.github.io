02

Business structure

Hyundai Everdigm makes and sells construction equipment attachments, concrete pump trucks, fire trucks and tower cranes, operating as a special-purpose machinery maker.

According to a JobKorea company profile as of March 2026, the sales mix is 49% hydraulic breakers and crushers, 32% concrete pump trucks and fire trucks, and 19% heavy equipment.

The attachments unit develops its own hydraulic breakers, crushers and grabs for excavators and supplies them to construction sites in Korea and abroad. Its concrete pump trucks are positioned on high-output, high-efficiency engines and hydraulic control that shorten construction time and reduce maintenance costs.

In fire trucks, the company signed a fire-rescue vehicle supply contract with Iraq's Ministry of Interior in August 2025, which it describes as its largest such contract to date.

In specialty equipment, a disaster-response facility supply contract for power plants with a state-run nuclear operator was signed in 2023 and is being delivered. Overseas, the company operates sales subsidiaries in China, the United States, Mongolia and Panama.

Its largest shareholder, which holds 45.35% as of September 3, 2026, controls the company. Doosan Bobcat and HD Construction Equipment are listed in the same machinery sector and serve as comparison points for the construction equipment business.

The attachments line, close to half of the sales mix, is the business most directly tied to excavator demand and therefore sensitive to construction cycles.

04

Earnings analysis

On a disclosed basis, revenue went from KRW 374.4 billion in 2022 to KRW 358.1 billion in 2023 (down 4.4% year on year), KRW 355.1 billion in 2024 (down 0.8%) and KRW 289.6 billion in 2025 (down 18.5%).

Revenue declined for three consecutive years from 2023, with 2022 the largest year and 2025 the smallest in the 2022-2025 period. The company attributed the 2025 revenue decline mainly to a slowdown in domestic construction.

Operating profit went from KRW 15.7 billion in 2022 to KRW 12 billion in 2023 (down 23.7%), KRW 9.95 billion in 2024 (down 17.0%) and KRW 5.41 billion in 2025 (down 45.7%), declining for three consecutive years from 2023.

The operating margin narrowed from 4.2% in 2022 to 1.9% in 2025, so margins thinned alongside the sales decline. Profit attributable to owners of the parent was KRW 10.7 billion in 2022, KRW 6.39 billion in 2023, KRW 6.58 billion in 2024 (up 3.0%) and KRW 4.64 billion in 2025 (down 29.4%).

Operating cash flow ran at KRW -16.9 billion in 2022, KRW 37.6 billion in 2023, KRW 8.01 billion in 2024 and KRW -4.99 billion in 2025, with wide swings year to year. Quarterly, revenue rose from KRW 64.7 billion with operating profit of KRW 500 million in Q3 2025 to KRW 77.5 billion with KRW 3.19 billion in Q4 2025.

Q1 2026 revenue was KRW 82 billion with operating profit of KRW 577 million, and Q2 2026 revenue was KRW 79.7 billion with operating profit of KRW 3.2 billion.

In Q3 2026, revenue fell to KRW 57 billion (down 28.5% from the prior quarter), the company posted an operating loss of KRW 1.52 billion, and the loss attributable to owners of the parent was KRW 4.92 billion. Over the last four quarters, profit attributable to owners of the parent totaled KRW 3.05 billion.

05

Industry analysis

Demand for construction equipment has moved in different directions at home and abroad. One securities data service reported that weaker domestic construction cut sales of concrete pump trucks and excavator attachments.

The same source pointed to a pickup in overseas mining and expanding Middle East construction projects as factors that lifted results. Because the attachments line holds the largest share of the sales mix, the pace of a domestic excavator recovery is a key driver of revenue.

Overseas, the Mongolian mining market is served through the company's sales subsidiary network. Tightening carbon-emission rules in Europe and North America have led the company to pursue electrified and hybrid pump truck development.

A rating-agency report described the company as seeking growth from eco-friendly and intelligent technology and from diversifying into overseas markets.

The cycle is currently split between a domestic construction slump and improvement in overseas mining and Middle East construction, and which side recovers faster will shape the company's position in the sector.

Compared with peers such as Doosan Bobcat and HD Construction Equipment, the company has a relatively heavier mix of specialty vehicles such as concrete pump trucks and fire trucks.

06

Outlook

A consolidated aggregate for the first half of 2026 shows revenue up 9.7% and operating profit up 120.2% year on year, with net income turning positive. However, Q3 revenue fell to KRW 57 billion and the company posted an operating loss of KRW 1.52 billion, interrupting the first-half improvement.

Deliveries under the August 2025 fire-rescue vehicle supply contract with Iraq's Ministry of Interior are in progress, and how that volume is reflected in revenue after Q4 is a checkpoint. The company says it is diversifying its customer base by opening new markets in North America, Latin America and Europe.

Electrified and hybrid pump trucks and smart fire trucks with remote control are described as in development, and no public timetable for commercialization has been confirmed. For disaster-response equipment, the company is seeking to extend from power plants in Korea to overseas power and plant markets.

The next checkpoint is the 2026 fourth-quarter preliminary results in early February 2027, where the questions are whether the Q3 loss continues and whether revenue recovers toward the Q1 2026 level of KRW 82 billion.

After that, the 2026 annual report due in late March 2027 will show how the full-year operating margin compares with 1.9% in 2025.

08

Bull factors

Overseas mining and Middle East demand

One securities data service pointed to a revival in overseas mining and expanding Middle East construction projects as factors that lifted first-half 2026 results. The overseas sales subsidiary network, including Mongolia, is the channel that captures this demand.

In the same aggregate, first-half 2026 revenue rose 9.7% and operating profit rose 120.2% year on year. Overseas demand can be read as partly filling the gap left by the domestic construction slowdown.

Return to operating profit in 2026

Operating profit was KRW 3.19 billion in Q4 2025, then KRW 577 million in Q1 2026 and KRW 3.2 billion in Q2 2026, a run of profitable quarters. In Q1 2026 preliminary results, revenue rose 32.1% year on year and operating profit moved from a loss in the prior-year quarter to a profit. Supporters point to this profitable run before the Q3 loss as evidence of the earnings base.

Export diversification and new products

The company says it is diversifying its markets by opening new territories in North America, Latin America and Europe. It is developing electrified and hybrid pump trucks in response to tightening emission rules in Europe and North America, and researching smart fire trucks with remote control. Its disaster-response equipment is being positioned for overseas power plant and plant markets.

09

Bear factors

Domestic construction slump

Revenue in 2025 fell 18.5% to KRW 289.6 billion, and the company cited the domestic construction slowdown as the main cause. Sales of concrete pump trucks and excavator attachments also declined in the same period.

Attachments and pump trucks are closely tied to domestic construction investment, so a slow demand recovery constrains a rebound in revenue.

Swing back to loss in Q3 2026

Q3 2026 revenue fell 28.5% from the prior quarter to KRW 57 billion, and the company recorded an operating loss of KRW 1.52 billion and a loss attributable to owners of KRW 4.92 billion. Revenue has declined for two consecutive quarters since the Q1 2026 peak.

Compared with an operating profit of KRW 500 million in Q3 2025, the quarter also turned to a loss year on year.

Thin earnings and volatile cash flow

The operating margin narrowed from 4.2% in 2022 to 1.9% in 2025, and operating cash flow was KRW -4.99 billion in 2025. Profit attributable to owners over the last four quarters totaled KRW 3.05 billion, a thin base. The Q3 loss further reduces the earnings cushion.

10

Risk factors

Construction demand concentration

A large share of sales comes from excavator attachments and concrete pump trucks, which are sensitive to construction investment cycles. A prolonged domestic slump could extend declines in core product sales. How far overseas expansion offsets this gap is the key variable.

Volatility in overseas mining demand

Because overseas mining conditions were one driver of the first-half 2026 improvement, a downturn in mining demand could weaken that recovery. Greater reliance on particular regions, such as through the Mongolian subsidiary, raises regional risk. Currency movements and export conditions could also affect the profitability of overseas sales.

Earnings and cash flow volatility

Operating profit fell from KRW 15.7 billion in 2022 to KRW 5.41 billion in 2025 and turned to a loss in Q3 2026. Operating cash flow also changed direction several times, from KRW -16.9 billion in 2022 to KRW 37.6 billion in 2023, KRW 8.01 billion in 2024 and KRW -4.99 billion in 2025. Quarterly profit moving between profit and loss widens the range of possible outcomes.

Overall view

Hyundai Everdigm is a machinery company that covers both construction equipment and specialty vehicles, and its results combine a first-half 2026 improvement with a Q3 2026 swing to loss, against a backdrop of declining revenue and operating profit since 2022.

On a disclosed basis, revenue fell from KRW 374.4 billion in 2022 to KRW 289.6 billion in 2025, and the operating margin narrowed from 4.2% to 1.9%.

Profitable operations in Q1 and Q2 2026 gave way in Q3, when revenue fell to KRW 57 billion and the company posted an operating loss of KRW 1.52 billion and a loss attributable to owners of KRW 4.92 billion.

Strengths include overseas mining and Middle East demand, overseas fire-rescue and disaster-response contracts, and electrified product development. Weaknesses include the domestic construction slump, the Q3 loss, a thin earnings base and volatile cash flow.

Valuation sits low relative to book net assets, but the earnings-based reading is wide-ranging. The next checkpoints are the 2026 fourth-quarter and full-year preliminary results in early February 2027, the 2026 annual report in late March 2027, and the Q1 2027 preliminary results in early May 2027.
