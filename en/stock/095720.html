02

Business structure

Woongjin Thinkbig operates children's education services built on membership-based learning programs and book sets, along with online and offline learning and publishing. Its core products are the digital learning service Woongjin Smart All and Woongjin BookClub.

In 2025 Q3, Smart All revenue was KRW 58.7 billion, down KRW 3.3 billion year on year, while the Thinkbig and Smart Thinkbig learning programs fell 9.5% to KRW 46.4 billion. A tablet-based learning market with intense competition was cited as the main cause of the decline.

On a consolidated basis, the group includes Woongjin Thinkbig (education and future-education), an English education and mobile platform unit, and a publication distribution and logistics unit.

In the segment table of the 2026 Q1 quarterly report, Woongjin Thinkbig's segment revenue was KRW 134.5 billion with a segment operating loss of KRW 3.1 billion.

The English education unit posted revenue of KRW 8.2 billion and an operating loss of KRW 0.4 billion, while the distribution and logistics unit posted revenue of KRW 40.5 billion and operating profit of KRW 0.05 billion; these figures are before intersegment eliminations.

The company exited the AI digital textbook (AIDT) business and shifted toward a platform strategy centered on Smart All. In the public sector it is expanding B2G business through the AI reading solution Book Story, backed by an in-house AI lab and 63 edtech patents.

04

Earnings analysis

Annual revenue went from KRW 933.3 billion in 2022 to KRW 890.1 billion in 2023 (down 4.6% year on year), KRW 867.2 billion in 2024 (down 2.6%) and KRW 797.4 billion in 2025 (down 8.1%). Revenue declined for three consecutive years from 2023 to 2025; 2022 was the largest year and 2025 the smallest within 2022 to 2025.

Operating profit fell from KRW 27.6 billion in 2022 to KRW 5.57 billion in 2023 (down 79.8%), recovered to KRW 9.25 billion in 2024 (up 65.9%), and turned into an operating loss of KRW 10.5 billion in 2025.

Reporting on the regulatory filings said 2025 was the first operating loss for the education and culture business since its 2007 spin-off. The same reports linked the 2025 loss to impairment charges from exiting the AI digital textbook business and to organizational restructuring costs.

Net income attributable to controlling shareholders moved from KRW 3.11 billion in 2022 to a net loss of KRW 32.2 billion in 2023, a loss of KRW 19.5 billion in 2024 (narrower) and a loss of KRW 22.7 billion in 2025 (wider).

Net income including non-controlling interests was a loss of KRW 19.9 billion in 2024 and KRW 22.7 billion in 2025. The operating margin declined from 3.0% in 2022 to -1.3% in 2025. Operating cash flow was KRW 94.6 billion in 2022, KRW 73.4 billion in 2023, KRW 63.4 billion in 2024 and KRW 78.8 billion in 2025.

On a quarterly basis, operating losses of KRW 926 million in 2025 Q3, KRW 2.38 billion in Q4 and KRW 3.37 billion in 2026 Q1 were followed by operating profit of KRW 3.81 billion in 2026 Q2.

Q3 operating profit of KRW 4.12 billion rose 8.1% from the prior quarter, while Q3 revenue of KRW 181.2 billion fell 9.2% year on year. Net income attributable to controlling shareholders was KRW 988 million in Q2 and KRW 718 million in Q3, but the last four quarters total a net loss of KRW 11.2 billion.

05

Industry analysis

The shrinking school-age population is cited as a structural drag on Woongjin Thinkbig's revenue. The company attributes the education and culture unit's revenue decline to the shrinking school-age population and government measures to reduce private tutoring costs.

In 2025 Q3 the group said the shrinking school-age population and an economic slowdown weakened the business climate. According to a report by The Electronic Times, Smart All revenue fell from KRW 62.0 billion in 2024 Q3 to KRW 58.7 billion in 2025 Q3.

Intensifying competition among tablet-based learning services compounds this trend. On the cycle, the company appears to be in a phase where cost normalization and restructuring came before any recovery in top-line growth.

Edumorning Lab described 2025 as the year restructuring costs appeared first and 2026 as the year the company must prove results. The same report suggested that cost efficiency and a leaner organization could gradually show up in earnings, with signs of a bottom possible. Listed learning-content peers such as MegaStudy and The Pinkfong Company operate in adjacent markets.

06

Outlook

For 2026 the company said it would upgrade the AI features of Woongjin Smart All and step up its B2G push through the Book Story platform.

At a board meeting on March 11, 2026, it finalized a value-up package covering the cancellation of 1.85 million treasury shares, cash dividends per share, and the transfer of KRW 100 billion of capital reserves into retained earnings.

It also plans a 2-to-1 par value consolidation of common shares to improve share price stability. Alongside shareholder returns, it is strengthening services: a time-limited Big Plus offer lets existing BookClub, Superpod English and Smart All members use points for visiting learning, study rooms and academy services.

Point usage has been extended to one-on-one visiting lessons, study rooms and academies, and conversion into a regular service is under review depending on results. A certification-based Master program is also being introduced.

The next checkpoints are whether the revenue decline stops in the 2026 annual report and how far the operating margin recovers from -1.3% in 2025. The timing of treasury share cancellation and par value consolidation has not been confirmed in filings yet.

08

Bull factors

Cost efficiency returns operating profit to the black

Operating profit was KRW 3.81 billion in 2026 Q2 after a turnaround to the black, and rose 8.1% to KRW 4.12 billion in Q3. Reporting on the Q1 loss narrowing pointed to lower selling and administrative expenses. If cost restructuring keeps operating profit positive even as revenue declines, that supports the bullish case.

Value-up package and shareholder returns

The package also includes a 2-to-1 par value consolidation of common shares. Whether the treasury share cancellation and dividends are actually carried out is the item to confirm for this bullish case.

Smart All AI features and public-sector expansion

Plans for 2026 include upgrading AI features in Woongjin Smart All and entering B2G business through Book Story. The company cites its in-house AI lab and 63 edtech patents as the basis of its technology edge.

Because Smart All revenue fell to KRW 58.7 billion in 2025 Q3, whether the AI features lead to a revenue recovery is the key question.

Bear factors

Continued revenue decline

Revenue declined for three consecutive years from 2023 to 2025, and 2026 Q3 revenue of KRW 181.2 billion fell 9.2% year on year. The company attributes part of the education and culture decline to the shrinking school-age population and measures to reduce private tutoring costs. Even with operating profit in the black, a continued shrinkage of the top line limits the scale of earnings.

Rising financial burden

The debt ratio was 182.6% at end-2025 and was reported at 190.3% at end-March 2026. Total borrowings stood at KRW 254.1 billion at end-September 2025, up 88.9% from end-2024. Working capital that relies on borrowing makes interest burden and liquidity management binding constraints.

Smart All revenue decline and intensifying competition

Woongjin Smart All revenue declined from KRW 62.0 billion in 2024 Q3 to KRW 58.7 billion in 2025 Q3. Commentary points to fierce competition in tablet-based learning.

The 2025 cost burden was heavy because of the AIDT exit and impairments, and whether AI services can build a repeatable revenue model is still under test.

10

Risk factors

Structural demand decline

The shrinking school-age population and measures to reduce private tutoring costs are external conditions that are hard to reverse in the short term. Q3 2026 revenue down 9.2% year on year fits this pattern. If the revenue decline does not stop, cost cuts alone have limits in sustaining operating profit.

Financial and liquidity

Cash and cash equivalents fell to about KRW 52.9 billion at end-March 2026 from about KRW 59.9 billion at end-2025, according to filing-based reporting. Over the same period equity declined, pushing up the debt ratio. Short-term debt repayment and interest payments produced a net cash outflow from financing activities.

Execution of the business restructuring

Whether the Smart All-centered strategy and AI upgrades turn into real revenue after the AIDT exit is not yet proven. The 2025 Q4 operating loss widened to KRW 2.38 billion, which reflects restructuring costs hitting results. Contribution from the B2G public-sector push is likely limited until the business scales.

Overall view

Woongjin Thinkbig returned to operating profit in 2026 Q2 and Q3 through cost efficiency, and its Q1 loss structure changed from the 2025 Q4 pattern. Revenue, however, declined for three consecutive years through 2025, and 2026 Q3 revenue was down 9.2% year on year.

Net loss attributable to controlling shareholders was recorded from 2023 to 2025, and the last four quarters total a KRW 11.2 billion loss. The debt ratio rose from 116.7% in 2022 to 182.6% at end-2025 and was reported at 190.3% at end-March 2026.

The value-up package and the proposed 2-to-1 par value consolidation mark a shift in shareholder returns, and their execution will be confirmed through future filings.

Bullish factors are the cost structure improvement and the shareholder return policy, while bearish factors are the shrinking top line and financial burden. The next checkpoint is how far the revenue decline trend and the operating margin recover in the 2026 annual report.
