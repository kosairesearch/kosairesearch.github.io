02

Business structure

PaperCorea is headquartered in Gunsan, North Jeolla Province, where it runs paper manufacturing and also carries on real estate development. The Gunsan plant produces industrial paper such as kraft paper, while its Cheongju subsidiary produces newsprint.

Industrial paper is a high-strength packaging paper used as base material for shopping bags, rice sacks and cement bags. The company describes itself as the domestic market share leader in kraft paper and says it is raising the share of recycled raw materials to meet demand for eco-friendly products.

In the Q1 2026 quarterly report, sales by product were 34 percent domestic industrial paper, 21 percent exports of industrial paper, 26 percent domestic newsprint and 19 percent exports of newsprint.

In the 2025 sales table, industrial paper and related products account for a larger share than newsprint and related products. Newsprint demand is shrinking as digital media grows, while quarterly commentary points to supply-demand balance recovering after capacity closures in the sector.

Real estate development uses a former plant site and operates alongside the manufacturing base. Listed peers in the same sector include Hansol Paper, Moorim P&P and DaelimPaper. Raw material pulp and paper resources are sourced from a market with many suppliers and no monopoly structure.

04

Earnings analysis

Annual revenue was KRW 410 billion in 2022, KRW 362.1 billion in 2023 (down 11.7% year on year), KRW 290.2 billion in 2024 (down 19.9%) and KRW 266.7 billion in 2025 (down 8.1%), falling for three consecutive years from 2023 through 2025. Among 2022 to 2025, 2022 was the largest year and 2025 the smallest.

Operating profit rose from KRW 25.8 billion in 2022 to KRW 31.2 billion in 2023 (up 20.6%), then fell to KRW 2.04 billion in 2024 (down 93.5%) and turned into an operating loss of KRW 5.28 billion in 2025.

Operating margin moved from 6.3 percent in 2022 and 8.6 percent in 2023 to 0.7 percent in 2024 and -2.0 percent in 2025. Net profit attributable to controlling shareholders swung from KRW 15.2 billion in 2023 to a net loss of KRW 9.68 billion in 2024 and KRW 19.3 billion in 2025, two consecutive years of losses.

Net income including non-controlling interests shows the same direction, with net losses of KRW 9.68 billion in 2024 and KRW 19.3 billion in 2025. The company attributed the 2025 decline to a temporary halt in operations during energy facility investment aimed at productivity gains and cost reduction.

Q1 2026 revenue was KRW 67.2 billion, operating profit KRW 1.24 billion, and net loss attributable to controlling shareholders KRW 665 million. Q2 2026 revenue rose to KRW 80.3 billion with operating profit of KRW 5.45 billion and net profit attributable to controlling shareholders of KRW 2.61 billion.

Q3 2026 revenue was KRW 71.7 billion, operating profit KRW 1.93 billion and net profit attributable to controlling shareholders KRW 66.7 million. Over the four quarters from Q4 2025 to Q3 2026, net loss attributable to controlling shareholders totaled KRW 5.07 billion.

Operating cash flow stayed positive every year, at KRW 13.4 billion in 2022, KRW 56.4 billion in 2023, KRW 6.53 billion in 2024 and KRW 20.3 billion in 2025.

Debt ratio fell from 714.5 percent in 2022 to 66.6 percent in 2023, then rose to 72.7 percent in 2024 and 77.9 percent in 2025, with total liabilities of KRW 205.5 billion and total equity of KRW 263.7 billion at year-end 2025.

05

Industry analysis

In domestic packaging paper, PaperCorea is described as the market leader in kraft paper. Its industrial paper business is exposed to rising demand for eco-friendly materials.

In the company's 2025 nine-month commentary, production and sales of linerboard, kraft paper and impregnated paper were expanded on eco-friendly demand. The Q1 2026 commentary notes expanded production and sales of industrial paper on higher packaging and storage demand.

Newsprint demand is shrinking with the spread of digital media, while commentary points to supply-demand balance recovering after capacity closures. On raw materials, lower input prices were cited as easing cost pressure.

Across the cycle, demand for eco-friendly packaging is a medium-term growth driver, while newsprint is in a contraction phase. The company's competitive position rests on its leadership in industrial paper, while its newsprint business is directly exposed to shrinking market volume.

Compared with peers such as Hansol Paper, Moorim P&P and DaelimPaper, its industrial paper-centered mix is the main point of difference. Raw material and energy cost swings remain key variables for margins.

Outlook

The company is pursuing a sales strategy aimed at raising operating margin in line with growing demand for eco-friendly materials. Development of the Jochon-dong plant site in Gunsan is proceeding in stages.

The Q1 2026 commentary cites eco-friendly demand and lower raw material prices as drivers of improved profitability.

Q2 2026 posted operating profit of KRW 5.45 billion and net profit attributable to controlling shareholders of KRW 2.61 billion, while Q3 2026 operating profit fell to KRW 1.93 billion, so whether the Q3 level holds is a point to confirm.

Full-year 2026 results have not yet been filed in a periodic report, so whether full-year operating profit turns positive from the KRW 5.28 billion loss in 2025 is the most direct test. How the temporary halt from energy facility investment in 2025 is reflected in 2026 quarterly results is also a point to watch.

Newsprint supply conditions have improved, but declining demand from digital transition remains a variable. The timing of real estate revenue from the Gunsan site may vary by quarter and can be traced through notes and business sections of the next annual report.

Bull factors

Quarterly operating profit has turned positive

Operating profit turned positive in Q1 2026 at KRW 1.24 billion, and Q2 2026 improved to KRW 5.45 billion on KRW 80.3 billion in revenue. Q3 2026 remained positive at KRW 1.93 billion. The company cited eco-friendly material demand and lower raw material prices as drivers of improved profitability.

Market leadership in kraft paper

The company is described as the domestic market share leader in kraft paper. Industrial paper is used for shopping bags, rice sacks and cement bags, and eco-friendly demand has supported higher sales of linerboard and kraft paper. Raising the share of recycled raw materials to serve eco-friendly demand is also cited as a strength.

Plant-site development and asset base

A redevelopment project using a former plant site is described as a revenue source alongside the manufacturing base. Development of the Jochon-dong plant site in Gunsan is proceeding in stages. Total equity stood at KRW 263.7 billion at year-end 2025, so an asset base remains on the balance sheet.

Bear factors

Two consecutive years of losses and operating loss

Net loss attributable to controlling shareholders was KRW 9.68 billion in 2024 and KRW 19.3 billion in 2025, two consecutive years of losses. The 2025 operating loss of KRW 5.28 billion came as the company cited a temporary operating halt during energy facility investment. Operating margin fell to -2.0 percent in 2025.

Shrinking newsprint demand

Newsprint demand is shrinking as digital media grows. Raw material price increases were cited as a factor weighing on sales in the same commentary. Revenue fell for three consecutive years from KRW 410 billion in 2022 to KRW 266.7 billion in 2025, so the pace of mix shift is a variable for recovery.

Quarterly earnings volatility and rising leverage

Net profit attributable to controlling shareholders fell 97.4 percent quarter on quarter to KRW 66.7 million in Q3 2026, and operating profit fell 64.5 percent to KRW 1.93 billion. Debt ratio rose from 66.6 percent in 2023 to 77.9 percent in 2025.

10

Risk factors

Raw material and energy costs

Rising raw material prices weighed on newsprint sales in 2025. Lower input prices eased cost pressure in Q1 2026, but a reversal could pressure margins again. In 2025, a temporary operating halt during energy facility investment reduced both sales and profit. Cost volatility flows directly into quarterly results.

Liquidity and ownership structure

The largest shareholder is a turnaround asset manager. The free float ratio is relatively low, which can limit trading liquidity. A share consolidation in May 2026 resumed trading and was read as a move to improve trading stability.

Business concentration and demand structure

Revenue is concentrated in industrial paper and newsprint, so shrinking newsprint demand has a large effect on total sales. If kraft paper leadership weakens, the earnings defense could weaken. Real estate revenue from the Gunsan site will arrive in stages and may vary by quarter.

Overall view

PaperCorea produces kraft paper, where it holds the domestic market share lead, and newsprint. Revenue has fallen for three consecutive years since 2022.

The 2025 operating loss of KRW 5.28 billion and net loss attributable to controlling shareholders of KRW 19.3 billion widened losses, and the company attributed this to a temporary halt during facility investment.

Quarterly operating profit has been positive in each quarter of 2026 through Q3, and net profit attributable to controlling shareholders turned positive in Q2. However, Q3 net profit fell to KRW 66.7 million, and the four quarters to Q3 2026 total a net loss of KRW 5.07 billion. Debt ratio has risen since 2023.

Positive factors are the quarterly turnaround and eco-friendly demand for industrial paper, while negative factors are shrinking newsprint demand and the accumulation of annual losses. The next checkpoints are full-year 2026 results and the operating margin in the 2026 annual report.
