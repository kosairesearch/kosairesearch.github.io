From tree distribution to ecosystem restoration

Suppro is widening its reach from landscape tree distribution into overseas greening and government-funded ecosystem restoration, while carrying the constraints of share price volatility and reliance on overseas orders.

02

Business structure

Suppro is an environmental greening company whose core capabilities are research, production and distribution of landscape trees. Its business falls into three areas: domestic landscape tree distribution, wall-greening products, and overseas reforestation and greening projects.

A 2021 Kiwoom Securities report introduced Suppro as the leading domestic specialist in landscape tree distribution. The same report cited its diversified supplier and customer base as a competitive advantage in a market dominated by small traders.

In in-house production, container landscape-tree growing technology was presented as the core. Kiwoom Securities explained that this technology raises output per unit area compared with open-field cultivation and lowers tree mortality.

The company's wall-greening product lines plant woody species on urban building walls, and one of them was designated an innovative product on the Public Procurement Service marketplace. In February 2024, Suppro formed a wall-greening joint venture with GS Energy.

Its overseas division has run reforestation projects since 2008 in areas including Inner Mongolia and Shaanxi in China, Tunisia and Kyrgyzstan, together with KOICA.

In 2024 it won an 8.5 billion won Uzbekistan forest and urban greening project, and reports describe public-sector landscaping orders as the center of the domestic market.

03

Recent trends

On April 24, 2026, Suppro announced that it had been selected as a joint research and development institution for a national project on ecosystem restoration and management for climate change adaptation, run by the Ministry of Climate, Energy and Environment.

The project runs from April 2026 to December 2030, with a total budget of KRW 12.4 billion, of which KRW 11.5 billion is government funding. Suppro will handle field demonstration of ecosystem restoration technology for carbon absorption together with Kookmin University's industry-academic cooperation foundation.

The research team is reported to be developing an AI model that analyzes factors contributing to carbon absorption and recommends optimal restoration designs. On October 28, 2026, a Korean-style nursery was inaugurated in Peskent, Tashkent Province, Uzbekistan.

The facility is introduced as the first modern Korean-style nursery in Central Asia, and according to a November 2, 2026 report, the company describes it as core infrastructure for a KOICA-commissioned project worth about KRW 10 billion.

According to a January 5, 2026 report, the company won the R&D innovation category at the 2025 Korea Corporate Awards for the third consecutive year with nature-based solutions. The share price closed at KRW 1,088 on September 30, 2026, up 14.77%, and fell 8.63% to KRW 910 on October 6.

On November 6, 2026, the stock traded up 14.94% at KRW 823 intraday while the commercial services sector weakened, and the report attributed the move to low public float drawing attention. At 02:10 KST on November 11, 2026, the share price was KRW 870, down 2.25% from the previous close.

04

Outlook

Future business indicators can be gauged by the operation of the overseas nursery and progress in the national research project's field demonstrations. Because the Peskent nursery serves as a seedling production base, whether production results and follow-on orders in the region are linked is a key checkpoint.

The KOICA project of roughly KRW 10 billion is under way, so whether follow-on contracts arrive to fill revenue gaps after completion is important.

The KRW 12.4 billion national project continues through December 2030, so whether demonstration results are disclosed in stages will serve as a measure of technical validation.

On the positive side, the expansion of scope into nature-based solutions and carbon monitoring, supported by government R&D and international cooperation, is cited.

On the negative side, overseas work carries a structural constraint in that the timing of revenue recognition can shift with client budgets, schedules and local conditions.

A 2021 Kiwoom Securities report projected growth in the tree market and public-sector landscaping, but that analysis rests on assumptions from its time. Room for improvement in business indicators can be found in diversifying overseas orders and expanding public deliveries of wall-greening products.

Bull factors

Expanding government and overseas contracts

Suppro was selected as a joint research and development institution for a KRW 12.4 billion national project in April 2026. Government funding of KRW 11.5 billion makes up most of the research budget. The Uzbekistan project commissioned by KOICA, worth about KRW 10 billion, is ongoing. Overseas reforestation work built on experience accumulated since 2008.

R&D awards and technology assets

A January 2026 report said Suppro won the R&D innovation category at the 2025 Korea Corporate Awards for three consecutive years, based on nature-based solutions. Its wall-greening product lines have been designated innovative products on the Public Procurement Service marketplace.

The company is combining container tree-growing technology built over about 20 years with AI and carbon monitoring. How far these technical assets convert into revenue will show up in follow-on contract wins.

Position in domestic tree distribution

Suppro's standing in the domestic market rests on the assessment in a 2021 Kiwoom Securities report. Its diversified supplier and customer base was presented as a point of difference from small, fragmented traders. Growth in public-sector landscaping projects was cited as a driver of expansion. The assessment reflects conditions as of 2021.

06

Bear factors

Dependence on overseas orders

Overseas reforestation work relies heavily on orders from KOICA. The KRW 8.5 billion contract won in 2024 and the project of roughly KRW 10 billion now underway share the same client structure. The timing of orders and project execution can shift when revenue is recognized. Local conditions and budget changes in partner countries also affect the business.

Share price volatility and small float

The 52-week range of KRW 651 to KRW 1,145 is wide. The stock closed at KRW 1,088 on September 30, 2026, up 14.77%, and fell 8.63% to KRW 910 on October 6, 2026. A small public float makes prices more sensitive to individual buying. KONEX trading volume is small, which can limit price formation.

Sector-wide weakness in trading flows

A November 6, 2026 market report said the commercial services sector weakened on concerns over slower business spending. Conservative spending on marketing and IT could reduce demand for related services, according to the report.

On the same day, Suppro rose 14.94% to KRW 823, which the report attributed to contract expectations and individual buying. Sector-wide selling can add to short-term swings in individual stocks.

07

Risk factors

Macro

If domestic companies cut marketing and IT spending, revenue growth at small and mid-sized service firms could slow. Suppro's core landscaping and greening work depends more on public orders, which limits the direct effect of a private slowdown. Private greening demand is still linked to construction activity.

Policy and budget

National research projects and public orders depend on government budget allocation and execution schedules. Government funding of KRW 11.5 billion within a KRW 12.4 billion project makes the financing largely public. Budget adjustments or evaluation outcomes could change when revenue is recognized. Commercialization of research results also remains uncertain.

Overseas execution

Projects in Uzbekistan and Central Asia are exposed to local permits, logistics and climate conditions. The Peskent nursery was completed, but whether seedling production and local sales proceed as planned remains the key test. Exchange-rate movements can affect won-denominated results. A larger overseas share also raises dependence on specific clients.

08

Overall view

Suppro is an environmental greening company that combines landscape tree distribution, wall-greening and overseas reforestation, and in 2026 it added a national research project and a completed overseas nursery.

The business direction is broadening from simple distribution toward technology-based ecosystem restoration and carbon-related work. Strengths include a contract base built on government projects and KOICA orders, accumulated technology and an award record.

Weaknesses include reliance on overseas orders, price volatility from a small float, and sector-wide trading pressure in commercial services. Because policy funding makes up a large share of the business, project execution schedules and funding structure are key variables.

At 02:10 KST on November 11, 2026, the share price of KRW 870 was down 2.25% from the previous close and within the 52-week range. On profitability, the key open question is how far contract wins convert into revenue and earnings.
