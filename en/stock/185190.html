A Landscape Tree Distributor Slips Into a Loss

Suppro, a Konex-listed landscape tree producer and distributor, saw revenue fall and swung to an operating loss in the fiscal year ended March 2026, which raised its financial burden.

  1. 1

    Revenue for the fiscal year ended March 2026 was KRW 22.1 billion, down 20.8% from the prior year.

  2. 2

    Operating profit of KRW 0.4 billion in the prior year turned into an operating loss of KRW 1.1 billion, and the net loss was KRW 1.1 billion.

  3. 3

    The debt ratio rose to 199.35% from 114.19%, and reliance on borrowings rose to 34.93%.

  4. 4

    The Tashkent urban greening project in Uzbekistan and an AI green-tech plan are the new business pillars the company is promoting.

  5. 5

    With a market capitalization of KRW 9.1 billion and trading value of KRW 10,478, trading is extremely thin and prices swing widely.

02

Business structure

Suppro was founded on July 26, 2000 and specializes in landscape trees. On its website, the company describes three areas: landscape tree production and distribution, landscaping construction and ecological restoration, and global climate and environment solutions.

According to a corporate information service, its model links about 1,200 growing farms and about 1,800 ordering companies through its own distribution management system. The same service describes container-based tree production under a patent as its core technology.

The company registered a patent for a 'flowerpot manufacturing component' in February 2024. In August 2024 it won a project management consulting contract for a smart-technology forest and urban greening capacity-building program in the Tashkent region of Uzbekistan.

According to a report dated March 4, 2026, the company plans to supply 300,000 park trees to the new Tashkent city and held a technical workshop under a Korea International Cooperation Agency (KOICA) program.

The company website presents AI green-tech as a new direction, linking 26 years of trading data through AI, and says it runs a nationwide map of landscape trees for sale called 'Namuwang'.

Public materials do not disclose revenue by segment or market share. The landscape tree market is generally understood to be fragmented among many growers and wholesalers and tied to construction and park-building demand.

The company's own account is that its edge lies in combining trading data, a distribution network and production technology. Suppro listed on Konex in October 2013, and as of March 31, 2025 Sangji Construction was a major shareholder with 10.01%.

03

Recent trends

For the fiscal year ended March 2026, Suppro's revenue was KRW 22.1 billion, down 20.8% from KRW 27.8 billion a year earlier. It posted an operating loss of KRW 1.1 billion, versus an operating profit of KRW 0.4 billion in the prior year.

The net loss was KRW 1.1 billion, versus net income of KRW 0.37 billion in the prior year. In the fiscal year ended March 2024, revenue was KRW 26.2 billion, with an operating loss of KRW 0.75 billion and a net loss of KRW 0.57 billion. Of the last three fiscal years, only the year ended March 2025 was profitable.

The financial structure weakened. The debt ratio rose from 114.19% to 199.35%, and the equity ratio fell from 46.69% to 33.41%. Reliance on borrowings rose from 11.40% to 34.93%. Operating cash flow was an outflow of KRW 2.4 billion, and investing cash flow was an outflow of KRW 3.2 billion.

The share price was volatile. Data from Hankyung Korea Market show a 52-week range of KRW 651 to KRW 1,145, and the shares closed at KRW 1,088 on September 30, 2026, up 14.77% on volume of only 11 shares. Investing.com data show a close of KRW 860 on October 16, 2026, down 6.52%.

On October 27, 2026 the price was KRW 989, down 0.90% from the previous day, and trading value was KRW 10,478. Trading is so thin that even small orders can move the price sharply.

04

Outlook

Suppro's path depends on a recovery in landscape tree sales and an improved cost structure. Whether the operating loss in the fiscal year ended March 2026 continues must be checked in subsequent quarterly and annual disclosures.

For the Tashkent project, the key points are when and how much of the 300,000-tree supply plan converts into recognized revenue. The contract value and revenue recognition timing disclosed by the company were not confirmed in this research.

The AI green-tech and trading platform plan is an attempt to widen the business model toward data-based intermediation. No public figures currently show its revenue contribution or profitability.

On the operating margin, the company moved from a 1.38% operating margin in the prior year to an operating loss margin of 4.86% in the year ended March 2026, so a return to break-even is the first business indicator to watch.

A debt ratio of 199.35% and higher borrowings are reasons to check interest costs and funding conditions. Low trading volume on Konex may also constrain the shareholder base and the ways the company can raise capital.

05

Bull factors

Overseas urban greening track record

In August 2024 the company won a project management consulting contract for a forest and urban greening program in the Tashkent region of Uzbekistan. According to a March 4, 2026 report, it plans to supply 300,000 landscape trees to the new Tashkent city.

The report also said production would take place at an 87-hectare smart nursery. This could help diversify a revenue base that leans on domestic construction demand.

26 years of trading data and a distribution network

The company promotes AI green-tech built on 26 years of trading data and its own distribution management system. According to a corporate information service, it connects about 1,200 growing farms and about 1,800 ordering companies nationwide.

The company's explanation is that this structure reduces information asymmetry in tree prices and inventory. If this data asset turns into actual earnings, the quality of the business model could change.

Technology-based differentiation and disclosure record

Container-based tree production under a patent and the February 2024 patent for a flowerpot component are the company's technology-based differentiators. In March 2025 Korea Exchange named the company an outstanding disclosure company on the Konex market for 2024.

In the prior year it recorded operating profit of KRW 0.4 billion and net income of KRW 0.37 billion, so it has shown it can return to profit. A small organization could also be read as carrying a relatively limited fixed-cost burden if revenue recovers.

06

Bear factors

Revenue decline and swing to operating loss

Revenue for the fiscal year ended March 2026 fell 20.8% to KRW 22.1 billion. The operating loss was KRW 1.1 billion, an operating loss margin of 4.86%. The company also posted an operating loss of KRW 0.75 billion in the year ended March 2024, so earnings have swung from year to year. This shows a structure in which profit changes sharply with revenue.

Weaker financial stability

The debt ratio rose sharply to 199.35% from 114.19%. The equity ratio fell to 33.41% and reliance on borrowings rose to 34.93%. An operating cash outflow of KRW 2.4 billion was compounded by an investing outflow of KRW 3.2 billion. The interest coverage ratio was also negative, meaning operating results did not cover interest costs.

Unclear earnings contribution from new businesses

AI green-tech and the Namuwang platform are directions the company has set out, but no public figures show their revenue contribution. The size of the Tashkent supply contract and the timing of revenue recognition were not confirmed in this research.

Overseas business depends on the budgets and schedules of the ordering agencies. It may take time before the new businesses offset weakness in the core business.

07

Risk factors

Macro

Demand for landscape trees is closely tied to construction, housing site development and park building. Weak interest-rate and construction conditions can delay orders and reduce revenue. With reliance on borrowings now higher, interest costs weigh more heavily on earnings. Overseas business is also exposed to exchange rates and changes in local policy.

Industry

The landscape tree market is generally understood to have many growers and wholesalers, which makes price competition more likely. Weather, pests and poor inventory management can raise production costs and write-offs.

Business with a high share of public orders depends on bidding competition and budget execution schedules. Because the company's market share is not disclosed in public materials, judging its competitive position has limits.

Company-specific

As a Konex-listed company with thin trading, Suppro's trading value on October 27, 2026 was only KRW 10,478. In such a market small trades can move the price sharply, and it may be hard to trade at a desired time. The controlling shareholders hold a large stake, so the free float is also limited.

If operating losses and cash outflows continue, funding decisions such as a capital increase or additional borrowing may become necessary.

08

Overall view

Suppro is a small Konex-listed company that started in landscape tree production and distribution and is trying to expand into overseas urban greening and a data-based platform.

In the fiscal year ended March 2026, revenue fell 20.8%, and it recorded an operating loss of KRW 1.1 billion and a net loss of KRW 1.1 billion, turning from profit to loss. A debt ratio of 199.35% and reliance on borrowings of 34.93% show a heavier financial burden.

On the other hand, the Tashkent urban greening project, patent-based production technology and 26 years of trading data are the growth rationale the company puts forward. However, the revenue contribution and contract size of the new businesses could not be confirmed from public materials.

The thin trading environment, with a market capitalization of KRW 9.1 billion and trading value of KRW 10,478, is also a factor to weigh. In subsequent disclosures, the items to check are revenue recovery, operating profit or loss, and changes in borrowings.
