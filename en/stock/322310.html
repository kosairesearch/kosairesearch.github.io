02

Business structure

Auros Technology makes metrology and inspection (MI) equipment used around the lithography step of front-end semiconductor manufacturing. Its core product is overlay metrology equipment that measures how accurately circuit layers on a wafer line up, and the 12-inch OL series accounts for most of revenue.

According to Daily Invest, the OL-12inch share of revenue was 64.65% in the third quarter of 2024, while a company profile site lists the same product family at 74.59% on a different basis and date, so the two figures should be read together. On the same site, service revenue is roughly 9% of the total.

On the back-end side, development targets include an overlay tool for HBM pad structures and an IR (infrared) overlay tool. The company's August 2025 IR materials stated that it is developing METIS, a 2D and 3D bump metrology tool, and HE-900ir, an IR overlay tool for hybrid bonding.

The product mix is therefore moving from a front-end focus toward packaging. Customers include SK hynix, which is reported to be a large share of sales, and Samsung Electronics, to which equipment was supplied according to a 2024 report. A February 2025 report said the company had supplied Kioxia in Japan in 2024.

Overseas, the company set up a production subsidiary in Wuxi, China and a branch in Tokyo in 2024, but withdrew its U.S. subsidiary in June 2026. Industry commentary says domestic metrology suppliers were historically rare in Korea.

The company's global market share is estimated to be low, and the leading players in metrology are large overseas firms such as KLA.

04

Earnings analysis

On the disclosed annual basis, revenue rose from KRW 35.4 billion in 2022 to KRW 45.5 billion in 2023, an increase of 28.6%, and then to KRW 61.4 billion in 2024, an increase of 34.9%. It fell to KRW 52.1 billion in 2025, a decrease of 15.1%. Across 2022 to 2025, 2024 was the largest year and 2022 was the smallest.

Operating results moved from an operating loss of KRW 3.29 billion in 2022 to an operating profit of KRW 2.37 billion in 2023, which marked a turn to profit. Operating profit then reached KRW 6.08 billion in 2024, a rise of 156.7%.

In 2025 the company posted an operating loss of KRW 8.37 billion, and the operating margin fell from 9.9% in 2024 to -16.1%. Net profit attributable to controlling shareholders moved from KRW 5.91 billion in 2024 to a net loss of KRW 6.42 billion in 2025, a turn to loss.

For 2025, the net loss including non-controlling interests was also disclosed as KRW 6.42 billion. On a consolidated basis compiled by FnGuide, 2025 cost of sales was KRW 26.8 billion, selling and administrative expenses were KRW 33.7 billion, and R&D expense was KRW 19.1 billion.

Revenue shrank while fixed-type costs grew, which widened the operating loss. The quarterly path shows that in the third quarter of 2025 revenue was KRW 7.57 billion with an operating loss of KRW 5.81 billion.

The fourth quarter of 2025 brought revenue of KRW 13.8 billion and a narrower operating loss of KRW 1.59 billion. The first quarter of 2026 was the low point, with revenue of KRW 6.81 billion, down 50.7% from the prior quarter, and an operating loss of KRW 6.82 billion.

The second quarter of 2026 recorded revenue of KRW 11.4 billion and an operating loss of KRW 2.29 billion. In the third quarter of 2026, revenue reached KRW 16.9 billion and operating profit was KRW 413 million, while net profit attributable to controlling shareholders was KRW 232 million.

Over the last four quarters, the cumulative net loss attributable to controlling shareholders was KRW 8.08 billion. Operating cash flow was -KRW 9.21 billion in 2022, KRW 7.41 billion in 2023, -KRW 5.28 billion in 2024, and KRW 1.67 billion in 2025.

The filings alone do not identify one-off items, so whether the third-quarter turn is structural or temporary will have to be checked in later quarters.

Industry analysis

Demand for metrology equipment is linked to both front-end capital spending and back-end advanced packaging investment. In early 2024, a Kiwoom Securities analyst said that expanding HBM process investment was rapidly increasing the adoption of overlay equipment in back-end processes.

A February 2025 report also pointed to rising needs for hybrid bonding in next-generation HBM. That trend matches the company's case for widening its back-end tool lineup.

However, search results did not establish where the 2026 semiconductor capital spending cycle currently stands, so this report does not characterize the cycle stage.

On the domestic side, the company says it completed supply of the first 8-inch overlay metrology tool in Korea, and it is cited as an example of localizing overlay metrology technology. Overseas competition centers on large metrology firms such as KLA.

Among same-sector peers, Hanmi Semiconductor and EO Technics are commonly used as comparison points in back-end equipment. Because the company's global share is estimated to be low, the timing of a single customer's equipment order has a large effect on quarterly revenue.

The full-year decline in 2025, the low in the first quarter of 2026, and the recovery to about KRW 16.9 billion in the third quarter illustrate this sensitivity. Industry-wide supply and demand indicators were not confirmed in this search, so the analysis centers on customer order timing.

Outlook

Search results did not confirm an official full-year earnings guidance from the company, so this outlook is limited to the development roadmap and confirmed events.

According to August 2025 IR materials, the HE-900ir IR overlay tool for hybrid bonding is being developed with the aim of first customer adoption, while the METIS 2D and 3D bump metrology tool is shown as preparing for customer entry after development is completed.

Entry into Japanese front-end processes and applications in glass substrates and PCBs were described as evaluation-stage projects. Customer adoption of these new tools is therefore the key variable for changing the revenue structure.

The annual shareholders' meeting in March 2026 approved the consolidated and separate financial statements for the 23rd fiscal year. The company relocated its headquarters to Dongtan, Hwaseong in March 2026 and withdrew its U.S. subsidiary in June 2026.

How the overseas restructuring affects sales strategy will be checked in the next periodic report. On April 29 and May 4, 2026, executive director Yoo Jae-man sold 10,000 common shares on the open market, reducing his stake to 0.11% according to a May 2026 filing.

The filing states the transactions but does not disclose the reason. Going forward, the points to check are whether fourth-quarter revenue holds near the third-quarter level and whether operating profit stays positive.

08

Bull factors

Quarterly turn to profit and revenue recovery

Third-quarter 2026 revenue reached KRW 16.9 billion, up 48.3% from the prior quarter and 122.7% from a year earlier. Operating profit of KRW 413 million and net profit attributable to controlling shareholders of KRW 232 million both turned positive. Revenue rose in consecutive quarters after the first-quarter 2026 low, which underpins the recovery.

Back-end and new product pipeline

The company lists HBM pad overlay, IR overlay, the METIS 2D and 3D bump metrology tool, and the HE-900ir hybrid bonding tool as development targets. As of the August 2025 IR materials, some of these were still before customer entry.

If the lineup broadens from front-end-centered sales into packaging, the customer base and revenue sources could diversify.

Broadening customer base

Reports indicate the company has expanded supply from its heavy reliance on SK hynix to Samsung Electronics, Kioxia in Japan and Chinese memory makers. The Tokyo branch and Wuxi production subsidiary set up in 2024 provide a base for overseas customers.

As the customer count grows, the effect of any one customer's capital spending swings on quarterly results may shrink.

Bear factors

Annual losses returned in 2025

2025 revenue was KRW 52.1 billion, down 15.1%, and the company posted an operating loss of KRW 8.37 billion and a net loss attributable to controlling shareholders of KRW 6.42 billion. The operating margin was -16.1%.

The cumulative net loss over the last four quarters is KRW 8.08 billion, so the third-quarter profit has not yet offset the accumulated losses.

Large swings in quarterly results

Quarterly revenue fell from KRW 13.8 billion in the fourth quarter of 2025 to KRW 6.81 billion in the first quarter of 2026, nearly halved, before recovering to KRW 11.4 billion and KRW 16.9 billion. Operating results swung between losses and profits over the same period.

In early 2025 Kiwoom Securities projected 2025 revenue of KRW 63.8 billion, but disclosed 2025 revenue was KRW 52.1 billion.

U.S. subsidiary exit and insider selling

The June 2026 withdrawal of the U.S. subsidiary may signal a change in the approach to North American customers. On April 29 and May 4, 2026, executive director Yoo Jae-man sold 10,000 common shares on the open market, reducing his stake to 0.11%. The filing does not state the reason for the sales, and this report records only the disclosed fact.

10

Risk factors

Customer concentration and capex cycles

Revenue depends on orders from a small number of customers, which produces large quarter-to-quarter swings. Reports of a high SK hynix share mean a cut in one customer's investment could hit results directly. The 50.7% quarter-on-quarter drop in the first quarter of 2026 is consistent with this structure.

Uncertain customer adoption of new tools

As of the August 2025 IR materials, back-end tools such as METIS and HE-900ir were still in development or preparing for customer entry. Delays in customer qualification would postpone revenue contributions. The pace of the industry's shift to hybrid bonding is also an external variable.

Fixed cost burden and R&D spending

On a consolidated basis compiled by FnGuide, 2025 R&D expense was KRW 19.1 billion and selling and administrative expenses were KRW 33.7 billion, which is high relative to revenue. If sales do not recover, the improvement in operating results may be limited. Because R&D underpins the new product pipeline, cutting costs has its own limits.

Overall view

Auros Technology is a semiconductor metrology company whose core product is 12-inch overlay metrology equipment.

After peaking at KRW 61.4 billion in revenue and KRW 6.08 billion in operating profit in 2024, it posted 2025 revenue of KRW 52.1 billion, an operating loss of KRW 8.37 billion, and a net loss attributable to controlling shareholders of KRW 6.42 billion.

Revenue bottomed at KRW 6.81 billion in the first quarter of 2026, then rose in consecutive quarters, and operating profit turned positive at KRW 413 million in the third quarter. Bullish factors are the quarterly return to profit, the back-end tool pipeline and a broadening customer base.

Bearish factors are the full-year 2025 loss, the cumulative net loss of KRW 8.08 billion over the last four quarters, customer concentration and quarterly volatility, and the U.S. subsidiary exit and insider selling. On valuation, PER cannot be calculated and PBR sits above 1x.
