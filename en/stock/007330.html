02

Business structure

Pureun Savings Bank operates under the Mutual Savings Banks Act, relying mainly on customer deposits as its funding source and centering its asset deployment on lending and securities investment.

Its deposit products include demand deposits, time deposits and installment savings, while its lending products include discounted bills, collateralized deposit loans and general corporate loans. Its head office is in Seocho-gu, Seoul, with branches in Jongno, Gangnam, Guro and Mapo (annual report).

Press reports describe the bank as tilted toward corporate lending rather than household loans, with real estate project financing long cited as a core product.

Total 2025 operating revenue of KRW 101.7 billion comprised KRW 79.5 billion in interest income, KRW 1.9 billion in fee income and KRW 20.3 billion in other operating income (annual report). Interest income was KRW 83.1 billion in 2024, so the interest base narrowed slightly.

Loans at nominal value rose from KRW 1.0306 trillion at end-2025 to KRW 1.1173 trillion at end-March 2026 (individual financial statements, aggregated by a financial information service). The bank is widely described as the only listed savings bank in Korea, which is its main point of distinction.

Listed bank holding companies and securities firms in the same financial sector differ in deposit base, scale and regulation, so they are not straightforward benchmarks. Press reports also cite efforts to expand into mid-rate personal credit loans and loans linked with commercial banks.

04

Earnings analysis

Annual operating profit fell from KRW 26.3 billion in 2022 to KRW 9.03 billion in 2023 (down 65.6% year on year) and KRW 6.85 billion in 2024 (down 24.2%), then recovered to KRW 16.9 billion in 2025 (up 147.5%). Among 2022 to 2025, 2022 was the largest year and 2024 the smallest.

Profit attributable to owners went from KRW 20.1 billion in 2022 to KRW 15.8 billion in 2023 (down 21.5%) and KRW 7.01 billion in 2024 (down 55.5%), before reaching KRW 19.9 billion in 2025 (up 183.3%).

According to the 2025 annual report, the main driver of the recovery was a reduction in the net loss of other operating activities, from minus KRW 16.7 billion to minus KRW 5.1 billion, while the net interest spread edged up from KRW 42.2 billion to KRW 42.9 billion.

Selling and administrative expenses rose from KRW 18.5 billion to KRW 20.1 billion over the same period, and interest income fell from KRW 83.1 billion to KRW 79.5 billion.

Net financial asset-related losses narrowed from minus KRW 20.9 billion in 2024 to minus KRW 12.3 billion in 2025 (individual basis, fnguide), which shows how much securities valuation and disposal results swing reported profit.

Operating cash flow alternated direction: minus KRW 1.12 billion in 2022, KRW 2.39 billion in 2023, minus KRW 1.19 billion in 2024 and KRW 1.8 billion in 2025. The debt-to-equity ratio rose from 330.9% in 2024 to 386.0% in 2025, so the earnings recovery came with a larger liability base.

Quarterly, operating profit went from KRW 5.38 billion in Q3 2025 to KRW 867 million in Q4 2025 (down 83.9% quarter on quarter) and then to an operating loss of KRW 3.99 billion in Q1 2026.

A corporate data service attributed the widening Q1 2026 operating loss to higher loan-loss provisions and a rising share of substandard and below loans. Operating profit returned to KRW 1.31 billion in Q2 2026, then reached KRW 4.55 billion in Q3 2026 (up 246.4% quarter on quarter but down 15.5% year on year).

Profit attributable to owners in Q3 2026 was KRW 385 million, down 94.0% year on year, and the last four quarters together total KRW 7.82 billion.

05

Industry analysis

Savings banks fund lending mainly with deposits, so their profitability is sensitive to the rate environment and the property market. Press reports describe broad difficulty in lending across the sector, which has pushed excess funds into securities purchases and private-fund investments.

Pureun Savings Bank's balance of equity investments in other companies was reported at KRW 164.9 billion at end-2024.

Weakness in real estate project financing surfaced as the property market slumped, with delinquency on property PF loans at 9.58% and on construction-sector loans at 25.63% as of the end of September 2024. Pureun's own delinquency ratio was 9.09% at that point.

Because it is the only listed savings bank in Korea, there are few listed peers for direct comparison. The 2025 earnings recovery reflects a combination of bad-loan clean-up and better securities results, but credit costs have again become a variable in 2026 quarterly results.

Judging the sector's position in the cycle requires looking at industry-wide delinquency and provisioning data published since 2025. Loans of KRW 1.1173 trillion at end-March 2026 and a BIS ratio of 21.48% at end-2025 indicate capital headroom well above the regulatory minimum. That headroom acts as a buffer when credit costs rise, so it should be read alongside asset-quality indicators.

06

Outlook

In its 2026 corporate value-up plan, the bank set out expanding sound lending, improving asset quality and maintaining a stable dividend policy, according to major press coverage.

Loans at nominal value rose from KRW 1.0306 trillion at end-2025 to KRW 1.1173 trillion at end-March 2026, which makes balancing growth against asset-quality control the central task. Loan-loss reserves increased from KRW 47.0 billion at end-2025 to KRW 61.1 billion at end-March 2026 (individual basis, fnguide).

A May 2026 disclosure said Buguk Feed Co. would sell its 7.21% stake in Pureun Savings Bank to Joo Shin-hong, CEO of Pureun Partners Asset Management.

After the February 27, 2026 disclosure of the embezzlement case, the bank has carried out compensation for affected customers and follow-up measures, and market attention has shifted to whether management improvements follow in practice.

Operating profit stayed positive in Q3 2026, but profit attributable to owners was only KRW 385 million, so whether operating profit carries through to net profit is the next point to watch. The bank raised its annual dividend in 2025 and has said it will keep a stable dividend policy.

In the 2026 annual report, the next periodic filing, the operating margin, loan-loss reserve balance and the share of substandard and below loans will be the key checkpoints. Implementation details of the value-up plan should be checked against future filings.

08

Bull factors

Earnings recovery and capital headroom in 2025

Operating profit rose 147.5% to KRW 16.9 billion in 2025, and profit attributable to owners rose 183.3% to KRW 19.9 billion. The BIS capital adequacy ratio stood at 21.48% at end-2025, well above the 8% requirement. Total equity of KRW 320.5 billion provides a buffer if credit costs rise.

Value-up plan and dividend stance

In its 2026 value-up plan, the bank set out expanding sound lending, improving asset quality and keeping dividends stable. It raised its per-share dividend in 2025. Disclosures in 2025 also show the largest shareholder and a subsidiary buying common shares on the open market.

Loan growth and trading resumed after listing review

Loans at nominal value rose from KRW 1.0306 trillion at end-2025 to KRW 1.1173 trillion at end-March 2026 on an individual basis. The Korea Exchange excluded the company from a listing-qualification review in April 2026, and trading resumed after about six weeks of suspension.

Press reports tied the decision to the business continuity and financial soundness not being seriously impaired.

09

Bear factors

Large swings in quarterly earnings

The bank posted an operating loss of KRW 3.99 billion and a net loss attributable to owners of KRW 834 million in Q1 2026. Profit attributable to owners in Q3 2026 was KRW 385 million, down 94.0% year on year.

Over the last four quarters, the total was KRW 7.82 billion, below the 2025 full-year figure of KRW 19.9 billion.

Pressure on asset quality

At end-2024 the substandard-and-below loan ratio was 12.65%, up year on year, and the delinquency ratio was 9.47%. A corporate data service cited higher loan-loss provisions as a driver of the Q1 2026 operating loss.

Loan-loss reserves rose from KRW 47.0 billion at end-2025 to KRW 61.1 billion at end-March 2026 (individual basis).

Restoring trust in internal controls

The embezzlement amount disclosed on February 27, 2026 was first put at KRW 9.92 billion and later expanded to KRW 10.42 billion after further review. The former executive involved was reported to the authorities, and the full amount was reflected as loan-loss provisions. Press coverage described restoring trust and improving management practices as an ongoing task.

Risk factors

Credit and asset-quality risk

The loan book is weighted toward corporate lending, and real estate PF weakness has been a sector-wide concern. Delinquency on property PF loans was reported at 9.58% and on construction loans at 25.63% as of end-September 2024. Higher loan-loss provisions feed directly into quarterly results.

Governance and internal-control risk

Press reports put the stake held by the largest shareholder and related parties above 61%. A May 2026 disclosure described the sale of Buguk Feed's 7.21% stake to Joo Shin-hong. The CEO's term is listed as expiring on March 21, 2027 in the annual report.

Earnings volatility and dividend sustainability

The net loss of other operating activities narrowed from KRW 16.7 billion in 2024 to KRW 5.1 billion in 2025, but securities-related results have swung reported profit. Dividends exceeded net profit in 2024.

If quarterly losses persist, the relationship between earnings levels and the dividend policy may be debated again.

12

Overall view

Pureun Savings Bank showed an earnings recovery in 2025, with operating profit up 147.5% and profit attributable to owners up 183.3%. The BIS ratio of 21.48% and total equity of KRW 320.5 billion at end-2025 indicate capital headroom.

Quarterly profit in 2026, however, has moved sharply, from an operating loss in Q1 to KRW 385 million of profit attributable to owners in Q3, and the last four quarters total only KRW 7.82 billion.

On asset quality, loan-loss reserves rose to KRW 61.1 billion at end-March 2026, and PF delinquency is a sector-wide concern. The February 2026 embezzlement disclosure, the listing-qualification review and the change in the shareholder base remain open questions for governance and internal-control trust.

The share price trading well below book value shows a gap between net assets and market valuation, and how that gap is read depends on credit costs and the durability of earnings.

The next checkpoints are the operating margin and loan-loss reserves in the 2026 annual report and the dividend decision at the March 2027 shareholder meeting.
