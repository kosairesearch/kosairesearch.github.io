02

Business structure

Wave Electronics manufactures defense systems and wired and wireless communication equipment, and has been listed on KOSDAQ since September 2007. By segment, defense accounted for 77% of revenue in Q2 2026, communications for 17%, and other businesses for 6%, according to aggregator Butler.

The defense unit supplies parts such as seekers, guidance control units and power supplies for guided weapons, and its main customer is LIG Nex1. In December 2022 the company signed a component supply contract with LIG Nex1 for a new overseas guided weapon system, and a subsidiary supplies power supply units.

An analyst described the company as having shifted from a base-station power amplifier business to a key defense component supplier that takes part in design from the early stage. The communications unit produces wired and wireless equipment based on power amplifier technology built over many years.

A November 2023 brokerage report noted that carriers concentrate investment in the fourth quarter, making it the strongest quarter for the company. At the March 2026 general meeting, a change to the business purpose was approved, and the company removed OLED manufacturing activities after ending its OLED business.

The business depends heavily on one main defense customer. Unlike large power equipment makers centered on transformers and distribution equipment, Wave Electronics is built around defense parts and communication amplifiers, so its demand drivers differ.

04

Earnings analysis

Annual revenue rose from KRW 59.2 billion in 2022 to KRW 78 billion in 2023 (up 31.8% year on year), held at KRW 78 billion in 2024 (up 0.1%), and reached KRW 80.5 billion in 2025 (up 3.2%).

Revenue has grown for three consecutive years from 2023 through 2025, with 2025 the largest year and 2022 the smallest within 2022 to 2025.

Operating results moved from an operating loss of KRW 5.94 billion in 2022 to operating profit of KRW 2.35 billion in 2023, a turn to profit, then to KRW 1.85 billion in 2024 (down 21.2%), and then to an operating loss of KRW 1.64 billion in 2025, a return to losses.

Operating margin was -10.0% in 2022, 3.0% in 2023, 2.4% in 2024 and -2.0% in 2025, so margins narrowed even as revenue grew after 2023. A brokerage summary of the first half of 2025 said 5G equipment investment in the communications unit rose, but cost burdens hurt profitability.

Attributable net income was a loss of KRW 7.59 billion in 2022, a profit of KRW 664 million in 2023 (a turn to profit), a loss of KRW 6.35 billion in 2024 (a return to losses), and a profit of KRW 1.48 billion in 2025 (a turn to profit).

Net income including non-controlling interests was KRW 1.12 billion in 2025, a different figure from attributable net income, and the gap reflects non-controlling interests.

Operating cash flow moved from negative KRW 9.38 billion in 2022 and negative KRW 1.74 billion in 2023 to KRW 6.43 billion in 2024 and KRW 10.6 billion in 2025. The debt ratio rose from 39.7% in 2022 to 93.9% in 2025, with total liabilities of KRW 49.9 billion and total equity of KRW 53.1 billion at end-2025.

In the fourth quarter of 2025, revenue was KRW 24.6 billion (up 13.3% quarter on quarter), operating profit KRW 279 million (a turn to profit), and attributable net income KRW 2.36 billion (up 397.6%).

In Q1 2026, revenue was KRW 15.8 billion (down 35.8%), with an operating loss of KRW 1.13 billion and an attributable net loss of KRW 1.06 billion. In Q2 2026, revenue recovered to KRW 21 billion (up 32.7%) with operating profit of KRW 985 million.

Q3 2026 revenue was KRW 19.3 billion (down 8.1% quarter on quarter and down 11.2% year on year), with an operating loss of KRW 528 million, and an attributable net loss of KRW 440 million, a turn to losses year on year. Attributable net income over the latest four quarters (Q4 2025 to Q3 2026) totaled KRW 1.63 billion.

05

Industry analysis

Demand in the defense unit rests on continuing requirements for weapon systems tied to national security. Brokerages see K-defense exports gaining momentum through contract wins, and one forecast calls for solid revenue growth over the next two to three years.

On the communications side, expectations for 6G adoption have also been mentioned. An analyst said the company has focused on developing small wireless communication equipment for IoT and autonomous driving, opening new markets.

The same analysis described the communications equipment business as volatile but with large growth potential. In defense components, the company's competitiveness lies in supplying key parts directly to large system integrators, which also raises its dependence on their order timing.

Large power equipment makers are tied to power infrastructure investment cycles such as transformers and distribution equipment, while Wave Electronics moves with defense procurement schedules and telecom equipment investment cycles.

Peers in the same sector differ in business structure and demand cycle, so comparisons should start from that difference. Export expansion in defense and volatility in communications equipment coexist, so the industry picture should be read segment by segment.

Defense demand is supported by continuing needs for weapon systems tied to national security. As defense weight rises, the earnings base shifts toward the timing of defense contracts.

Growing order backlog

The defense order backlog stood at KRW 183.3 billion in the first half of 2025, up 25.9% year on year. The same report compared it with 4.3 times the defense revenue of 2024. Forecasts for solid revenue growth over the next two to three years were also mentioned. If backlog converts to revenue faster, the defense unit's contribution to earnings could grow.

Signs of earnings recovery

Operating profit turned to a profit in 2023, and attributable net income turned to a profit in 2025 at KRW 1.48 billion. Attributable net income was KRW 2.36 billion in Q4 2025 and KRW 775 million in Q2 2026, both turns to profit.

Quarterly results have been uneven, so the durability of the recovery needs confirmation from additional quarters.

Bear factors

Continued operating losses

Operating loss of KRW 1.64 billion in 2025 marked a return to losses, followed by an operating loss of KRW 1.13 billion in Q1 2026 and KRW 528 million in Q3 2026. Q3 2026 revenue fell 11.2% year on year, and the operating loss widened versus the prior-year quarter. The failure of operating profit to stabilize remains a weakness.

Rising financial leverage

The debt ratio climbed from 39.7% in 2022 to 93.9% in 2025. Total liabilities of KRW 49.9 billion at end-2025 were close in size to total equity of KRW 53.1 billion. Operating cash flow turned positive in 2024 and 2025, but if debt grows faster than earnings improve, financial strain could increase.

Communications cost pressure and customer concentration

The communications unit saw 5G equipment investment rise, but cost pressure weighed on profitability. The defense unit depends on LIG Nex1 as its main customer. High dependence on one customer means changes in that customer's contract timing feed directly into the company's results.

Defense revenue depends on LIG Nex1 as the main customer. Contracts linked to overseas expansion, such as the December 2022 supply agreement for a new overseas guided weapon system, mean changes in the customer's export schedule could affect order flow. Timing gaps between contract signing and revenue recognition can also swing quarterly results.

Business restructuring and management changes
