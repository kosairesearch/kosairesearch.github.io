Report overview

LB Semicon: Qualcomm Ramp Against a Still-Loss-Making Base

LB Semicon returned to quarterly operating profit in the first quarter of 2026 and is expanding its Qualcomm and non-DDI packaging business, but cumulative losses and debt still limit the pace of recovery.

  1. 1

    Revenue rose for two consecutive years from 2024 to 2025 after the 2023 low of KRW 416.9 billion, but operating losses continued for three consecutive years from 2023 to 2025, with the 2025 operating loss at KRW 39.8 billion.

  2. 2

    Operating profit turned positive at KRW 7.64 billion in the first quarter of 2026, but in the third quarter operating profit fell to KRW 2.39 billion and net income attributable to controlling shareholders swung back to a net loss of KRW 3.99 billion.

  3. 3

    The company signed a ten-year supply agreement with Qualcomm, began mass production in early August 2026, and is reported to be raising its 200mm non-DDI wafer capacity from 26,000 to 36,000 wafers per month by 2028.

  4. 4

    The debt ratio rose from 101.3% in 2022 to 153.3% in 2025, with total liabilities of KRW 414.1 billion at the end of 2025, and press reports put debt due within one year at over KRW 240 billion as of the end of the third quarter of 2025.

02

Business structure

LB Semicon is an outsourced semiconductor assembly and test (OSAT) company founded in 2000 and listed on KOSDAQ since 2011. Related parties of the largest shareholder hold 28.17% of the shares. The company's core strength is a turnkey service covering bumping, wafer test and back-end packaging in one place.

Following the absorption merger of its subsidiary LB Lusem, the bumping and test processes previously run by LB Semicon and the wafer back-grinding and package processes run by LB Lusem now sit within a single company.

Display driver IC (DDI) back-end work remains the centerpiece of revenue, with more than 70% of first-half 2026 revenue coming from DDI products.

Earlier press reports put dependence on Samsung Electronics and LX Semicon at 73.06% of revenue, and identified sensitivity of earnings to display market swings as a weakness. To change this mix, the company is expanding into non-DDI system semiconductors and power semiconductors.

Mass production of back-end packaging for Renesas power semiconductors has ramped up, and back-end work for Qualcomm PMICs entered mass production in early August 2026, with first shipments completed at the end of that month.

Overseas sales were reported at about 10% of revenue, and the company has stated a goal of raising that to 40% within three years. Competition is shaped by customer volumes, and bumping and test capability together with new customer qualifications are cited as differentiators.

04

Earnings analysis

On the disclosed figures, revenue fell from KRW 524.6 billion in 2022 to KRW 416.9 billion in 2023, a 20.5% decline, then rose 8.2% to KRW 450.9 billion in 2024 and 6.4% to KRW 479.8 billion in 2025, which is two consecutive years of growth from 2024 to 2025.

Among 2022 to 2025, 2022 was the largest year and 2023 the smallest. Operating profit was KRW 56.8 billion in 2022, a 10.8% margin, and turned into an operating loss of KRW 12.7 billion in 2023, followed by an operating loss of KRW 18.8 billion in 2024 and KRW 39.8 billion in 2025, a widening loss.

The operating margin moved from -3.0% in 2023 to -4.2% in 2024 and -8.3% in 2025. Net income attributable to controlling shareholders went from KRW 40.2 billion in 2022 to a net loss of KRW 15.9 billion in 2023, KRW 22.2 billion in 2024 and KRW 150.9 billion in 2025.

Net loss including non-controlling interests was also KRW 150.9 billion in 2025. Earlier press reports attributed the 2025 deterioration to a display market slump that spread from LX Semicon to LB Semicon, pressure from customers to cut prices, and higher raw material costs tied to gold prices.

Quarterly, the company posted an operating loss of KRW 6.28 billion in the third quarter of 2025 and KRW 26.2 billion in the fourth quarter, when net loss attributable to controlling shareholders reached KRW 134.5 billion.

In 2026, operating profit was KRW 7.64 billion in the first quarter, turning positive, and KRW 13.1 billion in the second quarter, up 71.5% from the prior quarter.

In the third quarter revenue fell 11.3% from the prior quarter to KRW 128.5 billion, operating profit dropped to KRW 2.39 billion, and net loss attributable to controlling shareholders was KRW 3.99 billion. The net loss attributable to controlling shareholders for the last four quarters totals KRW 119.5 billion.

Total equity was KRW 270 billion at the end of 2025, of which KRW 266 billion belonged to controlling shareholders. Operating cash flow fell from KRW 124 billion in 2022 to KRW 9.53 billion in 2025.

The disclosed figures do not break down the KRW 134.5 billion fourth-quarter 2025 net loss by item, so the composition is a matter for the notes to the annual report.

05

Industry analysis

LB Semicon's core DDI back-end business moves with display panel cycles. Earlier press reports said a profit slump at LG Display spread through LX Semicon to LB Semicon, and that LB Semicon handles a large share of LX Semicon's DDI back-end work.

A 2025 report said gold prices had risen nearly 80% from the third quarter of 2023 and plating solution costs more than 40%, raising the cost burden.

LX Semicon, in the same DDI chain, reported a third-quarter 2026 operating loss of KRW 15.6 billion on revenue of KRW 336.0 billion, turning to a loss year on year, which the company linked to weaker demand from downstream industries and price-cut pressure.

That suggests DDI back-end demand and pricing have not clearly entered a recovery. A 2025 report also said one production line's design investment had been temporarily halted because of weak market demand.

In non-DDI areas, AI data center and automotive PMIC demand is cited as a new volume base, and LB Semicon handles back-end work for Qualcomm's AI data center and automotive PMIC products, which links this segment to downstream expansion.

Other listed OSAT companies are also shifting toward high-value packaging, so competition is moving toward technology and customer qualification. The size of LB Semicon's non-DDI revenue was not confirmed in disclosures or reports, so its weight in the business should be read qualitatively.

06

Outlook

The most concrete change the company has disclosed is the Qualcomm volume. After passing Qualcomm's technical review, quality management system audit and reliability verification, the company received mass production approval in August 2026 and built a dedicated Qualcomm line.

Press reports describe the arrangement as a long-term supply agreement in which price and delivery are not fixed in advance; Qualcomm issues purchase orders within the capacity LB Semicon provides, and the company must keep a 10% spare capacity at all times.

In August 2026 the company raised KRW 34.26 billion through a rights offering, of which KRW 30 billion is earmarked for equipment, with KRW 24.8 billion for bumping and KRW 5.2 billion for back-end processes.

The rights offering announced at about KRW 50 billion with the first-quarter 2026 results was therefore completed at a smaller amount. A press report also cited an industry view that Qualcomm PMIC revenue could reach about KRW 50 billion by 2028, but this is not formal company guidance.

The company plans to begin mass production of products for global customers in the second half of 2026 and to expand equipment in stages through 2027. Renesas power semiconductor production belongs to the same push.

A 2025 report quoted the company targeting KRW 500 billion in revenue and an operating profit turnaround that year, while the disclosed 2025 results show KRW 479.8 billion in revenue and an operating loss of KRW 39.8 billion.

Market data show first-half 2026 revenue up 20.0% year on year, with operating profit and net income both turning positive. Management continues to state that it will shift the mix toward higher-value back-end work, including power semiconductors and non-DDI products.

Bull factors

Ten-year Qualcomm supply agreement and mass production approval

LB Semicon signed a ten-year supply agreement with Qualcomm and received mass production approval in August 2026. Reports say non-DDI 200mm capacity is planned to rise to 36,000 wafers per month by 2028, with supply made within capacity as Qualcomm issues orders. This could diversify revenue beyond the DDI base.

Turn in the direction of 2026 earnings

Operating profit turned positive at KRW 7.64 billion in the first quarter of 2026, and rose to KRW 13.1 billion in the second quarter, up 71.5% from the prior quarter. A market data tally shows first-half 2026 revenue up 20.0% year on year, with both operating and net income turning positive. The quarterly turn after a long run of losses is the main bullish reading.

Expansion of non-DDI and power semiconductor customers

The company said mass production of back-end packaging for Renesas power semiconductors has ramped up and that it is working with additional global customers. Mass production for global customers is planned to start in the second half of 2026. Customer wins in the non-DDI area point to the company's stated diversification moving into actual contracts.

Bear factors

Weaker third quarter

Third-quarter 2026 revenue was KRW 128.5 billion, down 11.3% from the prior quarter, and operating profit fell 81.8% to KRW 2.39 billion. Net income attributable to controlling shareholders swung back to a net loss of KRW 3.99 billion.

With a cumulative four-quarter net loss attributable to controlling shareholders of KRW 119.5 billion, quarterly profits have not yet built into cumulative earnings.

Debt burden and capital structure

The share of debt relative to equity has kept growing since 2022. Press reports put short-term borrowings at KRW 139.8 billion and current portion of long-term borrowings at KRW 100.6 billion at the end of the third quarter of 2025, a repayment burden of more than KRW 240 billion within one year.

Funding from the 2026 rights offering and convertible bonds has eased this, but the debt structure remains a burden.

DDI concentration and cost and price pressure

More than 70% of revenue comes from DDI products, so results move directly with the display cycle. LX Semicon's third-quarter 2026 operating loss, linked to weaker downstream demand and price-cut pressure, is a weak signal in the same chain. Higher gold-linked raw material costs and customer demands for price cuts remain pressures on margins.

Risk factors

Customer concentration risk

Earlier reports put revenue dependence on Samsung Electronics and LX Semicon at 73.06%, and the Qualcomm ramp is changing that concentration. Because the Qualcomm arrangement is order-based and requires a 10% spare capacity at all times, equipment costs could remain if customer volumes fall.

Financial and dilution risk

Total liabilities stood at KRW 414.1 billion at the end of 2025, and the net loss attributable to controlling shareholders in the fourth quarter of 2025 was KRW 134.5 billion. Successive rights offerings and convertible bond issues add share count growth and financing costs.

The rights offering announced with the first-quarter 2026 results involved 12 million new shares, which dilutes existing holders.

Raw material and pricing risk

A 2025 report said gold prices had risen nearly 80% from the third quarter of 2023 and plating solution prices more than 40%. When raw material costs flow quickly into cost of sales and customers push for price cuts, operating margins can deteriorate fast. The operating margin fell to -8.3% in 2025.

12

Overall view

After three consecutive years of operating losses from 2023 and a KRW 150.9 billion net loss attributable to controlling shareholders in 2025, LB Semicon is changing course in 2026 with quarterly operating profit and Qualcomm mass production approval.

Revenue rose for two consecutive years in 2024 and 2025, yet the operating margin fell to -8.3%. In the third quarter of 2026 revenue and profit both fell and a net loss attributable to controlling shareholders reappeared, so quarterly results are not yet stable.

The ten-year Qualcomm agreement, the planned capacity expansion through 2028 and Renesas power semiconductor production are concrete grounds for customer diversification.

On the other side, a debt ratio of 153.3%, a repayment burden within one year, and continued dependence on a few customers and the DDI cycle still constrain results. The price-to-book ratio is below 1x, dividends are effectively absent, and a price-to-earnings ratio cannot be calculated.

The next checkpoints are the profit breakdown and borrowing levels in the 2026 annual report, and whether Qualcomm volume shows up in operating margins in the first quarter of 2027.
