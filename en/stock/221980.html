Kdchem: Merger Decision Meets a Return to Net Loss

02

Business structure

Kdchem is a fine chemicals company whose core business is PVC stabilizers, and it operates as a single PVC stabilizer business segment, so no segment revenue split is reported. Its main product, liquid organic stabilizers, is an additive blended into PVC resin and is essential in PVC processing.

Key applications include calendering, decorative and high-gloss films, and food packaging wrap, so demand tracks processing volumes in these markets. A securities information site notes that the company is building growth momentum on expanding overseas export sales. The affiliate being absorbed, J.K.

Materials, is an unlisted company producing BOPP and BOPE films, and most of its shares are held by CEO Min Nam-gyu and related parties. In 2024 it exited the BOPA and PE film business and sold its Shinpyeong plant, reorganizing its Dangjin Sunseong plant around BOPP and BOPE.

The BOPE Line 2 of the absorbed company returned to normal operation in September 2026. The Kdchem board judged that under the current structure, technology and sales cooperation between additives and films is split by legal entity, limiting diversification benefits.

After completion, the additive and film businesses would sit within a single legal entity. The competitive position starts from Kdchem's identity as a PVC stabilizer specialist, while the film business is a new area brought in through the merger.

04

Earnings analysis

On an annual basis, revenue fell from KRW 62.1 billion in 2022 to KRW 59.1 billion in 2023 (down 4.8% year on year), then rose to KRW 62.4 billion in 2024 (up 5.5%) and KRW 62.9 billion in 2025 (up 0.8%).

After the 2023 low, revenue rose for two consecutive years from 2024 through 2025, and 2025 was the largest year of 2022 to 2025. Operating profit went from KRW 5.7 billion in 2022 to KRW 6.25 billion in 2023 (up 9.7%) and KRW 7.47 billion in 2024 (up 19.5%), making 2024 the largest year of the period.

Operating profit then fell 6.7% to KRW 6.97 billion in 2025, so revenue grew while profit declined. The operating margin moved from 9.2% in 2022 to 10.6% in 2023 and 12.0% in 2024, before easing to 11.1% in 2025 and staying in double digits.

Net income attributable to controlling shareholders dropped from KRW 8.16 billion in 2022 to KRW 5.32 billion in 2023 (down 34.9%), then edged up 1.3% to KRW 5.39 billion in 2024 before falling 34.9% to KRW 3.51 billion in 2025, the smallest year of the period.

In 2024, operating profit rose 19.5% while controlling-shareholder net income rose only 1.3%, which means less profit was retained below the operating line. Net income including non-controlling interests was reported at the same amounts as controlling-shareholder net income in each year from 2022 to 2025.

At the end of 2025, total equity was KRW 102.6 billion and total liabilities were KRW 25.5 billion, with the debt ratio at 24.8%. Operating cash flow rose from KRW 3.09 billion in 2022 to KRW 6.42 billion in 2023, KRW 7.04 billion in 2024 and KRW 6.51 billion in 2025, staying above KRW 6 billion since 2023.

Quarterly, Q3 2025 began with revenue of KRW 15.8 billion, operating profit of KRW 1.98 billion and controlling-shareholder net income of KRW 2.3 billion, and Q1 2026 revenue of KRW 16.3 billion marked the peak.

Revenue then declined for two consecutive quarters, to KRW 16.2 billion in Q2 2026 and KRW 15.9 billion in Q3 2026. Operating profit fell from KRW 1.98 billion in Q1 2026 to KRW 1.56 billion in Q2 and KRW 1.23 billion in Q3 2026, a 38.1% year-on-year decline in Q3.

Controlling-shareholder net income jumped to KRW 4.43 billion in Q2 2026 and then swung to a net loss of KRW 6.15 billion in Q3 2026. The net loss fell far more steeply than operating profit, pointing to a heavy cost burden below the operating line.

05

Industry analysis

PVC stabilizers are additives used in PVC resin processing, so demand moves with PVC processing volumes. Applications spread across calendering, decorative and high-gloss films, and food packaging wrap, so the business is not concentrated in a single end market.

One securities information site describes the company as holding a stable position in the PVC stabilizer market. Within the chemicals sector, large petrochemical companies run multiple businesses, whereas Kdchem operates a single PVC stabilizer segment, so changes in demand feed directly into its results.

On the sector backdrop, LG Chem said that profitability in its petrochemical segment worsened in Q3 2026, a sign that swings in feedstock and product prices are weighing on broader chemicals profitability.

Kdchem's film business is the area the absorbed company narrowed to BOPP and BOPE after exiting BOPA and PE in 2024. The company's disclosure describes its own BOPE Line 1 as the only biaxially oriented BOPE production facility in Korea, according to the company's understanding.

Kdchem's differentiation within the sector rests on its focus on the niche of PVC additives and, after the merger, its extension into film materials. Because film earnings depend on BOPE demand and utilization, the additive business and the film business will need to be tracked separately after the merger.

Outlook

Kdchem's board decided on November 12, 2026 to absorb J.K. Materials through a merger. The merger will issue 825,609 new shares, equal to about 20.5% of existing shares outstanding.

The shareholder record date and the voting-rights record date are both November 27, 2026, and the window for shareholders to notify dissent runs from December 4 to December 20, 2026.

The extraordinary shareholder meeting is scheduled for December 21, 2026, with approval of the merger agreement and an amendment adding film-related business purposes on the agenda. The merger effective date is January 22, 2027, and the merger registration is expected on January 25, 2027.

The outside valuation firm Daejoo Accounting Corp. judged the merger ratio to be fair. In the outside valuation, the absorbed company's sales reflect higher volumes from BOPE Line 2 but use a utilization rate of about 80%, lowered from the business plan's roughly 93%.

The earnings-value estimate uses cash flows for 2026 to 2030 with a 0% perpetual growth rate. During the merger review, in October 2026 Kdchem notified cancellation of a contract over non-supply of raw materials and moved to seize related receivables.

The company's estimate of total liabilities after the merger is about KRW 55 billion, roughly KRW 29.5 billion higher than before the merger.

The items to track are the outcome of the extraordinary shareholder meeting, the post-merger debt level and the operating results of the film business, and the operating margin in the 2026 annual business report.

Bull factors

Operating margin held in double digits

The operating margin moved from 9.2% in 2022 to 12.0% in 2024 and 11.1% in 2025, staying in double digits. Revenue rose for two consecutive years through 2025, which was the largest year of 2022 to 2025. Sustained core margins in a single-segment structure are cited as a bullish factor.

Q2 2026 return to profit

Controlling-shareholder net income rose to KRW 4.43 billion in Q2 2026, up 374.8% from the prior quarter, showing a return to profit. Operating profit in Q1 2026 was KRW 1.98 billion, up 60.7% from the prior quarter. Quarterly profit having re-entered positive territory is a bullish point.

Financial headroom and cash generation

Total equity at the end of 2025 was KRW 102.6 billion and the debt ratio was 24.8%. Operating cash flow rose from KRW 3.09 billion in 2022 to KRW 6.51 billion in 2025 and stayed above KRW 6 billion since 2023. The modest debt burden before the merger is cited as a bullish factor.

09

Bear factors

Swing to a net loss in Q3 2026

Net income attributable to controlling shareholders swung to a loss of KRW 6.15 billion in Q3 2026. Operating profit was KRW 1.23 billion, down 38.1% year on year. The cumulative loss over the last four quarters totals KRW 27.7 million, so profitability has not been sustained.

Absorbing a loss-making affiliate and rising debt

The absorbed company, J.K. Materials, recorded an operating loss of KRW 9.1 billion in 2025 from its continuing BOPP and BOPE business. The post-merger total liabilities estimated by Kdchem are about KRW 55 billion, more than double total liabilities of KRW 25.5 billion at the end of 2025. Taking on a loss-making business together with its debt is a key bearish factor.

Dilution and a stronger controlling stake

After the merger, CEO Min Nam-gyu's stake rises from 37.68% to 43.98%. The general shareholders' stake falls from 21.84% to 18.13%. The new shares equal about 20.5% of existing shares, so dilution for general shareholders is clear.

Risk factors

Merger execution risk

Even after shareholder approval, procedures continue until the merger effective date of January 22, 2027, and a dissent notice or a rejected resolution could change the timetable. Absorbing the affiliate's loss-making business and debt would change the capital structure and earnings base from pre-merger levels. Post-merger results will need to be read separately from pre-merger figures.

Advance payment recovery and contract cancellation

During the merger review, in October 2026 Kdchem notified cancellation of a contract due to non-supply of raw materials and moved to seize related receivables.

In the merger ratio calculation, an advance payment of about KRW 8.2 billion made to a raw material supplier accounted for most of the deduction from asset value. Whether the advance is recovered affects how asset value is judged.

Single-business concentration and earnings volatility

The company runs a single PVC stabilizer segment, so demand changes and cost pressure feed directly into earnings. Controlling-shareholder net income fell 34.9% in 2023, rose 1.3% in 2024 and fell 34.9% in 2025, showing large swings. The structure leaves few other businesses to offset weakness in profit.

Overall view

Kdchem operates a single PVC stabilizer business, with revenue held in the KRW 60 billion range over 2022 to 2025 and an operating margin of 11.1% in 2025, which stayed in double digits.

However, net income attributable to controlling shareholders fell from KRW 8.16 billion in 2022 to KRW 3.51 billion in 2025, and turned into a KRW 6.15 billion net loss in Q3 2026.

At the same time, the board decided to absorb a loss-making affiliate, and the estimated post-merger total liabilities of about KRW 55 billion represent the key change in the capital structure.

Bullish points include sustained core margins, a return to profit in Q2 2026, and financial headroom with total equity of KRW 102.6 billion. Bearish points include the Q3 2026 loss, the debt and dilution from the merger, and the question of recovering the advance payment.

On valuation, the facts are that the price-to-book ratio and market capitalization sit below book value and equity levels.

The next checkpoints are the outcome of the December 21, 2026 extraordinary shareholder meeting, the January 22, 2027 merger effective date, and the operating margin and debt ratio in the 2026 annual business report.
