A Fabless Pivot From PMOLED Chips to Test Boards

02

Business structure

LDTInc is a fabless semiconductor company that designs and sells display driver ICs. Its core product is the PMOLED driver IC, supplied to customers including photo printer makers, and the company is expanding its applications toward e-cigarettes and industrial, medical and automotive displays.

The company estimates it holds about a 20% share of the global PMOLED driver IC market by unit volume, and its IR materials present it as the world's second-largest supplier.

The second pillar is a fire-detection IoT sensor network, with more than 68,000 sensors installed across industrial sites, public institutions and traditional markets nationwide.

In September 2026 the company said it would convert this business into an integrated industrial safety management platform linking temperature, humidity, gas, pressure, CCTV and worker location data, and it formally launched the Safemate Cloud service on September 2, 2026.

The third line, optical sensor ICs, began development in 2022, entered mass production in 2025, and began supplying infrared-receiving optical sensor ICs to two companies in China that year.

The largest shareholder is TSE, a semiconductor test equipment maker, and an affiliated PCB manufacturer completes the group structure. For 2025, the company cited higher sensor-business sales and lower semiconductor-segment costs as the drivers of its profit improvement.

Since the industrial safety platform accounts for about 10% of sales, the remainder appears to be driven mainly by the semiconductor business.

04

Earnings analysis

Annual revenue fell 23.9% from KRW 11.7 billion in 2022 to KRW 8.93 billion in 2023, then rose 12.3% to KRW 10 billion in 2024 and 10.1% to KRW 11 billion in 2025, which the filings record as two consecutive years of growth from 2024 through 2025.

The largest year in the 2022–2025 period was 2022, and the smallest was 2023. Operating profit of KRW 1.48 billion in 2022 turned into an operating loss of KRW 349 million in 2023, and the loss widened to KRW 836 million in 2024 as the operating deficit continued.

Operating profit of KRW 306 million in 2025 marked a return to profit. The operating margin moved from 12.7% in 2022 to -3.9% in 2023 and -8.3% in 2024, then recovered to 2.8% in 2025.

Net income attributable to controlling shareholders went from KRW 1.95 billion in 2022 to a net loss of KRW 140 million in 2023 and a net loss of KRW 269 million in 2024, before turning to KRW 1.24 billion in 2025. Net income including non-controlling interests was reported as KRW 1.24 billion for 2025.

Operating cash flow was KRW 3.3 billion in 2022, KRW 726 million in 2023, -KRW 55.38 million in 2024 and KRW 631 million in 2025.

On a quarterly basis, revenue was KRW 2.87 billion in the third quarter of 2025 and KRW 3.13 billion in the fourth quarter, a 9.2% increase on the prior quarter, then KRW 1.67 billion in the first quarter of 2026, a 46.5% decline, KRW 3.85 billion in the second quarter, up 130.2%, and KRW 1.56 billion in the third quarter, down 59.6% and 45.6% from a year earlier.

Operating profit was KRW 874 million in the second quarter of 2026, while the first and third quarters posted operating losses of KRW 440 million and KRW 323 million. Because revenue swings sharply from quarter to quarter, operating results move with it.

Net income attributable to controlling shareholders was KRW 314 million in the third quarter of 2025 and KRW 542 million in the fourth quarter, then a net loss of KRW 217 million in the first quarter of 2026, KRW 949 million in the second quarter, and a net loss of KRW 594 million in the third quarter.

05

Industry analysis

The PMOLED driver IC market is a niche that depends on small display applications such as photo printers and e-cigarettes. The company estimates a share of about 20% by unit volume and presents itself as the world's second-largest supplier in its IR materials.

Its share targets of 19% in 2026, 28% in 2028 and 40% in 2030 assume it secures new applications such as industrial, medical and automotive displays.

In test boards, the company cited Yole Group data in its IR materials, projecting the global probe card PCB market to grow from USD 517 million in 2026 to USD 686 million in 2030. Equipment demand is also improving.

A June 2026 report by Electronic Times said semiconductor equipment spending was expected to rise about 51.6% from the first quarter of 2025 to a record USD 60.5 billion in the fourth quarter of 2026, with test and related equipment at USD 11.4 billion in that quarter.

LDTInc's test-board business is still before volume production, with the company planning to secure a production base through 2027 before expanding from 2028. Its CEO acknowledged that in the early stage the company does not differ greatly from existing outsourced design firms and must win on fair competition. The competitive position in test boards therefore has not yet been confirmed by order results.

06

Outlook

The company said that from the second half of 2026 through 2027 it will use TSE's customer base to build a volume-production and revenue base for test boards. According to a report by Economic Review, the company plans to begin mass production of multilayer semiconductor test boards from the second half of 2026.

Its target is to pass customer quality qualification for its first project in the fourth quarter of 2026, with new domestic and overseas customers from 2028 and expansion into non-memory areas such as SoC from 2029.

In its existing business, the company set a PMOLED driver IC share target of 19% in 2026, 28% in 2028 and 40% in 2030. For optical sensor ICs, it plans to expand the customer base to four companies, including a Tier 1 supplier, during 2026, and to enter distance sensors and security CCTV modules over the medium term.

For the safety platform, it plans to combine rental and subscription models on top of Safemate Cloud, which launched on September 2, 2026, to build recurring revenue. Reaching the 60% share target by 2030 would require both the range of connected sensors and the number of subscribers to grow.

At the extraordinary shareholder meeting on September 22, 2026, shareholder proposals to elect a director and dismiss an auditor were all rejected, keeping the existing management in place. In the same period the company decided to cancel 421,732 treasury shares.

08

Bull factors

Entry into test boards through group customers

The company said it will use the customer network of its parent TSE to build a test-board mass-production base between the second half of 2026 and 2027. It plans to enter the probe card PCB design market using about 30 years of IC design and high-speed PCB design experience.

Its IR materials cite external forecasts of growth in the probe card PCB market through 2030, which frames room for expansion in the new business.

Low leverage and positive operating cash flow

The debt-to-equity ratio was 11.2% at the end of 2025, with total equity of KRW 20.3 billion. Operating cash flow was KRW 631 million in 2025, a positive figure. The company said its stable financial structure gives room to strengthen R&D and marketing.

Second-quarter profit in 2026 and an insider purchase

Operating profit in the second quarter of 2026 was KRW 874 million, when revenue reached KRW 3.85 billion. Executive director Jeong Nam-jin disclosed open-market purchases of 2,000 common shares between November 2 and November 9, 2026, bringing his holding to 11,000 shares. This is a disclosed fact, and its meaning cannot be determined with certainty.

Bear factors

Large swings in quarterly results

Operating losses appeared in the first and third quarters of 2026, and third-quarter revenue of KRW 1.56 billion fell 45.6% from a year earlier. Net income attributable to controlling shareholders over the last four quarters totaled KRW 680 million, and the third quarter of 2026 recorded a net loss of KRW 594 million. Because profit moves with revenue volume, the trend in results is hard to read.

New businesses contribute little revenue so far

Revenue tied to the industrial safety platform is about 10% of total sales. The test-board business is aiming to pass its first project's quality qualification in the fourth quarter of 2026, with a production base planned by 2027. The CEO said early on the company does not differ greatly from existing outsourced design firms.

Uncertainty in shareholder composition and management

At the extraordinary shareholder meeting on September 22, 2026, shareholder proposals from the Melfas side to elect a director and dismiss an auditor were all rejected. Media noted that the vote came against a party with a past dispute that led to a delisting.

The existing management was retained, but the shareholder conflict remains a factor in judging strategic continuity.

10

Risk factors

Business concentration and PMOLED dependence

A large part of revenue depends on PMOLED driver ICs and related applications. The share targets of 28% in 2028 and 40% in 2030 assume new applications in industrial, medical and automotive displays. If those applications are delayed, the timing of the share targets could slip as well.

Group dependence and competition from outsourced designers

The early customer base for test boards relies on parent TSE, while the design work must compete with outside firms to prove viability. The company has also raised concern about technology leakage where high-difficulty PCB design is supplied through outside vendors. Because group volume does not transfer directly, the speed of monetization is uncertain.

Earnings volatility and small-cap characteristics

The company posted losses in 2023 and 2024, and after returning to profit in 2025, its quarterly results in 2026 have changed direction several times. As a small-cap stock, thin trading can cause large price moves. Earnings volatility and trading characteristics together can act as risk factors.

What to watch next

    Whether the first test-board project passes customer quality qualification. It is the first indicator of whether a revenue base for the new business is actually being built.

  2. End of 2026

    Whether the plan to expand optical sensor IC customers to four companies, including a Tier 1 supplier, is carried out. It indicates the expansion of the semiconductor portfolio beyond the sensor business.

  3. Second half of 2026 through 2027

    The start of multilayer semiconductor test-board mass production and when revenue begins from TSE's customer base. A delay in the move to mass production would also affect the 2028 growth target.

  4. Late March 2027 (statutory deadline for the annual report)

    The 2026 annual operating margin, whether the company stays in profit, and the share of revenue from the industrial safety platform in the annual report. These three items in the next periodic report are the earnings checkpoints for the shift to new businesses.

Overall view

LDTInc returned to operating profit of KRW 306 million in 2025 after an operating loss in 2024, but its 2026 quarterly results alternate between the KRW 874 million operating profit of the second quarter and operating losses in the first and third quarters.

On the business side, it is positioning test boards and an industrial safety platform as new pillars built on PMOLED driver ICs and its fire-detection sensor network. Strengths include a debt-to-equity ratio of 11.2%, a test-board plan linked to the parent's customer network, and the second-quarter profit in 2026.

Weaknesses include new-business revenue still at about 10% of sales and wide swings in quarterly results. Shareholder proposals were all rejected at the September 2026 extraordinary meeting, keeping existing management in place, but the conflict has not fully resolved.

The next checkpoints are the quality qualification result for the test board in the fourth quarter of 2026 and the annual operating margin in the 2026 annual report.
