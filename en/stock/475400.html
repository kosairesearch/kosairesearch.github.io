02

Business structure

CMES Robotics combines 3D vision, AI and robot control technology to automate unstructured tasks in logistics centers and manufacturing sites. The company was founded in 2014, listed on KOSDAQ in 2024, and changed its name to CMES Robotics at its general shareholders meeting on March 27, 2026.

Its main products include piece picking, random palletizing, depalletizing, precision assembly, dispensing, robot guidance and inspection automation.

Logistics customers reportedly include Coupang, CJ Logistics and Lotte, while manufacturing customers include Hyundai, Kia, Mando and LG Electronics, according to a securities information service.

Revenue is generated mainly through supply contracts that combine equipment sales and installation, with repeat orders from large customers supporting sales. Overseas, the North American market, entered in 2021, generated about KRW 2.5 billion in sales in 2025, according to the company.

In 2026 the company signed an MOU with Rainbow Robotics on dual-arm robot commercialization and a joint R&D MOU with NC AI on physical AI. The company positions its ability to link data acquisition, AI training and mass-production deployment in-house as a competitive strength.

In competitive terms it is compared with listed robot automation companies such as Rainbow Robotics, Robotis and Doosan Robotics, although product mix and sales scale differ across them.

04

Earnings analysis

Based on DART-filed annual results, sales were KRW 7.64 billion in 2023, KRW 6.89 billion in 2024 (down 9.8% year on year) and KRW 13.1 billion in 2025 (up 89.5% year on year), with 2025 the largest year and 2024 the smallest within 2023 to 2025.

Over the same period the operating loss was KRW 9.99 billion in 2023, KRW 14.3 billion in 2024 (losses continued and widened from the prior year relative to the base) and KRW 18.4 billion in 2025 (losses continued and widened year on year), and the company recorded losses for three consecutive years from 2023 through 2025.

The operating margin was -130.7% in 2023, -207.4% in 2024 and -141.0% in 2025, so the margin loss narrowed in 2025 even as absolute losses grew.

The net loss attributable to controlling shareholders was KRW 15.7 billion in 2023, KRW 13.5 billion in 2024 (losses narrowed) and KRW 16.8 billion in 2025 (losses widened), and the net loss including non-controlling interests matched these figures.

At the end of 2025, total equity was KRW 59 billion, all of it attributable to controlling shareholders, with total liabilities of KRW 9.96 billion and a debt-to-equity ratio of 23.9% in 2023, 9.1% in 2024 and 16.9% in 2025.

Operating cash flow was negative in each of the three years, at -KRW 8.16 billion in 2023, -KRW 12.7 billion in 2024 and -KRW 14.5 billion in 2025, with outflows growing in size.

Quarterly sales were KRW 3.89 billion in Q3 2025, KRW 4.44 billion in Q4 2025 (up 14.1% from the prior quarter) and KRW 5.77 billion in Q1 2026 (up 30.1%), before falling to KRW 3.74 billion in Q2 2026 (down 35.1%) and rising to KRW 10.5 billion in Q3 2026 (up 180.5% from the prior quarter and up 170.2% year on year).

For the Q2 dip, the company attributed the lag to customers delaying equipment investment, which pushed back recognition in its robot solutions segment, according to a ZDNet report dated August 19, 2026.

Operating losses widened from KRW 3.8 billion in Q3 2025 to KRW 5.51 billion in Q4 2025 and then narrowed to KRW 5.13 billion in Q1 2026, KRW 4.77 billion in Q2 2026 and KRW 3.03 billion in Q3 2026, marking five consecutive loss-making quarters from Q3 2025 through Q3 2026.

The net loss attributable to controlling shareholders was KRW 2.47 billion in Q3 2026, narrowing from the prior year, and totaled KRW 16.7 billion over the last four quarters.

05

Industry analysis

The market CMES Robotics serves is demand for automation in logistics and manufacturing sites, where the aim is to reduce musculoskeletal injuries and accident risks for workers, and the company frames this as a shift toward physical AI.

However, the first-half 2026 results showed that revenue recognition depends on the timing of customers' equipment investment, according to a ZDNet report dated August 19, 2026.

The company says its combination of palletizing and piece picking on the logistics side with assembly and inspection on the manufacturing side gives it a broad range of applications, and it reported a top-grade (AA) technology evaluation for its automation equipment, according to Robot Newspaper.

Among listed robot automation peers, Rainbow Robotics, Robotis, Doosan Robotics and Yuilrobotics are commonly cited, but their business and profit structures differ, which makes direct comparison difficult.

CMES Robotics highlights its 3D vision technology, which recognizes objects whose shape and position are not fixed, as its point of differentiation.

A company official said repeat orders from large customers are driving the start of a full-scale sales growth phase, and the company plans to strengthen its physical AI capabilities through cooperation with domestic robot companies, according to the same ZDNet report.

Outlook

At its first-half 2026 results briefing on August 13, 2026, the company said that if order flow continued through the second half, it could achieve its largest annual results since founding (according to Robot Newspaper).

In the same briefing it said the 2026 order backlog stood at KRW 22.5 billion, about 2.4 times the prior-year level.

Key contracts include a supply agreement signed on June 23, 2026 with LG Energy Solution Arizona for electrode, coater and insulation thickness measuring equipment, worth about KRW 1.4 billion with a contract period running to August 31, 2028.

A supply agreement with Kenzig Corporation for logistics robot automation equipment worth KRW 1.42 billion was signed on August 11, 2026, with a contract period through March 31, 2027.

Three equipment supply contracts with a global sports brand manufacturer from May to July 2026 were reported to total roughly KRW 10 billion, according to a ZDNet report dated August 19, 2026. In October 2026 the company reportedly unveiled an AI random palletizer at the PACK EXPO in the United States.

The company says it will pursue cost structure improvements and higher profitability based on repeat orders from large customers. However, after the first-half break-even failed to materialize, reports said the 2026 break-even target set at the 2024 listing is under pressure.

The next items to track are how quickly the backlog converts into revenue and whether the narrowing of operating losses continues on a full-year basis.

Bull factors

Repeat orders widen the revenue base

Q3 2026 sales of KRW 10.5 billion rose 170.2% year on year. According to the company, cumulative sales from Q1 to Q3 2026 reached KRW 20 billion, surpassing full-year 2025 sales of KRW 13.1 billion.

The 2026 order backlog of KRW 22.5 billion is about 2.4 times the prior-year level, and repeat orders from large customers are cited as widening the revenue base.

Operating losses narrowing

Operating loss narrowed from KRW 5.51 billion in Q4 2025 to KRW 3.03 billion in Q3 2026, and the Q3 2026 loss was smaller than the KRW 3.8 billion recorded in Q3 2025. The company says it will raise profitability through a larger business scale and cost structure improvements. Reports also indicate the first-half operating margin improved from the same period a year earlier.

Physical AI partnerships and overseas expansion

In 2026 the company signed an MOU with Rainbow Robotics on commercializing dual-arm robots and a joint R&D MOU with NC AI. Its North American sales were about KRW 2.5 billion in 2025, according to the company, and in 2026 it signed a supply contract with LG Energy Solution Arizona. The renaming to CMES Robotics in March 2026 is part of consolidating its global brand.

Bear factors

Large swings in quarterly sales

Q2 2026 sales of KRW 3.74 billion fell 35.1% from the prior quarter, and Q3 2026 sales of KRW 10.5 billion rose 180.5% from that level. Whether a single quarter's surge reflects sustained demand is difficult to judge from quarterly data alone.

The company attributed the Q2 weakness to delayed customer equipment investment, which indicates that revenue timing depends on customer schedules.

Persistent operating losses and cash outflows

Operating losses widened to KRW 9.99 billion in 2023, KRW 14.3 billion in 2024 and KRW 18.4 billion in 2025, with losses recorded for three consecutive years from 2023 to 2025. Operating cash flow was negative in all three years, at -KRW 8.16 billion, -KRW 12.7 billion and -KRW 14.5 billion. The net loss attributable to controlling shareholders over the last four quarters totaled KRW 16.7 billion.

Setback to the break-even target set at listing

At its 2024 listing the company set a 2026 break-even target, but after the first-half 2026 break-even failed, reports said the target was under pressure. Total equity of KRW 59 billion at the end of 2025 provides capacity to absorb losses, but that capacity shrinks if losses persist.

If delays in customer equipment investment repeatedly push back revenue recognition, the break-even timing could also be delayed.

10

Risk factors

Customer concentration and revenue timing

Repeat orders are concentrated among a small number of large customers, so the equipment investment schedule of any one customer directly affects sales. The Q2 decline and Q3 surge in 2026 illustrate this structure.

Contract periods running to March 31, 2027 and beyond mean quarterly swings could continue depending on when performance obligations are completed.

Pace of earnings improvement

The 2026 break-even target was missed in the first half, and operating losses have persisted for five consecutive quarters. Narrowing losses only translate into profit if cost stabilization and expanded repeat orders happen together, and that timing can be checked in the 2026 annual report.

If cost and selling expense structures do not improve as sales rise, the scope for reducing operating losses could stay limited.

Ownership concentration and float

Lee Sung-ho and five related parties held 39.27% of total shares as of May 13, 2026, according to a securities information service. High insider ownership limits the portion of shares actively traded. Reports have described sharp share price moves in response to order news and earnings expectations.

Overall view

CMES Robotics grew 2025 sales 89.5% to KRW 13.1 billion, and the company announced that Q3 2026 sales of KRW 10.5 billion were its largest quarter since founding.

However, operating losses were recorded for three consecutive years from 2023 through 2025, and the company has posted five consecutive loss-making quarters from Q3 2025 through Q3 2026. Operating losses have narrowed each quarter in 2026, but the 2026 break-even target set at listing was missed in the first half.

A 2026 order backlog of KRW 22.5 billion and repeat orders from large customers are positive signs for the revenue base. Quarterly sales, however, have swung widely with customer equipment investment timing, so more quarters are needed to judge the trend.

The next items to check are the full-year operating loss in the 2026 annual report and the Q1 2027 quarterly results.
