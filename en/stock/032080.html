02

Business structure

Aztech WB began in 2069 as a woolen fabric maker and listed on KOSDAQ in 2000. The business is broadly split into a fabric segment and an apparel segment.

The fabric segment develops premium luxury fashion materials and specialty materials for protective clothing, while the apparel segment produces formalwear, casualwear and sportswear. The company runs an in-house process covering yarn spinning, dyeing, weaving, processing, knitting and garment production.

Its product lines are the fashion-market materials division for men's and women's suit fabrics, the uniform materials division, and the fashion promotion division in apparel. Main uses include suits, women's wear, group uniforms, work uniforms and workwear fabrics.

One stock information site put fabric-related sales at about 61% and apparel-related sales at about 38% in 2024. That mix is based on 2024 data. Workwear materials such as protective and firefighting clothing are cited as a field where development is producing results.

On technology, the company holds the GRS and RWS international eco-certifications, and its in-house research institute develops high-heat, flame-retardant and waterproof industrial protective fabrics. The 2026 value-up plan points to widening the apparel-fabric base toward higher-value industrial materials. An online fabric channel, Aztech Wool, serves as a secondary sales route.

04

Earnings analysis

Sales fell from KRW 45.5 billion in 2022 to KRW 37.1 billion in 2023 (down 18.4% year on year), then to KRW 35.4 billion in 2024 (down 4.6%) and KRW 30.2 billion in 2025 (down 14.5%). Sales declined for three consecutive years from 2023, with 2022 the largest year and 2025 the smallest in the 2022–2025 range.

Operating profit of KRW 2.15 billion in 2022 turned into an operating loss of KRW 75.13 million in 2023. It returned to a profit of KRW 545 million in 2024 and then fell 15.8% to KRW 459 million in 2025. The operating margin was 4.7% in 2022, -0.2% in 2023, 1.5% in 2024 and 1.5% in 2025.

Net profit attributable to controlling shareholders moved from a loss of KRW 1.42 billion in 2022 to a loss of KRW 1.32 billion in 2023 (a narrower loss), then a loss of KRW 2.04 billion in 2024 (a wider loss), before turning to a profit of KRW 929 million in 2025.

Net profit including non-controlling interests was disclosed at the same amounts in each of those four years. The debt-to-equity ratio fell from 29.1% in 2022 to 15.0% in 2023, 11.0% in 2024 and 10.4% in 2025.

At the end of 2025, total equity was KRW 107.2 billion, all of it attributable to controlling shareholders, and total liabilities were KRW 11.1 billion. Operating cash flow was KRW 4.03 billion in 2022, KRW 7.75 billion in 2023, KRW 2.57 billion in 2024 and KRW 6.33 billion in 2025, positive in all four years.

On a quarterly basis, Q3 2025 showed sales of KRW 7.94 billion, an operating loss of KRW 257 million and net profit attributable to controlling shareholders of KRW 396 million.

In Q4 2025, sales rose 15.9% quarter on quarter to KRW 9.19 billion and operating profit reached KRW 1.28 billion, but net loss attributable to controlling shareholders was KRW 2.02 billion, a swing into loss. Details of non-operating items are in the notes to the periodic reports.

Q1 2026 sales of KRW 4.83 billion, down 47.5% quarter on quarter, marked the low point, followed by KRW 5.82 billion in Q2 (up 20.5%) and KRW 8.18 billion in Q3 (up 40.5%), two straight quarterly increases. Q3 2026 sales were up 3.0% year on year.

Over the same period, operating losses were KRW 1.08 billion in Q1, KRW 1.45 billion in Q2 and KRW 884 million in Q3, three consecutive loss-making quarters. The Q3 operating loss narrowed from Q2 but widened from the year-earlier quarter.

Net profit attributable to controlling shareholders was KRW 350 million in Q1 and KRW 773 million in Q2, then a net loss of KRW 3.1 billion in Q3, a swing into loss. The Q3 net loss is larger than the operating loss, so non-operating items played a role, and their breakdown appears in the quarterly report.

The sum of net losses attributable to controlling shareholders over the latest four quarters (Q4 2025 to Q3 2026) is KRW 4 billion.

05

Industry analysis

The apparel and textile sector depends heavily on domestic demand, so it is highly sensitive to consumer sentiment. Commentary in 2026 pointed to high prices and a slowing economy weakening growth in the fashion market.

The competitive picture in fabrics, based on a report more than a decade old, described Italian products dominating high-end domestic fabrics and Chinese products competing on price in the mid- and low-end segments. The same report said Aztech WB held the number-one position in woolen fabrics.

Industrial protective materials are a quality-driven market requiring high-heat, flame-retardant, waterproof and antibacterial functions, and the company is working to build this area into a higher-value product line.

On eco materials, the GRS and RWS certifications cover recycled fiber use and animal welfare standards together. Peers in the same sector include Hansae, Ilshin Spinning, Kyungbang, Pangrim and Hyosung TNC. The company presents vertical integration and its in-house research institute as competitive strengths.

Price competition in the mid- and low-end segment could translate into margin pressure. Whether the two straight quarterly sales increases since Q2 2026 reflect a demand recovery or a temporary rebound will become clearer from upcoming quarterly sales.

06

Outlook

On November 9, 2026, the board approved a 2026–2028 value-up plan. The plan's first goal is to shift the portfolio from apparel fabrics toward higher-value industrial materials and to reorganize it around more profitable products.

It also includes securing cost competitiveness through global outsourcing, with direct overseas imports to expand sales. The company plans to lower costs and raise returns through autonomous factory operations and EMS-based production.

On shareholder returns, the plan resumes cash dividends that have been suspended since the fiscal year 2023 and gradually raises the payout ratio as profitability improves. Treasury share buybacks will be executed with consideration of the share price level and financial conditions.

Progress will be reviewed once a year and disclosed. In February 2026, the company decided to dispose of 501,000 treasury shares to a key raw-material supplier through an off-market block trade.

The company said the purpose was to strengthen its strategic partnership with the supplier and pursue medium- to long-term business cooperation. The company reported that it does not fall under the high-dividend company criteria.

The next checkpoints are whether the dividend restart is approved for the fiscal 2026 dividend and whether the buyback is carried out.

Bull factors

Capital base and financial stability

Total equity was KRW 107.2 billion at the end of 2025 against total liabilities of KRW 11.1 billion. The debt-to-equity ratio fell from 29.1% in 2022 to 10.4% in 2025. Net profit attributable to controlling shareholders turned from a loss to a profit of KRW 929 million in 2025. Operating cash flow rose to KRW 6.33 billion in 2025 from KRW 2.57 billion in 2024.

A shift in shareholder-return policy

The value-up plan disclosed on November 9, 2026 includes resuming cash dividends that have been suspended since the fiscal year 2023. The company said it will gradually raise the payout ratio as profitability improves.

Treasury share buybacks will be carried out with the share price level and financial conditions in mind. Whether the plan is executed while the company remains in a loss position will be shown in future disclosures.

Product mix and cost restructuring

The company holds GRS and RWS certified materials and industrial protective fabrics with high-heat and flame-retardant functions. It plans to reduce costs through autonomous factory operations and EMS-based production.

Global outsourcing through direct overseas imports is also presented as a way to secure cost competitiveness. Whether this direction shows up in results will be visible in the sales mix and operating margin of upcoming periodic reports.

Bear factors

Persistent operating losses and a wider net loss

Operating losses were KRW 1.08 billion in Q1 2026, KRW 1.45 billion in Q2 and KRW 884 million in Q3, three consecutive loss-making quarters. Net loss attributable to controlling shareholders reached KRW 3.1 billion in Q3 2026, a swing into loss.

The four-quarter total of such net losses through Q3 2026 is KRW 4 billion. Two straight quarters of sales growth did not yet translate into earnings recovery.

Three years of falling sales and weak consumption

Commentary pointed to high prices and a slowing economy weakening consumer sentiment and growth in the fashion market. The Q1 2026 deterioration was concentrated in the promotion and fabric segments. A structure that depends on domestic demand could delay a recovery if the consumption slowdown lasts.

Concentrated ownership and limited float

The largest shareholder and related parties reported holding 43.61%. The free float is reported at 56.39%, which limits the number of shares available to trade. The company's small market capitalization can lead to larger share price swings.

No dividend was paid for 2023, and cash returns to shareholders remain limited until a dividend restart is confirmed.

10

Risk factors

Demand and economic risk

If high prices and a slowing economy keep consumer sentiment weak, demand for apparel fabrics and promotional products could fall together. The Q1 2026 deterioration concentrated in these two areas illustrates this exposure. A prolonged slowdown in fashion market growth could extend the sales decline. How far the expansion into industrial materials offsets this trend is the key variable.

Raw material and supplier risk

The company disposed of treasury shares to strengthen its partnership with a key raw-material supplier. Greater reliance on a supplier means changes in procurement terms can quickly affect costs and delivery times. Raw material price and exchange rate swings can also weigh on the cost structure. The pace at which the cost-reduction plan is executed is the main factor in reducing this risk.

Shareholder-return execution risk

The dividend restart and treasury share buybacks are at the plan stage as disclosed on November 9, 2026. With dividends suspended since the fiscal year 2023 and losses continuing, the timing of a restart could slip. The plan calls for an annual review, so delays would surface in disclosures. The gap between the plan and actual execution will serve as the benchmark for judging its credibility.

Overall view

Aztech WB returned to an annual profit in 2025 and its debt-to-equity ratio of 10.4% points to a comparatively stable financial base.

Sales, however, fell from KRW 45.5 billion in 2022 to KRW 30.2 billion in 2025, declining three years in a row, and operating losses have continued for three straight quarters from Q1 to Q3 2026.

Q3 2026 sales rose for a second straight quarter, but an operating loss of KRW 884 million remained and the net loss attributable to controlling shareholders widened to KRW 3.1 billion.

The value-up plan disclosed on November 9, 2026 covers dividend resumption, treasury share buybacks, expansion into industrial materials and cost reduction. Whether the plan is executed will become visible in the annual business report and the regular shareholder meeting in 2027.

The bullish factors are the capital base and the shift toward shareholder returns, while the bearish factors are continued operating losses and exposure to weak consumption. Both sets of factors carry similar weight, and the next key checkpoint is whether operating results move out of loss in upcoming disclosures.
