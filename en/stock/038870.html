02

Business structure

EcoSimplex was founded in 1997 as an ESG specialist firm and listed on KOSDAQ in 2001 as a renewable and environmental energy company. In March 2026 the company changed its name from Eco Bio Holdings to EcoSimplex, positioning AI-based new businesses and expanded hydrogen operations as new growth pillars.

Its core business is the construction and operation of renewable energy infrastructure using landfill gas and biogas, with hydrogen charging station operations emerging as an additional pillar in recent years.

Its flagship site is the Seonam Water Reclamation Center in Magok, Seoul, where it completed the 'ECO Hydrogen Station' in June 2026, a facility that converts biogas generated during sewage treatment into clean hydrogen.

The facility processes roughly 4,000 Nm³ of biogas feedstock per day to produce 500 kg of clean hydrogen daily, supplying it on-site directly to an adjacent hydrogen charging station.

Commercial operation began on May 22, and the company added a 50 kg/h compressor and charger to accommodate large-vehicle demand such as city buses and police buses, enabling two vehicles to be charged consecutively.

The company also signed an agreement with the Seoul city government extending the operating term for the Seonam facility by ten years, securing a longer-term operational foundation.

In terms of competitive positioning, landfill-gas and biogas-based renewable energy businesses depend heavily on public contracts from environmental authorities and local governments, placing the company in competition mainly with other small and mid-sized environmental energy firms performing similar publicly commissioned projects rather than large private energy conglomerates.

04

Earnings analysis

Consolidated revenue in 2025 came to KRW 17.24 billion, down from KRW 18.02 billion in 2024, with an operating loss of KRW 2.66 billion and an operating margin of -15.4%.

The operating loss has persisted for four straight years, moving from KRW 0.15 billion in 2022 to KRW 2.60 billion in 2023, KRW 2.36 billion in 2024, and KRW 2.66 billion in 2025.

Net profit attributable to owners was positive only in 2023 at KRW 2.45 billion, while 2022 (-KRW 4.70 billion), 2024 (-KRW 3.67 billion) and 2025 (-KRW 2.26 billion) were all losses, reflecting an unstable earnings pattern.

Quarterly results show even greater volatility: the company posted a rare operating profit of KRW 0.25 billion on revenue of KRW 4.70 billion in the second quarter of 2025, but swung back to losses in the third quarter (revenue KRW 4.12 billion, operating loss KRW 0.55 billion) and fourth quarter (revenue KRW 4.33 billion, operating loss KRW 1.19 billion), with the fourth-quarter net loss attributable to owners widening to KRW 3.92 billion and driving much of the full-year deterioration.

In the first quarter of 2026, revenue contracted to KRW 2.85 billion with an operating loss of KRW 0.99 billion, a decline attributed in part to the temporary suspension of existing hydrogen charging station operations during construction of the Seonam clean hydrogen production facility.

Revenue recovered to KRW 3.48 billion in the second quarter of 2026 and net profit attributable to owners turned positive at KRW 1.98 billion, but the operating loss persisted (-KRW 0.53 billion), suggesting the profit was driven mainly by non-operating items; the trailing four-quarter sum (Q3 2025 to Q2 2026) for net profit attributable to owners remains negative at -KRW 0.41 billion.

Non-controlling interests also swung from +KRW 1.63 billion in 2024 to -KRW 3.41 billion in 2025, suggesting significant losses concentrated at the subsidiary level.

On the cash flow side, operating cash flow turned positive at KRW 0.14 billion in 2025 but did not fully offset the KRW 2.55 billion outflow in 2024, while the debt ratio rose from 27.2% in 2023 to 46.0% in 2024 and 45.9% in 2025, indicating somewhat higher financial leverage.

05

Industry analysis

South Korea's renewable energy and hydrogen industry is being shaped by the government's hydrogen economy roadmap, which promotes the expansion of clean hydrogen production and supply infrastructure.

Clean hydrogen production using biogas from landfills and sewage treatment facilities is drawing interest from local governments and environmental authorities because it generates fewer carbon emissions than conventional fossil-fuel reforming methods, and similar production hubs are being built simultaneously across the country.

For instance, Chungcheongnam-do province is pursuing a carbon-capture hydrogen production facility in Taean, clean hydrogen production and supply networks are being established in Dangjin and Boryeong, and an electrolysis-based hydrogen production facility in Buan, Jeollabuk-do has been completed with a daily capacity of one ton of clean hydrogen.

With publicly driven, distributed hydrogen production projects spreading nationwide in this manner, EcoSimplex's facility at the Seonam Water Reclamation Center in Magok can be seen as part of this broader policy trend.

However, small biogas-based production facilities have limited processing capacity—ranging from several hundred kilograms to a few tons per day—leaving a gap between the pace of demand growth from expanding charging infrastructure and actual production capacity as a variable.

In terms of competitive structure, landfill-gas and biogas power generation and facility operation businesses rely heavily on public-sector contracts, keeping barriers to new entry relatively moderate, while re-contracting and bidding outcomes with existing operators directly affect results.

In terms of scale, the company is small relative to the average revenue and market capitalization of peers in the KOSDAQ general services sector, making it relatively more sensitive to industry shifts and policy variables.

Outlook

Alongside its name change, the company has signaled a direction toward diversifying its portfolio by adding AI-based new businesses to its existing environmental energy operations, though a detailed business model and revenue contribution timeline have not yet been disclosed externally.

In the near term, stabilizing operations at the ECO Hydrogen Station at the Seonam Water Reclamation Center and expanding charging volumes are seen as key variables.

The facility, which began commercial operation in May 2026, is equipped with large-vehicle charging capability for buses, creating room for demand growth centered on public fleet vehicles, though its actual revenue contribution has not yet been clearly reflected in quarterly results.

The ten-year extension agreement with the Seoul city government is viewed as supporting medium- to long-term operational stability at this site.

The core renewable energy infrastructure construction and maintenance business is likely to maintain a quarter-to-quarter volatility pattern tied to public contract schedules, suggesting the choppy quarterly performance seen in the past could persist for some time.

How the government's clean hydrogen certification and subsidy policies take shape, and whether the company can secure a differentiated role amid competing production hubs being built nationwide, are medium-term points to watch.

Whether the general-investment stake recently disclosed by SYT Co., Ltd. evolves into greater management involvement or a larger holding is also worth monitoring.

Bull factors

Clean Hydrogen Facility Completion Brings New Business Into View

The ECO Hydrogen Station at the Seonam Water Reclamation Center in Magok, Seoul was completed in June 2026 and began commercial operation in May, establishing a daily clean hydrogen production capacity of 500 kg.

The addition of compressors and chargers for large vehicles has also positioned the company to serve demand from public fleets such as city buses and police buses. A ten-year extension agreement with the Seoul city government supports long-term operational stability at the site.

Cost Reductions Narrowing Losses

Despite declining revenue and margins, analysis has pointed to a narrowing of operating losses through cost reductions. Operating cash flow turned positive at KRW 0.14 billion in 2025, reversing the cash outflow seen in 2024.

Whether this restructuring translates into a sustained improvement in the balance sheet will need to be confirmed through further quarterly results.

Alignment With Policy Themes and New Investor Interest

Amid the government's push to expand clean hydrogen production and supply infrastructure under its hydrogen economy roadmap, the company's Magok facility aligns with this policy theme.

In October 2026, SYT Co., Ltd. disclosed a new 5.00% stake, stating the purpose as general investment, which can be read as one sign of a diversifying investor base.

Bear factors

Operating Losses Persisting for a Fourth Straight Year

Operating losses have persisted and widened, from KRW 0.15 billion in 2022 to KRW 2.60 billion in 2023, KRW 2.36 billion in 2024, and KRW 2.66 billion in 2025. The operating margin stood at -15.4% in 2025, showing no clear sign of profitability recovery. This prolonged loss trend could also constrain the company's capacity to invest in new businesses.

Revenue Volatility and Impact of Hydrogen Station Shutdown

Revenue in the first quarter of 2026 fell sharply year-on-year to KRW 2.85 billion, a decline attributed in part to the temporary suspension of existing hydrogen charging station operations during construction of the Seonam clean hydrogen facility.

Quarterly revenue has swung widely between roughly KRW 2.8 billion and KRW 4.7 billion, making earnings difficult to predict. Whether revenue stabilizes and recovers following the new facility's launch has not yet been confirmed.

Net Profit Dependent on Non-Operating Items

In both the third quarter of 2025 and the second quarter of 2026, the company recorded operating losses yet posted positive net profit attributable to owners of KRW 1.89 billion and KRW 1.98 billion respectively, a pattern attributed to swings in non-operating items.

As a result, the trailing four-quarter net profit attributable to owners remains negative at -KRW 0.41 billion. Non-controlling interests also turned negative at -KRW 3.41 billion in 2025, pointing to a growing loss burden at the subsidiary level.

Risk factors

Business Concentration Risk

A significant portion of revenue is concentrated in the operation and maintenance of landfill-gas and biogas-based renewable energy infrastructure, making results highly dependent on public-sector contract schedules and re-contracting outcomes. This concentration is likely to persist until the new hydrogen business gains meaningful scale.

New Business Uncertainty

The AI-based new business presented alongside the name change has not yet had its specific business model, investment scale, or revenue contribution timeline disclosed in detail.

The clean hydrogen production facility is also still in an early operational stage, and a gap could emerge between processing capacity and actual revenue contribution.

Capital Structure and Equity Volatility

The debt ratio rose from 27.2% in 2023 to 45.9% in 2025, and non-controlling interests turned negative at -KRW 3.41 billion in 2025, reflecting growing volatility in the capital structure. If accumulated losses continue to erode retained earnings, this could affect future financing conditions.

Overall view

EcoSimplex is pursuing a business transition by combining its existing environmental energy operations with a newly operational clean hydrogen production facility and a stated AI-based new business initiative.

However, operating losses have persisted from 2022 through 2025, and the 2025 operating margin of -15.4% does not yet show a clear profitability turning point. Quarterly results have swung between profit and loss depending on non-operating items, leaving even the trailing four-quarter sum in a modest loss position.

The ECO Hydrogen Station at the Seonam Water Reclamation Center in Magok, completed in June 2026, is still in an early operating stage, and how much it will ultimately contribute to revenue and profitability will require verification through further quarterly results.

The disclosure of a new shareholder stake in October 2026 also points to shifts in the investor base, warranting continued attention to ownership composition and any potential management involvement.

Overall, the direction of the business transition has been outlined, but the company appears to be in a transitional period that will need more time before it translates into financial performance.
