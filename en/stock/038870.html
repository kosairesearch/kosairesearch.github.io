02

Business structure

EcoSimplex is a renewable-energy company founded in 1997 that runs biogas-based electricity, gas and hydrogen production as well as a bio-sulfur business. On March 26, 2026 it changed its name from Ecobio Holdings to EcoSimplex and added AI-based and hydrogen-related businesses to its stated purposes.

Renewable-energy infrastructure construction and maintenance accounted for about 95% of revenue as of March 2026, while renewable energy operations (electricity, hydrogen and gas) accounted for about 4%.

The infrastructure segment is described as covering power generation and ancillary facility operation and management at the Sudokwon Landfill.

A clean hydrogen production and refueling business using biogas from the Seoul Seonam water reclamation center continues under an agreement with the City of Seoul that extends the existing project period by ten years.

The Magok hydrogen station uses roughly 4,000 Nm3 of biogas per day to produce 500 kg of hydrogen per day, and the company describes it as the first on-site case among government-backed projects to enter commercial operation. The company had two unlisted consolidated subsidiaries as of September 30, 2026.

Hydrogen expansion depends on whether government competitive grants are awarded, while infrastructure service revenue depends on the size of operating contracts for existing facilities.

04

Earnings analysis

Annual revenue went from KRW 16.8 billion in 2022 to KRW 15.8 billion in 2023 (down 5.9% year on year), then rose to KRW 18 billion in 2024 (up 14.1%), the largest year of 2022 to 2025, before falling to KRW 17.2 billion in 2025 (down 4.3%).

The operating loss was KRW 147 million in 2022, KRW 2.6 billion in 2023, KRW 2.36 billion in 2024 and KRW 2.66 billion in 2025, and the filings record four consecutive years of losses from 2022 through 2025.

The operating margin was -0.9% in 2022, -16.5% in 2023, -13.1% in 2024 and -15.4% in 2025, so the loss structure persisted even in 2024, the year of highest revenue.

Net profit attributable to controlling shareholders turned to a KRW 2.45 billion profit in 2023, swung to a KRW 3.67 billion loss in 2024, and narrowed to a KRW 2.26 billion loss in 2025.

Net loss including non-controlling interests was KRW 7.3 billion in 2025, well above the controlling-shareholder loss, which points to a larger loss burden at the non-controlling level.

Operating cash flow was KRW 4.88 billion in 2022 and KRW 1.5 billion in 2023, turned to a KRW 2.55 billion outflow in 2024, and recovered to KRW 136 million in 2025.

Quarterly results show an operating loss of KRW 548 million on KRW 4.12 billion of revenue in Q3 2025, widening to KRW 1.19 billion on KRW 4.33 billion of revenue in Q4 2025.

Revenue fell to KRW 2.85 billion in Q1 2026 (down 34.3% from the prior quarter), while the operating loss narrowed to KRW 986 million; in Q2 2026, revenue was KRW 3.48 billion, the operating loss KRW 531 million, and controlling-shareholder net profit KRW 1.98 billion, a swing to profit.

In Q3 2026, revenue was KRW 3.4 billion (down 17.6% year on year), the operating loss KRW 583 million (wider than a year earlier), and the controlling-shareholder net loss KRW 15.13 million, a swing from profit a year earlier. The controlling-shareholder net loss totaled KRW 2.32 billion over the last four quarters.

Operating results and controlling-shareholder net income moved in opposite directions across quarters, so the role of one-off items should be checked in the quarterly report notes.

05

Industry analysis

EcoSimplex's end demand comes from operation and maintenance of aging environmental infrastructure and renewable-energy facilities, plus investment in biogas-based clean hydrogen infrastructure.

In its annual report the company cites a facility maintenance market of about KRW 2 trillion or more in 2023 and 9,432 facilities aged 31 years or older in 2024. The company argues that facility aging supports steady maintenance demand.

On the other side, most revenue comes from construction and maintenance services, so revenue falls together with new construction volume, and the decline in Q1 2026 revenue was reported mainly as lower renewable-energy infrastructure construction sales.

The hydrogen segment is being expanded under the government's hydrogen economy roadmap, and the 2024 biogas-based clean hydrogen production facility program was carried out as a private-sector support project through a government competition.

Hydrogen expansion speed therefore depends heavily on policy selection and permitting schedules. In terms of cycle position, hydrogen infrastructure is at an early installation stage with a small revenue contribution, while infrastructure services rely on comparatively mature demand.

On competitive positioning, the company emphasizes that it has secured a commercial operation case in clean hydrogen as a differentiator. Market share data versus peers was not disclosed in figures, so the comparison rests on business structure differences.

A revenue base concentrated in services means profit swings can widen when competitive awards or operating contracts change.

06

Outlook

The most concrete schedule the company has disclosed concerns the Seonam clean hydrogen production base. In materials covering Q1 2026, the base was described as scheduled for completion in June 2026, and the company said it is expanding its clean hydrogen business under the government's hydrogen economy roadmap.

At the same time, the Seonam hydrogen refueling station was reported as temporarily suspended because of construction of the production base. Whether refueling operations resumed after completion needs to be confirmed from recent filings.

The Magok bio-gas hydrogen station began commercial operation in June 2026, and the produced hydrogen is described as supplied to route buses, police buses and hydrogen passenger cars.

Whether hydrogen sales volume grows enough to diversify a revenue base concentrated in infrastructure services is the next point to watch.

In November 2025 the company decided to invest in a biogas-based clean hydrogen production facility in Yeongcheon, Gyeongbuk, and the investment size and start-up timing should be confirmed through periodic reports and ad hoc disclosures.

On the earnings side, the key variable is whether new hydrogen revenue offsets the decline in infrastructure sales, given that Q3 2026 revenue fell year on year.

The price-to-book ratio (PBR), a balance-sheet measure, sits well below 1x, which means the share price is below the book value of controlling shareholders' equity.

Controlling shareholders' equity was KRW 77.2 billion at the end of 2025, and a market capitalization below that book value is a factual comparison rather than a judgment. Total equity was KRW 73.8 billion at the end of 2025, and the debt ratio of 45.9% was close to the 46.0% recorded in 2024.

The declining equity trend and the widening non-controlling loss raise the question of how far net asset value can be converted into business results. A share price that trades at a low multiple of net assets reflects the market's view on whether losses persist and whether profitability can recover.

If earnings recover, the basis for multiple comparisons changes, so during loss periods the balance-sheet measures and the operating-margin trend are the reference points.

08

Bull factors

Q2 2026 swing to profit and narrower losses

Controlling-shareholder net profit was KRW 1.98 billion in Q2 2026, a swing to profit from the prior quarter, and the operating loss narrowed to KRW 531 million. Revenue that quarter was KRW 3.48 billion, up 22.2% from the prior quarter.

The narrower quarterly loss can be read as an early sign of business normalization. However, the operating loss widened again to KRW 583 million in Q3 2026, so the trend needs more quarters to confirm.

Commercial hydrogen operation secured

The Magok hydrogen station entered commercial operation in June 2026, and the company described it as the first on-site case among government competition projects to begin commercial operation. It produces 500 kg of hydrogen per day from roughly 4,000 Nm3 of biogas.

The company also agreed with the City of Seoul to extend the existing project period for the Seonam clean hydrogen business by ten years. Growth in hydrogen sales could reduce the concentration of revenue in infrastructure services.

Stable balance sheet and asset base

The debt ratio was 45.9% at the end of 2025, close to the 46.0% of 2024, with total equity of KRW 73.8 billion and controlling shareholders' equity of KRW 77.2 billion. The price-to-book ratio sits well below 1x, so market valuation is low relative to book asset value.

For the bull case, the key premise is whether that asset base translates into business results. The absence of a sharp rise in liabilities can be read as a basis for capacity to fund new hydrogen investment.

Bear factors

Five straight quarters of operating losses

The company has recorded operating losses for five straight quarters from Q3 2025 through Q3 2026. The Q3 2026 operating loss of KRW 583 million was wider than a year earlier. Controlling-shareholder net loss of KRW 15.13 million in the same quarter was a swing from a profit a year earlier.

On an annual basis, the filings record four consecutive years of losses from 2022 to 2025, with a 2025 operating margin of -15.4%.

Continued revenue decline

Q3 2026 revenue was KRW 3.4 billion, down 17.6% year on year. Q1 2026 revenue also fell 30.3% year on year, and the decline in renewable-energy infrastructure construction sales was reported as the main cause. Annual revenue in 2025 fell 4.3% from the prior year. If operating costs cannot be reduced as the revenue base shrinks, the loss could widen.

Shrinking equity and thin cash generation

Total equity was KRW 73.8 billion at the end of 2025. The 2025 net loss including non-controlling interests was KRW 7.3 billion, well above the controlling-shareholder loss of KRW 2.26 billion, and the non-controlling loss widened from 2024.

Operating cash flow turned to a KRW 2.55 billion outflow in 2024 and was only KRW 136 million in 2025. If losses persist, funding new hydrogen investment from internal cash becomes more difficult.

Risk factors

Business concentration and policy dependence

About 95% of revenue sits in the renewable-energy infrastructure segment, so construction volume and changes in operating contracts directly affect results. New businesses such as hydrogen and bio-sulfur depend on government competitions and permits, so delays in policy schedules could slow expansion. A shift in policy priorities could also lengthen the payback period on investment.

Facility operation risk

The Seonam hydrogen refueling station was reported as temporarily suspended due to installation of the production base. Hydrogen segment revenue could vary sharply depending on when operations resume and on production volumes.

As a facility-based business, output can also be affected by changes in the quantity and quality of biogas feedstock.

Financial and non-controlling interest burden

Because the net loss including non-controlling interests is larger than the controlling-shareholder share, the non-controlling loss burden is significant, and consolidated results should be read by separating the two shares. Total equity declined to KRW 73.8 billion at the end of 2025.

12

Overall view

EcoSimplex's 2025 results were revenue of KRW 17.2 billion, an operating loss of KRW 2.66 billion and a net loss of KRW 7.3 billion including non-controlling interests, and the filings record losses in each year from 2022 through 2025.

In Q2 2026 controlling-shareholder net income swung to a profit and the operating loss narrowed, but in Q3 2026 the operating loss widened again, extending a run of five straight quarterly operating losses.

Renewable-energy infrastructure still accounts for about 95% of the business, while clean hydrogen is expanding around the Magok commercial station and the Seonam production base.

Bullish factors are the secured commercial hydrogen case, the Q2 2026 profit swing, and a balance sheet with total equity of KRW 73.8 billion and a price-to-book ratio below 1x. Bearish factors are falling revenue, continued operating losses, a widening non-controlling loss and low operating cash flow.

The pace at which these factors show up in results remains limited, and whether infrastructure sales recover and hydrogen sales volume grows is the turning point for the next assessment. The next checkpoints are the fiscal 2026 annual report, due by late March 2027, and the Q1 2027 quarterly report. This report does not offer an investment opinion or a target price.
