02

Business structure

Studio Dragon is a subsidiary of CJ ENM and is regarded as the largest drama studio in Korea. The company produces dramas and supplies them to broadcasters and OTT platforms, generating domestic revenue through domestic broadcast scheduling and overseas revenue through sales to overseas OTT and platforms.

According to company announcements, 2025 domestic revenue was KRW 195.2 billion, down 13.4% year on year as no hit titles were aired, while overseas revenue rose 3.3% to KRW 335.5 billion, supported by library titles supplied to Tving's overseas brand hub. Overseas revenue is now well above domestic revenue.

Total production in 2025 was 230 episodes, a slight increase from the prior year. Its buyers span domestic broadcasters such as tvN, SBS and MBC and OTT platforms such as Tving, Netflix and Disney+.

Content sales revenue in the second quarter of 2026 was KRW 126.9 billion, up 37.3% year on year, and TV and OTT episodes aired in that quarter totaled 77, 36 more than a year earlier. The company holds 278 intellectual property titles, with Queen of Tears, Crash Landing on You, Mr.

Sunshine and Goblin cited as representative works. The company is expanding commerce businesses built on its own IP. Because revenue is generated title by title through broadcaster scheduling and OTT purchases, the hit rate of individual titles widens quarterly earnings swings.

04

Earnings analysis

Studio Dragon's annual results have declined since 2022. Revenue went from KRW 697.9 billion in 2022 to KRW 753.1 billion in 2023, up 7.9%, then fell to KRW 550.1 billion in 2024, down 27.0%, and to KRW 530.7 billion in 2025, down 3.5%, a two-year decline from 2024 through 2025.

Operating profit was KRW 65.2 billion in 2022, KRW 55.9 billion in 2023, KRW 36.4 billion in 2024 and KRW 30.4 billion in 2025, a three-year decline from 2023 to 2025; 2022 was the largest year and 2025 the smallest within 2022 to 2025. The operating margin fell from 9.3% and 7.4% to 6.6% and then 5.7% in 2025.

Net profit attributable to controlling shareholders went from KRW 50.6 billion in 2022 to KRW 30.1 billion in 2023, down 40.5%, recovered to KRW 33.5 billion in 2024, up 11.3%, and dropped to KRW 10.3 billion in 2025, down 69.2%.

Net profit including non-controlling interests is reported separately at the same annual figures. Operating cash flow went from -KRW 62.9 billion in 2022 to KRW 44.7 billion in 2023 and KRW 179.6 billion in 2024, then turned negative at -KRW 9.81 billion in 2025.

Quarterly, operating profit of KRW 18.5 billion in the fourth quarter of 2025, up 77.0% from the prior quarter, marked the starting point of the rebound.

First-quarter 2026 operating profit was KRW 6.41 billion, down 65.4% from the prior quarter, which the company attributed to the absence of large tentpole titles and reduced global OTT pre-sales for TV titles.

The second quarter of 2026 returned to operating profit of KRW 15.4 billion, with an operating margin of 10.6%, back in double digits. Third-quarter 2026 revenue was KRW 159 billion, up 16.5% year on year, and operating profit was KRW 15.1 billion, up 44.4%.

However, net profit attributable to controlling shareholders was KRW 4.53 billion, down 49.1%, so the operating gain did not carry through to net profit.

Net profit attributable to controlling shareholders over the last four quarters, from the fourth quarter of 2025 to the third quarter of 2026, totaled KRW 26.9 billion.

05

Industry analysis

In its second-quarter 2026 results announcement, the company acknowledged that the drama industry as a whole is in a weak cycle and said it would respond by expanding its domestic and overseas supply chain, raising drama hit rates, deepening IP businesses and improving production cost efficiency.

Scheduling demand comes from domestic broadcasters and OTT platforms, and brokerage reports treat production volume as a core growth indicator. According to one brokerage report, production volume fell from 33 titles in 2022, the peak, to 18 in 2024, the low, and rose slightly to 19 in 2025.

A turn in the cycle depends on whether broadcaster scheduling volume recovers. One brokerage outlook for 2026 expects the Wednesday-Thursday drama slot on parent CJ ENM's tvN to resume and outsourced production for terrestrial and other broadcasters to expand.

On competition, its status as Korea's largest drama studio and its 278 IP titles are cited as strengths. Because overseas sales drive a large part of profit, the purchasing demand of global OTT platforms determines the company's selling prices.

Third-quarter 2026 episodes aired totaled 60, down 16.7% from 72 a year earlier, yet revenue and operating profit both grew at double-digit rates as overseas sales expanded.

06

Outlook

The direction the company outlined in its second-quarter 2026 results is production cost efficiency, AI use to improve production processes, and expanding commerce businesses based on its own IP.

Revenue for January to September 2026 totaled KRW 459.55 billion, up 19.4% year on year (provisional, disclosed November 5, 2026).

On scheduling, Eugene Investment & Securities said in its November 6, 2026 report that third-quarter results were in line with market consensus and that the key issue is scheduling recovery after the fourth quarter.

The same report cited full-episode delivery of Netflix original Slow and Steady, Strong as a driver of third-quarter overseas revenue. A September 2026 report introduced tvN's Choi Aer's Employee, Foreign Hands, 100 Days My Prince, and Gift, along with Netflix's Slow and Steady, Strong and SBS's Dr.

X: The White Mafia Era, as titles set to air in sequence.

The strategy of widening supply to terrestrial broadcasters and OTT platforms became visible from the second quarter of 2026, when SBS's Splendid New World and MBC's Fifty Percent each drew ratings above 10%, and Tving's Yumi's Cells Season 3 contributed to paid subscriber gains.

Supply to Netflix through a subsidiary also continued. Whether these hits coincide with a scheduling recovery is the variable that will shape earnings direction after 2027.

No quantitative company target was identified in the available material, so the earnings direction is best read through scheduling volume and the overseas sales mix.

Bull factors

Overseas sales lift profit

Overseas revenue in the third quarter of 2026 was KRW 132.4 billion, up 35% year on year and about 83.3% of total revenue. Content sales in the second quarter of 2026 rose 37.3% year on year to KRW 126.9 billion.

Library titles supplied through Tving's overseas brand hub, Disney+ Japan and HBO Max in Southeast Asia are adding revenue.

Profit grew even as episode count fell

Third-quarter 2026 episodes aired totaled 60, down 16.7% from 72 a year earlier, yet revenue and operating profit both grew at double-digit rates. Operating profit was KRW 15.1 billion, up 44.4%.

The company said the second-quarter 2026 operating margin of 10.6% returned to double digits after 12.7% in the fourth quarter of 2025.

Scheduling recovery and channel diversification expected

In a 2026 earnings outlook report, Kiwoom Securities identified recovery in production volume, measured by scheduled episodes, as the most visible driver of top-line growth. It also cited the return of the Wednesday-Thursday drama slot and expanded outsourced production for terrestrial broadcasters.

Samsung Securities projected in its 2026 report that operating profit would turn around on more titles and wider distribution channels.

Bear factors

Multi-year profit decline and thinner margins

Operating profit fell from KRW 65.2 billion in 2022 to KRW 30.4 billion in 2025, a three-year decline from 2023 to 2025. The operating margin dropped from 9.3% to 5.7%. Net profit attributable to controlling shareholders declined from KRW 50.6 billion in 2022 to KRW 10.3 billion in 2025. Total equity at year-end 2025 was KRW 800.3 billion, while earnings have not kept pace with that base.

Quarterly swings and reliance on hit titles

First-quarter 2026 operating profit was KRW 6.41 billion, down 65.4% from the prior quarter. The company attributed the drop to the absence of large tentpole titles and reduced global OTT pre-sales for TV titles. Domestic revenue fell 13.4% in 2025 as no hit titles aired. Title performance flows directly into quarterly results.

Operating gains not reaching net profit

Third-quarter 2026 operating profit rose 44.4% year on year, but net profit attributable to controlling shareholders fell 49.1% to KRW 4.53 billion. Episodes aired in the same quarter also declined from a year earlier, which limits volume expansion.

The company itself said the weak drama industry cycle must be overcome. Production volume fell from 33 titles in 2022 to 18 in 2024, so the supply base is still in recovery.

Risk factors

Scheduling and platform concentration

Revenue has widened beyond CJ ENM channels to terrestrial broadcasters and OTT platforms, but if scheduling terms or pre-sale conditions change, episode volume and revenue could move together. In the first quarter of 2026, a reduction in global OTT pre-sales fed directly into lower operating profit. With smaller supply volumes, title performance magnifies earnings swings.

Dependence on overseas sales

Overseas revenue accounted for about 83.3% of total revenue in the third quarter of 2026, so results lean more on overseas OTT sales than on domestic broadcast scheduling. Changes in platform contract terms or overseas purchasing demand could quickly reduce sales revenue. Library supply adds revenue, but how long repeat sales last varies by title.

Net profit volatility and production cost control

Net profit attributable to controlling shareholders fell 69.2% to KRW 10.3 billion in 2025 and declined 49.1% year on year in the third quarter of 2026. Quarters in which operating profit and net profit moved in opposite directions show that the link between them changes from quarter to quarter.

The company is pursuing production cost efficiency and AI adoption, so how much of that shows up in title-level margins is a key variable.

Overall view

Studio Dragon's disclosed results show operating profit and margin declining from 2022 and reaching a low in 2025. In 2026, operating profit went from KRW 6.41 billion in the first quarter to a KRW 15.4 billion return to profit in the second quarter and KRW 15.1 billion in the third quarter, up 44.4% year on year.

The drivers of the recovery are expanded overseas sales and high sales revenue per episode, while domestic scheduling recovery remains a task after the fourth quarter of 2026.

On the other hand, net profit attributable to controlling shareholders fell 49.1% to KRW 4.53 billion in the third quarter of 2026, so the operating gain did not carry through to net profit. Net profit attributable to controlling shareholders over the last four quarters totaled KRW 26.9 billion.

Strengths include a larger overseas sales mix, a return to double-digit operating margins and expectations for a recovery in scheduled episodes, while weaknesses include three consecutive years of lower operating profit, a weak industry cycle and reliance on scheduling and overseas OTT sales.

Eugene Investment & Securities said in its November 6, 2026 report that the key issue is scheduling recovery after the fourth quarter, and Kiwoom Securities pointed to a recovery in production volume as the most visible change.

The next checkpoints are provisional fourth-quarter 2026 results, expected in early February 2027, and the 2026 annual report, due by March 31, 2027.
