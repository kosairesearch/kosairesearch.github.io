02

Business structure

i-Scream Media is an edtech company founded in 2002 that listed on KOSDAQ in August 2024. Its business is divided into educational publishing, commerce, training and other, and a 2025 press report put the first-half 2025 revenue mix at 60.94% educational publishing, 32.6% commerce, 4.06% training and 2.4% other.

In the first half of 2026, educational publishing sales were KRW 59.8 billion and commerce sales were KRW 29.1 billion. Educational publishing centers on supplying approved elementary textbooks, and the company entered the approved-textbook market drawing on its experience operating the Ice Cream S platform since 2022.

A 2025 analysis said the company held the top share in elementary math, social studies, science and music approved textbooks in 2024. Commerce is run by the Ice Cream Mall, an education-goods e-commerce site, and the company has stressed a profit-focused product lineup and a larger share of its own-brand products.

Ice Cream S is currently free of charge, so its direct revenue is small; its value lies in the teacher user base that supports textbook, advertising and training businesses. The training business is led by its remote teacher-training academy, and the teacher-training association counted 15 remote academies in 2025.

The main competitive variable in training is the capital needed to keep developing new content. New businesses include the AI platform issamGPT for schools, KinderBoard for early-childhood teachers and the global AI lesson platform LumiTeach. Revenue from these new businesses does not appear as a separate line in the segment disclosures.

04

Earnings analysis

On a DART-filed basis, annual revenue rose from KRW 123.1 billion in 2023 to KRW 152.2 billion in 2024 (up 23.7% year on year) and KRW 195.9 billion in 2025 (up 28.7%).

Operating profit went from KRW 34 billion in 2023 to KRW 46.1 billion in 2024 (up 35.4%) and KRW 61.8 billion in 2025 (up 34.0%), rising two years in a row from 2024 through 2025, while operating margin moved from 27.7% to 30.3% and then 31.5%.

Profit attributable to controlling shareholders was KRW 30.2 billion in 2023, KRW 31.1 billion in 2024 (up 2.8%) and KRW 49.2 billion in 2025 (up 58.2%), and net profit including non-controlling interests was reported at the same amounts for each year.

In 2024 operating profit grew 35.4% but controlling-shareholder profit grew only 2.8%, so the earnings improvement became clearer in 2025.

The company attributed 2025 growth to expanded supply of the 22-revision grade 5-6 textbooks after approval, higher unit prices, platform advertising income, Tinkerbell sales, Ice Cream Mall sales and training-academy contract revenue.

Operating cash flow rose from KRW 30.1 billion in 2023 to KRW 40 billion in 2024 and KRW 57.3 billion in 2025.

At the end of 2025, total equity was KRW 204.4 billion, all of it attributable to controlling shareholders, total liabilities were KRW 62.4 billion, and the debt ratio was 37.0%, 21.6% and 30.5% for 2023 to 2025. Quarterly results are highly seasonal.

After an operating loss of KRW 8.07 billion in Q3 2025, Q4 2025 posted operating profit of KRW 55.3 billion, which accounted for most of the KRW 61.8 billion annual operating profit.

Q1 2026 revenue was KRW 15.3 billion with an operating loss of KRW 8.43 billion, while Q2 2026 revenue was KRW 77.2 billion with operating profit of KRW 32.3 billion.

Q3 2026 revenue was KRW 20.7 billion, up 30.7% year on year, but the company still posted an operating loss of KRW 4.95 billion, narrower than the KRW 8.07 billion loss a year earlier.

Its controlling-shareholder net loss widened to KRW 14.3 billion from KRW 4.05 billion a year earlier, so operating and net results moved in different directions. The gap appears to reflect non-operating items and taxes, and the detailed causes need confirmation from further filings. Net profit attributable to controlling shareholders over the last four quarters totaled KRW 61.8 billion.

05

Industry analysis

Educational publishing is a market where supply and unit prices move together with the curriculum revision cycle. In a 2025 report, Yuanta Securities said the price of 22-revision print textbooks rose by an average of more than 30% versus the 15-revision series, with differences by subject.

The same report noted that i-Scream Media entered the 22-revision textbook market later than rivals yet holds the top share in elementary grades 3-4 math, social studies and science print textbooks. Textbook volumes are set by school adoption results, so changes in adoption rates feed directly into revenue.

A 2025 press report put the Ice Cream Mall's share of the education-goods e-commerce market at 91%. In remote teacher training, the association counted 15 remote academies, and an analysis cited the capital needed for new content development as the key competitive factor.

With the adoption of digital textbooks made voluntary, government digital education policy has been cited as a driver of the share price. In March 2026, Yuanta Securities analyzed that new elementary textbook introductions and AI capability building would keep growth going in 2026.

A 2025 press report cited an average operating margin of about 3.5% for KOSDAQ-listed companies, a benchmark only valid as of that report date.

06

Outlook

In its first-half 2026 results announcement, the company said it would fully launch AI education services and new businesses in the second half to raise both growth and profitability.

Its main AI products are issamGPT, a classroom-focused AI platform, KinderBoard, a work-support service for early-childhood teachers, and LumiTeach, a global AI lesson platform. In senior care, the company plans to expand into care settings based on field-test results from its cognitive art program Geurim Yakbang.

On shareholder returns, it set a policy of paying 40% of net profit as cash dividends for the three years from 2026 through 2028, and in August 2026 it decided on an interim dividend of KRW 13.8 billion and a KRW 5 billion treasury share purchase trust, with 70% of the repurchased shares scheduled for cancellation.

Separately, a 2025 analysis noted that AI digital textbook development costs booked in selling and administrative expenses could lower operating margin slightly.

Of cumulative first-half 2026 revenue of KRW 92.5 billion, educational publishing accounted for KRW 59.8 billion and commerce for KRW 29.1 billion, so textbook supply timing and the durability of commerce sales are the largest swing factors.

Full-year 2026 results will be fixed in the 2026 annual report, which will also show the direction of operating profit in Q4 2026. When the new businesses start appearing as disclosed revenue items is a point still to be confirmed.

08

Bull factors

Earnings growth driven by textbooks

Operating profit rose from KRW 34 billion in 2023 to KRW 61.8 billion in 2025, and operating margin reached 31.5%. The company credited 2025 growth to expanded supply of the 22-revision grade 5-6 textbooks after approval and higher unit prices.

First-half 2026 operating profit was KRW 23.9 billion, up 64.5% from a year earlier. Yuanta Securities described 2025 results as record-high in a March 2026 report.

Expanding shareholder return policy

The company set a policy of paying out 40% of net profit as cash dividends over the three years from 2026 through 2028. In August 2026 it decided on an interim dividend totaling KRW 13.8 billion.

It also agreed to a KRW 5 billion treasury share purchase trust, with 70% of the repurchased shares scheduled for cancellation.

Commerce and AI businesses expanding

The commerce segment recorded KRW 29.1 billion in sales in the first half of 2026, and the company cited a profit-focused product lineup and a higher share of own-brand products as growth drivers.

It is building AI education services under issamGPT, KinderBoard and LumiTeach, and plans to widen its senior care business based on field-test results from Geurim Yakbang. The overlap between teacher-facing services and the textbook customer base is also cited as a basis for cross-selling potential.

09

Bear factors

Volatile quarterly earnings

Operating losses of KRW 8.43 billion in Q1 2026 and KRW 4.95 billion in Q3 2026 show that quarterly losses have recurred. The controlling-shareholder net loss in Q3 2026 was KRW 14.3 billion, larger than the KRW 4.05 billion loss in Q3 2025.

Q4 2025 operating profit of KRW 55.3 billion made up most of the KRW 61.8 billion annual figure, so profit is concentrated in particular quarters. A 2025 report identified seasonality with revenue and profit concentrated in the second quarter.

Dependence on textbook policy and adoption

Of first-half 2026 revenue of KRW 92.5 billion, educational publishing accounted for KRW 59.8 billion. Textbook demand depends on approval outcomes, adoption rates and choices made by schools.

With digital textbook adoption made voluntary, a change in government policy direction could challenge the assumptions behind the business, which is cited as a bearish factor.

Cost and uncertainty of new business investment

Revenue from AI education services and the new businesses does not appear as a separate line in the segment disclosures. A 2025 analysis noted that AI digital textbook development costs booked in selling and administrative expenses could slightly reduce operating margin. If costs rise before revenue contributions follow, the pace of margin improvement could slow.

10

Risk factors

Policy and regulation

Textbook approval results, adoption rates and the curriculum revision cycle are variables the company cannot control. As digital textbook policy has become voluntary, demand structure could also shift. Policy changes flow directly into educational publishing revenue, so the two move together.

Seasonality and non-operating swings

A 2025 report identified seasonality with revenue and profit concentrated in the second quarter. In Q3 2026 the operating loss narrowed from a year earlier, yet the controlling-shareholder net loss widened, suggesting that non-operating items and taxes weighed heavily on the result.

The larger the gap between quarterly operating and net results, the harder it becomes to read the trend from any single quarter.

New business investment and execution

Expanding AI services and the global platform LumiTeach requires development and marketing spending. A 2025 analysis pointed out that AI digital textbook development costs could be booked in selling and administrative expenses. Costs could be recognized before revenue from these businesses grows, which is the risk.

12

Overall view

The DART-filed results show revenue, operating profit and controlling-shareholder net profit all rising from 2023 through 2025. Operating margin climbed from 27.7% to 31.5%, and operating cash flow reached KRW 57.3 billion in 2025.

In the first half of 2026, growth in educational publishing and commerce lifted operating profit to KRW 23.9 billion. Quarterly results, however, alternated between profit and loss, and Q3 2026 still recorded an operating loss of KRW 4.95 billion and a controlling-shareholder net loss of KRW 14.3 billion.

On the bullish side, textbook price increases and an established adoption base, the shareholder return policy and room for AI businesses to expand are cited. On the bearish side, reliance on textbook policy and adoption, quarterly seasonality and the cost of new businesses are cited.

Brokerage views on valuation differ, and the dividend yield sits well above typical levels across industries. The next checkpoints are the full-year results in the 2026 annual report, the Q1 2027 quarterly report, and whether the treasury share cancellation is carried out.
