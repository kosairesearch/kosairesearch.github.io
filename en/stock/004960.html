02

Business structure

Hanshin Construction is built around contracting for buildings, civil works and in-house projects, with weight placed on public-sector orders and urban redevelopment.

According to the first-quarter 2026 quarterly report, the 2025 annual revenue mix (the 59th term) was 43.71% domestic building contracts, 25.07% domestic civil works and 26.99% in-house projects.

In the first quarter of 2026 the mix was 59.77% building, 19.57% civil works and 14.34% in-house projects, down from a 33.30% in-house share in the first quarter of 2025.

Press coverage attributed the drop in in-house revenue to the completion of large in-house sites such as Pohang Pentacity Hanshin The Hue and Asan Hanshin The Hue.

Contract backlog at the end of the first quarter of 2026 was KRW 7.1962 trillion, split into public KRW 2.0725 trillion, private KRW 4.6692 trillion and in-house KRW 454.5 billion.

At the end of 2025 the backlog was KRW 7.397 trillion, made up of KRW 5.4401 trillion of building, KRW 1.4662 trillion of civil works and KRW 490.7 billion of in-house projects.

Public projects highlighted in coverage include the GTX-B Section 2 works, the Gangnam deep-tunnel drainage tunnel, the Janghang line double-tracking and the Incheon Gyeyang Techno Valley development.

Urban redevelopment orders exceeded KRW 1 trillion in 2023 and came close to that level in 2024, and in February 2026 the company won the Hoewon 2 redevelopment in Changwon worth KRW 603.9 billion.

Customers include public agencies, redevelopment associations and private developers, and the company describes its approach as selective bidding in public and redevelopment work rather than head-on competition with large builders. Overseas civil works accounted for 1.69% of 2025 revenue, a small share.

04

Earnings analysis

Based on disclosed annual results, revenue rose from KRW 1.22 trillion in 2022 to KRW 1.31 trillion in 2023 (up 7.2%) and KRW 1.49 trillion in 2024 (up 13.9%), then fell to KRW 1.15 trillion in 2025 (down 22.9%). Across 2022 to 2025, 2024 was the largest year and 2025 the smallest.

Operating profit hit its low of KRW 14.8 billion in 2023 (down 62.6%) and then rose to KRW 37.3 billion in 2024 (up 152.5%) and KRW 64.5 billion in 2025 (up 73.2%), which is two consecutive years of growth from 2024 through 2025.

The operating margin moved from 3.2% in 2022 to 1.1% in 2023, 2.5% in 2024 and 5.6% in 2025, the highest of the four years even though revenue shrank in 2025.

Press coverage attributed the 2025 profit recovery to cost-ratio improvements and strong sales of in-house projects, and the full collection of KRW 31.1 billion in receivables from the Pohang Pentacity Hanshin The Hue site during the second quarter of 2025 also contributed, an effect that is unlikely to recur.

Profit attributable to controlling shareholders fell to KRW 6.86 billion in 2024 (down 78.5%) before recovering to KRW 59.9 billion in 2025 (up 772.6%), while net income including non-controlling interests was KRW 57.9 billion in 2025.

In 2022 the controlling figure of KRW 45.5 billion exceeded total net income of KRW 40 billion, which means non-controlling interests posted a loss that year. Operating cash flow moved from outflows of KRW 112.2 billion in 2023 to an inflow of KRW 134.6 billion in 2024 and KRW 22.4 billion in 2025.

The debt ratio fell from 223.3% in 2022 to 163.0% in 2025, with equity of KRW 841.5 billion at year-end 2025, of which KRW 836.8 billion was attributable to controlling shareholders.

In 2026, operating profit was KRW 19.3 billion in the first quarter and KRW 25.1 billion in the third quarter, the latter up 12.3% year on year, yet third-quarter profit attributable to controlling shareholders of KRW 24.2 billion fell 7.0% year on year, so the two measures moved in different directions.

Over the last four quarters, from the fourth quarter of 2025 through the third quarter of 2026, profit attributable to controlling shareholders totaled KRW 81.8 billion.

05

Industry analysis

Coverage describes the domestic construction market as being in a prolonged downturn, with mid-sized builders facing both weaker profitability and liquidity pressure. Amid a slump in the sales market, Hanshin Construction has reportedly been strengthening its push into public-sector and redevelopment orders.

A drop in new construction starts across the industry has been cited as a factor behind the decline in first-quarter 2026 revenue.

Reports forecast that average annual move-in volumes in the Changwon area from 2026 onward would fall to about 30% of previous levels, which was expected to draw more interest in newly sold apartments.

The position of the market in the cycle is hard to pin down from the available data, since a sales slump and fewer starts are being mentioned together. Against that backdrop, commentary has highlighted the company's financial soundness and conservative operating approach as standing out during the downturn.

A 2026 press report said the company's credit standing had moved up one notch after gains in profitability and financial structure. The company is also recruiting civil-engineering staff for a second round of hiring in 2026, which was described as preparation for expected growth in public infrastructure orders.

The in-house share of first-quarter 2026 revenue at 14.34% serves as a gauge of how much the slowdown in sales has weighed on results.

06

Outlook

The company has said it aims to keep steadily expanding revenue and operating profit on the back of a stable portfolio of public-sector and redevelopment orders.

In January 2026 it won the second section of the Asan-Cheongju Expressway No. 32 Inju-Yeomchi construction project worth KRW 123.2 billion, and in June it added the fifth section of the Jecheon-Yeongwol Expressway worth KRW 189.9 billion and the second section of Phase 1 of the Jeonnam-Gwangju Urban Rail Line 2 worth KRW 84.6 billion to its order list.

Coverage put the Jecheon-Yeongwol contract period at July 20, 2026 to April 18, 2032.

Preferred-bidder selection for the Ansan Jangsang A-10 and Seongnam Bokjeong 1 B2 privately participating public housing projects, with a combined scale of KRW 346.2 billion, was under way through a consortium as of July 2026 press reports.

Reports in April 2026 said the Gadeok Island airport access road (KRW 589.9 billion) and the Ulsan Urban Rail Line 1 (KRW 271.7 billion) were scheduled for contracting in 2026, with the consortium led by the company proceeding through a negotiated contract as the sole bidder.

Coverage said the Nogok Hanshin The Hue project in Daegu, with 928 households, would set its start and sales timing in light of the property market. For the Hoewon 2 redevelopment, the plan reported in February 2026 targeted construction start in April and sales in June.

Coverage said a backlog of about KRW 7 trillion equals roughly six to seven years of work at the current revenue scale, but growth in revenue remains an open task. The most direct checkpoint is whether revenue declines stop in the 2026 annual report, the company's next regular filing.

Bull factors

Margin recovery

The operating margin rose from 3.2% in 2022 to 5.6% in 2025, and 2025 operating profit of KRW 64.5 billion was up 73.2% from the prior year. Third-quarter 2026 operating profit of KRW 25.1 billion marked a third straight quarter of growth. Coverage has tied the improvement to cost management and selective bidding. Commentators have credited cost-structure gains with supporting the profit recovery.

Backlog and public pipeline

Contract backlog at the end of the first quarter of 2026 was KRW 7.1962 trillion, including KRW 2.0725 trillion of public-sector work. In June 2026 the company contracted the fifth section of the Jecheon-Yeongwol Expressway worth KRW 189.9 billion.

Preferred-bidder steps for two public housing projects were also reported. Coverage has described the backlog as roughly six to seven years of work.

Balance-sheet repair

The debt ratio fell from 223.3% in 2022 to 163.0% in 2025. Equity at the end of 2025 was KRW 841.5 billion, of which KRW 836.8 billion was attributable to controlling shareholders. A July 2026 press report said the company's credit standing had risen one notch. The company plans to continue in-house sales and redevelopment orders on the back of a sounder balance sheet.

09

Bear factors

Revenue still shrinking

Revenue in 2025 was KRW 1.15 trillion, down 22.9% from the prior year. First-quarter 2026 revenue fell 17.1% year on year, and third-quarter 2026 revenue fell 12.2%. Coverage linked the decline to the completion of in-house projects and a drop in new construction starts. Whether margins can hold while the top line keeps shrinking is the open question.

Cash flow and receivables

Operating cash flow turned positive in 2024 and was KRW 22.4 billion in 2025, but it was negative in 2022 and 2023. Commentary has pointed to rising receivables and cash-flow shortfalls as remaining challenges.

Whether cash generation is steady relative to reported profit needs to be checked again in later periodic reports.

Subsidiary losses and provisions

Subsidiary Sangwoo Construction was declared bankrupt after capital impairment deepened, and Nogok PFV had a negative equity of KRW 27.9 billion. Press coverage reported that litigation-loss provisions rose from KRW 3.9 billion in 2024 to KRW 7.1 billion in 2025. Commentators warned that a larger provision could translate into higher non-operating expenses.

Risk factors

Subsidiaries and contingent liabilities

Capital impairment at subsidiaries and rising litigation-related provisions could lead to higher non-operating costs, according to commentary. A Korea Ratings researcher described the company as holding a high degree of importance within its corporate group, based on an April 2026 press report.

How financial support within the group would work in practice needs to be checked in the notes to periodic reports.

Industry and sales slump

Fewer new construction starts and a weaker sales market could slow the conversion of orders into revenue. Quarterly revenue in 2026 has stayed in the KRW 250 billion range. Delays in public tender schedules or in redevelopment construction starts would add volatility to results.

One-off gains and site safety

The 2025 profit recovery included one-off support from the KRW 31.1 billion receivables collection, so whether profit can repeat needs to be verified. The company says it has recorded no serious industrial accidents since 2021.

Commentary still frames proving safety management capability after the 2021 business suspension as an open task.

Overall view

Hanshin Construction spent 2025 with revenue down 22.9% while operating profit and profit attributable to controlling shareholders rose sharply.

The operating margin of 5.6% is the highest of the four years reported, and operating profit has risen for three consecutive quarters through the third quarter of 2026, which marks the profitability side of the story.

On the other hand, quarterly revenue in 2026 has stayed in the KRW 250 billion range with year-on-year declines, so there is no clear sign yet of a top-line rebound.

Contract backlog of KRW 7.1962 trillion at the end of the first quarter of 2026 and the public and redevelopment pipeline are cited as support for future revenue. The debt ratio fell to 163.0%, but subsidiary capital impairment and litigation-related provisions remain points to monitor.

The 2025 profit includes one-time support from receivables collection, so whether operating profit holds at similar levels after 2026 is the checkpoint. Reading the low price-to-book level together with the earnings multiple, which includes one-time items, is a fair starting point for this stock.

The most direct test in the next regular filing, the 2026 annual report, is whether revenue declines have stopped and whether profit attributable to controlling shareholders can be sustained.
