One profitable quarter amid losses; recovery still to be proven

02

Business structure

SJ Group was founded in March 2008 and runs apparel and accessories manufacturing and sales as its main business. It obtained the master license for the Kangol brand in 2011, launched the children's line Kangol Kids in 2018, and also handles imports and sales of Helen Kaminski.

The company operates the self-developed fashion editorial brand LE CONTE DES CONTES and the space platform LCDC SEOUL. Its sales network centers on premium channels such as department stores, multi-complex shopping malls and duty-free shops.

By segment, fashion products made up 61%, fashion merchandise 38% and other businesses 2% of sales in the second quarter of 2026, according to a data service. By brand, Kangol accounted for 63.4%, Kangol Kids 28.4% and Helen Kaminski 6.9% of sales in the first quarter of 2022, according to a securities research report.

Channel mix in the same period was department stores 42%, online 32%, outlets 20% and duty-free 3%. Because brands are brought in through licenses and imports, royalties, purchase costs and inventory turnover shape profitability.

The company attributed its 2025 sales decline to a consumer slowdown at home and abroad and persistent abnormal weather.

04

Earnings analysis

On a disclosed basis, revenue rose from KRW 197.9 billion in 2022 to KRW 203.7 billion in 2023, the largest year in the 2022-2025 period, up 3.0% year on year. It then fell 10.8% to KRW 181.8 billion in 2024 and 10.1% to KRW 163.4 billion in 2025, a two-year decline.

Operating profit dropped from KRW 35.8 billion in 2022 to KRW 15.4 billion in 2023, a 56.9% fall, turned into an operating loss of KRW 3.24 billion in 2024, and widened to an operating loss of KRW 15.9 billion in 2025. The operating margin slid from 18.1% in 2022 to 7.6% in 2023, -1.8% in 2024 and -9.7% in 2025.

Net income attributable to controlling shareholders was KRW 29.1 billion in 2022 and KRW 13.1 billion in 2023, a 54.9% decline, then a loss of KRW 2.78 billion in 2024 and KRW 8.98 billion in 2025.

The net loss including non-controlling interests was KRW 10.7 billion in 2025, a different figure from the controlling-shareholder loss. According to news analysis of the 2025 annual report, the gross margin fell to 63.2% from 67.8%.

Selling and administrative expenses dropped 5.8%, but the decline in gross profit was larger, so the operating loss widened. By quarter, Q4 2025 revenue was KRW 39.8 billion, up 14.2% from the prior quarter, with an operating loss of KRW 5.24 billion.

Q1 2026 revenue was KRW 36.9 billion with an operating loss of KRW 3.85 billion, a narrower loss. Q2 2026 revenue was KRW 38.5 billion and operating profit KRW 288 million, a swing to profit.

Q3 2026 revenue was KRW 29.6 billion, down 23.0% quarter on quarter and 14.8% year on year, with an operating loss of KRW 5.01 billion. Attributable net losses over the last four quarters totaled KRW 10.9 billion.

Operating cash flow went from KRW 23.3 billion in 2022 and KRW 10 billion in 2023 to -KRW 1.2 billion in 2024 and -KRW 12.2 billion in 2025.

Industry analysis

A data service classifies the company in the apparel and fashion accessories industry. Among 71 stocks in that industry, on 2025 annual figures it ranks 64th by market capitalization, 37th by revenue and 67th by operating profit.

The industry's average operating margin is 2.22%, a positive figure, while the company's 2025 operating margin was -9.7%. The industry's average debt ratio is 112.37%, and the company's year-end 2025 debt ratio of 80.94% is below that average.

The company itself cited a consumer slowdown at home and abroad and persistent abnormal weather as demand factors. Department stores and duty-free shops are offline-centered channels sensitive to visitor flows and seasonal sell-through, so quarterly revenue trends most directly show industry conditions.

On competition, brands brought in under licenses see results vary with brand awareness and contract terms.

The company has allocated funds to new businesses such as the LCDC SEOUL space platform, and news reports said its 2025 annual report flagged investment in non-core areas such as restaurants and entertainment as a possible risk. Within the industry, the company ranks low on profitability and sits below average on leverage.

Outlook

News reports put first-half 2026 consolidated revenue at KRW 75.4 billion and an operating loss of KRW 3.6 billion. On the second-quarter swing to profit, one report noted the company is in the middle of adjusting its cost structure and said one profitable quarter is too early to confirm a full-year normalization.

The same report named the autumn and winter peak-season sell-through rate, inventory levels, costs of new brands and whether the second-quarter profit continues as the next tests.

With third-quarter 2026 revenue down 14.8% year on year and the operating loss wider, the fourth-quarter peak season is the next point that will show the direction.

For new businesses, reports said KRW 26.2 billion was allocated to investment related to the LCDC SEOUL space platform, and that the company did not disclose the expected payback period, citing trade secrets.

Loans to the related party SJ G Plus rose from KRW 4 billion to KRW 9 billion, and a bad-debt allowance of KRW 1.685 billion was set against part of it. The company said sales from that business continue to occur.

Whether these loans are collected and how the new businesses perform will show up in the annual report notes and full-year results.

Bull factors

Second-quarter profit turn and cost adjustment

Operating profit in Q2 2026 was KRW 288 million, a swing from an operating loss of KRW 3.85 billion in Q1, and revenue rose 4.2% quarter on quarter to KRW 38.5 billion. News reports describe this as a cost-structure adjustment underway. The same reports note that one profitable quarter is too early to confirm a full-year normalization.

Leverage below the industry average

The industry's average debt ratio is 112.37%, and the company's debt ratio at the end of 2025 was 80.9%, below that average. With total equity of KRW 119.1 billion remaining, the capital base to absorb losses is still in place, a point cited on the bullish side. The rise from 70.3% in 2024 to 80.9% in 2025 should be read alongside it.

Brand lineup and space platform

Beyond the three brands Kangol, Kangol Kids and Helen Kaminski, the company has widened its lineup with the in-house editorial brand LE CONTE DES CONTES and the space platform LCDC SEOUL. A business summary for the nine months through 2025 cited new license acquisitions.

Using department stores, multi-complex malls and duty-free shops together forms the base for channel diversification.

Risk factors

According to news reports, the company itself flagged investment and expansion into non-core areas such as restaurants and entertainment as a possible risk. The KRW 26.2 billion allocated to new businesses has no disclosed payback period, which makes results hard to judge. Consumer and channel risk

The reliance on department stores and duty-free shops makes the business sensitive to visitor flows.

Financial and share-price volatility risk

Overall view

SJ Group is a fashion company that sells licensed and imported brands such as Kangol, Kangol Kids and Helen Kaminski, centered on department stores and duty-free shops.

On disclosed figures, revenue peaked at KRW 203.7 billion in 2023 and fell for two straight years in 2024 and 2025, while the operating loss widened from KRW 3.24 billion in 2024 to KRW 15.9 billion in 2025.

The company returned to operating profit of KRW 288 million in Q2 2026, but Q3 2026 revenue of KRW 29.6 billion and an operating loss of KRW 5.01 billion brought it back to a loss. Strengths include the Q2 profit turn, a debt ratio below the industry average and a price-to-book ratio well below the industry average.

Weaknesses include two consecutive operating losses, a larger operating cash outflow, related-party loans with a bad-debt allowance and the burden of investment in non-core new businesses. With attributable net losses of KRW 10.9 billion over the last four quarters, earnings-based measures are still hard to apply.

The next checkpoints are the Q4 2026 peak-season sell-through and inventory, the preliminary full-year results around early February 2027, the 2026 annual report due in late March 2027, and the Q1 2027 quarterly report in mid-May 2027.
