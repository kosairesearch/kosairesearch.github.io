Report overview

Profit recovery continues on data security growth, still in progress

Data security demand and AI expansion kept operating profit in the black in the second and third quarters of 2026, but the 2025 operating margin of 5.4% remains well below the 11.8% recorded in 2022, so whether profitability fully recovers will be tested in the 2026 annual report.

02

Business structure

Fasoo AI is built on data security products that protect corporate information from leakage and misuse. Its application security subsidiary Sparrow runs a software supply chain security business, and the AI business sits alongside both.

The AI business began in earnest in 2024, when most engagements were proof-of-concept projects, and actual implementation projects increased from 2025, according to the company's CFO. In March 2026 the company added 'AI' to its name to signal to employees and customers that it is moving toward an AI business.

The revenue model is shifting from perpetual licenses toward annual subscription licenses, with new AI products leading the subscription push. The aim is to ease the concentration of revenue in the fourth quarter every year. The company says its maintenance contract renewal rate is above 95%.

Its main customers are in booming industries such as semiconductors, defense, shipbuilding and automotive, where large corporations are demanding stronger security from their suppliers, pushing security spending down the supply chain.

Data security and AI products are both driving results, and the Rhapsody Core AI-ready platform was launched in July 2026. Overseas, the U.S. subsidiary was combined with a local AI and cloud consulting firm to offer data management, security and AI together.

Competition comes from domestic security vendors such as Ahnlab, and the company differentiates by combining AI features with data security. Revenue by product line is not disclosed as numbers, so the contribution of data security and AI cannot be separated quantitatively.

04

Earnings analysis

On a disclosed annual basis, revenue fell from KRW 44.1 billion in 2022 to KRW 42.7 billion in 2023, a 3.3% decline, and then rose to KRW 46.1 billion in 2024, up 8.1%, and KRW 46.7 billion in 2025, up 1.2%. Revenue grew in both 2024 and 2025, and 2025 was the largest year of the 2022 to 2025 period.

Profit moved the other way during the same stretch. Operating profit went from KRW 5.2 billion in 2022 to KRW 3.81 billion in 2023, down 26.6%, then to KRW 3.9 billion in 2024, up 2.2%, and to KRW 2.54 billion in 2025, down 35.0%, making 2025 the smallest year of the period.

The operating margin slid from 11.8% in 2022 to 8.9%, 8.5% and 5.4% in 2025. Profit attributable to controlling shareholders fell from KRW 5.21 billion in 2022 to KRW 2.66 billion in 2025, down 40.7% in that year. Net income including non-controlling interests was KRW 2.72 billion in 2025.

The CFO said profit declined because the company invested in AI while revenue had stagnated, and the shift to subscriptions also deferred revenue in the near term. Operating cash flow dropped from KRW 10.6 billion in 2022 to KRW 3.27 billion in 2025.

At the end of 2025, total equity was KRW 38.1 billion, of which KRW 37.4 billion belonged to controlling shareholders, and total liabilities were KRW 10.7 billion, so the debt ratio fell from 59.5% to 28.2%. Quarterly results show a sharp swing around year-end.

Revenue rose from KRW 10.4 billion in the third quarter of 2025 to KRW 17.1 billion in the fourth quarter, up 63.6% quarter on quarter, and operating profit went from KRW 255 million to KRW 5.93 billion. The first quarter of 2026 returned to an operating loss of KRW 2.02 billion on revenue of KRW 9 billion, down 47.3%.

The second quarter returned to profit with revenue of KRW 13.1 billion and operating profit of KRW 1.01 billion. The third quarter brought revenue of KRW 13.5 billion, up 2.7% quarter on quarter and 29.0% year on year, and operating profit of KRW 1.25 billion, up 389.5% year on year.

Profit attributable to controlling shareholders was KRW 1.67 billion in the third quarter. Because revenue concentrates in the fourth quarter, quarterly profit remains volatile.

05

Industry analysis

The company and media both describe data security demand as rising on the back of recent hacking incidents and AI-driven cyber threats. The spread of generative AI has also increased the risk of corporate data leaking or being misused, adding to demand for document and data protection solutions.

On the demand side, revenue from customers in industries with rising orders and investment, such as semiconductors, defense and shipbuilding, is expanding. That pattern suggests security spending moves early in strong industries and could slow if those industries weaken.

On the supply chain side, growing need for software bills of materials (SBOM) helped Sparrow's application security business perform well. Demand is shaped by incidents and regulatory tightening, so the position in the cycle is difficult to pin down in one direction.

Within the IT and software sector, companies such as Samsung SDS and Hyundai Autoever are large system integrators, while Fasoo AI is a small specialist security software company by market capitalization. In security software, domestic listed firms such as Ahnlab and overseas vendors compete for the same customers.

Fasoo AI is positioning itself by layering AI products on top of its data security customer base. When comparing with peers, margins and growth should be read together, because specialist and large-scale firms have different earnings structures.

06

Outlook

The company expects 2026 annual revenue to grow by double digits. It cited the concentration of revenue in the second half and a sharp year-on-year increase in order backlog, as stated when it released preliminary second-quarter results on July 31, 2026.

In a September 2026 CFO interview, the company said revenue as of August 2026 had already exceeded full-year 2025 revenue, and noted that this figure includes contracts not yet recognized as accounting revenue.

When it announced third-quarter results on November 16, 2026, the company said existing order volumes would be recognized mainly in the fourth quarter.

Its medium-term targets, set in the value-up plan announced in June 2025, call for 20% average annual revenue growth, a 25% operating margin and a 30% shareholder return ratio by 2030. The company also announced a share buyback decision at the end of June 2026.

The 2025 operating margin was 5.4%, and the third quarter of 2026 was profitable on a quarterly basis, so the gap to the targets remains large. Whether that gap narrows as AI revenue grows is the central variable.

The U.S. subsidiary has been loss-making because of investment costs, and it was restructured with a target of breaking even in 2027. The next checkpoints are the annual growth rate and operating margin in the 2026 annual report, and whether the share of subscription revenue increases.

08

Bull factors

Rising data security demand and customer expansion

Third-quarter 2026 revenue reached KRW 13.5 billion, up 29.0% year on year. The company points to growing AI-driven cyber threats and expanding customers in semiconductors, defense and shipbuilding. Large companies demanding stronger security from their suppliers is also cited as supporting demand.

Subscription shift and an emerging AI revenue base

It is expanding annual subscription licenses, led by new AI products, to build a recurring revenue base. The company also said revenue as of August 2026 had exceeded full-year 2025 revenue, a figure that includes contracts not yet booked as accounting revenue.

Lower financial burden and stated shareholder return intent

The debt ratio fell to 28.2% at the end of 2025 from 59.5% in 2022. Operating profit for the first three quarters of 2026 turned to a cumulative profit. The company targets a 30% shareholder return ratio by 2030 and decided on a share buyback at the end of June 2026.

09

Bear factors

Declining profitability trend

The operating margin fell from 11.8% in 2022 to 5.4% in 2025. Operating profit of KRW 2.54 billion in 2025 was the smallest of the 2022 to 2025 period. Operating cash flow also dropped from KRW 10.6 billion in 2022 to KRW 3.27 billion in 2025. Revenue grew, but profit and cash generation did not keep pace.

Seasonality and volatility in quarterly results

The first quarter of 2026 swung to an operating loss of KRW 2.02 billion. Of the KRW 6.84 billion four-quarter total of profit attributable to controlling shareholders, KRW 6.11 billion came from the fourth quarter of 2025.

Revenue concentrates in the fourth quarter, so profit clusters in particular quarters, and changing this pattern requires a larger subscription share.

AI investment costs and U.S. business losses

The company said profit fell while it invested in AI during a period of stagnant revenue. The U.S. business has run losses because of investment costs, and breaking even in 2027 is the stated target. Until that target is reached, overseas investment costs could slow the pace of profitability recovery.

10

Risk factors

Industry cycle and customer concentration

The earnings recovery relies heavily on security spending by customers in booming industries such as semiconductors, defense and shipbuilding. If investment slows in these industries, order intake and revenue recognition could slow as well. As dependence on specific industries rises, changes in the customer mix need to be watched.

Competition and transition to AI products

Domestic security firms such as Ahnlab and overseas vendors compete in security software. If combining AI features with data security fails to win customers, growth in new AI revenue could fall short of expectations. The pace of product transition and customer adoption are key variables.

Revenue recognition timing and metric interpretation

The CFO said the shift to subscriptions defers near-term revenue recognition. The revenue figure the company gave for August 2026 includes contracts not yet booked as accounting revenue, so it should not be read as reported revenue. The expectation of double-digit annual growth depends on how much revenue is recognized in the fourth quarter.

12

Overall view

Fasoo AI kept operating profit in the black in the second and third quarters of 2026, supported by stronger data security demand and the start of its AI business, and third-quarter revenue rose 29.0% year on year.

However, the 2025 operating margin of 5.4% remains below the 11.8% of 2022, and the decline in operating profit and operating cash flow since 2022 has not yet been reversed. The debt ratio falling to 28.2% and total equity of KRW 38.1 billion point to a lighter financial burden.

Results depend heavily on fourth-quarter revenue concentration and the timing effects of the subscription shift, so quarterly swings are large. Supporting factors include customer expansion, a maintenance renewal rate above 95%, and the 2030 medium-term targets.

Offsetting factors are the lower profitability, the concentration of profit in single quarters and the U.S. business losses. The next checkpoints are the annual growth and operating margin in the 2026 annual report, and whether the U.S. subsidiary reaches breakeven in 2027.
