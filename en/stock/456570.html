Clinical Progress, Eroded Balance Sheet

02

Business structure

IMGT was founded in 2010 and develops anticancer treatment technology based on focused ultrasound. The company has been reported to hold four technology platforms: focused ultrasound, ultrasound-linked nanoparticle drug delivery, nanoparticles for hepatic arterial embolization, and antibody-loaded nanoparticles.

Its lead product, IMD10, is a pancreatic cancer device that uses focused ultrasound to create temporary micro-openings in cancer cell membranes so that drugs are absorbed more readily.

The other pipeline items are IMD20 (a focused ultrasound system for pain), IMP301 (an ultrasound-responsive nano anticancer agent) and IMP101 (a nano carrier for liver cancer), and all have entered clinical stages. Revenue is effectively nil, and reported results show revenue of zero for 2023 and 2024.

A revenue breakdown by segment therefore cannot be calculated, and the cost structure is centered on research and clinical expenses.

On competition, the company locates its differentiation in the mechanism of action. Whereas the US company HistoSonics destroys cancer tissue directly with ultrasound, IMD10 is described as a drug-delivery platform that helps anticancer drugs penetrate tissue.

The company also cites as a differentiator that it enhances drug delivery with focused ultrasound alone, without separate microbubbles.

IMGT is classified under biotech and pharmaceuticals on KONEX, but its core product is a medical device, so its business model differs from drug-centered listed peers such as Alteogen and HLB.

On funding, it has been reported to have raised a total of KRW 33.0 billion from TIPS through Series A to C and to receive research funding from government projects worth about KRW 10.0 billion.

Recent trends

Based on reported results, the operating loss for 2025 was KRW 5.6 billion and the net loss was KRW 40.0 billion. In 2024 the operating loss was KRW 5.6 billion and the net loss was KRW 12.2 billion, while in 2023 the operating loss was KRW 6.7 billion and net income was KRW 4.1 billion.

At the end of December 2025, total assets were KRW 15.1 billion, total liabilities were KRW 75.3 billion, and total equity was negative KRW 60.2 billion. The operating loss was the same as in 2024, but the net loss widened sharply.

The cause of the wider net loss could not be confirmed in this research, and the notes to the annual report would need to be checked.

To secure working capital, the company decided on a third-party allotment rights offering of 166,912 common shares and 961,537 redeemable convertible preferred shares, raising about KRW 14.7 billion.

Subscribers of the redeemable convertible preferred shares included the Industrial Bank of Korea, JB Woori Capital and investment partnerships.

On the clinical side, the company received approval of the pivotal-trial IDE for IMD10 from the US FDA on November 22, 2025.

On March 24, 2026, a company official said the earlier two technology evaluations had focused on IMD10, and that it would speed up commercialization of other pipeline items and seek partners before reapplying.

According to a filing on March 31, 2026, the annual general meeting approved the financial statements, the remuneration limit and the grant of stock options. A report dated July 28, 2026 said the FDA had approved a renewed IDE and that the company was aiming to enroll its first patient in September 2026.

This research did not find a filing or report confirming that enrollment has actually begun. The share price was KRW 10,280 (+2.49%) on October 27, 2026, and the closing price on March 13, 2024 was KRW 17,020.

04

Outlook

The biggest variable ahead is the enrollment pace and results of the IMD10 pivotal trial. The company plans to run a multicenter, randomized, controlled trial in Korea and the United States and then seek US marketing authorization through the De Novo pathway.

The earlier plan was to run the trial at five sites in Korea and the United States through the end of 2029. With the trial size raised to 360 patients, the schedule and cost may differ, but no confirmed figures were found.

The company has said it is discussing the scale of US trial-cost support with the Focused Ultrasound Foundation.

On funding, the company has almost no revenue during the trial period and therefore relies heavily on external financing. The company has said it intends to add pipeline products that can generate revenue until the trials conclude, and to prepare a technology evaluation application.

The company also said it had received a partnership proposal from a large global medical device maker, but whether any agreement was signed has not been confirmed. The earlier goal of a KOSDAQ listing within 2025 has already passed, and the company is now at the stage of reapplying for the technology evaluation.

Removing the capital erosion is likely to depend on how capital is raised, for example through equity issuance or conversion. In terms of business indicators, pipeline expansion and trial progress could eventually create revenue sources, but the timing and scale remain unclear.

05

Bull factors

Regulatory milestones with the FDA

The company says IMD10 received FDA Breakthrough Device Designation in February 2025, giving it a favorable position for expedited review and reimbursement listing.

Reports say it is the first anticancer medical device from Korea to receive FDA pivotal-trial IDE approval, and the first in the world for a pancreatic cancer indication. Ongoing consultation with the regulator adds concreteness to the clinical pathway.

Exploratory results and a differentiated mechanism

The exploratory trial was run at Seoul National University Hospital with 30 patients, randomly assigned 15 each to the test and control groups. The company stated that the analysis showed a significant improvement in survival for the combination therapy over standard treatment.

Improving drug delivery with focused ultrasound alone, without separate microbubbles, is another differentiator the company emphasizes.

External partnership and funding discussions

Major US cancer centers such as Brigham and Women's Hospital, UVA Health and Mays Cancer Center were reported to be set to take part in the pivotal trial. The company is also discussing the scale of US trial-cost support with the Focused Ultrasound Foundation.

It has said it received a partnership proposal from a large global medical device maker, which could become a route to outside funding and business cooperation.

Bear factors

Full capital erosion and accumulated losses

Based on reported results, total equity at the end of 2025 was negative KRW 60.2 billion, total liabilities were KRW 75.3 billion and total assets were KRW 15.1 billion. The net loss for 2025 was KRW 40.0 billion, sharply higher than KRW 12.2 billion in 2024. With no revenue and continuing losses, financial soundness indicators are weak across the board.

Reliance on a small exploratory trial and pivotal-trial uncertainty

The survival improvement came from an exploratory trial of 30 patients, and its interpretation relies on company announcements. The pivotal trial has been expanded to 360 patients, and it has not been confirmed that the same effect will be reproduced. Slow enrollment or a missed endpoint could extend the approval timeline.

Delayed listing plans and low liquidity

The company had earlier stated a goal of a KOSDAQ listing within 2025. However, it has gone through two technology evaluation applications and is now preparing to reapply. Trading value on October 27, 2026 was only KRW 6,599,270, so even small volumes could move the share price considerably.

Risk factors

Macro

Interest rates and sentiment toward biotech and healthcare directly affect the financing conditions of clinical-stage companies on KONEX and in private markets. A company with no revenue could face delays to its trial schedule if the external funding environment worsens. Because KONEX trading is thin, price swings can widen when the broader market turns risk-averse.

Regulatory and clinical

The company plans to obtain US marketing authorization through the De Novo pathway after the pivotal trial. If the trial results do not meet efficacy criteria or the FDA requests additional data, approval could be delayed or fail.

Whether the first patient has been enrolled in the pivotal trial, and the schedule, could not be confirmed in this research.

Financing and dilution

The company has previously raised about KRW 14.7 billion by issuing redeemable convertible preferred shares. Depending on the redemption or conversion terms of those shares, cash outflows or share dilution could occur. At the annual general meeting on March 31, 2026, a proposal to grant stock options was also approved. Additional equity issuance and the exercise of stock options could affect existing shareholders' ownership.

08

Overall view

IMGT is a clinical-stage company that has secured FDA Breakthrough Device Designation and pivotal-trial IDE approval with a relatively distinctive approach: a focused ultrasound device that helps anticancer drugs penetrate tumors.

On the other hand, based on reported results, total equity at the end of 2025 was negative KRW 60.2 billion and there is no revenue, so there is a wide gap between business progress and the financial structure.

The key items to watch are the enrollment pace and results of the pivotal trial, the reapplication for the technology evaluation, and the method of raising capital.

The market capitalization of KRW 54.1 billion and the trading value of KRW 6,599,270 can be read as reflecting both expectations about clinical progress and liquidity constraints.

Although the company is classified under biotech and pharmaceuticals, its core product is a medical device, so its approval pathway and earnings structure differ from those of drug developers.

This report is a qualitative summary for information only, and readers should verify filings and clinical progress themselves before making any judgment.
