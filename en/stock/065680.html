02

Business structure

UJU Electronics is regarded as a specialist in ultra-precision connectors. Its products are used in mobile devices, displays and automobiles, and its revenue comes from mobile connectors, automotive connectors and display connectors.

The company is organized into an IT division, which handles mobile and display connectors, and an AD division, which handles automotive connectors.

A Korea IR Council analysis from 2024 said IT division revenue had declined every year since 2021, while the AD division had grown steadily since 2020 and offset that weakness.

UJU Electronics runs design, development and mass production under one system and has an in-house mold processing center, which lets it respond quickly to customers' design changes. It entered the automotive connector market in 2013 as a late entrant but has kept launching new products with improved specifications.

Automotive revenue began to exceed 10% of total sales in 2017, after customers completed reliability and quality testing that took four to five years. A 2022 report cited automotive revenue at 27% of the total.

The company has secured connector patents over the past five years to broaden its product lineup and sources key raw materials from domestic suppliers. Its peers in the electronic components sector include large companies such as Samsung Electro-Mechanics and LG Innotek.

UJU Electronics is cited as a company that localized ultra-precision connectors that had previously been imported.

Earnings analysis

Annual revenue fell from KRW 187.2 billion in 2022 to KRW 159.9 billion in 2023, a 14.6% decline, then recovered to KRW 176.2 billion in 2024, up 10.2%, and KRW 186.4 billion in 2025, up 5.8%.

Operating profit was KRW 17.8 billion in 2022, KRW 8.01 billion in 2023 (down 55.0%), KRW 20.1 billion in 2024 (up 151.1%) and KRW 21.8 billion in 2025 (up 8.4%), and it rose for two consecutive years from 2024 through 2025. Within 2022 to 2025, 2025 was the largest year and 2023 was the smallest.

The operating margin fell from 9.5% in 2022 to 5.0% in 2023 before rising to 11.4% in 2024 and 11.7% in 2025. In 2024, operating profit growth of 151.1% far outpaced revenue growth of 10.2%, which made the margin improvement stand out.

Net profit attributable to controlling shareholders was KRW 12.2 billion in 2022, KRW 5.62 billion in 2023 (down 54.0%), KRW 19.9 billion in 2024 (up 254.1%) and KRW 21.6 billion in 2025 (up 8.5%).

Net profit including non-controlling interests was KRW 21.5 billion in 2025, which is a different figure from profit attributable to controlling shareholders.

The 2023 weakness was mainly attributed to lower revenue in the IT division, while the 2025 annual summary pointed to growth in mobile and TV demand alongside higher automotive connector demand.

Operating cash flow was KRW 38.7 billion in 2022, KRW 14.5 billion in 2023, KRW 33.2 billion in 2024 and KRW 22.4 billion in 2025, so cash flow declined in 2025 even as operating profit rose. On a quarterly basis, the third quarter of 2025 showed revenue of KRW 48.3 billion and operating profit of KRW 6.38 billion.

The fourth quarter brought revenue of KRW 45.4 billion, down 6.2% from the prior quarter, and operating profit of KRW 3.76 billion, down 41.1%. The first quarter of 2026 showed revenue of KRW 49.3 billion and operating profit of KRW 4.63 billion, up 23.2%.

In the second quarter, revenue fell to KRW 45 billion, down 8.8%, and operating profit fell to KRW 447 million, down 90.3%, a decline far steeper than the revenue drop.

In the third quarter, revenue was KRW 48.1 billion, up 6.9% from the prior quarter and down 0.5% year on year, and operating profit was KRW 1.25 billion, up 179.4% from the prior quarter and down 80.4% year on year.

Profit attributable to controlling shareholders was KRW 5.77 billion in the fourth quarter of 2025, KRW 6.81 billion in the first quarter of 2026 and KRW 3.53 billion in the second quarter, before the third quarter swung to a net loss of KRW 5.77 billion.

Over the last four quarters, from the fourth quarter of 2025 through the third quarter of 2026, profit attributable to controlling shareholders totaled KRW 10.3 billion. Whether one-off items affected quarterly results should be checked in the notes to the annual report.

Industry analysis

Demand from downstream markets has two drivers. Growth in mobile and TV markets supported the IT division's top line, while demand for automotive electronic components has widened connector demand.

However, IT division revenue has declined every year since 2021, while the AD division, which handles automotive connectors, has grown since 2020. The shifting weight between these two divisions is the key variable for the company's earnings profile.

Automotive connectors face long validation cycles with automakers and parts suppliers, which raises entry barriers. A Korea IR Council analyst noted that automotive revenue ramped only after customers completed validation that took four to five years.

On the technology side, UJU Electronics is regarded as a company that localized ultra-precision connectors. It is a late entrant to the automotive connector market, which entered in 2013, but analysts also point to its steady launches of new products as a source of competitiveness.

Large players in the same electronic components sector include Samsung Electro-Mechanics and LG Innotek, and the difference in scale is substantial. Listed companies grouped as peers for comparison include Isupetasys, TAIHAN Fiberoptics, KMW and Intellian Technologies.

The current phase can be read as a transition, with weakness in IT and growth in automotive. How this shift shows up in 2026 quarterly results is the central checkpoint for judging the industry cycle.
