02

Business structure

Hansaemk is a fashion company within the Hansae YES24 group that focuses on kids' wear and casual brands. Kids' wear accounted for 63% of sales in the first half of 2025, up from 48.8% in 2023 and 58.1% in 2024.

Its main brands are Nike Kids, operated through the kids sports multi-store Playkids-Pro, the kids brand Moimolrn, and Huggies baby wear, which it launched domestically in early 2025. Nike Kids sales rose 73.1% year on year in Q1 2026, and its standalone stores increased from 18 to 31.

By contrast, the company ended production of a multi-store kids brand after the spring/summer 2025 season, and it is running BUCKAROO mainly in the domestic market after liquidating the US subsidiary. Overseas, the China subsidiary Mankun (Shanghai) decided to cease operations and exited the NBA brand business.

The Shanghai operating subsidiary ran 87 Moimolrn stores in China at end-2025. After absorbing the kids-wear specialist Hansae Dream in 2022, the Japanese subsidiary has been expanding its kids-wear business. ThinkPool noted that Hansaemk's 0.6% operating margin in Q1 2026 was among the lower levels versus peers.

04

Earnings analysis

Annual sales rose from KRW 271.5 billion in 2022 to KRW 316.8 billion in 2023, a 16.7% increase, then fell 19.1% to KRW 256.3 billion in 2024 and 2.9% to KRW 248.9 billion in 2025. The 2024 sales decline coincided with a wider operating loss of KRW 21.6 billion.

The operating loss narrowed to KRW 12.1 billion in 2025, and the operating margin was -4.9%. Operating margins were -7.8% in 2022, -1.3% in 2023, -8.4% in 2024 and -4.9% in 2025.

Net loss attributable to controlling shareholders was KRW 37.8 billion in 2022, KRW 6.43 billion in 2023, KRW 39.3 billion in 2024 and KRW 37.9 billion in 2025, and the net loss including non-controlling interests was reported at the same amounts for each year.

Press reports tie the losses back to 2019, when the operating loss was KRW 23.9 billion, so the streak began before the disclosed 2022 to 2025 period. Operating cash flow was -KRW 5.22 billion, -KRW 11.8 billion, -KRW 21.1 billion and -KRW 27.9 billion across 2022 to 2025.

The debt-to-equity ratio moved from 482.0% in 2022 and 205.8% in 2023 to 483.7% in 2024 and 1,403.7% in 2025, with total equity falling to KRW 18.8 billion.

Quarterly sales rose from KRW 50.9 billion in Q3 2025 to KRW 75.6 billion in Q4 2025, a 48.3% increase on the prior quarter, and the company posted an operating profit of KRW 8.04 billion that quarter.

Q1 2026 sales were KRW 66 billion and operating profit was KRW 374 million, an operating margin of 0.6%, and the company said sales grew 9.9% year on year. Q2 2026 sales were KRW 61.3 billion with an operating loss of KRW 734 million, and Q3 2026 sales were KRW 52 billion with an operating loss of KRW 3.53 billion.

The Q3 2026 operating loss was narrower than the KRW 4.84 billion loss a year earlier but wider than the prior quarter. Net loss attributable to controlling shareholders has been reported for five consecutive quarters from Q3 2025 to Q3 2026, totaling KRW 18.5 billion over the last four quarters.

05

Industry analysis

Reports from 2025 described the broader fashion industry as weighed down by weaker consumer sentiment, and Hansaemk has responded by raising the weight of kids' wear. Kids' wear reached 63% of sales in the first half of 2025, up from 48.8% in 2023.

Industry observers see rising demand for kids' sportswear and stronger brand preference as supportive for its Q1 2026 results. Handsome, a listed peer, disclosed preliminary Q3 2026 sales of KRW 324.8 billion, up 4.9% year on year, and operating profit of KRW 7.57 billion, up 206.1%.

In the same quarter Hansaemk posted an operating loss of KRW 3.53 billion, a clear gap in profitability among peers. Overseas, the exit from China operations and the liquidation of the US subsidiary are shrinking that part of the business.

A 2025 report noted that the overseas subsidiaries operating in China and Japan were all in full capital impairment. On the demand side, the kids' sportswear category shows growth, while evidence of a recovery in the broader casual apparel category is not visible in the company's disclosures. Whether the industry is turning is best tracked through quarterly results of listed peers.

Outlook

In its Q1 2026 results announcement, the company said it would strengthen competitiveness centered on high-growth brands and continue expanding AI-based digital transformation and efficiency-focused management.

The brand portfolio restructuring and operating streamlining began in the second half of 2025, and reduced fixed costs are cited as a factor behind the Q1 2026 return to operating profit. Nike Kids continues to grow as standalone stores expand, which makes the kids sports segment the central driver of any recovery.

The China subsidiary Mankun (Shanghai) decided to cease operations in Q4 2025 after its NBA license agreement ended, and the business is in a wind-down stage. The company said it is reviewing the subsidiary's future direction from several angles.

A 2026 press report said the KRW 23.1 billion loan extended to Mankun (Shanghai) was unlikely to be recovered.

A share consolidation that took effect on April 28, 2026 cut the total number of issued shares roughly in half, which the company described as a step to maintain an appropriate float and stabilize the share price.

On the financial side, the question is whether loss reduction translates into equity recovery, given total equity of KRW 18.8 billion and a debt-to-equity ratio of 1,403.7% at end-2025.

Quarterly return to operating profit

Q4 2025 also produced an operating profit of KRW 8.04 billion, showing that profitable quarters have occurred. Cost efficiency efforts are spreading across operations, including AI-based marketing.

Exit from loss-making overseas units

If loss-making overseas operations shrink, the swings in consolidated results could narrow, and that effect will show up in future quarterly disclosures.

Overseas funding burden and shrinking scope

Risk factors

Listing status and low-price share rules

Regulators have moved to include low-priced shares among delisting candidates, and the exchange has been tightening its watch-list criteria. Hansaemk responded with a share consolidation, but industry observers argued that without improved financial structure, delisting risk is hard to escape. If losses continue with equity at KRW 18.8 billion, meeting listing standards may become an additional issue.

Overseas subsidiary funding and impairment

Funds provided to overseas subsidiaries have been fully impaired in the past, and cumulative support to the China subsidiary has been large. Press reports said recovery of those funds appears unlikely after operations were halted. Additional impairments or liquidation costs during the wind-down could weigh further on profit and equity.

Concentration in kids' wear and specific brands

Kids' wear reached 63% of sales in the first half of 2025, so revenue is concentrated in one category. A slowdown at Nike Kids would have a large effect on total sales. If weakness in general casual apparel persists, the diversification benefit of the business mix may be limited.

Overall view

Hansaemk swung to an operating profit in Q1 2026, which improved quarterly results, but operating losses in Q2 and Q3 2026 show the loss structure has not been fully resolved.

Annual operating losses were KRW 21.1 billion, KRW 4.22 billion, KRW 21.6 billion and KRW 12.1 billion from 2022 to 2025, an operating loss in each of the four years, with the loss narrowing in 2025. Nike Kids, the core of the kids sports segment, rose 73.1% in Q1 2026 and stood out as a strength.

Against that, total equity was KRW 18.8 billion at end-2025 and the debt-to-equity ratio was 1,403.7%, so the financial base is thin, and operating cash flow was negative for four years.

Exiting loss-making overseas units and restructuring brands can reduce earnings volatility, and those effects will show up in the 2026 annual report and future quarterly disclosures.
