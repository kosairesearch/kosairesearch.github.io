Report overview

Third-Quarter Turn to Profit, Core Recovery Still Pending

Ilyang Pharmaceutical returned to operating profit in the third quarter of 2026, but its cumulative operating loss for the first three quarters remains, so whether the core business recovers is the next point to track.

02

Business structure

Ilyang Pharmaceutical was founded in 1946 and listed on the KOSPI market in 1974, and it holds subsidiaries including Ilyang Biopharm.

Its main products are the domestically developed anti-ulcer drug Noltec, the leukemia treatment Supect (radotinib), seasonal influenza vaccines among prescription drugs, and the OTC brand Wonbidi.

In cumulative revenue for the first three quarters of 2026, excluding intercompany sales, the prescription drug (ETC) segment contributed KRW 87.1 billion, or 41.37% of the total. The export segment reached KRW 42.8 billion, up 16.4% from KRW 36.8 billion a year earlier, and accounted for 20.33%.

The OTC segment recorded KRW 41.1 billion (19.53%), and other segments, including health supplements, recorded KRW 34.4 billion (16.33%). Noltec sits at the center of revenue and is described as expanding its prescription share as a core earnings source.

The vaccine business centers on influenza vaccines made at the Eumseong vaccine plant, which was completed in 2011, and its customers include the prescription market and government National Immunization Program (NIP) procurement volumes.

In the past, Chinese joint ventures were deeply tied to reported results, but in early 2025 the company restated its 2022 and 2023 annual reports and reclassified the two entities as jointly controlled companies rather than subsidiaries.

It has since been dismantling the joint-venture-centered structure, including transferring its stake in Tongwha Ilyang to the Chinese shareholder. On the competitive side, operating results in the first half of 2026 diverged across the sector.

Unlike traditional pharma companies such as Hanmi Pharm, whose operating profit grew faster than revenue, Ilyang Pharmaceutical moved from operating profit to operating loss.

04

Earnings analysis

Annual revenue rose from KRW 247.8 billion in 2022 to KRW 266.7 billion in 2023 (up 7.6%), KRW 268.9 billion in 2024 (up 0.8%) and KRW 272.4 billion in 2025 (up 1.3%), growing for three consecutive years from 2023 through 2025.

The pace of growth narrowed to about 1% in 2024 and 2025, and 2025 was the largest year of the 2022–2025 period.

Operating profit went from KRW 14.2 billion in 2022 to KRW 16.4 billion in 2023 (up 14.9%), the peak of the period, before falling to KRW 11 billion in 2024 (down 32.8%) and KRW 4.96 billion in 2025 (down 54.9%), a two-year decline from 2024 through 2025.

The operating margin slid from 5.8% in 2022 and 6.1% in 2023 to 4.1% in 2024 and 1.8% in 2025.

The margin fell while revenue grew, which appears to reflect cost pressure: in the first half of 2026, the cost of sales ratio rose from 55.6% to 62.5%, and selling, general and administrative expenses increased from KRW 38.7 billion to KRW 48.2 billion.

Net income attributable to controlling shareholders went from KRW 19.4 billion in 2022 to a net loss of KRW 2.41 billion in 2023, a swing back to profit at KRW 10.3 billion in 2024, and KRW 6.9 billion in 2025 (down 32.7%). Reports attributed the 2024 recovery partly to the liquidation of a Chinese subsidiary.

Net income including non-controlling interests, a different figure, was KRW 7.13 billion in 2025. The debt-to-equity ratio climbed from 69.4% in 2022 to 89.4% in 2025.

At the end of 2025, total equity was KRW 241.4 billion, of which KRW 237.6 billion belonged to controlling shareholders, and total liabilities were KRW 215.9 billion.

Operating cash flow was KRW 5.88 billion in 2022, KRW 14.6 billion in 2023, KRW 5.61 billion in 2024 and KRW 12 billion in 2025, rising in 2025 even as operating profit fell.

Quarterly, the company moved from an operating loss of KRW 158 million in the third quarter of 2025 to operating profit of KRW 566 million in the fourth quarter (a swing to profit), then to an operating loss of KRW 7.37 billion in the first quarter of 2026 (a swing to loss), an operating loss of KRW 6.77 billion in the second quarter (loss continued, but narrowed), and operating profit of KRW 5.23 billion in the third quarter (a swing back to profit).

Revenue was KRW 65.1 billion in the first quarter of 2026, KRW 65.1 billion in the second (up 0.1% quarter on quarter), and KRW 80.3 billion in the third (up 23.3%), rising for two consecutive quarters.

Controlling shareholders' net income swung to KRW 16.8 billion in the first quarter of 2026, a period in which about KRW 24.5 billion of dividend income was reflected.

Net income attributable to controlling shareholders over the last four quarters (fourth quarter of 2025 to third quarter of 2026) totaled KRW 12.3 billion.

05

Industry analysis

Earnings diverged sharply even within the pharmaceutical and biotech sector. According to a 2026 first-half analysis, new-drug developers continued to post losses under the weight of R&D and overseas expansion costs.

Among traditional pharma companies, Hanmi Pharm grew operating profit faster than revenue, whereas Ilyang Pharmaceutical belonged to a group that, together with Shinpoong Pharm, Jeil Pharmaceutical and Chong Kun Dang Bio, moved from operating profit to operating loss in the first half.

The vaccine business depends heavily on government National Immunization Program (NIP) procurement volumes. Ilyang's NIP influenza vaccine supply contract fell 32% from KRW 20.9 billion in the 2024–2025 season to KRW 14.3 billion in the 2025–2026 season.

In the 2026–2027 season competitive bidding, the company bid for 1.5 million doses but was allocated 1.05 million, and its unit bid price was the highest and its allocated volume the smallest among the six winning bidders.

Some view vaccines as only about 5% of NIP contract value and therefore a limited impact, but the KRW 30 billion investment in the vaccine finishing line exceeds 20% of market capitalization and has been cited as a main driver of higher borrowings.

On inventory, the first-half 2026 inventory valuation loss of KRW 3.34 billion equaled 24% of the operating loss. Noltec remains the core earnings source in prescription drugs, so its prescription share is the main measure of the company's position against industry conditions. In China, delayed marketing approval for Supect has pushed back the timing of commercialization.

06

Outlook

A company official said the company spent a difficult period because of delayed commercialization of its Chinese drug business and regulatory uncertainty, but expects steady growth in 2027 once one-time costs are resolved and the business transition is complete.

For that expectation to show up in results, the operating margin needs to stay positive quarter after quarter.

On vaccines, the finishing line is in equipment stabilization and the company is pursuing GMP approval, with trial production of influenza vaccines under way, and approval will be a turning point for production capacity and cost structure.

In products, Noltec Plus Mini tablets obtained approval, laying the groundwork to widen the lineup in digestive disorders such as gastroesophageal reflux disease.

In China, the company withdrew the new drug application for Supect and plans to re-file after selecting a new contract research organization, though approval delays and additional costs are expected.

Under its corporate value enhancement plan, the company has pursued governance steps such as ending overlapping directorships with affiliates, setting up ethics and executive compensation committees, and strengthening the audit committee.

The change in the largest shareholder was disclosed on July 30, 2026, and CEO Jung Yu-seok now holds the largest shareholder position alongside management control. A single-sales and supply contract termination was disclosed on September 9, 2026, and its revenue impact is a point to confirm in the next periodic report.

08

Bull factors

Third-quarter profit swing and revenue growth

Third-quarter 2026 revenue rose 16.7% year on year to KRW 80.3 billion, and operating profit of KRW 5.23 billion marked a swing to profit. Revenue has risen for two consecutive quarters since the first-quarter 2026 low. Quarterly confirmation of the operating turnaround is the main basis for this case.

Noltec and export-led earnings base

In cumulative revenue for the first three quarters of 2026, the prescription drug segment contributed KRW 87.1 billion, or 41.37%, and exports rose 16.4% to KRW 42.8 billion. Approval of Noltec Plus Mini tablets lays groundwork for a broader digestive-disease lineup. Growth in the core product and exports together forms the axis of the revenue recovery.

Joint-venture unwinding and better cash generation

Dividends and compensation from the Chinese joint venture supported net income in the first half of 2026, and operating cash flow rose to KRW 12 billion in 2025 from KRW 5.61 billion in 2024.

The company is unwinding its joint-venture-centered structure by transferring its Tongwha Ilyang stake and acquiring trademarks and rights related to Wonbidi. Completing this process would reduce structural uncertainty in the business.

Bear factors

Weakening core profitability

The operating margin fell from 6.1% in 2023 to 1.8% in 2025, and operating profit of KRW 4.96 billion in 2025 was down 54.9%. The operating loss in the first half of 2026 was KRW 14.1 billion, with the cost of sales ratio rising from 55.6% to 62.5% and selling, general and administrative expenses up 24.7%. The bear case rests on revenue growth failing to offset cost increases.

Net income leaning on non-recurring gains

In the first half of 2026, dividend income of KRW 26.9 billion made up most of KRW 27.5 billion in other gains, and net income of KRW 14.6 billion came despite an operating loss of KRW 14.1 billion. Cumulative net income through the third quarter also reflects dividends and compensation from the Chinese subsidiary. It is hard to attribute net income to a core business recovery.

Financial burden and pipeline delays

The debt-to-equity ratio rose from 69.4% in 2022 to 89.4% in 2025, and the current ratio stood at 90.3% in the third-quarter 2026 report. The KRW 30 billion vaccine finishing-line investment has been cited as a main reason for higher borrowings. In China, Supect's marketing application was withdrawn, pushing the approval timeline back.

Risk factors

Accounting and governance sanctions

Trading was suspended from September 10, 2025, and in November 2025 the Financial Services Commission imposed a total fine of KRW 7.49 billion, including a company fine of KRW 6.23 billion, along with a recommendation to dismiss co-CEOs, a six-month suspension of duties and a three-year designation of auditors.

The largest shareholder changed in July 2026. Whether internal control remediation takes hold is a follow-up item.

Vaccine volumes and inventory losses

The NIP influenza vaccine contract fell to KRW 14.3 billion in the 2025–2026 season, and the allocation for 2026–2027 was 1.05 million doses. The inventory valuation allowance rose to KRW 9.02 billion by the end of June 2026. If procurement volumes do not recover, inventory losses could recur.

Contract termination and legal costs

A single-sales and supply contract termination was disclosed on September 9, 2026, and its size affects the revenue base. Commission expenses, including legal advisory costs tied to the Chinese joint venture dispute, rose from KRW 3.9 billion in the first half of 2025 to KRW 10.4 billion in the first half of 2026.

Legal costs look largely one-off as the joint venture is unwound, but the termination could still reduce revenue.

12

Overall view

Ilyang Pharmaceutical posted quarterly revenue of KRW 80.3 billion and operating profit of KRW 5.23 billion in the third quarter of 2026, a swing to profit, and revenue has risen for two consecutive quarters.

Yet the cumulative operating loss for the first three quarters of 2026 remains at KRW 8.9 billion, and the operating margin fell to 1.8% in 2025. One-time items such as Chinese joint-venture dividends and compensation contributed significantly to the net income recovery.

In vaccines, lower NIP volumes and inventory losses are weighing on results, and GMP approval for the finishing line is the next variable. The re-filing timeline for Supect in China and the prescription growth of Noltec are the two axes for improving the earnings structure.

On the financial side, the debt-to-equity ratio rose to 89.4%, and the accounting sanctions and governance changes of 2025 continue to shape the story. The next checkpoints are the full-year operating margin in the 2026 annual report and whether operating profit stays positive in the first quarter of 2027.
