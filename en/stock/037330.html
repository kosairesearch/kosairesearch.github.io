Operating Profit Recovers, Net Income Swings at Inzi Display

02

Business structure

Inzi Display Company operates a display division and an automotive division. The display division makes TOP and BTM chassis components used in TV panel modules, while the automotive division produces aluminum die-casting parts for engines and transmissions.

The company listed on the KOSDAQ market in 1999 and holds six unlisted subsidiaries. Since 2003 it has pursued global operations, setting up local subsidiaries in China, the Americas and Vietnam to broaden its production network. Display sales fell in 2025 as demand for LCD panels softened.

Meanwhile, sales of eco-friendly vehicle parts built on high-precision casting technology grew, improving overall profitability.

The company is developing next-generation display products such as OLED, flexible displays and Micro LED, while expanding its EV battery parts business and increasing the weight of automotive parts.

The company attributed the main driver of the 2025 operating profit increase to better profitability at subsidiaries, so the earnings of overseas subsidiaries act as a key swing factor for consolidated results.

The display business is tied to panel makers' utilization rates and investment decisions, making the panel industry cycle the central variable. The automotive business depends on automaker demand and the pace of eco-friendly vehicle adoption.

04

Earnings analysis

On a reported basis, annual revenue fell from KRW 701 billion in 2022 to KRW 687.3 billion in 2023 (down 2.0% year on year), then rose to KRW 732.7 billion in 2024 (up 6.6%), the largest year in the 2022–2025 period, before easing to KRW 721.8 billion in 2025 (down 1.5%).

Operating profit dropped from KRW 11.4 billion in 2022 to KRW 5.26 billion in 2023 (down 53.8%), the smallest year, then rose to KRW 10.9 billion in 2024 (up 107.9%) and KRW 17.1 billion in 2025 (up 56.5%), marking two consecutive years of growth.

The operating margin went from 1.6% in 2022 to 0.8% in 2023, 1.5% in 2024 and 2.4% in 2025, the highest of the four years.

Net income attributable to controlling shareholders swung from a loss of KRW 1.33 billion in 2022 to KRW 9.79 billion in 2023 (a turn to profit), a loss of KRW 2.47 billion in 2024 (a turn to loss) and KRW 6.04 billion in 2025 (a turn to profit).

Net income including non-controlling interests, which is a different figure, was KRW 11.3 billion in 2023, a loss of KRW 1.26 billion in 2024 and KRW 9.75 billion in 2025.

The debt ratio declined from 140.5% in 2022 to 125.9% in 2025, and at the end of 2025 total equity stood at KRW 213.4 billion, of which equity attributable to controlling shareholders was KRW 183.5 billion. Operating cash flow fell from KRW 39.6 billion in 2024 to KRW 21.6 billion in 2025.

The quarterly pattern is more volatile. After revenue of KRW 191.2 billion and operating profit of KRW 5.8 billion in the third quarter of 2025, the fourth quarter brought revenue of KRW 158.5 billion (down 17.1% from the prior quarter) and an operating loss of KRW 20.82 million, a swing into deficit.

The first quarter of 2026 returned to operating profit of KRW 386 million, but the second quarter posted an operating loss of KRW 2.11 billion.

In the third quarter of 2026, revenue was KRW 187.7 billion (up 14.5% from the prior quarter, down 1.8% year on year) and operating profit was KRW 1.61 billion (a turn to profit from the prior quarter, down 72.2% year on year).

The net loss attributable to controlling shareholders of KRW 18.1 billion in that quarter was a swing into deficit from a profit a year earlier. The gap between operating profit and this net loss is large, which points to a meaningful impact from non-operating items or taxes below the operating line.

Over the last four quarters, from the fourth quarter of 2025 to the third quarter of 2026, the cumulative net loss attributable to controlling shareholders totaled KRW 15.9 billion.

05

Industry analysis

The display division of Inzi Display is directly tied to the panel industry cycle. Large-scale production in China has kept LCD panels oversupplied, while OLED is replacing LCD in the market.

LG Display reported third-quarter 2026 preliminary results on October 22, 2026, with revenue of KRW 6.277 trillion and operating profit of KRW 250.6 billion, and operating profit fell 42% year on year.

The same company said OLED's share of total revenue reached a quarterly high, while noting that IT LCD demand is slowing and competition is intensifying, which makes cost competitiveness critical. LG Display expects fourth-quarter 2026 shipment area to rise about 10% from the prior quarter, citing seasonal peak demand.

On the automotive side, LG Display said automotive makes up roughly 10% of its revenue and that automaker demand recovery is slow while competition among display makers is intensifying.

Inzi Display's automotive business connects to demand for eco-friendly vehicle parts made through aluminum die-casting, and the expansion of electric and hybrid vehicles is cited as the main market trend.

In its competitive position, Inzi Display operates in the narrow niche of TV panel module components, so the production allocation decisions of panel makers largely determine its volumes. When utilization falls in the panel industry, component suppliers generally have less pricing power.

Outlook

The nearest scheduled disclosure is the 2026 annual report.

In the value-up plan disclosed on March 31, 2026, the company set out a policy of keeping the dividend payout ratio at 30% or higher, strengthening overseas subsidiary profitability, improving the timeliness of receivables collection, liquidating low-profit sites, focusing on high-profit core businesses and cutting unnecessary costs.

The payout ratio was 25.45% in 2025, and the 2025 dividend amount was KRW 1.5 billion, up 52.36% from 2024. Because strengthening overseas subsidiaries is the same lever the company cited for the 2025 operating profit increase, it will serve as a gauge of the earnings direction.

A share consolidation with a record date of October 13, 2026 changed the par value from KRW 500 to KRW 2,500, and the new shares were re-listed on November 6, 2026. The reduced share count makes direct comparisons of per-share figures with earlier periods harder.

In the automotive division, expanding eco-friendly vehicle parts sales is presented as the axis of earnings recovery, but the disclosed material on order volumes and capacity expansion is limited.

The expansion of the EV battery parts business is also a stated direction, and its specific timing and scale should be confirmed in the report. In the display division, volumes are likely to move with LG Display's outlook for higher fourth-quarter shipments and panel industry utilization rates.

Bull factors

Operating profit recovery

Operating profit reached KRW 17.1 billion in 2025, up 56.5% from the prior year, and rose for two consecutive years from 2024. The operating margin climbed from 1.6% in 2022, 0.8% in 2023 and 1.5% in 2024 to 2.4% in 2025, the highest of the four years. Growth in eco-friendly vehicle parts sales was cited as a driver of the profitability improvement.

Stronger shareholder return policy

The value-up plan disclosed on March 31, 2026 includes keeping the dividend payout ratio at 30% or higher. The 2025 payout ratio of 25.45% is below this target, so how close it moves to the target is the next point to check.

The company also outlined plans to raise overseas subsidiary profitability through closing low-profit sites and cutting costs.

Improving balance sheet

The debt ratio declined from 140.5% in 2022 to 125.9% in 2025. Total equity at the end of 2025 was KRW 213.4 billion, of which KRW 183.5 billion was attributable to controlling shareholders. Expanding demand for eco-friendly automotive parts is cited as a medium-term growth axis.

Bear factors

Third-quarter 2026 net loss

The net loss attributable to controlling shareholders in the third quarter of 2026 was KRW 18.1 billion, a swing into deficit from a year earlier. Operating profit in the same quarter was KRW 1.61 billion, down 72.2% year on year.

The cumulative net loss attributable to controlling shareholders over the last four quarters totaled KRW 15.9 billion, and no PER can be calculated for that period.

Weak display industry conditions

Company commentary attributes the weakening of the display business to LCD panel oversupply and OLED substitution of LCD in the market. In the same period, LG Display's third-quarter 2026 operating profit of KRW 250.6 billion fell 42% year on year. Industry-wide margin pressure is also reaching the display division of Inzi Display.

Cash flow and quarterly volatility

Fourth-quarter 2025 revenue fell 17.1% from the prior quarter and turned into an operating loss, and the second quarter of 2026 also posted an operating loss of KRW 2.11 billion. Frequent reversals in quarterly results are a weakness.

Risk factors

Industry cycle risk

If LCD panel oversupply persists, volumes and prices in the display division could both come under pressure. Faster shrinkage of the LCD market from large-scale production in China and the OLED shift could repeat the decline in display sales seen in 2025. Whether the automotive division can offset this is the key question.

Earnings stability risk

The third-quarter 2026 net loss attributable to controlling shareholders of KRW 18.1 billion is far larger than operating profit of KRW 1.61 billion, so the interpretation of earnings power depends on whether non-operating or tax-related one-off items are included.

The cumulative four-quarter loss of KRW 15.9 billion shows that earnings stability has not yet been established. Item-level details should be checked in the notes to the 2026 annual report.

Customer demand and automotive recovery pace

LG Display said automaker demand recovery has been slow and competition among display makers is intensifying. If growth in automotive parts sales does not continue, the thin margin in the low-2% operating margin range could come under pressure again.

The decline in operating cash flow from KRW 39.6 billion in 2024 to KRW 21.6 billion in 2025 also warrants attention.

Overall view

Inzi Display's operating margin ranged from 0.8% to 2.4% in the reported 2022–2025 period, which is low, but operating profit rose for two consecutive years from 2024 and the debt ratio declined, showing signs of a stronger business base.

By contrast, quarterly results in 2026 moved from a first-quarter profit to a second-quarter loss and then a third-quarter net loss attributable to controlling shareholders of KRW 18.1 billion, so the direction has changed often.

The cumulative net loss attributable to controlling shareholders over the last four quarters was KRW 15.9 billion, so no PER can be calculated.

The display industry is not easily turning around given the combination of Chinese oversupply and the OLED shift, while the automotive parts business has the strength of growing eco-friendly vehicle sales.

On shareholder returns, the plan to keep the payout ratio at 30% or higher and the increase in the 2025 dividend amount are confirmed facts. Because per-share figures changed after the share consolidation, comparisons with earlier figures must account for the consolidation ratio.

The next checkpoints are the fourth-quarter 2026 preliminary results and the 2026 annual report, and the key question is whether the items that separated operating profit from the net loss were one-off in nature.
