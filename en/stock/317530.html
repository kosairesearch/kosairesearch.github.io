Report overview

Revenue surges as losses persist during a shift toward commerce and content

Episode Company's quarterly revenue has climbed above KRW 15.3 billion since early 2026, yet operating losses and cash outflows continue as it reshapes its business around commerce and content.

  1. 1

    Revenue reached KRW 15.3 billion in Q1 2026, KRW 19.4 billion in Q2 and KRW 19.7 billion in Q3, rising for three straight quarters from the Q4 2025 trough of KRW 1.12 billion.

  2. 2

    Operating losses persist. The operating loss widened to KRW 3.1 billion in Q2 2026 and was KRW 1.92 billion in Q3. The company posted losses for four consecutive years from 2022 to 2025.

  3. 3

    Content and commerce form the core of revenue. Media reports put first-half 2026 content revenue at KRW 16.9 billion and commerce revenue at KRW 13.8 billion, and the company plans to raise the commerce share to 47% in 2027.

  4. 4

    Cash has fallen quickly. Cash and equivalents dropped from KRW 9.5 billion at the end of 2025 to KRW 1.9 billion at the end of June 2026, and a KRW 16.0 billion rights offering was paid in November 2026.

  5. 5

    The KRW 4.86 billion net profit attributable to controlling shareholders in Q3 2026 came with a one-time disposal gain reported in the quarterly filing, so it should be read separately from operating performance.

02

Business structure

Episode Company was renamed from Carrysoft, a children's content producer, in March 2026. It is widening its business from character-based early childhood content into a diversified IP company spanning films, dramas, web content, commerce and actor management.

Since Q1 2026 the company has reported revenue in three segments: content, commerce, and IP-related business. Media reports put Q1 2026 consolidated revenue of KRW 15.3 billion at roughly 64% content, about KRW 9.7 billion, and roughly 36% commerce, about KRW 5.5 billion.

The same reports said IP-related business grew 133% year on year. For the first half of 2026, reported content revenue was KRW 16.9 billion (49%) and commerce revenue was KRW 13.8 billion (40%).

Content growth reflects the consolidation of a web-content production subsidiary acquired in January 2026 and a drama production and actor management subsidiary acquired in February 2026.

On the film side, the company is the distributor of Dora, which was invited to the Directors' Fortnight at the Cannes Film Festival and is being prepared for release in 2027.

Commerce consists of supplying its character IP products to Daiso and other large domestic retail channels, media commerce linked to celebrity channels, and overseas cross-border sales through platforms such as Shopee and Lazada. Its main customers are domestic retail channels and overseas online shoppers.

On the content side, the company competes with drama and film producers and web-content studios, while in commerce the key is securing IP-backed brands and distribution channels.

04

Earnings analysis

On an annual basis, revenue fell from KRW 10.1 billion in 2022 to KRW 8.48 billion in 2023 (down 15.9% year on year) and KRW 5.68 billion in 2024 (down 33.1%), then was flat at KRW 5.68 billion in 2025 (up 0.1%). 2022 was the largest year and 2024 the smallest within 2022 to 2025.

The operating loss widened from KRW 2.09 billion in 2022 to KRW 3.68 billion in 2023, then narrowed to KRW 3.61 billion in 2024 and KRW 3.56 billion in 2025. The operating margin moved from -20.7% in 2022 to -43.3% in 2023 and -63.6% in 2024, before improving slightly to -62.6% in 2025.

The net loss attributable to controlling shareholders was KRW 1.15 billion in 2022, KRW 5.49 billion in 2023, KRW 4.69 billion in 2024 and KRW 6.09 billion in 2025, while the net loss including non-controlling interests was KRW 6.32 billion in 2025.

Operating cash outflow grew from KRW 1.12 billion in 2022 to KRW 9.01 billion in 2025. At the end of 2025, total equity was KRW 21.3 billion, of which KRW 21.6 billion was attributable to controlling shareholders, total liabilities were KRW 6.89 billion, and the debt-to-equity ratio was 32.4%.

Quarterly revenue rose from KRW 1.23 billion in Q3 2025 and KRW 1.12 billion in Q4 2025 to KRW 15.3 billion in Q1 2026 (up 1,261.3% from the prior quarter). It then reached KRW 19.4 billion in Q2 (up 26.9%) and KRW 19.7 billion in Q3 (up 1.6% quarter on quarter and up 1,508.5% year on year).

Over the same period the operating loss was KRW 180 million in Q1, KRW 3.1 billion in Q2 and KRW 1.92 billion in Q3. The company attributed the wider Q2 loss to up-front new business setup costs and content investment and production expenses.

The Q3 swing to a net profit attributable to controlling shareholders, KRW 4.86 billion, was reported to reflect a KRW 6.3 billion gain on disposal of stakes in Peterpan Lab and Episode Media on a cumulative third-quarter basis. The net loss attributable to controlling shareholders for the last four quarters totals KRW 1.92 billion.

05

Industry analysis

The children's content market faces structural limits as low birth rates shrink its core audience, and the company cites this as the reason for expanding into films, dramas, sports and commerce.

In film, an animated title produced and distributed with China's state-owned Baoli Group opened nationwide in China in March 2026, marking an early overseas IP expansion.

The China market was previously constrained by the Hallyu restrictions, but reports say easing in international relations has improved visibility for results. In Southeast Asia, Vietnam's film market grew as audiences reached roughly 70 million in 2025, up 32% from the prior year.

The company also entered short-form drama with a vertical series. It has said it will apply AI across the content production process to shorten schedules and cut costs.

Competitively, Episode Company operates in the same arena as drama producers such as CJ ENM and Studio Dragon, but its consolidated revenue base is much smaller. In commerce, its media-commerce model, which converts celebrity audience traffic into product sales, is presented as its main point of differentiation.

06

Outlook

In its September 30, 2026 corporate value enhancement plan, the company set a target of KRW 110 billion in consolidated revenue for 2027. It plans to raise commerce revenue from an expected KRW 29.9 billion in 2026 to KRW 51.1 billion in 2027, lifting its share of total revenue to 47%, and to exceed 50% in 2028.

Commerce expansion rests on large domestic retail channels, overseas cross-border sales, and wider distribution for its own K-beauty brands.

On the content side, revenue from the drama series co-produced as Promoter is expected to be recognized from the second half of 2026, and the film Dora is being prepared for a 2027 release.

Of the KRW 16.0 billion rights offering completed in November 2026, KRW 14.0 billion came from a third-party allotment, and media reports said KRW 10.0 billion is earmarked for acquiring securities of other companies and KRW 4.0 billion for operating funds. New shares are scheduled to list on November 30, 2026.

On shareholder returns, the company said it is considering share buybacks as early as within 2026, and dividends subject to a return to profitability and distributable profit.

In sports, it is co-hosting the Korea-Japan Dream Players Game 2026 at Gocheok Sky Dome on December 5, 2026 with Rico Sports Agency, with ticket sales opening November 20, 2026.

The 2026 annual report will show the full-year operating margin and commerce share, and it will be the point at which progress toward the 2027 revenue target can be gauged.

08

Bull factors

Pace of revenue growth

Quarterly revenue rose to KRW 15.3 billion in Q1 2026, KRW 19.4 billion in Q2 and KRW 19.7 billion in Q3, a third straight quarterly gain. Quarterly sales now far exceed the full-year 2025 revenue of KRW 5.68 billion. Growth in both content and commerce supports the expansion. The company has set a 2027 consolidated revenue target of KRW 110 billion.

Rising commerce share

First-half 2026 commerce revenue of KRW 13.8 billion was reported to account for about 40% of consolidated revenue. In its September 2026 corporate value enhancement plan, the company said it would lift commerce revenue from an expected KRW 29.9 billion in 2026 to KRW 51.1 billion in 2027, raising the share to 47%. Whether its IP products and celebrity channels translate into wider distribution is the key question.

Strengthened funding base

The KRW 16.0 billion rights offering was paid in November 2026, with KRW 10.0 billion of the third-party allotment taken up by the company's largest shareholder. The largest shareholder changed as a result.

In March 2026 the company transferred KRW 18.8 billion from capital reserves to retained earnings to create a base for distributable profit, and it is considering share buybacks and dividends.

09

Bear factors

Four consecutive years of operating losses

The company posted losses for four consecutive years from 2022 to 2025, with an operating margin of -62.6% in 2025. The Q2 2026 operating loss widened to KRW 3.1 billion, and operating losses have run for five straight quarters.

The Q3 profit attributable to controlling shareholders was reported to reflect a KRW 6.3 billion disposal gain on a cumulative basis, while the operating loss in the same quarter was KRW 1.92 billion.

Cash drawdown and reliance on outside funding

Cash and equivalents fell from KRW 9.5 billion at the end of 2025 to KRW 1.9 billion at the end of June 2026. First-half 2026 operating cash flow was reported as a net outflow of KRW 3.5 billion. Operating cash outflow in 2025 was KRW 9.01 billion, larger than in the prior year.

The KRW 16.0 billion offering is earmarked for investments and acquisitions, separate from cash generated by operations.

Proof of business results still needed

Critics have said the outdoor sports partnership signed in April 2026 has so far produced little beyond event sponsorship and giveaways, and no launch timing has been set for academies or travel products. Content revenue can swing sharply with film and drama performance. The 2027 revenue target can only be checked against annual reports.

10

Risk factors

Earnings volatility

Quarterly revenue has swung widely, from KRW 1.12 billion in Q4 2025 to KRW 19.7 billion in Q3 2026. Much of the increase depends on consolidating acquired subsidiaries and on orders for specific IP products. Whether the consolidation effect is one-time or recurring is not yet clear from the filings.

Dilution and ownership change

The KRW 16.0 billion rights offering issued 4,861,108 new shares through a third-party allotment and 947,867 shares in the public offering. The change in largest shareholder could alter the ownership structure and business direction. Once the new shares are listed, the increase in tradable shares is a supply-side point to check.

Business concentration and overseas constraints

Commerce depends heavily on specific retail channels and purchase orders, including supply to a major variety-store chain. The China business has been constrained by past Hallyu restrictions.

Weak box office or drama results would delay returns on investment, and the performance of the large rights-offering proceeds is still uncertain.

12

Overall view

Episode Company's quarterly revenue jumped to KRW 15.3 billion, KRW 19.4 billion and KRW 19.7 billion in the first three quarters of 2026, rising for three straight quarters.

Media reports attribute this growth to consolidating a web-content studio and a drama production and management company, along with wider commerce sales.

On the other hand, the company posted operating losses for four consecutive years from 2022 to 2025, with operating losses of KRW 3.1 billion in Q2 2026 and KRW 1.92 billion in Q3. The Q3 profit attributable to controlling shareholders was reported to reflect a KRW 6.3 billion disposal gain.

Cash and equivalents fell to KRW 1.9 billion by the end of June 2026, and the KRW 16.0 billion rights offering has reinforced funding. Strengths include the larger revenue base, a rising commerce share and a stronger funding base.

Weaknesses include continuing operating losses, cash drawdown, and the need to prove business results. The next checkpoints are the listing of new shares and disclosure of fund use on November 30, 2026, the operating margin and commerce share in the 2026 annual report, and progress toward the 2027 revenue target.
