After Fine Provisioning, Profitability Recovery in Focus

02

Business structure

Sajodongaone is a flour- and feed-focused affiliate of SAJO Group, operating two main segments: a flour milling business producing wheat flour, and a biological resources business covering compound feed and pet food.

By product mix, the flour segment accounts for 62.73% of sales and the biological resources segment 37.27%, with flour remaining the core revenue source. Production facilities are split between Dangjin in South Chungcheong Province and Busan, with total capacity estimated at roughly 840,000 tonnes.

The domestic flour market is an oligopoly in which Sajodongaone, another leading domestic flour producer, and CJ Cheiljedang together hold more than 70% share, making stable customer relationships more important than price competition.

On the customer side, supplies to major instant-noodle makers such as Nongshim and Samyang Foods make up about 30% of total production, leaving the company sensitive to shifts in ramyeon demand.

The controlling shareholder base consists of SAJO Group affiliates, and the group, through companies such as Sajo Industries, has continued to purchase Sajodongaone shares in the open market.

The company also runs a pet food business under the 'Venty' brand, though its revenue contribution remains small relative to flour and biological resources. Headcount stood at about 381 as of end-June 2026.

Sajodaerim, Sajo Industries, Sajo SEA Food and Oyang share similar names but are separate listed companies with distinct financials and ownership structures.

04

Earnings analysis

Annual revenue rose from KRW 631.6 billion in 2022 to a peak of KRW 678.5 billion in 2023 (up 7.4% year over year), then declined for two straight years to KRW 678.1 billion in 2024 (down 0.1%) and KRW 674.3 billion in 2025 (down 0.6%).

Operating profit grew quickly from KRW 15.3 billion in 2022 to KRW 38.6 billion in 2023 (up 153.3%) and KRW 50 billion in 2024 (up 29.5%), before falling 15.1% to KRW 42.5 billion in 2025. The operating margin improved from 2.4% in 2022 to 7.4% in 2024 before slipping to 6.3% in 2025.

By contrast, net profit attributable to controlling shareholders rose steadily from 2022 to 2024 (KRW 1.04 billion to KRW 10.7 billion to KRW 24.3 billion) but swung to a net loss of KRW 137.3 billion in 2025, reflecting a non-operating provision tied to the flour price-fixing fine, even though operating profit itself stayed positive that year.

This provision pushed the year-end 2025 debt-to-equity ratio to 265.3%, sharply up from 68.2% a year earlier, and shrank total equity to KRW 127 billion. Because the provision was a non-cash item, operating cash flow actually improved to KRW 37.8 billion in 2025 from KRW 34.8 billion in 2024.

On a quarterly basis, revenue and operating profit peaked in Q3 2025 at KRW 182 billion and KRW 11.7 billion respectively, after which operating profit fell for three consecutive quarters through Q2 2026, reaching KRW 4.79 billion (down 57.0% year over year).

Net profit attributable to controlling shareholders swung from a KRW 164.2 billion loss in Q4 2025 upon finalization of the fine, to a KRW 4.91 billion profit in Q1 2026, and back to a KRW 13.1 billion loss in Q2 2026, with the trailing four quarters (Q3 2025–Q2 2026) accumulating a net loss of KRW 164.3 billion.

05

Industry analysis

The domestic flour industry has matured, with growth in domestic consumption stagnating, while weak consumer spending and rising imported flour volumes are pressuring both volumes and profitability. The Q1 2026 revenue decline was likewise attributed to slower domestic consumption and higher imports of flour.

On the other hand, the overseas expansion of Korean ramyeon exports by makers such as Nongshim and Samyang Foods is seen as a favorable demand driver for flour producers, and Sajodongaone is directly exposed to this trend given that supplies to these two customers make up roughly 30% of its total production.

On the supply side, one market research estimate noted that utilization at the Dangjin and Busan plants rose from about 70% in 2022 to roughly 76% in 2023, suggesting some spare capacity exists to meet additional ramyeon-driven demand.

However, the broader industry backdrop shifted in 2026 as the entire sector came under a sweeping antitrust penalty from the Korea Fair Trade Commission. The regulator fined seven flour makers, including Sajodongaone, a combined KRW 671.0 billion, the largest cartel penalty on record in Korea.

The seven sanctioned companies control 87.7% of the business-to-business flour market, and the KFTC also ordered each to independently reset prices within three months and report any subsequent price changes for three years.

This could reshape pricing practices and cost structures across the industry, making relative competitive positioning versus peers worth monitoring going forward.

06

Outlook

In the near term, a court's September 2026 decision to stay execution of the fine confirmed that the actual cash outflow has been deferred.

However, the underlying lawsuit disputing the fine's legality and calculation basis (including the scope of sales and the duration of the alleged cartel) remains unresolved, meaning the final financial burden could still change.

To address a penny-stock delisting rule that took effect in July 2026, the company completed a 10-for-1 par-value consolidation raising face value from KRW 500 to KRW 5,000; this reduces the number of shares outstanding to meet the rule but does not itself change corporate value or market capitalization.

In a June 2026 corporate governance report, the company stated it had not established a value-up plan over the prior three years and had no separate mid- to long-term shareholder-return policy, leaving capital-return direction such as dividends or buybacks undefined for now.

Cash and cash equivalents stood at about KRW 48.3 billion as of end-Q1 2026, making liquidity management an important variable should the fine eventually become payable.

On a standalone basis, H1 2026 revenue fell 2.2% year over year, operating profit fell 48.7%, and net income swung to a loss, confirming that core profitability weakened alongside the much larger consolidated loss.

The next scheduled disclosure is the Q3 2026 report, which will show whether the operating-profit decline continues and how any adjustments to the fine-related provision are reflected.

08

Bull factors

Structural Demand Tied to Ramyeon Export Growth

Supplies to major instant-noodle makers such as Nongshim and Samyang Foods account for roughly 30% of total production, creating a structural link between their global export growth and flour demand.

The domestic flour market remains an oligopoly with the top three players holding more than 70% share, limiting new-entrant threats.

Some analysis has noted utilization at the Dangjin and Busan plants rising from around 70% to about 76%, suggesting there is additional production capacity available should demand increase.

Stay of Execution Eases Near-Term Cash-Outflow Risk

A court's September 2026 decision to grant Sajodongaone's request for a stay of execution on the flour price-fixing fine pushed back the actual payment timeline for the roughly KRW 183 billion penalty. This eases immediate liquidity pressure.

However, the possibility remains that the stay could be reversed depending on the outcome of the underlying lawsuit, so this decision does not represent a final resolution of the fine issue.

Operating Cash Flow Stayed Positive Despite the Provision

Despite the large 2025 net loss attributable to controlling shareholders, operating cash flow rose to KRW 37.8 billion from KRW 34.8 billion a year earlier, because the fine-related provision was a non-cash accounting item.

Operating profit itself also stayed positive at KRW 42.5 billion in 2025, meaning the core business's cash-generating capacity was not impaired to the same degree as the headline net loss. Still, with quarterly operating profit slowing through 2026, whether this cash-flow pattern persists needs to be confirmed.

Bear factors

Three Straight Quarters of Operating-Profit Decline

Operating profit peaked at KRW 11.7 billion in Q3 2025 and then fell for three consecutive quarters through Q2 2026, reaching KRW 4.79 billion. The Q1 2026 slowdown was attributed to softer domestic consumption and rising imports of flour.

If this trend continues, the annual operating margin, which fell from 7.4% in 2024 to 6.3% in 2025, could decline further.

Weaker Balance Sheet and Shrinking Equity

The debt-to-equity ratio jumped to 265.3% at end-2025 from 68.2% a year earlier, and total equity shrank to KRW 127 billion, reflecting both the liability increase from the fine-related provision and the equity reduction from the net loss.

With cash and cash equivalents of only about KRW 48.3 billion at end-Q1 2026, financial flexibility could be constrained if the fine eventually has to be paid.

Absence of a Capital-Return Policy and Continued No Dividend

In a corporate governance report disclosed in June 2026, the company stated it had not drawn up a value-up plan over the prior three years and had no mid- to long-term shareholder-return policy. The dividend yield remains at 0%, with no concrete capital-allocation direction laid out.

The 10-for-1 par-value consolidation carried out in 2026 was primarily aimed at meeting listing-maintenance requirements and is unlikely to be viewed as resolving the underlying gap in capital-return policy.

Risk factors

Regulatory and Litigation Risk

The KRW 183.097 billion fine imposed by the Korea Fair Trade Commission in May 2026 equals 144.21% of the company's equity.

Although a court has stayed enforcement, the final burden could change depending on the outcome of the underlying lawsuit, which disputes the sales scope and cartel duration used to calculate the fine. Other flour makers are pursuing similar litigation, so the eventual rulings could affect the industry as a whole.

Liquidity and Financial Risk

Cash and cash equivalents of only about KRW 48.3 billion at end-Q1 2026 could strain liquidity management if large cash outflows such as the fine materialize. The debt-to-equity ratio rose sharply to 265.3% at end-2025 from the prior year. Any further one-off charges or provision adjustments could again pressure financial soundness indicators.

Industry and Competitive Risk

The domestic flour market has matured and consumption growth has stalled, while rising imports of flour pressure domestic producers' volumes and margins.

Under the KFTC's price-resetting order, the entire industry must report price changes for the next three years, which could reshape pricing practices and the competitive landscape. International grain price and foreign-exchange volatility also remain sources of cost-side uncertainty.

Overall view

Despite posting a positive operating profit in 2025, Sajodongaone recorded a large consolidated net loss due to a provision tied to the flour price-fixing fine, and heading into 2026 it has faced the added pressure of operating profit itself declining for three consecutive quarters.

A court's stay of execution has eased near-term cash-outflow risk, but the underlying lawsuit disputing the fine's legality and amount remains unresolved.

The sharp rise in the debt-to-equity ratio and thin cash balance are indicators that warrant attention from a financial-flexibility standpoint, and the absence of a mid- to long-term shareholder-return policy adds further uncertainty to capital policy.

On the other hand, the structural demand base tied to export growth at key customers such as Nongshim and Samyang Foods, and the positive operating cash flow sustained by the non-cash nature of the provision, serve as offsetting factors.

The 10-for-1 par-value consolidation carried out in 2026 was primarily a procedural response to listing-maintenance requirements and does not itself change corporate value or the earnings structure.

The upcoming quarterly report and the progress of the main lawsuit will be the key variables for gauging the company's future financial structure and capital-policy direction.
