Report overview

Collusion Fine Losses Test the Earnings Recovery

After booking a large net loss in 2025 due to a provision for a flour-price collusion fine from the Korea Fair Trade Commission, Sajo Dong-A One continued to face additional cost burdens into 2026, making the recovery of its operating margin the key issue to watch.

  3. 3

  4. 4

02

Business structure

Sajo Dong-A One traces its roots to Chosun Flour Mills, founded in 1953, and went through Dong-A Flour Mills and Dong-A One before joining the Sajo Group in 2016 and merging with Korea Flour Mills in 2017. The company operates across three segments: flour milling, biological resources (feed), and pet food.

By revenue, flour-related products account for roughly 63.3% and compound feed products for roughly 31.4%, with flour and feed merchandise sales adding a few percentage points each.

The flour business has diversified into premium, all-purpose, cake, and bread flours as well as organic, whole-wheat, domestic-wheat, and buckwheat flours, while the feed business supplies products for chickens, pigs, cattle, dogs, cats, and fish.

Pet food, launched under the "Venty" brand in 2020, is positioned as an emerging growth area tied to the expanding pet market.

Ownership is concentrated within the Sajo Group, with affiliates Sajo Seafood and Sajo Daerim each holding 26.03%, and the top five shareholders together controlling 64.88%, reflecting a vertically integrated structure that supplies flour to the group's other food affiliates.

The domestic flour and feed industries are generally viewed as mature, oligopolistic markets dominated by a handful of large players.

04

Earnings analysis

Consolidated revenue has stayed in a KRW 630-680 billion range for four straight years: KRW 631.6 billion in 2022, KRW 678.5 billion in 2023, KRW 678.1 billion in 2024, and KRW 674.3 billion in 2025.

Operating profit improved from KRW 15.3 billion (2.4% margin) in 2022 to KRW 38.6 billion (5.7%) in 2023 and KRW 50.0 billion (7.4%) in 2024, before slipping to KRW 42.5 billion (6.3%) in 2025. The bigger issue is the bottom line.

Net income attributable to owners was modestly positive from 2022 to 2024 at KRW 1.0 billion, KRW 10.7 billion, and KRW 24.3 billion respectively, but reversed to a large net loss of KRW 137.3 billion in 2025.

This stemmed from a provision booked ahead of a fine imposed by the Korea Fair Trade Commission for flour-price collusion, making it more of a one-off charge than a reflection of underlying operating deterioration.

On a quarterly basis, the company still posted profits of KRW 11.4 billion and KRW 8.1 billion through the third quarter of 2025, before a concentrated net loss of KRW 164.2 billion hit in the fourth quarter.

The first quarter of 2026 swung back to a KRW 4.9 billion profit, but the second quarter fell into another net loss of KRW 13.1 billion, suggesting fine-related costs are being recognized across multiple quarters.

Operating margin over the last five quarters has declined in sequence from 6.51% to 6.45%, 5.84%, 3.96%, and 2.84%, indicating that underlying profitability has softened independent of the one-off charge.

On a more positive note, operating cash flow remained positive at KRW 37.8 billion in 2025, showing the company's actual cash generation held up relatively well despite the accounting net loss.

05

Industry analysis

The domestic flour milling industry is considered a mature sector, with demand pressured by demographic shifts and dietary diversification while imported flour competition intensifies. The feed segment faces similar external pressure from rising imported meat volumes and periodic livestock disease outbreaks.

Because most raw materials are imported, international grain prices and exchange-rate swings directly affect costs across the industry. At the same time, the organic and functional flour market is emerging as a new growth driver, offering a potential differentiation opportunity for companies with premium product lines.

The domestic flour market remains an oligopoly dominated by a small number of large players, and Sajo Dong-A One's stable supply relationship with its group's food affiliates is one element of its competitive positioning.

The 2026 Korea Fair Trade Commission decision to fine seven flour milling companies for collusion illustrates intensifying regulatory scrutiny of industry pricing practices, which could influence pricing and volume policies across the sector going forward.

06

Outlook

The company showed signs of moving past the one-off shock from its collusion-fine provision with a return to profit in the first quarter of 2026, but the second-quarter net loss suggests full normalization will take more time.

Given the gap between the finally confirmed fine (KRW 183.0 billion) and the previously booked provision (KRW 167.2 billion), additional cost recognition in coming quarters cannot be ruled out.

The pet food business, cultivated as a new growth driver, continues to see revenue growth on the back of an expanding pet care market. Expansion of premium product lines such as organic and functional flour could also serve as a point of differentiation in an otherwise stagnant domestic flour market.

Financially, given the sharp reduction in equity and rise in the debt ratio following the 2025 loss, the pace of capital rebuilding through future earnings accumulation is a key point to monitor.

The company has stated it has not established a formal mid- to long-term shareholder return policy over the past three years, making any future value-up policy announcement another item worth watching.

With the 10-for-1 share consolidation completed in September 2026, changes in the number of shares in circulation and trading patterns following the consolidation also warrant attention.

08

Bull factors

Return to Profit in Q1 2026

In Q1 2026, net income attributable to controlling shareholders turned positive at KRW 4.9 billion, supporting the view that the large loss in Q4 2025 was close to a one-off factor.

Operating cash flow also remained positive at KRW 37.8 billion even in 2025, when a net loss occurred, indicating that actual cash-generating capacity was relatively solid. This suggests the need to separate the accounting shock from the fine-related provision from the cash flow of the core business.

Pet Food as a Growth Driver

Driven by growth in the pet market, the pet food business is being fostered as a new growth engine, and the expansion of premium product lines such as organic and functional flour is also cited as a differentiation opportunity in the stagnant domestic milling market.

The stable raw material supply structure to affiliated food companies within the group serves as a factor underpinning the stability of the revenue base.

Cost Visibility from Prior Provisioning

The fact that the company recognized a substantial portion of fine-related costs as a provision in 2025 in advance, from an accounting perspective, also suggests that the scope of additional future cost recognition may be limited. This can be interpreted as a factor that reduces earnings uncertainty to some extent.

Bear factors

Recurrence of Fine-Related Costs

In Q2 2026, the net loss attributable to controlling shareholders reached KRW 13.1 billion, showing that the return to profit in Q1 did not mean a complete normalization.

As the finally confirmed fine (KRW 183.0 billion) exceeded the previously recognized provision amount (KRW 167.2 billion), there remains the possibility that additional costs will continue to be recognized in a distributed manner going forward.

Declining Operating Margin Trend

Over the most recent five quarters, the operating margin has declined sequentially from 6.51% to 2.84%, confirming that, separate from one-off factors, the profitability of the core business itself is weakening.

The stagnation of the domestic flour and feed markets and intensifying competition from imported products are cited as background factors.

Deterioration in Financial Health

Equity attributable to controlling shareholders fell by more than half, from KRW 270.7 billion in 2024 to KRW 127.0 billion in 2025, while the debt ratio rose sharply from 68.2% to 265.3%. If the pace of capital replenishment is slow, it could take considerable time to improve the financial structure.

10

Risk factors

Regulatory and Litigation Risk

In May 2026, the Korea Fair Trade Commission imposed fines on seven flour milling companies, including Sajo Dongah One, for alleged collusion on flour prices and volumes, and the fine imposed on Sajo Dongah One, at KRW 183.0 billion, was the largest among the companies caught.

If the company files an administrative lawsuit, the size of the provision could be readjusted depending on the outcome, leaving room for further financial changes.

Raw Material and Foreign Exchange Risk

Given the structure in which most raw materials are imported from overseas, fluctuations in international grain prices and exchange rates directly affect costs. Increases in imported meat and the occurrence of livestock diseases can also act as burdens on the profitability of the bioresources segment.

Governance and Shareholder Return Risk

The company has disclosed that it has not established a corporate value enhancement plan or held related board meetings over the past three years, and that it has not separately established a formalized mid- to long-term shareholder return policy, including dividends.

It is also worth monitoring how the change in the number of outstanding shares following the 10-to-1 reverse stock split, completed in September 2026, affects trading liquidity.

12

Overall view

Sajo Dong-A One has maintained a stable revenue base centered on its flour milling and biological resources (feed) businesses, but its financial structure was significantly shaken in 2025 by a large net loss tied to a provision for the Korea Fair Trade Commission's collusion fine.

Despite returning to profit in the first quarter of 2026, another net loss in the second quarter shows the cost burden has not fully cleared.

The consecutive decline in operating margin over the last five quarters suggests possible softening in core profitability separate from the one-off charge, while the sharp drop in owners' equity and rise in the debt ratio are headwinds for financial health.

On the other hand, growth in the pet food business, expansion of premium product lines such as organic and functional flour, and resilient operating cash flow stand out as positive factors.

Going forward, third-quarter results, developments in any administrative litigation over the fine, the pace of capital rebuilding, and whether a shareholder return policy is established will be key variables in gauging whether earnings power normalizes.
