Report overview

Milling Recovery Is the Key Test Amid Losses

Sajodongaone swung to a net loss attributable to owners of the parent of KRW 137.3 billion in 2025, and operating profit has fallen for four straight quarters from Q4 2025 through Q3 2026. Whether its flour milling business recovers is the next key test, given the fine and balance-sheet pressure.

  2. 2

02

Business structure

Sajodongaone was founded in 1972 to sell compound feed and later expanded into flour milling through a 2008 merger with another flour miller, operating within the Sajo Group. Its business has three segments: milling, bio-resources and pet food.

The milling unit sells wheat flour under the Maekseon brand, mainly to ramen makers, while bio-resources supplies compound feed for livestock and farmed fish. Pet food covers companion-animal feed. At year-end 2022 the product mix was roughly 82% milling and 18.3% bio-resources.

At year-end 2025, all four sales categories covering products and goods were at least 98% domestic, so overseas exposure is small. On customers, a 2025 press report said Nongshim and Samyang Foods together account for about 30% of sales.

Milling capacity totals 840,000 tonnes across the Busan plant (384,300 tonnes) and the Dangjin plant (460,428 tonnes), and the utilization rate was 77% in 2024, up 7 percentage points. Flour sales reached about 640,000 tonnes in 2024, up 8% year on year. CJ Cheiljedang and Samyang are cited as competitors.

A 2023 company IR document said pet food sales have grown each year with the expansion of the companion-animal market.

04

Earnings analysis

Annual revenue rose from KRW 631.6 billion in 2022 to KRW 678.5 billion in 2023, a 7.4% increase, before declining 0.1% to KRW 678.1 billion in 2024 and 0.6% to KRW 674.3 billion in 2025. Revenue fell for two consecutive years after 2023, which was the largest year of 2022 to 2025.

Operating profit climbed from KRW 15.3 billion in 2022 to KRW 38.6 billion in 2023, a 153.3% increase, and to KRW 50 billion in 2024, up 29.5%, which was the largest year in the period. It then fell 15.1% to KRW 42.5 billion in 2025.

The operating margin rose from 2.4% in 2022 to 5.7% in 2023 and 7.4% in 2024, then eased to 6.3% in 2025.

Net income attributable to owners of the parent grew from KRW 10.7 billion in 2023, up 937.2%, to KRW 24.3 billion in 2024, up 125.9%, before the company swung to a net loss attributable to owners of the parent of KRW 137.3 billion in 2025.

Net loss including non-controlling interests was also KRW 137.3 billion in 2025. With operating profit positive, the swing to loss implies large losses below the operating line, and the breakdown is a point to check in the notes to the annual report.

Quarterly, Q3 2025 operating profit was KRW 11.7 billion with attributable net income of KRW 8.09 billion. In Q4 2025 operating profit fell 19.9% to KRW 9.39 billion and the attributable net loss widened to KRW 164.2 billion.

Q1 2026 revenue of KRW 155.4 billion was the lowest of the last five quarters, with operating profit of KRW 6.16 billion. Q2 2026 revenue rose 8.4% to KRW 168.5 billion, but operating profit fell to KRW 4.79 billion and an attributable net loss of KRW 13.1 billion emerged.

Q3 2026 revenue rose 3.9% to KRW 175.1 billion, yet operating profit was KRW 2.43 billion and the attributable net loss was KRW 3.59 billion. The attributable net loss for the last four quarters totals KRW 176 billion.

05

Industry analysis

Flour milling is a mature market, and domestic consumption has stalled amid weak economic conditions and rising imports of flour. The company's Q1 2026 earnings commentary also pointed to a domestic slowdown and imported flour as causes of weaker sales.

Because wheat is imported, global grain prices and exchange rates directly affect profitability. The bio-resources unit is affected by rising imported meat and livestock disease, according to the same commentary. Organic and functional flour is cited as a new growth factor.

In ramen, a 2025 press report said expanding plants at Samyang Foods and Nongshim were expected to lift flour demand. Within a nine-company feed sector comparison, the 2025 full-year operating margin of 6.30% was above the sector average of 1.22%.

In the same comparison the debt ratio of 265.25% was higher than the sector average of 147.89%, so financial leverage is relatively high. CJ Cheiljedang and Samyang are cited as domestic competitors.

Spare milling capacity and a ramen customer base are strengths, but earnings depend on imported raw material prices and consumption trends, a challenge common across the industry.

Outlook

Revenue has risen for two consecutive quarters since Q2 2026, but there is not yet evidence that this has translated into profit recovery.

Commentary on the milling unit continues to point to stagnant consumption and rising imported flour, so whether the sales rebound converts into better profitability is the key test for upcoming quarters.

The Korea Fair Trade Commission's KRW 183 billion penalty was reported in May 2026, and the payment schedule and funding method need to be checked in company disclosures. With cash and cash equivalents of KRW 48.3 billion at the end of Q1 2026, operating cash generation is seen as the main variable for liquidity.

In its governance report issued in June 2026, the company said it had not formulated a value-enhancement plan or held related board meetings in the past three years.

The same report said it does not have a formal medium-to-long-term shareholder return policy, and that it plans to establish a stable dividend policy after reviewing business conditions and financial resources. At the March 2026 shareholder meeting, the cash dividend proposal was approved as no dividend.

On governance, the largest shareholder changed from Sajodaerim to Sajo SEA Food in December 2024, and affiliates of the group have reportedly kept buying shares on the open market.

08

Bull factors

Track record of margin recovery

The operating margin rose from 2.4% in 2022 to 7.4% in 2024, showing the core business has improved its profitability before. It held at 6.3% in 2025, above the 1.22% average of nine feed-sector companies. If this margin base holds, it could be a starting point for profit improvement when milling demand recovers.

Customer base tied to ramen demand

A 2025 press report said Nongshim and Samyang Foods together account for about 30% of sales. Flour sales rose 8% to about 640,000 tonnes in 2024, reflecting how ramen makers' output expansion fed through to volumes.

Because customer production plans link directly to sales volumes, demand changes can show up quickly in results.

Spare milling capacity

The milling utilization rate was 77% in 2024, up 7 percentage points. A press report calculated spare capacity of about 190,000 tonnes without additional equipment investment. Revenue growth for two straight quarters since Q2 2026 can be read as a sign that this spare capacity may be put to use.

Bear factors

Continued weakening of earnings

Operating profit has declined for four straight quarters from Q4 2025 through Q3 2026. Q3 2026 operating profit was KRW 2.43 billion, down 79.2% year on year. Cumulative operating profit for January to September 2026 was KRW 13.4 billion, down 59.5%, according to a press report citing the quarterly filing.

Penalty and financial burden

The Korea Fair Trade Commission imposed a KRW 183 billion penalty for flour collusion in May 2026, the largest among the sanctioned companies. Cash and cash equivalents were KRW 48.3 billion at the end of Q1 2026.

The debt ratio was 265.3% at the end of 2025, with total liabilities of KRW 336.8 billion well above total equity of KRW 127 billion.

Mature market and raw material swings

Milling and bio-resources are described as mature markets where consumption has stalled amid weak demand and rising imported flour. Reliance on imported wheat means global grain prices and exchange rates can swing profitability. Q1 2026 results were attributed to a domestic slowdown and imported flour, which reduced sales.

Risk factors

Regulatory and legal risk

The KRW 183 billion penalty is a direct financial burden, and its payment timing and funding method could pressure cash flow. Collusion cases may lead to follow-up proceedings, so contingent-liability notes need to be reviewed as well. How the penalty is reflected in the financial statements should be confirmed in the periodic reports.

Liquidity and capital structure

The debt ratio was 265.3% at the end of 2025, and cash and cash equivalents were KRW 48.3 billion at the end of Q1 2026. If earnings keep falling, borrowing capacity and interest burden could come into sharper focus.

Operating cash flow stayed positive at KRW 37.8 billion in 2025, but that cushion would thin if falling profits coincide with large outlays.

Listing maintenance and shareholder returns

The 10-for-1 share consolidation decided in June 2026 was read by the market as a response to penny-stock listing rules. The company has no codified medium-to-long-term return policy, and the cash dividend proposal was approved as no dividend at the March 2026 meeting. Market commentary has called for a fundamental value-enhancement plan.

Overall view

Sajodongaone's revenue has declined for two consecutive years since peaking in 2023, and operating profit fell from KRW 50 billion in 2024 to KRW 42.5 billion in 2025. After swinging to an attributable net loss of KRW 137.3 billion in 2025, operating profit has fallen for four straight quarters through Q3 2026.

Revenue has risen for two quarters since Q2 2026, but profit recovery has not yet appeared. The KRW 183 billion penalty, a 265.3% debt ratio and KRW 48.3 billion in cash overlap as financial conditions.

Positive factors include the operating margin reaching 7.4% in 2024, a customer base tied to ramen demand, and spare milling capacity. Negative factors include the continuing decline in profit, the penalty and financial burden, and stagnant milling consumption alongside rising imported flour.

On valuation, the net loss means no P/E can be calculated, the price-to-book ratio is below 1x, and the company has no dividend policy. The next checkpoints are the full-year 2026 operating margin in the annual report and whether profit recovers in the Q1 2027 quarterly report.
