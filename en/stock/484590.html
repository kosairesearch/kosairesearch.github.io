02

Business structure

Samyang Comtech is a ballistic and protection specialist whose core business is tank armor, complemented by vehicle and armored-vehicle add-on armor, aviation parts, and R&D work.

In 2024, of KRW 141.6 billion in revenue, KRW 82.3 billion came from tank special armor, or 58.1%, while tactical vehicles and armored vehicles contributed KRW 34.4 billion and aviation parts and other items KRW 24.9 billion. The company said revenue grew across all segments in 2025.

Exports accounted for roughly 35% of 2025 total revenue according to press reports. The core K2 tank armor business supplies protection parts for Poland's K2 tanks, and the company says it holds an exclusive supply position in export-type K2 armor.

In December 2025 it signed a KRW 86.8 billion supply contract for Poland-bound K2 tanks with a domestic systems prime, covering special armor and fuel tanks for 116 K2GF units in the second batch.

Its projects run through systems integrators and through direct contracts with state agencies such as the Defense Acquisition Program Administration and the Agency for Defense Development. In March 2026 it signed a KRW 3.0 billion supply deal with a Turkish defense company for SiC-based ballistic ceramics.

Production runs at the Gumi No. 1 and No. 2 plants, which are described as among the largest ballistic ceramics facilities in Korea, plus the Gwangju and Anseong plants. Competition is concentrated among domestic systems primes and foreign tank makers that supply their own armor.

04

Earnings analysis

On a reported basis, revenue rose 9.2% from KRW 141.6 billion in 2024 to KRW 154.6 billion in 2025, and operating profit increased 47.5% from KRW 18.1 billion to KRW 26.6 billion. The operating margin rose from 12.8% to 17.2%, so profit grew faster than sales.

Profit attributable to controlling shareholders increased 37.5% from KRW 16.5 billion to KRW 22.7 billion, while net income including non-controlling interests went from KRW 16.5 billion to KRW 22.7 billion.

The company attributed the 2025 improvement to export expansion, cost savings from supply-chain diversification, process automation and higher yields, and a larger share of high-value products. Operating cash flow turned positive, moving from KRW -6.94 billion in 2024 to KRW 17.7 billion in 2025.

By quarter, revenue was KRW 36.7 billion with operating profit of KRW 5.0 billion in the third quarter of 2025, rising to KRW 52.4 billion and KRW 8.23 billion in the fourth quarter.

In the first quarter of 2026, revenue fell to KRW 16.5 billion and operating profit to KRW 2.0 billion; the company said the drop reflected deferred deliveries rather than weaker demand or production problems.

Second-quarter 2026 revenue recovered to KRW 46.8 billion with operating profit of KRW 11.5 billion, which the company described as a record quarterly operating profit.

Third-quarter 2026 revenue was KRW 34.6 billion, down 26.1% from the prior quarter and 5.7% lower year on year, while operating profit of KRW 6.16 billion rose 23.2% year on year. Profit attributable to controlling shareholders was KRW 5.28 billion, up 49.5% year on year. Over the last four reported quarters, profit attributable to controlling shareholders totaled KRW 26.3 billion.

05

Industry analysis

Industry sources describe defense demand as rising globally as the war between Russia and Ukraine drags on and Middle East tensions have increased.

According to the company, Romania is pursuing about KRW 14 trillion in procurement of 250 next-generation main battle tanks, where it faces competition from domestic rivals and from Rheinmetall and KNDS models. The company also says Iraq is pursuing a KRW 9 trillion program for 250 K2 tanks.

Export opportunities extend beyond Poland, with Chunmoo add-on armor deliveries to Estonia and the UAE under pursuit. NH Investment & Securities described the company in a December 2025 report as one of the few domestic firms that effectively holds exclusive production capability in ballistic and protection products.

Industry observers view it as holding a strong market position in protection systems, where technical entry barriers are high.

Because large defense contracts are recognized at specific points in time, quarterly results are inherently volatile, and customer inspections concentrated in the fourth quarter amplify this pattern.

An April 2026 press report cited an industry view that Redback armored vehicle production and Middle East export volumes could steepen the earnings trajectory if added. However, timing of large export decisions depends on foreign government procurement schedules, which makes the outlook uncertain.

06

Outlook

In a May 2026 disclosure, the company said deferred volumes and new production would be recognized together from the third quarter, supporting a more stable earnings flow.

In a September 2026 press report, a company official said planned production and deliveries would continue in the second half and that the fourth quarter, when customers concentrate annual inspections, would deliver the highest quarterly profit of the year.

The same report showed inventory at the end of the first half of 2026 up 43.5% from the end of 2025, to about KRW 69.0 billion, which is an item to track as stock built up in the first half is converted to sales.

Gumi No. 3 is targeted to start operations in November 2026, and part of the IPO proceeds was allocated to a ceramics line expansion at Gumi.

The company said the Turkish ballistic ceramics order would complete testing and first-batch delivery in the first half, with a second batch scheduled for the second half of 2026. Chunmoo add-on armor exports to Estonia and the UAE remain in progress, and the company reports urgent Middle East requests.

The company has also signed a cooperation agreement with the Korea Institute of Ceramic Engineering and Technology to commercialize aerospace ceramics under a medium- to long-term roadmap. A delay in the R&D center schedule was also reported, so the timing of new-business revenue has not been fixed.

Bull factors

Visibility from export contracts

A KRW 86.8 billion supply contract covers special armor and fuel tanks for 116 K2GF units in Poland's second batch. Industry observers see it as providing two to three years of earnings visibility. The operating margin rose from 12.8% in 2024 to 17.2% in 2025, which confirms profitability gains from higher volumes.

Balance-sheet repair

The debt-to-equity ratio fell from 202.2% in 2024 to 53.2% in 2025, and total liabilities stood at KRW 83.2 billion at the end of 2025. Operating cash flow also moved from negative in 2024 to KRW 17.7 billion in 2025.

Contract liabilities rose in the first half of 2026 from the end of 2025, enlarging the advance-payment base that can convert into revenue.

A wider export pipeline

A KRW 3.0 billion ballistic ceramics contract with a Turkish company was signed in March 2026. The company is pursuing Chunmoo add-on armor exports to Estonia and the UAE, and it describes large K2 procurement programs in Romania and Iraq as in progress. Aerospace ceramics commercialization is also underway, which broadens the product mix.

Bear factors

Quarterly earnings swings

First-quarter 2026 revenue fell to KRW 16.5 billion, down 68.5% from the prior quarter, and operating profit was KRW 2.0 billion. The company attributed the drop to delivery timing, but the result shows how sharply quarterly profit can swing. Third-quarter revenue of KRW 34.6 billion was 5.7% lower year on year.

Concentration in customers and products

Tank special armor accounted for 58.1% of 2024 revenue, so the business depends heavily on K2 tank production and delivery timing. Poland's second batch is in production, and any slippage in delivery could again weigh on quarterly sales.

Romanian and Iraqi procurement programs remain at the pursuit stage and face rival platforms, so their timing is hard to fix.

Execution load from expansion and new businesses

The Gumi No. 3 plant is targeted to start in November 2026, while a delay in the R&D center schedule has also been reported. Aerospace ceramics remains at the cooperation stage with a research institute, so its revenue timing is not set. Lock-up expirations and possible share supply have been cited as an overhang.

Risk factors

Delivery and inspection timing

Defense deliveries shift revenue recognition depending on customer inspection schedules. In the first quarter of 2026, inventory built up but could not be recognized as revenue because inspections were delayed.

If the concentrated fourth-quarter inspections do not proceed as planned, the full-year earnings pattern could differ from expectations.

Export and policy variables

Export volumes depend on foreign government procurement decisions, budgets, and political conditions. Exports accounted for roughly 35% of 2025 revenue, so changes in the export environment feed directly into results. If geopolitical tensions ease, the slope of overseas defense demand could also change.

Capacity and new-business execution

The Gumi No. 3 plant and the ceramics line expansion are being financed with IPO proceeds. If the expansion starts later than planned or utilization falls, the payback on the investment would also be delayed. A slowdown in the aerospace ceramics and R&D center schedule would push back the benefits of diversification.

Overall view

Samyang Comtech built its 2025 results around K2 tank armor and export volumes, posting revenue of KRW 154.6 billion and operating profit of KRW 26.6 billion, with the operating margin rising to 17.2%. The debt-to-equity ratio fell to 53.2% and operating cash flow turned positive, which eased the balance-sheet burden.

On the other side, quarterly results swung sharply in 2026, with first-quarter revenue falling to KRW 16.5 billion as deliveries were deferred. In the third quarter of 2026, revenue was 5.7% lower year on year, while operating profit and profit attributable to controlling shareholders rose 23.2% and 49.5%.

Strengths include the Polish K2 program, the KRW 86.8 billion contract, rising advance payments, and export deals such as the Turkish order. Weaknesses include concentration in customers and products, dependence on inspection timing, and the execution load of capacity expansion and new businesses.

The next checkpoints are the start of Gumi No. 3 after late November 2026, fourth-quarter 2026 results, and the 2026 annual report, with the timing of the large Romanian and Iraqi contracts also worth tracking.
