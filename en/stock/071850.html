02

Business structure

Castec Korea was founded in December 1998 as an automotive parts maker, and its business is concentrated in automotive parts manufacturing. Its core products are turbine housings and center housings, the key cast components of turbochargers for vehicles.

In a first-quarter breakdown from an article published after the 2020 results, automotive turbo parts accounted for 62.3% of sales, electronic compressor parts for 13.7%, heavy equipment hydraulic parts for 8.3%, other automotive parts for 6.3%, and automotive compressor parts for 4.3%.

Electronic parts include air-conditioner refrigerant compressor components and pump and motor parts, and a 2024-era source put this segment at about 14% of total sales.

A 2014 report named Honeywell, Serb Warner Turbosystems and Kyungyang Precision among customers, and automakers such as Hyundai Motor, Audi, BMW and Ford were also cited. A 2019 report, based on 2018 results, said Hyundai and Kia accounted for 50% of sales, indicating a high dependence on a few automaker groups.

A March 2026 report described the company as holding a 3rd to 4th global share in turbocharger parts. In 2025 the company sold its Suzhou factory in China, leased it back, and expanded production at its Vietnam subsidiary, according to press coverage. Segment sales are not part of the disclosed figures, so the mix above is shown with the date of each source.

05

Industry analysis

Demand from downstream automakers is shaped by an electric vehicle transition that is proceeding more slowly than the market had expected, while hybrid vehicles continue to adopt turbochargers.

A March 2026 report said the company's core turbochargers have continued to be adopted in hybrid vehicles over the past three to four years, easing concerns about downstream demand. This setup is presented as favorable for a company with a high share of combustion and hybrid powertrain parts.

However, a faster shift to electric vehicles could change the long-term direction of turbocharger demand, so the pace of the transition is a key variable.

Quarterly revenue was KRW 37.3 billion in the first quarter of 2026, KRW 43.5 billion in the second and KRW 42.1 billion in the third, an upward move from KRW 36.3 billion in the fourth quarter of 2025. On competition, a March 2026 report described the company as holding a 3rd to 4th global share in turbocharger parts.

A 2015 report cited an 85% domestic share and first place in Korea, but that figure is dated and should not be read as the current position. A customer base concentrated among a few automaker groups means production changes at key customers can have a large effect on revenue.

The exchange rate is also a factor, as foreign exchange gains from a rising rate contributed to the first-quarter 2026 net profit turnaround, according to the company.

06

Outlook

In a March 2026 interview, the company set out a goal of focusing on its core business and reaching breakeven, and it expressed expectations for higher sales and a return to profit as downstream industries expand.

The company said total assets at the end of 2025 were KRW 183.7 billion, including KRW 30 billion of investment property, and that it is pursuing asset securitization. In a May 2026 report, a company official said inquiries for new turbocharger development continue steadily.

The same report said the company expects earnings to improve from 2026 on restructuring and cost reduction. However, the operating loss of KRW 996 million in the third quarter of 2026 showed losses returning, so the company's expectations and quarterly results are not yet moving in the same direction.

The next checkpoints are the annual operating margin and controlling-shareholder net income trend in the 2026 annual report, and whether asset securitization is carried out. Whether new development inquiries turn into mass production sales can be confirmed through changes in the revenue mix.

Whether the downstream expansion the company cited shows up in quarterly results after the first quarter of 2027 is another point to watch.

Bear factors

Revenue fell for three consecutive years from 2023 through 2025 to KRW 155.3 billion. Operating losses and controlling-shareholder net losses continued for four consecutive years from 2022 through 2025, and the 2025 controlling-shareholder net loss of KRW 21.3 billion was larger than in 2024.

Durability of Quarterly Profits

The operating income of KRW 63.94 million in the second quarter of 2026 was followed by an operating loss of KRW 996 million in the third quarter. Controlling-shareholder net losses totaled KRW 10.4 billion over the last four quarters.

The KRW 9.27 billion controlling-shareholder net profit in the second quarter of 2026 has not yet been followed by a sustained run of profits. Frequent reversals in quarterly results raise questions about the stability of earnings power.

Debt Burden and Shrinking Equity

The debt ratio rose from 164.6% in 2024 to 231.7% in 2025. Total liabilities at the end of 2025 of KRW 128.3 billion exceeded total equity of KRW 55.4 billion. The company said foreign exchange gains from a rising exchange rate contributed to the first-quarter 2026 net profit turnaround. Reliance on gains from outside operations, such as exchange gains, is cited as a bearish point.

Risk factors

Financial Structure and Debt Burden

Total liabilities at the end of 2025 were KRW 128.3 billion, above total equity of KRW 55.4 billion, and the debt ratio stood at 231.7%. Debt repayment capacity could depend on whether asset securitization is carried out, given the continued operating losses.

Total assets of KRW 183.7 billion include KRW 30 billion of investment property, so the timing and price of asset sales are variables for financial improvement.

Volatility of Exchange-Rate-Driven Profit

The company said the first-quarter 2026 net profit turnaround reflected foreign exchange gains from a rising exchange rate. If the exchange rate moves the other way, the same item could turn into a loss. Exchange rates can move operating and non-operating results together in an export-linked business.

Separating the exchange rate effect when quarterly net income moves differently from operating income is necessary.

EV Transition and Customer Concentration

A 2019 report, based on 2018 results, put Hyundai and Kia at 50% of sales. A faster shift to electric vehicles could reduce turbocharger demand, which is cited as a long-term risk.

A March 2026 report described the electric vehicle transition as slower than expected, but a change in that view would alter the demand base. Production changes at a specific automaker group feed directly into revenue.
