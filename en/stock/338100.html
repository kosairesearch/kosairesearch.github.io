02

Business structure

NH Prime REIT, listed in December 2019, is an indirect (fund-of-funds style) REIT that does not directly own buildings but instead invests in beneficiary certificates, preferred shares, or class shares of funds and REITs holding office properties, distributing the resulting income as dividends.

At listing it held four assets—Seoul Square, Gangnam N Tower, The Asset (Samsung C&T's Seocho headquarters), and the Samsung SDS Tower—which were sold sequentially as their holding periods matured.

The Samsung SDS Tower was sold in January 2024, Gangnam N Tower in August 2025, The Asset in September 2025, and Seoul Square in February 2026.

Sale proceeds were reinvested in turn into Arc Place, Pangyo TechOne, Signature Tower, and Centerpoint Gwanghwamun, and most recently the KRW 41 billion in proceeds from the Seoul Square sale was invested into a Gangnam office building leased by a Hana Financial Group affiliate and into Fine Avenue Tower A, under a long-term lease to Shinhan Card, bringing the total portfolio to six assets.

As an indirect REIT, the company is subject to rules requiring at least 40% of total assets to be invested in real estate funds or REITs, meaning much of the operational decision-making authority rests with the external asset management companies (AMCs) holding the underlying properties.

Listed peers such as SK Reit, Lotte Reit, and ESR Kendall Square REIT directly own office or logistics real estate, whereas NH Prime REIT gains exposure to prime-grade assets through minority stakes with relatively modest capital.

Its relatively small market capitalization had constrained institutional demand, but a partial easing in 2024 of restrictions on ETFs investing in indirect REITs opened the door to potential new inflows.

04

Earnings analysis

Annual revenue rose from KRW 7.63 billion in 2022 to KRW 14.6 billion in 2023 (up 91.7% year-on-year), remained flat at KRW 14.6 billion in 2024, then fell 72.8% to KRW 3.98 billion in 2025. Among 2022–2025, revenue was highest in 2024 and lowest in 2025.

Operating profit rose from KRW 7.35 billion in 2022 to KRW 12.8 billion in 2023 (up 74.9%), then declined to KRW 7.02 billion in 2024 (down 45.4%), before the company recorded an operating loss of KRW 201 million in 2025, a swing to loss from the prior year.

Net profit attributable to owners peaked at KRW 12.8 billion in 2023 before falling for two consecutive years to KRW 7.05 billion in 2024 and KRW 144 million in 2025, making 2023 the highest year and 2025 the lowest in the 2022–2025 span.

Net profit including non-controlling interests followed the same pattern: KRW 7.35 billion in 2022, KRW 12.8 billion in 2023, KRW 7.05 billion in 2024, and KRW 144 million in 2025. The operating margin fell from 96.2% in 2022 and 87.8% in 2023 to 48.0% in 2024, before dropping to -5.0% in 2025.

The sharp 2025 decline in revenue and profit reflects a gap in distribution income following the sales of Gangnam N Tower and The Asset, compounded by a transitional income gap during the Seoul Square sale process.

Operating cash flow also swung from KRW 10.5 billion in 2024 to -KRW 541 million in 2025, underscoring the cash-flow volatility of the asset-transition period.

That said, the sum of net profit attributable to owners over the last four quarters (Q3 2025–Q2 2026) reached KRW 2.55 billion, exceeding the full-year 2025 figure, suggesting that the dividend contribution from newly acquired assets has begun to show through.

05

Industry analysis

The listed REIT market in Korea is segmented by sector—office, logistics, retail—and the office segment has been sensitive to changes in vacancy rates and capitalization rates amid higher interest rates.

NH Prime REIT invests minority stakes in prime office assets in Seoul's central business district (CBD) and Gangnam business district (GBD) through an indirect structure, differing from peers such as SK Reit, Lotte Reit, and ESR Kendall Square REIT, which directly own real estate.

As an indirect REIT subject to rules requiring at least 40% of total assets to be invested in fund or REIT beneficiary certificates, a significant share of decision-making over asset sales and reinvestment timing rests with the external asset management companies (AMCs) that hold the underlying properties.

A partial easing in January 2024 of restrictions on ETF investment in indirect REITs opened the door to potential new capital inflows for a stock that had previously been constrained by low trading volume and a small market capitalization.

However, given that its market cap remains well below the sector average, whether it can meet index or ETF inclusion thresholds remains to be seen.

Core Gangnam office assets have generally maintained low vacancy on steady leasing demand, while the funding costs and dividend rates on newly acquired assets can fluctuate with prevailing market interest rates.

06

Outlook

In February 2026, NH Prime REIT sold Seoul Square, the last of its original listing-era assets, and reinvested the full KRW 41 billion in recovered principal into two new properties.

It invested KRW 15 billion in equity securities tied to a Gangnam office building leased by a Hana Financial Group affiliate and KRW 26 billion in beneficiary certificates tied to Fine Avenue Tower A, under long-term lease to Shinhan Card, with both investments completed.

The company's stated average annual dividend yield over the investment period is 6.8% for the Gangnam headquarters investment and 7.0% for the Fine Avenue Tower A investment.

With this reinvestment, NH Prime REIT's portfolio has grown to six assets, comprising Arc Place, Pangyo TechOne, Signature Tower, Centerpoint Gwanghwamun, and the two newly added properties.

The asset manager has set a regular dividend target of at least KRW 130 per share on a semiannual basis starting in 2027 following the reinvestment, noting that this is not directly comparable to the prior special dividend of KRW 837 per share that included the Seoul Square sale gain.

With portfolio diversification now complete, dividend volatility tied to individual asset-sale events may gradually ease, though the actual rental income stability of the newly acquired assets has yet to be confirmed through reported operating results.

08

Bull factors

Full Portfolio Replacement Completed

With the sale of Seoul Square, all four assets held since the 2019 listing have now been replaced. The portfolio currently comprises six assets: Arc Place, Pangyo TechOne, Signature Tower, Centerpoint Gwanghwamun, a Gangnam office leased by a Hana Financial Group affiliate, and Fine Avenue Tower A.

The larger number of assets, locations, and tenants offers greater diversification than the previous concentration in a handful of buildings.

Low Leverage, Stable Balance Sheet

The debt-to-equity ratio stood at 0.3% at the end of 2025, with total liabilities of only KRW 296 million against total equity of KRW 109.5 billion. Across the entire 2022–2025 period, the ratio never exceeded roughly 2%, keeping the structure essentially unleveraged.

The near-absence of interest expense from financial leverage reduces one source of volatility in distributable income.

Track Record of Recurring Sale-Gain Special Dividends

The company has a history of distributing sale gains as special dividends each time it sold an asset—Samsung SDS Tower, The Asset, and Gangnam N Tower. Most recently, the Seoul Square sale resulted in a per-share dividend of KRW 837.

This structure, in which asset appreciation is passed directly to shareholders, is a notable feature of the model.

Bear factors

2025 Earnings Gap

2025 revenue fell 72.8% year-on-year and the company swung to an operating loss. This appears to reflect a gap in distribution income during the asset-transition period. Further earnings volatility cannot be ruled out until the new assets' income contribution becomes fully established.

Limited Operating Control Under the Indirect Structure

NH Prime REIT does not directly manage its underlying assets but instead invests in fund or REIT interests managed by external asset management companies (AMCs).

A significant share of the decision-making on asset sale and reinvestment timing and terms lies outside the company, limiting its ability to pursue active value-enhancement strategies. This is a structural constraint distinguishing it from listed REITs that directly own and operate real estate.

Small Market Cap and Low Liquidity

The company's relatively small market capitalization within the real estate sector has constrained institutional participation. Even after the 2024 easing of ETF investment restrictions, it remains to be confirmed whether this translates into actual index or fund inclusion. Low trading volume can also contribute to higher share price volatility.

10

Risk factors

Tenant and Asset Concentration Risk

The newly acquired Gangnam office and Fine Avenue Tower A are each heavily dependent on leases to a Hana Financial Group affiliate and Shinhan Card, respectively. Changes in lease terms or relocation decisions by these key tenants could directly affect the dividend contribution of the relevant assets.

Even with more assets in the portfolio, reliance on a small number of tenants persists at the individual asset level.

Interest Rate and Funding Risk

Financing terms for the newly acquired assets include a senior loan rate of 4.90% and a subordinated loan rate of 5.80%, both fixed. If market rates move, terms for future refinancing or additional investment could change. Under the indirect REIT structure, such loan terms have a direct bearing on distributable income.

AMC and Counterparty Decision Risk

Given the indirect structure, the timing of asset sales, sale prices, and the selection of reinvestment targets are largely determined by the asset managers or counterparties holding the underlying properties.

NH Prime REIT is not well positioned to independently dispose of assets or execute value-enhancement strategies. The possibility that AMC decisions diverge from shareholder interests is a matter that warrants ongoing monitoring.

12

Overall view

In 2026, with the sale of Seoul Square, NH Prime REIT completed the replacement of all four assets it held since listing and reorganized its portfolio into six assets: Arc Place, Pangyo TechOne, Signature Tower, Centerpoint Gwanghwamun, a Gangnam office leased by a Hana Financial Group affiliate, and Fine Avenue Tower A.

Over this period, 2025 revenue fell 72.8% year-on-year and the company swung to an operating loss, though the sum of net profit attributable to owners over the last four quarters reached KRW 2.55 billion, exceeding the full-year 2025 figure and signaling some recovery.

The debt-to-equity ratio remains around 0.3%, preserving the low-leverage structure typical of indirect REITs. At the same time, limited operating control under the indirect structure and a small market capitalization and trading volume remain structural constraints.

Whether the newly acquired assets generate rental income as steadily as the asset manager's guidance of at least KRW 130 per share semiannually starting in 2027 is something to confirm through the next settlement period's results.

Investors will want to monitor both the sustainability of regular dividends and tenant-level risk at each asset now that the portfolio transition is complete.
