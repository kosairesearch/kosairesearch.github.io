02

Business structure

NH Prime REIT, managed by NongHyup REITs Management Co., Ltd., was Korea's first publicly listed fund-of-funds REIT, investing minority stakes in beneficiary certificates or equity securities of funds that hold prime office buildings rather than acquiring buildings directly, using rental income and disposal gains as dividend sources.

At its 2019 listing, the portfolio comprised beneficiary certificates in four core assets: Seoul Square, Samsung SDS Tower, Gangnam N Tower, and The Asset (Samsung C&T Seocho Office).

As the underlying funds' operating terms expired sequentially, the REIT sold Samsung SDS Tower in 2023, The Asset in 2024, Gangnam N Tower in 2025, and Seoul Square in 2026, reinvesting the proceeds from each sale.

The current portfolio consists of six assets: Ark Place, Pangyo Techwon, Signature Tower, Centerpoint Gwanghwamun, an equity stake in a Hana Financial Group office building in Gangnam, and beneficiary certificates tied to Pine Avenue A.

Because of its fund-of-funds structure, the REIT does not directly own or manage the underlying buildings, limiting its ability to directly influence tenant composition or asset value-enhancement strategies.

Among the newly acquired assets, Pine Avenue A is leased on a long-term basis to Shinhan Card, while the Gangnam office building is occupied by Hana Financial Group affiliates.

While peers such as SK Reit, Lotte Reit, and Shinhan Alpha REIT operate as sponsor REITs that continuously acquire assets from parent groups, NH Prime REIT instead purchases stakes in funds or REITs managed by other asset managers, a structure seen as resulting in relatively slower new-asset acquisition despite active disposals.

04

Earnings analysis

NH Prime REIT's revenue rose 91.7% from KRW 7.63 billion in 2022 to KRW 14.6 billion in 2023, held flat at KRW 14.6 billion in 2024, then fell 72.8% year on year to KRW 3.98 billion in 2025.

Operating profit climbed 74.9% from KRW 7.35 billion in 2022 to KRW 12.8 billion in 2023, fell 45.4% to KRW 7.02 billion in 2024, and swung to an operating loss of KRW 201 million in 2025.

Net income attributable to owners likewise peaked at KRW 12.8 billion in 2023 before declining for two straight years, to KRW 7.05 billion (-44.8%) in 2024 and KRW 144 million (-98.0%) in 2025. Net income including non-controlling interests followed the same trajectory in each year.

The operating margin stayed high at 96.2% in 2022 and 87.8% in 2023, fell to 48.0% in 2024, and turned negative at -5.0% in 2025.

This pattern reflects the fund-of-funds structure's characteristic cycle, in which gains from disposing of an underlying asset lift a given year's revenue and profit before disappearing the following year; the elevated 2023-2024 results reflect gains tied to the Samsung SDS Tower and The Asset disposals, while the 2025 decline reflects the base effect once those gains faded.

The debt ratio remained very low, at 0.3%-1.8%, throughout 2022-2025, with total equity and equity attributable to owners each at KRW 109.5 billion and total liabilities at just KRW 296 million at the end of 2025, preserving financial stability.

Operating cash flow fluctuated from year to year — KRW 2.13 billion in 2022, -KRW 429 million in 2023, KRW 10.5 billion in 2024, and -KRW 541 million in 2025 — a pattern also linked to the timing of asset sales and reinvestment.

Net income attributable to owners over the most recent four quarters (Q3 2025 through Q2 2026) totaled KRW 2.55 billion.

05

Industry analysis

Korea's listed REIT market comprises roughly 20 names spanning office, logistics, and gas-station asset classes, and within this group NH Prime REIT follows a fund-of-funds structure that invests in beneficiary certificates or equity securities of funds and REITs rather than acquiring buildings outright.

Sponsor REITs such as SK Reit, Lotte Reit, and Hanwha REIT can continuously acquire assets from parent groups, making expansion easier, but disposals are comparatively rare for them.

NH Prime REIT, by contrast, periodically realizes gains by disposing of assets as the underlying funds' terms mature, though industry commentary notes that new-asset acquisition has been relatively slow.

In the office market, a high weighting toward core-location assets including Seoul's Gangnam Business District is seen as supporting relatively stable leasing demand.

However, the REIT is classified as a small-cap vehicle with one of the lowest assets under management in the industry, a structural constraint cited as limiting trading volume and exclusion from index and ETF inclusion.

Valuation and dividend appeal across the REIT sector are sensitive to the interest-rate environment, with the gap between the reinvested assets' targeted average annual dividend rate of 6.8%-7.0% and prevailing market rates cited as a key variable for relative appeal.

06

Outlook

NH Prime REIT completed the sale of Seoul Square in February 2026 and fully reinvested the recovered principal of KRW 41 billion.

It invested KRW 15 billion in equity securities of a Gangnam office building leased by Hana Financial Group affiliates and KRW 26 billion in beneficiary certificates tied to Pine Avenue A, which is leased long-term by Shinhan Card, completing the two investments in August and September 2026, respectively.

The manager's targeted average annual dividend rate over the investment period is 6.8% for the Gangnam office investment and 7.0% for Pine Avenue A.

With this, all four original assets from the 2019 listing — Seoul Square, Samsung SDS Tower, Gangnam N Tower, and The Asset — have now been replaced, expanding the portfolio to six assets: Ark Place, Pangyo Techwon, Signature Tower, Centerpoint Gwanghwamun, the Gangnam office building, and Pine Avenue A.

NongHyup REITs Management stated that, following the reinvestment, it has set regular dividend targets of KRW 100+ per share for the November 2026 settlement and KRW 130+ per share for each of the May and November 2027 settlements.

A company representative explained this represents a step up from the previous semiannual average dividend in the KRW 120 range. It was also noted that this target is not directly comparable to the KRW 837 figure, which included gains from the Seoul Square sale.

With the portfolio transition now complete, the leasing stability of the newly acquired assets and whether the dividend guidance is met are cited as the key factors to watch going forward.

08

Bull factors

Track Record of Special Dividends Tied to Asset Sales

NH Prime REIT has sequentially sold investment assets — Samsung SDS Tower in 2023, The Asset in 2024, Gangnam N Tower in 2025, and Seoul Square in 2026 — using the disposal gains from each as a special dividend source every time.

The 14th-term settlement, which reflected gains from the Seoul Square sale, set a dividend of KRW 837 per share with a 19.1% yield on market price. This repeated cycle of disposal, reinvestment, and special dividends is seen as an unusual track record for a small-cap REIT.

Stable Financial Structure with a Low Debt Ratio

The debt ratio stayed very low, between 0.3% and 1.8%, from 2022 through 2025, and total liabilities at end-2025 were just KRW 296 million, limiting interest expense exposure even during periods of rising rates.

Total equity is composed almost entirely of equity attributable to owners, reflecting a financial structure with low reliance on external borrowing.

Portfolio Overhaul Complete, Regular Dividend Target Raised

With the 2026 sale and reinvestment of Seoul Square, all four original listing-era assets have now been replaced with new holdings.

Following completion of the reinvestment, the manager stated it has set a semiannual settlement dividend target of KRW 130+ per share for 2027, aiming above the previous average in the KRW 120 range.

The new investments in the Hana Financial Group Gangnam office building and Pine Avenue A carry targeted average annual dividend rates of 6.8% and 7.0%, respectively.

09

Bear factors

Sharp 2025 Earnings Decline, Shrinking Revenue Base

2025 revenue fell 72.8% year on year to just KRW 3.98 billion, and the operating result swung to an operating loss of KRW 201 million. Net income attributable to owners also plunged 98.0% year on year to KRW 144 million, marking two consecutive years of decline since the 2023 peak.

While this largely reflects the base effect of fading asset-disposal gains, the accompanying decline in underlying operating earning power is itself a concern.

Structural Constraints of a Small-Cap REIT

Classified as a small-cap REIT, the company faces limited trading volume and liquidity, and industry commentary notes it has been excluded from REIT ETF inclusion.

Because of its fund-of-funds structure, it cannot directly manage underlying real assets, resulting in slower new-asset acquisition compared with sponsor REITs.

Dividend Volatility

There is a wide gap in dividend amounts between years with and without asset-disposal gains — KRW 625 per share in the 13th term versus KRW 837 in the 14th — showing dividends swing significantly depending on whether a special dividend is paid.

With the portfolio overhaul now complete, large disposal gains are less likely in the near term, meaning future dividends may converge toward the regular dividend target of KRW 130+ per share.

10

Risk factors

Tenant and Vacancy Risk

Rental income from the underlying fund-of-funds assets depends on key tenants' lease renewals and vacancy rates.

Seoul Square was reported to have experienced a period of significant vacancy in the past, underscoring the need for ongoing monitoring of leasing conditions at the underlying assets of the funds and REITs in which the company invests.

Interest Rate and Capital Market Risk

The newly reinvested assets carry targeted dividend rates of 6.8%-7.0%, but if market interest rates rise, their relative appeal could diminish and funding costs for the underlying funds and REITs could increase.

Liquidity and Supply-Demand Risk

Given its small market capitalization, average daily trading value is limited and institutional/foreign ownership is thin, which can amplify price volatility. Commentary also notes that the company does not meet REIT ETF inclusion criteria, limiting passive fund inflow channels.

12

Overall view

NH Prime REIT has sold all four core office assets it held at its 2019 listing and completed reinvestment into a new six-asset portfolio.

FY2025 results showed a 72.8% drop in revenue, a swing to an operating loss, and a 98.0% decline in net income attributable to owners, which largely reflects the base effect of fading asset-disposal gains that had funded prior special dividends.

Net income attributable to owners over the most recent four quarters totaled KRW 2.55 billion, exceeding the full-year 2025 result and suggesting earnings may be past a trough.

Financial stability has held up, with the debt ratio remaining very low, and the manager has raised its regular dividend target to KRW 130+ per share on a semiannual basis following the reinvestment.

That said, structural challenges remain, including the low liquidity and lack of ETF inclusion typical of a small-cap REIT, as well as the slower pace of new-asset acquisition inherent to its fund-of-funds structure.

Going forward, the key points to watch are how stably the newly acquired assets' rental income flows into quarterly results and whether the company's dividend guidance is met in actual settlements.
