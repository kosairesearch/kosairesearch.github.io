02

Business structure

BHI makes equipment for LNG combined-cycle and nuclear power plants. Its core product is the heat recovery steam generator (HRSG).

In cumulative third-quarter 2025 revenue, the HRSG segment accounted for 57%, the other segment, including EPC projects, for 27%, the boiler segment for 15%, and the balance-of-plant (BOP) segment for 1% (Samsung Securities research, October 2025).

The company says the HRSG is one of the three main equipment items in a combined-cycle plant, alongside the gas turbine and steam turbine, and that it maintains partnerships with major gas turbine makers including the big three.

The Middle East is a prominent order market: BHI signed five Middle East supply contracts in 2024 and four in 2025, and in 2026 it added HRSG orders for the Tzafit and Eshkol plants in Israel and the Az-Zour North project in Kuwait.

In Korea, the company disclosed a KRW 42 billion LNG combined-cycle supply contract with a domestic EPC contractor in January 2026, a KRW 72 billion contract with Korea South-East Power in July 2026, and a KRW 223.55 billion contract with a domestic power utility in September 2026.

Its nuclear work includes supply of four item types for the Shin Hanul Units 3 and 4, namely containment liner plates, stainless steel liners, containment penetrations and condensers. Abroad it works with Japan's IHI and Toshiba.

In October 2025, iM Securities said Westinghouse lacks main equipment and BOP capacity and is therefore likely to partner with BHI. In a February 2026 company notice, BHI said it had ranked first in the world in HRSG for a second consecutive year.

04

Earnings analysis

On an annual basis, revenue went from KRW 330.2 billion in 2022 to KRW 367.4 billion in 2023 (up 11.3% year on year), KRW 404.7 billion in 2024 (up 10.2%) and KRW 774.1 billion in 2025 (up 91.3%). Revenue rose for three consecutive years from 2023 through 2025, and 2025 was the largest year in the 2022–2025 period.

Operating profit went from KRW 8.11 billion in 2022 to KRW 15.1 billion in 2023, KRW 21.9 billion in 2024 and KRW 75.5 billion in 2025 (up 244.3%), and the operating margin rose from 2.5% to 9.8% over the same span.

In its third-quarter 2025 results, the company attributed the improvement to higher revenue recognition on previously won projects.

Profit attributable to controlling shareholders swung from a net loss of KRW 19.1 billion in 2022 to net profit of KRW 7.51 billion in 2023, a turnaround to profit, then rose to KRW 19.6 billion in 2024 (up 160.9%) and KRW 65.2 billion in 2025 (up 232.6%), growing for two consecutive years.

Net profit including non-controlling interests was KRW 7.46 billion in 2023, KRW 19.6 billion in 2024 and KRW 65.2 billion in 2025. The debt ratio fell from 807.1% in 2022 to 367.6% in 2025 but remains high, with total liabilities of KRW 653.1 billion against total equity of KRW 177.6 billion at the end of 2025.

Operating cash flow rose from KRW 15.2 billion in 2022 to KRW 112.3 billion in 2025. Quarterly results are more volatile.

Revenue was KRW 204.7 billion in the third quarter of 2025, KRW 264.8 billion in the fourth quarter (up 29.4% quarter on quarter), KRW 280.8 billion in the first quarter of 2026, KRW 296.3 billion in the second quarter and KRW 286.5 billion in the third quarter.

Operating profit was KRW 18.4 billion, KRW 24.3 billion, KRW 35.3 billion, KRW 45.5 billion and KRW 40.3 billion over the same quarters. Profit attributable to controlling shareholders was KRW 11.7 billion, KRW 15 billion, KRW 5.1 billion, KRW 20.3 billion and KRW 79.8 billion.

In the first quarter of 2026 operating profit rose while controlling-shareholder profit fell 65.9% quarter on quarter.

In the third quarter of 2026 operating profit declined 11.3% quarter on quarter while controlling-shareholder profit rose 292.8%, and the company linked that gap to derivative valuation moving from losses to gains. Quarterly operating margins have been in double digits in each quarter since the first quarter of 2026.

Profit attributable to controlling shareholders summed to KRW 120.2 billion over the last four quarters, from the fourth quarter of 2025 to the third quarter of 2026.

05

Industry analysis

Demand is driven mainly by rising global power needs. The company says AI-driven power demand is accelerating LNG and nuclear infrastructure buildout around the world, and that LNG demand has grown to exceed the production capacity of major gas turbine makers.

The company also says the equipment it won in the Middle East over the last two years exceeds 15 GW. In nuclear, iM Securities cited expansion of US nuclear projects, the extension of export markets into the Middle East and the start of SMR orders as favorable conditions (October 2025).

Japan is a market where aging thermal plants are being converted to LNG combined-cycle, and the company referred to this trend in its IHI contract. On the cycle position, the company describes a period in which strong power-sector activity is flowing into orders and revenue recognition.

On competition, BHI's claim of ranking first worldwide in HRSG for two consecutive years comes from its own notice. The company points to its partnerships with the big three gas turbine makers and its relationships with buyers in the Middle East and Japan as the basis for its order competitiveness.

In nuclear, its work with global nuclear companies such as Westinghouse and Toshiba is intended to widen its scope of supply at home and abroad. Because LNG and nuclear procurement cycles are moving at the same time, a change at one buyer or region is less likely to determine total results.

On the supply side, lead times for linked equipment such as gas turbines and boilers can affect the timing of HRSG revenue recognition.

Outlook

The company set a 2026 new-order target of KRW 2 trillion and said it would focus on new results in North America and Europe (as of its March 2026 disclosure).

Contracts confirmed in 2026 include a March agreement with a China-linked joint venture for an LNG HRSG supply worth KRW 56.6 billion, a May follow-on order worth KRW 57.5 billion, a June disclosure of an HRSG supply contract for a large Japanese LNG combined-cycle plant, a July contract worth KRW 72 billion with Korea South-East Power running through November 2029, and two September contracts: four HRSG units for the Az-Zour North 2 and 3 project in Kuwait worth KRW 145.3 billion, and a KRW 223.55 billion contract with a domestic power utility running into 2029.

Contract terms extend beyond 2029, so revenue recognition will spread across several years. Cumulative backlog was announced at KRW 2.4 trillion in March 2026. The additional Shin Hanul 3 and 4 orders in May 2026 widen the domestic nuclear reference base.

The company's disclosures note that contract periods and amounts may change as construction progresses. The next checkpoint is how close new 2026 orders come to the KRW 2 trillion target and whether North American and European orders are announced. The 2026 annual business report will show year-end backlog and the revenue recognition plan.

Bull factors

Order backlog and contract visibility

Backlog stood at KRW 2.4 trillion as announced in March 2026. Supply contracts continued through 2026 across the Middle East, Korea and Japan, with terms running into 2029 and beyond. The company set a 2026 new-order target of KRW 2 trillion.

Margin expansion

The operating margin rose from 2.5% in 2022 to 9.8% in 2025. Quarterly operating margins stayed in double digits in each of the three quarters from the first quarter of 2026. Operating profit reached KRW 75.5 billion in 2025, up 244.3%.

Exposure to both LNG and nuclear

The company says it has secured supply contracts for four item types for Shin Hanul Units 3 and 4. In October 2025, iM Securities assessed that Westinghouse's shortfall in BOP capacity makes a partnership with BHI likely. The company says AI data-center demand is lifting investment in both LNG and nuclear infrastructure.

Bear factors

Large swings in quarterly profit

Profit attributable to controlling shareholders fell 65.9% quarter on quarter to KRW 5.1 billion in the first quarter of 2026. It rose 292.8% to KRW 79.8 billion in the third quarter, which the company linked to derivative valuation changes. Operating profit fell 11.3% in the same third quarter, so the two measures moved in opposite directions.

High leverage

At the end of 2025 the debt ratio was 367.6%, with total liabilities of KRW 653.1 billion against total equity of KRW 177.6 billion. The ratio fell from 807.1% in 2022 but remains high relative to equity. Financial strain could grow with interest rates, exchange rates or the timing of project payments.

Middle East concentration and contract terms

The company says it has won ten Middle East projects over the last two years, with equipment capacity exceeding 15 GW. Its disclosures state that contract periods and amounts may change as construction progresses.

Because results lean on orders from one region, shifts in regional policy or project schedules could have an outsized effect.

10

Risk factors

FX and derivative gains and losses

The company uses currency forwards and other derivatives to reduce exchange-rate risk on exports. Derivative valuation was a loss in the first half of 2026 and turned to a gain in the third quarter, which materially changed controlling-shareholder profit. A sharp exchange-rate move could widen quarterly swings again.

Project timing and revenue recognition

HRSG revenue is recognized as equipment is delivered and construction advances, so schedule changes can shift quarterly results. Third-quarter 2026 revenue fell 3.3% quarter on quarter. In its contract disclosures the company noted that terms may change as construction progresses.

Balance sheet and regional concentration

Total liabilities at the end of 2025 were KRW 653.1 billion, well above total equity of KRW 177.6 billion. Heavy reliance on Middle East orders means regional developments could hit both bookings and revenue at once. Even as orders grow, cash collection and financial burden can rise together.

Overall view

BHI has grown revenue and operating profit substantially since 2022, and its January–September 2026 cumulative revenue passed full-year 2025 revenue.

On orders, multiple HRSG and power equipment contracts were disclosed in 2026 across the Middle East, Korea and Japan, and cumulative backlog was announced at KRW 2.4 trillion. On profitability, the operating margin rose from 2.5% in 2022 to 9.8% in 2025, and quarterly operating margins stayed in double digits in 2026.

Quarterly controlling-shareholder profit, however, swung between KRW 5.1 billion and KRW 79.8 billion, largely reflecting derivative valuation. On the balance sheet, the debt ratio fell to 367.6% at the end of 2025 but remains high, making project payment flows and exchange rates important variables.

Strengths are the order backlog and profit recovery, while weaknesses are quarterly volatility, Middle East concentration and high leverage.

The next checkpoints are whether new 2026 orders reach the KRW 2 trillion target, and the operating margin and debt ratio in the 2026 preliminary results and annual business report.
