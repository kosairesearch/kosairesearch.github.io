DRB Holding: Sales Up, Profit Swings Widen

02

Business structure

DRB Holding is a rubber products manufacturer listed on KOSPI in 1976 and operates as the holding company of its group. Its business falls into three segments: industrial rubber products, total rubber parts, and other operations such as automation and medical devices.

The industrial rubber products segment makes power transmission belts, conveyor belts and rubber tracks, while the total rubber parts segment supplies automotive rubber components and rubber products for civil engineering. The other segment covers automation businesses such as robots and medical equipment.

On FnGuide data, the industrial segment's share of sales fell from 61.89 percent in 2022 to 47.24 percent in 2024, total rubber parts rose from 28.17 percent to 35.70 percent, and the other segment grew from 9.93 percent to 17.06 percent.

In the 2023 business report, segment sales were KRW 424.6 billion for industrial rubber products, KRW 206.0 billion for total rubber parts and KRW 107.2 billion for other operations.

That year the industrial segment earned operating profit of KRW 57.95 billion, while total rubber parts posted an operating loss of KRW 3.43 billion and other operations an operating loss of KRW 18.67 billion.

Overseas, the group runs subsidiaries in China, Vietnam, Slovakia, the United States, Japan and Russia, so sales are exposed to exchange rates and global industrial cycles.

Unlike petrochemical-listed peers such as Lotte Chemical or Kumho Petro Chemical, DRB Holding is weighted toward rubber applications, so its raw material and end-demand drivers differ.

04

Earnings analysis

Annual sales rose from KRW 661.8 billion in 2022 to KRW 737.8 billion in 2023 (up 11.5 percent), KRW 752.7 billion in 2024 (up 2.0 percent) and KRW 802.7 billion in 2025 (up 6.7 percent). Sales have grown three years in a row from 2023 through 2025, and 2025 is the largest year of the four.

Operating profit went from KRW 12.1 billion in 2022 to KRW 35.8 billion in 2023 (up 196.0 percent), KRW 21.9 billion in 2024 (down 39.0 percent) and KRW 24.9 billion in 2025 (up 13.8 percent), with 2023 the largest year.

Operating margin was 1.8 percent, 4.9 percent, 2.9 percent and 3.1 percent over 2022 to 2025, so margins stayed in the low single digits even as sales grew. The 2023 jump came mainly from the industrial rubber products segment, while the other two segments posted losses that year.

Net profit attributable to controlling shareholders was KRW 1.53 billion in 2022, KRW 15.4 billion in 2023 (up 901.7 percent), KRW 9.47 billion in 2024 (down 38.4 percent) and KRW 4.47 billion in 2025 (down 52.8 percent), declining two years in a row after the 2023 peak.

In 2025 operating profit rose while controlling-shareholder profit fell, so the gap sits below the operating line and should be traced in the next business report.

Quarterly operating profit went from KRW 13.1 billion in Q3 2025 to KRW 2.41 billion in Q4 2025 (down 81.6 percent quarter on quarter), then KRW 5.87 billion in Q1 2026 and KRW 11.2 billion in Q2 2026, before falling to KRW 1.39 billion in Q3 2026 (down 89.5 percent year on year).

Q3 2026 sales of KRW 223.2 billion rose year on year, yet the operating margin stayed below 1 percent. Controlling-shareholder results swung to losses in Q4 2025 (net loss of KRW 3.84 billion) and Q3 2026 (net loss of KRW 8.47 billion), and the last four quarters total a net loss of KRW 5.7 billion.

At end-2025, total equity was KRW 529 billion, of which KRW 404.1 billion belongs to controlling shareholders, and total liabilities were KRW 445.9 billion. Operating cash flow was -KRW 11.6 billion in 2022, KRW 102.9 billion in 2023, KRW 28.2 billion in 2024 and KRW 43.2 billion in 2025.

05

Industry analysis

DRB Holding's core industrial rubber products segment serves demand tied to the operating rates and replacement cycles of industrial equipment, such as conveyor belts and rubber tracks used in mining, steel and cement.

When equipment utilization slows, both new and replacement demand can fall, so the segment is exposed to the capital spending cycle. Total rubber parts centers on automotive components and is linked to vehicle production cycles.

The automation, robot and medical device businesses in the other segment are growth areas, but they posted an operating loss of KRW 18.67 billion in 2023, so their earnings contribution was negative that year.

Within the chemicals sector, peers such as Lotte Chemical, Kumho Petro Chemical and Korea Petro Chemical IND depend on petrochemical feedstock prices, whereas DRB Holding sits closer to processing and application of rubber products.

Its cost base still reflects petroleum-linked inputs such as synthetic rubber and carbon black, so raw material swings affect margins. Sales rose year on year in Q3 2026 while operating profit fell, which points to possible cost or selling expense pressure.

The current position in the cycle is hard to define with certainty, and matching segment demand indicators with reported results will rely on segment disclosures in the next business report.

Competitive positioning rests on the mix of application products and the spread of overseas production bases, which together determine how exposed the group is to exchange rates and regional demand.

06

Outlook

Several business milestones are confirmed. In late October 2026, the company signed an innovation agreement with an R&D special zone foundation, under which four zone companies will run demonstrations at DRB Holding facilities in Korea and abroad.

Demonstration costs will be supported, and follow-on investment will be considered based on the results. This reads as a commercialization opportunity in automation and manufacturing technology, but its effect on sales has not yet appeared in disclosed figures.

On governance, Cho Hyun-min was appointed CEO on March 27, 2026, replacing Ryu Young-sik, and the same shareholder meeting approved the 60th-term financial statements and a cash dividend.

On staffing, headcount at end-2025 was 42.5 percent higher than at end-2023, and four new units were set up from 2024 to 2025 to address digital transformation, AI and new business. Whether that expansion adds fixed cost can be gauged from selling and administrative expense trends in the next business report.

The next checkpoint is the operating margin in the upcoming annual report, and whether it moves above the 3.1 percent recorded in 2025. Also worth tracking is whether the Q3 2026 sales growth carries into Q4 and whether the demonstration results show up in the revenue mix.

Quarterly profit improvement

Balance sheet repair and cash generation

Risk factors

Earnings volatility

Quarterly operating profit swung from KRW 13.1 billion in Q3 2025 to KRW 2.41 billion in Q4, and from KRW 11.2 billion in Q2 2026 to KRW 1.39 billion in Q3. Large quarterly swings reduce the predictability of annual results. Simultaneous softening in industrial equipment and automotive demand could widen these swings further.

Governance and management change

As a holding company, DRB Holding's earnings depend on the results and dividend flows of its subsidiaries. A board resolution on March 27, 2026 appointed Cho Hyun-min as CEO, with Ryu Young-sik stepping down.

The direction of business strategy under new management can be traced through the next business report and shareholder meeting agendas.

Currency and overseas operations

Overseas subsidiaries in China, Vietnam, Slovakia, the United States, Japan and Russia expose sales and profit to exchange rate moves. Automotive parts follow vehicle production cycles, while industrial products follow equipment investment cycles. The profit contribution of overseas units can be checked in the notes to the business report.

Overall view

DRB Holding's sales have risen three years in a row since 2023 to KRW 802.7 billion in 2025, and growth extended into Q2 and Q3 2026. The operating margin, however, stayed between 1.8 percent in 2022 and 3.1 percent in 2025, so profitability remains thin.

Controlling-shareholder net profit fell from KRW 15.4 billion in 2023 to KRW 4.47 billion in 2025, and posted losses in Q4 2025 and Q3 2026.

Q3 2026 sales rose, yet operating profit reached only KRW 1.39 billion, so the two moved in opposite directions, which is why the cost and selling expense structure should be examined in the next business report.

The balance sheet improved, with the debt-to-equity ratio falling from 97.0 percent in 2022 to 84.3 percent in 2025, total equity of KRW 529 billion at end-2025, and operating cash flow staying positive.

Strengths include sales momentum, the two-quarter profit recovery, the improved balance sheet and the new demonstration opportunity under the special zone agreement. Weaknesses include profit volatility, recurring loss quarters, and the loss-making structure in the total rubber parts and other segments.

With no earnings-based multiple available and the price-to-book ratio well below 1 times, the reading depends on whether earnings recover. The next checkpoints are segment profit in the 2026 annual report, Q1 2027 operating profit against KRW 5.87 billion in Q1 2026, and follow-up on the special zone demonstrations.
