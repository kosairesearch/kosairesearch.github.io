02

Business structure

Sammok S-Form is a construction formwork specialist founded in 1985 and listed on KOSDAQ in 1996. The company manufactures aluminum forms and panel forms and sells or rents them to apartment construction sites. Aluminum forms account for more than 80% of total sales.

A new-technology aluminum formwork system has been described as replacing conventional methods and slowing the pace of sales declines, and the company holds a large set of formwork systems built through long-term investment that serve as a stable revenue base.

Production sites are located in Korea, India and Malaysia, and expanding overseas sales is one pillar of the diversification plan. Because the main customers are apartment builders, revenue is tied to construction starts and sales schedules.

On competition, intensifying bidding for orders and lower order prices are cited as the core explanation for the 2025 decline. On governance, the largest shareholder, a private company, holds 38.80% of the shares.

After a 2021 restructuring moved manufacturing to Sammok S-Form, that affiliate became an investment holding company. Including related parties, the largest shareholder group holds 69.47%, according to press reports.

04

Earnings analysis

On a disclosed annual basis, revenue peaked at KRW 439.4 billion in 2023 and then fell to KRW 401.9 billion in 2024 (down 8.5% year on year) and KRW 317 billion in 2025 (down 21.1%), declining two years in a row from 2024 through 2025.

Operating profit was KRW 68.1 billion in 2022 and KRW 124.1 billion in 2023, the largest year in the period, then KRW 75.3 billion in 2024 (down 39.3%) and KRW 6.44 billion in 2025 (down 91.4%), declining two years in a row from 2024 through 2025.

The operating margin fell from 20.2% in 2022 and 28.3% in 2023 to 18.7% in 2024 and 2.0% in 2025. In 2025 the operating profit decline was far larger than the revenue decline, so margin compression amplified the drop in earnings.

The company cited lower segment sales and falling order prices as the main drivers of the 2025 earnings swing.

Attributable net income was KRW 58.5 billion in 2022, KRW 119.4 billion in 2023 (up 103.9%), KRW 73.4 billion in 2024 (down 38.5%) and KRW 10.2 billion in 2025 (down 86.1%), and net income including non-controlling interests was disclosed at the same amounts.

Operating cash flow went from KRW 60.9 billion in 2022 to KRW 92.2 billion in 2023, KRW 81.1 billion in 2024 and KRW 41.5 billion in 2025, remaining positive throughout. At the end of 2025, total equity was KRW 653.5 billion, all attributable to shareholders of the parent, and total liabilities were KRW 113.1 billion.

The debt ratio declined from 46.0% in 2022 to 17.3% in 2025, so financial burden eased.

On a quarterly basis, operating profit of KRW 834 million in Q3 2025 turned into an operating loss of KRW 10.9 billion in Q4 2025, and the losses then narrowed to KRW 6.1 billion in Q1 2026, KRW 3.06 billion in Q2 and KRW 2.6 billion in Q3.

The cumulative operating loss for the first nine months of 2026 was reported at about KRW 11.8 billion. Attributable net income was positive in Q1 2026 (KRW 1.18 billion) and Q2 2026 (KRW 2.36 billion), but Q3 2026 posted an attributable net loss of KRW 12.3 billion.

The attributable net loss over the last four quarters totals KRW 12.4 billion, so the loss streak has not yet ended.

05

Industry analysis

The company's results are tied to the apartment construction market. Press reports say the apartment construction slowdown and delayed recovery in the property market have kept results on a downward trend. A fnguide summary attributed the lower order prices to fewer construction starts and intensifying bidding.

The same summary also cited mortgage-rate regulation and political uncertainty as factors weighing on consumer sentiment. From a cycle perspective, order prices and volumes are unlikely to improve together unless construction starts recover.

On technology, a new aluminum formwork system has been described as replacing conventional methods and slowing the pace of sales declines. In a shrinking market, the question for an aluminum-form focused company is whether it can hold share against conventional methods.

On competition, the question is whether cost competitiveness and utilization of owned equipment become differentiators while order prices are falling. Production in India and Malaysia gives the company some exposure to demand outside the domestic apartment cycle. Overall, the industry shows no clear recovery signal yet in either construction starts or order prices.

Outlook

The company disclosed a shareholder value plan in 2026 and stated that it will focus on profitability-driven management, expand overseas sales and secure stable cash flow through diversified business.

Overseas sales growth should appear in the operating results of the Korean, Indian and Malaysian plants, so regional sales disclosures are the checkpoint.

On shareholder returns, retained earnings were reported at KRW 5.883 trillion as of the end of Q3 2025 (press report, based on Q3 2025 disclosure), and the company said it is exploring various shareholder return policies.

The largest shareholder also ran a tender offer for up to 8.16% of the shares, stating the purpose was to strengthen management stability and control. That move concerns control and should be read apart from operating performance.

On the business side, the pace of recovery in order prices and construction starts will determine the direction of quarterly operating results. The next annual report, covering fiscal 2026, will show whether the operating margin improves from 2.0% in 2025.

With losses continuing across recent quarters, the timing of a return to operating profit and the trend in attributable net income both need to be confirmed.

The specific figures behind the overseas sales and diversification plans have not yet appeared in the disclosed results, so the outlook rests on the direction of the plan and the timing of its confirmation in reported numbers.

Bull factors

Balance sheet cushion

Total liabilities were KRW 113.1 billion at the end of 2025, small against total equity of KRW 653.5 billion, and the debt ratio fell from 46.0% in 2022 to 17.3% in 2025. Operating cash flow stayed positive at KRW 41.5 billion in 2025.

Even with operating losses continuing, the capital base is thick, so short-term funding pressure is relatively modest.

Narrowing quarterly losses

Operating losses shrank from KRW 10.9 billion in Q4 2025 to KRW 6.1 billion in Q1 2026, KRW 3.06 billion in Q2 and KRW 2.6 billion in Q3. Attributable net income was positive in Q1 and Q2 2026. Q2 2026 revenue rose 13.1% from the prior quarter to KRW 82.9 billion.

Q3 2026 returned to an attributable net loss of KRW 12.3 billion, but the operating loss itself got smaller each quarter.

Aluminum-form core and overseas plants

Because most sales come from the single aluminum form business, the trend in that business largely drives company results. An assessment says a new aluminum formwork system has replaced conventional methods and slowed the pace of sales declines.

Production sites are in Korea, India and Malaysia, and the company has stated that expanding overseas sales is a priority.

Bear factors

Sharp drop in profitability

The operating margin fell to 2.0% in 2025 from 18.7% in 2024. Operating losses have run for four consecutive quarters from Q4 2025 through Q3 2026. The cumulative operating loss through the first nine months of 2026 was about KRW 11.8 billion, a swing from an operating profit a year earlier. If the margin stays low even with stable revenue, earnings recovery is limited.

Construction cycle and order prices

Press reports describe a sluggish apartment construction market and a delayed recovery in the property market. The company cited intensifying bidding and lower order prices as main drivers of the 2025 earnings swing. When both factors operate together, higher volume may not be matched by pricing support.

Governance and dividends

The largest shareholder and related parties together hold 69.47%. Press reports said the company had allegedly steered work to the largest shareholder over a long period. Despite retained earnings of KRW 5.883 trillion, the low payout has kept dividend criticism alive.

Risk factors

Demand and cycle

If apartment starts and sales recovery stay delayed, demand for aluminum forms could fall further. Policy variables such as mortgage-rate regulation have been reported to weigh on consumer sentiment, so rates and policy direction remain key demand variables. How far overseas plants can offset domestic weakness also needs to be confirmed.

Profitability and pricing

If bidding competition persists, order prices may stay low, so the operating margin could remain thin even with steady revenue. The 2025 operating margin was 2.0%, and operating losses continued for four quarters into 2026. If cost cuts and better equipment utilization fail to offset price declines, the loss pattern could persist.

Governance and shareholder returns

Criticism over dealings with the largest shareholder and related companies, together with a low payout, weighs on minority shareholder confidence. The company said it is exploring various shareholder return policies, but concrete direction is a matter for the next annual report. With concentrated ownership, policy decisions may be less influenced by minority holders.

Overall view

Sammok S-Form's consolidated revenue, operating profit and attributable net income each declined for two years in a row from 2024 through 2025 after peaking in 2023, and the 2025 operating margin fell to 2.0%.

Quarterly operating losses narrowed through 2026 to KRW 6.1 billion, KRW 3.06 billion and KRW 2.6 billion, but four straight quarters of operating losses and a Q3 2026 attributable net loss of KRW 12.3 billion mean the earnings recovery is not yet visible in the reported figures.

The balance sheet looks comfortable, with a 17.3% debt ratio and total equity of KRW 653.5 billion, and operating cash flow stayed positive. On the industry side, fewer apartment starts and lower order prices remain variables the company cannot directly control.

Bullish factors include the aluminum-form core business, overseas plants and the shareholder value plan. Bearish factors include the sharp profitability decline, a weak construction cycle and governance and dividend disputes.

The share price sits below the bottom of its historical quarter-end price-to-book range, which is a factual comparison only, and no earnings-based multiple can be calculated while losses continue.

The next checkpoints are the operating margin and dividend policy in the FY2026 annual report and operating results for Q1 2027.
