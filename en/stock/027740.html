02

Business structure

Maniker is a poultry processor that slaughters chickens and produces and sells chicken products, and its business falls under a single fresh-meat segment.

In the 2025 annual business report, partial cuts accounted for 42.5% of product sales, whole chicken for 34.5%, salted and processed meat for 16.0%, and other items, including rendering feed, for 5.4%.

Partial cuts are portioned for home-cooking convenience, while salted meat is supplied in bulk to fast-food chains and franchise chicken outlets. Whole chicken is sold as raw material for home cooking.

The 2024 mix was 40.0% partial cuts, 34.9% whole chicken and 17.2% salted meat, so the product mix has shifted toward partial cuts. Customers fall into two groups: franchise food-service operators and household consumers.

Live-bird production is handled by an agricultural subsidiary in which the company holds a 55.00% stake. According to a September 2026 media report, the largest shareholder's stake reached 40.63%. Domestic poultry processors compete in the same market, and Harim is another poultry processor in the same food sector.

Earnings analysis

Annual sales moved from KRW 320.6 billion in 2022 to KRW 339.6 billion in 2023 (up 5.9% year on year), fell to KRW 332.8 billion in 2024 (down 2.0%), and reached KRW 368.9 billion in 2025 (up 10.8%), the largest year of the period.

Operating results swung from an operating loss of KRW 6.97 billion in 2022 to operating income of KRW 2.21 billion in 2023, which marked a turn to profit, then to an operating loss of KRW 9.05 billion in 2024, and back to operating income of KRW 2.01 billion in 2025.

Operating margins were -2.2% in 2022, 0.7% in 2023, -2.7% in 2024 and 0.5% in 2025, so the company stayed close to breakeven throughout.

Net income attributable to controlling shareholders went from KRW 987 million in 2022 to a net loss of KRW 1.55 billion in 2023, a loss of KRW 10.9 billion in 2024, and a narrower loss of KRW 43.88 million in 2025, which means losses for three consecutive years from 2023 to 2025.

Net income including non-controlling interests is a separate figure, moving from KRW 655 million in 2022 to a net loss of KRW 317 million in 2025. Operating cash flow shifted from -KRW 4.63 billion in 2022 and -KRW 3.5 billion in 2023 to KRW 1.5 billion in 2024 and KRW 6.37 billion in 2025.

Media reported that 2025 free cash flow remained negative at about KRW 3.6 billion. At the end of 2025 total equity was KRW 41.6 billion, of which KRW 42.2 billion belonged to controlling shareholders, total liabilities were KRW 113.9 billion, and the debt ratio eased to 274.1% from 278.3% in 2024.

On a quarterly basis, operating income of KRW 2.03 billion in Q4 2025 (up 53.6% from the prior quarter) was followed by an operating loss of KRW 3.09 billion in Q1 2026 after a swing into loss. Cost of sales absorbed most of sales in Q1 2026, which left little room for profit.

The Q2 2026 operating loss narrowed to KRW 16.02 million, while the Q3 2026 operating loss of KRW 1.21 billion reflected a swing from a year-earlier profit. Net losses attributable to controlling shareholders over the last four quarters totaled KRW 4.63 billion.

According to media reports, finance costs in the first half of 2026 reached about KRW 1.5 billion, roughly KRW 0.3 billion higher than a year earlier.

05

Industry analysis

Demand for Maniker's chicken products comes from both food-service and household channels. A company comment for Q1 2026 cited growth in franchise and restaurant demand and rising interest in convenience, which lifted demand for partial cuts and salted meat.

A preference for white meat in line with wellness trends was also noted. This aligns with the rise in partial cuts from 40.0% of the 2024 mix to 42.5% in 2025.

By contrast, media reported in June 2026 that enthusiasm around the World Cup, a key demand event for chicken sellers, faded quickly after the national team exited early. Dependence on short-lived event demand adds volatility to quarterly results.

Compared with large food-sector companies in the same industry, Maniker has a smaller capital base and relies more heavily on borrowing. With total equity of KRW 41.6 billion at the end of 2025 and short-term borrowings of a larger scale, its capacity to absorb cost and demand swings is limited.

How far raw-material costs can be passed through to selling prices is the variable that determines margins in this market.

On the financing side, media reported in September 2026 that short-term borrowings stood at KRW 66.6 billion at the end of the first half of 2026, up sharply from KRW 48.7 billion at the end of 2025.

All of this debt falls due within one year, and contractual cash flows are concentrated at KRW 31.1 billion in the one-to-three-month bucket and KRW 36.9 billion in the three-month-to-one-year bucket.

The largest shareholder stopped additional share purchases after spending more than KRW 5 billion to defend the share price, and its stake reached 40.63%. On the listing side, a 2-for-1 share consolidation approved at the March 2026 general meeting raised the par value to KRW 1,000.

The same report said the market-capitalization listing standard is set to tighten to KRW 50 billion from 2027. On the business side, whether demand for partial cuts and salted meat holds up will determine the pace of sales recovery.

The next checkpoints are whether the Q4 2026 results break the operating-loss pattern, and whether the short-term debt at the end of 2026 is repaid or rolled over. After that, the 2026 annual business report will show the debt balance and the shareholder-return plan.

Bull factors

Narrowing quarterly losses

The operating loss narrowed to KRW 16.02 million in Q2 2026 from KRW 3.09 billion in Q1 2026. Media reported in September 2026 that losses concentrated in Q1 shrank considerably in Q2. Operating income of KRW 2.01 billion in 2025 followed an operating loss in 2024. The improving direction of earnings is cited as the main support for the bull case.

Sales up for two straight quarters

Sales reached KRW 106.7 billion in Q2 2026, up 7.0% from the prior quarter, and KRW 107.1 billion in Q3 2026, up 3.7% year on year. Sales have risen for two consecutive quarters since the Q1 2026 low.

Partial cuts are the largest product line at 42.5% of 2025 sales, and a company comment for Q1 2026 linked demand for this line to franchise food-service growth and convenience.

Largest shareholder support and governance

The largest shareholder spent more than KRW 5 billion to defend the share price and raised its stake to 40.63%. A 2-for-1 share consolidation approved at the March 2026 general meeting raised the par value to KRW 1,000 and reduced common shares outstanding to 31,755,614. A stake above 40% gives the largest shareholder a stabilizing role in governance.

Bear factors

Three straight quarters of operating losses

Operating losses have persisted for three consecutive quarters from Q1 through Q3 2026. The Q3 2026 operating loss of KRW 1.21 billion followed a profit a year earlier. Net loss attributable to controlling shareholders widened to KRW 1.76 billion in Q3 2026.

Over the last four quarters that total came to KRW 4.63 billion. Core operating earnings have not yet stabilized, which is the main basis for the bear case.

Short-term debt and accumulated deficit burden

According to a media report, short-term borrowings stood at KRW 66.6 billion at the end of the first half of 2026, and all of it falls due within one year. Accumulated deficit reached KRW 78.2 billion, and finance costs in the first half rose by about KRW 0.3 billion year on year to KRW 1.5 billion.

The debt ratio was 274.1% in 2025, and the same report put it at 358.7% at the end of the first half of 2026. Borrowing costs could offset the benefit of returning to operating profit, which is the main bear factor.

No dividends and a tightening listing standard

The company said in its corporate governance report that it could not provide a dividend plan because it has no distributable profit. It also stated that it has not set a specific shareholder-return policy, so returns are hard to expect for now.

The market-capitalization standard is set to rise to KRW 50 billion from 2027, and the current market capitalization is below that level. Keeping the share price at its current level alone has been flagged as insufficient for meeting the listing requirement.

Risk factors

Financial liquidity

Short-term borrowings of KRW 66.6 billion at the end of the first half of 2026 all fall due within one year, according to media reports.

Contractual cash flows are concentrated at KRW 31.1 billion in the one-to-three-month bucket and KRW 36.9 billion in the three-month-to-one-year bucket, so slower operating cash generation would raise refinancing pressure.

Operating cash flow was KRW 6.37 billion in 2025, but free cash flow was reported at negative KRW 3.6 billion for the same year.

Listing maintenance requirements

Media reports say the market-capitalization listing standard will tighten to KRW 50 billion from 2027. Current market capitalization is below that threshold, so meeting it would require changes in the share price or the share structure.

Even after the March 2026 share consolidation, the largest shareholder's open-market purchases were used to defend the price, and volatility could increase now that those purchases have stopped.

Demand and cost volatility

Media reported in June 2026 that World Cup demand, a major event for chicken sellers, faded quickly after the national team was knocked out early. Because partial cuts and salted meat depend on franchise demand, results may be sensitive to restaurant trading conditions and store openings. How much raw-material cost can be passed through to selling prices remains a key driver of quarterly profit.

Overall view

On a 2025 annual basis, Maniker posted sales of KRW 368.9 billion and operating income of KRW 2.01 billion, turning from a 2024 operating loss to a profit.

However, operating losses have continued for three consecutive quarters from Q1 through Q3 2026, and net losses attributable to controlling shareholders over the last four quarters totaled KRW 4.63 billion.

Sales rose for two consecutive quarters in Q2 and Q3 2026, and the Q2 operating loss narrowed to KRW 16.02 million. The Q3 operating loss of KRW 1.21 billion widened again.

On the financing side, short-term borrowings of KRW 66.6 billion at the end of the first half of 2026 all fall due within one year, making liquidity management the key variable.

Shareholder returns have no plan because there is no distributable profit, and the market-capitalization standard tightening from 2027 is another factor for listing maintenance.

Bull factors are the narrowing losses and support from the largest shareholder, while bear factors are the consecutive operating losses and borrowing burden. The next checkpoints are the Q4 2026 results, the 2026 annual report, and how the short-term debt is repaid.
