DI: HBM Test Equipment Lifts Profit Base

02

Business structure

DI is a Korean equipment specialist whose core business is semiconductor test equipment.

Its main products are burn-in test equipment, which verifies whether chips operate under extreme conditions, final test equipment that screens finished products, and wafer memory test equipment (EDS) that detects defects at the wafer stage.

The company began by localizing NAND wafer testers and has since extended its coverage through DDR4, DDR5 and HBM3/3E to HBM4 test equipment. As an official Samsung Electronics vendor, the parent supplies burn-in equipment for DRAM and NAND.

Its consolidated subsidiary, in which DI holds a 65.2% stake, localized wafer test equipment and supplies HBM-related testers to SK hynix. The business therefore runs on two axes: the parent's burn-in and package test equipment, and the subsidiary's HBM wafer testers. Its main customers are two domestic memory makers.

Shinhan Investment Corp. said in an October 2026 report that these customers' oligopolistic market positions could let DI benefit from expanded back-end investment. Because customer dependence is high, changes in memory makers' equipment investment schedules move both delivery timing and revenue recognition. In industry comparison tables, DI is grouped with equipment makers such as HANMI Semiconductor.

04

Earnings analysis

From 2022 to 2024, DI's profitability weakened before rebounding in 2025.

Revenue was KRW 231 billion in 2022, KRW 214.5 billion in 2023 (down 7.1% year on year) and KRW 214 billion in 2024 (down 0.3%), while operating profit fell from KRW 11.2 billion in 2022 to KRW 6.14 billion in 2023 (down 45.0%) and KRW 3.08 billion in 2024 (down 49.8%).

In 2025, revenue reached KRW 432.3 billion (up 102.0%) and operating profit KRW 36.5 billion (up 1,084.8%), the largest year in the 2022-2025 period, and the operating margin rose from 1.4% in 2024 to 8.4%.

Net profit attributable to controlling shareholders fell from KRW 14.6 billion in 2022 to KRW 1.1 billion in 2024 (down 65.3%) before recovering to KRW 5.1 billion in 2025 (up 365.2%). Total net income, which includes non-controlling interests, was KRW 13.5 billion in 2025 and is a different figure.

Operating cash flow went from KRW 16.7 billion in 2022 and KRW 14.9 billion in 2023 to -KRW 14.9 billion in 2024, then turned to KRW 49 billion in 2025.

Over the same period the liabilities-to-equity ratio rose from 68.1% in 2022 to 101.1% in 2025, with total liabilities of KRW 187.9 billion exceeding total equity of KRW 185.9 billion at year-end 2025, of which KRW 163.6 billion was attributable to controlling shareholders.

Quarterly results bottomed in Q4 2025: revenue of KRW 93.9 billion (down 13.9% quarter on quarter), operating profit of KRW 4.33 billion (down 56.7%) and a controlling-shareholder net loss of KRW 11.2 billion, which turned the quarter loss-making from the prior quarter.

Operating profit then rose in each quarter from Q1 2026 (KRW 18.9 billion, up 336.0% quarter on quarter) through Q3 2026, when revenue reached KRW 217.7 billion and operating profit KRW 59.3 billion (up 493.1% year on year).

Controlling-shareholder net profit went from KRW 11 billion in Q1 2026, a swing to profit from the prior quarter, to KRW 23.9 billion in Q2 and KRW 34.4 billion in Q3, and the last four quarters total KRW 58.1 billion.

Securities analysts described Q3 2026 as a record-level result, and Hyundai Motor Securities put the Q3 operating margin at 27.3%, citing higher equipment shipments at the parent and early delivery of HBM equipment by the subsidiary Digital Frontier.

Because some volume planned for Q4 was brought forward into Q3, how much of the Q3 figure reflects timing will become clearer from the quarters that follow.

05

Industry analysis

The market DI serves is in a phase where back-end investment by Korean memory makers is lifting demand. Shinhan Investment Corp. noted in an October 2026 report that back-end investment is proceeding at a larger scale and pace than expected.

The same report cited the two customers' oligopolistic positions (DRAM share above 70%, NAND near-monopoly) and said DI could fully benefit from expanded investment.

In a May 2026 report, the same brokerage said both the parent and the subsidiary had entered the early stage of a growth phase and expected domestic memory investment to keep rising for at least two more years.

SK hynix is pursuing a new P&T7 facility in the Cheongju Technopolis industrial complex, and Hana Securities put the investment at about KRW 19 trillion in a February 2026 report. Once P&T7 is completed, SK hynix will have seven P&T facilities in total.

On the cycle, securities firms described Q3 2026 as a record-level quarter in an October 2026 report and expected growth to continue into next year.

In competition, DI's exposure to two domestic memory makers at once is cited as a differentiator, and industry comparison tables group DI with equipment makers such as HANMI Semiconductor. HBM4 wafer testers, which check reliability and defects at the wafer stage, are classified as a high-value product group.

However, if memory investment execution slips, equipment delivery and revenue recognition can slip with it, so industry timing is a variable for the earnings path.

Outlook

The largest disclosed order is a supply contract with SK hynix for HBM4 wafer testers, worth about KRW 99.8 billion, disclosed on January 20, 2026.

Samsung Electronics contracts followed, including a contract of about KRW 72.4 billion signed on June 9, 2026, a KRW 16.9155 billion contract disclosed on July 2, 2026, and a KRW 15.3 billion contract disclosed on October 1, 2026.

In an August 13, 2026 report, NEWS1 said DI had recently signed supply contracts with Samsung Electronics worth more than KRW 130 billion, and that the focus of second-half results is whether shipments to Samsung, including its China unit, convert into and are recognized as revenue.

Many of these contracts end on December 31, 2026, so whether deliveries and inspections become revenue by year-end is the second-half checkpoint. Analyst estimates for full-year 2026 cluster at revenue in the KRW 600 billion range and operating profit above KRW 120 billion.

Hyundai Motor Securities estimated KRW 607.1 billion in revenue and KRW 130 billion in operating profit in a November 2026 report, and Shinhan Investment Corp. estimated KRW 609 billion and KRW 124.2 billion in an October 2026 report.

Hyundai Motor Securities also projected 2027 consolidated revenue of KRW 771.5 billion and operating profit of KRW 167.7 billion, with operating margins above 20% continuing. Reports said Digital Frontier shipped a large volume of HBM customer wafer testers in Q3 that had been planned for Q4.

Whether Q4 revenue carries forward Q3-level deliveries or falls as timing shifts is therefore the next checkpoint. Among regular filings, the 2026 annual report is the first point to confirm the Q4 and full-year results, with full-year operating margin and cash flow set by disclosed figures.

08

Bull factors

HBM tester orders are accumulating

A supply contract for HBM4 wafer testers with SK hynix, worth about KRW 99.8 billion, was disclosed on January 20, 2026, and several Samsung Electronics test equipment contracts were disclosed from June to October.

The subsidiary Digital Frontier, in which DI holds 65.2%, handles HBM wafer testers, so the structure supplies both memory customers. Shinhan Investment Corp. cited securing both domestic memory makers as a basis for this strength.

Rapid recovery in operating margin

The operating margin rose from 1.4% in 2024 to 8.4% in 2025. According to Shinhan Investment Corp., the Q1 2026 operating margin was 17.9%, up sharply from 4.6% in Q4 2025. Hyundai Motor Securities put the Q3 2026 operating margin at 27.3%.

Brokerage target prices moved higher

Daishin Securities, Shinhan Investment Corp. and Hyundai Motor Securities said they raised their target prices on October 12, October 29 and November 3, 2026, respectively. Daishin Securities and Hyundai Motor Securities each cited KRW 60,000, and Shinhan Investment Corp. cited KRW 56,000. All three raised their targets, which signals higher earnings expectations among brokerages.

Bear factors

Quarterly earnings volatility

In Q4 2025 the controlling-shareholder net result swung to a KRW 11.2 billion loss from the prior quarter. Analysts said Q3 2026 results included HBM wafer testers shipped ahead of their planned Q4 timing. Early shipments can create a timing gap in revenue for later quarters.

Dependence on two customers

The main customers are two domestic memory makers, so a change in either one's equipment investment schedule can affect both orders and revenue recognition. The large 2026 disclosed contracts also concentrate on Samsung Electronics and SK hynix. Brokerage growth forecasts appear to assume that both customers keep investing.

Financial leverage and cash flow

The liabilities-to-equity ratio rose from 68.1% in 2022 to 101.1% in 2025, and total liabilities of KRW 187.9 billion exceeded total equity of KRW 185.9 billion at year-end 2025. Operating cash flow was -KRW 14.9 billion in 2024 before turning to KRW 49 billion in 2025. Whether the cash-flow recovery holds is a point to confirm in the 2026 annual report.

Risk factors

Customer equipment investment timing

If memory makers change their equipment investment schedules, DI's deliveries and revenue recognition shift with them. Many Samsung Electronics contracts end on December 31, 2026, so year-end inspections could decide quarterly revenue.

Shinhan Investment Corp. expects memory investment to rise for at least two more years, and if that assumption fails, the order flow would change too.

Timing gaps in quarterly revenue

Analysts said Q3 2026 revenue included volume planned for Q4, so Q4 revenue could come in below Q3. That timing effect will determine whether the quarter-on-quarter rise continues. Because operating profit has moved sharply with revenue, timing gaps can widen into earnings swings.

Possible revisions to brokerage estimates

Brokerage annual estimates put 2026 revenue in the KRW 600 billion range, but they could be revised after Q4 2026 results and 2027 expansion timing. Lower estimates would also change the basis for target prices. Target prices reflect assumptions set at different times, so they are hard to compare on one basis.

Overall view

DI's operating margin rose to 8.4% in 2025 from 1.4% in 2024, and quarterly earnings capacity has grown through Q3 2026. The growth centers on HBM wafer testers and burn-in test equipment for SK hynix and Samsung Electronics, and this equipment demand is tied to back-end investment by domestic memory makers.

Bullish factors are the large order flow, the improving operating margin and the upward trend in brokerage target prices. Bearish factors are the timing effect of early Q3 shipments, dependence on two customers, quarterly volatility after the Q4 2025 net loss, and the rise in the liabilities-to-equity ratio.

Interpretation of the current share price depends on how durable these two sets of factors prove to be.

The points to confirm are revenue recognition on Samsung Electronics volume whose contract period ends on December 31, 2026, preliminary Q4 2026 results in early 2027, and full-year cash flow and the liabilities-to-equity ratio in the 2026 annual report.

If deliveries stay near Q3 levels after Q4, the evidence for sustained equipment demand builds; if the early-delivery effect fades, a drop in quarterly revenue could show first. This report is for information only and does not include a buy or sell opinion.
