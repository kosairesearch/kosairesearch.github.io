02

Business structure

MS Autotech Company was founded in 1990 to manufacture and sell automotive body parts and listed on KOSDAQ in 2010. Its core products are hot-stamped structural components such as center pillars, bumper beams and side sills that are directly tied to crash safety.

Its customers include Hyundai Motor and Kia, and it is also reported to supply body parts to Tesla. Because it is a first-tier supplier to the Hyundai Motor Group, its results move with vehicle production volumes and new model programs.

The company moved to a holding-company structure through a physical spin-off completed in August 2024, and WiseReport's company monitor describes one-off costs from the restructuring.

Automotive body parts are the core of sales, while the mold and equipment segment's sales fell sharply in the third quarter of 2024 according to press reports.

Exports were the larger share of revenue: as reported in 2024, export sales reached KRW 1.38 trillion in 2023, up 17.8% year on year, while domestic sales were KRW 700.7 billion, up 2.3%. The group structure also matters.

As reported in 2024, the company held an 89.4% stake in its unlisted EV-parts subsidiary and a 33.9% stake in Myoung Shin Industry. That subsidiary was reported to have posted an operating loss of KRW 37.5 billion and a net loss of KRW 53.5 billion in 2023. In body parts, it competes with peers such as Sungwoo Hitech.

04

Earnings analysis

Based on reported results, annual revenue peaked at KRW 2,081.8 billion in 2023 (up 2.8% year on year) from KRW 2,025.1 billion in 2022.

It then declined for two consecutive years, from 2024 to 2025, falling to KRW 1,777.5 billion in 2024 (down 14.6% year on year) and KRW 1,740.6 billion in 2025 (down 2.1% year on year). Operating profit fell even more sharply.

It went from KRW 174.8 billion in 2023 (up 17.3% year on year) to KRW 96.3 billion in 2024 (down 44.9% year on year) and KRW 31.3 billion in 2025 (down 67.5% year on year), and the operating margin declined from 8.4% in 2023 to 5.4% in 2024 and 1.8% in 2025.

The company explained that the decline in 2025 earnings was due to a partial drop in revenue, higher cost burdens from tariffs and other expenses, and increased financial costs such as interest expense and losses on bond redemption.

Net profit attributable to controlling shareholders fell from KRW 43.4 billion in 2022 to KRW 12.0 billion in 2023 (down 72.3% year on year) and KRW 1.59 billion in 2024 (down 86.8% year on year), and in 2025 it swung to a net loss of KRW 108.6 billion.

Net loss for the same year (including non-controlling interests) was KRW 74.9 billion, smaller than the loss attributable to controlling shareholders, which means the non-controlling interests' share was positive.

Looking at the quarterly trend, third-quarter 2025 revenue was KRW 467.5 billion and operating profit was KRW 13.9 billion, but in the fourth quarter revenue was KRW 428.9 billion (down 8.3% quarter on quarter) and there was an operating loss of KRW 12.3 billion.

Operating profit returned to positive at KRW 11.9 billion in the first quarter of 2026, and in the second quarter revenue was KRW 504.8 billion (up 20.2% quarter on quarter) and operating profit was KRW 35.5 billion (up 197.4% quarter on quarter), the highest of the last five quarters.

Third-quarter operating profit fell again to KRW 21.0 billion (down 40.9% quarter on quarter).

Net loss attributable to controlling shareholders was KRW 16.3 billion in the third quarter of 2025, KRW 28.6 billion in the fourth quarter, KRW 4.8 billion in the first quarter of 2026, KRW 14.0 billion in the second quarter, and KRW 6.23 billion in the third quarter, for a total of KRW 53.6 billion over the last four quarters.

Operating profit has recovered, but the structure is one in which losses remain on a controlling-shareholder basis. Company Monitor tallied that first-quarter 2026 operating profit fell 47.5% from the same period a year earlier, but this is a figure compiled by an external source.

05

Industry analysis

MS Autotech Company's end market is domestic and overseas vehicle production. Its heavy dependence on the Hyundai Motor Group means that vehicle output drives its revenue, and when industry activity slows, body-parts volumes tend to decline with it.

In 2025 press reports pointed to the chip supply shortage, production disruptions at automakers, and the upfront investment burden of its EV parts business as causes of the earnings decline. Tariffs also acted as a cost pressure.

A 2024 analysis expected a limited recovery in body-parts sales because of a global economic slowdown and constrained auto demand.

The most direct evidence of where the company sits in the cycle is its own earnings trend: revenue declines and margin compression overlapped from 2024 onward, and operating profit recovered in the second quarter of 2026. On competition, the body-parts market includes domestic players such as Sungwoo Hitech.

Hot-stamping technology has registered patents in Korea and abroad, and WiseReport's company monitor notes that new technology patents, including a battery thermal runaway barrier system for EVs, have been filed to prepare for future mobility markets.

On the subsidiary, a company official said in 2024 that the company was working to improve Myungshin's results. Other growth plans, such as new technology patents, remain medium-term items where the company has not set out dated milestones.

08

Bull factors

Signs of operating profit recovery

Operating profit was positive for three straight quarters from the first quarter through the third quarter of 2026, and the KRW 35.5 billion in the second quarter was the largest of the last five quarters. Third-quarter operating profit of KRW 21 billion was up 51.0% from a year earlier.

Because the margin had fallen to 1.8% in 2025, the recovery can be read as evidence of room for cost structure improvement.

Customer base and technology assets

In addition to domestic automakers such as Hyundai Motor and Kia, the company is reported to supply body parts to Tesla. Its hot-stamping technology holds patents in Korea and abroad, and new technology patents such as a battery thermal runaway barrier system have also been filed. These assets could matter if demand for body parts continues during the shift to electric vehicles.

Gradual improvement in the balance sheet

The consolidated debt ratio declined from 233.9% in 2022 to 166.2% in 2025. Operating cash flow stayed positive at KRW 104.9 billion in 2025, showing that operations continued to generate cash. Conversion of exchangeable bonds would also reduce debt.

Bear factors

Two years of decline and prolonged losses

Revenue declined in both 2024 and 2025, and the operating margin fell to 1.8% in 2025. The net loss attributable to controlling shareholders was KRW 108.6 billion in 2025, and losses have continued for five straight quarters from the third quarter of 2025 through the third quarter of 2026. The four-quarter total is KRW 53.6 billion.

Subsidiary losses and financing burden

The unlisted subsidiary was reported to have posted a KRW 37.5 billion operating loss in 2023. Press reports in 2024 said the company funded a KRW 100 billion loan to the subsidiary through exchangeable bonds and raised close to KRW 200 billion through three issues.

A KRW 130 billion private placement at an 8% rate followed in late September 2025. If interest costs rise, the operating recovery may not translate into shareholder-level profit.

Cost, tariff and chip supply pressure

Tariffs and higher costs were cited as drivers of the 2025 earnings decline. WiseReport's company monitor noted that profitability weakened in the first quarter of 2026 due to cost pressure and higher expenses. Chip shortages and automaker production disruptions remain sources of volume volatility.

Risk factors

Customer concentration risk

Revenue is tied to a small set of automakers, mainly the Hyundai Motor Group and Tesla. A 2024 report said results at the subsidiary's Gunsan plant deteriorated sharply as EV demand weakened. Changes in customers' production plans or EV strategies could shift volumes quickly.

Financial leverage risk

At the end of 2025, total liabilities were KRW 1.06 trillion and total equity was KRW 637.4 billion, of which KRW 183.3 billion is attributable to controlling shareholders. Reports said exchanges of bonds into shares could weaken control as held stakes change hands. With private bond financing at around 8%, interest costs could weigh on earnings.

Subsidiary support risk

If losses at the subsidiary persist, the parent may need to keep providing funding and loan guarantees. A 2024 report said the company provided loans and guarantees to the subsidiary. Larger support would add to consolidated financial strain and to the volatility of shareholder-level results.

Overall view

In 2026 operating profit was positive for three straight quarters at KRW 11.9 billion, KRW 35.5 billion and KRW 21 billion, but losses attributable to controlling shareholders have persisted for five quarters, totaling KRW 53.6 billion over the last four.

On the balance sheet, the consolidated debt ratio fell from 233.9% in 2022 to 166.2% in 2025, yet financing through private bonds, including KRW 130 billion placed in late September 2025, has continued. Losses and support obligations at the subsidiary remain a group-level risk.
