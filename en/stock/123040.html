Holding Co. Transition: Q2 Rebound Amid Ongoing Losses

02

Business structure

MS Autotech Company was established in 1990 as an auto body-parts specialist and listed on KOSDAQ in 2010. Through an August 2024 spin-off, the company split into surviving holding entity MS Autotech and newly formed MS Autosys, converting the group into a holding-company structure.

After the split, the legacy body-parts manufacturing and sales business is now run entirely by wholly owned subsidiary MS Autosys, while MS Autotech itself functions mainly as a holding company managing equity stakes.

Key subsidiaries and affiliates include listed hot-stamping maker Myoung Shin Industry (45.4% stake), Myoung Shin (89.6%), MS Energytech (100%), MS-T (28%), and TM Engineering (23.5%), with a total of 19 entities under the group including Myoung Shin Industry.

Myoung Shin Industry, founded in 1982, is a Tier-1 vendor to Hyundai and Kia and also ships hot-stamping parts directly to Tesla through its U.S. plant, with Tesla reportedly accounting for about 70% of its total revenue.

Prior to the split, MS Autotech acquired a former GM plant in Gunsan, South Korea in June 2019 to enter EV contract manufacturing, but as of a 2025 media report it had reportedly yet to reach meaningful production.

Within Korea's body-parts sector, Sungwoo Hitech, Seoyon E-Hwa, and SL are considered major peers, while Myoung Shin Industry is cited as one of the recognized technology leaders in hot stamping.

Overall, the group combines a traditional body-parts business serving established automakers with an EV-focused hot-stamping business.

04

Earnings analysis

Annual revenue peaked at KRW 2.08 trillion in 2023 (up 2.8% YoY from KRW 2.03 trillion in 2022), then declined for two straight years to KRW 1.78 trillion in 2024 (down 14.6%) and KRW 1.74 trillion in 2025 (down 2.1%).

Operating profit followed the same pattern, peaking at KRW 174.8 billion in 2023 (up 17.3% YoY) before falling for two consecutive years to KRW 96.3 billion in 2024 (down 44.9%) and KRW 31.3 billion in 2025 (down 67.5%), with the operating margin sliding from 8.4% in 2023 to 1.8% in 2025.

Controlling-shareholder net income shrank steadily from KRW 43.4 billion in 2022 to KRW 12 billion in 2023 (down 72.3%) and KRW 1.59 billion in 2024 (down 86.8%), before turning into a controlling-shareholder net loss of KRW 108.6 billion in 2025.

Net income including non-controlling interests also swung from KRW 113 billion in 2022, KRW 91.7 billion in 2023 and KRW 77.4 billion in 2024 to a net loss of KRW 74.9 billion in 2025.

The company's disclosures cited declining revenue, higher tariffs and raw-material costs, and rising financial costs including interest expense and bond-redemption losses as drivers of the deterioration.

By quarter, revenue rose from KRW 441.8 billion in Q2 2025 to KRW 467.5 billion in Q3 2025 (up 5.8% QoQ), then softened to KRW 428.9 billion in Q4 2025 (down 8.3%) and KRW 420.1 billion in Q1 2026 (down 2.0%), before rebounding to KRW 504.8 billion in Q2 2026 (up 20.2% QoQ, up 14.3% YoY).

Operating profit swung to an operating loss of KRW 12.3 billion in Q4 2025 before turning profitable again at KRW 11.9 billion in Q1 2026 and improving sharply to KRW 35.5 billion in Q2 2026 (up 197.4% QoQ, up 407.6% YoY).

Controlling-shareholder net losses continued for five consecutive quarters from Q2 2025 (KRW 51 billion) through Q2 2026 (KRW 14 billion), with the loss size alternately narrowing and widening each quarter, bringing the trailing four-quarter (Q3 2025-Q2 2026) controlling-shareholder net loss total to KRW 63.6 billion.

The debt ratio rose back to 166.2% at end-2025 from 156.3% a year earlier, while operating cash flow has trended down from KRW 291.6 billion in 2023 to KRW 104.9 billion in 2025.

05

Industry analysis

Slowing global EV demand and automaker production adjustments drove a broad profitability decline across the auto-parts industry in 2024-2025, and the MS Autotech group's earnings trajectory tracked this industry cycle.

Hot-stamping-focused subsidiary Myoung Shin Industry's factory utilization fell from 85.7% in 2023 to 75.2% in 2024, a decline attributed to fixed-cost burdens from earlier capacity expansions coinciding with the demand slowdown.

Myoung Shin Industry's preliminary 2025 settlement (disclosed February 25, 2026, provisional) showed revenue edging up to KRW 1.6191 trillion while operating profit and net income reportedly fell 32% and 49% YoY, respectively.

Signs of a rebound have emerged in 2026, however, as Myoung Shin Industry's Texas-based Simwon North America unit reportedly posted a 44% YoY revenue increase in Q2 2026 on higher customer production.

Following Tesla's demonstration of its new Roadster on October 15, 2026, reports noted increased investor interest in Myoung Shin Industry given its North American revenue exposure.

Within Korea's body-parts sector, peers such as Sungwoo Hitech, Seoyon E-Hwa, and SL face similar exposure to end-automaker demand, currency moves, and tariff conditions, with U.S. and Chinese tariff policy and exchange-rate swings acting as common cost variables across the industry.

The EV-specific hot-stamping parts market is heavily dependent on a small number of customers including Tesla, making those customers' production plans and new-model launch schedules key variables for the industry outlook.

Outlook

As of an April 22, 2026 report, the company was pursuing issuance of KRW 54 billion in exchangeable bonds backed by its Myoung Shin Industry stake, with Shinhan Investment arranging the deal. This was interpreted as a move to reduce bond interest costs while securing operating funds for subsidiaries.

Around the same period, reports indicated MS Autotech had recovered a large amount of capital from subsidiary Myoung Shin Industry and disposed of its Indian subsidiary to bolster cash.

Since converting to a holding-company structure, the group has stated a goal of gaining flexibility to restructure through transfers or spin-offs of underperforming affiliates and businesses, suggesting further disclosures on equity stake adjustments or financing could follow.

On the Myoung Shin Industry side, reports have discussed expectations tied to Tesla's new Roadster demonstration and potential Cybercab-related orders as variables shaping the direction of the Tesla-facing business.

However, whether such new-model orders or production schedules have been concretely finalized remains unconfirmed, and the timing and scale of any resulting revenue will need ongoing monitoring through quarterly disclosures.

MS Autosys, which runs the core auto-parts business, posted higher revenue YoY in Q1 2026, but profitability reportedly deteriorated due to cost pressures, with temporary restructuring costs tied to the holding-company conversion also cited as a factor.

Bull factors

Operating profit also surged to KRW 35.5 billion, up 197.4% quarter-on-quarter and up 407.6% year-on-year, marking a recovery from the operating loss in Q4 2025 and extending profitability to two consecutive quarters. Reports also noted that investor sentiment toward Myungshin Industry was stimulated following Tesla's demonstration of its new Roadster in October 2026. If orders related to Tesla's new models materialize, this could be a positive variable for the group's overall performance.

Trading at a Discount to Net Asset Value

Both revenue and operating profit declined for two consecutive years in 2024 and 2025 after peaking in 2023. In 2025, the company posted a net loss attributable to controlling shareholders of KRW 108.6 billion and a net loss of KRW 74.9 billion, turning to losses. Five Straight Quarters of Controlling-Shareholder Net Losses

Net losses attributable to controlling shareholders continued for five consecutive quarters from Q2 2025 through Q2 2026. In Q2 2026, the loss widened again compared to the previous quarter.

Cumulative losses over the most recent four quarters reached KRW 63.6 billion, suggesting that improvement in the financial structure may take time.

Financial Leverage and Funding Burden

The debt-to-equity ratio at the end of 2025 rose again to 166.2% compared to the previous year. The company has shown reliance on external funding, including pursuing the issuance of KRW 54 billion in exchangeable bonds in April 2026.

Since rising financial costs were cited as one of the causes of the deteriorating 2025 results, additional interest burden could constrain the pace of profitability recovery.

Risk factors

Financial Structure & Liquidity

The debt-to-equity ratio stood at 166.2% at the end of 2025, and the company has reportedly raised funds through private placement bonds and exchangeable bonds. Operating cash flow has been on a declining trend, falling from KRW 291.6 billion in 2023 to KRW 104.9 billion in 2025.

As rising financial costs have been cited as one of the factors behind the deteriorating performance, the burden from additional borrowing or changes in the interest rate environment could persist.

Customer Concentration Risk

Myungshin Industry, the subsidiary handling the hot stamping business, is reportedly dependent on Tesla for about 70% of its total revenue. Changes in Tesla's production plans or new model launch schedules could directly affect Myungshin Industry's utilization rate and performance.

Indeed, the factory utilization rate declined in 2024 compared to 2023, confirming the volatility associated with dependence on a specific customer.

Tariff & Exchange-Rate Variables

The company has cited tariffs and rising costs as causes of the deteriorated performance in 2025. Changes in tariff policies and exchange rate fluctuations in major markets, including the US and China, are variables that can directly affect the cost structure.

Given the group's characteristic of having overseas production bases, its exposure to these external variables is relatively high.

Overall view

Since converting to a holding company structure in August 2024, MS Autotech has divided its business into the automotive parts business under its subsidiary MS Autosys and the hot stamping-based EV parts business under its listed subsidiary Myungshin Industry.

Based on disclosed results, both revenue and operating profit declined for two consecutive years after peaking in 2023, and the company turned to a net loss attributable to controlling shareholders of KRW 108.6 billion in 2025, with net losses attributable to controlling shareholders continuing for five consecutive quarters from Q2 2025 through Q2 2026.

However, in Q2 2026, revenue and operating profit improved significantly compared to both the previous quarter and the same period last year, showing signs of a profitability recovery.

The subsidiary Myungshin Industry has a structure heavily weighted toward Tesla-bound revenue, and expectations around increased Texas subsidiary revenue and potential new model-related orders have been raised in 2026, though specific order confirmations have not been verified.

On the financial side, the debt-to-equity ratio of 166.2% at the end of 2025 and continued reliance on external funding warrant close monitoring of the pace of financial structure improvement.

Before making investment decisions, it is important to verify whether the profitability recovery is sustained through upcoming quarterly disclosures and subsidiary-related events. This report is not intended as an investment solicitation and does not include a buy or sell recommendation.
