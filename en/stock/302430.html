02

Business structure

Innometry is a manufacturer of X-ray and CT non-destructive inspection equipment, founded in June 2008. Its core products detect internal defects in batteries without damaging them, and it has supplied inspection systems to Korean battery makers and to BYD and BAK in China.

Its lineup covers process-specific equipment such as electrode alignment checks, micro foreign-object detection and full-cell inline CT inspection, and its application range has extended to ESS, 46-series cylindrical and all-solid-state batteries.

Smallinsight Research said in October 2026 that Innometry keeps the leading global share in non-destructive battery inspection. Revenue moves with battery makers' capital spending schedules, and growth in overseas maintenance, repair and operations (MRO) revenue is cited as a variable for margin improvement.

New businesses cover internal defect inspection of glass substrate through-glass vias (TGV), FPCB inspection for foldable smartphones, and inline CT inspection for HBM.

TGV inspection equipment has been supplied to JWMT, Extol and Anycasting, producing initial revenue, while the HBM inspection technology is in mass-production validation with a global semiconductor maker.

On competition, long-standing delivery records with battery makers are cited as a barrier to entry in battery inspection, whereas semiconductor inspection is at an early stage where customer qualification is decisive.

The business remains centered on secondary battery inspection, and semiconductor inspection still contributes little revenue.

04

Earnings analysis

On a fiscal-year basis, revenue was largest in 2023 at KRW 83.8 billion, then fell 36.7% to KRW 53 billion in 2024 and 27.2% to KRW 38.6 billion in 2025, a decline in each of the last two years.

Operating profit was KRW 8.21 billion in 2023, up 117.7% year on year, before turning to an operating loss of KRW 7.23 billion in 2024; the 2025 operating loss of KRW 5.7 billion was smaller than in 2024.

The company attributed the 2025 revenue decline to major battery customers deferring capital spending into 2026, as reported in February 2026. Net profit attributable to controlling shareholders went from KRW 7.81 billion in 2023 to a net loss of KRW 1.47 billion in 2024 and KRW 4.28 billion in 2025, a widening loss.

Net income including non-controlling interests was a loss of KRW 4.3 billion in 2025, which differs from the controlling-shareholder figure. The operating margin fell from 5.0% in 2022 and 9.8% in 2023 to -13.6% in 2024 and -14.8% in 2025.

The debt-to-equity ratio dropped from 43.5% in 2022 to 21.8% in 2024 before rising to 29.3% in 2025. Operating cash flow was KRW -2.29 billion in 2023, KRW 14.8 billion in 2024 and KRW 5.68 billion in 2025.

Quarterly, the first quarter of 2026 was the revenue peak at KRW 17.5 billion with a KRW 246 million operating profit that turned the quarter profitable, but the second and third quarters posted operating losses of KRW 2.16 billion and KRW 1.02 billion, two consecutive quarters of losses.

Third-quarter 2026 revenue rose 38.2% year on year, while the operating loss widened over the same period.

The company credited the first-quarter turn to profit to resumed customer orders and cost-structure improvements, and noted that net income in that quarter was partly lifted by non-operating items such as foreign-currency translation gains.

05

Industry analysis

Demand for battery inspection equipment is shaped by the shift of EV production lines toward ESS. Smallinsight Research analyzed that when lines are converted to ESS or cell formats change, electrode structure, cell size and inspection items also change, so inspection equipment must be replaced.

Customer orders contracted in 2025 amid a slowdown in EVs and delayed investment across the global battery industry, and the company said orders resumed in 2026. The expansion of 46-series cylindrical batteries is also cited as a driver of demand for full inline CT inspection.

Innometry shipped 46-series volume-line equipment in August 2026 for an order received in February. In semiconductors, glass substrates and HBM are cited as medium-term growth areas, but both are still in customer validation, so revenue is limited.

In competition, long-standing delivery records in battery inspection are cited as helping the company absorb line conversions and capacity additions at existing customers.

As a sector reference, Alphasquare's electrical equipment peer group of 80 stocks (full-year 2025) shows an average operating margin of about -4.7%, above Innometry's -14.8%. This peer average is a general electrical-equipment benchmark rather than a semiconductor-specific measure and is offered for reference only.

06

Outlook

In its first-quarter 2026 results announcement, the company said it held a backlog of about KRW 28.5 billion at the end of the first quarter and expected results to improve gradually through the second half.

The second and third quarters, however, saw revenue fall for two consecutive quarters and operating losses persist, so whether this outlook materializes is the next point to check. On costs, a cost-reduction task force has operated since September 2026 with a target of a 30% reduction at the design stage.

Improvements in project-level cost ratios and product mix, along with growth in overseas maintenance revenue, are cited as key variables for restoring operating profit.

On business milestones, a supply contract worth about KRW 4.96 billion for ESS inspection equipment bound for the United States was signed in August 2026, and 46-series inline CT equipment shipped the same month.

Two FPCB inspection units for foldable smartphones have been supplied, with one more scheduled for delivery in the fourth quarter of 2026. Glass-substrate inspection is targeted to add meaningful revenue from 2027, and the next-generation helical CT is being developed for mass-production use in 2027.

HBM inline CT is in mass-production validation with a global chipmaker, and the company targets supply to a global customer within 2026.

Smallinsight Research's October 2026 report said further improvement is needed before earnings normalize, and that restored profitability and actual mass-production adoption of TGV and HBM equipment would mark the turning point for a re-rating.

Third-quarter 2026 revenue rose year on year, but the operating loss widened, so the second-half improvement the company described is not yet visible in profitability.

Bull factors

Battery inspection share and order recovery

Smallinsight Research assessed in October 2026 that Innometry holds the leading share of the global non-destructive battery inspection market.

Secondary-battery inspection orders in the first half of 2026 were about KRW 22 billion, and in August 2026 a supply contract worth KRW 4.96 billion for U.S.-bound ESS inspection equipment was signed. The backlog at the end of the first quarter of 2026 was about KRW 28.5 billion.

Early revenue and validation in new businesses

TGV inspection equipment has been supplied to three customers, producing initial revenue. Inline CT for 46-series volume lines shipped in August 2026, and two FPCB inspection units for foldable smartphones have been delivered. HBM inline CT is in mass-production validation with a global semiconductor maker.

Financial room and shareholder-return intent

The debt-to-equity ratio was 29.3% at the end of 2025, below 43.5% in 2022. In November 2025 the company stressed debt-free management and cash holdings above KRW 30 billion as room for talent and new-business investment. In August 2026 it decided to cancel treasury shares.

09

Bear factors

Persistent losses and a low operating margin

The company posted operating losses in both 2024 and 2025, and losses continued in the second and third quarters of 2026. The net loss attributable to controlling shareholders over the last four quarters totals KRW 8.03 billion. The 2025 operating margin was -14.8%.

Lower gross margin and cost pressure

According to Smallinsight Research, first-half 2026 revenue rose but gross margin fell from 20.6% to 11.8%. R&D and up-front investment for new businesses limited the gain in operating profit. The 30% cost-reduction target is a design-stage plan that has not yet appeared in reported results.

Dependence on customer orders and new-business timing

The 2025 revenue decline was attributed to major customers pushing capital spending into 2026. Third-quarter 2026 revenue fell from the prior quarter. TGV revenue is targeted to grow from 2027, and HBM supply timing is not fixed while mass-production validation continues.

10

Risk factors

Customer capex timing

Battery makers' capital spending moves with EV demand and the pace of the shift to ESS, and delays in 2025 cut revenue. Because orders tend to cluster in specific periods, quarterly results are volatile. Reliance on particular customers and regional projects adds to this swing.

Profitability and cost execution

The operating margin has been negative since 2024, and gross margin has continued to decline. Whether cost-reduction targets are reflected in actual product design and manufacturing efficiency is still unproven.

Foreign-currency translation gains and losses can swing net income, so the reported figure may differ from the controlling-shareholder figure.

New-business validation and technology competition

HBM and TGV inspection equipment must pass customer mass-production validation before it becomes meaningful revenue. Expansion into new businesses has raised R&D and hiring costs. In semiconductor inspection, business pace can change sharply depending on customer qualification results.

12

Overall view

Innometry is cited as holding the leading share of global battery inspection, and its revenue has recovered in 2026 as battery orders resumed. The first quarter of 2026 turned operating and net income positive, but operating losses continued in the second and third quarters.

The 2025 operating margin was -14.8%, and the net loss attributable to controlling shareholders over the last four quarters totals KRW 8.03 billion. Strengths include resumed battery orders, supply of 46-series and ESS equipment, mass-production validation of TGV and HBM businesses, and a debt-to-equity ratio of 29.3%.

Weaknesses include a lower gross margin, uncertain execution of the cost-reduction target, earnings swings tied to customer capital spending, and new-business revenue that is expected to become meaningful only from 2027.

Because no earnings-based multiple is calculable, the comparison with net assets and the trend in operating margin are the most direct reference points for valuation.

The next checkpoints are HBM supply and the fourth-quarter FPCB delivery by late December 2026, along with the operating margin in the 2026 annual business report.
