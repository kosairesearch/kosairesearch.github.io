Report overview

Optical parts growth, but four straight loss quarters

A-Tech Solution is a die and precision parts maker for the auto sector. Revenue has grown, but operating losses have continued for four straight quarters from the fourth quarter of 2025 through the third quarter of 2026, widening the gap between sales growth and profitability.

  1. 1

    Revenue rose from KRW 249.5 billion in 2022 to KRW 305.5 billion in 2025, while the operating margin fell from 2.6% to 0.2% over the same period.

  2. 2

    Third-quarter 2026 revenue was KRW 85 billion, up 23.2% year on year, but the company posted an operating loss of KRW 488 million and a net loss attributable to controlling shareholders of KRW 2.33 billion.

  3. 3

  4. 4

  5. 5

02

Business structure

A-Tech Solution was spun off in 2001 from the mold division of Samsung Electronics and listed on KOSDAQ in 2009.

The business is split into large plastic injection molds, press molds, and precision parts assembled through injection molding, and a 2026 second-half company analysis adds optical products such as plastic and silicone lenses and head-up display components as a third pillar.

By segment, precision parts made up 46.8% of sales in 2023, molds 36.6%, and press dies 16.5%. Based on a tally by the financial data site Butler, the mix in the second quarter of 2026 was molds 45%, precision parts 43%, and press dies 13%.

By end-market, automotive accounted for 68.7% of sales in 2023, home appliances 21.8%, optical parts 7.4%, and other 2.1%. Hyundai Mobis is the largest automotive customer, receiving plastic injection and assembly work for Hyundai Motor headlamps as well as lens parts for head-up displays.

In 2023, Hyundai Motor and Kia together accounted for 23.0% of automotive-related sales and Seoyon E-Hwa for 15.0%, while the Samsung group made up 29.2% in the third quarter of 2023. Overseas customers reportedly include GM, Ford, Magna, Denso, Bosch, Panasonic and Honda.

Samsung Electronics was the second-largest shareholder with a 15.92% stake as of 2023. The company cites close-to-design customer involvement from the die design stage and delivery capability as its strengths.

04

Earnings analysis

Annual revenue rose from KRW 249.5 billion in 2022 to KRW 268.5 billion in 2023 (up 7.6% year on year), KRW 289.5 billion in 2024 (up 7.8%) and KRW 305.5 billion in 2025 (up 5.5%), marking three consecutive years of growth from 2023 through 2025.

Over the same span, operating profit fell from KRW 6.46 billion in 2022 to KRW 3.94 billion in 2023 (down 39.1%) and KRW 492 million in 2024 (down 87.5%), before recovering to KRW 611 million in 2025 (up 24.2%).

A March 2024 report by the Korea IR Council attributed the 2023 decline to steel prices rising faster than purchase costs while mold selling prices stayed flat for years, compressing product margins. The operating margin moved from 2.6% in 2022 to 1.5% in 2023 and 0.2% in both 2024 and 2025.

Net income attributable to controlling shareholders fell from KRW 4.7 billion in 2022 to KRW 1.59 billion in 2023 (down 66.1%), KRW 612 million in 2024 (down 61.6%) and KRW 461 million in 2025 (down 24.6%), marking three consecutive years of decline from 2023.

Net income including non-controlling interests was KRW 330 million in 2025, below the controlling-shareholder figure, which suggests the non-controlling share was a loss.

Operating cash flow was -KRW 4.58 billion in 2022, -KRW 4.38 billion in 2023, KRW 545 million in 2024 and -KRW 1.15 billion in 2025, so outside 2024 it stayed negative.

On a quarterly basis, revenue of KRW 69 billion with operating profit of KRW 123 million in the third quarter of 2025 gave way to KRW 94.1 billion in the fourth quarter (up 36.5% from the prior quarter), but the company swung to an operating loss of KRW 1.56 billion.

Revenue then came in at KRW 68.7 billion in the first quarter of 2026 (down 27.0%) with an operating loss of KRW 949 million, followed by KRW 73 billion with a KRW 328 million loss in the second quarter and KRW 85 billion with a KRW 488 million loss in the third quarter, so revenue has risen for two straight quarters since the second quarter.

The operating loss narrowed in the second quarter and widened again in the third.

Net income attributable to controlling shareholders moved from KRW 102 million in the fourth quarter of 2025 to a KRW 907 million loss in the first quarter of 2026, then KRW 725 million and KRW 2.33 billion losses in the second and third quarters.

The cumulative net loss attributable to controlling shareholders over the last four quarters was KRW 3.86 billion. Because the third-quarter net loss was much larger than the operating loss, the gap has to be read through non-operating items and non-controlling interests in the detailed notes.

05

Industry analysis

End-market demand is shifting from home appliances toward automotive. A March 2024 report by the Korea IR Council said automotive molds had expanded to 68.7% of sales by 2023.

Expanding autonomous driving is cited as a driver of demand for optical components such as head-up display lenses, headlamps, and camera and sensor lenses. The company also says it is developing technology for LiDAR and smart-car parts.

On the other side, demand for home-appliance molds for Samsung Electronics declined after the COVID-19 period ended, a risk the same report flagged. Raw materials remain a variable, since steel price increases have squeezed product margins.

The 2024 report viewed stable raw-material costs, material diversification, mold standardization and AI-based production management as sources of margin improvement.

Against larger listed auto-parts peers such as Hyundai Mobis, Hanon Systems and HL Mando, A-Tech Solution has a smaller revenue base and remains sensitive to changes in order volumes from its customers.

In molds, design capability and delivery speed are the factors that tend to decide awards, and the company presents these as its strengths. In optics, its autonomous-driving sensor and lens design capability is positioned as its differentiator.

With home-appliance and automotive demand moving in different directions, how fast the automotive share rises will determine how well the company adapts to its market.

06

Outlook

The most specific outlook among the figures already public is the growth path for optical parts.

A March 2024 report by the Korea IR Council projected optical parts sales of KRW 13.1 billion in 2022 and KRW 26.5 billion in 2023, rising to KRW 43 billion in 2024 and KRW 79 billion in 2026, a compound annual growth rate of 56.7% for 2022 to 2026.

The same report expected optical parts sales to reach roughly KRW 100 billion from 2027 onward. That report also projected 2024 consolidated revenue of KRW 290.5 billion and operating profit of KRW 5.1 billion.

Reported 2024 figures were revenue of KRW 289.5 billion and operating profit of KRW 492 million, so revenue came close to the projection while operating profit fell well short. Whether the KRW 79 billion optical parts projection for 2026 materialized will be visible in the segment sales in the 2026 annual report.

The optical lineup cited for expansion includes head-up display lenses, headlamps, camera and sensor lenses, and aspheric plastic and silicone lenses. Third-quarter 2026 revenue rose 23.2%, but operating losses continue, so whether higher sales translate into a better operating margin is the next point to watch.

Hyundai Mobis headlamp and vehicle lens volumes form a core part of the revenue base, so those volumes weigh heavily on total sales. The direction of the mold business depends on whether home-appliance demand recovers.

Preliminary fourth-quarter 2026 results are the first checkpoint for whether the third-quarter operating loss trend persists. The 2026 annual report, with segment sales and operating margin disclosed together, will then show how far the optical shift has progressed in numbers.

Bull factors

Growth path in optical parts

A March 2024 Korea IR Council report noted optical parts sales more than doubled from KRW 13.1 billion in 2022 to KRW 26.5 billion in 2023 and projected KRW 79 billion for 2026. Optical parts are still a small share of total sales but were framed as a business with room to grow alongside autonomous driving.

If the 2026 projection shows up in segment sales in the 2026 annual report, the shift in business mix will be visible in numbers.

Continued top-line growth

Revenue has grown for three consecutive years from 2023 through 2025, reaching KRW 305.5 billion in 2025. Third-quarter 2026 revenue of KRW 85 billion was up 23.2% year on year, and sales have risen for two straight quarters since the second quarter of 2026. The operating margin is thin, but a larger revenue base is a scale advantage.

Share price low relative to book value

The price-to-book ratio is well below 1x, and total equity stands at KRW 88.9 billion. On the automotive side, the company's customers include Hyundai Mobis, Hyundai Motor and Kia, and Seoyon E-Hwa. A share price this low relative to book value serves as a reference point if business recovery is confirmed.

Bear factors

Four straight quarters of operating losses

Operating losses have run from the fourth quarter of 2025 through the third quarter of 2026.

The third-quarter operating loss of KRW 488 million was larger than the KRW 328 million loss in the second quarter, and the third-quarter net loss attributable to controlling shareholders of KRW 2.33 billion was the largest in the period. The core bearish point is that rising sales have not converted into profit.

Balance-sheet strain and cash flow

The debt-to-equity ratio rose from 190.7% in 2022 to 263.7% in 2025, with total liabilities of KRW 234.4 billion at the end of 2025 far exceeding total equity of KRW 88.9 billion. Operating cash flow was -KRW 1.15 billion in 2025. Cash generation is weak while the debt burden is rising.

Customer concentration and cost pressure

In 2023, Hyundai Motor and Kia and Seoyon E-Hwa together made up a large share of automotive-related sales, and the Samsung group accounted for 29.2% in the third quarter of 2023. Order changes from a few customers can move total revenue. Because mold prices have stayed flat, rising steel costs compress margins first.

Risk factors

Cost and margin

Steel and other raw material costs rising faster than mold selling prices was cited as the cause of the 2023 profit decline. If cost stability does not materialize, an operating margin near 0.2% could persist. Revenue growth without relief on costs would keep the loss structure in place.

Balance sheet and cash flow

A debt-to-equity ratio of 263.7% and total liabilities of KRW 234.4 billion warrant attention on interest burden and borrowing capacity. With operating cash flow at -KRW 1.15 billion in 2025, capital spending or debt repayment could further tighten finances. The detailed borrowing structure should be checked in the notes to the annual report.

Customer and demand concentration

Results depend heavily on a few customers, including Hyundai Mobis, Hyundai Motor and Kia, and the Samsung group. If home-appliance mold demand stays weak after the end of the COVID-19 period, mold sales could keep softening. If optical parts growth falls short of projections, dependence on automotive customers will remain.
