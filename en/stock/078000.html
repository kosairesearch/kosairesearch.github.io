02

Business structure

Telcoware develops software for mobile core networks and supplies it to telecom operators.

Its flagship products are the integrated subscriber information management system (e-UM), which manages and authenticates subscriber data for tens of millions of users, and the IP multimedia subsystem (i-IMS), which controls IP-based voice and multimedia services such as VoLTE.

Revenue comes from license sales tied to software supply and from maintenance services that keep the systems running. In the first-quarter 2025 quarterly report, the wireless data solution accounted for 83.3% of sales, the voice core network for 15.3%, and other products for 1.4%.

The company reports a single business segment, and its customers are concentrated among major domestic telecom operators led by SK Telecom. A KRW 9.4 billion maintenance contract with SK Telecom, disclosed on March 31, 2026, runs from April 1, 2026 to March 31, 2027 and equals 21.0% of recent sales.

A virtualized equipment technical support contract with SK Telecom was disclosed the same day. On June 23, 2026, separate contracts with SK Telecom for IMS rebuilding (KRW 2.2 billion) and UDM rebuilding (KRW 4.36 billion) were disclosed.

The company had 169 employees as of June 30, 2026, and its subsidiary Telcoware USA handles telecom equipment sales and services overseas.

04

Earnings analysis

Annual revenue moved from KRW 42.6 billion in 2022 to KRW 40.6 billion in 2023 (down 4.5% year on year), KRW 44.9 billion in 2024 (up 10.4%), and KRW 38.4 billion in 2025 (down 14.5%), making 2024 the largest year and 2025 the smallest in the 2022–2025 span.

An analysis of the nine-month 2025 results attributed the revenue decline in the wireless data solution to changes in telecom operators' investment plans and project schedule adjustments.

Operating profit was KRW 2.86 billion in 2022, KRW 3.04 billion in 2023 (up 6.6%), KRW 3.38 billion in 2024 (up 10.9%), and KRW 3.29 billion in 2025 (down 2.6%). The operating margin rose from 6.7% in 2022 to 8.6% in 2025, so profitability improved even as 2025 revenue fell.

Quarterly, an operating loss of KRW 29.98 million in the third quarter of 2025 turned into operating profit of KRW 6.52 billion in the fourth quarter, which alone exceeded the full-year 2025 operating profit of KRW 3.29 billion.

Fourth-quarter 2025 revenue of KRW 18.7 billion, up 162.4% from the prior quarter, shows how sales can concentrate in particular periods.

In 2026, first-quarter revenue was KRW 6.82 billion with an operating loss of KRW 1.35 billion, second-quarter revenue reached KRW 10.3 billion with operating profit of KRW 3.14 billion, and third-quarter revenue was KRW 9.52 billion with operating profit of KRW 2.19 billion.

Controlling-shareholder net income peaked at KRW 6.93 billion in the fourth quarter of 2025 and then declined to KRW 5.23 billion, KRW 3.08 billion, and KRW 2.43 billion across the next three quarters, although the third-quarter 2026 figure was up 234.6% from a year earlier.

Annual controlling-shareholder net income rose from KRW 3.87 billion in 2022 to KRW 5.34 billion in 2023 (up 37.9%), fell to KRW 4.98 billion in 2024 (down 6.8%), and reached KRW 6.17 billion in 2025 (up 24.0%), the largest year in the span.

Operating cash flow grew from KRW 7.33 billion in 2022 and KRW 3.13 billion in 2023 to KRW 4.44 billion in 2024 and KRW 16.9 billion in 2025. The debt ratio, however, rose from 13.4% in 2024 to 26.5% in 2025, with total liabilities of KRW 29.8 billion at year-end 2025.

05

Industry analysis

Telcoware's demand is set by the network investment plans of mobile operators. In the nine-month 2025 results, changes in operator investment plans and project schedule adjustments were cited as reasons for lower revenue.

Since 2019 the company has supplied NRF and NSSF functions for MEC and 5G SA core networks and has strengthened its core technologies. Because its revenue comes from project-based software supply and maintenance tied to core network upgrades, operator investment timing directly drives quarterly results.

The SK Telecom contracts disclosed in 2026, covering maintenance, virtualization equipment support, and IMS and UDM rebuilding, point to continued demand for core network work. Customer concentration among major domestic operators means that one customer's investment adjustment flows quickly into the company's results.

Compared with large system integrators and platform companies in the same IT and software sector, the company has a business structure specialized in telecom core network software.

The wider quarterly swings since the fourth quarter of 2025 appear to reflect differences in when projects are recognized from quarter to quarter.

10

Risk factors

Customer concentration risk

Most revenue comes from a small number of major domestic operators, so a change in one operator's equipment investment plan quickly moves sales. A single SK Telecom contract equals about 21% of recent revenue, so one customer carries a large share of results. Renewal after the contract ends on March 31, 2027 is the key variable.

Project timing and quarterly volatility risk

Quarterly revenue swings widely, from KRW 18.7 billion in the fourth quarter of 2025 to KRW 6.82 billion in the first quarter of 2026. A shift in project timing can push quarterly profit between operating losses and operating profit. The first-quarter 2026 operating loss of KRW 1.35 billion illustrates this pattern.

Ownership structure and float risk

As of September 2025, treasury shares accounted for 44.11% and the largest shareholder's side for 41.09%, limiting the float. With a thin float, the share price can respond more strongly to supply and demand factors unrelated to earnings. Governance and the treatment of treasury shares should be tracked through future filings.
