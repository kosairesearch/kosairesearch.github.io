Report overview

Rating-led model: profit recovered in 2025, quarterly swings since

Korea Ratings is a rating-led business in which credit ratings made up 75.55% of revenue in 2025. Profit recovered in 2024 and 2025, but operating profit fell 15.0% year on year in the third quarter of 2026 and quarterly results have swung widely.

02

Business structure

Korea Ratings is a credit rating agency that assesses the creditworthiness of financial institutions and companies and assigns ratings. Its revenue comes from three segments, and in 2025 credit ratings accounted for 75.55%, investment evaluation for 22.63%, and information services for 1.81%.

Credit ratings are the core business, assigned when debt is issued, and their share rose from 69.32% in 2022 to 73.06% in 2024 and 75.55% in 2025. In the 2022 to 2024 tables the business-value evaluation line carried a separate item, which was 25.27% in 2024, but the 2025 table shows it under investment evaluation.

Information services is small at 1.81% but grew slightly in the first half of 2026, while investment evaluation declined in the same period.

The company has two consolidated subsidiaries: E-Credible, which issues electronic credit certificates and publishes transaction risk reports, and E-Credible Networks, which runs a payment-type B2B business. The company had 169 employees as of June 30, 2026.

Its customers are companies and financial institutions that issue debt. The company operates an ESG evaluation unit and is expanding green finance evaluation and advisory services. Domestic bond credit rating is a market where several agencies compete.

Financial holding companies in the same sector, such as KB Financial Group and Shinhan Financial Group, earn mainly from lending spreads, so their revenue structures differ and direct comparison is limited.

04

Earnings analysis

On a disclosed annual basis, revenue fell from KRW 101.6 billion in 2022 to KRW 95.6 billion in 2023, down 5.9%, then rose 8.9% to KRW 104.1 billion in 2024 and 5.5% to KRW 109.8 billion in 2025. 2025 is the largest year in the 2022 to 2025 span, and 2023 is the smallest.

Operating profit went from KRW 29.9 billion in 2022 to KRW 25.8 billion in 2023, down 13.7%, then rose 26.5% to KRW 32.7 billion in 2024 and 5.1% to KRW 34.3 billion in 2025. In 2024 operating profit grew much faster than revenue, lifting the operating margin from 27.0% to 31.4%, and in 2025 the margin held at 31.3%.

Profit attributable to owners of the parent was KRW 20.4 billion in 2022, KRW 20.4 billion in 2023 (down 0.3%), KRW 24.4 billion in 2024 (up 19.9%) and KRW 25.2 billion in 2025 (up 3.3%).

Net profit including non-controlling interests was KRW 29.4 billion in 2025, above the owners' share, and the gap reflects non-controlling interests. Operating cash flow fell from KRW 35.4 billion in 2024 to KRW 31.9 billion in 2025.

Quarterly, the third quarter of 2025 showed revenue of KRW 22.2 billion and operating profit of KRW 4.59 billion, and the fourth quarter showed revenue of KRW 22.9 billion (up 3.2% from the prior quarter) and operating profit of KRW 4.19 billion (down 8.7%).

In the first quarter of 2026 revenue was KRW 22.7 billion (down 0.9%) and operating profit was KRW 4.71 billion (up 12.5%). In the second quarter revenue jumped to KRW 43 billion (up 89.5%) and operating profit to KRW 20.9 billion (up 342.7%).

In the third quarter revenue fell to KRW 21.6 billion (down 49.9% from the prior quarter and 2.8% from a year earlier) and operating profit to KRW 3.9 billion (down 81.3% and 15.0% year on year), a level close to the first quarter.

Whether the second quarter spike was one-off cannot be judged from quarterly figures alone. Profit attributable to owners over the last four quarters totaled KRW 25 billion.

05

Industry analysis

Demand for credit ratings is tied to the size of domestic bond issuance. The brokerage company report summary says the credit rating business is expected to grow over the long term as the domestic bond market expands.

A summary of cumulative results through the third quarter of 2025 said credit rating revenue rose on stable demand and a larger bond market.

The company's public rating-action list shows daily evaluations across commercial paper, short-term electronic bonds, corporate bonds, financial bonds and asset-backed securities, so the breadth of the issuance market underpins demand. Investment evaluation is more sensitive to market conditions.

In the cumulative 2025 summary it declined slightly on changes in the market environment, and it declined again in the first half of 2026. Information services is growing but is small enough that its effect on total results is limited.

Financial holding companies and securities firms in the same sector have different capital bases and revenue structures, so their operating margins are not directly comparable. The company's exposure is concentrated in the domestic bond issuance market.

A decline in issuance volume or a change in issuance structure could affect credit rating revenue quickly. Growth in new issuance types such as ESG and green bonds could widen evaluation demand, and the company is expanding services in that direction.

The disclosed second-quarter figures of KRW 43 billion in revenue and KRW 20.9 billion in operating profit account for most of the first-half total. With third-quarter operating profit down to KRW 3.9 billion, the fourth quarter becomes the next checkpoint.

The credit rating segment held near its first-half 2025 level, information services rose slightly, and investment evaluation declined. Growth in the domestic bond market is cited as the long-term driver for credit ratings.

The company runs an ESG evaluation unit and is expanding green finance evaluation and advisory, so how much these services grow within revenue is worth tracking.

In the 2026 annual report, the next regular filing, the key comparison is whether the operating margin and segment mix move away from the 2025 figures of a 31.3% operating margin and a 75.55% credit rating share.

Bull factors

Stable demand in the core rating business

Credit ratings were 75.55% of revenue in 2025, and the segment held near its first-half 2025 level in the first half of 2026. A growing bond issuance market is cited as the long-term growth driver for this core business.

Profit recovery and a high operating margin

Operating profit recovered after its 2023 low, rising 26.5% in 2024 and 5.1% in 2025. The operating margin held at 31.4% in 2024 and 31.3% in 2025, above 29.5% in 2022. Owner-attributable net profit also rose to KRW 25.2 billion in 2025 after growing 19.9% in 2024.

Financial capacity and expanding ESG services

The debt ratio fell to 28.4% at the end of 2025 from 30.1% in 2024, and owner-attributable equity stood at KRW 132.2 billion. Operating cash flow remained sizable at KRW 31.9 billion in 2025. The company runs an ESG evaluation unit and is expanding green finance evaluation and advisory, adding another source of demand.

Bear factors

Decline in the investment evaluation segment

The investment evaluation segment declined in the first half of 2026. It also slipped in the cumulative 2025 results on a changed market environment. At 22.63% of 2025 revenue, the segment is large enough that its recovery matters for total revenue.

Lower profit in the third quarter of 2026

Revenue in the third quarter of 2026 was KRW 21.6 billion, down 2.8% year on year. Operating profit fell 15.0% to KRW 3.9 billion, and owner-attributable net profit fell 9.3% to KRW 3.41 billion. After the second-quarter jump, the third-quarter decline makes earnings durability a weakness to watch.

Slower first-half growth

Consolidated revenue rose only 1.5%, operating profit rose 0.1% and net profit rose 0.8% year on year in the first half of 2026. Growth slowed compared with the cumulative revenue increase of 5.5% through the third quarter of 2025, and first-half growth at or below about 1.5% points to limits on top-line expansion.

Risk factors

Quarterly earnings volatility

The gap between second-quarter operating profit of KRW 20.9 billion and third-quarter operating profit of KRW 3.9 billion shows wide quarterly swings. Quarterly figures alone make a one-off judgment difficult, so annual trends and quarterly flow need to be read together. Owner-attributable profit over the last four quarters totaled about KRW 25 billion.

Volatility in non-rating segments

Investment evaluation is sensitive to market conditions and declined again in the first half of 2026. Information services is growing but at 1.81% of revenue it is too small to move total results much. The risk is that weakness in non-rating segments could offset the stability of credit ratings.

Regulation and dependence on the bond market

Credit rating demand depends on the size of domestic bond issuance. A change in issuance volume or structure would affect revenue quickly. Because credit rating is a regulated business, changes to rules that affect fee structures or the scope of business are also items to monitor.

Overall view

Korea Ratings is a business in which credit ratings make up 75.55% of revenue. Revenue and operating profit rose for two consecutive years in 2024 and 2025, and profit recovered. The 2025 operating margin of 31.3% stayed above the 29.5% of 2022, and the debt ratio at the end of 2025 was 28.4%.

On the other side, third-quarter 2026 operating profit fell 15.0% year on year to KRW 3.9 billion, and first-half 2026 growth in revenue and profit stayed at or below about 1.5%. Because the second quarter of 2026 was the largest quarter in the disclosed results, durability of quarterly profit is the central question.

The decline in investment evaluation and the reliance on the bond issuance market are also weaknesses to weigh. The share price sits at a large premium to owner-attributable equity, so how the multiple is read depends on whether earnings hold. The next checkpoints are the fourth-quarter 2026 results release and the 2026 annual report.
