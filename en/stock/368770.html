02

Business structure

Fiberpro, founded in 1997, is a specialist in optical fiber sensors and precision optical technology that traces its roots to a KAIST optical fiber laboratory.

Its core technology is polarization control, which underpins fiber optic gyroscope (FOG)-based inertial sensors, inertial measurement units (IMU), optical measurement instruments, distributed temperature sensing (DTS) systems, and PLC-based photonic integrated devices.

Since 2008 the company has served as the primary partner for domestic defense companies in the inertial sensor field, maintaining a continuing relationship that has given it a distinctive position in the domestic market.

For the first quarter of 2026, disclosed segment revenue included KRW 3.675 billion from optical measurement instruments, KRW 1.539 billion from photonic integrated devices, and KRW 281 million from fiber optic sensing systems, with the remainder attributable to the inertial sensor and integrated navigation system segment.

Major customers include domestic defense firms Hanwha Aerospace and LIG Nex1, while overseas the company has signed individual supply contracts with Santec LIS Corporation in Japan and Inertial Labs, Halcon Systems, and Viavi Solutions in the United States.

More recently the company has expanded into integrated navigation systems (PNT Solutions) that combine inertial sensors with Anti-Jamming GNSS technology, addressing demand from the defense and autonomous driving sectors.

Fiberpro moved to the KOSDAQ market in 2022 through a merger with a special purpose acquisition company, Korea No.9 SPAC, with CEO and largest shareholder Ko Yeon-wan and related parties holding roughly 38% and financial investor InterVest holding around 15%.

The company has also pursued facility investment, including the acquisition of factory real estate to expand mid-to-long-term integrated navigation system production capacity.

04

Earnings analysis

Annual revenue rose from KRW 18.8 billion in 2022 to KRW 19.4 billion in 2023 (up 3.0% year on year), KRW 32.9 billion in 2024 (up 69.9%), and KRW 43.5 billion in 2025 (up 32.0%), marking three consecutive years of growth from 2023 through 2025.

Operating profit fell from KRW 3.42 billion in 2022 to KRW 2.62 billion in 2023, a 23.5% decline, before rising for two consecutive years from that 2023 trough to KRW 7.21 billion in 2024 (up 175.4%) and KRW 10.1 billion in 2025 (up 40.4%).

The operating margin likewise dipped to 18.2% in 2022 and 13.5% in 2023 before improving markedly to 21.9% in 2024 and 23.3% in 2025.

Net profit attributable to owners swung from a loss of KRW 1.73 billion in 2022 to a profit of KRW 2.85 billion in 2023, then rose for two consecutive years to KRW 7.15 billion in 2024 (up 150.8%) and KRW 9.31 billion in 2025 (up 30.2%).

On a quarterly basis, revenue moved from KRW 10.4 billion in the second quarter of 2025 to KRW 13.8 billion in the third quarter (up 32.7%), KRW 12.2 billion in the fourth quarter (down 11.1%), KRW 14.3 billion in the first quarter of 2026 (up 16.8%), and KRW 13.3 billion in the second quarter of 2026 (down 6.6% quarter on quarter, up 28.6% year on year).

Operating profit rose to KRW 3.83 billion in the third quarter of 2025, fell to KRW 2.97 billion in the fourth quarter, reached a quarterly high of KRW 4.76 billion in the first quarter of 2026, and then eased to KRW 2.7 billion in the second quarter.

Net profit attributable to owners likewise fell 50.5% quarter on quarter to KRW 2.36 billion in the second quarter of 2026 from KRW 4.77 billion in the prior quarter, though it was up 50.6% year on year.

The sum of net profit attributable to owners over the most recent four quarters (third quarter 2025 through second quarter 2026) was KRW 13.5 billion, reflecting the order-driven nature of the business in which quarterly contribution varies with the timing of individual contracts.

Operating cash flow turned negative at KRW 1.26 billion in 2023 before returning to positive territory at KRW 5.91 billion in 2024 and KRW 5.2 billion in 2025.

05

Industry analysis

The optical fiber sensor and precision optics market in which Fiberpro operates spans two distinct demand drivers: defense/aerospace and optical communications/data centers.

According to industry information provider FnGuide, the high-performance fiber optic gyroscope-based precision inertial sensor market is expected to grow at an average annual rate of 6% through 2029, while the Anti-Jamming GNSS market is projected to grow at an average annual rate of 9.4%.

The same source notes that in the photonic integrated device segment, rising demand for quantum cryptography communication and high-speed data center communication has been a factor supporting revenue growth.

In the domestic defense sector, demand for guided weapons and inertial navigation systems channeled through system integrators such as Hanwha Aerospace and LIG Nex1 has remained steady, with expanding defense exports to regions such as the Middle East and Eastern Europe also affecting component suppliers.

On the optical communications and data center side, expanding investment in submarine cables and optical transceivers tied to AI infrastructure build-out is cited as a backdrop supporting demand for optical measurement instruments and photonic devices.

Fiberpro is classified under the KOSDAQ medical and precision instruments industry, which includes a heterogeneous mix of comparison companies, and it is more often grouped in media coverage with defense-component or optical-communication thematic stocks than compared directly against close industry peers.

The company is known to hold a distinctive position in certain processes, such as integrating optical devices on silicon wafers, though no specific official market share figures have been confirmed.

Outlook

In March 2026 the company disclosed a 'corporate value-up plan' setting out goals of building a growth structure oriented toward enhancing shareholder value, securing quality leadership, and diversifying into a higher-margin business portfolio.

At the same general shareholders' meeting it also resolved to pay a cash dividend, continuing its shareholder return policy.

On the order front, 2026 has brought a steady stream of contracts, including a supply agreement with Hanwha Aerospace for optical sensor assemblies related to the IM3 program (contract period from January 2026 to November 2029), a supply agreement with Halcon Systems in the United States for inertial measurement units (contract period from July to December 2026), and multichannel optical power meter supply agreements with Santec LIS Corporation in Japan signed in April and August 2026, broadening the company's domestic and overseas customer base.

In June 2026 the company's self-developed fiber optic gyroscope for low-earth-orbit satellites was designated a new space technology by the Korea AeroSpace Administration, providing a technical basis for expanding into satellite attitude-control applications.

In November 2025 the company decided to acquire factory real estate to expand mid-to-long-term integrated navigation system production capacity, indicating continued facility investment in response to rising orders.

However, results from the third quarter of 2026 onward have not yet been confirmed through a formal quarterly report, and quarter-to-quarter volatility may continue depending on the timing and scale of revenue recognition from individual supply contracts.

Bull factors

Diversified global order base

Individual supply contracts continue to be signed not only with domestic defense system integrators such as Hanwha Aerospace but also with overseas customers including Santec LIS in Japan and Halcon Systems, Inertial Labs, and Viavi Solutions in the United States.

This geographically and industrially diversified order structure can reduce dependence on any single customer or region. However, individual contracts often represent 10% to 20% of recent revenue, meaning the signing or delay of a single contract can still materially affect quarterly results.

Entering a higher profit-margin phase

The operating margin rose markedly from 13.5% in 2023 to 21.9% in 2024 and 23.3% in 2025. A growing mix of higher-priced, higher-margin inertial sensor and integrated navigation system products is cited as a background factor. If this product mix shift continues, there is room for profitability to improve alongside revenue growth.

New technology certification and facility investment

In June 2026, a fiber optic gyroscope for low-earth-orbit satellites was designated a new space technology by the Korea AeroSpace Administration, providing a basis for expansion into the satellite sector.

In November 2025 the company decided to acquire factory real estate to expand integrated navigation system production capacity, building a mid-to-long-term order-response framework. The combination of technology certification and facility investment can be interpreted as preparation for anticipated order growth.

Bear factors

High quarterly earnings volatility

Operating profit fell from KRW 3.83 billion in the third quarter of 2025 to KRW 2.97 billion in the fourth quarter, then rose to KRW 4.76 billion in the first quarter of 2026 before falling again to KRW 2.7 billion in the second quarter, showing wide quarter-to-quarter swings.

Net profit attributable to owners also fell 50.5% quarter on quarter, from KRW 4.77 billion to KRW 2.36 billion. Given a business structure dependent on the timing of revenue recognition from individual supply contracts, it is difficult to draw firm trend conclusions from any single quarter's results.

Execution risk from contract concentration

Individual contracts represent a sizable share of overall revenue—for example, the Halcon Systems contract equaled 14.2% of recent revenue and the August Santec LIS contract equaled 21.41%.

Many of these contracts run for only a few months, so any delivery delay or specification change could directly affect the results of the relevant quarter. Because the structure relies on an accumulation of many small-to-mid-sized contracts, tracking the progress of each individual agreement is necessary.

Market expectations embedded in profit multiples

At certain past fiscal-year-end points the price-to-earnings ratio has been observed to significantly exceed the sector average. This suggests that expectations for future profit growth may already be substantially reflected in the share price multiple.

If order and revenue growth were to slow, or margin improvement were to stall, a gap between such expectations and actual results could become more apparent.

Risk factors

Order timing risk

A significant portion of revenue depends on individually disclosed single-sale supply contracts, so the timing of contract signing and delivery completion is directly reflected in quarterly results. Delays or renegotiations could result in lower-than-expected revenue and profit for the relevant quarter.

Continued monitoring of contract execution with counterparties such as Santec LIS and Halcon Systems will be necessary through the second half of 2026.

Foreign exchange risk

The contract with Santec LIS in Japan was disclosed in won terms after conversion from a U.S. dollar-denominated amount, illustrating exposure to exchange-rate movements as the share of overseas revenue grows.

Depending on whether the won weakens or strengthens, the won-converted value of contracts and resulting profitability can vary. As the proportion of overseas customers increases, the importance of managing foreign-exchange exposure also grows.

Small-cap share price volatility

Fiberpro has repeatedly been associated with multiple investment themes including defense, optical communications, and quantum technology, and its share price has shown large short-term swings tied to related news flow. Trading volume and volatility can expand as thematic buying interest flows in and out.

Attention should be paid to the possibility of short-term price swings driven by factors unrelated to underlying results.

Overall view

Since turning profitable in 2022, Fiberpro has shown a clear recovery and growth path in revenue, operating profit, and net profit attributable to owners through 2025, with the 2025 operating margin of 23.3% the highest level within the disclosed period.

At the same time, quarterly operating profit fell from KRW 4.76 billion in the first quarter of 2026 to KRW 2.7 billion in the second quarter, confirming that volatility tied to the timing of individual supply contracts remains significant.

The expansion of contracts with overseas customers in Japan and the United States, in addition to domestic defense customers, is notable as a change in the company's business structure, broadening its customer and geographic base.

The new space technology designation, the acquisition of real estate to expand production capacity, and the corporate value-up plan are all facts pointing to an intent to expand the business over the medium to long term.
