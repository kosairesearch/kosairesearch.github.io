02

Business structure

Fiberpro is a company that measures equipment conditions using optical fiber.

Its core products are optical-sensor-based infrastructure monitoring solutions, including distributed temperature sensing systems and FBG sensors, used to secure the safety of power grids, tunnels, railways, subsea cables and industrial plants.

The ability to measure long sections with a single fiber is presented as a technical strength in long-distance infrastructure monitoring.

The company has also developed an integrated navigation system that combines Anti-Jamming GNSS receiving technology, extending its business into precision navigation solutions used where GPS interference exists.

Demand for defense navigation can be lumpy, with orders clustering around defense budgets and export contract timing. Besides monitoring solutions, the business includes an energy storage segment, and a March 2026 report said this segment fell short of expectations.

At the regular shareholders meeting in March 2026, a proposal to add solar power generation, electricity sales and real estate leasing to the articles of incorporation passed by special resolution. This reads as an effort to broaden a measurement-equipment business into power, energy and property leasing.

However, adding business purposes to the articles does not by itself change the revenue structure, and the actual start and scale of these businesses must be confirmed through later disclosures. Revenue mix by segment should be checked in the business-segment disclosures of the regular report.

Competition centers on specialized optical measurement and sensor makers, and the customers and product characteristics differ from those of large electronic component makers.

Earnings analysis

Annual results show a recovery from a net loss in 2022 to net income attributable to controlling shareholders of KRW 9.31 billion in 2025. Revenue rose from KRW 18.8 billion in 2022 to KRW 19.4 billion in 2023 (up 3.0% year on year), then to KRW 32.9 billion in 2024 (up 69.9%) and KRW 43.5 billion in 2025 (up 32.0%).

Revenue increased for three consecutive years from 2023 to 2025, and 2025 was the largest year among 2022 to 2025.

Operating profit fell from KRW 3.42 billion in 2022 to KRW 2.62 billion in 2023 (down 23.5%), which marked the low point, and then rose to KRW 7.21 billion in 2024 (up 175.4%) and KRW 10.1 billion in 2025 (up 40.4%), growing for two consecutive years.

The operating margin moved from 18.2% in 2022 to 13.5% in 2023, 21.9% in 2024 and 23.3% in 2025. In 2024, operating profit growth of 175.4% far outpaced revenue growth of 69.9%, a year in which expansion translated into margin improvement.

Net income attributable to controlling shareholders turned positive in 2023 at KRW 2.85 billion, a swing from loss, and then rose to KRW 7.15 billion in 2024 and KRW 9.31 billion in 2025.

Operating cash flow was KRW -1.26 billion in 2023, negative, before recovering to KRW 5.91 billion in 2024 and KRW 5.2 billion in 2025. The debt ratio fell from 36.3% in 2022 to 32.6% in 2025, though it has edged up since 2023.

At the end of 2025, total equity of KRW 45.8 billion was entirely attributable to controlling shareholders, and total liabilities were KRW 14.9 billion.

On a quarterly basis, revenue of KRW 13.8 billion and operating profit of KRW 3.83 billion in 3Q 2025 fell to KRW 12.2 billion and KRW 2.97 billion in 4Q 2025, recovered to KRW 14.3 billion and KRW 4.76 billion in 1Q 2026, and dropped again to KRW 13.3 billion and KRW 2.7 billion in 2Q 2026.

In 3Q 2026, revenue reached KRW 19.7 billion, operating profit KRW 6.44 billion and net income attributable to controlling shareholders KRW 4.05 billion, the highest among the last five quarters.

Because quarterly swings are large, the KRW 14 billion sum of net income attributable to controlling shareholders over the last four quarters should be read alongside the annual trend.

Industry analysis

The fiber-optic sensor market starts from demand to monitor aging infrastructure such as power grids, railways and tunnels continuously. Conventional point sensors struggle to cover long sections densely, so fiber-based distributed measurement is used as an alternative.

Subsea cable monitoring is tied to the buildout of offshore power grids and undersea communications networks, which can widen the customer base. On the defense side, demand for navigation technology that works under GPS interference is also mentioned.

On where the cycle stands, one reading sees infrastructure replacement demand building up in its early stage, while another sees a business in which project-based contracts cluster in particular quarters and make results volatile.

Samsung Electro-Mechanics and LG Innotek, which sit in the same electronic components category, are centered on smartphone and automotive components, so their demand structure and customer base differ. Even within the same sector classification, valuation is hard to compare on the same basis.

Fiberpro's position rests on a small company with optical measurement expertise and navigation technology as its two pillars. To gauge market size, one can track how the amount and frequency of order disclosures accumulate over time. The share of overseas exports should be checked in the business description of the regular report.

Risk factors

Policy and Order Dependence

Demand for defense navigation and infrastructure monitoring tends to depend on defense budgets and public procurement schedules. When orders cluster in particular quarters, revenue and profit move together, which lowers the predictability of quarterly results.

When a single supply contract is announced, both its size and the timing of its recognition in results need to be checked.

New Business Execution

Solar power generation, electricity sales and real estate leasing are newly added business areas in the articles of incorporation. Their technological link to the existing optical measurement business appears limited, so they require separate investment and operating capability. The effect of these businesses on capital and cash flow must be checked in later regular reports.

Cash Flow and Capital Structure

Operating cash flow was KRW -1.26 billion in 2023, a negative year, so earnings and cash do not always move together. The debt burden is low, but the debt ratio has edged up since 2023, so whether this trend continues is a point to watch.

Given the large swings in quarterly operating profit, the stability of cash generation also needs to be checked.
