02

Business structure

Wonik PNE, founded in 1999, makes equipment for the battery manufacturing process, centered on cyclers for charging and discharging cells and on assembly and formation lines.

Company profiles list formation and cycler equipment, automated charge-discharge systems, assembly-process equipment and PCR power-supply units as core products, with a charging-infrastructure business alongside.

Battery equipment accounted for 90.1% of revenue in January–September 2025 and 55.8% in January–September 2026, according to press reports citing quarterly filings.

Power-supply sales in the same 2026 period were about KRW 20.0 billion, more than 3.5 times the KRW 5.6 billion of a year earlier, and made up 15.9% of revenue.

These units keep voltage stable at power plants and help gas turbines start up, and reports say the company supplies them exclusively to a Korean gas-turbine maker and has delivered to more than 120 plants at home and abroad. Gas-fired plants make up over 90% of that supply.

The company previously competed with overseas suppliers such as ABB, but after localizing the product its sales have concentrated on one customer.

A 2023 securities-firm analysis named LG Energy Solution and Samsung SDI among its battery-equipment clients, and all three major Korean battery makers were described as customers.

A November 2025 report said talks with Samsung SDI on a battery-cell performance testing contract were under way, and the company has built a validation center in Cheongju to offer test services. Press reports in November 2025 said its charging subsidiary P&E Systems would be merged into the parent in December 2025.

04

Earnings analysis

Annual revenue went from KRW 288.8 billion in 2022 to KRW 306.8 billion in 2023 (up 6.2% year on year), then KRW 294.7 billion in 2024 (down 4.0%) and KRW 390.7 billion in 2025 (up 32.6%). Of the 2022–2025 years, 2025 was the largest and 2022 the smallest.

Operating profit was KRW 3.95 billion in 2022 and KRW 2.89 billion in 2023 (down 26.7%), then an operating loss of KRW 48.7 billion in 2024. In 2025 operating profit was KRW 1.4 billion, turning positive, with an operating margin of 0.4%.

In a March 10, 2026 filing the company attributed the 2025 turnaround to revenue growth, selective order intake focused on profitability, and tighter cost control.

Attributable net income was KRW 2.69 billion in 2022, followed by an attributable net loss of KRW 3.65 billion in 2023 and KRW 63.7 billion in 2024, before returning to attributable net income of KRW 964 million in 2025.

Net income including non-controlling interests, which is a different figure, was KRW 915 million in 2025. In 2024, press reports pointed to weak demand in downstream industries and a sharp rise in inventory.

Operating cash flow went from KRW -26.3 billion in 2022, KRW -4.21 billion in 2023 and KRW -42.4 billion in 2024 to KRW 61.4 billion in 2025. Quarterly results show Q3 2025 revenue of KRW 95.2 billion, operating profit of KRW 3.88 billion and attributable net income of KRW 5.7 billion.

Q4 2025 revenue was KRW 74 billion, down 22.3% from the previous quarter, and the company posted an operating loss of KRW 9.78 billion. Q1 2026 revenue of KRW 27.3 billion, down 63.1% quarter on quarter, was the low point, followed by KRW 43.2 billion in Q2 and KRW 54.7 billion in Q3.

Operating loss narrowed from KRW 6.08 billion in Q1 to KRW 938 million in Q2 and widened to KRW 1.56 billion in Q3. Attributable net loss in Q3 2026 was KRW 6.08 billion.

05

Industry analysis

The battery-equipment cycle has been under pressure since 2022 as electric-vehicle demand slowed, and a November 2025 press report described battery equipment as 90% of the company's revenue. Against that backdrop the company has moved to expand into battery inspection and test services.

Reports explaining its 2026 quarterly results said major customers including LG Energy Solution and Samsung SDI cut equipment investment and pushed back delivery schedules on some projects, delaying revenue recognition.

At the same time, customers are completing the conversion of production lines to energy storage system (ESS) batteries, and delayed deliveries are expected to follow. Whether the battery-equipment cycle is turning up will depend on new capital orders from customers and the pace of the ESS line conversions.

In battery process equipment, domestic and overseas suppliers compete product by product, including assembly and formation equipment and cyclers. In power-supply equipment, the company competed with overseas suppliers such as ABB before localization and now supplies a single customer exclusively.

Demand in that segment rests on rising gas-turbine orders, while demand in batteries depends on the ESS shift and a resumption of capital spending. Industry commentary links the testing business to cell makers' preference for outsourcing validation to cut costs.

06

Outlook

Press reports put the company's backlog at KRW 255.4 billion at the end of Q3 2026, above KRW 200 billion. How fast that backlog converts into revenue depends on customers' line conversions and delivery schedules.

Reports said power-supply sales are expected to exceed KRW 30.0 billion in 2026, roughly three times the prior year. Against KRW 20.0 billion booked in the first three quarters, that target would require more than KRW 10.0 billion in Q4.

Battery-equipment sales rose to KRW 35.1 billion in Q3 2026, and whether that recovery holds in Q4 can be tracked through company disclosures.

The Cheongju validation center offers cell-testing services to battery makers, and talks on a cell-service contract with Samsung SDI were reported as ongoing as of November 2025.

In the same period, press reports said the company planned to invest several hundred billion won in its Cheongju base and raised its short-term borrowing limit to fund the testing expansion. The merger of P&E Systems was described as a step to raise operating efficiency.

The next periodic filing is the 2026 annual report, which will fix full-year operating margin, year-end backlog, the debt-to-equity ratio and operating cash flow. Whether those figures continue the 2025 trend is the next checkpoint.

Bull factors

Rapid growth in power-supply sales

Press reports said power-supply sales reached KRW 20.0 billion in January–September 2026, more than 3.5 times the KRW 5.6 billion of a year earlier. Their share of revenue rose from 1.8% to 15.9%. Rising gas-turbine orders at a Korean maker are cited as the demand base for this business.

Backlog and a Q3 rebound in battery equipment

Press reports put the backlog at KRW 255.4 billion at the end of Q3 2026, above KRW 200 billion. Battery-equipment sales rose to KRW 35.1 billion in Q3 2026 from KRW 17.7 billion in Q1 and KRW 17.1 billion in Q2. Reports also said delayed deliveries could be recognized as customers complete their ESS line conversions.

2025 turnaround and better cash flow

Full-year 2025 operating profit was KRW 1.4 billion, turning positive from a KRW 48.7 billion operating loss in 2024. Operating cash flow moved from KRW -42.4 billion in 2024 to KRW 61.4 billion in 2025. The debt-to-equity ratio fell from 503.9% to 208.8%. The company attributed the improvement to profit-focused selective orders and tighter cost control.

Bear factors

Sharp drop in battery-equipment sales

Battery-equipment sales were KRW 69.8 billion in January–September 2026, down 75.5% from KRW 285.3 billion a year earlier, according to press reports citing quarterly filings. Reports cited capital-spending cuts by major customers including LG Energy Solution and Samsung SDI, along with delayed deliveries. The decline in this segment's share is the main reason for the revenue drop.

Four straight loss quarters and cumulative losses

The company has posted losses for four consecutive quarters from Q4 2025 through Q3 2026, with attributable net losses totaling KRW 21.7 billion over the last four quarters.

The Q3 2026 operating loss of KRW 1.56 billion was larger than the KRW 938 million loss in Q2 and compares with KRW 3.88 billion of operating profit in Q3 2025. Revenue rose in Q2 and Q3 2026, yet the operating loss widened again.

Debt burden remains high

Total liabilities at the end of 2025 were KRW 213.9 billion, more than twice shareholders' equity of KRW 102.4 billion. The debt-to-equity ratio fell to 208.8% in 2025 from 503.9% in 2024 but remains high.

Operating cash flow turned positive at KRW 61.4 billion in 2025, yet with quarterly losses continuing, whether earnings will keep generating cash is uncertain.

Risk factors

Timing of backlog conversion

How fast the KRW 255.4 billion backlog at the end of Q3 2026 converts into revenue is the key variable for a quarterly recovery. If customers' ESS line conversions take longer than planned, recognition of delayed deliveries could also slip. The share of orders whose timing depends on customer schedule changes will drive the size of earnings swings.

New-business investment and funding

Press reports in November 2025 said the company planned to invest several hundred billion won in Cheongju equipment to expand its testing business and raised its short-term borrowing limit by KRW 10.0 billion. The cell-service contract with Samsung SDI was reported as still under negotiation.

If the contract is delayed while capital spending proceeds, fixed-cost pressure could flow directly into earnings.

Single-customer dependence in power supply

Power-supply sales depend on exclusive supply to one Korean gas-turbine maker, and gas-fired plants account for more than 90% of that volume. A drop in gas-turbine orders or a change in the customer's procurement policy could slow this segment. If overseas suppliers that previously competed re-enter, pricing terms could also shift.

Overall view

Wonik PNE's results have changed direction with the full-year 2025 turnaround to operating profit, a lower debt-to-equity ratio and positive operating cash flow.

Yet the company has posted losses in each of the four quarters from Q4 2025 through Q3 2026, with attributable net losses totaling KRW 21.7 billion over the last four quarters.

The business mix is shifting toward power-supply equipment, as battery equipment's share of revenue fell from 90.1% in January–September 2025 to 55.8% in January–September 2026. Power-supply sales depend on a single Korean gas-turbine maker, which brings both growth and concentration risk.

Battery-equipment sales rebounded to KRW 35.1 billion in Q3 2026, supported by a backlog of KRW 255.4 billion, but the pace of customer capital spending and ESS line conversions will determine revenue recognition.

Valuation is compared only on a price-to-book basis, since net losses leave the price-earnings ratio uncalculated. The next checkpoints are the Q4 2026 preliminary results and the operating margin, backlog and debt-to-equity ratio in the 2026 annual report.
