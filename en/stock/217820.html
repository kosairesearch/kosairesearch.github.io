02

Business structure

Wonik PNE started out aiming to build process automation equipment for lithium polymer secondary battery mass production and has grown into a mid-sized battery equipment company with six consolidated subsidiaries.

Its main products are Formation and Cycler equipment, automated charging and discharging equipment, assembly process equipment, and PCR power supply units, and it has extended into charging infrastructure. Charging infrastructure sales were KRW 10.7 billion in the third quarter of 2024, or 8.7% of total sales.

The subsidiary P&E Systems was absorbed by merger into the parent on December 5, 2025 and ceased to exist. The power supply business is the segment whose share of results grew in 2026.

The equipment keeps voltage stable at power plants and helps gas turbines start up, and it is supplied exclusively to Doosan Enerbility.

According to a THE ELEC report, sales in this business reached KRW 20 billion in the first three quarters of 2026, more than 3.5 times the KRW 5.6 billion of the same period a year earlier, and its share of total sales rose from 1.8% to 15.9%.

The battery equipment segment has continued order talks with domestic battery makers including Samsung SDI, LG Energy Solution and SK On, and a November 2025 report said new cell service contract talks with Samsung SDI had emerged.

The Cheongju Validation Center provides test and evaluation services to battery companies as a site for recurring revenue beyond equipment sales. The order backlog at the end of the third quarter of 2026 was KRW 255.4 billion, staying above KRW 200 billion.

04

Earnings analysis

Annual revenue rose from KRW 288.8 billion in 2022 to KRW 306.8 billion in 2023 (up 6.2% year on year), then fell to KRW 294.7 billion in 2024 (down 4.0% year on year), before reaching KRW 390.7 billion in 2025 (up 32.6% year on year), the largest annual figure for 2022–2025.

Operating profit was KRW 3.95 billion in 2022 and KRW 2.89 billion in 2023 (down 26.7% year on year); it turned to an operating loss of KRW 48.7 billion in 2024 and then returned to an operating profit of KRW 1.4 billion in 2025.

The company cited three reasons for the 2025 return to profit: top-line growth from higher revenue, selective order intake focused on profitability, and stronger company-wide cost management.

Net income attributable to controlling shareholders turned from a net loss of KRW 63.7 billion in 2024 to a profit of KRW 0.96 billion in 2025, while net income including non-controlling interests was KRW 0.91 billion in 2025, a different figure from net income attributable to controlling shareholders.

The operating margin improved from -16.5% in 2024 to 0.4% in 2025 but remains low. Cash flow from operating activities turned positive, from KRW -42.4 billion in 2024 to KRW 61.4 billion in 2025.

The debt-to-equity ratio fell from 503.9% in 2024 to 208.8% in 2025, and at the end of 2025 total liabilities were KRW 213.9 billion and total equity was KRW 102.4 billion.

By quarter, third-quarter 2025 revenue was KRW 95.2 billion, operating profit was KRW 3.88 billion, and net income attributable to controlling shareholders was KRW 5.7 billion, but in the fourth quarter the company swung to an operating loss of KRW 9.78 billion on revenue of KRW 74.0 billion (down 22.3% quarter on quarter).

First-quarter 2026 revenue fell to KRW 27.3 billion (down 63.1% quarter on quarter) before rising to KRW 43.2 billion in the second quarter and KRW 54.7 billion in the third quarter.

The operating loss narrowed from KRW 6.08 billion in the first quarter to KRW 0.94 billion in the second quarter but widened again to KRW 1.56 billion in the third quarter, and the third-quarter net loss attributable to controlling shareholders was KRW 6.08 billion.

Third-quarter revenue was down 42.6% from the same period a year earlier, so revenue has not yet returned to the level of the third quarter of 2025.

05

Industry analysis

Demand for battery equipment weakened after the electric vehicle slowdown that began in 2022, and battery makers also cut capital spending. Wonik PNE has tried to broaden its business through battery cell test services during this period.

Recent reports said major customers are finishing the conversion of battery lines to energy storage system (ESS) production, which is expected to bring delayed equipment deliveries into sales. This points to the direction of a recovery rather than a confirmed scale.

Sales in the third quarter of 2026 were 42.6% lower than a year earlier, so the pace of the battery equipment recovery has not yet shown up in results.

In the power supply market, gas power generation accounts for more than 90% of supply volume, and rising gas turbine orders at Doosan Enerbility have increased demand for related equipment.

Wonik PNE localized the equipment together with Doosan Enerbility and now supplies it exclusively to Doosan, after competing with overseas players such as ABB.

In battery equipment, the company emphasizes differentiation by combining assembly and formation process equipment with inspection technologies such as 3D CT and EIS non-destructive testing. Market share figures versus peers are not available in verifiable sources, so the comparison can only be qualitative.

06

Outlook

The order backlog at the end of the third quarter of 2026 was KRW 255.4 billion, staying above KRW 200 billion, which can be read as support for sales after 2026. Power supply sales are tied to Doosan Enerbility's gas turbine order flow, so whether power plant equipment orders continue is the key issue.

In battery equipment, the next checkpoint is whether delayed deliveries from customers that have finished the ESS conversion actually flow into sales.

Test services were reported as a plan to expand contracts with Samsung SDI and others centered on the Cheongju Validation Center, and as of a November 2025 report, the talks were still at the negotiation stage.

In the same report the company said it plans to gradually expand the test business, and a plan to invest several hundred billion won in equipment at the Cheongju production base in 2026 was also reported. Fourth-quarter 2026 results have not yet been disclosed in a regular report.

The 2026 annual report will show the composition of annual sales, the operating margin and the year-end order backlog. Whether the operating margin rises from 0.4% in 2025 is the measure for the quality of the return to profit. Any company targets should be read only within what is confirmed in disclosures.

Once a test service contract is signed, the extent to which it offsets swings in battery equipment sales becomes the next point to confirm.

08

Bull factors

Power supply as a new earnings pillar

Per a THE ELEC report, the power supply business generated KRW 20 billion in sales in the first three quarters of 2026, more than 3.5 times the KRW 5.6 billion a year earlier, lifting its share of total sales to 15.9%.

Because it is supplied exclusively to Doosan Enerbility, related equipment supply rises as gas turbine orders increase. With gas power accounting for more than 90% of supply volume, power plant order flow largely determines the growth of this business.

Signs of a battery equipment recovery

Reports said major customers are finishing the conversion of battery lines to ESS production, which is expected to bring delayed equipment deliveries into sales. The related business reportedly grew from KRW 17.7 billion in the first quarter of 2026 and KRW 17.1 billion in the second to KRW 35.1 billion in the third.

The operating loss also narrowed from KRW 6.08 billion in the first quarter to KRW 938 million in the second, which could be read as a sign of recovery.

Order backlog and test service expansion

The order backlog is recognized as sales as equipment deliveries proceed. The Cheongju Validation Center provides test and evaluation services to battery makers including Samsung SDI, a route to revenue beyond equipment sales. A November 2025 report said new cell service contract talks with Samsung SDI had emerged.

Bear factors

Slow recovery in battery equipment demand

Third-quarter 2026 sales of KRW 54.7 billion were 42.6% lower than a year earlier. The company has posted losses for four consecutive quarters since the fourth quarter of 2025, with a combined net loss attributable to controlling shareholders of KRW 21.7 billion over the last four quarters. The electric vehicle slowdown since 2022 and reduced battery investment are cited as obstacles to recovery.

Losses widened again in the third quarter

The operating loss in the third quarter of 2026 was KRW 1.56 billion, larger than the KRW 938 million in the second quarter. The net loss attributable to controlling shareholders in the third quarter was KRW 6.08 billion, larger than the operating loss.

Losses have continued since a net profit attributable to controlling shareholders of KRW 5.7 billion in the third quarter of 2025, so the direction of quarterly earnings is not yet stable.

Balance sheet burden and customer concentration

Total liabilities at the end of 2025 were KRW 213.9 billion, more than twice total equity of KRW 102.4 billion. The debt ratio fell to 208.8% from 503.9% in 2024 but remains high. The power supply business depends on a single customer, Doosan Enerbility, which adds to customer concentration risk.

10

Risk factors

Customer investment cycle

Battery equipment sales depend heavily on when customers such as electric vehicle and ESS makers invest in capacity. As third-quarter 2026 sales fell 42.6% from a year earlier, prolonged investment delays could again push quarterly results lower.

Whether delayed deliveries are actually booked as sales after customers finish their line conversions is the point to watch.

Concentration in power supply customers

The power supply business is supplied exclusively to Doosan Enerbility, so changes in one customer's orders feed directly into sales. Demand centered on gas power also ties the business to the power plant equipment cycle. As this business has grown as a share of total sales, the effect of customer concentration has grown with it.

Balance sheet and earnings volatility

Total liabilities at the end of 2025 of KRW 213.9 billion exceed total equity of KRW 102.4 billion. The combined net loss attributable to controlling shareholders over the last four quarters was KRW 21.7 billion, and quarterly earnings have swung widely.

Whether profits continue for several quarters after the return to profitability, and whether the operating margin improves, are the benchmarks for judging the financial burden.

12

Overall view

Wonik PNE's disclosed results for 2022 to 2025 show large swings in sales and a structure that moved from an operating loss of KRW 48.7 billion in 2024 to operating profit of KRW 1.4 billion in 2025.

Quarterly sales rose for two straight quarters after the first-quarter 2026 low and the operating loss narrowed through the second quarter, but in the third quarter the operating loss widened to KRW 1.56 billion and the net loss attributable to controlling shareholders to KRW 6.08 billion.

Strengths include the rapid growth of the power supply business (KRW 20 billion in the first three quarters of 2026, 15.9% of total sales), an order backlog of KRW 255.4 billion at the end of the third quarter of 2026, and the possibility that delayed deliveries from the ESS conversion are booked.

Weaknesses include four consecutive loss quarters with a combined net loss attributable to controlling shareholders of KRW 21.7 billion over the last four quarters, third-quarter 2026 sales down 42.6% from a year earlier, and dependence on Doosan Enerbility in power supply.

On the balance sheet, the debt ratio fell from 503.9% in 2024 to 208.8% in 2025 and operating cash flow turned positive at KRW 61.4 billion, but total liabilities of KRW 213.9 billion remain a burden. How the valuation is read depends on whether the return to profit lasts several quarters.

The next checkpoints are the operating margin and order backlog in the 2026 annual report, due by March 31, 2027.
