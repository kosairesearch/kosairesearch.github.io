02

Business structure

Megatouch, founded in 2010 and listed on KOSDAQ in 2023, manufactures test pins. Its battery business makes charge-discharge test pins and clips, while its semiconductor business makes wafer test and final test pins.

A corporate database places semiconductors at 62.71 percent of product sales mix, batteries at 21.89 percent and other products at 15.40 percent. The battery unit is reported to supply test pins to three major Korean battery makers, so customers' equipment investment schedules feed directly into sales.

Samsung Electronics, SK hynix and Intel are reported by a Korean business daily as key semiconductor customers. Reports say orders for pogo pins and interposer pins for HBM and AI chips have exceeded monthly capacity of about 3.5 million units.

The company plans to add 100 CNC machines to raise machining capacity from about 5 million to about 6.5 million units per month. Revenue, however, depends more on finished assembly capacity than on machining, and the company is pairing assembly automation with hiring.

It has secured pogo pin production at 80 micrometers and is developing 50-micrometer pins. Concentration among three battery makers and a few large chipmakers defines the customer structure.

Mix and customer data rely on outside compilations and press reports, so segment detail should be checked against the business report notes.

Earnings analysis

Annual revenue rose from KRW 49 billion in 2022 to KRW 50.1 billion in 2023 (up 2.2% year on year) and KRW 53 billion in 2024 (up 5.7%), then fell to KRW 43.2 billion in 2025 (down 18.4%). 2024 was the largest year and 2025 the smallest within 2022 to 2025.

Operating profit of KRW 8.09 billion in 2022 turned into an operating loss of KRW 253 million in 2023, returned to operating profit of KRW 4 billion in 2024, and turned into an operating loss of KRW 4.17 billion in 2025.

Operating margins were 16.5 percent, -0.5 percent, 7.5 percent and -9.7 percent for 2022 through 2025. Controlling-shareholder net profit was KRW 6.58 billion in 2022 and KRW 5.04 billion in 2024, with controlling-shareholder net losses of KRW 88.21 million in 2023 and KRW 2.86 billion in 2025.

Press reports attribute the 2025 loss to weaker battery-market sales and a 100 percent bad-debt provision on KRW 4.873 billion of receivables from a customer facing liquidity strain; these figures are on a standalone basis.

Quarterly, an operating loss of KRW 616 million in Q3 2025 became operating profit of KRW 1.26 billion in Q4 2025. Revenue then reached KRW 15.9 billion in Q1 2026 (up 35.2 percent quarter on quarter) and KRW 25.2 billion in Q2 2026 (up 58.8 percent).

Q2 2026 operating profit of KRW 4.77 billion and controlling-shareholder net profit of KRW 4.08 billion were the largest quarterly figures in the period. Q3 2026 revenue of KRW 21.1 billion fell 16.2 percent quarter on quarter but rose 111.3 percent year on year.

Q3 2026 operating profit of KRW 1.94 billion sat alongside a controlling-shareholder net loss of KRW 10.67 million, and the reason for that gap should be checked in the quarterly report notes.

Controlling-shareholder net profit over the last four quarters totaled KRW 7.85 billion, a recovery against the 2025 full-year controlling-shareholder net loss of KRW 2.86 billion. Operating cash flow stayed positive at KRW 5.66 billion in 2025.

05

Industry analysis

Megatouch makes semiconductor test pins and battery charge-discharge pins, so its results track capital spending and conditions in downstream industries.

NH Investment & Securities, in a December 2025 report, said semiconductor and battery downstream industries were both weak in 2025 and treated 2025 as the earnings bottom.

On the battery side, a review of the US IRA electric-vehicle tax credit and changes to EU battery rules are cited as sources of oversupply and heavier global competition for three major Korean battery makers.

Conversely, AI data-center growth and renewable energy expansion are shifting battery lines toward EV and ESS, and all-solid-state battery commercialization is seen creating new markets.

In semiconductors, HBM and AI chips add input-output terminals and narrow contact spacing, which raises both pin counts and precision requirements. The company expects test-pin volume to rise and average selling prices to increase as AI chips advance.

A Korean business daily reported that first-half 2026 semiconductor revenue reached KRW 25.77 billion, or 62.7 percent of total sales. Market-share comparisons with listed peers are not disclosed, so the position is best read through fine-pin technology and customer mix. The key question for the sector is whether a battery recovery can offset semiconductor growth.

Bear factors

Battery demand and policy variables

The company attributed 2025 sales declines partly to a weaker battery market. Reviews of the US IRA electric-vehicle tax credit and changes to EU battery rules are cited as sources of oversupply and global competition for three major Korean battery makers. With batteries at about 21.9 percent of product mix, a slow battery recovery could offset semiconductor gains.

Volatility in quarterly net results

Q3 2026 operating profit was KRW 1.94 billion, yet the controlling-shareholder result swung to a net loss of KRW 10.67 million. The 2025 full-year operating margin was -9.7 percent, with a controlling-shareholder net loss of KRW 2.86 billion. Until profitable quarters accumulate, the direction of net results can change from one quarter to the next.

Customer credit and funding burden

A customer's liquidity problems led the company to book a 100 percent bad-debt provision against KRW 4.873 billion in receivables in 2025. A December 2025 third-party allotment issued 5,192,749 new shares at KRW 3,165 each, for a planned total of KRW 16.44 billion. Higher share count and customer credit risk exist side by side.

Risk factors

Customer concentration

The battery unit is reported to serve three major Korean battery makers, and semiconductor sales are concentrated among a few large chipmakers. A cut in orders or a customer's liquidity strain can hit quarterly sales and create bad-debt exposure. The 2025 provision on customer receivables shows how this structure creates risk.

Capacity expansion and execution

Revenue depends more on finished assembly capacity than on machining capacity, and the company has described manual assembly as a bottleneck. If the addition of 100 CNC machines and assembly automation slips, order backlog may not convert into revenue. Hiring must keep pace as well.

Financing and governance

After a December 2025 third-party allotment, a rights-issue decision was filed again in April 2026. In March 2026, Park Jin was newly appointed as CEO, and a change of representative director was disclosed around the same time. Leadership change and repeated funding should be reviewed together with execution timing and dilution.
