02

Business structure

SJ-Chem was founded in 2003 and listed on KONEX in 2018 as a basic inorganic chemicals manufacturer. Its main businesses are water-treatment coagulants and recycled polypropylene.

The coagulant line produces inorganic coagulants, aluminum sulfate and sodium aluminate, which remove pollutants from sewage, wastewater and raw water sources. The company website lists patents and certifications on aluminum-compound processes, including a water-treatment method using sodium aluminate.

One job-information site reports a segment mix of recycled resin at 50.9% and water-treatment coagulants at 49.1%. The reference date for that split is not stated, so it should be treated as indicative only.

The recycled-resin line took shape with the October 2022 entry into post-consumer recycled (PCR) compound resin and widened in August 2023 to automotive PCR PP linked to EU end-of-life vehicle rules. R&D spending in 2025 was KRW 389 million, or 3.77% of revenue. As of December 2025 the company had 38 employees and was headquartered in Iksan, North Jeolla Province.

On competition, a patent document notes that many domestic manufacturers sell water-treatment agents. The coagulant line is therefore largely a commodity business in which price and supply reliability matter most.

Recycled PP draws both large chemical groups and specialist recyclers, while SJ-Chem is a small company with revenue in the KRW 10 billion range.

Outlook

The external backdrop for recycled PP is set by regulation. The EU end-of-life vehicle regulation requires 15% recycled content in new-car plastics from 2032, rising to 25% by 2036.

According to a Shinyoung Securities industry report, at least 20% of that must come from plastics recovered from scrapped or in-use vehicles, so general household recycling alone cannot meet it. Whether SJ-Chem's PCR PP has the feedstock and certifications to satisfy this requirement has not been confirmed.

The coagulant line is a stable-demand business with moderate market growth. Research firm Fortune Business Insights projects that the global aluminum sulfate market will grow at a compound annual rate of 4.5% from 2026 to 2034. That is a worldwide forecast and does not translate directly into domestic demand or SJ-Chem's revenue.

On the financial side, room for operating improvement sits alongside a heavy balance sheet. The operating margin moved out of loss territory in 2025, but total liabilities far exceed total equity, so interest and borrowing costs may continue to absorb earnings.

The convertible bond's next call window runs from November 14 to November 24, 2026, with a settlement date of December 4, 2026. We could not confirm the conversion price or the outstanding balance.

Quarterly results for 2026 were not found in our research, so future filings will be needed to check revenue recovery and non-operating items.

Risk factors

Macro

Inorganic chemicals and recycled PP are exposed to energy and feedstock costs, exchange rates and the business cycle. If public-sector demand is a large share, coagulant sales could be sensitive to local-government budgets and tendering, but we could not confirm the company's customer mix.

Profitability in recycled PP depends on its price competitiveness against virgin plastic. These external variables can amplify earnings volatility for a small company.

Industry and regulation

The EU vehicle regulation requires at least 20% of recycled content to come from scrapped-vehicle plastics, so general household recyclate cannot meet it. Without feedstock access and quality certification, regulatory demand may not convert into revenue.

Vehicle development lead times typically run three to five years, so delivery growth can lag. Competition from large chemical groups and specialist recyclers adds to the pressure.

Financial and liquidity

The convertible bond issued in December 2024 is an unguaranteed private placement with a face value of KRW 1,000 million. Its coupon rate is 0.0%. The filing sets out a quarterly call schedule and purchase prices.

Conversion would raise the share count and dilute existing holders, and low trading volume on KONEX makes both fundraising and share trading harder.
