02

Business structure

LIG Defense&Aerospace is a defense company centered on guided weapons, surveillance and reconnaissance, and command, control and communications systems. At its annual shareholders meeting on March 31, 2026, it changed its name from LIG Nex1 to the current name, signaling an intent to extend its scope into aerospace.

Its core products include the Long-Range Air Defense Missile System LAMD, the Medium-Range Surface-to-Air Missile M-SAM and the Long-Range Surface-to-Air Missile L-SAM.

According to Hankyung reporting, most of these major air defense programs have already been awarded, with development and production running through 2028. In exports, Middle East demand, including Qatar, is cited as the main pillar for the Cheongung-II system.

Hyundai Motor Securities said in a June 2026 report that a global shortage of air defense missiles should support additional Cheongung-II orders. Backlog was disclosed at KRW 24.54 trillion at the end of the third quarter of 2026, and it will be recognized as revenue over several years.

Domestic customers are led by the Defense Acquisition Program Administration, while overseas buyers are foreign governments. Hanwha Aerospace, which has self-propelled howitzers, aircraft engines and space businesses, is the usual reference point among defense exporters. Revenue mix by business segment is best confirmed in the segment information of the annual business report.

04

Earnings analysis

Annual revenue rose from KRW 2.22 trillion in 2022 to KRW 4.31 trillion in 2025, and operating profit grew from KRW 179.1 billion to KRW 319.4 billion over the same period.

Revenue, operating profit and net profit attributable to controlling shareholders each increased for three consecutive years from 2023 through 2025, and 2025 was the largest year in the 2022 to 2025 span. In 2025, revenue rose 31.5% and operating profit rose 39.0%, so profit growth outpaced sales growth.

In 2024, however, the operating margin fell from 8.1% to 7.0% even as revenue jumped 41.9%, before recovering modestly to 7.4% in 2025. Over the same stretch the debt-to-equity ratio climbed from 222.2% to 446.4%, with total liabilities of KRW 6.59 trillion and total equity of KRW 1.48 trillion at year-end 2025.

Operating cash flow moved from KRW 952 billion in 2024 to -KRW 584.3 billion in 2025, so profit and cash generation diverged. By quarter, revenue peaked at KRW 1.4 trillion in the fourth quarter of 2025, while operating profit fell 56.8% from the prior quarter to KRW 38.7 billion.

Operating profit in the first quarter of 2026 was KRW 171.1 billion, up 342.4% from the prior quarter, and in a May 2026 report Meritz Securities attributed this to early delivery requests from the UAE and a one-time recognition of high-margin spare parts business.

Operating profit then declined for two consecutive quarters, to KRW 105.7 billion in the second quarter and KRW 67.6 billion in the third quarter, with the third quarter down 24.6% from a year earlier.

Yuanta Securities pointed to UAE-related revenue falling from an average of KRW 120 billion to KRW 80 billion per quarter and a KRW 9 billion loss provision on a new order business as drags.

Hankyung analysis attributed the third-quarter weakness to delivery delays, a weaker exchange rate and one-off costs rather than a deterioration in the industry. The third-quarter net loss attributable to controlling shareholders of KRW 4.65 billion compares with a KRW 59.3 billion profit a year earlier.

05

Industry analysis

The backdrop for defense demand is geopolitical tension. In a November 9, 2026 report, Yuanta Securities projected that an escalation of the Middle East conflict and an acceleration of European defense self-reliance would support demand.

In the air defense missile segment, the discussion centers on global supply falling short of demand. Hyundai Motor Securities, in a June 19, 2026 report, cited a global shortage of air defense missiles as the basis for additional Cheongung-II orders.

On the cycle, orders are accumulating, but the pace at which contracts convert into revenue remains slow. Samsung Securities said on November 9, 2026 that large contracts capable of meeting the expectations raised by geopolitical disputes have not yet materialized.

Out of a backlog of about KRW 24.6 trillion in the first half of 2026, domestic backlog was reported at KRW 10.5 trillion.

In competitive terms, Hanwha Aerospace is compared as a diversified defense company spanning self-propelled howitzers, aircraft engines and space, while Korea Aerospace Industries relies mainly on exports of fighter and trainer aircraft.

Meritz Securities assessed that, given rivals' situations, the company still has production capacity and is expanding capacity. Quarterly revenue swings depend more on export delivery schedules than on the industry cycle itself, which is the key point when reading its earnings.

06

Outlook

The most specific guidance the company has offered is a year-end 2026 backlog of KRW 28 trillion. Yuanta Securities noted that cumulative new orders through the third quarter of 2026 reached KRW 1.14 trillion and expected roughly KRW 5 to 6 trillion in orders for the full year.

The same firm projected that the missile contracts it had expected for November and December would be signed and judged the year-end backlog guidance to be highly visible.

In a May 2026 report, Meritz Securities judged that a further revision of the three-country Cheongung-II contracts makes a mid-year guidance raise likely.

The counter-scenario is that the Defense Acquisition Program Administration sanction could derail a mass production contract for the counter-drone air defense system. Over the longer term, SK Securities kept its view that growth from expanding overseas orders would become pronounced from 2028.

Meritz Securities used 2028 as the reference year for its fair value, when peak sales of Cheongung-II for the three Middle East countries overlap.

On earnings, one-off items such as the first-quarter pull-forward and the third-quarter loss provision mean that the level at which quarterly operating margins settle remains the key question.

The next points to confirm are whether the KRW 28 trillion year-end backlog is reached, the outcome of the counter-drone mass production contract, and the fourth-quarter 2026 preliminary results expected in early 2027.

Bull factors

Backlog scale and visibility

Backlog was disclosed at KRW 24.54 trillion at the end of the third quarter of 2026, and the company has pointed to KRW 28 trillion by year-end. Yuanta Securities judged that visibility on the year-end backlog guidance is high.

Because backlog is recognized as revenue over several years, it can cushion quarter-to-quarter swings in earnings.

Middle East demand and Cheongung-II exports

Hyundai Motor Securities said in a June 2026 report that additional Cheongung-II orders are expected amid a global shortage of air defense missiles. Meritz Securities viewed positively the demand confirmed for Cheongung-II in the Middle East, including Qatar, and beyond the region. Expansion of export countries for other products such as Bigung and Haegung is also under discussion.

Product diversification and longer-term growth

Hyundai Motor Securities projected that development contracts in maritime, unmanned systems and space would diversify mass production and export items and sustain earnings growth. SK Securities also maintained a positive view on medium-term growth from expanding overseas orders. The renaming toward aerospace also signals an intent to widen the business scope.

Bear factors

The gap versus market expectations is a checkpoint for how much operating profit recovers next quarter. Operating profit fell 24.6% from a year earlier, and profit attributable to controlling shareholders turned into a KRW 4.65 billion loss.

In an October 19, 2026 report, Daishin Securities lowered its target price over concerns about the third quarter and cost burdens in the fourth quarter.

Size of quarterly earnings swings

Quarterly operating profit moved from KRW 38.7 billion in the fourth quarter of 2025 to KRW 171.1 billion in the first quarter of 2026 and then fell to KRW 67.6 billion in the third quarter. Because one-time items and loss provisions are mixed in, the direction of next quarter's profit is hard to gauge. A structure in which results swing with the timing of export deliveries reduces the predictability of earnings.

Rising cash flow and balance sheet strain

Operating cash flow moved from KRW 952 billion in 2024 to -KRW 584.3 billion in 2025. The debt-to-equity ratio rose from 222.2% in 2022 to 446.4% in 2025, with total liabilities of KRW 6.59 trillion at year-end 2025.

Cash generation and debt burden both grew during a period of rising profit, which leaves the balance sheet as a weak point.

Risk factors

Government sanction and contract timing

The Defense Acquisition Program Administration approved a 24-month bidding restriction, and a court has temporarily suspended the sanction through November 20, 2026. Reports have said a mass production contract for the counter-drone air defense system could collapse if the sanction is upheld. Delays in new orders could weigh on revenue recognition in later years.

Overseas deliveries, exchange rates and supply chain

Quarterly revenue moves sharply with the allocation and timing of deliveries for higher-margin UAE business. In the third quarter, a weaker exchange rate and external component supply disruptions occurred together, which SK Securities also cited as drags. As exports grow in weight, the company is more directly exposed to exchange rate and supply chain variables.

Possible shrinkage of the share price premium

Samsung Securities said on November 9, 2026 that the share price premium attached to the guided-weapons business could weaken. If large orders are delayed, the growth path the market expected could be pushed back, and valuation adjustments could continue. If demand expectations tied to geopolitical tension ease, similar pressure on valuation could follow.
