Sales Rebound, but the Profit Turnaround Is Still to Be Shown

02

Business structure

Busan Industrial is a mid-sized company founded in 1976 that mainly produces concrete sleepers and ready-mixed concrete. According to a third-party data aggregator (Butler, based on second-quarter 2026 sales), concrete sleepers and related products account for about 80% of sales and ready-mixed concrete for about 20%.

JobKorea put headcount at 30 as of September 30, 2025. Consolidated sales were KRW 124.1 billion in 2024, while separate sales were KRW 28.6 billion according to FnGuide, a gap that suggests subsidiaries account for a large share of consolidated revenue.

In its summary of the first nine months of 2025, FnGuide cited two units: the building precast concrete (PC) unit, where profitability weakened amid a construction slump and prolonged high interest rates, and the segment business, where sales fell as smaller rivals competed aggressively.

Both core areas are therefore exposed to weaker demand and outside competition. Concrete sleepers are used in railway track, so they are directly linked to infrastructure orders. Ready-mixed concrete shipments, by contrast, follow construction starts, so it is more directly affected by a slowdown in building activity.

The core of the competitive picture is that aggressive selling by smaller rivals has already translated into lower sales in the segment business.

04

Earnings analysis

Annual sales were KRW 152.6 billion in 2022, KRW 140.5 billion in 2023 (down 7.9% year on year), KRW 124.1 billion in 2024 (down 11.7%) and KRW 107.5 billion in 2025 (down 13.4%), so sales fell for three years in a row from 2023 to 2025. 2022 was the largest year in the 2022 to 2025 window and 2025 the smallest.

Operating profit rose from KRW 5.26 billion in 2022 to KRW 7.15 billion in 2023 (up 36.0%), then fell to KRW 3.36 billion in 2024 (down 53.1%), and in 2025 the company recorded an operating loss of KRW 2.77 billion, a swing into deficit.

The operating margin was 3.4% in 2022, 5.1% in 2023, 2.7% in 2024 and -2.6% in 2025.

Net income attributable to controlling shareholders was KRW 2.59 billion in 2022 and KRW 3.52 billion in 2023, then turned into a net loss of KRW 2.74 billion in 2024 and widened to a net loss of KRW 3.45 billion in 2025, making two straight years of losses.

Net income including non-controlling interests was a loss of KRW 6.3 billion in 2025, larger than the loss attributable to controlling shareholders, which suggests non-controlling interests absorbed part of the loss.

Quarterly, an operating loss of KRW 911 million in the third quarter of 2025 and KRW 1.12 billion in the fourth quarter continued, before an operating profit of KRW 497 million in the first quarter of 2026 marked a turn to profit.

Operating profit of KRW 1.36 billion in the second quarter of 2026 was the highest of the recent quarters, up 173.6% from the prior quarter, and in the third quarter it fell to KRW 396 million, down 70.9% from the prior quarter.

Sales peaked at KRW 29.9 billion in the fourth quarter of 2025, fell to KRW 23.3 billion in the first quarter of 2026 and recovered to KRW 31.2 billion in the third quarter of 2026, up 47.1% from a year earlier.

Operating cash flow fell from KRW 2.96 billion in 2022, KRW 8.3 billion in 2023 and KRW 9.17 billion in 2024 to KRW 96.36 million in 2025.

For the first nine months of 2025, FnGuide attributed the weakness to a construction slump and infrastructure project timing, lower profitability in the building PC unit, and competition from smaller rivals in the segment business. Whether any one-off items affected the quarterly results can be checked in the notes to the periodic reports.

05

Industry analysis

FnGuide's summary of the first nine months of 2025 describes a construction market in a downturn, with prolonged high interest rates weakening building-related demand.

The company's concrete sleeper demand is tied to railway and other infrastructure projects, so it can be a relatively steadier source of volume when infrastructure orders continue. FnGuide also cited infrastructure project progress as a factor.

In ready-mixed concrete, aggressive selling by smaller rivals is pressuring prices and volumes, and this contributed to lower sales in the segment business.

Compared with listed companies in the same construction and building materials sector, such as HANIL CEMENTCO, SAMPYO Cement and Asia Cement, Busan Industrial is smaller and posted an annual operating loss in 2025, so its earnings capacity is positioned differently.

The industry cycle position can be summarized as a construction slowdown combined with high interest rates. Whether building starts recover and whether infrastructure orders keep flowing are the external variables that will most influence the company's results.

Outlook

The recent pattern is one in which sales are recovering while profit moves around sharply. Sales were KRW 24.8 billion in the second quarter of 2026, up 6.4% from the prior quarter, and KRW 31.2 billion in the third quarter, up 47.1% from a year earlier, so the top line is expanding.

Operating profit, however, moved from KRW 497 million in the first quarter of 2026 to KRW 1.36 billion in the second and KRW 396 million in the third.

Net income attributable to controlling shareholders returned to a KRW 1.73 billion loss in the third quarter after a KRW 203 million profit in the second, and the four quarters from the fourth quarter of 2025 through the third quarter of 2026 total a net loss attributable to controlling shareholders of KRW 2.66 billion.

Whether rising sales translate into steadier operating profit and narrower net losses is the central open question. The next disclosure is the fourth-quarter 2026 provisional result.

The 2026 annual business report is due by the end of March 2027, when full-year 2026 sales, operating profit and profit attributable to controlling shareholders become confirmed figures.

The points to watch are whether operating profit stays positive in the fourth quarter and whether any operating improvement narrows the net loss attributable to controlling shareholders. Sales of KRW 29.9 billion in the fourth quarter of 2025 serve as the year-earlier reference point.

Bull factors

Sales up for two straight quarters

Sales in the second quarter of 2026 were KRW 24.8 billion, up 6.4% from the prior quarter, and in the third quarter they reached KRW 31.2 billion, up 26.1% from the prior quarter and 47.1% from a year earlier. The top line has recovered since the first-quarter low of KRW 23.3 billion. FnGuide cited infrastructure project progress, which can be read as support for concrete sleeper demand.

Quarterly operating profit turned positive

Operating profit turned positive at KRW 497 million in the first quarter of 2026, then rose to KRW 1.36 billion in the second quarter, up 173.6% from the prior quarter. Compared with the operating losses of the third and fourth quarters of 2025, this can be read as a sign of operating improvement. Operating profit fell to KRW 396 million in the third quarter, so the quarter-to-quarter variation is large.

Product mix tied to infrastructure demand

One third-party data aggregator puts concrete sleepers and related products at about 80% of sales, which implies a high share of demand linked to railway and other infrastructure orders. If infrastructure orders continue while building activity is weak, this mix could help defend sales. Ready-mixed concrete accounts for about 20%.

Risk factors

Construction cycle and interest rates

A construction slowdown and prolonged high interest rates weakened demand and worsened profitability in the building PC unit, according to FnGuide. If the interest rate burden persists, recovery in the building business could be slower. Whether quarterly sales gains offset this pressure is the point to monitor.

Competition and profitability

In the segment business, sales fell as smaller rivals competed aggressively, according to FnGuide. If competition persists, pressure on both prices and volumes could continue. With the operating margin at -2.6% in 2025, whether quarterly profits translate into a sustained margin recovery is the key question.

Balance sheet and non-controlling interests

The debt-to-equity ratio reached 95.6% in 2025, and operating cash flow fell to KRW 96.36 million. The 2025 net loss of KRW 6.3 billion was larger than the KRW 3.45 billion loss attributable to controlling shareholders, so swings in non-controlling interests also move reported results. Overall view

Busan Industrial's sales fell from KRW 152.6 billion in 2022 to KRW 107.5 billion in 2025, three straight years of decline, and the company posted operating and net losses attributable to controlling shareholders in 2025, two straight years of losses.

Sales have risen for two straight quarters since the first-quarter 2026 low, reaching KRW 31.2 billion in the third quarter.

Profitability improved with operating profit in the first and second quarters of 2026, but third-quarter operating profit fell to KRW 396 million and the net loss attributable to controlling shareholders widened to KRW 1.73 billion.

The bullish factors are the sales recovery, the quarterly return to operating profit and a product mix linked to infrastructure demand, while the bearish factors are the accumulated annual losses, the higher debt-to-equity ratio and shrinking operating cash flow.

A weak construction market and prolonged high interest rates are external variables that affect both sides. The price-to-earnings ratio cannot be calculated given four quarters of net losses, and the price-to-book ratio sits below its recent quarter-end range.

The next checkpoints are the fourth-quarter 2026 provisional result and the 2026 annual business report, and the key questions are whether quarterly operating profit holds positive and whether the net loss attributable to controlling shareholders narrows.
