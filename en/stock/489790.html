02

Business structure

Hanwha Vision operates two segments: the Vision segment, which makes video security equipment, and a semiconductor and industrial equipment subsidiary that forms the equipment segment. In 2025 the Security segment's revenue was KRW 1.3351 trillion, up 10% from 2024, and it accounts for roughly 75% of group revenue.

The core product is network cameras, and the AI camera share within that line has risen steadily. Growth has been led by North America, and Middle East revenue jumped 22% in 2025.

Customer wins include a multi-camera integrated security system at a Nashville convention center and an AI camera deployment of about 2,000 units in Peru. Chinese vendors, which once dominated global CCTV, have faced security-related setbacks, lifting demand for higher-value AI products.

Omdia puts Hanwha Vision at a 6% share of the global CCTV market excluding China, ranking it in the top five. The equipment segment posted Q3 2026 revenue of KRW 169.8 billion and operating profit of KRW 37.3 billion, and it runs SMT equipment and machine tool businesses alongside semiconductor equipment. The company was spun off from Hanwha Aerospace in 2024 and relisted.

05

Industry analysis

For the global security market, the company cited Omdia data projecting average annual growth of 9.4% through 2029, a forecast it published in February 2026. On the demand side, AI-enabled camera demand is rising alongside security concerns over Chinese products.

In the UK security market, Omdia ranked Hanwha Vision second in share as of 2024. In the Middle East, its security systems are set to be installed in the Burj Aziz tower in Dubai. On costs, memory chip prices, which account for about 20% of product cost, have pushed up raw material expenses in the Security segment.

On trade, a 20% US tariff applies to products made in Vietnam and shipped to the US. In semiconductor equipment, the business is tilted toward advanced packaging.

Revenue recognition for panel-level packaging (FO-PLP) equipment began with large orders received in the first half of 2026, while thermocompression bonder (TCB) demand has slowed. A domestic brokerage expects TCB order recovery to start in the second half of 2027 after the P&T7 facility is completed.

Hybrid bonders are under customer evaluation. The company expects the SMT industry to return to medium-term growth.

06

Outlook

The company expects revenue and profit in its Vision segment to rise together in 2027, supported by wider AI camera adoption and demand from new sectors such as data centers, while noting that geopolitical and supply chain risks will persist.

In the equipment segment it expects the SMT industry to return to medium-term growth and semiconductor equipment to grow faster in both conventional and advanced packaging. It plans to keep launching products built on its in-house Wisenet9 AI chipset.

A domestic brokerage estimates 2026 revenue at KRW 2.06 trillion and operating profit at KRW 240 billion, up 48% from 2025. Because cumulative operating profit for the first three quarters was KRW 190.2 billion, reaching that estimate implies roughly KRW 50 billion in Q4 operating profit.

The same brokerage expects most FO-PLP equipment ordered in the first half to be delivered in Q4. The next checkpoints are how much Q4 equipment delivery shows up in reported results and whether inventory and borrowings move in step with revenue.

08

Bull factors

AI camera mix improving

AI cameras rose from 47% to 63% of network camera revenue between Q3 2025 and Q3 2026. The Vision segment's operating margin reached 16.2% in Q3 2026, which the company attributed to steady global demand and price increases. Middle East revenue grew 22% in 2025, and UK share was second as of 2024.

Equipment segment turned profitable

The equipment segment posted Q3 2026 revenue of KRW 169.8 billion and an operating profit of KRW 37.3 billion, turning from a loss a year earlier. Semiconductor equipment sales of KRW 84.8 billion reflected revenue recognition for panel-level packaging systems. A domestic brokerage said both segments outperformed expectations even excluding one-time gains.

Cumulative profit recovery

Revenue in 2025 rose 263.0% to KRW 1.79 trillion, and operating profit turned positive. Cumulative revenue for the first three quarters of 2026 reached KRW 1.56 trillion, with operating profit of KRW 190.2 billion. Net profit attributable to controlling shareholders built up to KRW 178.6 billion over the last four quarters.

09

Bear factors

Raw material and tariff pressure

Raw material purchases in the Security segment rose 94.5% in the first half of 2026 to KRW 174.5 billion, while revenue grew only 7.9%. The standalone cost of sales ratio rose from 50.7% to 54%. A 20% US tariff on Vietnam-made products adds further pressure on profitability.

Quarterly volatility in equipment

Semiconductor equipment revenue swung from KRW 6.6 billion in Q1 2026 to KRW 35.5 billion in Q2 and KRW 84.8 billion in Q3. Most of the second-quarter orders were recognized as revenue in the third quarter. TCB demand has slowed, and order recovery is expected from the second half of 2027.

Debt and inventory burden

Borrowings and bonds stood at KRW 537.8 billion at end-Q3 2026, up 49.4%, while cash fell 27.2%. Inventories rose 45.5% to KRW 460 billion, outpacing the 35.6% revenue growth in the same period. Net debt to equity was reported to have risen from 19.0% to 39.9%.

10

Risk factors

Trade and tariffs

Because exports to the US are central to the Security business, changes in tariff policy directly affect segment margins. Whether the 20% US tariff on Vietnam-made products stays in place shapes the cost structure. Tariff refunds linked to a court ruling were booked as one-time gains and can distort quarterly comparisons.

Raw materials and supply chain

Rising memory chip prices push up component costs, which make up about 20% of product cost. The company has said geopolitical and supply chain risks will persist. Whether price increases can absorb cost inflation without denting demand is the key question.

Balance sheet and inventory

The debt-to-equity ratio was 108.9% at end-2025 and was reported at 139.9% at end-Q3 2026. Inventory and borrowings are growing quickly, so cash flow and interest burden need monitoring. Other expenses rising to KRW 54.7 billion in Q3 reduced net profit, making non-operating items a risk to watch.

12

Overall view

Hanwha Vision's earnings improved on two fronts from the 2025 swing to operating profit through Q3 2026: a rising AI camera share in the Vision segment and a sharp quarter-to-quarter jump in semiconductor equipment sales.

Operating profit rose for two consecutive quarters in Q2 and Q3 2026, but Q3 included a one-time tariff refund. Net profit attributable to controlling shareholders fell in Q3 as other expenses rose.

Higher raw material and tariff costs held Security segment operating profit at KRW 51.6 billion in both Q1 2024 and Q2 2026, according to press reports. Borrowings and inventories grew quickly, and the debt-to-equity ratio rose from 108.9% at end-2025 to a reported 139.9% at end-Q3 2026.

The next checkpoints are Q4 2026 equipment deliveries, the 2026 full-year preliminary results in early 2027, and inventory and borrowing levels in the March 2027 annual report.
