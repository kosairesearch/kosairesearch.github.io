Partronco: Q1 Revenue Rebound, Then a Q3 Net Loss

Partronco's revenue rose to KRW 451 billion in Q1 2026, but the company swung to an attributable net loss of KRW 4.5 billion in Q3 2026, leaving its earnings direction mixed.

  1. 1

    Full-year 2025 revenue was KRW 1.35 trillion (down 9.2%) and operating profit was KRW 44.7 billion (down 27.5%), slowing after the 2024 peak.

  2. 2

    Q1 2026 revenue reached KRW 451 billion (up 33.2% from the prior quarter), but operating profit fell to KRW 12.2 billion, down 21.1% from the prior quarter.

  3. 3

    Q3 2026 revenue was KRW 356.2 billion, roughly flat on the prior quarter, while operating profit was KRW 5.64 billion and the attributable result turned to a KRW 4.5 billion loss.

  4. 4

  5. 5

02

Business structure

Partronco was founded in 2003 as an electronic components maker and runs three divisions: Gwangmeka, Sensor & Package, and Electronics. The Gwangmeka division is the core, producing camera modules for smartphones and for vehicles.

Smartphone camera modules are reported to be mounted in the S series, A series and Fold series lines of Samsung Electronics. Automotive camera modules reach vehicle makers through parts supply channels that include Hyundai Mobis.

The company is reported to have about 150 customers, with Samsung Electronics and Hyundai Mobis cited as key clients. The customer mix leans heavily toward Samsung Electronics. A Newsworker report put Samsung dependence at roughly 80% and treated it as a risk compounding margin pressure.

Production runs through a network that includes subsidiaries in China and Vietnam. Smartphone camera module demand moves quarter to quarter with the shipment schedules of Samsung's new models and the mix of high-pixel products.

Vehicle cameras are cited as beneficiaries of wider autonomous driving and a rising number of cameras per car.

The largest shareholder group, Kim Jong-koo and 12 related persons, held 30.40% as of September 1, 2026, and press coverage also points to an owner-centered management structure under founder-chairman Kim Jong-koo.

Earnings analysis

Annual revenue fell from KRW 1.22 trillion in 2022 to KRW 1.17 trillion in 2023 (down 4.1%) and then rose sharply to KRW 1.49 trillion in 2024 (up 26.8%). It declined again to KRW 1.35 trillion in 2025 (down 9.2%), and 2024 was the largest year and 2023 the smallest within 2022 to 2025.

Operating profit moved from KRW 56.8 billion in 2022 to KRW 41.7 billion in 2023 (down 26.6%), KRW 61.7 billion in 2024 (up 48.0%) and KRW 44.7 billion in 2025 (down 27.5%).

The operating margin declined from 4.6% in 2022 to 3.6% in 2023, 4.2% in 2024 and 3.3% in 2025, so margins stayed in a narrow low band even in years when sales grew.

Attributable net income was KRW 38.5 billion in 2022, KRW 28.8 billion in 2023 (down 25.2%), KRW 53.4 billion in 2024 (up 85.4%) and KRW 33.7 billion in 2025 (down 37.0%). Net income including non-controlling interests was KRW 33.1 billion in 2025, a different figure from attributable net income.

On a quarterly basis, revenue of KRW 381.4 billion and operating profit of KRW 16.5 billion in Q3 2025 gave way to revenue of KRW 338.6 billion and operating profit of KRW 15.5 billion in Q4 2025. Q1 2026 revenue rose to KRW 451 billion, but operating profit fell to KRW 12.2 billion.

Q2 2026 revenue was KRW 355 billion (down 21.3% from the prior quarter) and operating profit was KRW 4.48 billion (down 63.4%).

Q3 2026 revenue was KRW 356.2 billion (up 0.3% from the prior quarter and down 6.6% year on year), and operating profit was KRW 5.64 billion (up 26.0% from the prior quarter and down 65.8% year on year). The attributable net loss of KRW 4.5 billion followed attributable net income of KRW 15.1 billion in Q3 2025.

Pre-tax loss in the same quarter was KRW 7.69 billion, which widened the loss below the operating line (preliminary figure released October 30, 2026). Operating cash flow fell from KRW 99.3 billion in 2024 to KRW 54.2 billion in 2025, and the debt ratio rose from 28.8% in 2023 to 38.3% in 2025.

05

Industry analysis

Camera modules face demand from both higher-pixel smartphone cameras and a rising number of cameras per vehicle. Partronco's first-quarter 2026 improvement was credited in part to wider autonomous driving and more cameras fitted per car, which lifted automotive sales.

The same explanation pointed to AI smartphone launches lifting demand for optical modules. Earlier in 2026, brokerage analysts attributed the first-quarter revenue gain to a higher share of Samsung Electronics smartphones and a supply shift toward high-pixel models.

Smartphone camera module revenue moves with new-model launch cycles, so Q3 2026 revenue staying close to the prior quarter points to a flat shipment pattern from key customers. In competition, LG Innotek and Samsung Electro-Mechanics are large listed peers with camera module and component businesses.

Partronco's customer concentration is higher than theirs, and dependence on a few large customers is cited as a structural challenge. Currency is another variable: early-2026 brokerage commentary cited a favorable won-dollar rate as a contributor to margin improvement.

Automotive cameras require parts certification and long-term supply relationships, so new vehicle-camera awards will shape how visible the diversification becomes.

Bull factors

Q1 revenue recovery

Q1 2026 revenue reached KRW 451 billion, up 33.2% from the prior quarter, and press reports put the year-on-year gain at 37.5% (preliminary release on April 30, 2026). A higher share of high-pixel smartphone camera modules and expanded supply to Samsung Electronics were cited as drivers.

The larger revenue base spreads fixed costs over more output. Whether operating profit follows the same path is a separate question.

Automotive camera growth

Wider autonomous driving and rising camera counts per vehicle were cited as drivers of automotive camera module sales and of the Q1 2026 improvement. Vehicle cameras run on a demand cycle different from smartphones, which supports diversification. Automotive parts can also lead to long-term supply relationships with vehicle makers.

Balance sheet and cash generation

Total equity at the end of 2025 was KRW 560.3 billion and total liabilities were KRW 214.9 billion. Operating cash flow remained positive at KRW 54.2 billion in 2025. A cash dividend was decided alongside approval of the 23rd fiscal year financial statements in March 2026.

Bear factors

Q3 swing to a loss

The attributable net loss in Q3 2026 was KRW 4.5 billion, against attributable net income of KRW 15.1 billion in Q3 2025. Operating profit of KRW 5.64 billion was down 65.8% year on year. The pre-tax loss was KRW 7.69 billion (preliminary release on October 30, 2026). Operating profit rose from KRW 4.48 billion in Q2 2026, but the recovery remains limited.

Dependence on Samsung

A large share of revenue comes from the Samsung Electronics supply chain, which is cited as a weakness. Newsworker described the Samsung dependence as a dual risk combined with margin deterioration. If Samsung's new-model shipments fall or its supply share shrinks, quarterly earnings swings could widen. Concentration in one customer also weighs on negotiating leverage.

Slower profitability and cash flow

The operating margin declined from 4.6% in 2022 to 3.3% in 2025. Operating cash flow fell from KRW 99.3 billion in 2024 to KRW 54.2 billion in 2025. The debt ratio rose from 28.8% in 2023 to 38.3% in 2025, moving in the direction of thinner financial room as earnings weakened.

Risk factors

Customer concentration

Dependence on a few large customers, Samsung Electronics above all, means weaker smartphone sales or changes in parts adoption pass straight into revenue. Press coverage links the 27.5% decline in 2025 operating profit to this structure. The pace of customer diversification is the key variable in reducing this risk.

Currency and overseas production

The won-dollar rate affects profitability for parts makers with high export exposure. Early-2026 brokerage commentary treated a favorable rate as a margin tailwind, but a reversal could pressure margins.

Operating costs at the China and Vietnam subsidiaries and adjustments to local production are also variables to watch.

Governance and shareholder structure

The Kim Jong-koo group and 12 related persons hold 30.40%, and press coverage raises concerns about owner-centered management. Changes in the largest shareholder group's stake or management decisions could become points of contention for minority holders. Governance improvement is a separate item to monitor from operating risk.
