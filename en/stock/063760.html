02

Business structure

ELP was founded in 1999 and listed on KOSDAQ in 2017 as a maker of display inspection equipment. Its core products are aging and inspection equipment for OLED panels, complemented by micro display inspection equipment and semiconductor packaging inspection equipment.

By Butler's tally, display inspection equipment accounted for 100% of revenue in the second quarter of 2026. The product line now extends to inspection tools for flexible, foldable and wearable displays, built on high-speed signal processing, precision measurement, precision lighting jigs and contactor technology.

Main customers are domestic display makers including Samsung Display and LG Display. Production and customer support are handled by the Hwaseong headquarters and a Suzhou subsidiary in China.

Recent orders began with a domestic customer's 8.6G investment and continued with large-substrate investments by a Chinese customer, which also generated micro LED equipment sales.

Although ELP sits in the electrical equipment sector, its business is centered on display equipment, so its demand drivers differ from those of power-infrastructure peers such as LS ELECTRIC and HD Hyundai Electric.

Competition is shaped by customers' equipment investment schedules and inspection technology levels, and with nearly all revenue from one business, the company's results track the investment cycle of a small number of display makers.

04

Earnings analysis

On a DART-filed annual basis, revenue was KRW 23.6 billion in 2022, KRW 21.4 billion in 2023 (down 9.3% year on year), KRW 28 billion in 2024 (up 30.8%), and KRW 27.3 billion in 2025 (down 2.5%). Among 2022 to 2025, 2024 was the largest year and 2023 the smallest.

Operating losses were KRW 3.22 billion in 2022 and widened to KRW 5.63 billion in 2023, then narrowed to KRW 4.2 billion in 2024. The company moved to an operating profit of KRW 2.3 billion in 2025, an operating margin of 8.4%. Operating margins were -13.6% in 2022, -26.3% in 2023 and -15.0% in 2024.

In 2024 revenue rose 30.8% yet an operating loss of KRW 4.2 billion persisted, so higher sales did not translate directly into profit.

Profit attributable to controlling shareholders alternated between losses and profits: a loss of KRW 1.61 billion in 2022, a profit of KRW 568 million in 2023, a loss of KRW 1.32 billion in 2024 and a profit of KRW 2.6 billion in 2025.

Net profit including non-controlling interests was reported at the same amounts in each year.

A news report on the February 2026 disclosure said the company attributed the 2025 revenue decline to timing effects of customer equipment investment and deliveries, while higher-margin project revenue and cost cuts improved profitability.

By quarter, profit attributable to controlling shareholders peaked at KRW 2.84 billion in the fourth quarter of 2025, after KRW 2.76 billion in the third quarter of 2025.

It then fell to KRW 1.21 billion in the first quarter of 2026, KRW 626 million in the second quarter and KRW 28.36 million in the third, three straight quarterly declines. Third-quarter 2026 revenue was KRW 7.93 billion, down 23.9% year on year, and operating profit was KRW 578 million, down 77.8%.

A news report noted that cumulative operating profit for the first nine months of 2026 nonetheless turned positive. The financial statements in the third-quarter 2026 report were recorded as neither audited nor reviewed.

At the end of 2025, total equity was KRW 64.5 billion, all attributable to controlling shareholders, total liabilities were KRW 15.8 billion and the debt ratio was 24.5%, compared with 31.8% in 2022, 11.1% in 2023 and 21.2% in 2024.

Operating cash flow was KRW 5 billion in 2022, -KRW 3.24 billion in 2023, KRW 5.36 billion in 2024 and KRW 842 million in 2025.

Industry analysis

Demand from downstream customers moves with OLED panel makers' equipment investment and the expansion of micro displays. An equity information site summarized that a domestic customer's 8.6G investment was followed by large-substrate investment from a Chinese customer, leading to equipment orders and deliveries.

The same source viewed large-substrate investment by OLED makers and the growth of the micro LED market as sources of growth potential. In contrast, another analysis published about two years ago cited a downturn in the display industry as the main cause of falling sales.

The two views differ depending on which point of the same customer investment cycle they emphasize. The 2025 return to profit and the quarterly profit decline in 2026 appearing back to back can also be read as swings within this cycle.

The cycle position can be checked through new order disclosures and the trend of quarterly revenue. On competition, ELP emphasizes its OLED panel aging and inspection equipment and lighting jig technology.

Power-infrastructure peers in the same electrical equipment sector, such as LS ELECTRIC, Hyosung Heavy Industries and HD Hyundai Electric, are centered on transformer and distribution demand, so their demand structure differs. Semiconductor packaging inspection equipment is a business area the company is pursuing as diversification.

Outlook

The mid-term target the company has made public is the FY2030 set of figures in its 2026 corporate value-up plan. The plan includes an operating margin of 10% or more and a price-to-book ratio target of 0.70 times.

Progress reported since a March 2026 short-form disclosure includes maintaining a profit trend, paying quarterly dividends, launching English-language disclosures and providing IR books in Korean, English and Chinese.

The company said it will disclose an evaluation of implementation and its results within one year of the plan's disclosure. On shareholder returns, dividends attributable to 2025 totaled KRW 850 million, up 100% from 2024, with a payout ratio of 32.7%.

The company stated that it qualifies as a high-dividend company under the Restriction of Special Taxation Act. The first checkpoint on the business side is whether revenue recovers.

Third-quarter 2026 revenue was KRW 7.93 billion, down 23.9% year on year, and quarterly revenue from here may vary with customer investment and delivery timing. The second checkpoint is whether equipment sales tied to large substrates and micro LEDs continue.

Measured against the 8.4% operating margin of 2025, the 10% or higher target implies further improvement is needed. Full-year 2026 figures will be fixed when the 2026 annual report is filed.

Risk factors

Customer concentration and capex cycle

Revenue depends heavily on investment schedules of domestic display makers including Samsung Display and LG Display. If customer investment is delayed, deliveries slip and quarterly revenue can fall sharply. Second-quarter 2026 revenue fell 38.4% from the prior quarter, illustrating this structure.

Display industry cycle and technology transition

An analysis published about two years ago identified the downturn in the display industry as the main cause of falling sales. If the pace of the shift toward next-generation equipment such as micro LED differs across customers, demand for existing OLED inspection equipment could weaken. Semiconductor packaging inspection equipment is presented as a business still in the diversification stage.

Audit level and small-cap liquidity

The third-quarter 2026 report's financial statements were recorded as neither audited nor reviewed. Whether full-year figures are confirmed through audit in the 2026 annual report is a checkpoint. As a small KOSDAQ company, ELP may see larger share price swings and thinner trading liquidity.

Overall view

ELP is a specialist in OLED and micro display inspection equipment. It moved to an operating profit of KRW 2.3 billion in 2025, an operating margin of 8.4%.

However, profit attributable to controlling shareholders peaked at KRW 2.84 billion in the fourth quarter of 2025 and fell to KRW 28.36 million in the third quarter of 2026, three straight quarterly declines, and third-quarter 2026 revenue was down 23.9% year on year.

Total equity at the end of 2025 was KRW 64.5 billion, with a debt ratio of 24.5%. Bullish factors are the return to profit, the size of equity, higher 2025 dividends and the value-up plan. Bearish factors are the quarterly profit decline, reliance on customer capex and the operating losses of 2022 to 2024.

The gap between the FY2030 targets and the current multiple, along with the display capex cycle, remains the two axes of overall assessment. The next checkpoints are the full-year figures in the 2026 annual report, the first-quarter 2027 report and the implementation evaluation of the value-up plan.
