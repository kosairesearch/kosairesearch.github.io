02

Business structure

Eusu Holdings is a holding company that spun off its core shipping business and now oversees logistics and IT subsidiaries. After Hanjin Shipping was transferred to the Hanjin Group in 2014, the company has been seeking new businesses.

As of 2026, its main subsidiaries are Eusu Logistics, a third-party logistics (3PL) provider, and Cyberlogitec, which develops and sells software. Overseas, the group runs logistics entities in China, India, Vietnam, Singapore and several other countries.

The logistics segment covers ocean and air freight forwarding, customs clearance, warehousing and plant projects, and the company's Q1 2025 IR material said ocean freight sales rose 27% and air freight sales rose 24% year on year.

According to a Dealsite report, first-half 2025 logistics revenue was KRW 148.4 billion with operating profit of KRW 1.5 billion, a margin of about 1%. The same report put IT revenue at KRW 58.9 billion with operating profit of KRW 12.5 billion, a margin of 21.2%. Logistics drives top-line size while IT drives margins.

A securities analysis from early 2024 named Hanjin, CJ Logistics, Hyundai Glovis and LX International as comparison targets. The company is also expanding raw-material procurement and inventory management services for apparel manufacturers as part of its supply chain management business.

04

Earnings analysis

Annual revenue fell from KRW 557.6 billion in 2022 to KRW 341.8 billion in 2023, a 38.7% decline, then rose 27.5% to KRW 435.9 billion in 2024 and eased 4.0% to KRW 418.5 billion in 2025. Among 2022 to 2025, 2022 was the largest year and 2023 the smallest.

Figures from 2023 onward reflect discontinued operations, so the comparison with 2022 should be read with that in mind. Operating profit moved from KRW 29.6 billion in 2022 to KRW 21 billion in 2023 (down 29.1%), KRW 18.3 billion in 2024 (down 12.8%) and KRW 21.2 billion in 2025 (up 15.9%).

Operating margin was 5.3% in 2022, 6.1% in 2023, 4.2% in 2024 and 5.1% in 2025, with 2024 the lowest of the four years. In its Q1 2025 IR material, the company explained that the Q4 2024 operating loss stemmed from contributing 600,000 treasury shares, worth KRW 9.3 billion, to an employee welfare fund.

For the 2025 operating profit increase, the company cited higher new IT orders, while a rise in one-off bad-debt provisions in logistics weighed on results.

Net income attributable to controlling shareholders was KRW 29.1 billion in 2022, KRW 10.2 billion in 2023 (down 64.9%), KRW 16.5 billion in 2024 (up 61.2%) and KRW 4.57 billion in 2025 (down 72.2%).

Net income including non-controlling interests fell from KRW 44.1 billion in 2022 to KRW 16.8 billion in 2025, and the gap between the two measures is the non-controlling share.

Quarterly revenue bottomed at KRW 103.2 billion in Q4 2025 and then rose across three consecutive quarters to KRW 106.3 billion in Q1 2026, KRW 121.8 billion in Q2 and KRW 135.4 billion in Q3.

Operating profit swung to a loss of KRW 3.15 billion in Q4 2025, then recovered to KRW 9.95 billion in Q1 2026, KRW 12.8 billion in Q2 (up 28.6% quarter on quarter) and KRW 10.1 billion in Q3 (down 21.3%).

Attributable net income returned to profit at KRW 7.74 billion in Q1 2026, then fell to KRW 5.91 billion in Q2 and turned into a KRW 406 million loss in Q3. Q4 2026 results have not yet been published in a periodic report.

05

Industry analysis

The logistics business in Eusu Holdings is tied to trade volumes and freight rates through its ocean and air forwarding and customs services. In its Q1 2025 IR material, ocean and air sales rose, but sales at the US-based IMT unit fell 46%, showing that the cycle has differed by region and business line.

Dealsite wrote in 2025 that after spinning off its shipping business, the company had shrunk to a mid-sized company from its earlier revenue scale.

The IT business touches port and logistics automation demand, including terminal operating systems, digital twins and safety-control systems, and the company history lists a Taiwanese TS LINES build contract in June 2025 and Spanish and Busan terminal contracts in June 2026.

Against the peers named in a 2024 securities analysis, Hanjin, CJ Logistics, Hyundai Glovis and LX International, the logistics revenue base is comparatively small. Dealsite also noted that the share of the higher-margin IT business has been rising.

The same report said that none of the new businesses the company promised has yet taken root, according to a view circulating in the press. The direction of the ocean and air freight cycle should be read against freight rates and trade volume trends.

Outlook

The most concrete basis for the outlook is the company's disclosed contract wins. In June 2025, a Cyberlogitec contract to build the ALLEGRO system for Taiwan's TS LINES appeared in the company history.

In June 2026, a supply contract for a terminal operating system and digital twin solution for Spain's TTIA terminal and a safety-control system build for the Busan BNCT terminal were listed together. These are port and terminal system wins, so IT revenue can vary by quarter depending on delivery and recognition timing.

Q3 2026 revenue of KRW 135.4 billion is the highest quarterly figure in the disclosed results, and whether the three-quarter growth streak continues is the next checkpoint. For logistics, the key question is whether the rise in ocean and air sales continues through 2026.

New IT orders drove the 2025 operating profit increase, so whether they carry into 2026 is the central variable for profit recovery. Since bad-debt provisions in logistics weighed on 2025 operating profit, the trend of such costs in 2026 is also a item to track.

The gap between Q2 2026 operating profit of KRW 12.8 billion and Q3 of KRW 10.1 billion shows that profit lagged revenue growth in the latest quarter.

On the new business front, Dealsite wrote in 2025 that concern exists that the heir in his thirties, who leads new-business efforts, may find it hard to justify succession without securing growth drivers.

The Q1 2026 results were released on May 15, 2026, and they served as the starting point for the quarterly revenue rise that followed.

08

Bull factors

Revenue recovery

After the Q4 2025 low, revenue rose for three straight quarters to KRW 106.3 billion in Q1 2026, KRW 121.8 billion in Q2 and KRW 135.4 billion in Q3. Q3 revenue was up 28.8% year on year. The combined top line of logistics and IT is regaining size, which is cited as a bullish point.

IT contract track record

The company history lists a June 2025 TS LINES build contract in Taiwan, a June 2026 terminal operating system supply contract for Spain's TTIA, and a Busan BNCT terminal safety-control system project. Continued port and terminal system wins are a source of growth potential for the IT business. Contract amounts are not publicly disclosed, so the contribution must be read from quarterly results.

Low leverage and cash flow recovery

At end-2025, total liabilities were KRW 67.5 billion against total equity of KRW 412.2 billion, a debt-to-equity ratio of 16.4%. Operating cash flow recovered from KRW 4.59 billion in 2024 to KRW 34.1 billion in 2025. Liabilities are small relative to equity, which is a financial strength.

09

Bear factors

Weaker earnings power

Net income attributable to controlling shareholders fell 72.2% to KRW 4.57 billion in 2025. In Q3 2026 it turned into a KRW 406 million loss compared with a year earlier, and operating profit fell 21.3% from the prior quarter. Revenue growth has not translated into profit growth, which is a bearish point.

Low logistics profitability

According to a Dealsite report, first-half 2025 logistics margin was about 1%, far below the 21.2% of the IT business. Higher one-off bad-debt provisions in logistics weighed on 2025 operating profit. A large logistics business with low margins drags down the group's overall margin, which is a bearish point.

External concerns on new businesses and succession

In a 2025 report, Dealsite said that a view exists that none of the new businesses the company promised has yet taken root. The same report raised concern that the third-generation heir leading new-business efforts may find it hard to justify succession without securing growth drivers. Delays in new-business results are a bearish point.

10

Risk factors

Logistics bad debt and profitability

Even as operating profit rose in 2025, one-off bad-debt provisions in logistics increased and weighed on results. Because logistics margins are low, changes in receivable collection or counterparty credit can swing quarterly profit sharply. The quarterly trend of bad-debt costs is the next item to track.

Overseas subsidiaries and cyclical demand

Eusu Logistics operates several overseas entities in China, India, Vietnam, Singapore and the United States, so local economic conditions and currency moves apply. When ocean and air freight rates and trade volumes shift, logistics results can change quickly. Software sales are mostly exported, so the IT business is also exposed to currency movements.

Governance and new-business uncertainty

Because succession and new-business execution are linked, a change in direction could affect capital allocation and dividend policy. With profit attributable to controlling shareholders having declined, dividend capacity and investment capacity both become items to examine. The timing of monetizing new businesses remains unclear, which is a risk.

Overall view

Eusu Holdings is a holding company built on logistics and IT. Operating profit rose in 2025 while net income attributable to controlling shareholders fell sharply.

Revenue rose for three straight quarters in 2026 and Q2 operating profit reached KRW 12.8 billion, but in Q3 operating profit fell to KRW 10.1 billion and attributable net income turned into a KRW 406 million loss.

A 2025 press report confirmed that the IT business carries high margins while the logistics business runs low margins. Bullish factors include the revenue recovery, IT port and terminal contracts, a debt-to-equity ratio of 16.4% and the recovery in 2025 operating cash flow.

Bearish factors include lower attributable earnings, low logistics margins with bad-debt burdens, and outside concern over new businesses and succession. On valuation, the price-to-book ratio sits below 1x, but on earnings the reading is mixed because quarterly results swing widely.

The next checkpoints are the 2026 full-year preliminary results, the 2026 annual business report and the Q1 2027 results.
