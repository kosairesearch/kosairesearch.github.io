Report overview

Earnings Recovery and Shareholder Returns, with Fee Income as the Swing Factor

Net income attributable to controlling shareholders reached KRW 4.97 trillion in 2025, the largest year of 2022–2025, while fee income fell sharply in the third quarter of 2026 even as interest income and shareholder returns kept growing.

02

Business structure

Shinhan Financial Group is a financial holding company with banking, securities, card, life insurance, asset management and capital financing units. Interest income forms the core of group earnings.

Cumulative group interest income for the first three quarters of 2026 was KRW 9.35 trillion, up 7.9% from a year earlier, according to results released on October 27, 2026. Group net interest margin was 1.91% in the third quarter, down 0.02 percentage points from the prior quarter.

Cumulative net income of the bank unit reached KRW 3.68 trillion, up 9.8%. The securities unit's cumulative net income was KRW 642.9 billion, up 78.9%, but its third-quarter net income alone was KRW 65.3 billion, down 77.4% from the prior quarter.

The card unit's cumulative net income was KRW 485.8 billion, up 27.7%, while the life insurance unit's was KRW 419.4 billion, down 18.5%. Overseas business posted cumulative net income of KRW 698.3 billion, up 7.4%.

The group's CET1 ratio stood at 13.62% at the end of the third quarter, up 0.12 percentage points from the prior quarter. In competition, the group is compared with KB Financial Group, Hana Financial Group and Woori Financial Group, and its securities unit competes with Mirae Asset Securities and KIWOOM Securities.

04

Earnings analysis

Annual net income attributable to controlling shareholders fell from KRW 4.67 trillion in 2022 to KRW 4.37 trillion in 2023, down 6.4%, before rising to KRW 4.45 trillion in 2024, up 1.9%, and KRW 4.97 trillion in 2025, up 11.7%.

Growth has continued for two consecutive years from 2024 to 2025, and 2025 was the largest year of 2022–2025.

Operating profit rose from KRW 5.91 trillion in 2022 to KRW 6.1 trillion in 2023, up 3.3%, KRW 6.46 trillion in 2024, up 5.9%, and KRW 7.02 trillion in 2025, up 8.7%, increasing for three consecutive years from 2023 to 2025.

In 2023, operating profit rose while net income attributable to controlling shareholders declined, which shows that profit fell at a stage after operating profit.

Net income including non-controlling interests was KRW 4.76 trillion in 2022, KRW 4.48 trillion in 2023, KRW 4.56 trillion in 2024 and KRW 5.08 trillion in 2025. Insurance revenue, an account on its own, rose 8.0% in 2025 to KRW 3.36 trillion from KRW 3.12 trillion.

On a quarterly basis, net income attributable to controlling shareholders bottomed at KRW 510.6 billion in the fourth quarter of 2025, down 64.1% from the prior quarter, then recovered to KRW 1.62 trillion in the first quarter of 2026, up 217.8%, and KRW 1.82 trillion in the second quarter, up 12.2%.

It stood at KRW 1.58 trillion in the third quarter, down 12.9% from the prior quarter. Operating profit was KRW 1.91 trillion in the third quarter of 2026, down 22.9% from the prior quarter.

In the third quarter, fee income fell 27.7% from the prior quarter as stock market activity weakened, and valuation losses on securities rose with interest rates. Operating cash flow was KRW 7.19 trillion in 2022, KRW 529.8 billion in 2023, KRW 4.63 trillion in 2024 and KRW 9.73 trillion in 2025.

At the end of 2025, total equity stood at KRW 60.4 trillion, with equity attributable to controlling shareholders at KRW 58 trillion, and the debt ratio (total liabilities ÷ total equity) rose from 1,143.7% in 2022 to 1,201.9% in 2025.

Because financial holding companies carry large liabilities by structure, this ratio is best read against peer financial holding companies.

05

Industry analysis

Bank-led holding company earnings depend on the direction of interest rates and on trading volumes in capital markets. In the third quarter of 2026, a drop in stock trading value and rising market rates occurred together, which weakened brokerage commissions and valuation gains on bonds and reduced non-interest income.

Interest income rose on growth in won-denominated loans, which stood at KRW 345.25 trillion at the end of September, up 3.3% from the end of 2025. Loan repricing has lagged deposit repricing, which analysts cited as a reason net interest margin has held up better than expected.

NH Investment & Securities, in a report ahead of the third-quarter results, expected NIM to rebound from the fourth quarter as loan repricing effects are reflected.

NIM gaps against KB Financial Group, Hana Financial Group and Woori Financial Group are best checked by comparing each company's disclosures on the same basis. The securities unit's earnings track whether stock trading value recovers.

Credit costs have stayed contained: third-quarter provisions rose 11.2% from the prior quarter to KRW 486.3 billion, while the cumulative credit cost ratio stood at 0.42%. Whether asset quality deteriorates with an economic slowdown, and how the property market moves, are common sector variables to watch. Changes in monetary policy are also an external factor that could shift the direction of NIM.

Outlook

Shareholder return plans presented by the company are central to this outlook. Total shareholder returns for 2026 were presented at about KRW 3 trillion, including an expected cash dividend of KRW 1.4 trillion. Cumulative total shareholder return ratio for the first three quarters of 2026 was 50.2%.

On October 27, 2026, the board approved an additional KRW 450 billion share buyback, of which KRW 200 billion will be purchased in October to December 2026 and KRW 250 billion in January 2027. The CET1 ratio of 13.62% at the end of the third quarter, up from the prior quarter, also supports these returns.

On earnings, the key items for the next quarters are a NIM rebound and a recovery in non-interest income. NH Investment & Securities expects NIM to rebound as loan repricing effects are reflected from the fourth quarter.

KIWOOM Securities described the second quarter of 2026 as a record for a single quarter and expects the earnings increase to support larger shareholder returns. The non-life insurer acquisition remains an open item, and the pace at which capital and profit normalize at the insurance arm is a variable.

Full-year 2026 results have not yet been disclosed and will be fixed in the 2026 annual report, due by the end of March 2027. The next checkpoints are the execution of the share buybacks and the NIM trend in the first quarter of 2027.

08

Bull factors

Three Years of Rising Operating Profit

Operating profit rose from KRW 5.91 trillion in 2022 to KRW 7.02 trillion in 2025, increasing for three consecutive years from 2023. Net income attributable to controlling shareholders of KRW 4.97 trillion in 2025 was the largest year of 2022–2025.

Cumulative interest income for the first three quarters of 2026 reached KRW 9.35 trillion, up 7.9%, showing that the core lending business is expanding.

Expanding Shareholder Returns

Total shareholder returns for 2026 were presented at about KRW 3 trillion, and the cumulative total shareholder return ratio for the first three quarters was 50.2%. On October 27, 2026, the board approved an additional KRW 450 billion share buyback. Of this, KRW 200 billion will be bought in October to December 2026 and KRW 250 billion in January 2027.

Capital Headroom

The CET1 ratio rose to 13.62% at the end of the third quarter of 2026, up 0.12 percentage points from the prior quarter. The effect of the non-life insurer acquisition on the CET1 ratio was estimated as a limited decline. Pairing share buybacks with dividends while holding this capital headroom is cited as a supporting argument.

09

Bear factors

Sharp Drop in Fee Income

Non-interest income fell to KRW 790.2 billion in the third quarter of 2026, down 45.5% from the prior quarter. Fee income fell 27.7%, and valuation losses on bonds from rising rates added to the decline.

Operating profit was KRW 1.91 trillion, down 22.9% from the prior quarter, making weaker non-interest income the largest driver of the slowdown.

Sensitivity of the Securities Unit to Trading Volumes

The securities unit's third-quarter 2026 net income alone was KRW 65.3 billion, down 77.4% from the prior quarter. Lower trading volumes reduced brokerage commissions, and valuation losses in the product investment business were recognized.

Even though cumulative net income rose 78.9% year on year, quarterly earnings are volatile, and this dependence on the securities unit is a bearish factor.

Burden of Normalizing the Non-Life Insurer

After the non-life insurer acquisition, whether the insurance arm's losses narrow and its capital ratio recovers is the key point to monitor. One brokerage's report indicated that 2026 may be a record but that earnings could decline slightly in 2027 given the high base and normalization of the acquired insurer.

10

Risk factors

Interest Rates and NIM

Group NIM was 1.91% in the third quarter of 2026, down 0.02 percentage points from the prior quarter. The timing of any NIM rebound depends on whether loan repricing effects appear from the fourth quarter. If monetary policy changes, the gap between the pace of loan rate and deposit rate adjustments could widen again.

Stock Trading Volumes and Fee Income

When stock trading value falls, brokerage commissions and fees from funds and trusts fall together. In the third quarter both occurred at once and non-interest income dropped sharply. If the slowdown in market activity persists, the scope for quarterly earnings to recover could be limited.

Asset Quality and Capital Strain from the Acquisition

Third-quarter provisions rose 11.2% from the prior quarter to KRW 486.3 billion. If the economic slowdown deepens, asset quality could deteriorate and provisioning burdens could increase. If losses at the acquired insurer continue, they could weigh on group capital headroom and the shareholder return plan.

  2. January 2027

  3. By the end of March 2027

    The 2026 annual report will be filed, fixing full-year 2026 operating profit, net income attributable to controlling shareholders and dividends. This is when 2026 is first added to the annual disclosed results.

  4. Late April 2027 (expected)

    The first-quarter 2027 results should show whether NIM rebounded, whether non-interest income recovered and whether losses at the insurance arm narrowed.

Overall view

Shinhan Financial Group's operating profit increased for three consecutive years from 2023 to 2025, and net income attributable to controlling shareholders was highest in 2025 among 2022–2025.

On a cumulative basis through the third quarter of 2026, interest income and the securities unit's cumulative net income both rose from a year earlier.

On a standalone third-quarter basis, however, non-interest income fell 45.5% and net income attributable to controlling shareholders fell 12.9% from the prior quarter, showing quarterly volatility tied to stock trading volumes and the rate environment.

Shareholder returns are moving toward a larger scale, with total returns for 2026 presented at about KRW 3 trillion and additional share buybacks, and the CET1 ratio of 13.62% is cited as support.

Dependence of securities earnings on trading volumes and the normalization burden at the non-life insurer remain bearish factors.

Target prices of KRW 150,000 from KIWOOM Securities, presented on July 24, 2026, and KRW 132,000 from Hanwha INVESTMENT&SECURITIES, presented on October 28, 2026, are the views of those brokerages and not opinions of this report.

The next checkpoints are execution of the share buybacks through December 2026, the 2026 annual report due by the end of March 2027, and the NIM trend in the first quarter of 2027.
