Report overview

Earnings Rebound Meets a Lotte Insurance Bet

Shinhan Financial Group lifted quarterly profit for two straight quarters in the first half of 2026, leaving behind the one-off cost shock of 4Q 2025. Since it signed the Lotte Non-Life Insurance deal in October 2026, however, the market has been reassessing its capital ratio and its room for shareholder returns.

  1. 1

    Operating profit reached KRW 7.02 trillion in 2025, the third straight annual increase since 2023. Net profit attributable to owners rose 11.7% year on year to KRW 4.97 trillion.

  2. 2

    Net profit attributable to owners was KRW 1.82 trillion in 2Q 2026, which the company called a quarterly record. Hana Securities noted that about KRW 164.5 billion of this came from reversals, including a refunded penalty.

  3. 3

    The group is buying back and cancelling KRW 1.4 trillion of shares from January to October 2026, more than the KRW 1.25 trillion it bought in all of 2025. The size of any extra fourth-quarter buyback will be set at the board meeting on October 27, 2026.

  4. 4

    On October 13, 2026, the group agreed to buy 77.04% of Lotte Non-Life Insurance for KRW 693.3 billion. Hana Securities estimates the CET1 ratio could fall by up to 33bp once the remaining stake and a capital injection are included.

  5. 5

    Brokers expect the net interest margin (NIM) to dip in 3Q because funding costs are rising. They expect it to recover from 4Q as loan rates reset.

02

Business structure

Shinhan Financial Group is a financial holding company built around Shinhan Bank, with subsidiaries in cards, securities, life insurance, asset management and capital.

In 2Q 2026, net profit by subsidiary was KRW 1.30 trillion at Shinhan Bank, KRW 289.3 billion at Shinhan Securities, KRW 187.5 billion at Shinhan Life, KRW 138.0 billion at Shinhan Card and KRW 35.3 billion at Shinhan Asset Management.

Non-bank earnings in the first half of 2026 totalled KRW 1.33 trillion, up 38.3% year on year. In 2Q 2026, non-interest income rose to 30% of total income. Overseas operations earned KRW 824.3 billion in 2025, or 16.6% of group profit, with Vietnam, Japan and Kazakhstan as key markets.

The consolidated insurance revenue line was KRW 3.36 trillion in 2025, showing how much the insurance affiliates add to the group's scale. At home, the group competes with KB Financial Group, Hana Financial Group and Woori Financial Group, the other big four holding companies.

Shinhan Bank earns more than KB Kookmin Bank, but KB Financial Group leads on group net profit thanks to its stronger insurance arm.

The non-life affiliate, Shinhan EZ General Insurance, posted a net loss of KRW 18.2 billion in the first half of 2026 and has long been seen as the group's weak spot; the Lotte Non-Life Insurance deal is meant to fill that gap.

04

Earnings analysis

Annual operating profit rose for three straight years, from KRW 5.91 trillion in 2022 to KRW 6.1 trillion in 2023, KRW 6.46 trillion in 2024 and KRW 7.02 trillion in 2025. 2025 was the largest year in the 2022-2025 period.

Net profit attributable to owners bottomed at KRW 4.37 trillion in 2023 and then rose for two straight years, to KRW 4.45 trillion in 2024 and KRW 4.97 trillion in 2025. Press reports described the 2025 figure as an annual record.

The company credited the 2025 increase to growth in non-interest income (KRW 3.74 trillion, up 14.4%), better results at the securities unit, and the absence of the prior year's one-off losses.

On a quarterly basis, operating profit fell to KRW 1.11 trillion and net profit attributable to owners to KRW 510.6 billion in 4Q 2025.

That quarter was hit by early-retirement costs, pre-emptive provisions for real estate project finance (PF), regulatory penalties tied to LTV and ELT cases, and a contribution to a policy fund, which together produced a non-operating loss of KRW 209.6 billion.

Net profit attributable to owners then rose for two straight quarters, to KRW 1.62 trillion in 1Q 2026 and KRW 1.82 trillion in 2Q 2026, with 2Q up 17.5% year on year.

In 2Q, both non-interest income (KRW 1.45 trillion, up 22% quarter on quarter) and interest income (KRW 3.13 trillion, up 3.6%) grew, while loan-loss provisions fell 14.7% to KRW 437.3 billion. Net profit attributable to owners over the last four quarters totalled KRW 5.38 trillion, above the full-year 2025 figure.

Net income including non-controlling interests was KRW 5.08 trillion in 2025. Insurance revenue rose for four straight quarters to KRW 916.4 billion in 2Q 2026.

As is typical for a financial group, operating cash flow swings widely with loan and deposit movements, from KRW 529.8 billion in 2023 to KRW 9.73 trillion in 2025.

05

Industry analysis

In the second half of 2026, Korean banks face rising market rates while low-cost deposits drain away and funding costs climb. Yuanta Securities expects major banks' 3Q NIM to slip by 0.02-0.04 percentage points from the prior quarter. Loan rates usually take three to six months to reset.

Brokers therefore expect the base-rate hikes of July and August 2026 to start showing up in lending rates from 4Q. The KRX Bank index fell 8.13% in the month to October 8, 2026. Over the same period, Shinhan Financial Group dropped 12.10%, more than Hana Financial Group, KB Financial Group and Woori Financial Group.

According to Hana Securities, bank stocks fell only 2.9% in the second week of October 2026, when the KOSPI lost 14.5% on a semiconductor sell-off. In that same week, however, Shinhan Financial Group fell 6.1% after the Lotte Non-Life Insurance announcement, the biggest drop among bank stocks.

Competition among holding companies is shifting from profit size toward CET1 ratios and buyback-and-cancellation volumes, and quarterly dividends plus large buybacks are now standard practice across the big four.

06

Outlook

The next checkpoint is the 3Q results release on October 27, 2026. NH Investment & Securities estimates 3Q net profit attributable to owners at KRW 1.56 trillion, slightly below the market consensus of KRW 1.60 trillion. SK Securities estimates KRW 1.62 trillion, in line with consensus.

Both expect a gain of about KRW 200 billion from selling real estate, including an office building, to partly offset the lower NIM and weaker fee income. For full-year 2026, NH Investment & Securities forecasts net profit attributable to owners of KRW 5.69 trillion and SK Securities KRW 5.94 trillion.

On the July 2026 earnings call, CFO Jang Jeong-hoon said the annual shareholder return ratio could reach a maximum of about 53%. The company stressed that this is not a firm target. The final ratio will be set after the annual closing in early February 2027.

For Lotte Non-Life Insurance, the group will acquire the remaining 22.96% after regulatory approval and then merge it with Shinhan EZ General Insurance. It plans to inject more capital ahead of the core-capital K-ICS solvency rule that takes effect in 2027. In its 2024 value-up plan, the group set a target of cutting its share count by 50 million by 2027.

08

Bull factors

Non-bank units broaden the earnings base

Non-bank earnings rose 38.3% year on year in the first half of 2026, reducing reliance on bank interest income. Net profit at the securities subsidiary was up 91.6% year on year in 2Q. If stock-market turnover holds up, fee income can keep supporting profit.

The fact that the last four quarters' profit already exceeds the full-year 2025 figure also shows the earnings base has grown.

Formula-based shareholder returns

The group sets its payout ratio with a formula linked to the growth of risk-weighted assets. The shareholder return ratio topped 50% for the first time in 2025, and KRW 1.4 trillion of buybacks will be completed by October 2026.

It has also set a goal of cutting 50 million shares by 2027 and a plan to raise the dividend per share by about 10% a year.

NIM recovery and filling the non-life gap

NH Investment & Securities expects the group NIM to rise from 1.93% in 2026 to 1.98% in 2027. Lotte Non-Life Insurance has 3.34 million customers and about KRW 2.2 trillion of contractual service margin (CSM) as of mid-2026.

The deal gives the group a base for narrowing its insurance earnings gap with KB Financial Group. LS Securities said the price looks reasonable and expects group synergies to lift profitability over the medium to long term.

09

Bear factors

Capital strain from the acquisition

Hana Securities estimates the KRW 693.3 billion purchase alone cuts the CET1 ratio by 19bp, rising to 33bp with the remaining stake and a KRW 300 billion capital injection. The market worries that more capital may be needed than the company expects.

LS Securities noted that the group is unlikely to receive dividends from Lotte Non-Life Insurance for a long time, which weighs somewhat on cash flow.

Weaker 3Q margins and fees

NH Investment & Securities expects the 3Q group NIM to be 1.90%, 3bp lower than in 2Q. The same broker estimates non-interest income will fall 12.9% from the prior quarter as trading volumes decline. Hana Securities said 3Q earnings are held up mainly by one-off items, so near-term drivers look limited.

Recurring fourth-quarter costs

In both 2024 and 2025, early-retirement costs and provisions piled up in the fourth quarter and sharply cut quarterly profit. Even the record 2Q 2026 result included non-recurring items such as reversals of penalties and litigation provisions. It remains to be confirmed how much of the profit is recurring.

10

Risk factors

Credit risk

Base-rate hikes help interest income but raise repayment burdens for weaker borrowers, which can push up credit costs. The group booked pre-emptive real estate PF provisions in 4Q 2025, and the capital subsidiary also took PF-related credit costs. An economic slowdown could push the credit cost ratio back up.

Regulatory and policy risk

In 4Q 2025, LTV and ELT-related penalties and a policy fund contribution were booked as non-operating losses. Household lending rules cap loan growth. The core-capital K-ICS rule that takes effect in 2027 may require more capital for the merged non-life insurer.

Market risk

With securities and asset-management profit now a bigger share, quarterly results can swing sharply with market turnover and securities valuation gains or losses. A rise in the won-dollar exchange rate increases risk-weighted assets, which lowers the CET1 ratio and the room for shareholder returns. Hana Securities warned that if the market keeps falling, bank-stock multiples could drop as well.

What to watch next

  1. October 22, 2026

    The KRW 700 billion buyback window closes, completing the KRW 1.4 trillion annual program. Watch whether trading demand thins out before any new buyback is announced.

  2. October 27, 2026

    3Q results and a board meeting. Key items are the size of the NIM decline, recurring profit excluding the property sale gain, the end-September CET1 ratio, the size of any fourth-quarter buyback, and management's explanation of the deal's capital impact.

  3. From 4Q 2026 (approval timing not yet set)

    Regulatory approval to make Lotte Non-Life Insurance a subsidiary, and the method for acquiring the remaining 22.96% (tender offer or share swap). The key question is whether the extra capital injection exceeds Hana Securities' estimate.

  4. Early February 2027

    The final 2026 shareholder return ratio will be confirmed with full-year results. Watch whether seasonal fourth-quarter costs rise again and how close the ratio comes to the roughly 53% maximum management cited.

12

Overall view

Shinhan Financial Group's profit has recovered since its 2023 low, and the last four quarters now exceed the full-year 2025 figure. The growth has been driven by non-bank units such as securities and by non-interest income.

Still, the record 2Q 2026 result included one-off reversals, and in 3Q a property sale gain is expected to offset the lower NIM. A formula-based payout and a share-reduction target have made shareholder returns more predictable. At the same time, the Lotte Non-Life Insurance deal has used up part of the CET1 headroom.

The stock fell more than the bank index in September and October 2026, which suggests the market is pricing in the deal cost and possible further capital needs. The extra buyback size and capital-ratio guidance due on October 27, 2026 will be the first evidence of which of these two forces weighs more.
