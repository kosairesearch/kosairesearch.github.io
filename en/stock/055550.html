Report overview

Non-Bank Earnings Growth Meets Tighter Capital Rules

Record quarterly profits driven by surging non-bank earnings in securities and asset management are running up against tightening capital-floor rules and policy-finance obligations that will test shareholder-return capacity.

  1. 1

    Second-quarter 2026 net profit attributable to owners reached KRW 1.8201 trillion and operating profit KRW 2.4763 trillion, both record quarterly highs, up 17.5% and 22.9% year on year respectively.

  2. 2

    First-half non-bank earnings rose 38.3% to KRW 1.3277 trillion, with Shinhan Securities net profit surging 123.1% to KRW 577.7 billion, making it the largest non-bank contributor to group profit.

  3. 3

    The Value-Up 2.0 plan unveiled in April 2026 introduced an uncapped payout-ratio formula linking profitability targets to growth, applicable from 2026 through 2028.

  4. 4

    The group has guided to total shareholder returns of 'KRW 2.8 trillion plus alpha' for 2026, combining KRW 1.4 trillion of buybacks and cancellations with roughly KRW 1.4 trillion of cash dividends, with an additional fourth-quarter amount to be disclosed in late October.

  5. 5

    Shinhan Bank became the first of Korea's five major commercial banks to breach the regulatory capital floor in the second quarter of 2026, adding KRW 1.8076 trillion of risk-weighted assets, with the floor rising to 70% in 2027 and 72.5% in 2028.

02

Business structure

Shinhan Financial Group is a bank-centered holding company whose core subsidiary is Shinhan Bank, alongside Shinhan Card, Shinhan Securities, Shinhan Life, Shinhan Capital and Shinhan Asset Management, with earnings resting on two pillars: net interest income and non-interest income from fees, insurance and cards.

In the first half of 2026, group net interest income rose 7.7% year on year to KRW 6.1585 trillion, the group net interest margin held at 1.93% versus the prior quarter, and the bank-level margin edged up 1bp to 1.61%. Over the same period non-interest income reached a record KRW 2.6381 trillion, up 19.7% year on year.

First-half non-bank earnings climbed 38.3% to KRW 1.3277 trillion, driven by a sharp improvement at the securities arm and better asset-management profitability centered on exchange-traded funds.

Shinhan Securities net profit rose 123.1% to KRW 577.7 billion and Shinhan Asset Management rose 135.1% to KRW 53.6 billion, with the securities arm overtaking Shinhan Card and Shinhan Life as the largest non-bank contributor to group profit.

The 'insurance revenue' line in the income statement reflects insurance contract revenue at subsidiaries such as Shinhan Life, recorded at KRW 3.3643 trillion in 2025 and KRW 3.1166 trillion in 2024.

On the lending side, large-corporate loans grew 6.6% in the first half while small and medium enterprise loans rose 1.8% and household loans just 0.5%.

The competitive landscape is an oligopoly of four holding companies alongside KB Financial, Hana Financial and Woori Financial, and with differentiation in deposit-and-loan spreads increasingly narrow, the profit contribution of securities, insurance and card units has become the key variable separating the groups.

On the policy front, the group is executing a KRW 20 trillion productive-finance program for 2026, comprising KRW 2 trillion for the National Growth Fund, KRW 2 trillion of proprietary investment, KRW 13 trillion of credit support and KRW 3 trillion of inclusive finance.

04

Earnings analysis

On an annual basis, operating profit rose for three consecutive years, from KRW 5.9056 trillion in 2022 to KRW 6.1009 trillion in 2023, KRW 6.4587 trillion in 2024 and KRW 7.0234 trillion in 2025.

Net profit attributable to owners slipped from KRW 4.6656 trillion in 2022 to KRW 4.3680 trillion in 2023 before recovering to KRW 4.4502 trillion in 2024 and KRW 4.9716 trillion in 2025, an 11.7% gain in the latest year.

Operating cash flow has been volatile, falling to KRW 529.8 billion in 2023 before expanding to KRW 4.6263 trillion in 2024 and KRW 9.7309 trillion in 2025.

Quarterly, operating profit eased from KRW 2.0143 trillion with KRW 1.5491 trillion of owner net profit in the second quarter of 2025 to KRW 1.9546 trillion and KRW 1.4235 trillion in the third quarter, then dropped sharply to KRW 1.1103 trillion and KRW 510.6 billion in the fourth quarter of 2025.

That reflects the sector's usual fourth-quarter seasonality, when voluntary retirement costs and conservative provisioning tend to be concentrated, a factor worth bearing in mind when reading the annual trend.

Momentum then improved for two straight quarters, to KRW 2.1545 trillion of operating profit and KRW 1.6226 trillion of owner net profit in the first quarter of 2026 and KRW 2.4763 trillion and KRW 1.8201 trillion in the second quarter, and cumulative first-half 2026 net profit of KRW 3.4427 trillion marked record highs on both a quarterly and half-year basis.

Group credit-loss provisions fell 14.7% quarter on quarter to KRW 437.3 billion in the second quarter and 10.8% year on year to KRW 949.8 billion for the first half, easing the cost burden.

Insurance revenue rose steadily from KRW 823.6 billion in the second quarter of 2025 to KRW 916.4 billion in the second quarter of 2026, though its swing factor within group earnings is limited.

Summing the four most recent quarters, from the third quarter of 2025 through the second quarter of 2026, owner net profit totaled KRW 5.3769 trillion, above the full-year 2025 figure, while end-2025 equity attributable to owners stood at KRW 57.9593 trillion against liabilities of KRW 725.6412 trillion, reflecting the high balance-sheet leverage typical of a financial holding company.

05

Industry analysis

Korea's rate environment reversed direction in 2026. The Bank of Korea base rate was raised to 2.75% on July 16, 2026 and to 3.00% on August 27, 2026. That marked the first back-to-back hikes since early 2023.

A tightening cycle can support loan-deposit spreads, but higher funding costs and heavier borrower repayment burdens operate simultaneously, so the net effect depends on each bank's asset and liability structure.

On its first-half 2026 earnings call, the company flagged an expectation that the net interest margin would rise 3 to 4 basis points in the second half.

On regulation, the floor the Bank for International Settlements places under internally modeled risk-weighted assets rose from 60% in 2025 to 65% in 2026 and is set to climb to 70% in 2027.

Shinhan Bank recorded an adjustment of KRW 1.8076 trillion in the second quarter of 2026 under the capital floor, the first instance among Korea's five major commercial banks of the rule adding risk-weighted assets since it took effect in 2023. There have also been offsetting easing measures.

At the fifth productive-finance transition meeting in April 2026, the Financial Services Commission said it would relax capital rules for banks and insurers to unlock up to KRW 98.7 trillion of additional funding capacity, comprising KRW 74.5 trillion for banks and KRW 24.2 trillion for insurers.

With all four major holding companies being asked to expand shareholder returns and productive finance at the same time, capital-ratio management is emerging as a differentiator between groups.

06

Outlook

The medium-term capital policy framework the company has disclosed is relatively clear.

On April 23, 2026, Shinhan Financial announced 'Shinhan Value-Up 2.0,' introducing a payout-ratio formula that links profitability targets and growth rates on the basis of managing the common equity tier 1 ratio at an appropriate level. The plan applies from 2026 through 2028.

Management said it targets a CET1 ratio above 13% but will in practice run a 13.0% to 13.4% management band, deciding how to deploy excess capital above that range at year-end or early next year.

On dividends, the group will begin three years of tax-exempt dividends from the 2026 year-end payout, maintain level quarterly dividends, and target annual dividend-per-share growth of at least 10%.

For 2026 it is buying back and cancelling an additional KRW 700 billion of shares by October, bringing the cumulative total to KRW 1.4 trillion versus KRW 1.25 trillion for all of last year; adding roughly KRW 1.4 trillion of annual cash dividends, total shareholder returns are expected to exceed KRW 2.8 trillion, with any further fourth-quarter buyback to be decided based on earnings and capital adequacy.

On the business side, the company expects growth at the securities and asset-management units to moderate in the second half as market turnover declines, while fee income tied to financial product sales continues through the year.

The total payout ratio rose from 36.0% in 2023 to 50.2% in 2025, and the group won the top prize at the Korea IR Awards hosted by the Korea IR Council in September 2026.

The central question from the second half of 2026 onward narrows to whether the group can absorb both the durability test for non-bank earnings and the rising capital-floor burden while delivering the return plan it has laid out.

08

Bull factors

Structural Shift in Non-Bank Earnings

First-half 2026 non-bank earnings rose 38.3% year on year to KRW 1.3277 trillion. Shinhan Securities grew 123.1% to KRW 577.7 billion, overtaking Shinhan Card and Shinhan Life as the largest non-bank contributor, while Shinhan Asset Management rose 135.1%.

A mix shift that reduces reliance on bank net interest income can cushion earnings volatility when the rate cycle turns. That said, much of this growth is tied to equity market turnover and needs to be monitored separately.

A More Predictable Shareholder Return Framework

Under Value-Up 2.0, the previous 50% payout target was reset to an uncapped payout ratio linked to profitability targets and growth rates.

The total payout ratio rose from 36.0% in 2023 to 50.2% in 2025, and a further KRW 700 billion buyback approved in the third quarter of 2026 lifted cumulative annual buybacks to KRW 1.4 trillion. The group also committed to maintaining level quarterly dividends while raising dividend per share by at least 10% a year.

A formula-based policy improves visibility on return size, but the inputs to that formula, capital ratios and growth rates, remain exposed to regulatory change.

Lower Credit Costs Alongside Interest Income Growth

Group credit-loss provisions fell 14.7% quarter on quarter to KRW 437.3 billion in the second quarter of 2026 and 10.8% year on year to KRW 949.8 billion for the first half. Over the same period, group net interest income rose 7.7% to KRW 6.1585 trillion.

Falling costs combined with rising core revenue underpinned the record first-half result. Credit costs, however, can reverse quickly with the economic cycle, so the durability of this trend requires quarter-by-quarter verification.

09

Bear factors

Step-Up in the Regulatory Capital Floor

Shinhan Bank breached the capital floor for the first time in the second quarter of 2026, adding an adjustment of KRW 1.8076 trillion to risk-weighted assets. The floor rises to 70% in 2027, and analysis suggests that if current capital structures hold, all five major banks would fall below the threshold next year.

If risk-weighted assets grow faster than expected because of the rule, defending the CET1 ratio becomes harder and capacity for dividends or buybacks could shrink. Shinhan Bank has said it already has a capital management plan in place that incorporates the move to 70% next year.

Volatility in Capital-Market-Linked Earnings

A large share of the first-half 2026 earnings increase came from capital-market affiliates such as securities and asset management. The company noted that these subsidiaries doubled their profits year on year but that growth would moderate in the second half as market turnover declines.

Revenue sensitive to turnover and index levels can unwind quickly when direction reverses. Whether the rising non-bank share represents structural improvement or a cyclical effect will take several more quarters to distinguish.

Tension Between Policy Finance and Capital Allocation

The group committed to KRW 20 trillion of productive finance in 2026, comprising KRW 2 trillion for the National Growth Fund, KRW 2 trillion of proprietary investment, KRW 13 trillion of credit support and KRW 3 trillion of inclusive finance.

Observers note that pursuing both expanded productive finance at the government's request and stronger shareholder returns is increasing the capital management burden on financial groups.

As the capital floor rises, the incentive to favor low-risk-weight quality assets grows, which could reduce room to fund higher-risk-weight exposures such as innovative firms and smaller enterprises.

With two policy goals competing for the same capital, how the prioritization process feeds through to earnings is the key question.

10

Risk factors

Asset Quality and Credit Risk

Credit-loss provisions declined year on year in the first half of 2026, but if the tightening cycle continues, heavier borrower repayment burdens could push delinquency rates back up.

Analysts have argued that even with rate cuts, high debt levels and the pace of economic recovery mean the risk of rising delinquencies cannot be fully dismissed. Real estate project financing and lending to smaller enterprises and the self-employed are particularly cycle-sensitive. Quarterly credit cost ratios and the substandard-and-below loan ratio should be tracked together.

Regulatory and Policy Change

A credit rating agency noted that dividend burdens from expanded shareholder returns, won-translation increases in foreign-currency assets as the exchange rate rises, the higher risk-weighted asset floor under the Basel III endgame, and mortgage risk-weight floors are all exerting downward pressure on bank capital ratios.

It also cited ongoing institutional changes such as a higher education tax rate, a raised deposit protection limit, and amendments to supervisory rules on risk-weighted assets. Regulatory variables lie outside any single company's control and can reshape an entire capital plan at once.

Conversely, easing measures such as capital rule rationalization are proceeding in parallel, so judgment on the net effect should remain reserved.

Rate and Currency Volatility

The company's chief financial officer explained that buybacks had typically been executed on a half-year basis but are now being decided in three-month increments given heightened volatility in rates, exchange rates and share prices.

This shows that execution of capital policy itself is being shaped by market volatility. The group's BIS total capital ratio was reported to have fallen 0.46 percentage points, from 16.20% to 15.74%.

A rising exchange rate inflates the won-translated value of foreign-currency assets, directly affecting risk-weighted assets and capital ratios.

What to watch next

  1. Late October 2026

    The company said it would announce the size of its fourth-quarter buyback and cancellation in late October, taking market volatility into account. The additional return disclosed alongside third-quarter results will indicate how large the 'plus alpha' on the KRW 2.8 trillion annual target turns out to be.

  2. Third-quarter 2026 results, late October

    The durability of non-bank earnings is the key item. Since the company expects growth at its securities and asset-management units to slow in the second half on lower market turnover, actual fee income trends should be checked alongside the direction of the net interest margin.

  3. Bank of Korea rate decisions in the fourth quarter of 2026

    The base rate was raised to 2.75% in July 2026 and 3.00% in August 2026. Whether further hikes or a pause follow will shift the balance between funding costs and loan-deposit spreads, serving as the reference point for whether the second-half margin improvement the company flagged materializes.

  4. January to February 2027

    This is when full-year 2026 results are finalized and the year-end dividend is approved. Since the company said it would begin three years of tax-exempt dividends from the 2026 year-end payout, the actual implementation and the tax treatment applied should be verified.

  5. During 2027

    This is when the capital floor rises to 70%. Shinhan Bank has stated it already has a capital management plan reflecting this, so whether the CET1 ratio stays within the 13.0% to 13.4% management band the company set out will be the first gauge of return capacity.

12

Overall view

Shinhan Financial Group's first half of 2026 was, by the numbers, a clear period of improvement. Second-quarter net profit attributable to owners was KRW 1.8201 trillion and the first-half cumulative figure KRW 3.4427 trillion, both record highs on a quarterly and half-year basis.

Annual owner net profit also rose for two consecutive years, from KRW 4.3680 trillion in 2023 to KRW 4.4502 trillion in 2024 and KRW 4.9716 trillion in 2025, while operating profit increased for three straight years from 2022.

Yet the wide quarterly dispersion must be read alongside this, as shown by owner net profit falling to KRW 510.6 billion in the fourth quarter of 2025.

The positive factors are expanding non-bank earnings centered on securities and asset management, lower credit costs, and a formula-based shareholder return framework with improved visibility.

On the other side sit the capital floor rising to 70% in 2027 and 72.5% in 2028, capital allocation pressure from executing KRW 20 trillion of productive finance, and the possibility that capital-market earnings tied to turnover reverse.

The decisive checkpoints for how these forces balance are the late-October third-quarter results and the additional fourth-quarter return announcement, followed by the CET1 trajectory after the 2027 capital floor increase; this report is for informational purposes and contains no buy or sell recommendation.
