02

Business structure

Seoyon is a holding company that controls automotive parts subsidiaries. Its subsidiary Seoyon E-Hwa supplies interior products, seats and electrical components to Hyundai Motor and KIA, and Seoyon holds 48.70% of Seoyon E-Hwa.

Its stakes in Seoyon Topmetal and Seoyon Intech are 37.50% and 84.02%, respectively, as of end-December 2025 according to Wisereport. Product lines center on automotive interior and exterior parts, with molds and construction heavy-equipment parts also produced.

Production is spread widely overseas, with Seoyon E-Hwa operating units in India, China, Slovakia, Poland, Mexico and the United States, and a Seoyon E-Hwa Indiana entity was established in February 2026. A March 2026 news report put Seoyon E-Hwa's overseas revenue share at 74%.

Customers are mostly automakers, so results track production volumes and sales trends. The holding structure also changed, with subsidiary-consolidation disclosures issued on May 15 and August 14, 2026.

The holding company had 27 employees as of June 2026, so revenue and profit are generated mainly at the subsidiary level.

04

Earnings analysis

On a DART-filed basis, revenue rose for three consecutive years from 2023 to 2025. It went from KRW 4.02 trillion in 2023 (up 23.7% year on year) to KRW 4.5 trillion in 2024 (up 11.9%), then to KRW 4.92 trillion in 2025 (up 9.3%), the largest year in the 2022-2025 span.

Operating profit peaked at KRW 256.9 billion in 2023, then fell 5.8% to KRW 241.9 billion in 2024 and 17.5% to KRW 199.6 billion in 2025, a two-year decline. Operating margin was 5.8% in 2022, 6.4% in 2023, 5.4% in 2024 and 4.1% in 2025, the lowest of the four years, so revenue grew while margins compressed.

Net income attributable to controlling shareholders was KRW 134 billion in 2024, the largest year, and fell 58.3% to KRW 55.9 billion in 2025. Net income including non-controlling interests was KRW 93.8 billion in 2025, a different figure, with the gap reflecting the non-controlling share.

Operating cash flow was KRW 323 billion in 2024 and KRW 238.4 billion in 2025. The debt ratio rebounded to 128.2% in 2025 from 124.2% in 2024.

Quarterly operating profit fell from KRW 68.1 billion in Q3 2025 to KRW 15.2 billion in Q4 2025, a 77.7% drop from the prior quarter, then recovered to KRW 52.5 billion in Q1 2026 and KRW 67 billion in Q2 2026.

Q3 2026 operating profit was KRW 32.6 billion, down 51.3% from the prior quarter and 52.1% year on year, while revenue rose 8.9% year on year to KRW 1.29 trillion.

The Q3 2026 net loss attributable to controlling shareholders of KRW 14.4 billion marked a swing to loss from both the prior quarter and the year-earlier quarter.

05

Industry analysis

Seoyon's earnings depend on automaker production and sales as well as on the exchange rate. Wisereport tallied Seoyon E-Hwa's first-half 2026 consolidated revenue at up 7.8% year on year and operating profit at up 8.8%, as of September 2, 2026. Over the same period, Seoyon E-Hwa's net income rose 520.6%.

Reasons cited for the improvement include a recovery in domestic auto demand, sales gains tied to a temporary cut in the special consumption tax and replacement support for older vehicles, and new customers including Mercedes-Benz, Ford and Volkswagen.

On the other side, a strong dollar has persisted amid higher Middle East tensions and US trade protection policies, which weighs on Seoyon E-Hwa given its high overseas revenue share. A March 2026 news report said net income fell sharply in a period of rising exchange rates.

Seoyon's sustainability report cites growing demand for electric and other eco-friendly vehicles as an opportunity for expanded orders for lightweight interior and exterior parts. Other holding companies in the sector include SK Square, Hanjinkal and LG.

06

Outlook

The company's identifiable plans center on expanding overseas production. Reports said Seoyon E-Hwa's new plants in Savannah, Georgia and Texas in the United States have entered full operation, which increases its dollar exposure.

A Seoyon E-Hwa Indiana entity established in February 2026 also broadens the North American production base. Subsidiary-consolidation disclosures came twice in 2026, on May 15 and on August 14, the latter covering Seoyon CNF's inclusion as a subsidiary.

For the holding company, changes in subsidiary stakes alter the scope of consolidated results. Seoyon E-Hwa has designated 2026 as the first year of its AI and DX push and plans to embed data-driven decision-making across operations.

Its medium-term targets use 2019 as the base year, aiming for a 10% cut in emissions by 2030 and carbon neutrality by 2050. The holding company's next periodic report is the 2026 annual report, which will confirm fourth-quarter and full-year 2026 results on a DART-filed basis.

The next checkpoints are how far annual operating margin recovers from 4.1% in 2025 and how quickly the new US plants feed into revenue.

Bull factors

Subsidiary sales recovering

Seoyon E-Hwa's first-half 2026 consolidated revenue rose 7.8% year on year and operating profit rose 8.8%. The recovery in domestic auto demand, the consumption tax cut and replacement support for older vehicles are cited as drivers of automaker sales.

Wisereport also points to new customers including Mercedes-Benz, Ford and Volkswagen. For the holding company, operating momentum at its key subsidiary is moving in a positive direction.

Q1 2026 return to profit and record quarterly revenue

Net income attributable to controlling shareholders returned to KRW 29.9 billion in Q1 2026 after a net loss in Q4 2025. Operating profit rose 27.6% from the prior quarter to KRW 67 billion in Q2 2026. Q2 2026 revenue of KRW 1.36 trillion was the largest of the last five quarters. On a quarterly basis, the profit recovery shows up in this stretch.

Expanding North American production

Reports say Seoyon E-Hwa's new plants in Savannah, Georgia and Texas have entered full operation. A Seoyon E-Hwa Indiana entity was established in February 2026, widening the North American production base.

The company cites rising demand for electric vehicles as a source of orders for lightweight interior and exterior parts. Whether this base translates into revenue will show up in upcoming quarterly results.

Bear factors

Operating profit down two years running

Operating profit peaked at KRW 256.9 billion in 2023 and then declined to KRW 241.9 billion in 2024 and KRW 199.6 billion in 2025, a two-year fall. Operating margin was 4.1% in 2025, the lowest of 2022-2025. Revenue rose for three years, but profitability did not follow the same path. The bearish point is that margins have not kept pace with revenue growth.

Q3 2026 swing to loss

Net loss attributable to controlling shareholders was KRW 14.4 billion in Q3 2026, a swing to loss. Operating profit fell 52.1% year on year to KRW 32.6 billion while revenue rose 8.9%. Net income attributable to controlling shareholders totaled only KRW 36.4 billion over the last four quarters. The core of the bearish view is that revenue growth has not translated into profit growth.

FX and financial income pressure

Reports say that a period of rising exchange rates reduced net income through non-operating items, given the high overseas revenue mix. The debt ratio rose to 128.2% at end-2025 from 124.2% in 2024. Exchange-rate and financing-cost swings remain a burden on holding-company net income.

10

Risk factors

Exchange rates and financial income

High overseas revenue exposes results to exchange-rate moves, which can flow through to non-operating income. As a strong dollar persists and new US plants ramp up, dollar exposure is growing. This adds volatility to holding-company net income. If the exchange-rate trend does not reverse, the financial-income burden could persist.

Dependence on automaker production

Subsidiary Seoyon E-Hwa supplies interior products, seats and electrical components to Hyundai Motor and KIA. Production plans and sales softness at these two customers directly affect subsidiary revenue and profit. New customers including Mercedes-Benz, Ford and Volkswagen are diversifying the customer mix. How quickly that diversification shows up in reported results remains to be seen.

Labor, quality and holding structure

The company's sustainability report identifies strikes arising from labor disputes and customer demands for minimal defect rates as key risks. A strike or quality failure could halt automaker production lines and trigger penalties for late delivery.

The holding company's consolidated scope changes with subsidiary stake changes, and subsidiary-consolidation disclosures came twice in 2026.

Overall view

Seoyon is a holding company that presents the results of its automotive parts subsidiaries on a consolidated basis. Revenue rose for three straight years to KRW 4.92 trillion in 2025, but operating profit fell for two years, pushing operating margin down to 4.1%.

Net income attributable to controlling shareholders dropped from KRW 134 billion in 2024 to KRW 55.9 billion in 2025 and turned into a KRW 14.4 billion net loss in Q3 2026. Subsidiary Seoyon E-Hwa grew revenue and operating profit in the first half of 2026, and its new US plants are ramping up.

Bullish factors are domestic demand recovery, new customers and a wider overseas production base, while bearish factors are the lower operating margin, the quarterly swing to loss and exchange-driven swings in financial income.

Both sets of factors coexist, and the DART-filed figures alone do not settle which side dominates. The next checkpoints are the operating margin and controlling-shareholder net income trends in the 2026 annual report and the Q1 2027 quarterly report.
