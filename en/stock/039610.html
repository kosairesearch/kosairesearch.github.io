02

Business structure

HS Valve manufactures industrial valves for gas and piping systems, with its headquarters in Daegu and plants in Gyeongsan and Gimhae. Flanged valves are the core product line, followed by threaded valves, and the company also produces LPG cylinder valves and buried welded-type valves.

Distribution runs mainly through about 350 dealers across Korea and 33 city gas piping contractors. Wisereport's company summary, dated September 7, 2026, states that HS Valve keeps the number one domestic market share in its core product lines.

Korea's valve market is estimated at roughly KRW 8 trillion, and more than half of it is supplied by imports from Germany and Japan, according to a Today Energy report from September 2026.

The company invests at least 3% of revenue in R&D to compete with imported valves and has completed development of a hydrogen piping ball valve. It is also localizing cryogenic valves rated below minus 162 degrees Celsius, a project aimed at large LNG infrastructure orders.

On the customer side, the company's corporate information page lists qualified supplier registration with five major Korean power generation companies in December 2025 and supplier registration with S-OIL in September 2025.

Overseas, it operates a US subsidiary and a China joint venture, and plans to expand into Europe and the Middle East on CE certification and to widen its large-diameter plant valve range. Its competitive position is the domestic number one share, contested by imported products on technology.

04

Earnings analysis

On a disclosed annual basis, revenue rose from KRW 85.5 billion in 2022 to KRW 91.6 billion in 2023, up 7.0% year on year, making 2023 the largest year of 2022 to 2025. It then slipped to KRW 91.3 billion in 2024, down 0.3%, and to KRW 81.4 billion in 2025, down 10.8%.

Operating profit climbed from KRW 3.7 billion in 2022 to KRW 10 billion in 2023, up 170.9%, then fell to KRW 7.96 billion in 2024, down 20.6%, and in 2025 turned into an operating loss of KRW 1.65 billion. The operating margin moved from 11.0% in 2023 to 8.7% in 2024 and -2.0% in 2025.

Profit attributable to controlling shareholders dropped from KRW 8.07 billion in 2023 to KRW 7.1 billion in 2024, down 11.9%, and in 2025 became a net loss of KRW 1.97 billion. Net income including non-controlling interests showed the same 2025 net loss of KRW 1.97 billion.

The company attributed the 2025 swing mainly to a higher cost of sales ratio from rising raw material prices, and also cited inventory valuation losses and higher provisions tied to inventory efficiency.

It also said a decline in the value of a subsidiary's equity was booked as an impairment-type valuation loss, which reduced net income.

On a quarterly basis, Q3 2025 revenue was KRW 18.3 billion with an operating loss of KRW 823 million, and Q4 2025 revenue rose 23.3% quarter on quarter to KRW 22.5 billion while the operating loss widened to KRW 2.21 billion.

Q1 2026 revenue was KRW 20 billion, down 11.2% quarter on quarter, with operating profit of KRW 794 million, a swing back to profit. Q2 2026 revenue rose 27.9% to KRW 25.6 billion, with operating profit of KRW 759 million and profit attributable to controlling shareholders of KRW 2.5 billion.

Q3 2026 revenue fell 24.0% quarter on quarter to KRW 19.5 billion, up 6.4% year on year, with operating profit of KRW 261 million and a net loss attributable to controlling shareholders of KRW 997 million.

Over the last four quarters, the cumulative net loss attributable to controlling shareholders totals KRW 279 million. The Q2 2026 profit narrowed the earlier losses, but the Q3 loss kept the four-quarter total in the red.

The company said it recognized inventory valuation losses early, so whether this cost recurs will be checked in the next periodic report.

Industry analysis

Valve demand links to capital spending across city gas piping, LPG, petrochemicals, LNG and offshore plants, and shipbuilding. A November 11, 2026 report by Pinpoint News said valve-related stocks strengthened on hopes of a shipbuilding upturn and wider global energy plant investment.

The same report pointed to expectations of rising energy infrastructure spending and replacement demand for aging piping as the drivers of buying interest.

An industry representative said fittings and valves that work reliably under high pressure and temperature are becoming more important, and that companies with order backlogs, high value-added supply capability and real operating profit improvement deserve attention.

The cycle position can be read from whether the order flow actually converts into orders and revenue. The same report noted that raw material prices, exchange rates, project delivery schedules and profitability create differences across companies, and the 2025 cost of sales increase shows that effect.

According to Today Energy, Trump's January 2026 remarks on starting an Alaska natural gas pipeline project and his April 2026 comments on supporting Iran's reconstruction renewed attention on HS Valve.

Participation in these projects remains at the stage of analyst expectation, and contract size and timing have not been confirmed through company disclosures.

Competitively, the company has a position as a domestic producer in a market where imports account for a large share, but technology and price competition with large overseas valve makers continues.
