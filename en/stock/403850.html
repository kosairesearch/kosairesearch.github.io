Overseas IP expansion meets slowing core earnings

02

Business structure

The Pinkfong Company builds video and music content around youth character IP such as Pinkfong, Baby Shark, Hogi, Seal Look and Bebefin, and extends it into licensing and merchandise.

In the first half of 2026, content accounted for about 63% of consolidated revenue of KRW 42.36 billion, according to financial regulator filings as reported in the press. Merchandise and product sales rose 14.6% to KRW 7.52 billion in the same period, while licensing revenue rose 3.3% to KRW 4.74 billion.

By region, overseas sales fell 13.1% to KRW 29.69 billion and domestic sales rose 15.0% to KRW 12.67 billion. The company says Bebefin's cumulative YouTube views reached 68 billion and its subscribers 85 million.

Analysts note that the core young-child audience has limited purchasing power, so views do not translate directly into merchandise sales (reported by Pinpoint News).

In Japan, Bebefin's music toy and piano-keyboard listings ranked first on Amazon Japan, and the Alphabet Bus from Baby Shark ranked first in English learning toys on Rakuten. In September 2026 the company released the Bebefin theatrical film with KADOKAWA in more than 120 cinemas across Japan.

Before the listing, securities research houses cited SAMG Entertainment, KADOKAWA, Sanrio and Toei Animation as valuation peers. As of the listing, KT was the largest shareholder with a 7.9% stake.

04

Earnings analysis

Annual results moved in different directions for revenue and profit.

Revenue fell from KRW 117 billion in 2022 to KRW 94.6 billion in 2023 (down 19.1% YoY), rose to KRW 97.4 billion in 2024 (up 2.9% YoY), and then declined to KRW 93.9 billion in 2025 (down 3.6% YoY). 2022 was the largest year in the period and 2025 the smallest.

Operating profit moved from KRW 3.7 billion in 2022 to an operating loss of KRW 3.22 billion in 2023, a swing from profit to loss, then to KRW 18.8 billion in 2024, a return to profit, and KRW 19.4 billion in 2025 (up 2.9% YoY). The operating margin was 3.2% in 2022, -3.4% in 2023, 19.3% in 2024 and 20.6% in 2025.

Profit recovered in 2025 even as revenue declined, so the improvement came from profitability rather than top-line growth.

Net income attributable to controlling shareholders was a loss of KRW 62.17 million in 2022, a loss of KRW 16.3 billion in 2023 (continued losses with a larger deficit), a profit of KRW 7.94 billion in 2024 (a swing to profit), and KRW 18.2 billion in 2025 (up 128.8% YoY).

Net income including non-controlling interests was a loss of KRW 244 million in 2022, a loss of KRW 18.3 billion in 2023, a profit of KRW 4.99 billion in 2024 and KRW 18.1 billion in 2025, so the two measures differ.

At the end of 2025, total equity was KRW 184.2 billion, equity attributable to controlling shareholders was KRW 184.1 billion and total liabilities were KRW 19.7 billion, with the debt-to-equity ratio falling from 69.2% in 2022 to 10.7% in 2025.

Operating cash flow went from -KRW 14.7 billion in 2022 to KRW 16.5 billion in 2023, KRW 27.6 billion in 2024 and KRW 20.9 billion in 2025. On a quarterly basis, 4Q 2025 was the peak with revenue of KRW 25.7 billion (up 12.0% QoQ) and operating profit of KRW 6.72 billion.

In 1Q 2026 revenue fell to KRW 20.7 billion (down 19.7% QoQ) and operating profit to KRW 2.94 billion (down 56.3% QoQ). In 2Q 2026 revenue recovered to KRW 21.7 billion (up 5.1% QoQ), but operating profit was KRW 2.66 billion (down 9.5% QoQ).

In 3Q 2026 revenue was KRW 21.8 billion (down 4.9% YoY) and operating profit KRW 1.94 billion (down 47.3% YoY). The same quarter's net loss attributable to controlling shareholders of KRW 8.71 billion marked a swing to loss.

In the first half of 2026, selling and administrative expenses were KRW 25.27 billion, flat with the prior year, which the press linked to weaker profitability (based on financial regulator data).

First-half net income of KRW 27.69 billion was driven by financial asset disposal and valuation gains of about KRW 24.4 billion pre-tax, so it should be read apart from core operating profit.

05

Industry analysis

The kids IP business is highly sensitive to how quickly new characters can replace aging franchises. Consumer tastes in content shift fast and competing content keeps emerging, so analysts point to whether new IP can generate repeat revenue as the key test for medium-term growth (reported by Business Report).

On platforms, the company said it ranked first in the kids category on Netflix in 12 countries including the United States, and that it has confirmed an original animated series for Amazon Kids Plus (press reports of company announcements from November 2026).

Offline, the company said cumulative visitors to its 2025 shopping mall tour in Indonesia reached 5 million (reported in January 2026). In Japan, a top e-commerce toy ranking together with pop-up and hotel expansion is widening the routes to commercialize its IP.

The conversion of content popularity into merchandise sales remains a variable, because the young-child audience has limited purchasing power.

Peers that securities firms cited before the listing, SAMG Entertainment, KADOKAWA, Sanrio and Toei Animation, all hold animation and character IP, and the company differs from them in operating profit scale and in how it monetizes its IP.

06

Outlook

The company has outlined three lines of business plans.

First, in Japan it plans to open a Baby Shark themed hotel room in November 2026 and to hold pop-up stores at Marui Shibuya Modi in Tokyo on December 4, 2026 and at Marui Namba in Osaka on December 11, featuring more than 50 new Bebefin products (based on press reports).

Second, on the content side, it said in July 2026 that it will expand a generative AI interactive exhibition titled Baby Shark Secret Invitation: Becoming Shark, along with theatrical films, a new spin-off series and location-based entertainment (LBE).

Third, it plans to release the animated series Momomo Ninimo, produced with TV Asahi, in January 2027.

On earnings expectations, the company projected 2026 operating profit of KRW 28.1 billion at the time of its listing, while a recent Korea IR Council report puts 2026 operating profit at KRW 12 billion, about 42.7% of the listing-stage figure.

Quarterly operating profit of KRW 2.94 billion, KRW 2.66 billion and KRW 1.94 billion in the first three quarters of 2026 indicates that the gap has not yet narrowed.

As of November 10, 2026, KRW 18.6 billion earmarked for external IP investment had not been spent, and the company said it is reviewing such deals carefully in light of growth potential and long-term synergies.

On shareholder returns, the company entered into a KRW 5 billion share buyback trust agreement in July 2026 and resolved in October to cancel 434,362 shares including previously held treasury stock.

The company said it will hold an investor relations event within 2026 to share business results and medium-term strategy, with the schedule to be disclosed later.

Risk factors

IP concentration and new IP performance

Revenue depends heavily on established IP such as Baby Shark, so a failure of newer IP like Bebefin or Seal Look to sustain hits could leave gaps in earnings. The first-half 2026 content decline was driven by the base effect of a major 2025 theatrical release, showing how hit cycles amplify swings. The performance of the new animated series can be assessed after its January 2027 release.

Volatility from financial asset gains and losses

First-half 2026 net income was heavily affected by financial asset disposal and valuation gains, and in 3Q 2026 the company posted a net loss attributable to controlling shareholders despite positive operating profit.

When core operations and financial gains move in different directions, net income trends are hard to read as core business performance. Valuation swings in financial assets can affect later quarters as well.

Operating burden from overseas expansion

Expanding into Japanese hotels, pop-up stores, theatrical releases and location-based entertainment could raise operating costs and partnership burdens. Overseas sales fell 13.1% in the first half of 2026, while domestic sales rose 15.0%, so regional trends diverged. Whether overseas expansion restores overseas sales will show up in upcoming results.

Overall view

The Pinkfong Company's full-year 2025 results showed revenue of KRW 93.9 billion (down 3.6% YoY) and operating profit of KRW 19.4 billion (up 2.9% YoY), so the top line shrank while the operating margin rose to 20.6%.

In 2026, however, quarterly operating profit fell to KRW 2.94 billion in 1Q, KRW 2.66 billion in 2Q and KRW 1.94 billion in 3Q, three consecutive declines, and 3Q 2026 swung to a net loss attributable to controlling shareholders of KRW 8.71 billion.

Financial asset gains weighed heavily on the KRW 21.5 billion profit in 2Q 2026 and on first-half net income. Japan news flow, including a top e-commerce toy ranking, the Bebefin theatrical release and hotel and pop-up expansion, has continued, but content revenue declined over the same period.

The Korea IR Council's 2026 operating profit forecast of KRW 12 billion is about 42.7% of the KRW 28.1 billion the company projected at its listing. On the strength side are the margin recovery and a balance sheet with KRW 184.2 billion in total equity and a 10.7% debt-to-equity ratio.

On the weakness side are consecutive declines in core profit and uncertainty over new IP performance.

The next checkpoints are the response to the December 2026 Japan pop-ups, the medium-term strategy to be presented at the investor relations event within 2026, the January 2027 animated series broadcast, and the operating margin and financial gains share in the 2026 annual report.
