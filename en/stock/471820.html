Business structure

Cellromax Science was founded in October 2014 and, according to its company profile, supplies health functional foods, cosmetics and over-the-counter drugs whose formulations are designed by licensed pharmacists.

Its business profile lists the industry as health functional food manufacturing, and the company listed on KOSDAQ on December 13, 2024. Revenue scale is best gauged from the reported total revenue trend.

Media reports from April 2026 said the company had launched two Doraji (platycodon) products for spring bronchial care.

Its company history includes a January 2023 patent for a composition for preventing or treating osteoporosis in women based on Angelica, Peony and Saffron extracts, and a July 2022 patent for an immune-boosting composition based on a plant powder.

The company emphasizes a network of member pharmacies as its sales channel, so demand shifts in that channel can feed quickly into revenue. In September 2025 it acquired a 17.17% equity stake in a Korean bio company.

In April and May 2026 it set up subsidiaries in Beijing and Asia, widening its overseas consolidated group. The product line centers on health functional foods but also spans cosmetics and OTC drugs. Its core customers appear to be individual consumers and member pharmacies.

Earnings analysis

On a reported basis, revenue rose from KRW 19.3 billion in 2023 to KRW 21.6 billion in 2024 (up 12.0% year on year) and reached KRW 22.2 billion in 2025 (up 2.6%).

Revenue grew in both 2024 and 2025, two consecutive years of growth on the reported figures, and 2025 was the largest year of the 2023 to 2025 span while 2023 was the smallest.

Operating profit went from KRW 4.62 billion in 2023 to KRW 5.5 billion in 2024 (up 19.1%) and then to KRW 2.84 billion in 2025 (down 48.5%), with 2024 the largest year and 2025 the smallest. The operating margin moved from 23.9% to 25.4% and then to 12.8%.

Because revenue still rose in 2025 while the margin fell sharply, the pressure appears to have come from costs rather than from sales slowing alone, and the line-item drivers belong in the full-year business report.

Profit attributable to owners of the parent fell from KRW 4.5 billion in 2023 to KRW 3.82 billion in 2024 (down 15.2%), and net profit including non-controlling interests was reported at the same two amounts for those years.

Operating cash flow declined from KRW 5.07 billion in 2023 and KRW 6.29 billion in 2024 to KRW 1.79 billion in 2025, moving in the same direction as operating profit. Quarterly operating profit bottomed at KRW 86.39 million in the fourth quarter of 2025, down 88.3% from KRW 737 million in the third quarter of 2025.

It then recovered to KRW 348 million in the first quarter of 2026 (up 303.3%) and KRW 981 million in the second quarter (up 181.6%). The first-quarter figure remained below the 2025 third-quarter level, while the second quarter exceeded it.

Third-quarter 2026 revenue of KRW 4.93 billion fell 3.0% quarter on quarter and 7.5% year on year, yet operating profit of KRW 826 million rose 12.1% year on year, so profit grew even as sales declined.

05

Industry analysis

Health functional foods, cosmetics and OTC drugs, the company's core categories, each operate under different regulatory frameworks.

In health functional foods, functional ingredients and labeling rules shape product competitiveness, while OTC drugs fall under pharmaceutical affairs regulation that tightly controls sales channels.

As a result, even a company with ingredient patents and a pharmacy network may see its pace of product expansion shaped by the regulatory environment.

Product lines are organized around function, such as bronchial care, immunity and bone health, so competition tends to turn on ingredient differentiation and distribution reach.

The exchange classifies the company under biopharma and pharmaceuticals, but its own profile centers on health functional food manufacturing, so its revenue structure and earnings model differ from those of listed drug developers.

At KRW 22.2 billion of 2025 revenue, the company is small next to large listed biopharma names, and its profit depends heavily on product margins. The Beijing and Asia subsidiaries can be read as an attempt to take a domestic channel-led business overseas.

Industry cycle data and market-size figures are not reflected here, so the industry read is best framed around how the company's reported results line up with regulatory and channel conditions.
