Report overview

Samwha Capacitor: MLCC Recovery Versus Earnings Base

Samwha Capacitor's MLCC share gain has returned quarterly operating profit to positive territory since the first quarter of 2026, yet full-year 2025 operating profit of KRW 12.9 billion stayed below half of its 2022 level.

  2. 2

  3. 3

  4. 4

  5. 5

02

Business structure

Samwha Capacitor was founded in 1956 and is positioned as a comprehensive capacitor maker that, in Korea, produces film, ceramic and MLCC lines but not electrolytic capacitors.

In the first quarter of 2026, according to a domestic brokerage research note, MLCCs accounted for about 53% of revenue, DC-link capacitors 20%, film capacitors 11%, DCC 8% and other products 8%.

MLCC revenue grew 24.2% year on year, and the same note said this growth offset lower sales of film capacitors and home-appliance capacitors. DC-link capacitors are used in power conversion equipment such as EV inverters and accounted for roughly one-fifth of revenue in that quarter.

Film capacitors improve power factor and reduce current losses, and they are supplied to Korea Electric Power, EPC contractors and substations. A February 2026 domestic brokerage report put automotive-grade MLCCs at close to the high-30% range of MLCC revenue.

Customers span power utilities such as Korea Electric Power, EPC contractors, EV and secondary-battery makers, and global automakers. The company operates sales subsidiaries in five countries including the United States, Hong Kong and Europe, and production plants in China, Thailand and Indonesia.

A 2025 analysis cited exports at about 60% of total revenue. Samwha Electric, a separate listed company in the same group, focuses on aluminum electrolytic capacitors and has a different business structure.

04

Earnings analysis

Annual revenue rose from KRW 263.5 billion in 2022 to KRW 295.4 billion in 2024, the largest year in the 2022 to 2025 span, before edging down 0.3% to KRW 294.5 billion in 2025.

Operating profit fell from KRW 30.5 billion in 2022 to KRW 23.7 billion in 2023 (down 22.4%), KRW 17.8 billion in 2024 (down 24.7%) and KRW 12.9 billion in 2025 (down 27.9%), marking three consecutive years of decline from 2023.

Over the same period the operating margin slid from 11.6% to 8.4%, 6.0% and 4.4%, showing thinner profitability while revenue stayed at roughly the same level in 2024 and 2025. Commentary on the 2025 decline pointed to weaker demand in IT sets, higher labor costs and higher raw-material costs.

Net profit attributable to controlling shareholders went from KRW 29.1 billion in 2022 to KRW 20.8 billion in 2023 (down 28.6%), recovered to KRW 21.8 billion in 2024 (up 4.9%) and then fell to KRW 12.5 billion in 2025 (down 42.6%).

In 2024 that profit rose even though operating profit fell, which suggests non-operating items cushioned the result.

Net profit including non-controlling interests, at KRW 12.6 billion in 2025, is a separate figure from controlling-shareholder net profit, and year-end 2025 total equity of KRW 279.6 billion included KRW 277.6 billion attributable to controlling shareholders, with total liabilities of KRW 60.6 billion.

The debt ratio declined from 27.5% in 2022 to 21.7% in 2025, while operating cash flow fell from KRW 48.5 billion to KRW 25.9 billion over the same span.

Quarterly results ran as follows: operating profit of KRW 2.66 billion in the third quarter of 2025, an operating loss of KRW 174 million in the fourth quarter, a return to operating profit of KRW 4.7 billion in the first quarter of 2026, KRW 8.64 billion in the second quarter (up 83.9% from the prior quarter) and KRW 6.69 billion in the third quarter (down 22.6% from the prior quarter).

Third-quarter revenue of KRW 81.4 billion was 1.7% lower than the prior quarter but 12.3% higher year on year, and controlling-shareholder net profit turned to a loss of KRW 303 million, a year-on-year swing into deficit. Controlling-shareholder net profit for the last four quarters totals KRW 14.8 billion.

05

Industry analysis

MLCCs are core passive components used in smartphones, servers and automotive electronics. The company highlights that, apart from electrolytic capacitors, it produces the full range of film, ceramic and MLCC products in Korea.

A February 2026 domestic brokerage report said surging demand for AI-server MLCCs was creating supply gaps across the MLCC market.

The same report explained that Samsung Electro-Mechanics and others were expanding output of high-margin MLCCs for AI servers and automotive use, which was shrinking distributors' inventories of general-purpose MLCCs.

A May 2026 brokerage review noted that leading MLCC makers had announced price increases because demand exceeded their supply capacity by more than two times. The same review expected increases of 5% to 10% from the second quarter and projected a spillover to Samwha Capacitor's MLCC prices.

It also presented the ongoing data-center investment cycle as one pillar of MLCC demand. Some analysts see film-capacitor demand continuing to benefit from rising power consumption tied to AI adoption.

On competition, Samsung Electro-Mechanics is a large MLCC maker expanding its high-margin AI-server and automotive lines, so changes in its production allocation act as a competitive variable.

Samwha Electric relies mainly on electrolytic capacitors for AI data-center power supplies, so its direct overlap with Samwha Capacitor is limited. Raw-material prices and labor costs remain the variables that determine how much of any price increase reaches profit.

06

Outlook

The company invested KRW 14.5 billion in 2025 in MLCC capacity expansion and productivity improvements, and the construction was reported to be in its final stage. Brokerage analysis expected the expanded capacity to affect earnings from the second half of 2026.

On pricing, the May 2026 review projected increases of 5% to 10% from the second quarter at leading MLCC makers. A February 2026 domestic brokerage report had projected full-year 2026 consolidated revenue of KRW 323.0 billion and operating profit of KRW 20.3 billion.

The same report said the rapid electrification of vehicles for driver-convenience features was expected to lift automotive MLCC sales. Film-capacitor demand is seen continuing as rising power consumption linked to AI adoption lifts requirements.

Analysts also note that new capacity only becomes profit once yields and utilization stabilize. Inventory levels in downstream industries and product prices are cited as factors that determine quarterly profitability.

The 2026 annual report, the next periodic filing, will show the full-year operating margin and the MLCC share of revenue.

08

Bull factors

Rising MLCC share and announced price increases

MLCC revenue rose 24.2% year on year in the first quarter of 2026, lifting its share of revenue to about 53%. A domestic brokerage research note argued that price increases announced by leading MLCC makers could spill over to Samwha Capacitor's MLCC prices. Operating profit in the second quarter reached KRW 8.64 billion, up 83.9% from the prior quarter.

Yongin expansion and automotive demand

The Yongin MLCC expansion, funded with KRW 14.5 billion in 2025, was reported to be near completion, with the added capacity expected to affect earnings from the second half of 2026. A February 2026 domestic brokerage report noted that automotive-grade MLCCs approach the high-30% range of MLCC revenue. Analysts link the growth of that share to the progress of vehicle electrification.

Balance-sheet capacity and a low debt ratio

The debt ratio at the end of 2025 was 21.7%, down from 27.5% in 2022, and total liabilities were KRW 60.6 billion. Of total equity of KRW 279.6 billion, KRW 277.6 billion is attributable to controlling shareholders. Operating cash flow fell to KRW 25.9 billion in 2025, but the overall debt load remains small.

Bear factors

Third-quarter earnings setback

Third-quarter 2026 revenue of KRW 81.4 billion was 1.7% lower than the prior quarter, and operating profit of KRW 6.69 billion fell 22.6% from the prior quarter. Controlling-shareholder net profit swung to a loss of KRW 303 million, a year-on-year turn to deficit. The reversal of the quarterly profit run within a single quarter is cited as a weakness.

Long-run profit erosion

Operating profit declined from KRW 30.5 billion in 2022 to KRW 12.9 billion in 2025, and the operating margin fell from 11.6% to 4.4% over the same period. Controlling-shareholder net profit fell 42.6% to KRW 12.5 billion in 2025. Weaker IT-set demand and higher labor and raw-material costs were cited as the background to the 2025 decline.

Multiple burden and reliance on expectations

The share price sits at a high multiple of the KRW 14.8 billion of controlling-shareholder net profit over the last four quarters, and the premium to book value is large.

The MLCC price increases remain at the stage of announcements and forecasts, while the expansion benefit depends on yields and utilization of the new capacity stabilizing.

10

Risk factors

Cost and demand cycle

Higher raw-material and labor costs were cited as causes of the 2025 operating-profit decline. If IT-set demand weakens again, MLCC shipments and utilization could fall together. If price increases fail to offset cost increases, margin recovery will be limited.

Expansion execution

Capacity added at Yongin turns into profit only once yields and utilization of the new equipment stabilize. If output grows faster than demand, price pressure could emerge. The lag between investment and returns is also a source of quarterly volatility.

Customer and competitive concentration

Because growth leans on AI-server and automotive MLCC demand, swings in those segments could feed directly into results. As Samsung Electro-Mechanics expands its high-margin AI-server and automotive MLCC output, competitive intensity is likely to rise.

Production sites in China, Thailand and Indonesia also expose the company to regional cost and trade-environment changes.

12

Overall view

Samwha Capacitor's reported results show two trends overlapping.

Operating profit fell from KRW 30.5 billion in 2022 to KRW 12.9 billion in 2025 and the operating margin dropped to 4.4%, yet quarterly operating profit stayed positive through 2026, reaching KRW 4.7 billion in the first quarter and KRW 8.64 billion in the second.

Brokerage commentary links the second-quarter improvement to the rising MLCC share and demand from AI servers and automotive applications, and also cites potential MLCC price increases and the effect of the Yongin expansion.

In the third quarter, however, operating profit fell to KRW 6.69 billion and controlling-shareholder net profit turned to a loss of KRW 303 million, so the continuity of quarterly results remains unproven.

Against controlling-shareholder net profit of KRW 14.8 billion over the last four quarters, the share price sits at a high multiple, and the premium to book value is large, so the question is whether the price assumes earnings well above the current base.

Strengths include the rising MLCC share, the new Yongin capacity and a debt ratio of 21.7% at the end of 2025, while weaknesses include three years of falling operating profit, the third-quarter swing into deficit and cost pressure.

The next checkpoints are the 2026 annual report, due by the end of March 2027, and the first-quarter 2027 results.
