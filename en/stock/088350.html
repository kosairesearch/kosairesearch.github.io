Report overview

Insurance Profit Recovers as Investment Losses Hit Hanwha Life

Hanwha Life's insurance profit and its overseas and non-life subsidiary earnings grew, but investment losses in the third quarter of 2026 pushed it to an operating loss of KRW 156.5 billion and a net loss attributable to controlling shareholders of KRW 103.5 billion.

  2. 2

  3. 3

02

Business structure

Hanwha Life Insurance combines a domestic life insurance core business with non-life, overseas and insurance agency (GA) subsidiaries. The parent's insurance profit is the main gauge of its core business, and on a standalone basis it rose 176% year on year to KRW 385.1 billion in the first nine months of 2026.

The company is expanding mid- to long-term payment whole life and health insurance, and standalone new business CSM reached KRW 2.086 trillion in the first nine months, up 40%. Single-pay whole life products were discontinued, and managing new contracts afterward is seen as a challenge.

Hanwha General Insurance posted standalone net profit of KRW 311.4 billion in the first nine months, up 5.8%, with improved loss ratios in long-term insurance cited as a driver.

Overseas subsidiaries' cumulative net profit rose 74.5% to KRW 146.6 billion, split among the Vietnam unit (KRW 46.7 billion), Bank Nobu in Indonesia (KRW 46.1 billion) and Velocity Securities in the United States (KRW 38.8 billion).

The insurance agency subsidiary's cumulative net profit was KRW 88.3 billion, up 10.2%. As subsidiaries contribute more to consolidated earnings, standalone net profit for the first nine months fell 7.3%, widening the gap between parent-only and consolidated results.

04

Earnings analysis

Based on annual figures from the regular filings, operating profit fell from KRW 1.26 trillion in 2022 to KRW 1.06 trillion in 2023, a decline of 15.9%. It then rose to KRW 1.1 trillion in 2024 (up 3.8%) and KRW 1.15 trillion in 2025 (up 4.6%), marking two consecutive years of increases from 2024 to 2025.

Within 2022 to 2025, 2022 was the largest year and 2023 the smallest.

Net profit attributable to controlling shareholders moved the other way: it fell from KRW 1.03 trillion in 2022 to KRW 758.5 billion in 2023 (down 26.4%), KRW 737.3 billion in 2024 (down 2.8%) and KRW 641.3 billion in 2025 (down 13.0%), a decline for three consecutive years from 2023 to 2025.

The fact that this profit fell in 2024 and 2025, even as operating profit rose, shows a gap between operating and bottom-line results. Net profit including non-controlling interests was KRW 866 billion in 2024 and KRW 836.3 billion in 2025, so the growing share going to minority interests is part of the gap.

Operating cash flow rose from KRW 1.19 trillion in 2022 to KRW 5.43 trillion in 2024 and was KRW 4.38 trillion in 2025. The debt-to-equity ratio (total liabilities divided by total equity) rose from 642.9% in 2022 to 855.7% in 2023 and 1,021.6% in 2024, then eased to 974.0% in 2025.

Total equity at the end of 2025 was KRW 16.6 trillion, of which KRW 13.9 trillion belonged to controlling shareholders.

On a quarterly basis, operating profit fell from KRW 354.9 billion in Q3 2025 to KRW 180.3 billion in Q4 2025 (down 49.2% from the prior quarter), then recovered to KRW 480.8 billion in Q1 2026 and KRW 626.8 billion in Q2 2026.

Q3 2026 swung to an operating loss of KRW 156.5 billion, and net profit attributable to controlling shareholders moved from KRW 447.6 billion in Q2 2026 to a net loss of KRW 103.5 billion.

The company attributed the third-quarter loss mainly to investment results, with non-recurring items of KRW 430.3 billion, made up of KRW 183.6 billion in foreign-exchange losses on overseas hybrid securities and KRW 246.7 billion in variable insurance hedge losses.

05

Industry analysis

Life insurers' quarterly earnings are highly sensitive to interest rates and financial market volatility.

As the third-quarter 2026 results show, insurance profit improved on narrower experience variances and CSM growth, but financial market factors such as variable insurance hedging and fair-value swings on overseas hybrid securities drove the quarterly outcome.

Daol Investment & Securities said in a May 2026 report that rising interest rates ease the burden of discount rate assumptions, and it added that the effect must be proven in reported results.

On regulation, financial authorities are reported to be pushing to revise the formula for policy reserves for surrender refunds (April 2026 report), a factor that determines distributable profit for life insurers.

SK Securities pointed in a May 2025 report to a phased tightening of discount rate rules, scheduled through 2027, as a constraint on shareholder returns.

Across the sector, the pace of CSM growth varies with sales channels and product mix, so the share of protection products and the scale of savings-type sales shape each insurer's position. Hanwha Life holds an expanded sales network, a non-life subsidiary and overseas units, so it does not depend on the parent alone. Volatility in parent-level investment results, however, remains a shared challenge for the sector.

06

Outlook

The company has not published a quantitative guidance figure that can be confirmed, but several disclosed points stand out.

First, the preliminary K-ICS solvency ratio at the end of Q3 2026 was 171.0%, up from 168.0% at the end of Q2, and the final figure is due to be corrected in a revised disclosure by the end of November 2026.

Second, Lee Chang-ju, head of investment planning, said most variable insurance hedge losses occurred during the July market plunge, that the company reduced mismatched positions after the October peak, and that investment-related profit management could be stable in Q4.

He also said the share of fair-value-through-profit-or-loss financial assets would be lowered to about 23% by year-end.

Third, the company plans to announce a corporate value-up plan in the first half of 2027, and it is reviewing shareholder return measures including dividends alongside its core insurance, asset management and overseas strategies.

Fourth, KB Securities in a November 13, 2026 report cut its 2026 standalone net profit forecast by 14.9% to KRW 409.6 billion and raised its 2027 forecast by 4.2% to KRW 562.7 billion.

The same report said continuation of the insurance profit improvement needs further confirmation, and it also flagged sales expense burdens from new contract growth and the build-up of policy reserves for surrender refunds.

The next checkpoints are how insurance profit and investment profit are composed in the Q4 2026 results and in the 2026 annual business report.

08

Bull factors

Recovery in insurance profit

Standalone insurance profit for the first nine months of 2026 reached KRW 385.1 billion, up 176% year on year and above the full-year 2025 figure of KRW 344.4 billion. Experience variance losses on insurance payments fell to KRW 216.5 billion, down KRW 53.1 billion from a year earlier.

Loss-recognition-related items also turned into reversals. The core business improvement is cited as a basis for offsetting quarterly investment losses.

Growth in new and in-force CSM

Standalone new business CSM for the first nine months of 2026 reached KRW 2.086 trillion, up 40% from a year earlier. In-force CSM at the end of Q3 was KRW 9.4144 trillion, KRW 700.7 billion higher than at the end of 2025. CSM adjustments narrowed from KRW 592.0 billion in Q2 to KRW 201.0 billion in Q3. The larger CSM base is cited as a foundation for future insurance profit.

Earnings contribution from subsidiaries and overseas units

Cumulative net profit from overseas subsidiaries rose 74.5% to KRW 146.6 billion in the first nine months of 2026. Hanwha General Insurance's standalone net profit reached KRW 311.4 billion, up 5.8%, and its Q3 standalone net profit alone rose 34.1% to KRW 96.1 billion.

Cumulative consolidated net profit increased 12.6% to KRW 865.6 billion. Some analysts say subsidiary earnings partly offset the parent's losses.

09

Bear factors

Large swings in investment results

Standalone investment results in Q3 2026 showed a loss of KRW 462.1 billion, of which KRW 430.3 billion was non-recurring. Cumulative investment results for the first nine months swung to a loss of KRW 107.3 billion from a profit of KRW 255.0 billion a year earlier.

The operating loss of KRW 156.5 billion and net loss of KRW 103.5 billion attributable to controlling shareholders show investment losses outweighing the core business gains. The company points to more stable investment-related management in Q4, but that claim can only be tested in later results.

Constraints on restarting shareholder returns

Many analysts say that even with positive consolidated profit, the formula for policy reserves for surrender refunds leaves too little distributable profit to resume dividends. NH Investment & Securities said in an April 2026 report that the timing of a dividend restart remains uncertain.

The company has signaled a value-up plan for the first half of 2027, but specific dividend measures have not yet been disclosed. If shareholder returns normalize slowly, the low market valuation could persist.

Earnings trend and new contract management

Net profit attributable to controlling shareholders fell for three consecutive years from 2023 to 2025, and the sum for the last four quarters, from Q4 2025 to Q3 2026, was KRW 691.6 billion. Standalone net profit for the first nine months of 2026 fell 7.3% to KRW 292.8 billion.

Managing new contracts after the discontinuation of single-pay whole life products is seen as a challenge. KB Securities also said the sustainability of the insurance profit improvement needs further confirmation.

10

Risk factors

Financial market and currency risk

Third-quarter 2026 losses came from fair-value swings on foreign-currency hybrid securities and from mismatches in variable insurance hedging linked to currency and equity market moves.

The company said it reduced hedge positions and plans to lower its FVPL share, but renewed market volatility could again disrupt quarterly investment results. Variable insurance hedge results tend to widen when equity markets become volatile. This keeps quarterly earnings volatile.

Regulatory risk

The formula for policy reserves for surrender refunds is seen as the key regulation determining distributable profit and shareholder returns. Reports say financial authorities are pushing to revise the formula, but timing and content have not been finalized.

SK Securities pointed in a May 2025 report to phased tightening of discount rate rules scheduled through 2027. Stricter discount rates can raise liability valuation burdens and affect capital adequacy measures.

Quality of insurance profit and experience variance risk

Insurance profit improvement was supported by narrower experience variance losses on insurance payments and by reversals in loss-recognition contracts. Reversal effects can be one-off, so their repetition needs to be checked.

One report said the second-quarter insurance profit of KRW 222.9 billion included KRW 91.0 billion of such reversals, and that without this effect third-quarter insurance profit fell 55.2% from the prior quarter. The durability of insurance profit should be verified in upcoming disclosures.

12

Overall view

Hanwha Life's regular-filing results show operating profit rising for two consecutive years in 2024 and 2025, while net profit attributable to controlling shareholders fell for three consecutive years from 2023 to 2025, so the direction of earnings is mixed.

In Q3 2026, standalone insurance profit rose 176% year on year, but foreign-exchange losses and variable insurance hedge losses together produced an operating loss of KRW 156.5 billion and a net loss attributable to controlling shareholders of KRW 103.5 billion.

Strengths include cumulative new business CSM of KRW 2.086 trillion for the first nine months, in-force CSM of KRW 9.4144 trillion, narrower experience variance losses, and a preliminary solvency ratio of 171.0%.

Weaknesses include large swings in investment results, a surrender refund reserve structure that constrains dividends, and the challenge of managing new contracts after single-pay whole life products were discontinued.

KB Securities said in its November 13, 2026 report that it saw a possible recovery in insurance profit but that its sustainability needs further confirmation.

The next checkpoints are the final K-ICS correction disclosure due by late November 2026, the corporate value-up plan in the first half of 2027, and the annual insurance and controlling-shareholder profit figures in the 2026 annual business report.
