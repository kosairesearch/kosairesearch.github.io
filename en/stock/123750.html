Alton: Profit Turn Followed by Sharp Quarterly Swings

02

Business structure

Alton is a smart mobility company centered on electric bicycles. Industry commentary describes the bicycle sector shifting toward a market led by e-bikes, with the spread of sharing platforms changing its structure. The company has leaned toward widening its sales channels through business-to-business (B2B) sales.

In its Q2 2026 results, Alton said stronger B2B supply and new product sales drove revenue growth. A KRW 11.8 billion supply contract with a Korean IT company, signed early in 2026, was largely recognized in Q2 2026 results.

In the same period the company launched e-bikes using lithium manganese iron phosphate (LMFP) batteries. It set up a special sales team for corporate clients to strengthen its B2B sales capacity.

Early in 2026 it also entered a strategic partnership with a venture capital investor to find new core businesses beyond e-bikes. The fourth quarter is seen as an off-season for bicycles, and the company said it will focus on launching new products for the 2027 market rather than boosting second-half sales volume.

Among domestic bicycle makers, Alton is one of two leading players, while another large peer has focused on strengthening consumer-facing product lines. Because revenue depends on when large supply contracts are booked, quarterly sales have varied sharply.

04

Earnings analysis

Annual revenue fell from KRW 51.3 billion in 2022 to KRW 42.4 billion in 2023 (down 17.4% year on year) and KRW 28.2 billion in 2024 (down 33.5% year on year), then rose to KRW 32.2 billion in 2025 (up 14.1% year on year). 2022 was the largest year and 2024 the smallest among 2022 to 2025.

Operating profit fell from KRW 2.09 billion in 2022 to KRW 650 million in 2023 (down 68.9% year on year), then turned into an operating loss of KRW 5.49 billion in 2024 (a swing into loss from the prior year). In 2025 it returned to an operating profit of KRW 227 million (a swing into profit from the prior year).

The operating margin ran at 4.1% in 2022, 1.5% in 2023, -19.5% in 2024 and 0.7% in 2025. The company credited the 2025 turnaround to deep restructuring and innovation despite high exchange rates and weak domestic demand.

Profit attributable to owners of the parent was a net loss of KRW 707 million in 2022, a net loss of KRW 363 million in 2023 (loss narrowed), a net loss of KRW 6.35 billion in 2024 (loss widened), and a net profit of KRW 212 million in 2025 (a swing into profit).

Net income including non-controlling interests was reported at the same amounts as profit attributable to owners in each year from 2022 to 2025. Operating cash flow was KRW 1.87 billion in 2022, KRW 8.19 billion in 2023, -KRW 3.82 billion in 2024 and KRW 479 million in 2025.

Quarterly, Q3 2025 revenue was KRW 4.75 billion with an operating loss of KRW 632 million. Q4 2025 revenue rose to KRW 5.58 billion (up 17.5% from the prior quarter), with an operating loss of KRW 360 million and profit attributable to owners of KRW 722 million, a swing into profit.

Q1 2026 revenue was KRW 7.08 billion (up 26.8% from the prior quarter), but the operating loss widened to KRW 427 million and the net loss attributable to owners was KRW 267 million.

Q2 2026 revenue jumped to KRW 18 billion (up 153.9% from the prior quarter), with operating profit of KRW 1.66 billion, a swing into profit, and profit attributable to owners of KRW 1.24 billion.

Q3 2026 revenue was KRW 5.56 billion (down 69.1% from the prior quarter and up 17.0% year on year), with an operating loss of KRW 1.17 billion (a swing into loss from the prior quarter and a wider loss year on year) and a net loss attributable to owners of KRW 1.12 billion (loss narrowed year on year).

Profit attributable to owners over the last four quarters (Q4 2025 to Q3 2026) totaled KRW 580 million. The concentration of large supply volume in Q2 is read as not carrying into Q3 revenue at the same scale, and wider swings in quarterly results mark the recent pattern.

Industry analysis

The domestic bicycle industry is moving toward a smart mobility market centered on electric bicycles. Some industry commentary argues that the spread of sharing platforms is changing the industry's structure. Within this shift, Alton has chosen to widen its sales channels through B2B sales.

Another large peer in the same industry has focused on strengthening its consumer-facing product lines, so the two companies' sales approaches clearly differ. In its 2025 results commentary, the company cited high exchange rates and weak domestic demand as adverse conditions.

The bicycle business is strongly seasonal, with the fourth quarter viewed as an off-season, and the timing of large supply contracts weighs heavily on quarterly results.

On competition, Alton is one of the two leading domestic players and is trying to differentiate through corporate-client sales capability and new products. On the battery side, it launched e-bikes using LMFP batteries early in 2026, broadening its product lineup.

On new businesses, the company is looking for new core businesses beyond e-bikes, so it appears to be in a phase of diversifying products and business areas.

Outlook

The most specific plan the company has stated is to focus on launching new products for the 2027 market rather than boosting second-half sales volume. Its stated earnings strategy is to strengthen product competitiveness with high-performance new products and expand sales to corporate clients.

The KRW 11.8 billion supply contract signed early in 2026 was reflected in Q2 2026 revenue, but with Q3 revenue falling sharply from Q2, the size and timing of follow-on large contracts is the next point to watch.

Backed by its partnership with a venture capital investor, the company said it will pursue growth strategies that include M&A of promising companies. It also said it will strengthen its mobility business by supplying customized products to corporate clients and expanding sales to them.

The 2026 annual report is due by the end of March 2027, when full-year 2026 revenue and operating margin will be confirmed. The Q1 2027 quarterly report is due in mid-May 2027, and it will show whether sales from the new product launch are reflected in results.

This outlook rests on company plans and filings, and whether those plans translate into reported numbers is the key variable.

Bear factors

Q3 2026 revenue was KRW 5.56 billion, down 69.1% from the prior quarter. The operating loss of KRW 1.17 billion was a swing back into loss from the prior quarter, and the loss widened from a year earlier. The fact that the Q2 swing into profit did not carry into Q3 is a bear-side point.

Thin earnings base

Profit attributable to owners over the last four quarters totaled only KRW 580 million. The operating margin was 4.1% in 2022, -19.5% in 2024 and 0.7% in 2025, all at low levels. With a small earnings base, a swing in one or two quarters can move annual results substantially.

External pressures and dependence on B2B orders

The company itself cited high exchange rates and weak domestic demand as adverse conditions in its 2025 results. While another large peer focuses on consumer-facing product lines, a higher reliance on B2B orders could widen revenue gaps during periods without large contracts.

Risk factors

Reliance on large contracts

The gap between Q2 and Q3 2026 revenue showed how much the business depends on large contracts. If follow-on contracts stop, quarterly revenue could fall back toward the KRW 5.56 billion level seen in Q3 2026. The fourth-quarter off-season in the bicycle industry could add pressure in the same direction.

Exchange rates and domestic demand

The company cited high exchange rates and weak domestic demand as adverse conditions. If cost pressure and consumer sentiment worsen together, margin recovery could slow. With the operating margin at only 0.7% in 2025, the cushion against cost swings is thin.

Cash flow and execution of new businesses

Operating cash flow was -KRW 3.82 billion in 2024, a period of cash outflow. The partnership with a venture capital investor and the pursuit of M&A bring funding needs and uncertainty over acquisition results. Whether new businesses dilute the profitability of existing operations is also a point to monitor.
