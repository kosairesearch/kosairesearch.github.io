02

Business structure

Taewoong Logistics was founded in 1996 as an integrated logistics service provider and listed on KOSDAQ in December 2019.

Third-party logistics (3PL) across sea, air and rail is its core business, and in 2024 integrated logistics accounted for 71.6% of sales, while subsidiaries accounted for 28.4% after consolidation adjustments. Its subsidiary base expanded after the 2019 listing through M&A and overseas subsidiaries.

In Korea it acquired GLS Korea, which handles overseas relocation and exhibition cargo, and Sejung Logistics, an inland transport operator in Busan and Ulsan.

Overseas it set up local units in the United States, Indonesia, Spain and Germany, and as of the reporting date it held 15 unlisted subsidiaries across 4 domestic and 15 overseas countries.

Its scope now spans project logistics, Eurasian multimodal transport through its Kazakhstan unit (including TCR and TSR routes), customs clearance and insurance.

Customers include petrochemical shippers such as Lotte Chemical and LG Chem, along with construction, plant and electronics companies, and at its 2024 preliminary results announcement the company said it had signed an overseas EPC project transport contract with Samsung E&A.

Its ISO tank container business for liquid and gas cargo is expanding through leasing, sales and new builds, and it is extending into gas containers. T&C BUSAN, a dedicated special-cargo hub at Busan New Port, was set up to raise handling capacity for higher-value energy and chemical cargo.

In competition it is compared with large logistics companies such as Hyundai Glovis, CJ Logistics and HANJIN Logistics, and it seeks differentiation in project logistics and specialty cargo niches.

04

Earnings analysis

Based on DART periodic reports, annual sales fell from KRW 1.33 trillion in 2022 to KRW 770.3 billion in 2023 (down 42.0% year on year), then rose to KRW 1.04 trillion in 2024 (up 35.5%) and KRW 1.12 trillion in 2025 (up 7.0%).

After the 2023 trough, sales grew for two consecutive years from 2024 through 2025, and 2022 was the largest year of the 2022–2025 period.

Operating profit went from KRW 116.6 billion in 2022 to KRW 36 billion in 2023 (down 69.1%), KRW 40.8 billion in 2024 (up 13.3%), and KRW 2.25 billion in 2025 (down 94.5%), the smallest year in the period.

In 2024 and 2025, when sales rose, the operating margin was only 3.9% and 0.2%, so top-line growth and margin moved separately. The company attributed the 2025 profit decline to lower ocean freight rates, foreign exchange losses and additional bad-debt provisions on major customer receivables.

Net income attributable to controlling shareholders moved from KRW 30.2 billion in 2024 to a net loss of KRW 5.38 billion in 2025, a swing into deficit, while net income including non-controlling interests was a net loss of KRW 5.04 billion.

Operating cash flow was negative KRW 2.71 billion in 2023, then recovered to KRW 31.9 billion in 2024 and KRW 30.3 billion in 2025.

Quarterly results began with sales of KRW 268.9 billion and an operating loss of KRW 8.91 billion in the third quarter of 2025, and the fourth-quarter operating loss narrowed to KRW 3.27 billion.

Operating profit turned positive at KRW 546 million in the first quarter of 2026, then rose to KRW 5.9 billion in the second quarter and KRW 15.8 billion in the third, with third-quarter sales of KRW 426.5 billion.

Net income attributable to controlling shareholders for the four quarters from the fourth quarter of 2025 through the third quarter of 2026 totals KRW 6.07 billion, so profit scale remains small even after the turn to profit.

The debt ratio climbed from 79.2% in 2022 to 85.0%, 110.4% and 120.3% by 2025, so the earnings recovery and financial burden both remain.

05

Industry analysis

Taewoong Logistics' earnings are widely read through their link to ocean freight rates. One researcher found that the correlation between quarter-end share prices and the Shanghai Containerized Freight Index (SCFI) was very high at 0.8 over five years.

The same analysis found that the correlation between subsidiary sales and the SCFI was only 0.11, close to none. The researcher argued that revenue trends would decouple from the freight index as subsidiary sales take a larger share.

On the freight side, the SCFI had fallen to 55% of its prior-year average at the time of a 2025 report, which was cited as a weakness. The company said cargo volume in the first nine months of 2025 rose about 10% year on year.

In the same statement it said freight unit prices fell amid intensified competition, while logistics and fixed costs rose, and exchange rate swings and tariff increases compounded the pressure.

In competitive terms, a 2025 analysis noted that most listed 2PL logistics companies other than Hyundai Glovis traded at valuations similar to or below Taewoong Logistics.

The cycle position is one where freight index direction and volume growth are diverging, so whether freight recovery or volume growth drives earnings is the point to watch.

06

Outlook

In its third-quarter 2025 results announcement, the company said it expanded multimodal transport networks across Central Asia and China to strengthen project logistics competitiveness and its order pipeline.

It plans to increase high-value project volume and key global partnerships, and to improve profitability through cost efficiency and portfolio restructuring.

In its first-half 2025 announcement it outlined a dedicated special-cargo logistics center in Busan to expand warehousing in the port hinterland, and a wider warehouse business in Central Asia. T&C BUSAN at Busan New Port was positioned as a hub to add handling capacity for higher-value energy and chemical cargo.

Gas ISO tank containers were cited as a source of expected profitability, since they require higher technical standards than liquid-cargo tanks and long-term contracts of three to five years are common.

Overseas, payment delays on Central Asian major customers' receivables, tied to local government payments, led to additional bad-debt provisions in the third quarter of 2025. The company said a reversal is expected when the receivables are collected.

The points to check next are how much the operating profit gains in the second and third quarters of 2026 depended on such one-off items like reversals, and whether core volume offsets them from the fourth quarter of 2026 onward.

Medium-term priorities are overseas network expansion and portfolio growth through M&A, and the company has said it will select companies and assets with large synergies to widen its warehousing and transport bases.

08

Bull factors

Quarterly profit recovery

Operating profit turned positive at KRW 546 million in the first quarter of 2026, then rose to KRW 5.9 billion in the second quarter and KRW 15.8 billion in the third, growing for two straight quarters. Third-quarter 2026 sales of KRW 426.5 billion rose 58.6% year on year, so top line and profit improved together. Moving out of the 2025 operating loss zone can be read as a direction of profit recovery.

Overseas network and project logistics expansion

The company said cargo volume rose about 10% year on year in the first nine months of 2025. Expansion of multimodal networks across Central Asia and China, along with the T&C BUSAN hub at Busan New Port, was presented as a base for more high-value project volume. Its overseas subsidiaries number 15 across 15 countries as of the reporting date.

Shareholder return policy

The 2025 payout ratio was 25%, and a March 2026 corrected disclosure classified the company as a high-dividend firm. A 2025 report said the company would retire 1 million treasury shares in stages over three years and raise its minimum dividend step by step. Whether these shareholder return measures continue regardless of earnings swings is the point to check.

Bear factors

Dependence on ocean freight

One researcher found the correlation between quarter-end share prices and the SCFI was high at 0.8. The SCFI had fallen to 55% of its prior-year average at the time of a 2025 report, which is cited as a weakness. The structure in which core earnings fall along with freight rates still remains.

Weaker profitability and financial burden

The operating margin fell from 8.8% in 2022 to 0.2% in 2025. The debt-to-equity ratio rose from 79.2% to 120.3%, and net income attributable to controlling shareholders was a KRW 5.38 billion net loss in 2025. The core bear point is that sales grew while profit did not follow.

Receivable payment delays

In the third quarter of 2025, payment delays from local government payers at a major Central Asian customer led to additional bad-debt provisions. Additional bad-debt recognition on major customer receivables was also part of the 2025 full-year profit decline. Until receivables are collected, the same kind of profit shock could recur.

Risk factors

Currency, tariff and freight rate swings

The company said freight unit prices fell while logistics and fixed costs rose, and exchange rate swings and tariff increases added pressure. In the first half of 2025, a stronger won led to foreign exchange losses and valuation losses that weighed on net income. Currency and tariff policy affect both the won translation of overseas sales and freight unit prices.

Customer credit and bad debt

When a Central Asian customer's payments are held up by local government payment delays, bad-debt provisions build up. The 2025 full-year results also reflected additional provisions on major customer receivables. Even with a reversal expected on collection, earnings volatility from customer concentration remains.

Execution of expansion plans

The company has outlined a strategy that pairs overseas subsidiary investment, warehouse expansion and M&A. Sales growth from expansion did not translate into profit, with 2025 operating profit at KRW 2.25 billion. If monetization of these investments is slow, financial burden could increase.

Overall view

Taewoong Logistics' sales rose to KRW 1.12 trillion in 2025, but operating profit of KRW 2.25 billion and a controlling-shareholder net loss of KRW 5.38 billion meant the earnings base weakened sharply.

In 2026, operating profit turned positive in the first quarter at KRW 546 million and then rose to KRW 5.9 billion in the second quarter and KRW 15.8 billion in the third, a recovery confirmed in filings.

However, the four-quarter total of net income attributable to controlling shareholders from the fourth quarter of 2025 through the third quarter of 2026 is KRW 6.07 billion, so profit scale remains small.

An analysis linking the company's shares closely to the SCFI, together with the 2025 freight weakness, keeps core earnings exposed to external variables. The debt-to-equity ratio rose to 120.3% at the end of 2025, which is also a financial point to verify.

Shareholder return measures such as a 25% payout ratio and treasury share cancellation have been presented even amid earnings swings. On the bull side, the quarterly return to profit and overseas network expansion stand against freight-linked earnings and receivable risk on the bear side.

The next checks are the annual operating margin in the 2026 annual report, first-quarter 2027 results, and whether Central Asian receivables are collected.
