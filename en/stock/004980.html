02

Business structure

Sungshin Cement is a mid-sized maker of cement and ready-mixed concrete, and its trading business has grown quickly within the sales mix.

The parent's Danyang plant has annual cement capacity of 11 million tons, a subsidiary handles ready-mixed concrete, and JinSung International, its Singapore unit, runs international trading.

Cumulative sales for January to September 2026 of KRW 1.13 trillion were split into trading KRW 519.8 billion, cement KRW 471.8 billion, ready-mixed concrete KRW 115.4 billion and other KRW 18.3 billion (Vegannews, November 2026).

In 2023, the cement segment accounted for 74% of sales and ready-mixed concrete for about 16% (Business Post). Trading began in 2017 with coal and anthracite, and the Singapore unit has since expanded into clinker trading and silver and other non-ferrous metals.

A cement subsidiary was newly consolidated from 2026, which affected raw material purchases and capacity figures. By domestic cement shipments the company ranks fifth, and it is compared with HANIL CEMENTCO, SAMPYO Cement and Asia Cement.

Ready-mixed concrete is a mix of cement, sand and gravel used in the frames of apartments and office buildings. Overseas, the company keeps local production and sales running in Vietnam, which is cited as a point of difference.

04

Earnings analysis

On a reported basis, sales rose from KRW 1.03 trillion in 2022 to KRW 1.22 trillion in 2025, increasing for three consecutive years from 2023 to 2025.

Operating profit went from KRW 1.83 billion in 2022 to KRW 73.3 billion in 2023, then declined for two consecutive years to KRW 53.2 billion in 2024 and KRW 43.7 billion in 2025.

Business Post attributes the 2023 jump of 3,899.3% to two cement price increases carried out in 2022, with finished-product price increases larger than the rise in electricity, coal and other input costs.

The average cement selling price then moved from KRW 96,761 per ton in 2024 to KRW 94,818 per ton in 2025, and the operating margin fell from 6.6% in 2023 to 4.6% in 2024 and 3.6% in 2025.

Profit attributable to controlling shareholders swung from a net loss of KRW 26.6 billion in 2022 to a profit of KRW 66.7 billion in 2023, then declined for two consecutive years to KRW 45.2 billion in 2024 and KRW 26 billion in 2025. Net income including non-controlling interests was KRW 26 billion in 2025.

In the first half of 2026, external trading sales more than doubled to nearly half of total sales, while the consolidated gross margin fell from 13.9% to 10.7% (Asia Today, September 2026).

Quarterly sales rose from KRW 318.1 billion in the third quarter of 2025 to KRW 334.2 billion in the fourth quarter, with operating profit moving from KRW 12.6 billion to KRW 14.9 billion.

In the first quarter of 2026, sales fell to KRW 285 billion and operating profit to KRW 792 million, and profit attributable to controlling shareholders turned into a loss of KRW 5.81 billion.

The second quarter rebounded to sales of KRW 503.2 billion, operating profit of KRW 18.9 billion and controlling-shareholder profit of KRW 19.3 billion. In the third quarter, sales rose 6.0% year on year to KRW 337.1 billion, but operating profit fell 79.8% to KRW 2.55 billion. Profit attributable to controlling shareholders for the last four quarters totaled KRW 26.6 billion.

05

Industry analysis

Domestic cement shipments fell from 43.71 million tons in 2024 to about 38 million tons in 2025, and they are expected to stay at a similar level in 2026 (Asia Today, September 2026).

The company expects second-half 2026 demand to be similar to or slightly above the 2025 level, while acknowledging that the domestic construction slump will persist. The clinker utilization rate on a parent-company basis was 50.9% in the first half of 2026.

A securities analysis notes that the company's large, modern kilns give strong operating leverage when the market is good, but high fixed costs weigh more when utilization is low (Business Post).

The company expects trucking costs alone to rise by more than KRW 10 billion in 2026, partly because of safe freight rate rules, and it sees possible electricity tariff increases and emissions-control costs such as selective catalytic reduction systems as making domestic plant operations hard to make profitable (Daehan Kyungje, January 2026).

The Korea Cement Association projects that the eco-friendly cement market will grow 7.1% a year from 2026 to 2031 (Dongheng Media Sidae, February 2026). Ranked fifth by domestic shipments, the company plans to target this market with low-carbon cement.

Its expansion overseas while rivals hold back is seen by some observers as a sign of the sector's structural limits.

06

Outlook

The company expects second-half 2026 cement demand to be similar to or slightly above the 2025 level, and it plans to offset the domestic slump with trading through overseas affiliates and global business.

A February 2026 report put the start of low-carbon cement production at the third quarter of 2026 at the earliest and the fourth quarter at the latest, conditional on completing trial research and obtaining Ministry of Trade, Industry and Energy approval.

The product was described as having at least 50% less limestone content than ordinary cement. In May 2026 the company said a direct renewable power purchase agreement project had been finalized after about 15 months of technical review, which it expects to lower electricity costs at its power-intensive plants.

On the production side, it is focusing on fewer equipment breakdowns and lower maintenance costs to reduce energy spending on electricity and bituminous coal. Overseas, it is expanding local production and sales in Vietnam and non-ferrous metal trading through its Singapore unit.

The profit contribution of these overseas and new businesses can be traced in the segment figures of periodic filings. The next checkpoint is the full-year 2026 operating margin in the annual business report.

08

Bull factors

Trading holds up the top line

Cumulative sales for January to September 2026 rose 27.6% to KRW 1.13 trillion, with trading the largest segment at KRW 519.8 billion (Vegannews, November 2026). External trading sales more than doubled in the first half of 2026 to nearly half of total sales (Asia Today, September 2026). With domestic cement demand weaker, this support for sales is the key positive.

Quarterly return to profit

Operating profit in the second quarter of 2026 rose to KRW 18.9 billion, and profit attributable to controlling shareholders returned to KRW 19.3 billion. In the first quarter of 2026 the same measure had turned into a loss of KRW 5.81 billion. Because quarterly swings are large, whether this recovery holds will show up in later quarterly results.

Eco-friendly and overseas expansion

Low-carbon cement was targeted for production as early as the third quarter of 2026 and no later than the fourth quarter (Dongheng Media Sidae, February 2026). The Korea Cement Association expects the eco-friendly cement market to grow 7.1% a year from 2026 to 2031.

The company said in May 2026 that its direct renewable power purchase agreement project had been finalized, and it continues local production and sales in Vietnam.

09

Bear factors

Weak domestic volume and prices

Domestic cement shipments fell from 43.71 million tons in 2024 to about 38 million tons in 2025, and 2026 is expected to be similar to the prior year (Asia Today, September 2026). The average selling price in the first half of 2026 was KRW 92,677 per ton, below KRW 96,761 in 2024.

Clinker utilization on a parent-company basis stayed at 50.9% in the first half. Low utilization raises the burden of fixed costs, which is the main negative.

Margins slip despite sales growth

Operating profit fell for two consecutive years from KRW 73.3 billion in 2023 to KRW 43.7 billion in 2025, and in the third quarter of 2026 it dropped 79.8% to KRW 2.55 billion. The consolidated gross margin in the first half of 2026 fell from 13.9% to 10.7% (Asia Today, September 2026).

Sales grew while margins narrowed, so whether trading expansion has translated into better profitability is still open.

Rising costs and fixed-cost burden

A securities analysis flagged that fixed costs weigh more heavily when utilization is low (Business Post). The company expected trucking costs alone to rise by more than KRW 10 billion in 2026 because of safe freight rate rules (Daehan Kyungje, January 2026).

Possible electricity tariff increases and emissions-control costs were also cited as pressure on domestic plant economics.

10

Risk factors

Construction slump and domestic demand

The company cited lower cement shipments from the domestic construction slump as the main reason for the decline in 2025 net income. The construction downturn is also expected to persist (Asia Today, September 2026).

Construction firms are struggling, so asking for price increases is difficult, the company said (Daehan Kyungje, January 2026). Limited room for domestic price increases constrains any profit recovery.

Uncertainty in trading and non-ferrous metals

JinSung International's Singapore unit has widened its business into silver and other non-ferrous metal trading, and some in the industry read this as a search for new revenue because the core business alone struggles to secure profitability (Daehan Kyungje, January 2026).

An industry source said it remains to be seen whether such diversification becomes a winning move or adds uncertainty. As trading's share of sales grows, swings in trading volumes can weigh more on reported results.

Debt load and regulatory costs

Borrowings at the end of 2024 were KRW 496 billion, up 16.3% from a year earlier, with KRW 346.5 billion of short-term debt due within a year (Newspim, May 2025). Total liabilities at the end of 2025 were KRW 714.4 billion, and the debt-to-equity ratio was 120.5%.

Rising carbon prices and tighter environmental rules may also raise the burden of low-carbon equipment investment (Dongheng Media Sidae, February 2026).

12

Overall view

Sungshin Cement has shifted to a structure that holds up sales through trading, in an environment where domestic cement demand has shrunk since 2024 and is expected to stay at a similar level in 2026.

On a reported basis, sales rose for three consecutive years from 2023 to 2025, but operating profit has declined for two consecutive years since its 2023 peak, and the 2025 operating margin was 3.6%.

Quarterly results in 2026 have been volatile, with operating profit recovering to KRW 18.9 billion in the second quarter before dropping to KRW 2.55 billion in the third.

Positives include trading expansion, the improvement in the debt-to-equity ratio from 199.7% to 120.5%, and eco-friendly moves such as low-carbon cement and renewable power. Negatives include lower cement selling prices and utilization, and higher trucking, electricity and emissions-control costs.

As trading's share has grown, the gross margin has narrowed, so separating sales growth from profitability is the starting point for reading the current picture. With the share price trading low against book net assets, whether the decline in earnings has stopped is the central item to follow.
