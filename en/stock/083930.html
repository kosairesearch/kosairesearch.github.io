02

Business structure

Avacoco was founded in 2000 to make flat-panel display equipment and listed on KOSDAQ in 2005. Its business is split into four divisions: vacuum equipment, automation equipment, dedicated equipment, and industrial materials manufacturing equipment.

Based on the first-half 2026 semiannual report, LCD and OLED manufacturing equipment generated about KRW 15.3 billion, or 49.0% of the total, while secondary battery manufacturing equipment generated about KRW 7 billion, or 22.5%.

In vacuum equipment, sputtering systems and OLED in-line vacuum systems are the core products, and the automation division supplies automation systems for secondary battery manufacturing. The dedicated equipment division produces bending equipment and POL attachers.

Chinese panel maker BOE, LG Display, and LG Energy Solution were named as key customers in a 2025 interview, in which the CEO said about 90% of total sales come from exports. In June 2024 the company won an order from BOE for an OLED in-line vacuum system.

In display equipment, Sunic System is mentioned alongside Avacoco in reports on BOE 8.6-generation OLED equipment orders.

New businesses include TGV process equipment for glass substrates and Low Damage Sputter systems, and in October 2025 the company signed an evaluation contract with a domestic back-end semiconductor provider for a metal sputter production line. That equipment targets AI semiconductor packaging processes such as HBM.

04

Earnings analysis

On the annual reported figures, revenue fell from KRW 217.3 billion in 2022 to KRW 186.9 billion in 2023 (down 14.0% year on year), then rose to KRW 305.5 billion in 2024 (up 63.5%) and KRW 392.9 billion in 2025 (up 28.6%), increasing for two consecutive years from 2024 through 2025.

Operating profit was KRW 13.6 billion in 2022, KRW 4.41 billion in 2023 (down 67.5%), KRW 21.1 billion in 2024 (up 378.7%), and KRW 34.6 billion in 2025 (up 64.0%), also rising for two consecutive years from 2024 through 2025.

Net profit attributable to controlling shareholders was KRW 18.1 billion in 2022, fell to KRW 4.26 billion in 2023 (down 76.5%), then recovered to KRW 26.4 billion in 2024 (up 520.0%) and KRW 28.7 billion in 2025 (up 8.6%), with 2025 the largest year of the 2022 to 2025 period.

Net profit including non-controlling interests was reported as KRW 18.1 billion, KRW 4.26 billion, KRW 26.4 billion, and KRW 28.7 billion for 2022 through 2025. The operating margin moved from 6.2% in 2022 to 2.4% in 2023, then recovered to 6.9% in 2024 and 8.8% in 2025.

The CEO attributed the 2024 increase in a 2025 interview to equipment deliveries to a North American battery joint venture of LG Energy Solution and GM, which raised the weight of the secondary battery business.

Operating cash flow was -KRW 6.2 billion in 2022, KRW 18.2 billion in 2023, -KRW 31.8 billion in 2024, and KRW 15.7 billion in 2025, a wide swing from year to year.

The debt-to-equity ratio fell to 45.8% at the end of 2025 from 94.2% a year earlier, with total equity of KRW 221.8 billion and total liabilities of KRW 101.7 billion.

Quarterly, revenue went from KRW 50.9 billion in the third quarter of 2025, with operating profit of KRW 6.97 billion, to KRW 156.9 billion in the fourth quarter (up 208.2% from the prior quarter), with operating profit of KRW 11.6 billion (up 66.9%).

Revenue then dropped to KRW 14.8 billion in the first quarter of 2026 (down 90.5% from the prior quarter), and the company swung to an operating loss of KRW 4 billion.

In its preliminary first-quarter announcement in May 2026, the company attributed the decline mainly to delivery schedules and revenue recognition on key projects moving into the next quarter.

Second-quarter revenue was KRW 16.5 billion (up 11.1% from the prior quarter), but the operating loss widened to KRW 11 billion and the net loss attributable to controlling shareholders was KRW 7.8 billion.

In its August 2026 second-quarter announcement, the company said revenue recognition timing had been adjusted along with project production and delivery schedules, a feature of the equipment industry.

Third-quarter revenue was KRW 52 billion (up 2.2% from a year earlier), the operating loss narrowed to KRW 4.43 billion from the prior quarter but swung from profit a year earlier, and the net loss attributable to controlling shareholders widened to KRW 21.1 billion.

The net loss attributable to controlling shareholders was far larger than the operating loss in the third quarter, indicating a large share of non-operating items.

Consolidated revenue in the first half of 2026 fell 83.1% from a year earlier, and the cumulative net loss attributable to controlling shareholders over the last four quarters was KRW 13.6 billion.

05

Industry analysis

Avacoco's core end market is OLED investment by display panel makers. In its second-quarter 2026 announcement, the company said it signed a supply contract with LG Display for vacuum deposition and automation systems for OLED production lines, and secured additional supply contracts tied to BOE's follow-on investment.

It also said it will supply core equipment, including Low Damage Sputter and Chuck/Mask Return System, for CSOT's next-generation 8.6-generation OLED investment.

In 2025, brokerage analysts argued that as Chinese panel makers' 8.6-generation OLED investment gathered pace, orders could continue from customers beyond BOE.

On the cycle, a company profile from 2026 describes continued investment adjustment in the display and secondary battery industries, which has weighed on equipment orders. In secondary batteries, analysts in 2025 expected orders to be weak as cell makers slowed their investment.

In positioning, Avacoco relies heavily on orders from Chinese panel makers such as BOE and CSOT, so the pace of Chinese panel investment is the main variable for its order flow.

In new areas, it is evaluating metal sputter equipment for HBM and other AI semiconductor processes with a domestic OSAT company, and it is in technical verification and mass-production discussions with customers for TGV process equipment for glass substrates.

In the end, the OLED investment cycle and the speed of commercialization of new semiconductor and glass substrate equipment will shape its position in the industry.

Outlook

In its third-quarter 2026 preliminary announcement on November 6, 2026, the company said its main projects had entered the final delivery stage and that revenue from those projects is expected to be recognized in the fourth quarter.

In its May 2026 first-quarter announcement, it said the order backlog had already exceeded record levels and that quarterly results would gradually recover as ongoing projects are recognized in sequence.

In its August 2026 second-quarter announcement, it said new and additional orders continued, centered on display equipment, so the company's recovery narrative rests on secured orders.

For TGV process equipment, the company is refining a process solution that links its second-generation laser processing system, AXIUM AP-250K, with plasma-based deposition and etching equipment, and it is in technical verification and mass-production discussions with domestic and overseas customers.

Low Damage Sputter is expanding into electrode processes for OLED and perovskite solar cells. In secondary batteries, a company profile from 2026 says its tandem roll press machine completed development and secured its first order.

In its recent announcements, the company has framed its outlook in terms of order backlog and delivery schedules. The next checkpoint is whether fourth-quarter 2026 deliveries are reflected in the 2026 annual report, which will confirm whether the full-year operating margin recovered.

Bull factors

Order backlog at record levels

In its May 2026 first-quarter announcement and its August 2026 second-quarter announcement, the company said its order backlog was at a record level. In the second quarter it said new and additional orders continued, centered on display equipment.

Because recovery depends on orders converting into revenue, the backlog is presented as the basis for filling the gap in quarterly results.

Earnings recovery through 2025

Operating profit in 2025 was KRW 34.6 billion, up 64.0% from a year earlier, with an operating margin of 8.8%. Revenue and operating profit both increased for two consecutive years from 2024 through 2025. The recovery that began from the 2023 low continued until the swing into losses in 2026.

Expansion into new growth areas

The company said it is pursuing commercialization of TGV process equipment for glass substrates and Low Damage Sputter. Its metal sputter is being evaluated with a domestic OSAT provider for possible use in AI semiconductor processes such as HBM.

The strategy is to extend vacuum and thin-film technology built in display equipment into semiconductor packaging and glass substrates.

Bear factors

Operating losses persist in 2026

The company has recorded operating losses for three consecutive quarters from the first quarter through the third quarter of 2026. Consolidated revenue in the first half of 2026 fell 83.1% year on year.

The third-quarter net loss attributable to controlling shareholders was KRW 21.1 billion, wider than the prior quarter, and the four-quarter cumulative total is KRW 13.6 billion.

Revenue depends on delivery timing

In both the first and second quarters of 2026, the company cited delayed delivery and revenue recognition timing as reasons for weak results. The drop from KRW 156.9 billion in the fourth quarter of 2025 to KRW 14.8 billion in the first quarter of 2026 is a clear example. The company has itself said the equipment industry can produce quarterly earnings volatility.

Dependence on Chinese panel investment and the cycle

In a 2025 interview, the CEO said about 90% of total sales come from exports. A company profile says continued investment adjustments in the display and secondary battery industries have weighed on equipment orders.

If Chinese panel makers' investment schedules slip, both order intake and revenue recognition could be affected.

Risk factors

Customer and regional concentration

Key customers include Chinese panel makers such as BOE and CSOT, and export exposure is high. A change in one customer's investment schedule can move both order flow and revenue timing. Chinese policy and exchange-rate changes are additional variables that could affect the profitability of an export-heavy company.

Quarterly earnings volatility

Quarterly results vary widely, as the gap between KRW 156.9 billion of revenue in the fourth quarter of 2025 and KRW 14.8 billion in the first quarter of 2026 shows. If a delivery slips by one quarter, the sign of operating profit can change.

Whether the fourth-quarter delivery recognition the company has flagged materializes is the item to be verified.

Financial capacity and new business results

The debt-to-equity ratio fell to 45.8% at the end of 2025, but continued losses would shrink equity and could weaken financial capacity. TGV process equipment and Low Damage Sputter are still in customer verification and discussion stages, and no mass-production timing has been disclosed. It may take time before these new businesses translate into revenue.

Overall view

Avacoco's earnings recovered from the 2023 low through 2025, with revenue of KRW 392.9 billion and operating profit of KRW 34.6 billion.

However, it recorded operating losses for three consecutive quarters from the first quarter through the third quarter of 2026, and consolidated revenue in the first half of 2026 fell 83.1% year on year.

The company cites delays in delivery and revenue recognition as the main cause and says its order backlog is at a record level. Strengths include the backlog, the 2025 earnings recovery, and new areas such as TGV and metal sputter.

Weaknesses include quarterly losses, dependence on Chinese panel investment, and a weaker industry cycle. The cumulative net loss attributable to controlling shareholders over the last four quarters was KRW 13.6 billion, so no PER is calculated, and the price-to-book ratio is below 1x.

These are two sides of the same company, and the timing of orders converting into revenue and whether the operating margin recovers will shape how the picture is read. The next checkpoints are fourth-quarter 2026 deliveries and the operating margin in the 2026 annual report.
