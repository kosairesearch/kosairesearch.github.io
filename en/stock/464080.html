Robot LiDAR Supply Grows, Losses Persist

SOS LAB's supply to Hyundai Motor Group robot platforms and its infrastructure orders are expanding, yet the company has posted operating losses for five consecutive quarters from the third quarter of 2025 through the third quarter of 2026.

02

Business structure

SOS LAB develops and manufactures LiDAR sensors. The company was founded in 2016 and listed on KOSDAQ in June 2024. Its core products are the solid-state 3D ML series and the 2D GL series, which the company says are compact and light enough for embedded installation in vehicles and robots.

The business spans robotics, automotive (autonomous driving) and smart infrastructure. A securities report published in December 2024 estimated the 2025 revenue mix at 48% infrastructure, 28% automotive and 19% robotics; these are estimates, and actual segment revenue should be checked against the business reports.

In robotics, SOS LAB supplies LiDAR for the MobED platform from Hyundai Motor Group's robotics unit, and supply for the follow-on PluD platform has also been confirmed, with reports putting the combined scale at roughly KRW 20 billion.

In infrastructure, its LiDAR is applied at airports, rest areas, highways and industrial safety sites. A disclosed exclusive smart-tolling LiDAR contract dated June 16, 2026 is worth KRW 840 million, with E-Traffic as the counterparty.

A supply contract with autonomous-driving company a2z, disclosed September 1, 2026, is worth KRW 714 million. Production and module work was described as running through SL, a Tier-1 parts maker.

Among global peers, Chinese players Hesai and RoboSense and overseas firms Ouster, Luminar and Innoviz are the usual comparisons.

04

Earnings analysis

The annual figures show revenue growth alongside a widening loss. Revenue rose from KRW 4.1 billion in 2023 to KRW 4.94 billion in 2024 (up 20.3% year on year) and KRW 6.09 billion in 2025 (up 23.3%), an increase in each of the two years from 2024 to 2025.

Operating losses were KRW 8.33 billion in 2023, KRW 12.8 billion in 2024 and KRW 16.1 billion in 2025, a loss in each of the three years from 2023 to 2025, with the size of the loss growing every year. The operating margin was -202.9%, -259.9% and -265.1%, so the losses did not narrow even as revenue grew.

Net income attributable to controlling shareholders was KRW 6.02 billion in 2023, turned to a net loss of KRW 12 billion in 2024, and widened to a net loss of KRW 17.7 billion in 2025.

The reasons for the 2023 profit despite the operating loss, which likely lie in non-operating items, should be checked in the business report notes.

The debt-to-equity ratio rose from 30.0% in 2023 and 22.2% in 2024 to 197.1% in 2025, when total liabilities were KRW 52.1 billion against total equity of KRW 26.5 billion. A reported KRW 43 billion convertible bond issue appears linked to the rise in liabilities, though the breakdown needs confirmation.

Operating cash flow was negative in all three years: KRW -8.8 billion, KRW -12.4 billion and KRW -11.3 billion.

Quarterly revenue rose to KRW 2.52 billion in the fourth quarter of 2025, fell to KRW 581 million in the first quarter of 2026 and KRW 456 million in the second, and recovered to KRW 1.39 billion in the third.

Operating losses ranged from KRW 3.52 billion to KRW 5.5 billion over the same stretch, with KRW 4.49 billion in the third quarter of 2026.

The lumpy quarterly revenue appears tied to robot shipment timing; LS Securities said in May 2026 that delayed initial MobED shipments would limit 2026 recognition to about 1,000 units, roughly KRW 4 billion.

A report on the first half of 2025 noted R&D spending of KRW 3.3 billion against KRW 2.1 billion of revenue, about 1.5 times sales.

05

Industry analysis

Market adoption of LiDAR is seen as slower than expected because commercialization of autonomous driving has been delayed. Blott, a Korean tech outlet, identified the slower market opening and intensifying competition as the reasons behind SOS LAB's slowing growth.

On the other side, adoption is rising in robotics and industrial and infrastructure uses such as logistics robots, wafer-handling robots and airport traffic control.

A February 2026 report described SOS LAB as the only listed LiDAR company in Korea chosen as the eyes of Hyundai Motor Group's next-generation robot platform. Because volume production timing of robot platforms drives revenue, Hyundai Motor Group's robot production schedule is the key variable for the upstream cycle.

On competition, Hesai and RoboSense are Chinese, while Ouster, Luminar and Innoviz are US and Israeli, and all serve as global benchmarks. In the December 2024 report's comparison table, these companies were much larger in revenue and operating profit than SOS LAB.

The company's points of differentiation include its solid-state 3D LiDAR design and its in-house SPAD (Single Photon Avalanche Diode) chip development. Korea Investment & Securities projected that SPAD development would finish around mid-2026, and whether it has been completed should be confirmed through disclosures.

06

Outlook

Securities analysts' outlook centers on a return to profitability in 2027. In a May 2026 report, LS Securities forecast a 2027 turn to profit on wider LiDAR supply for Hyundai robots and autonomous driving, with revenue rising sharply from 2028.

The same report expected a decision in the second half of 2026 on whether SOS LAB wins LiDAR supply for a next-generation humanoid from a North American robot maker.

The report also cited an illustrative scenario in which one or two LiDAR units per humanoid at an initial unit price of about KRW 2 million would produce more than KRW 60 billion in humanoid revenue at 30,000 units; this is an estimate built on those assumptions.

In a January 2026 report, Korea Investment & Securities said the PluD volume ramp could enable a profit turn in the second half of 2026, but the third-quarter 2026 operating loss of KRW 4.49 billion means that outlook was not realized by that quarter.

On the commercial side, SOS LAB unveiled new autonomous-driving and smart-infrastructure products and technologies at the ITS World Congress in Gangneung on October 19, 2026.

The a2z contract disclosed in September and the smart-tolling contract disclosed in June broaden the infrastructure and autonomous-driving revenue base. A 2027 profit turn would require quarterly robot volume to stabilize while the operating loss narrows.

The next checkpoints are the North American humanoid award decision and the operating margin in the 2026 annual report.

Bull factors

Wider supply to Hyundai robot platforms

After MobED, LiDAR supply for the follow-on PluD robot platform has been confirmed, and reports put the combined scale of the two platforms at about KRW 20 billion. In May 2026, LS Securities cited wider Hyundai supply as the basis for a 2027 profit turn. Robotics is cited, alongside infrastructure, as a pillar of revenue growth.

Orders and expansion into infrastructure and autonomy

An exclusive smart-tolling LiDAR contract worth KRW 840 million, with E-Traffic as counterparty, was disclosed on June 16, 2026. A KRW 714 million supply contract with a2z was disclosed on September 1, 2026.

In October 2026, the company showcased new autonomous-driving and smart-infrastructure products at the ITS World Congress.

Quarterly revenue recovery and expected cost gains

Third-quarter 2026 revenue of KRW 1.39 billion rose 204.2% from the prior quarter, though it was still 5.5% lower than a year earlier. LS Securities explained that cost reductions from in-house SPAD development could support earnings in 2027.

Bear factors

Five straight quarters of operating losses

Operating losses have persisted for five consecutive quarters from the third quarter of 2025 through the third quarter of 2026, with KRW 4.49 billion in the latest quarter. The net loss attributable to controlling shareholders totaled KRW 23.1 billion over the last four quarters.

A profit turn in the second half of 2026, cited in a January 2026 report, had not materialized by the third quarter.

Balance-sheet strain and convertible bonds

The debt-to-equity ratio jumped to 197.1% at end-2025 from 22.2% in 2024, with total liabilities of KRW 52.1 billion exceeding total equity of KRW 26.5 billion. A reported KRW 43 billion convertible bond carried a zero coupon and zero yield to maturity.

The first-round conversion price was adjusted on October 12, 2026, which could raise dilution if conversions occur.

Gap between forecasts and results

The 2025 revenue estimate of KRW 35.3 billion presented at the IPO compares with actual 2025 revenue of KRW 6.09 billion, a wide gap. Blott reported that R&D spending in the first half of 2025 was KRW 3.3 billion, about 1.5 times revenue. Analysts also point to slower autonomous-driving commercialization and intensifying competition.

10

Risk factors

Customer concentration

As supply to Hyundai robot platforms grows, results depend on those platforms' production schedules and order volumes. LS Securities noted that delayed initial MobED shipments reduce the revenue recognized in the year. A schedule change at a single major customer can swing quarterly revenue sharply.

Funding and dilution

Ongoing convertible bond issuance and conversion price adjustments point to possible further fundraising and a rising share count. Cash keeps flowing out of operations, so operating funds must be covered by external financing. Until cash generation improves, reliance on external funding may remain high.

Technology in-housing and competition

Analysts have noted that a delay in completing the in-house SPAD chip could push back order timing with automakers such as GM and global robot makers. Competition from Chinese LiDAR companies could weigh on pricing and cost structure.

How far the company can overcome the limited field of view of solid-state LiDAR is cited as key to its competitiveness.

Overall view

SOS LAB is a LiDAR sensor specialist, and its supply to Hyundai Motor Group robot platforms along with infrastructure and autonomous-driving orders form the core of the business.

Revenue in 2025 was KRW 6.09 billion, rising for two straight years, but the operating loss of KRW 16.1 billion marked a third consecutive year of losses with a wider deficit.

Third-quarter 2026 revenue of KRW 1.39 billion showed a quarterly recovery but was 5.5% lower than a year earlier, and the operating loss was KRW 4.49 billion.

The bull points are the expansion of robot platform volumes and the 2027 profit turn that analysts have projected, while the bear points are five consecutive quarters of operating losses, a 197.1% debt-to-equity ratio and the conversion price adjustment on convertible bonds.

The price-to-earnings ratio cannot be calculated and the price-to-book ratio sits at a substantial premium to net asset value, so the pace at which the operating loss narrows is the key variable for interpretation.

The next checkpoints are the operating margin and debt structure in the 2026 annual report, and whether a profit appears in the first-quarter 2027 report.
