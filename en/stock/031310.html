02

Business structure

Eyesvision, founded in 1992 and listed on KOSDAQ in 1997, runs the MVNO brand Eyesmobile on leased mobile networks. About 99% of its subscribers sign up through its online mall, and it adds customers through roughly 300 post offices and partner firms. The business has three pillars.

The first is the MVNO service, where co-branded plans tied to Olive Young, Daiso and Ediya Coffee are driving growth. The second is used-phone trading, where exports were widened to Central and South Asia and cumulative 9M 2026 revenue reached KRW 16.8 billion, double the KRW 8.4 billion of a year earlier.

The third is the consolidated subsidiary Mercury, which makes telecom equipment such as AP units and optical cable and has moved into electric motorbikes and battery-sharing stations. Segment mix differs by source.

Butler, using 2Q 2026 revenue, shows telecom equipment manufacturing at 57%, mobile at 37%, environment and safety at 3% and food at 3%. Valueline shows 64.15%, 28.64%, 4.35% and 2.86%, without a stated base date. Both place telecom equipment manufacturing first.

The MVNO business competes for subscribers on plans and networks leased from SK Telecom, KT and LG Uplus. Headcount was 59 as of September 2026.

04

Earnings analysis

Annual revenue moved from KRW 191.6 billion in 2022 to KRW 191 billion in 2023 (down 0.3%), KRW 182.8 billion in 2024 (down 4.3%) and KRW 194.4 billion in 2025 (up 6.3%). 2025 was the largest year and 2024 the smallest among 2022 to 2025.

Operating profit went from KRW 6.34 billion in 2022 and KRW 6.79 billion in 2023 (up 7.0%) to KRW 1.02 billion in 2024 (down 84.9%), then recovered to KRW 3.38 billion in 2025 (up 229.6%). Operating margin was 3.3%, 3.6%, 0.6% and 1.7% from 2022 to 2025, so 2025 has not yet regained the 2023 level.

Attributable net results were a net loss of KRW 5.78 billion in 2022, a net loss of KRW 1.99 billion in 2023 and a net loss of KRW 1.38 billion in 2024, with the losses narrowing each year, before turning to attributable net income of KRW 9.62 billion in 2025.

Net income including non-controlling interests was KRW 8.8 billion in 2025, a different figure. The company attributed the 2025 improvement to higher used-device sales and a stronger MVNO business, and said the disposal of equity stakes in other companies added to net income.

Because that gain is unlikely to recur, the 2025 net result should be separated from the operating trend.

Quarterly revenue rose from KRW 46.6 billion in 3Q 2025 to KRW 64.5 billion in 4Q 2025 (up 38.5% quarter on quarter), but operating profit fell to KRW 1.18 billion (down 26.8%) and attributable results swung to a net loss of KRW 990 million.

In 1Q 2026 revenue fell to KRW 46.7 billion (down 27.6%) and the company posted an operating loss of KRW 16.68 million. In 2Q 2026 revenue was KRW 53.3 billion (up 14.0%) and operating profit was KRW 2.22 billion, but attributable net loss was KRW 3.29 billion.

In 3Q 2026 revenue was KRW 60.1 billion and operating profit KRW 5.32 billion (up 139.5%), while the attributable net loss narrowed to KRW 2.04 billion. The attributable net loss over the last four quarters totals KRW 5.69 billion.

Digital Today reported that the pre-tax result turned to a loss in 3Q 2026, so the operating gain did not carry through to the bottom line.

Cumulative operating profit for January to September 2026 was KRW 7.5 billion on a preliminary basis announced on November 9, 2026, up 242.0%, and the press described this as the largest result since the company joined the Itoday group.

05

Industry analysis

Eyesvision operates in the MVNO market, where operators lease networks from SK Telecom, KT and LG Uplus and compete on lower-priced plans. On the cost side, press reports in November 2026 described continued pressure from rising wholesale network fees and radio-spectrum usage fees.

Against that backdrop the company has relied on lifestyle co-branded plans to add subscribers. Average revenue per user reportedly rose about 3%, from KRW 8,325 in March 2026 to KRW 8,541 in August 2026, per The Bell's November 2026 coverage, so subscriber growth and higher spend per subscriber occurred together.

Its port-in share rose from 7.1% in 1Q 2026 to 8.5% in 3Q 2026, which the company described as among the highest among independent MVNO operators. Scale remains small compared with the three national carriers, which limits its bargaining power on wholesale terms.

The used-phone business faced headwinds from unstable global supply of used devices, higher logistics costs, exchange rates and geopolitical tension in the Middle East, according to The Bell. Valuefinder has argued that growth in optical cable supply for AI data centers at Mercury can support consolidated results.

Where the MVNO market sits in its cycle is hard to call, because it depends on carrier pricing and wholesale policy. Share gains are a positive signal relative to peers, while absolute scale and cost structure remain open issues.

06

Outlook

The company said it expects 2026 used-phone revenue of about KRW 24 billion, announced on November 9, 2026. Cumulative used-phone revenue was KRW 16.8 billion through September 2026, so reaching the full-year estimate would require roughly KRW 7.2 billion more in 4Q 2026.

In March 2026 the company said it would expand its plan portfolio and introduce a subscription-based paid model to raise ARPU and customer lifetime value. In May 2026 it said it would accelerate AI-based service upgrades and links to new lifestyle platforms.

A used-device trading platform was also cited as a growth pillar. For Mercury, the company's 1Q 2026 results release said efficiency measures and a normalization of results began to show in the consolidated statements. Valuefinder expects the expansion of Mercury's optical cable supply to support consolidated results.

These views reflect the company's and an outside analyst's expectations, and no firm contract values or terms have been disclosed. The next checkpoints are whether the used-phone estimate is met in 2026 and whether subscriber growth translates into higher ARPU.

Attention also falls on whether attributable net results move in line with the operating improvement after 4Q 2026.

08

Bull factors

Sharp recovery in operating profit

Operating profit in 3Q 2026 was KRW 5.32 billion, up 229.9% year on year. Cumulative operating profit for January to September 2026 reached KRW 7.5 billion on a preliminary basis announced on November 9, 2026, above full-year 2025 operating profit of KRW 3.38 billion. Operating margin is moving in the same direction, which points to better core profitability.

Subscribers and spend per user rising together

The growth in co-branded plan subscribers came alongside a higher share of port-in new subscriptions. Average revenue per user rose about 3%, from KRW 8,325 in March 2026 to KRW 8,541 in August 2026, so subscriber growth did not come with lower spend per user.

External analysis of asset value

Valuefinder said in a November 2026 report that the Mercury stake alone exceeds the market capitalization, and estimated that listed-company stakes of KRW 44.9 billion plus real estate fund book value of KRW 35.9 billion total about three times the market capitalization. This is an outside estimate, and whether these assets can be realized in cash is a separate question.

09

Bear factors

Recurring quarterly net losses

Attributable net losses were KRW 990 million in 4Q 2025, KRW 3.29 billion in 2Q 2026 and KRW 2.04 billion in 3Q 2026, with two consecutive loss-making quarters since 2Q 2026. Operating gains alongside pre-tax losses raise questions about earnings quality.

Cost pressure and external variables

Wholesale network fees and radio-spectrum usage fees have kept rising, and The Bell reported in November 2026 that logistics costs, exchange rates and unstable global used-device supply weighed on the business. The export-led used-phone strategy is directly exposed to these variables.

Thin margins and reliance on one-off gains

Operating margin was 0.6% in 2024 and 1.7% in 2025, both thin. The company said the disposal of equity stakes in other companies added to 2025 net income. Quarterly volatility is high, including an operating loss in 1Q 2026, so whether the annual profit reflects a stable improvement needs more quarters to confirm.

10

Risk factors

Wholesale policy and cost

Wholesale fees paid to the three national carriers and spectrum usage fees form a large part of MVNO costs. If costs rise faster than prices, the price competitiveness of co-branded plans could weaken. The timing and size of policy changes are hard to predict, so sensitivity of earnings cannot be pinned down.

Used-phone supply and FX

Reports point to used-device supply, logistics costs, exchange rates and Middle East geopolitical risk as factors that could affect export used-phone profits. Because cumulative used-phone revenue of KRW 16.8 billion through September 2026 comes from export channels, sensitivity to these factors is relatively high. The KRW 24 billion full-year estimate could move with them.

Quarterly volatility and small-cap profile

Revenue fell 27.6% quarter on quarter in 1Q 2026 and operating profit turned to a loss, showing how volatile quarterly results can be. With 59 employees, the company is small, so a single quarter can weigh heavily on attributable results. The gap between asset estimates and realized earnings may also contribute to share price swings.

Overall view

Eyesvision's operating profit rose 229.9% year on year in 3Q 2026 as subscriber growth, used-phone exports and a recovery at subsidiary Mercury coincided.

Cumulative operating profit for January to September 2026 of KRW 7.5 billion, on a preliminary basis announced on November 9, 2026, exceeds full-year 2025 operating profit of KRW 3.38 billion.

Yet attributable net results were losses in both 2Q and 3Q 2026, so the operating improvement has not yet reached shareholders' net income. The swing to attributable profit in 2025 included a gain from disposing of equity stakes in other companies, which should be separated when judging the improvement.

An outside analyst's view that held assets exceed the market value is an estimate, and realized value depends on sale timing and taxes.

The bull case rests on subscriber, spend-per-user and used-phone export gains, while the bear case rests on wholesale fee increases, used-phone supply and FX swings, and recurring loss quarters.

The next checkpoints are the 2026 full-year results and the 1Q 2027 results, and the key question at both is whether operating profit stays positive and attributable results move in the same direction.
