Report overview

Profit Recovery Tested by a Weak STS Cycle

Hyundai BNG Steel's revenue fell for three straight years from 2023 to 2025, and although it returned to operating profit in 2024 and stayed profitable through 2025, it posted an operating loss of KRW 532 million in the third quarter of 2026 as the STS market weakened.

02

Business structure

Hyundai BNG Steel operates a single-business model centered on stainless steel (STS) cold-rolled products. The company was founded in 1966 as Samyang Special Steel, came under the Hyundai Motor Group when Hyundai Steel Company acquired it in 2000, and later adopted its current name.

It buys hot-rolled stainless steel from major domestic steelmakers and processes it into cold-rolled products for sale. It ranks second in the domestic stainless cold-rolled market, behind the leading producer.

Annual production capacity is 300,000 tons, supplying appliances, transportation, construction and electrical equipment. The contract-manufacturing agreement with Hyundai Steel Company ended in late September 2023, which the company cited as the main cause of the 2024 revenue decline.

Since then it has cut general-grade output and expanded high-value products such as high-surface-finish processed goods. The Dangjin plant, which handled automotive parts, was reported to be under sale, and the company denied reports that it would exit automotive parts.

As of January 16, 2026, Hyundai Steel Company and three related parties held 45.98% of the shares. Automotive parts demand has been affected by Hyundai Motor and KIA expanding local production in the United States.

Because the business is concentrated in STS cold-rolled products, STS market conditions and downstream demand flow directly into results.

04

Earnings analysis

Annual revenue went from KRW 1.28 trillion in 2022 to KRW 1.05 trillion in 2023 (down 18.4% year on year), KRW 795.4 billion in 2024 (down 23.9%) and KRW 741.3 billion in 2025 (down 6.8%), so revenue has declined for three consecutive years from 2023 through 2025.

Among the years 2022 to 2025, 2022 was the largest year for revenue and 2025 the smallest. Operating profit of KRW 33.5 billion in 2022 turned into an operating loss of KRW 35.2 billion in 2023, then returned to operating profit of KRW 30.6 billion in 2024 before falling 15.5% to KRW 25.9 billion in 2025.

Net profit attributable to controlling shareholders swung from a loss of KRW 30.2 billion in 2023 to a profit of KRW 25 billion in 2024, then declined 40.6% to KRW 14.8 billion in 2025. The operating margin was 2.6% in 2022, -3.4% in 2023, 3.9% in 2024 and 3.5% in 2025.

The company attributed the 2024 return to profit to portfolio restructuring, cost reduction and an emergency management system in place from 2024. The 2025 operating decline was attributed to continued weak demand in downstream industries, while the drop in net profit was linked to equity-method losses from affiliates.

At the end of 2025, total equity was KRW 521 billion and total liabilities were KRW 192.2 billion, and the debt-to-equity ratio fell from 63.5% in 2022 to 36.9% in 2025. Operating cash flow was -KRW 49.1 billion in 2022, then KRW 59.3 billion in 2023, KRW 74.5 billion in 2024 and KRW 63.2 billion in 2025.

Quarterly revenue moved from KRW 193.1 billion in the third quarter of 2025 to KRW 172.2 billion in the fourth quarter (down 10.8% from the prior quarter), with operating profit of KRW 7.79 billion and KRW 5.82 billion.

In the first quarter of 2026, revenue rose 16.0% quarter on quarter to KRW 199.7 billion and operating profit rose 44.1% to KRW 8.39 billion; the second quarter brought revenue of KRW 202.8 billion and operating profit of KRW 9.53 billion.

Third-quarter revenue was KRW 181.5 billion, down 10.5% from the prior quarter and 6.0% from a year earlier, and the company posted an operating loss of KRW 532 million.

Net profit attributable to controlling shareholders also turned to a loss of KRW 1.01 billion, and the four-quarter total from the fourth quarter of 2025 through the third quarter of 2026 is KRW 14.3 billion.

According to a Steel & Metal News report, the third-quarter loss reflected the weak second-half STS market, and the pre-tax loss was larger than the operating loss, which points to deterioration in non-operating items as well.

05

Industry analysis

The STS cold-rolled market weakened visibly in the third quarter of 2026, and analysts say the slowdown was reflected directly in company-level results.

Weak domestic economic conditions and inflows of lower-priced Chinese imports reduced stainless cold-rolled sales, and tariff risk has added uncertainty to the steel market. On the demand side, weakness in appliances, construction and machinery was cited as a reason for the 2023 deterioration.

In automotive, Hyundai Motor and KIA are expanding local production in the United States to reduce tariff burdens, which analysts say is reducing demand for domestic automotive parts.

Industry observers noted that cash and short-term financial instruments fell to KRW 44.1 billion at the end of 2024, the lowest level since 2012, as the prolonged steel downturn weighed on the company.

In competition, the company has said general-grade products face near-perfect competition from low-priced imports, and has stressed differentiation through specialty items such as high-surface-finish processed goods.

With its parent Hyundai Steel Company continuing restructuring, analysts expect asset efficiency and conservative management to continue for now. The key variables for a profit recovery are current STS supply and demand, import flows and price levels.

06

Outlook

The direction management has set out centers on a portfolio shift toward high-value products and cost reduction. A 2025 report expected the company to focus on changing its mix toward high-value products such as high-surface-finish processed goods and on reducing cost of sales.

The company also said it plans to diversify its product lineup around high-quality items and widen its customer base. On assets, the sale of the Dangjin automotive parts plant is being pursued, and the company said it is reviewing various options premised on that sale.

On R&D, a 2024 report said the company had started developing ultra-thin INVAR cold-rolled material for OLED applications.

Because operating profit improved to KRW 8.39 billion in the first quarter and KRW 9.53 billion in the second quarter of 2026, whether the third-quarter loss reflects market conditions will become clearer from later quarterly and annual figures.

The next checkpoint is the annual operating margin in the 2026 annual report. Completion of the Dangjin plant sale and its profit and loss effect are also matters to verify in later disclosures.

If the automotive market and import flows do not change, the key question is how far the shift to high-value products can offset the revenue decline.

08

Bull factors

Operating margin held in the 3% range

The operating margin was 3.9% in 2024 and 3.5% in 2025, staying in the 3% range. Outside tallies based on the second-quarter filing put the second-quarter 2026 margin at 4.7%. Since the return to profit in 2024, the earnings structure has held at a steady level.

Stronger balance sheet

The debt-to-equity ratio fell from 63.5% in 2022 to 36.9% in 2025. Total equity at the end of 2025 was KRW 521 billion and total liabilities were KRW 192.2 billion. Operating cash flow has also stayed positive since 2023.

Shift to high-value products and cost structure

The company attributes the 2024 return to profit from a 2023 loss to a larger share of high-value products, cost reduction and an emergency management system. First-quarter 2026 operating profit rose 44.1% from the prior quarter to KRW 8.39 billion.

Whether avoiding general-grade price competition helps defend profitability is the point to watch in coming quarters.

Bear factors

Weakening revenue base

Revenue declined for three consecutive years from 2023 through 2025, falling to KRW 741.3 billion in 2025. Third-quarter 2026 revenue was KRW 181.5 billion, down 6.0% year on year. Cumulative operating profit through the third quarter also fell 13.4% from a year earlier, based on the disclosure.

Third-quarter swing to loss

The operating loss of KRW 532 million in the third quarter of 2026 followed operating profit of KRW 9.53 billion in the second quarter. Net loss attributable to controlling shareholders of KRW 1.01 billion indicates non-operating losses also added to the pressure. Because earnings reversed direction within one quarter, the following quarters will be important.

Imports and weak demand

Analysts have pointed to weak domestic economic conditions and inflows of low-priced Chinese imports as reasons stainless cold-rolled sales declined. The company attributed 2025 results to continued weakness in downstream demand.

Reports also noted that cash and short-term financial instruments fell to KRW 44.1 billion at the end of 2024, the lowest since 2012.

10

Risk factors

Market and import risk

When STS cold-rolled market conditions weaken, both selling prices and volumes can move down together. Low-priced Chinese imports and tariff uncertainty can limit domestic pricing power. When weak market conditions coincide, as in the third quarter of 2026, the operating margin can fall quickly toward zero.

Parent dependence and concentration risk

Hyundai Steel Company and three related parties hold 45.98% of the shares. The sharp revenue decline after the contract-manufacturing agreement ended in September 2023 shows the risk of reliance on parent-linked volume. The single-business structure also makes results sensitive to changes in one product category.

Investment capacity and asset-adjustment risk

The Dangjin automotive parts plant is being pursued for sale, and the company denied reports that it would exit the business. Industry observers say room for new investment is limited because cash has declined.

One-off gains or losses from asset adjustment and a narrower business scope could complicate future earnings interpretation.

Overall view

Hyundai BNG Steel has moved through a 2023 loss, a 2024 return to profit, a 2025 decline in earnings and a third-quarter 2026 swing to loss within a single-business STS cold-rolled structure.

Annual revenue fell for three consecutive years from 2023, yet the debt-to-equity ratio dropped to 36.9% in 2025 and operating cash flow stayed positive from 2023.

Operating profit improved in the first and second quarters of 2026, but the third quarter brought a weak STS market and an operating loss of KRW 532 million.

Financial strength and a cost structure shifting toward high-value products are positive factors, while rising imports, tariffs and weaker automotive demand that shrink revenue are negative factors.

The price-to-book ratio sits well below 1x, but on earnings the four-quarter total of KRW 14.3 billion is the starting point, and quarterly swings are large. Whether the Dangjin plant sale and the parent's restructuring narrow the business scope is also worth monitoring.

The next checkpoints are the annual operating margin in the 2026 annual report and the first-quarter 2027 results.
