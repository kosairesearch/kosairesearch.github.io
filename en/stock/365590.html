HiDeep: Touch IC Maker Rebuilding Capital Amid Mass-Production Delays

HiDeep's revenue has fallen for three straight years as touch IC mass-production schedules slipped, and its capital base is being rebuilt through shareholder funding and a 2026 rights issue.

02

Business structure

HiDeep designs touch and stylus semiconductor ICs for smart devices and supplies them to OEM customers as a fabless company. Its core lineup centers on touch ICs for smartphones, smartwatches, tablets and notebooks, and the company profile also lists products compatible with casino displays.

The key technology is an independent approach that secures stable touch performance on LTPO-based on-cell flexible OLED panels, which the company profile describes as in mass production. Its product line consists of touch ICs, an integrated touch and stylus solution, and stylus pens.

Of these, the integrated solution is positioned as the next-generation product, and a corporate summary says it passed performance evaluation by a global customer. Mass-production model confirmation, however, is taking time.

Customers are smart device makers, and revenue arises only after a customer adopts the chip and sets a mass-production schedule, so sales are sensitive to adoption decisions and changes in customer strategy.

One press analysis says the company's profitability metrics are markedly below those of its peers, which highlights a gap between technology evaluation and earnings in this business structure.

04

Earnings analysis

Annual revenue fell from KRW 18.1 billion in 2022 to KRW 10.5 billion in 2023 (down 42.2% year on year), KRW 5.89 billion in 2024 (down 43.8%), and KRW 1.77 billion in 2025 (down 70.0%).

Over the same period the operating loss widened from KRW 2.27 billion in 2022 to KRW 8.61 billion in 2023, KRW 9.12 billion in 2024 and KRW 11.7 billion in 2025, and the operating margin moved from -12.5% in 2022 to -664.1% in 2025.

The company attributed the 2025 revenue decline to strategy changes at customers and partners, which partly adjusted mass-production schedules for mobile touch IC and stylus products. Costs did not fall in step with revenue.

Research and development expense was KRW 1.613 billion in Q1 2026 and KRW 1.756 billion in Q2 2026 according to Google Finance data, so most quarterly operating spending appears to be R&D.

On a quarterly basis, the operating loss was KRW 2.29 billion in Q3 2025 and KRW 4.92 billion in Q4 2025, narrowed to KRW 2.82 billion in Q1 2026, and widened again to KRW 2.93 billion in Q2 and KRW 3.1 billion in Q3.

In Q4 2025 the net loss attributable to controlling shareholders, at KRW 7.73 billion, was larger than the operating loss, and Google Finance data show pre-tax losses increasing once specific items are included.

Net loss attributable to controlling shareholders rose from KRW 6.98 billion in 2022 to KRW 9.38 billion in 2023, KRW 9.44 billion in 2024 and KRW 14.8 billion in 2025.

Operating cash flow was negative in every year from 2022 to 2025, at -KRW 3.77 billion, -KRW 3.5 billion, -KRW 3.93 billion and -KRW 7.09 billion, and the 2025 outflow was larger than in 2024.

At the end of 2025, total liabilities were KRW 10.3 billion against total equity of KRW 2.24 billion, and the debt-to-equity ratio was 461.8%, up from 144.2% in 2024.

05

Industry analysis

HiDeep's end market is mobile devices spanning smartphones, smartwatches, tablets and notebooks. The company positions on-device AI and integrated touch and stylus semiconductors for OLED displays as its core areas, and demand in these areas depends on customers' new-product launches and adoption schedules.

Press reports say that delays in global customers' adoption of the next-generation integrated solution weighed on results. In terms of cycle position, revenue has been in a contraction phase since peaking at KRW 18.1 billion in 2022.

Q2 2026 revenue of KRW 25.04 million rose 14.2% from the prior quarter but remains at a low level. Technology has drawn some recognition in the market: the third convertible bond issued in April 2025 carried a 0% zero-coupon structure and a 120% premium to market price, according to press coverage.

Direct comparison with Samsung Electro-Mechanics or LG Innotek is limited because their business scale and customer structure differ. Competitive position depends on design wins at the customer adoption stage, and once a chip is adopted and enters mass production, revenue visibility tends to improve.

06

Outlook

On June 10, 2026, the board resolved a third-party rights issue of roughly KRW 6.5 billion. The new shares are 5,808,766 common shares, and the proceeds are for operating funds of KRW 3.09 billion and debt repayment of KRW 3.5 billion.

The largest shareholder, CEO Ko Beom-gyu, and related parties offset the full KRW 3.5 billion of existing loans against the subscription price, and Mr. Ko additionally paid in about KRW 1.4 billion in cash. Press reports said the offering was completed in June 2026.

Reports also said that total equity returned to positive after the rights issue and the debt-to-equity conversion.

At the August 7 extraordinary shareholders meeting, agenda items included a 2-to-1 share consolidation and an expansion of the limits on new share and mezzanine issuance under the articles of incorporation. The share count was presented as falling from 36,814,550 to 18,407,275.

On the business side, the fact to watch is whether a global customer adopts the integrated solution for mass production. The company has said that finalizing a mass-production model takes time after performance evaluation.

Whether the schedule adjustments that caused the 2025 revenue decline continue in 2026 is the first checkpoint for a revenue recovery. In the 2026 annual report, the next regular filing, the path of annual revenue and the operating margin will be the core earnings test.

08

Bull factors

Mass-production validation and global talks

According to a corporate summary, the integrated touch and stylus solution passed performance evaluation at a global customer, completing mass-production validation, and discussions with the customer have moved into full swing.

The company profile lists its LTPO on-cell flexible OLED touch technology as in mass production. Confirmation of a mass-production model would be the starting point for a revenue recovery, which forms the bullish case.

Progress in restructuring the balance sheet

A third-party rights issue completed in June 2026 raised roughly KRW 6.5 billion, and the largest shareholder's KRW 3.5 billion loan was converted into equity. According to an Etoday report, these steps lifted the company out of the full capital impairment that arose in Q1 2026. Lower borrowing burden is read positively as creating funding room to continue R&D.

Larger stake and cash commitment from the largest shareholder

CEO Ko Beom-gyu's personal stake rose from 31.08% to 36.21% after the capital raise, and including related parties, common shares held reached 49.78%. Direct cash injections and short-term borrowing by the CEO are read as a sign of management's commitment to funding the business. However, with ownership concentrating in one individual, the share held by minority shareholders declines.

09

Bear factors

Prolonged revenue gap

Q1 2026 revenue of KRW 21.93 million fell 89.7% from the prior quarter, and Q2 2026 revenue of KRW 25.04 million edged up but remains far below the KRW 193 million recorded in Q3 2025. At this scale, revenue cannot absorb research spending. If mass-production confirmation keeps slipping, the timing of any revenue recovery remains uncertain.

Widening losses and cash outflow

The operating loss widened to KRW 11.7 billion in 2025, and operating cash outflow of -KRW 7.09 billion was larger than the prior year. With revenue this small, research spending continues as a largely fixed cost, which keeps losses accumulating. As long as cash outflow persists, the company depends on outside funding for longer.

Eroded equity and high leverage

Total equity was KRW 2.24 billion at the end of 2025 against total liabilities of KRW 10.3 billion, with a debt-to-equity ratio of 461.8%, up from 144.2% in 2024. Press reports put total equity at roughly negative KRW 700 million at the end of Q1 2026, a full capital impairment. With a thin equity base, any further losses would increase reliance on external funding.

Risk factors

Listing maintenance requirement

According to a press report, annual revenue of at least KRW 3 billion in 2026 is needed to avoid the risk of designation as an administrative-issue stock. Q1 and Q2 revenue combined fall short of KRW 50 million, so reaching KRW 3 billion would require a sharp increase in second-half sales. Whether the threshold is met becomes clear when the 2026 annual report is finalized.

Dilution and further fundraising

The third convertible bond issued in April 2025 carried a zero-coupon structure, and conversion would reduce existing shareholders' ownership percentage. With operating funding and debt repayment still pending, further rights issues or mezzanine issuance remain possible. The expanded issuance limits in the articles of incorporation would facilitate such financing.

Exchange measures and share-price volatility

On June 15, 2026, the stock was designated an investment-caution stock for one day after a 28.31% rise over three trading days and trading-concentration criteria tied to specific accounts. Such designations are read as signals of short-term overheating, and price swings can widen afterward.

Some analysis also argues that a low foreign ownership base makes supply and demand more easily swayed by specific trades.

Overall view

HiDeep's revenue fell for three consecutive years from 2023 to 2025 as touch IC mass-production schedules slipped on customer-side factors, and its operating losses and net losses attributable to controlling shareholders have been recorded for four consecutive years from 2022 to 2025.

The 2025 operating margin was -664.1% and the debt-to-equity ratio was 461.8%, and total equity was reported to have turned negative at the end of Q1 2026. Reports said the company exited capital impairment after a June 2026 third-party rights issue and the conversion of the largest shareholder's loans into equity.

On the business side, the largest variable is whether a global customer adopts the integrated touch and stylus solution for mass production.

Bullish factors include completed mass-production validation, ongoing global talks, and the largest shareholder's cash participation, while bearish factors include the revenue gap, cash outflow and eroded equity.

The next checkpoint on the filed numbers is the 2026 annual report, where the KRW 3 billion annual revenue threshold and the trajectory of the operating margin will be central.
