Bonne at a Crossroads: Ownership Change Amid Persistent Losses

Bonne, now under new majority shareholder Koo Mi-hyun, the former chairwoman of Ourhome, faces a capital infusion and a governance overhaul even as operating losses have persisted for five straight quarters since turning negative in 2025.

  3. 3

02

Business structure

Bonne is a KOSDAQ-listed cosmetics company built on two pillars: contract development and manufacturing (ODM) and its own brands. The company pursues an asset-light, factory-less ODM model that concentrates on R&D and product planning while outsourcing manufacturing to an external network.

Its core customer base consists of indie beauty brands in the United States and Europe, and in past quarters U.S. exports have accounted for as much as 65-75% of total sales.

Its own brands include the color-cosmetics lines Touch in Sol and Stage Q, which are carried through global retail channels such as Sephora and Ulta.

Per the fiscal 2024 annual report, ODM/OEM sales accounted for a 52% share of revenue (KRW 35.8 billion) versus a 30% share for brand sales (KRW 20.5 billion), with ODM the larger segment. Overseas sales and exports made up 58.1% of revenue (KRW 40 billion) in the same period.

Consolidated subsidiaries include AtoSafe, which handles household goods and detergent products, and a Shanghai-based China unit, with AtoSafe's domestic weakness cited as one driver of the 2025 earnings deterioration.

Competitively, Bonne sits alongside large-scale ODM players such as Cosmax and Kolmar Korea as well as smaller peers like C&C International, with Bonne positioned around small-batch, multi-item production for indie brands.

04

Earnings analysis

Annual revenue rose from KRW 60.7 billion in 2022 to a peak of KRW 72.9 billion in 2023 (up 20.1% year on year), then declined for two straight years to KRW 68.7 billion in 2024 (down 5.8%) and KRW 46 billion in 2025 (down 33.0%).

Operating profit surged from KRW 735 million in 2022 to KRW 6.74 billion in 2023 (up 816.3%), fell to KRW 1.72 billion in 2024 (down 74.4%), and then swung to an operating loss of KRW 8.33 billion in 2025.

Net profit attributable to controlling shareholders went from KRW 986 million in 2022 to KRW 136 million in 2023 (down 86.2%), turned to a net loss of KRW 8.83 billion in 2024, and widened to a net loss of KRW 14.8 billion in 2025.

Net income including non-controlling interests followed a similar path, from KRW 45.83 million in 2022 and KRW 500 million in 2023 to a net loss of KRW 9.38 billion in 2024 and KRW 15.9 billion in 2025.

The operating margin, which had climbed to 9.2% in 2023, dropped to -18.1% in 2025, underscoring a sharp deterioration in profitability.

On a quarterly basis, revenue fell from KRW 12.3 billion in Q2 2025 to KRW 12 billion in Q3 (down 2.5%) and KRW 8.57 billion in Q4 (down 28.7%), rebounded to KRW 9.27 billion in Q1 2026 (up 8.2%), then slipped again to KRW 8.54 billion in Q2 2026 (down 7.8% quarter on quarter and 30.7% year on year).

Operating losses persisted for five straight quarters, from KRW 2.6 billion in Q2 2025 to KRW 2.28 billion in Q2 2026, and net losses attributable to controlling shareholders also stayed in the red for five consecutive quarters, with the trailing four quarters (Q3 2025-Q2 2026) totaling a combined net loss of KRW 21.9 billion.

The company attributed the 2024-2025 downturn to the termination of product contracts with major clients that reduced ODM sales, domestic weakness at subsidiary AtoSafe, and higher marketing expenses tied to expanding new product lines.

05

Industry analysis

Global K-beauty exports have clearly diversified away from China and toward the United States, Europe, and Japan. China's share of Korean cosmetics exports, once above 40%, fell to 24.5% in 2024, while the shares held by the U.S., Europe, and Japan have steadily risen.

The indie-brand-focused ODM market that Bonne serves grew alongside the U.S. consumption recovery in 2023-2024, a period in which Bonne itself expanded revenue by adding new client brands.

However, the order volatility typical of indie brands becomes a structural risk when a company is heavily dependent on a small number of accounts.

Within the KOSDAQ cosmetics sector, large-scale ODM players such as Cosmax and Kolmar Korea have sustained steadier growth on the back of economies of scale and diversified client portfolios, while smaller ODM companies like Bonne tend to see sharper earnings swings when key accounts are lost.

A number of KOSDAQ cosmetics names trade below KRW 1,000 per share, reflecting a pronounced divergence in performance across the sector.

06

Outlook

On July 31, 2026, Koo Mi-hyun, the former chairwoman of Ourhome and a member of the broader LG family, signed a share purchase agreement to acquire a 37.70% stake held by former largest shareholder Lim Sung-ki and related parties, and a new board was installed at an extraordinary shareholders' meeting on September 10.

A disclosure dated October 19, 2026 shows the former largest shareholder's stake had fallen to 0%, indicating the share transfer has been completed.

Separately from the share acquisition, Koo also participated in roughly KRW 22.2 billion in fundraising, comprising KRW 20 billion in fifth- and sixth-series convertible bonds and a KRW 2.2 billion third-party rights offering, providing funds aimed at improving the balance sheet.

However, in August 2026 the company was designated an administrative issue after its half-year report filing was delayed due to an external investigation and an auditor forensic review tied to an internal employee whistleblower report, during which embezzlement and breach-of-trust allegations involving roughly KRW 100 million tied to current and former management were confirmed.

Whether the administrative-issue designation has been lifted following the half-year filing, and whether further accounting issues remain, are matters to confirm through future disclosures.

New business lines in pet supplies, health food, and agricultural import-export that the company had pursued have been on hold since the first half of 2026 with no plan to resume within a year, as disclosed, with the priority instead placed on expanding distribution tied to the existing cosmetics business, including reopening the company's own online mall, expanding online platform sales, and launching new brands.

Whether the shelved new businesses will be revived under the new management, and how the newly raised funds will specifically be deployed, have not yet been disclosed.

08

Bull factors

Fresh Capital From the New Controlling Shareholder

Separately from the control acquisition, Koo Mi-hyun participated in roughly KRW 22.2 billion of fundraising, including KRW 20 billion in fifth- and sixth-series convertible bonds and a KRW 2.2 billion rights offering.

That is a meaningful amount of liquidity relative to the company's total equity of KRW 36.1 billion at the end of 2025. The disclosed plan is to use the funds to strengthen the cosmetics brand business and build a mid- to long-term growth foundation. How quickly and specifically the funds are deployed will be key to any improvement in the balance sheet.

Indie-Beauty ODM Network in North America and Europe

Bonne previously secured a broad base of U.S. and European indie-brand clients that drove revenue growth in 2023-2024. Its relationships with global retail channels such as Sephora and Ulta, along with its small-batch, multi-item production capability, are existing assets that could be leveraged to win new clients.

Overseas sales and exports accounted for 58.1% of revenue in 2024, maintaining a structure weighted more toward overseas than domestic sales. Still, the pace of acquiring new clients and retaining existing accounts remains a variable for any revenue recovery.

Brand and Distribution Realignment

Bonne has outlined plans to expand distribution tied to its existing cosmetics business, including reopening its own online mall, expanding online platform sales, and launching new brands.

Unlike the pet supplies and health food ventures that have been shelved, the cosmetics brand and online distribution business has reportedly continued to expand. If the new management concentrates the newly raised funds on this area, it could become a catalyst for recovering the brand sales mix. However, a concrete execution plan and timeline have not yet been disclosed.

Bear factors

Five Straight Quarters of Operating Losses

Bonne posted operating losses for five consecutive quarters from Q2 2025 through Q2 2026. Net losses attributable to controlling shareholders also persisted for five straight quarters over the same period, with the trailing four quarters totaling a combined loss of KRW 21.9 billion.

The loss narrowed in Q1 2026 but widened again in Q2, so a clear turnaround signal has not yet emerged. The timing of any profitability recovery will need to be confirmed through future quarterly results.

Two Years of Revenue Decline and Client Concentration

Annual revenue declined for two straight years in 2024 and 2025 after peaking in 2023, falling 33.0% year on year in 2025. The company cited the termination of product contracts with major clients as reducing ODM sales as a core cause of the downturn.

Given the nature of indie brands, heavy reliance on a small number of accounts means contract terminations or order cuts can directly hit revenue. If the pace of acquiring new clients fails to offset the loss of existing accounts, the revenue decline could continue.

Administrative-Issue Designation and Internal Control Concerns

Bonne was designated an administrative issue in August 2026 for failing to file its half-year report, with the delay attributed to an external investigation and an auditor forensic review tied to an internal employee whistleblower report.

During that process, embezzlement and breach-of-trust allegations involving roughly KRW 100 million tied to current and former management were reportedly confirmed. Issues of this kind occurring alongside a change in control add to concerns about governance stability.

Whether the administrative-issue designation has been fully resolved and whether further accounting issues exist will need to be confirmed through future disclosures.

Risk factors

Accounting and Internal Control Risk

The delay in the 2026 half-year report stemmed from an external investigation triggered by an internal employee whistleblower report and a subsequent auditor forensic review, which confirmed embezzlement and breach-of-trust allegations of roughly KRW 100 million involving current and former management.

This indicates the external auditor required additional review of the completeness of the financial statements. If similar issues recur or further accounting concerns surface, disclosure credibility could again be affected.

Governance Transition Risk

Following the July 2026 control acquisition agreement, the board and chief executive were replaced at a September extraordinary shareholders' meeting, and an October 19 disclosure confirmed the share transfer had been completed.

The unusual sequence in which management was replaced before the share acquisition was fully completed leaves governance-related uncertainty.

The nature of the investment vehicles that participated in the new fundraising, future changes to the shareholding structure, and the timing of any convertible bond conversion requests are variables to watch given the potential for share dilution.

Client Concentration and Industry Cycle Risk

Bonne's ODM revenue structure relies heavily on a small number of indie-brand clients, and the termination of major client contracts was a direct cause of the 2024-2025 earnings downturn.

Because indie brands can see order volumes shift sharply with trends, the success or failure of a particular client brand adds to earnings volatility. Weakness in the domestic household goods business at subsidiary AtoSafe has also added further pressure to consolidated results.

12

Overall view

Bonne has seen revenue decline for two straight years after peaking in 2023 and net losses attributable to controlling shareholders for two consecutive years in 2024-2025, and it remains in a prolonged downturn in which both operating losses and controlling-shareholder net losses persisted for five straight quarters from Q2 2025 through Q2 2026.

At the same time, a major governance shift has occurred, with Koo Mi-hyun, former chairwoman of Ourhome and a member of the broader LG family, becoming the controlling shareholder in July 2026 alongside roughly KRW 22.2 billion in new fundraising.

However, that process also exposed internal control issues, including an administrative-issue designation tied to the delayed half-year filing and embezzlement and breach-of-trust allegations involving current and former management.

Whether the company's core competitive strengths—its North American and European indie-beauty ODM network and its plan to expand its own brand distribution—will translate the newly raised capital into an actual earnings recovery remains unconfirmed.

Investors should watch the revenue and profit trajectory in the upcoming Q3 quarterly report, whether the administrative-issue designation has been fully resolved, and the progress of follow-up actions from the internal investigation.
