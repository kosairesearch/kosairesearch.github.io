At a Crossroads: Ownership Change Amid Accounting Risk

02

Business structure

Founded in March 2009, Bonne is a cosmetics development and manufacturing company that runs both an OEM/ODM business serving overseas private-label retailers and color-cosmetics brands, and its own brand business.

Its in-house brands include the base-makeup-focused Touch in Sol as well as Stay Youth and Four Hill, distributed through domestic and overseas online channels and retail partners such as AS Watson and SaSa in Hong Kong.

The company operates local subsidiaries in Shanghai, China, and California, United States, which serve as overseas cosmetics wholesale and retail bases.

Beyond its core cosmetics business, Bonne holds a 67.87% stake in household-detergent maker Atosafe and a 78.09% stake in advertising/services firm Cubrick Corporation, reflecting an attempt at diversification through subsidiaries.

As of June 2026 the company had 53 employees, a markedly smaller organizational scale than large ODM peers such as Cosmax (1,578 employees) and Kolmar Korea (1,460 employees).

More recently, Bonne has focused on expanding its own-brand retail channels: from July, Touch in Sol began operating dedicated display counters at major CJ Olive Young Town stores that draw heavy foreign-tourist traffic.

The company has stated a strategy of converting in-store experiences into repeat purchases and overseas sales growth through channels such as Amazon in the United States.

Competitively, Bonne operates in a similar space to large ODM makers like Cosmax, Kolmar Korea, and Cosmecca Korea, but lags them substantially in scale.

04

Earnings analysis

Consolidated revenue rose from KRW60.7 billion in 2022 to KRW72.9 billion in 2023, then declined for two straight years to KRW68.7 billion in 2024 and KRW46.0 billion in 2025.

The operating margin peaked at 9.2% in 2023, slowed to 2.5% in 2024, and turned negative in 2025 with an operating loss of KRW8.3 billion (margin of -18.1%).

Owner net income stayed modestly positive at KRW1.0 billion in 2022 and KRW0.1 billion in 2023, but losses widened to KRW8.8 billion in 2024 and KRW14.8 billion in 2025.

On a quarterly basis, the company posted operating losses for five consecutive quarters from 2025Q2 through 2026Q2 (KRW-2.6 billion, KRW-1.7 billion, KRW-2.6 billion, KRW-2.1 billion, and KRW-2.3 billion, respectively).

Notably, owner net losses in 2025Q4 (KRW-9.4 billion) and 2026Q2 (KRW-9.5 billion) far exceeded the operating losses recorded in those same quarters (KRW-2.6 billion and KRW-2.3 billion), pointing to the likely inclusion of additional non-operating costs or one-off impairment charges.

Operating cash flow also flipped from net inflows of KRW4.6 billion in 2023 and KRW4.3 billion in 2024 to a net outflow of KRW6.1 billion in 2025, indicating that cash generation, not just accounting profit, deteriorated.

On the balance sheet, the debt ratio fell sharply from 182.0% in 2022 to 56.1% in 2025, reflecting a combination of shrinking equity and reduced liabilities.

Overall, the simultaneous contraction in revenue and profitability, together with unusually large net losses in recent quarters, warrants attention to potential non-recurring factors beyond core operations.

05

Industry analysis

South Korea's cosmetics ODM industry has continued to grow in scale on the back of strong K-beauty export demand, led by large players such as Cosmax and Kolmar Korea, but performance has diverged sharply among small and mid-sized firms.

One industry tally comparing fiscal years noted that operating profit at several smaller ODM and brand companies, including Bonne, fell by more than 70%, reporting that "operating profit at SLC (-74.8%), Yejin Cosmetics (-74.5%), Bonne (-74.4%), and Cosbeauty (-71.8%) also fell by more than 70%." This figure is broadly consistent with Bonne's operating profit decline from 2023 to 2024, underscoring a pronounced polarization between large and small players within the sector.

Bonne has pursued a strategy of increasing the weight of its own-brand business alongside its core OEM/ODM operations, but brand businesses carry heavier marketing and channel-investment costs that add to earnings volatility.

Given its smaller scale relative to competitors, Bonne may be at a disadvantage in cost negotiation and production efficiency compared with large ODM makers, and the recent ownership change and accounting issues could also weigh on client confidence.

That said, expanded placement in major domestic retail channels such as Olive Young and efforts to enter overseas online channels represent potential growth avenues available to smaller brand companies.

06

Outlook

The most visible near-term variable is the change of control. Koo acquired 3,162,940 shares held by former CEO Lim Seong-gi and related parties for KRW14.0 billion, representing 37.70% of total outstanding shares.

On September 10, final payment to four related sellers was completed, transferring 16.36% of ownership, but former CEO Lim's own 21.33% stake remains in escrow pending payment on October 12, with the official change-of-control date also moved to October 12.

On the funding side, a KRW2.2 billion third-party rights issue to H Investment Association was completed, and a KRW20.0 billion fifth-and-sixth-round convertible bond issuance is also being pursued, with proceeds earmarked for strengthening the brand business and funding operations.

However, a legal dispute with a minority shareholder led to a one-time delay in the listing of new shares from the third-party rights issue due to an injunction, suggesting that the capital-raising and ownership-transfer timeline could still shift further.

On the accounting front, reports indicate that embezzlement and breach-of-trust allegations worth roughly KRW100 million involving former and current management were confirmed, though the scope identified so far reportedly does not meet the threshold for a trading halt, meaning further investigation results and the auditor's final opinion still need to be confirmed.

On the business front, the performance of Touch in Sol's Olive Young Town displays and its expansion on Amazon in the United States will serve as a test of whether the own-brand business can turn around, and it remains to be seen what concrete management strategy emerges once the new controlling shareholder replaces the board via an extraordinary shareholders' meeting.

08

Bull factors

New Controlling Shareholder's Capital Injection and Potential Balance-Sheet Repair

The Pan-LG group, including former Aourhome Chairwoman Koo Mi-hyun, is injecting additional funds beyond the KRW 14 billion equity stake acquisition, including a KRW 20 billion convertible bond subscription and participation in a rights offering.

As the raised funds are planned to be used for strengthening brand competitiveness and operating capital, completion of the management control transfer could lead to a stabilized financial structure and securing of resources for new investments.

With the debt ratio already having fallen sharply from 182.0% in 2022 to 56.1% in 2025, the additional capital inflow could further ease the financial leverage burden.

Brand Channel Expansion via Olive Young and Amazon

Since July, Touch in Sol has been operating a dedicated display at CJ Olive Young Town stores, which have a high proportion of foreign customers, expanding offline experience opportunities.

The company has stated its strategy to connect this to repeat purchases on overseas online channels such as Amazon in the US, building a virtuous cycle structure. As the proportion of own-brand sales grows, the business structure can be expected to deliver relatively higher margins compared to OEM/ODM.

Risk Diversification via Value-Chain Diversification

In addition to cosmetics ODM, Bonne diversifies its business portfolio by holding household chemical company Atosafe (67.87% stake) and advertising company Cubrick Corporation (78.09% stake).

It also holds overseas subsidiaries in Shanghai, China and the US as distribution bases, a structure that partially diversifies exposure to fluctuations in the cosmetics industry.

Bear factors

Three Straight Years of Revenue Decline and a Swing to Operating Losses

Revenue declined from KRW 72.9 billion in 2023 to KRW 46 billion in 2025, and in 2025 the company recorded an operating loss with an operating margin of -18.1%. Operating losses have continued for five consecutive quarters from Q2 2025 through Q2 2026, and a clear turnaround signal has not yet been confirmed.

Operating cash flow also turned negative to -KRW 6.08 billion in 2025, accompanied by an actual weakening in cash-generating capacity.

Uncertainty Around Large One-Off Net Losses

In Q4 2025 and Q2 2026, net losses attributable to controlling shareholders were -KRW 9.42 billion and -KRW 9.49 billion respectively, significantly exceeding the operating losses for the same quarters (-KRW 2.57 billion, -KRW 2.28 billion).

This suggests the possible reflection of non-recurring factors such as impairment losses in addition to operating profit/loss, and specific causes need to be confirmed through future disclosures. This discrepancy raises questions about the quality and predictability of earnings.

Accounting and Governance Risk, Including Administrative-Issue Designation

Submission of the 2026 semi-annual report was delayed due to an investigation into alleged embezzlement and breach of trust triggered by an internal whistleblower report from employees, leading to designation as an administrative issue stock.

Bonne has previously experienced trading suspension and administrative issue stock designation due to violations of accounting standards, and given that this case appears to be a repetition of a similar pattern, restoring investor trust may take time.

Injunctions and lawsuits filed by minority shareholders related to new share issuance are also acting as an additional variable in the capital raising schedule.

Risk factors

Accounting and Audit Risk

An investigation by an external investigation agency and a forensic verification process by the external auditor, triggered by an internal whistleblower report from employees, are underway, and allegations of embezzlement and breach of trust involving former and current management have been confirmed.

The confirmed scale itself is reported to fall short of the trading suspension threshold, but the possibility of financial statement restatements or additional disclosures depending on the investigation results cannot be ruled out.

Given the past history of trading suspension and administrative issue stock designation due to accounting standard violations, continuous monitoring of recurrence risk is necessary.

Governance and Litigation Risk

The remaining balance for a portion (21.33%) of the shares under the largest shareholder change contract is scheduled to be paid on October 12 via escrow, with additional procedures remaining before the complete transfer of management control.

As injunctions and nullification lawsuits related to new share issuance filed by minority shareholders are ongoing, there is a possibility that the capital raising schedule, including rights offerings and convertible bonds, could be further delayed.

Even after the new management takes office, variables such as continuity with the existing management strategy and the burden of legal dispute costs remain.

Earnings and Financial Risk

Amid continued revenue decline and operating losses, operating cash flow turned negative to -KRW 6.08 billion in 2025, weakening actual cash-generating capacity. Equity capital decreased from KRW 52.8 billion in 2024 to KRW 36.1 billion in 2025, and if losses continue, the trend of capital reduction could persist.

The burden of marketing and channel investment costs associated with brand business expansion may also delay the recovery of profitability in the short term.

12

Overall view

Bonne maintained a profitable trend through 2023, but since 2024, revenue decline and profitability deterioration have become pronounced, with operating losses appearing entrenched in 2025 and the first half of 2026.

In addition, the large net losses in Q4 2025 and Q2 2026 suggest the possibility of overlapping one-off non-operating factors, requiring further confirmation of earnings quality.

At the same time, the company is at a transitional point, with a management change bringing in former Aourhome Chairwoman Koo Mi-hyun of the Pan-LG group as the new largest shareholder and fundraising including a KRW 20 billion convertible bond underway, which could bring changes to the financial structure and business strategy upon completion.

However, governance and accounting risks—including the delayed semi-annual report submission due to alleged employee embezzlement and breach of trust, administrative issue stock designation, and minority shareholder lawsuits—are simultaneously at play, creating schedule uncertainty as well.

Touch in Sol's expansion into Olive Young and Amazon channels is an attempt showing the potential for a rebound in the brand business, but it has not yet been clearly reflected as a turnaround in quarterly results.

Investors should take a sequential approach, checking the completion of the management control transfer on October 12, the timing of resolution of the administrative issue stock designation reasons, and the first quarterly results under the new management system.
