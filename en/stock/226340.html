Bonne: Losses Persist as Control Changes Hands

Bonne has posted operating losses since its 2025 swing into the red as ODM revenue shrank, and a change of control plus about KRW 22.2 billion in new funding now sets up the test of its turnaround.

  2. 2

  3. 3

02

Business structure

Bonne is an asset-light cosmetics ODM company. It has no manufacturing plants, focuses on R&D and product planning, and outsources production to third-party manufacturers.

It supplies base makeup, color cosmetics, hair and body care products to domestic and overseas brand owners, with brand clients in the United States, Europe and China.

The company also runs its own brand, Touch in Sol, which was relaunched as a skincare-focused brand and entered CJ Olive Young online in March 2026 and offline stores nationwide in May.

According to the company, a dedicated display at CJ Olive Young Town stores started in July 2026, targeting areas such as Myeongdong, Gangnam and Hongdae where foreign tourists make up a larger share of shoppers. Its consolidated subsidiaries include Atosafe, an eco-friendly detergent maker.

The 2026 half-year report, however, classified the pet goods, health food and agricultural import-export businesses as provisionally suspended, with no plans to resume within one year.

The main driver of the revenue decline was the end of a major ODM client's product contract, while the in-house brand is in an early channel-expansion phase. Because ODM depends on a small number of clients, a single contract change moves total revenue materially.

Among domestic cosmetics ODM players, large manufacturers such as Cosmax and Kolmar Korea dominate, and Bonne is comparatively small. Low capital needs allow flexible response to new brands, but reliance on outside manufacturers keeps supplier selection and quality and delivery control central to competitiveness.

04

Earnings analysis

Annual revenue rose from KRW 60.7 billion in 2022 to KRW 72.9 billion in 2023, the peak year, then fell to KRW 68.7 billion in 2024 (down 5.8% year on year) and KRW 46 billion in 2025 (down 33.0% year on year), declining two years in a row from 2024 through 2025.

Operating profit grew from KRW 7.35 million... restated correctly: operating profit went from KRW 735 million in 2022 to KRW 6.74 billion in 2023 (up 816.3% year on year), then to KRW 1.72 billion in 2024 (down 74.4%), and turned into an operating loss of KRW 8.33 billion in 2025.

The operating margin moved from 9.2% in 2023 to 2.5% in 2024 and -18.1% in 2025. The company attributed the 2025 decline to lower ODM sales after a major client's product contract ended, weaker domestic sales at its subsidiary Atosafe, and higher marketing costs for new product lines.

Net income attributable to controlling shareholders was KRW 986 million in 2022 and KRW 136 million in 2023 (down 86.2%), then turned into a net loss of KRW 8.83 billion in 2024 and widened to a net loss of KRW 14.8 billion in 2025, two straight loss years.

Reports said the 2025 result also included a KRW 7.9 billion impairment of goodwill and other intangible assets, a non-cash charge. Net income including non-controlling interests was a net loss of KRW 15.9 billion in 2025, which differs from the figure attributable to controlling shareholders.

Quarterly revenue was KRW 12 billion in the third quarter of 2025 and KRW 8.57 billion in the fourth quarter (down 28.7% from the prior quarter), then KRW 9.27 billion, KRW 8.54 billion and KRW 8.9 billion across the first three quarters of 2026, hovering in the upper-80 to low-90 billion won range.

Operating losses over the same five quarters were KRW 1.69 billion, KRW 2.57 billion, KRW 2.09 billion, KRW 2.28 billion and KRW 1.7 billion.

Net losses attributable to controlling shareholders were largest in the fourth quarter of 2025 at KRW 9.42 billion and in the second quarter of 2026 at KRW 9.49 billion, and total KRW 22.1 billion over the last four quarters.

Operating cash flow swung from positive KRW 4.61 billion in 2023 and KRW 4.3 billion in 2024 to negative KRW 6.08 billion in 2025. The debt-to-equity ratio fell from 182.0% in 2022 to 56.1% in 2025.

05

Industry analysis

Cosmetics ODM and OEM earnings move with brand owners' launch and order schedules. Bonne's revenue decline was driven largely by the end of one major client's product contract, so company-specific factors should be separated from sector-wide trends.

Domestically, placement on channels such as CJ Olive Young largely determines brand visibility and repeat purchases, which explains why Bonne is using Touch in Sol to expand distribution. Overseas, the company has cited sales through US online channels, its overseas brand site and social media as future priorities.

A larger share of sales from districts with heavy foreign tourist traffic also brings more exposure to external swings. In competition, large manufacturers such as Cosmax and Kolmar Korea hold the top tier of ODM, while large brand owners such as Amorepacific, LG H&H and APR control channel leadership.

Bonne is much smaller than these players, so its earnings are sensitive to changes in individual client contracts. Some observers see the small scale as allowing faster addition of new brands and more flexible contract structures.

The indicators to watch for a recovery are new ODM contract wins, the share of revenue from the in-house brand and the burden of selling and administrative expenses.

06

Outlook

Management has not yet disclosed detailed medium-term plans, and reports said a clearer strategy is expected after the new management team is formed. Gu Mi-hyun agreed to buy the former controlling holders' stake for KRW 14 billion and to subscribe in full to a KRW 10 billion fifth private convertible bond issue.

A KRW 10 billion sixth convertible bond and a KRW 2.2 billion rights-type capital raise were also reported as planned. The proceeds are to be spent in stages on brand development, marketing, domestic and overseas distribution, raw material purchases and operating costs.

Cash and equivalents were KRW 5.4 billion at the end of the first quarter of 2026, and reports expected that figure to rise to the KRW 27 billion range once the new capital arrived.

Injected capital is a separate matter from operating profitability, so any profit recovery will depend on winning new ODM contracts and controlling selling and administrative costs.

The company bought 423,545 treasury shares, or 5.05% of the total, on the open market between April and July 2026, at a total cost of KRW 999.97 million. A stock consolidation at a 5-to-1 ratio was completed in May 2026.

The Korea Exchange designated the stock as an investment-warning issue on August 5, 2026, citing a sharp short-term rise.

Points to watch are whether new ODM clients are secured under the new control structure, how much Touch in Sol's CJ Olive Young channel contributes to sales, and whether the suspended businesses are revived.

None of these has yet been settled in the disclosed figures, so they should be tracked through the next periodic report and ad hoc disclosures.

Bull factors

Change of control and new capital

Gu Mi-hyun agreed to buy the former controlling holders' stake for KRW 14 billion and to subscribe in full to a KRW 10 billion convertible bond.

If the funding plan is executed, about KRW 22.2 billion in new capital would arrive, and reports expected cash to rise sharply from KRW 5.4 billion at the end of the first quarter of 2026. That would ease short-term liquidity pressure and create room for brand marketing and distribution spending.

Wider channel reach for the in-house brand

Touch in Sol entered CJ Olive Young online in March 2026, went into nationwide offline stores in May, and began running a dedicated display at Olive Young Town stores from July 2026. The placement in districts with heavy foreign tourist traffic targets domestic shoppers and overseas visitors at once.

Whether the in-house brand can grow its revenue share, reducing reliance on ODM clients, is the next thing to watch.

Asset-light model with low capex

Bonne owns no production plants, concentrates on R&D and product planning, and outsources manufacturing. Commentators say the low capital burden allows fast responses to market shifts and flexible development of new brands. The debt-to-equity ratio also fell from 182.0% in 2022 to 56.1% in 2025, a sign of easing financial strain.

Bear factors

Revenue decline and year-on-year contraction

Revenue fell for two years in a row, from 2024 and through 2025, when it dropped 33.0% to KRW 46 billion. Third-quarter 2026 revenue of KRW 8.9 billion rose from the prior quarter but was down 26.0% year on year.

The company attributed the contraction mainly to the end of a major ODM client's product contract, and the basis for a revenue recovery is not yet visible.

Consecutive losses and weaker cash flow

Operating losses have continued for five straight quarters through the third quarter of 2026, and net losses attributable to controlling shareholders total KRW 22.1 billion over the last four quarters. Operating cash flow flipped from a positive KRW 4.3 billion in 2024 to negative KRW 6.08 billion in 2025. The operating margin fell to -18.1% in 2025, pointing to weaker earnings power in the core business.

Suspended businesses and an unsettled strategy

The 2026 half-year report classified the pet goods, health food and agricultural export businesses as provisionally suspended, with no plans to resume within one year. At the time of reporting, the company said no specific medium-term plans had been set under the new management.

If diversification remains paused and ODM clients are not regained, the route to an earnings rebound stays narrow.

Risk factors

Funding and share dilution

A sixth convertible bond and a rights-type capital raise were reported as planned. Conversion can dilute existing shareholders, and reports said the conversion price was set below the share price at the time of the control-deal announcement. If the planned funds arrive late or losses continue, further financing needs could build up.

Governance and internal controls

Reports described an earlier case in which securities regulators referred the company and its then-CEO to prosecutors and imposed a fine and recommended removal of an executive. The company later replaced its CEO and board and reorganized its internal controls.

With the controlling shareholder changed, transparency and disclosure practice under the new structure are key items to check.

Share price volatility and market alerts

The Korea Exchange designated Bonne an investment-warning stock on August 5, 2026, after a sharp short-term rally that included four straight limit-up sessions following the control-deal announcement.

The 52-week beta is in the three range, so the shares tend to move far more than the market and price swings can occur regardless of earnings.

Overall view

Bonne's revenue peaked at KRW 72.9 billion in 2023 and fell to KRW 46 billion in 2025, when it swung to an operating loss of KRW 8.33 billion, and it has remained loss-making for five straight quarters through the third quarter of 2026.

Net losses attributable to controlling shareholders total KRW 22.1 billion over the last four quarters, and operating cash flow turned negative again in 2025.

On the other side, the change of control, the planned KRW 22.2 billion in new funding, the asset-light ODM model and the channel expansion of Touch in Sol are cited as the basis for a restructuring. The debt-to-equity ratio fell from 182.0% in 2022 to 56.1% in 2025, which also eases financial strain.

Whether the funding is completed, and whether operating profit recovers once it arrives, remain unconfirmed. With no earnings-based multiple available, valuation is read mainly against book value and the funding structure.

The next checkpoints are the preliminary fourth-quarter 2026 results and the operating margin in the 2026 annual report, and winning new ODM contracts is the variable that will decide the direction of earnings.
