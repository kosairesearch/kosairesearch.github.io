02

Business structure

NSCompany began in 1994 as Seohae Plant (the filing name is rendered here in translation). It absorbed Seil Industrial in 2011, changed its name to the current one and listed on Konex in 2015.

Its core business is piping, tank, equipment, firefighting, electrical, steel structure and insulation work at industrial facilities such as thermal and nuclear plants, LNG terminals, refineries and steel mills.

The project table on the company website lists Daewoo Engineering & Construction, Hyundai Engineering & Construction, GS Engineering & Construction and KUMHO Engineering & Construction among its main contractors.

Alongside construction it runs power plant maintenance and auto parts manufacturing, including front knuckles and rear carriers. Auto parts are produced at the Gyeongju plant, but construction accounts for the larger share of the company overall.

Based on revenue composition compiled by JobKorea for December 2025, other construction work was 41%, auto parts 16%, the Dangjin LNG tank project 16% and the SATORP Saudi Amiral package 10%.

Unlike large EPC firms such as Samsung E&A, NSCompany operates more as a specialist installation and maintenance partner than as a prime contractor. Its revenue is therefore read as depending heavily on prime contractors' project schedules and clients' investment plans. The company had 188 employees as of December 31, 2025.

03

Recent trends

Revenue in 2025 was KRW 71.6 billion (71.6 billion won in the filing unit), up 0.3% year on year and effectively flat. The operating loss was KRW 6.3 billion, reversing the KRW 0.5 billion operating profit of 2024, and the operating margin was -8.78%.

Gross profit was negative by about KRW 1.0 billion, so losses arose even before selling and administrative expenses. The net loss was KRW 3.0 billion, smaller than the operating loss, and the loss per share was KRW 758. Non-operating income of about KRW 3.2 billion accounts for the difference.

Results have swung widely: 2023 brought revenue of KRW 45.5 billion and an operating loss of KRW 9.1 billion, 2024 an operating profit, and 2025 an operating loss again.

At the end of 2025, total assets were KRW 73.6 billion, total liabilities KRW 39.5 billion and equity KRW 34.1 billion, for a debt-to-equity ratio of 115.79%. Borrowing dependence rose from 11.27% in 2024 to 20.39% in 2025.

In 2025, cash flow from operating activities was an outflow of about KRW 5.1 billion and cash flow from investing activities an outflow of about KRW 9.8 billion. On the intraday quote of October 13, 2026, the price was KRW 2,480 and market capitalization KRW 9.8 billion, with zero volume and turnover.

The 52-week range as of July 22 was KRW 2,090 to KRW 4,250. On April 1 the shares closed at KRW 3,305, up 14.76% from the prior day, and Bloomberg's quote shows KRW 2,605 on June 5, down 14.59%. The company held its 32nd annual general meeting on March 31, 2026. Quarterly results for 2026 could not be confirmed through search.

04

Outlook

The Construction & Economy Research Institute of Korea projected 2026 domestic construction orders at KRW 231.2 trillion (+4.0%) and construction investment at KRW 270.0 trillion (+2.0%). In the public sector it expects the 2026 SOC budget to rise 7.9% and civil engineering orders to grow 5.8%.

The institute nonetheless described the outlook as a base effect from the sharp 2025 decline, with limited scope as a genuine recovery. Cumulative building starts in the first eight months of 2025 were down 16% from a year earlier. Weak starts can feed through to progress billings and construction volumes with a lag.

The Research Institute of Construction Policy said 2026 construction investment would recover only by about 2% and that a full rebound needs more time.

NSCompany derives more of its work from power, energy and industrial facilities than from housing, so it is tied more directly to clients' capital spending and prime contractors' order flow than to the housing cycle.

How much of a sector-wide order recovery the company captures must be checked against individual contract disclosures and quarterly results. On operating metrics, room for improvement depends on whether it can recover from the -8.78% operating margin and negative gross profit of 2025. Items to track are new orders and backlog, site-level cost ratios, borrowing trends and operating cash flow.

05

Bull factors

Revenue base maintained near KRW 70 billion

Revenue grew from KRW 45.5 billion in 2023 to KRW 71.4 billion in 2024 and KRW 71.6 billion in 2025, staying near KRW 70 billion for two straight years. This indicates order activity continued despite the loss. A maintained revenue scale gives a base for earnings to recover if cost ratios improve. The size of any improvement depends on site-level cost control.

Track record with major contractors

The company's project table lists nuclear, thermal and LNG facility work for clients such as Korea Hydro & Nuclear Power and the Korea Gas Corporation, with Daewoo Engineering & Construction, Hyundai Engineering & Construction and GS Engineering & Construction as prime contractors.

This shows a history of taking part in large projects as a partner. The auto parts segment accounted for 16% of revenue, which partly offsets swings in construction orders.

Gap between book value and market value

Equity at the end of 2025 was KRW 34.1 billion, and book value per share was more than three times the current price. Market capitalization is KRW 9.8 billion. The current price of KRW 2,480 is below book value per share. The gap can be read as a discount to asset value, but it can equally reflect the loss and thin liquidity.

06

Bear factors

Losses arising at the cost level

Gross profit in 2025 was negative by about KRW 1.0 billion. The operating loss was KRW 6.3 billion and the operating margin was -8.78%. A loss before selling and administrative expenses suggests cost overruns at some sites or low-margin contracts.

With the 2024 operating profit at only KRW 0.5 billion, it is hard to say that profitability had been established.

Rising financial burden

The debt-to-equity ratio rose each year, from 71.51% in 2023 to 86.82% in 2024 and 115.79% in 2025. Borrowing dependence reached 20.39% in 2025, nearly double the 11.27% of 2024. Operating activities drew down about KRW 5.1 billion in cash while investing activities used about KRW 9.8 billion. If earnings do not recover, the borrowing burden could grow.

Thin liquidity and large price swings

On the intraday quote of October 13, 2026, both volume and turnover were zero. On April 1 the shares rose 14.76% in a day, and Bloomberg's quote shows a 14.59% decline on June 5. In thinly traded stocks even small orders can move the price sharply, and trades may be hard to execute at the desired time.

07

Risk factors

Macro

Cumulative building starts in the first eight months of 2025 fell 16% from a year earlier, which weighed on construction progress and delayed the investment recovery.

The Research Institute of Construction Policy said favorable signals such as expected rate cuts and reduced project-financing uncertainty coexist with weak starts and regional polarization that constrain recovery. A slower sector recovery could prolong order delays and price competition.

Business

Orders depend on subcontracting and partnership arrangements with large contractors, so schedule changes or reduced orders at a prime contractor directly affect revenue. The revenue mix has large weights in other construction work, the Dangjin LNG tank project and the SATORP Saudi package.

Cost overruns or delays at a single site can swing annual earnings materially. The gross loss in 2025 illustrates this volatility.

Market and regulatory

Konex is a market for early-stage small companies, so liquidity and information access are more limited than on KOSPI or KOSDAQ. The stock had a trading suspension lifted on June 28, 2021, together with a decision to maintain its listing.

A January 2019 report put its Konex market capitalization at KRW 216.7 billion at that time, compared with KRW 9.8 billion in October 2026. With the company's market value much smaller, listing maintenance requirements and disclosure obligations warrant continued monitoring.

08

Overall view

NSCompany is a Konex-listed company that combines industrial facility construction, power plant maintenance and auto parts. Revenue held near KRW 70 billion, but the company swung to an operating loss of KRW 6.3 billion in 2025.

The bullish case rests on the maintained revenue scale, a track record with large contractors and the gap between book value and market capitalization. The bearish case rests on losses at the gross profit level, rising leverage and borrowing dependence, cash outflows and thin trading liquidity.

On the sector, the Construction & Economy Research Institute of Korea projects 2026 construction investment up 2.0%, while noting a large base effect and continued weakness in building starts.

Quarterly results for 2026 and new order disclosures could not be confirmed, so evidence on whether earnings are recovering is incomplete. Items to monitor are a return to a positive operating margin, cost ratios, backlog, cash flow and borrowing trends.
