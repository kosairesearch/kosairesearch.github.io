Slow Cement Demand Keeps Asia Cement's Earnings Under Review

Revenue has fallen for two straight years since peaking in 2023, and operating profit dropped to KRW 77.1 billion in 2025. Whether cement shipments recover at a meaningful pace is the next checkpoint for earnings.

02

Business structure

Asia Cement is a cement-led construction materials maker that also runs ready-mix concrete and dry mortar businesses and a venture capital subsidiary. JobKorea's company profile shows cement at 77% of sales, ready-mix concrete at 9%, other businesses at 5%, and real estate leasing and services at 4%.

According to WiseReport, its market share as of June 2026 was 7.30% in domestic cement, 15.00% in dry mortar and 2.00% in domestic ready-mix concrete.

A group company profile cites annual capacity of about 12.4 million tonnes across its Okgye plant and three plants in Gwangyang, Incheon and Pohang, while an outside corporate analysis site cites about 9 million tonnes, so the filed plant data should be checked.

Domestic sales were 95.85% of cement sales in 2025, with exports at 4.15%. On costs, a corporate analysis site reports that the alternative fuel substitution rate reached 50% in 2024, roughly halving the use of bituminous coal.

A KRW 36.2 billion SCR system at the Jecheon plant is in operation and is designed to cut nitrogen oxide emissions by more than 90%. Its main domestic rivals include HANIL CEMENTCO and SAMPYO Cement, and its first-half 2026 operating profit decline was the largest among the five major producers.

04

Earnings analysis

Annual results from 2022 to 2025 are taken from regular filings. Revenue rose from KRW 1.04 trillion in 2022 to KRW 1.2 trillion in 2023 (up 15.4%), the peak year, then fell two years in a row to KRW 1.11 trillion in 2024 (down 7.5%) and KRW 1.02 trillion in 2025 (down 7.9%).

Operating profit moved from KRW 146.9 billion in 2023 (up 24.5%) to KRW 140.8 billion in 2024 (down 4.2%) and KRW 77.1 billion in 2025 (down 45.2%), the lowest year in the period.

Profit attributable to controlling shareholders fell from KRW 89.6 billion in 2023 to KRW 81.4 billion in 2024 and KRW 16.9 billion in 2025 (down 79.2%), while net income including non-controlling interests was KRW 16.4 billion in 2025, a different measure.

Operating margin rose to 12.7% in 2024 from 12.2% in 2023 even as revenue declined. That margin gain is consistent with cost improvements and greater alternative fuel use, but the filings alone do not separate the contribution of each factor.

The 2025 margin of 7.5% was the lowest in the 2022 to 2025 window, versus 11.3% in 2022. Operating cash flow fell from KRW 198.4 billion in 2023 to KRW 93.4 billion in 2025, less than half the earlier level.

The debt-to-equity ratio eased from 108.4% in 2022 to 97.8% in 2025, and year-end 2025 total equity was KRW 1.1 trillion, of which KRW 1.09 trillion belonged to controlling shareholders.

Quarterly results show fourth-quarter 2025 operating profit of KRW 7.57 billion (down 60.1% from the prior quarter) and a controlling-shareholder net loss of KRW 16.2 billion, a swing into deficit.

Operating profit recovered to KRW 11.4 billion in the first quarter of 2026 and KRW 23.6 billion in the second quarter, then fell to KRW 8.18 billion in the third quarter (down 56.9% year on year).

The first-quarter revenue gain was attributed to higher clinker output at a cement subsidiary and wider share for slag cement. Controlling-shareholder net income for the most recent four quarters totaled KRW 3.71 billion.

05

Industry analysis

Domestic cement shipments have stayed well below the level above 50 million tonnes seen in 2023. The Korea Cement Association's medium-term outlook puts 2026 shipments at 38.71 million tonnes, up only 1.2%, and 2027 at 39.65 million tonnes, up 2.4%.

Shipments are projected at 39.68 million tonnes in 2028, with a return above 40 million tonnes not expected until 2029 at 40.99 million tonnes. Even in 2030, projected shipments are about 17% below the 2023 level. The industry recorded its lowest shipment volume on record in 2025.

Industry capital spending plans for 2026 total KRW 429.7 billion, about 10% lower than the prior year, and have declined for two consecutive years. The industry warns that slower volume recovery could disrupt multi-thousand-billion-won circular resource investment plans.

On costs, bituminous coal, which makes up 30% of manufacturing costs, was reported up 18% from the start of the year, and Middle East tensions have disrupted petrochemical feedstock supply.

A Samyang-linked cement executive, from SAMPYO Cement, said 2026 new housing sales rose 43% to 310,000 units, but construction starts lead cement demand by a lag, so a full earnings turnaround could come only from 2027. In contrast, HANIL CEMENTCO's first-half 2026 revenue fell 5.9% while its operating profit rose 21.9%.

06

Outlook

In its March 27, 2026 corporate value enhancement plan, the company said it would keep dividends stable within at least 40% of standalone net income and would execute shareholder returns flexibly in light of operating results and cash flow.

It has planned environmental and safety equipment investments of KRW 15 billion each in 2026 and 2027. Raising the share of circular resource fuel is positioned as a central lever for cost competitiveness. Industry forecasts center on a 2027 shipment rebound.

NH Investment & Securities, in a late-July 2026 report, lowered its 2026 operating profit estimate to KRW 88.1 billion and projected KRW 156.8 billion for 2027, citing a slowdown in construction starts after the second quarter and weak public housing starts.

One industry view holds that if first-half 2026 construction starts translate into higher shipments, fixed cost absorption and greater alternative fuel use could improve profitability together.

The next filings to check are the preliminary fourth-quarter 2026 results, which are likely in early February 2027 based on the timing of the prior year's preliminary release, and the 2026 annual business report, which is expected around mid-March 2027. The first test is whether the full-year 2026 operating margin rises above the 7.5% recorded in 2025.

08

Bull factors

A 2027 shipment rebound and earnings turnaround

The Korea Cement Association projects 2027 shipments at 39.65 million tonnes, up 2.4% from 2026. A SAMPYO Cement executive said a full earnings turnaround could come from 2027 given the lag between construction starts and cement use.

NH Investment & Securities estimated 2027 operating profit at KRW 156.8 billion, up 78%, in a late-July 2026 report.

Cost structure built on alternative fuel and environmental capex

The company put KRW 36.2 billion into an SCR system at its Jecheon plant and has planned an additional KRW 15 billion in environmental and safety investment in each of 2026 and 2027. A corporate analysis site reports a 50% substitution rate in 2024, roughly halving the use of bituminous coal.

The 12.7% operating margin in 2024 was the highest in the four-year filing window, and the gap to 7.5% in 2025 is the item to check in the next annual report.

Shareholder return policy and repeated share cancellations

The March 2026 corporate value enhancement plan aims to keep dividends stable within at least 40% of standalone net income. The 2026 annual meeting approved cash dividends, and interim dividends are also being paid.

Share cancellations out of retained earnings were recorded five times between December 2024 and June 2026, and NH Investment & Securities described the shareholder return policy as a positive for limiting downside pressure on the stock.

09

Bear factors

Slow shipment recovery and a shallow rebound

The Korea Cement Association's outlook puts 2026 shipment growth at only 1.2%. Even in 2030, projected shipments of 41.9 million tonnes sit about 17% below the 2023 level. The association's outlook implies that even when demand rebounds, it will take considerable time to return to earlier levels. The fact that 2025 recorded the lowest shipment volume on record means the recovery starts from a low base.

Cost pressure and a steep first-half profit decline

First-half 2026 operating profit was KRW 35.0 billion, down 30.8% year on year, the steepest drop among the five major cement producers in a November 2026 report. Bituminous coal, which accounts for 30% of manufacturing costs, was reported up 18% from the start of the year. A SAMPYO Cement executive said cost pressure from oil prices and exchange rates could persist.

Shrinking profit base and quarterly volatility

Profit attributable to controlling shareholders was KRW 16.9 billion in 2025, down 79.2% from the prior year. The company swung to a controlling-shareholder net loss of KRW 16.2 billion in the fourth quarter of 2025, and third-quarter 2026 controlling-shareholder net income was KRW 632 million, down 92.7% year on year. The four-quarter total of KRW 3.71 billion shows how thin the current profit base has become.

10

Risk factors

Construction cycle and housing starts

NH Investment & Securities expects the pace of housing start recovery to slow after the second quarter. Weak public housing starts and recent base rate increases were cited as factors that could slow the pace of earnings recovery. The same report noted that direct LH housing supply was expected to gain momentum from the second half of 2026.

Coal, exchange rate and petrochemical feedstock

Bituminous coal accounts for 30% of manufacturing costs and was up 18% from the start of the year. Supply disruptions in naphtha and PVC linked to Middle East tensions were flagged as a risk to production.

If cost increases are not passed through to selling prices, the operating margin could fall below the 7.5% recorded in 2025.

Environmental regulation and capex burden

Following its alternative fuel conversion and SCR system, the company has planned an additional KRW 15 billion investment in each of 2026 and 2027. Industry concern is that slower shipment recovery could disrupt capex plans across the sector.

Whether this spending delays the earnings recovery should be checked against capex execution in the annual business report.

Overall view

Asia Cement's revenue peaked at KRW 1.2 trillion in 2023 and then fell for two consecutive years, while 2025 operating profit of KRW 77.1 billion was the lowest in the filing window.

The 12.7% operating margin in 2024 is consistent with cost improvements and greater alternative fuel use, but the margin fell to 7.5% in 2025. In 2026, the first and second quarters showed recovery, but third-quarter operating profit of KRW 8.18 billion was down 56.9% year on year.

On the industry side, the association's outlook puts 2026 shipment growth at only 1.2%, with a return above 40 million tonnes not seen until 2029.

Strengths include a possible 2027 shipment rebound, a cost structure supported by alternative fuel, and a shareholder return policy anchored to at least 40% of standalone net income along with a record of share cancellations.

Weaknesses include the 30.8% first-half operating profit decline, rising coal prices, the sharp drop in controlling-shareholder net income, and a thin four-quarter profit base of KRW 3.71 billion.

The next checkpoints are the preliminary fourth-quarter 2026 results, the operating margin in the 2026 annual business report, and first-half 2027 shipment trends.
