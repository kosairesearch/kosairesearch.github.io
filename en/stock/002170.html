02

Business structure

SYTS was founded in 1957, manufactures and trades leather materials, and has been listed on the KOSPI market since November 1989. According to Alphasquare's summary, leather operations account for 98.60% of sales, led by cowhide-based products, while rental income makes up 1.40%.

Main revenue comes from the sale of leather products for vehicles, so the leather segment carries most of the business. Concentration in one segment means that changes in car production and leather demand feed directly into company results.

The rental segment is in the low single digits of sales, so its effect on earnings is limited. Raw cowhide procurement costs and processing costs are the main drivers of profitability. Alphasquare's company profile describes a stable market share in the domestic leather material market.

Same-sector listed peers include Chokwang Leather, creating a competitive field in leather materials. On an annual basis for 2025, Alphasquare's industry comparison shows the operating margin above the average of the apparel and fashion accessories sector.

As of the March 2026 annual general meeting, the board has five members, three of them outside directors, so the outside director ratio is 60%.

04

Earnings analysis

Annual revenue in 2022-2025 was KRW 178.8 billion, KRW 179.3 billion, KRW 191.3 billion and KRW 174.7 billion. The largest year was 2024 at KRW 191.3 billion, and the smallest was 2025 at KRW 174.7 billion, down 8.7% from the prior year.

Operating profit went from KRW 16.4 billion in 2022 to KRW 17.7 billion in 2023 (up 7.5%) and KRW 20 billion in 2024 (up 13.1%), then fell to KRW 18.4 billion in 2025 (down 7.8%). The largest operating profit year was 2024 and the smallest was 2022.

The operating margin rose from 9.2% in 2022 to 10.6% in 2025, as operating profit fell less than revenue in 2025. Net income attributable to controlling shareholders was KRW 15.1 billion in 2022, KRW 21.6 billion in 2023 (up 43.3%), KRW 11 billion in 2024 (down 49.1%) and KRW 24 billion in 2025 (up 117.6%).

The 2025 rise came while operating profit declined, and the company attributed it mainly to a smaller financial asset valuation loss and a larger valuation gain. Operating cash flow rose from KRW 5.67 billion in 2022 to KRW 25.4 billion in 2023, KRW 34.7 billion in 2024 and KRW 34.5 billion in 2025.

At the end of 2025, total equity was KRW 453.5 billion, all of it attributable to controlling shareholders, and total liabilities were KRW 20.8 billion, with the debt ratio falling from 9.7% in 2022 to 4.6% in 2025.

Quarterly revenue was KRW 40.3 billion in the third quarter of 2025, KRW 40 billion in the fourth quarter (down 0.7% quarter on quarter), KRW 41.3 billion in the first quarter of 2026 (up 3.0%), KRW 35.3 billion in the second quarter (down 14.3%) and KRW 39.3 billion in the third quarter (up 11.2% quarter on quarter and down 2.6% year on year).

Quarterly operating profit peaked at KRW 4.47 billion in the fourth quarter of 2025 (up 10.8% quarter on quarter), then fell to KRW 2.15 billion in the first quarter of 2026 (down 52.0%), KRW 1.61 billion in the second quarter (down 24.9%) and KRW 2.2 billion in the third quarter (up 36.3% quarter on quarter but down 45.6% year on year).

Net income attributable to controlling shareholders rose to KRW 6.48 billion in the second quarter of 2026 (up 107.3% quarter on quarter) before falling to KRW 4.05 billion in the third quarter (down 45.6% year on year). The sum over the last four quarters was KRW 21.7 billion.

Operating profit and attributable net income moved in different directions across quarters, which points to a large role for non-operating items and financial asset valuation.

Industry analysis

Automotive leather demand moves with vehicle production volumes, model mix and changes in interior specifications. Raw hide prices and exchange rates affect cost, so the ability to pass cost changes through to selling prices is the central variable for profitability.

Alphasquare's company profile describes SYTS as holding a stable market share in the domestic leather material market. Within the apparel and fashion accessories sector, SYTS's operating margin for 2025 on an annual basis was above the sector average and ranked 13th in the sector.

Listed peers in the same sector include Chokwang Leather, Youngone, Hansae and Shinyoung Wacoal, though their business mixes differ. Unlike fashion brands that depend on consumer spending, SYTS's main revenue comes from products for vehicles, so it is directly exposed to the auto cycle.

Its position in the cycle should be read alongside automaker production indicators. In its March 2026 corporate value-up plan, the company named cost competitiveness and production efficiency as direct priorities.

Strengths include room for cost control and quality differentiation, while weaknesses include slower vehicle sales and raw hide price swings. With the leather segment at more than 98% of sales, diversification across businesses remains limited.

Reliance on financial factors for net income

Risk factors

Concentration in automotive demand

Main revenue comes from vehicle leather products, so lower automaker output or model changes feed straight into sales. With the leather segment above 98% of sales, room for diversification is limited. Stronger pricing power among automakers could add pressure to unit selling prices.

Volatility in financial asset valuation

The 2025 net income increase depended heavily on financial asset valuation effects. Operating profit and net income attributable to controlling shareholders moved in different directions across quarters, which complicates reading the earnings base. Because equity is large, the performance of financial asset holdings can widen swings in net income.

Execution risk on cost improvement

Cost competitiveness and production efficiency, the core tasks in the value-up plan, may not translate into a higher operating margin. If raw hide prices or exchange rates push costs up, passing those increases on to selling prices could lag.

Because the plan is framed around directions rather than numerical targets, the benchmarks for judging results are limited.
