Report overview

DDI Concentration Meets a Quarterly Loss; New Businesses Are the Test

LX Semicon swung to an operating loss in the third quarter of 2026 as its DDI business weakened, and whether new products such as automotive MCUs and heat-dissipation substrates add meaningful revenue is the next test.

  1. 1

    Revenue has declined for three consecutive years from 2023 through 2025, reaching KRW 1.64 trillion in 2025, the smallest year of the 2022–2025 period.

  2. 2

    The third-quarter 2026 operating loss of KRW 15.6 billion followed an operating profit of KRW 14 billion in the third quarter of 2025, and press reports described it as the first quarterly operating loss since the 2010 listing.

  3. 3

    DDIs accounted for 86.4% of revenue in the first half of 2026, and the largest customer's share rose to 62.1%.

  4. 4

  5. 5

02

Business structure

LX Semicon is a fabless chip designer that supplies display driver ICs (DDIs), the chips that drive display panels. Its third-quarter 2026 revenue split by application was 37% mobile, 32% TV and 24% IT, and by product 44% large DDIs and 37% small DDIs.

DDIs accounted for 89.64% of revenue in 2024 and 86.4% in the first half of 2026. A 2025 press report placed the company in the global DDI top three, behind Samsung Electronics and a Taiwanese rival. Customer concentration is high.

In the first half of 2026, the largest customer (Customer A) generated KRW 490.2 billion, or 62.1% of revenue, while the second-largest customer (Customer B) fell 38.2% to KRW 156.7 billion. The two customers together made up 81.9% of revenue.

The heat-dissipation substrate plant, built with an investment of about KRW 100 billion, began mass production in April 2025 in Siheung, Gyeonggi. Automotive MCUs entered mass production in August 2026, after the company was selected in 2023 as a supplier to Hyundai Motor and KIA.

Exports made up 98.87% of revenue in 2024, so currency movements feed directly into results. New businesses still contribute a small share of total revenue.

04

Earnings analysis

On a disclosed basis, annual revenue went from KRW 2.12 trillion in 2022 to KRW 1.9 trillion in 2023 (down 10.3% year on year), KRW 1.87 trillion in 2024 (down 1.9%) and KRW 1.64 trillion in 2025 (down 12.1%).

Revenue fell for three consecutive years from 2023 through 2025, with 2022 the largest year and 2025 the smallest in the 2022–2025 period.

Operating profit went from KRW 310.6 billion in 2022 to KRW 129 billion in 2023 (down 58.5%), recovered to KRW 167.1 billion in 2024 (up 29.5%), and fell to KRW 108.9 billion in 2025 (down 34.8%).

Operating margin was 14.7% in 2022, 6.8% in 2023, 9.0% in 2024 and 6.6% in 2025, and in both 2023 and 2025 the margin fell alongside lower revenue.

Net profit attributable to controlling shareholders went from KRW 233.7 billion in 2022 to KRW 101.2 billion in 2023, rebounded to KRW 130.5 billion in 2024 (up 29.0%), and was KRW 82.6 billion in 2025 (down 36.7%).

Operating cash flow was -KRW 81.7 billion in 2022 and stayed positive from 2023, at KRW 102.4 billion, KRW 168.9 billion and KRW 137.1 billion in 2023, 2024 and 2025.

Quarterly operating profit ran KRW 14 billion in the third quarter of 2025, KRW 24.9 billion in the fourth quarter (up 77.5% from the prior quarter), KRW 20.6 billion in the first quarter of 2026 (down 17.3%) and KRW 22 billion in the second quarter (up 6.9%).

The third quarter of 2026 brought revenue of KRW 336 billion (down 16.2% from the prior quarter and 14.8% from a year earlier) and an operating loss of KRW 15.6 billion, a swing into loss, while net loss attributable to controlling shareholders was KRW 11.2 billion.

Press reports put third-quarter 2026 gross margin at 24.3%, against 30.5% a year earlier, and SG&A at KRW 97.2 billion, down 8.6%. The company attributed the decline to weak real demand downstream, price-cut pressure tied to higher memory prices, and currency movements.

In the first quarter of 2025, a profit improvement was read by analysts as a temporary benefit from panel makers buying DDIs ahead of US chip tariffs.

05

Industry analysis

Demand for DDIs has weakened in both mobile and TV. Press reports say large-size DDIs have not rebounded for more than three years as TV demand stayed depressed.

In smartphones, a major customer widened its OLED DDI supply base to a Taiwanese supplier, splitting volumes, and a 2026 press report citing market analysts linked part of the volume decline to that dual sourcing.

Chinese panel makers' growing market power and expanding use of Chinese ICs add competitive pressure, according to the company's own analysis. Higher memory prices have raised cost burdens for set makers, who in turn have asked component suppliers such as DDI makers for price cuts.

On the cycle, revenue rose sharply during the pandemic-era chip shortage, then turned into a weaker earnings trend after supply chains normalized at the end of 2022. In heat-dissipation substrates, growth has slowed as the electric-vehicle demand lull that began in the second half of 2023 has persisted.

Relative to peers, one 2025 press report placed the company in the global DDI top three, behind Samsung Electronics and a Taiwanese rival.

Because DDI revenue depends heavily on a few customers, a change in one customer's orders moves results sharply, which makes the pace of expansion in small DDIs and automotive chips more important.

06

Outlook

The company has said it will reshape its portfolio around profitability to pursue revenue growth and better margins. Medium- to long-term growth areas named include automotive MCUs, OLED-related products and heat-dissipation substrates.

Automotive MCUs entered mass production in August 2026, and whether that product line becomes visible in quarterly results is the next checkpoint. A 2025 press report described a plan to double output at the Siheung heat-dissipation line by 2026, with securing demand cited as the prerequisite.

Revenue from the product group outside SoCs rose to KRW 107.6 billion in the first half of 2026 from KRW 86.8 billion a year earlier, but that group includes existing T-Con and PMIC products, so the contribution of new businesses alone cannot be separated.

R&D spending was KRW 231.7 billion in 2024, KRW 227.1 billion in 2025 and KRW 112.9 billion in the first half of 2026, a 14.3% share of revenue in the first half. SG&A fell 8.6% from a year earlier in the third quarter of 2026.

On 3 November 2026 the board named Na Jun-ho as CEO nominee, with formal appointment to follow through next year's shareholder meeting and board process. Beyond business-level targets, the DDI share and customer-level revenue in the 2026 annual report are the more concrete measures to track.

Bull factors

New products reach mass production

The heat-dissipation substrate plant began mass production in April 2025 after an investment of about KRW 100 billion. Automotive MCUs have moved into mass production since the company was selected as a supplier to Hyundai Motor and KIA, and the pace at which shipments show up in revenue is the next point to check.

Both product lines have moved past development into production, which is a positive factor. Their share of quarterly revenue remains small for now.

Quarterly profit recovery and lower SG&A

Second-quarter 2026 operating profit of KRW 22 billion rose 115.1% from KRW 10.2 billion a year earlier. Fourth-quarter 2025 operating profit of KRW 24.9 billion was up 77.5% from the prior quarter. Third-quarter 2026 SG&A of KRW 97.2 billion fell 8.6% from a year earlier. If cost discipline continues, it leaves room for operating margin to recover.

Balance sheet and currency exposure

The debt ratio was 24.4% at year-end 2025, below 38.9% at year-end 2024. Press reports put the third-quarter 2026 debt ratio at 20.4%, lower than at year-end 2025, with cash and cash equivalents rising to KRW 494.5 billion.

With exports at 98.87% of revenue in 2024, a weaker won could work in the company's favor, according to a 2025 press analysis.

Bear factors

DDI concentration and customer dependence

DDIs made up 86.4% of revenue in the first half of 2026. The largest customer's share rose to 62.1%, while the second-largest customer's revenue fell 38.2%. The top two customers together account for 81.9%, so one customer's order changes can move results sharply. Diversification of the customer base was not visible in first-half 2026 figures.

Price-cut pressure and dual sourcing

Higher memory prices have led set makers to press component suppliers for lower prices. A major smartphone maker widened its OLED DDI sourcing to a Taiwanese supplier, splitting volumes, according to press reports.

Third-quarter 2026 gross margin of 24.3% compared with 30.5% in the third quarter of 2025, a decline of 6.2 percentage points.

Three years of lower revenue and a third-quarter loss

Revenue fell for three consecutive years from 2023 through 2025. The third quarter of 2026 posted an operating loss of KRW 15.6 billion. New-business contribution remains limited, while R&D spending of KRW 112.9 billion in the first half of 2026 equaled 14.3% of revenue. R&D costs are cited as a constraint on profit recovery.

Risk factors

Display demand and currency

If mobile and TV demand does not recover, DDI orders stay under pressure. The company itself cited weak downstream real demand as a cause of the third-quarter 2026 results. With exports at 98.87% of revenue in 2024, currency swings flow directly into earnings.

Customer concentration and price competition

The top two customers accounted for 81.9% of first-half 2026 revenue, so a supplier change by one customer could shake sales. The growing market power of Chinese panel makers and wider use of Chinese ICs are cited as sources of price competition. Continued price-cut demands could constrain any margin recovery.

New-business execution and management transition

Growth in the heat-dissipation substrate market has slowed amid the electric-vehicle demand lull. The CEO transition, with Na Jun-ho named nominee in November 2026, means the execution leadership is changing. If new businesses do not offset the core DDI decline, investment costs will keep weighing on earnings.

Overall view

LX Semicon is a fabless company whose DDI-centered revenue structure and high customer concentration have driven earnings volatility. On a disclosed basis, revenue fell for three consecutive years from 2023 through 2025, and operating margin dropped from 14.7% in 2022 to 6.6% in 2025.

The third quarter of 2026 brought revenue of KRW 336 billion and an operating loss of KRW 15.6 billion, worse than both the prior quarter and the same quarter a year earlier.

Factors on the positive side include the entry of heat-dissipation substrates and automotive MCUs into mass production, lower SG&A and a lower debt ratio.

Factors on the negative side include DDIs at 86.4% of first-half 2026 revenue, a largest-customer share of 62.1%, dual sourcing at major customers and intensifying competition from Chinese panel makers. New-business revenue remains too small to offset the core DDI decline.

Price-to-book below 1x is a fact on an asset basis, while earnings-based multiples move sharply with quarterly profit. The next items to check are preliminary fourth-quarter 2026 results, the DDI share and customer-level revenue in the 2026 annual report, and the quarterly trend of new-business revenue.
