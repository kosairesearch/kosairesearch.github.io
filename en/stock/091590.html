02

Business structure

Nam Hwa Construction Company is a general contractor covering civil engineering and building construction, and its experience with social infrastructure such as roads, railways, water resources and ports forms the base of its business.

Its building division has extended into apartments, religious facilities, factories and office buildings. Coverage attributes 2025 revenue growth to a rise in private orders for knowledge industry centers and officetels and to steady execution of FED (Far East District, U.S.

Army Corps of Engineers) projects, including relocation facilities for U.S. Forces Korea. Those military projects demand precise process control, and the company is regarded as having met that standard. Its order mix rests on two pillars, public works and U.S. military and private construction.

Analysts point to increases in government social overhead capital (SOC) budgets and large national projects such as new towns as drivers of public-sector growth.

The company is diversifying its order portfolio toward regional private development, wider participation in public infrastructure and eco-friendly construction technology. For 2025 it cited selective orders focused on profitability and tighter cost control.

Choi Jae-hoon and 20 related parties held 58.85% as of October 16, 2026, which makes ownership structure and free float worth monitoring together. The company is also part of the Nam Hwa group, with 15 affiliates spanning construction, leisure and distribution.

Unlike large general contractors, its competitive position rests on selective orders from public and U.S. military clients and regional private work.

04

Earnings analysis

Annual revenue fell from KRW 90.8 billion in 2022 to KRW 71.8 billion in 2023, a 20.9% decline year on year, and then to KRW 56.7 billion in 2024, a 21.1% decline. It recovered to KRW 72.7 billion in 2025, up 28.3% year on year. 2022 was the largest year and 2024 the smallest within 2022 to 2025.

Operating profit went from KRW 4.47 billion in 2022 to an operating loss of KRW 4.37 billion in 2023, a swing into the red. The 2024 operating loss of KRW 2 billion represented a continued loss with a narrower gap. Operating profit returned to the black at KRW 3.75 billion in 2025.

The operating margin was 4.9% in 2022, -6.1% in 2023, -3.5% in 2024 and 5.2% in 2025, so the 2025 figure slightly exceeds 2022.

Net profit attributable to controlling shareholders was KRW 20.2 billion in 2022, a net loss of KRW 11.8 billion in 2023, KRW 7.42 billion in 2024 after a return to profit, and KRW 10.9 billion in 2025, up 47.3%.

Net profit including non-controlling interests for 2025 was KRW 11 billion, a different figure from the controlling-shareholder measure. Operating cash flow was KRW 266 million in 2022, -KRW 20.3 billion in 2023, KRW 9.5 billion in 2024 and KRW 1.36 billion in 2025.

Within 2025, revenue rose from KRW 20.3 billion in Q3 to KRW 23.3 billion in Q4, up 14.9% from the prior quarter, and operating profit rose from KRW 823 million to KRW 2.73 billion. Q4 net profit attributable to controlling shareholders was KRW 8.59 billion, up 777.2% from the prior quarter.

Q1 2026 revenue was KRW 17.6 billion, down 24.6% from the prior quarter, and operating profit was KRW 385 million, down 85.9%. Q2 2026 revenue was KRW 18.6 billion and operating profit was KRW 49.92 million, down 87.0% from the prior quarter.

Q3 2026 revenue was KRW 18.1 billion, down 10.9% year on year, and operating profit was KRW 626 million, down 23.9% year on year. Q3 net profit attributable to controlling shareholders was KRW 1.26 billion, up 28.5% year on year, moving in the opposite direction to operating profit.

Net profit attributable to controlling shareholders over the last four quarters totaled KRW 11.3 billion, of which Q4 2025 accounted for KRW 8.59 billion. The company attributed the 2025 return to profit to selective orders focused on profitability, tighter cost control and lower selling and administrative expenses.

According to a corporate profile aggregator (as of September 7, 2026), first-half 2026 cumulative revenue rose 24.1%, operating profit rose 118.6% and net profit rose 5.3%.

The gap between operating profit and controlling-shareholder net profit in several quarters points to non-operating items, which should be checked in the notes to the periodic reports.

Industry analysis

Construction investment is being pushed up by government social overhead capital (SOC) budget increases and large national projects, while private-sector recovery is expected to be slow, according to a 2026 outlook carried by a corporate information aggregator as of September 2026.

A 2025 analysis cited interest rate cuts, housing redevelopment projects and the push for third-wave new towns as second-half recovery drivers for construction orders.

Government agencies and U.S. military projects offer relatively stable payment, while private projects are taken selectively to limit the risk of losses.

In this setup, reliance on public and U.S. military clients can provide order stability while private volume recovers slowly, yet it also leaves the company exposed if private volume keeps shrinking.

A news report from October 2026 noted that the mix of public works and private building orders determines revenue visibility. The same report added that for builders, cost ratios, construction receivables and contingent liabilities matter alongside the order backlog.

Large general contractors such as Samsung C&T, Hyundai Engineering & Construction, DL E&C and Daewoo Engineering & Construction have broader order scopes spanning housing and plants.

Nam Hwa Construction Company is smaller, so individual projects carry a larger weight in results, and its exposure to concentrated clients produces larger swings. In cycle terms, public orders appear to be expanding while private orders sit at an early or slow stage of recovery.

Financial cushion

The debt-to-equity ratio was 12.8% at end-2025, with total liabilities of KRW 22.6 billion and total equity of KRW 177.3 billion. Equity attributable to controlling shareholders, at KRW 171.2 billion, makes up most of total equity, so the equity base is thick.

Low borrowing means the company has relatively more room to absorb payment delays or cost increases. This cushion, however, does not replace the profitability of individual orders.

Public orders and insider share increase

Forecasts point to government SOC budget increases and large national projects driving growth in the public sector. A disclosure showed that executive director Choi Hong-seok added common shares through an off-market trade, expanding insider ownership.

The increase is a disclosed fact and does not by itself indicate the direction of the share price. While public and U.S. military orders continue, the stability of the order base can be maintained.

Risk factors

Dependence on public and U.S. military orders

The contract amount for the Army Central Contracting Office fuel storage facility equaled 10.66% of the most recent revenue at the time, on a 2024 revenue basis. If public budgets or U.S. military facility schedules change, order intake and revenue flow could both be affected.

Because the structure centers on public and U.S. military work, changes in procurement policy could have a large impact.

Cost ratios and construction receivables

Commentary on construction companies stresses that cost ratios, construction receivables and contingent liabilities shape profitability and cash flow. Operating losses in 2023 and 2024 mean cost control must be sustained even after the 2025 return to profit. The quarterly swings in 2026 operating profit should be examined alongside changes in project cost ratios.

Low free float

According to a corporate information aggregator, the free float ratio is 41.14%, and Choi Jae-hoon and 20 related parties hold 58.85% as of October 16, 2026. With a low free float, price moves can become larger when trading volume is thin.

On October 12, 2026, the share price rose 14.26% in a single day on trading value of KRW 14.9 billion, showing how sharply supply and demand can move it.

Overall view

Nam Hwa Construction Company returned to operating profit of KRW 3.75 billion in 2025, and net profit attributable to controlling shareholders rose 47.3% to KRW 10.9 billion. The balance sheet looks comfortable, with a debt-to-equity ratio of 12.8% and total equity of KRW 177.3 billion at end-2025.

Its business mixes public works, U.S. military facility projects and private building, and higher government SOC budgets are favorable for public volume.

On the other hand, quarterly operating profit in 2026 swung sharply, at KRW 385 million in Q1, KRW 49.92 million in Q2 and KRW 626 million in Q3, and Q3 revenue fell 10.9% year on year. Forecasts expect a slow private-sector recovery, and operating cash flow has declined since 2024.
