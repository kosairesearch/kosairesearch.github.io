DongwonDevelopment: Shrinking Sales Set Against Backlog and PF Guarantees

02

Business structure

DongwonDevelopment is a housing-focused builder founded in 1975 and listed on KOSDAQ in 1994. Its core market is the Yeongnam region, covering Busan, Ulsan and Gyeongnam, and a 2025 press report cited it as ranked 32nd nationally and first in Busan in construction capability rankings.

Its business is split into housing construction contracts, civil engineering and self-developed housing sales. In the first half of 2026 on a consolidated basis, housing construction generated KRW 127.2 billion, or 88.1% of revenue, civil engineering KRW 12.2 billion (8.5%), and housing sales KRW 4.2 billion (2.9%).

Revenue is now overwhelmingly driven by contract work. Housing sales fell from KRW 160.7 billion in 2024 to KRW 1 billion in 2025. Its customers are split between public clients and private developers.

The Goyang Changneung S-4BL 6th lot is a contract with Korea Land and Housing Corporation, and the BEXCO Third Exhibition Hall was ordered by the Busan Metropolitan City construction headquarters. A public-sale project at Eco Delta City with Busan Urban Corporation was also disclosed in the first half of 2026.

Competitively, DongwonDevelopment is closer to a regional mid-sized builder that depends on local bidding and local sales markets than a national competitor to large groups such as Samsung C&T or Hyundai Engineering & Construction.

On costs, an August 2025 press report noted that the first-half 2025 cost ratio rose to 91.7% from 86.8% a year earlier, pointing to margin pressure in contract work.

04

Earnings analysis

Annual revenue rose from KRW 581.5 billion in 2022 to KRW 736.6 billion in 2023 (up 26.7% year on year), then fell 29.4% to KRW 520.4 billion in 2024 and 25.6% to KRW 387.3 billion in 2025, which is two consecutive years of decline. Among 2022 to 2025, 2023 was the largest year for revenue and 2025 the smallest.

Operating profit went from KRW 83.9 billion in 2022 to KRW 30 billion in 2023 (down 64.3%), KRW 21 billion in 2024 (down 30.0%) and KRW 17.1 billion in 2025 (down 18.6%), which is three consecutive years of decline.

The operating margin fell from 14.4% in 2022 to 4.1% in 2023, a drop of more than 10 percentage points, and then held at 4.0% in 2024 and 4.4% in 2025. A 2025 press report attributed the main cause of the 2024 decline to a smaller share of self-developed construction.

Net profit attributable to controlling shareholders went from KRW 66.9 billion in 2022 to KRW 42.6 billion in 2023 (down 36.4%) and KRW 18.3 billion in 2024 (down 56.9%), before recovering to KRW 26.2 billion in 2025 (up 42.8%).

Total net profit including non-controlling interests was KRW 26.1 billion in 2025, a different figure. Operating cash flow swung from KRW 218.7 billion in 2022 to -KRW 75.6 billion in 2023, KRW 96.1 billion in 2024 and -KRW 44.2 billion in 2025.

On a quarterly basis, revenue went from KRW 97.2 billion in Q3 2025 to KRW 93.9 billion in Q4 2025 (down 3.4% from the prior quarter), KRW 74.5 billion in Q1 2026 (down 20.6%) and KRW 69.8 billion in Q2 2026 (down 6.3%), before rebounding to KRW 86.1 billion in Q3 2026 (up 23.3% from the prior quarter).

Operating profit peaked at KRW 5.44 billion in Q1 2026, then fell to KRW 1.95 billion in Q2 and rose to KRW 3.07 billion in Q3.

Net profit attributable to controlling shareholders also peaked in Q1 2026 at KRW 6.07 billion and then declined for two consecutive quarters, to KRW 5.89 billion in Q2 and KRW 2.14 billion in Q3. The sum for the last four quarters was KRW 19.7 billion. A 2026 press report cited weak housing sales as a reason for the first-half 2026 revenue decline.

05

Industry analysis

The housing sales market remains in a downturn. A March 2026 press report said that the restructuring of real-estate project financing (PF) rules raised equity burdens, and that rising interest rates, weak sales and loan costs have together added pressure across the development market.

Subscription demand was also weak in Yeongnam. Projects in Busan and Ulsan supplied in April and May 2026 were reported to have failed to fill their target household counts. The three projects offered in 2024, totaling 714 households, drew an average competition ratio of 0.73 to 1.

On costs, rising materials and construction prices are squeezing margins on contract work. The first-half 2025 cost ratio of 91.7% was 4.9 percentage points above the year-earlier level. In cycle terms, housing demand has weakened while public-sector orders and contract wins now carry more of the earnings.

A backlog of KRW 2.2746 trillion, reported in August 2026, was described as about 5.9 years of revenue based on 2025 sales. The Yeongnam base can be a strength in local bidding, while the sales slump also raises concentration risk in one region, and both readings coexist. Compared with large builders, the portfolio is more concentrated geographically.

06

Outlook

Secured contracts are the main support for the revenue base. The Goyang Changneung S-4BL 6th-lot construction contract, disclosed on March 27, 2026, is worth KRW 69.1 billion and runs from March 31, 2026 to November 13, 2029. It has no down payment or advance payment and pays progress amounts every 30 days.

A public-sale housing project at Eco Delta City 3BL, disclosed in the first half of 2026, is worth KRW 64.4 billion, reflecting a 45% stake, with a contract period through July 31, 2031.

The BEXCO Third Exhibition Hall construction contract, signed on November 10, 2026, is worth KRW 35.1 billion and runs from December 21, 2026 to February 4, 2031. This contract also has no down payment or advance payment, and it will be carried out with a mix of in-house and outsourced production.

The disclosure states that the construction period and amount may change under the contract terms. A value-enhancement plan published on March 27, 2026 listed cost competitiveness and production efficiency as action items and targeted stable dividends within distributable profit.

Whether self-developed sales can recover remains uncertain, as a 2025 press report also noted. A KRW 63.6 billion PF guarantee decided in September 2026 raised the total guarantee balance to KRW 199.2 billion.

The points to check are how fast backlog converts into revenue as construction progresses and whether housing sales resume, in the 2026 annual report.

Bull factors

Both have no down payment or advance payment. A August 2026 press report put backlog at KRW 2.2746 trillion, about 5.9 years of 2025 revenue.

Maintained balance sheet

Net profit attributable to controlling shareholders recovered to KRW 26.2 billion in 2025, up 42.8% from the prior year.

Shareholder return plan

The value-enhancement plan disclosed on March 27, 2026 targets stable and sustainable dividends within distributable profit. The company reported a 2025 payout ratio of 30.34% and disclosed that the 2025 dividend amount rose 10.57% from the prior year.

Bear factors

Collapse in housing sales

Housing sales fell from KRW 160.7 billion in 2024 to KRW 1 billion in 2025. In the first half of 2026 housing sales were KRW 4.2 billion, or 2.9% of revenue. Projects in Busan and Ulsan supplied in April and May 2026 did not fill their target household counts.

Continued profit decline

The operating margin stayed in the 4% range, at 4.1% in 2023 and 4.4% in 2025. Q3 2026 operating profit of KRW 3.07 billion fell 32.4% year on year, and net profit attributable to controlling shareholders of KRW 2.14 billion fell 74.3%. A press report put first-half 2026 revenue at KRW 144.3 billion, down 26.5% from a year earlier.

Cash flow and PF burden

Operating cash flow was -KRW 44.2 billion in 2025. A press report said first-half 2026 operating cash flow was a KRW 22.6 billion deficit, larger than a year earlier. PF borrowings topped KRW 1 trillion, and after the September 2026 guarantee decision the total guarantee balance reached KRW 199.2 billion.

10

Risk factors

PF and guarantee risk

A March 2026 press report said PF borrowings exceed KRW 1 trillion and some projects carry completion guarantee conditions. The September 2026 guarantee decision lifted the total balance to KRW 199.2 billion. Reports note that delayed sales pass risk directly to the company.

Sales and liquidity risk

With housing sales falling to KRW 1 billion in 2025, cash recovery from self-developed projects has stalled. Receivables rose from KRW 154.8 billion at the end of 2025 to KRW 188.0 billion at the end of June 2026. A press report said this raised the working-capital burden.

Cost and contract-term risk

The first-half 2025 cost ratio rose 4.9 percentage points to 91.7%. The BEXCO contract disclosure states that the construction period and amount may change under contract terms. A 2025 press report also flagged reliance on self-developed projects where affiliated companies act as developers.

12

Overall view

DongwonDevelopment has seen sales and operating profit shrink since its 2023 revenue peak, while net profit attributable to controlling shareholders recovered in 2025, a mixed trend.

Q3 2026 revenue rose from the prior quarter but fell year on year, and operating profit and net profit attributable to controlling shareholders also declined year on year. Strengths include public-sector contract wins, a backlog of KRW 2.2746 trillion and total equity of KRW 1.06 trillion at the end of 2025.

Weaknesses include the collapse in housing sales, an operating margin stuck in the 4% range, recurring operating cash flow deficits, and PF borrowings alongside a KRW 199.2 billion guarantee balance. These two sides deserve equal weight.

The next points to check are how quickly backlog converts into revenue, whether housing sales resume, and how PF projects progress. The share price sits in a low range relative to net assets, but interpretations differ on whether that discount reflects falling earnings and guarantee burdens.
