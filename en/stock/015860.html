02

Business structure

Iljin Holdings is a pure holding company whose core role is holding subsidiary stakes and overseeing their management. With no separate operating segment at the holding level, its income comes from dividends received from subsidiaries and trademark royalties.

The company started as Iljin Electric Industrial in 1982 and converted into a holding company structure in 2008. The group's center of gravity sits with Iljin Electric, which handles electric wire and heavy electrical equipment and is the core company within the group.

Iljin Electric reported strong third-quarter 2026 results on demand for power equipment and cables. Iljin Diamond is presented as the only domestic producer of industrial diamonds.

Iljin Hysolus, which makes hydrogen fuel tanks, posted losses as hydrogen infrastructure expansion slowed, and that weighed on Iljin Diamond's consolidated operating profit as well.

Other subsidiaries include businesses in industrial diamonds, hydrogen storage vessels and medical devices such as Alpinion Medical Systems. The group is focusing on developing high-value extra-high-voltage gas-insulated switchgear while expanding into secondary battery materials.

Because revenue is consolidated from subsidiaries, a segment mix analysis requires the segment disclosures in each subsidiary's periodic reports.

04

Earnings analysis

Annual revenue rose from KRW 1.43 trillion in 2022 to KRW 2.28 trillion in 2025, and it increased for three consecutive years from 2023 to 2025. Revenue in 2025 grew 26.8% year on year and was the largest year in the 2022 to 2025 period.

Operating profit increased from KRW 44.9 billion in 2022 to KRW 145.6 billion in 2025, up 100.8% in 2025 alone. The operating margin moved from 3.1% in 2022 and 3.9% in 2023 to 4.0% in 2024 and 6.4% in 2025, a clear margin improvement.

Attributable net income was KRW 18.4 billion in 2022, KRW 27.4 billion in 2023, KRW 22.6 billion in 2024 and KRW 35.2 billion in 2025, falling 17.8% in 2024 before recovering 56.0% in 2025.

Net income including non-controlling interests was KRW 89.6 billion in 2025, well above attributable net income of KRW 35.2 billion, and the gap reflects the share of subsidiary profits held by minority shareholders. Holding-level profit therefore depends on subsidiary ownership ratios.

Operating cash flow fell from KRW 144.6 billion in 2024 to KRW 107.8 billion in 2025, while the debt-to-equity ratio rose from 88.4% to 103.4%. Quarterly results started in the third quarter of 2025 with revenue of KRW 517.3 billion, operating profit of KRW 32.9 billion and attributable net income of KRW 14.7 billion.

In the fourth quarter of 2025 revenue reached KRW 679.8 billion and operating profit KRW 44.3 billion, but the company posted an attributable net loss of KRW 2.77 billion. The first quarter of 2026 returned to attributable net income of KRW 18.6 billion.

In the second quarter of 2026 revenue was KRW 714.4 billion and operating profit KRW 78.9 billion, yet an attributable net loss of KRW 53.8 billion emerged.

The third quarter of 2026 recorded revenue of KRW 712.8 billion, operating profit of KRW 72.7 billion and attributable net income of KRW 27.4 billion, up 37.8% and 121.0% in revenue and operating profit from a year earlier. Attributable net losses over the last four quarters total KRW 10.6 billion.

The gap between operating profit and attributable net income comes from non-operating and equity-method items, which the income statement in the next periodic report will show line by line.

Subsidiary Iljin Electric said on Nov 11, 2026 that its preliminary third-quarter revenue was KRW 637.6 billion and operating profit KRW 70.0 billion, up 41.6% and 98.3% from a year earlier. These are preliminary subsidiary figures, not the holding company's audited results.

05

Industry analysis

A large part of the holding company's earnings is linked to the industry conditions of Iljin Electric, which makes power equipment and electric cables. Iljin Electric attributed its strong third-quarter 2026 results to rising demand for power equipment and cables.

On the overseas side, orders tied to aging U.S. infrastructure are cited as another driver of Iljin Electric's results. In the same report the company said that even if U.S. data-center regulations emerge, its own impact would be limited.

Demand for power equipment is sensitive to capital spending cycles, so a downturn could pass through to consolidated profit at the holding level.

Iljin Electric's order backlog stood at roughly KRW 3.05 trillion at the end of the third quarter of 2026, and how quickly that backlog converts into revenue is a practical gauge of industry strength. Business conditions differ across the group, since profit recovery has not appeared evenly across subsidiaries.

Industrial diamonds, hydrogen tanks and medical devices sit on different demand curves, which makes a single cycle assessment for the whole group difficult. The holding company itself earns dividends and royalties rather than manufacturing revenue, so its industry exposure comes indirectly through subsidiaries.

Changes in ownership also alter the share of consolidated profit attributable to the holding company, independent of industry conditions.

06

Outlook

The starting point for the outlook is the order backlog at subsidiary Iljin Electric. The backlog was about KRW 3.05 trillion at the end of the third quarter of 2026, and how it converts into revenue after the fourth quarter of 2026 is the first checkpoint for holding-level results.

Iljin Electric's cumulative revenue for the first three quarters of 2026 was KRW 1.78 trillion, up 24.5% year on year, and cumulative operating profit was KRW 192.7 billion, up 80.2%.

Reports in the first half of 2026 said the holding company pursued a KRW 100 billion price-return swap on 1,160,930 Iljin Electric shares, which would reduce its stake from 47.76% to 45.32% if completed.

The status of that contract affects both the ownership ratio and the profit attributable to the holding company, so it should be checked in the notes to the periodic report.

Iljin Hysolus posted losses as hydrogen infrastructure expansion slowed, and normalization of that business directly affects consolidated operating profit. Whether the 6.4% operating margin of 2025 holds in the 2026 annual report is another checkpoint.

Fourth-quarter and full-year 2026 results will first appear as reported figures in the 2026 annual report, so until then the direction of holding-level earnings can be read mainly through subsidiary preliminary results.

Power-equipment order backlog provides support

Iljin Electric's order backlog was about KRW 3.05 trillion at the end of the third quarter of 2026. Cumulative revenue for the first three quarters reached KRW 1.78 trillion, up 24.5%. Demand growth for power equipment and cables is cited as the backdrop, and the backlog provides a basis for future revenue.

Share price trades at a discount to book value

The price-to-book ratio sits below 1 times. A share price at a discount to net asset value reflects where the market currently prices the group's book equity. How long that discount persists depends on whether profit recovery is sustained and how the capital structure evolves.

Bear factors

Recurring quarterly losses at attributable level

Attributable net losses over the last four quarters total KRW 10.6 billion. The company swung to a loss of KRW 2.77 billion in the fourth quarter of 2025 and posted an attributable net loss of KRW 53.8 billion in the second quarter of 2026. Attributable results fluctuating even in quarters with higher operating profit is a point of weakness.

Rising leverage and financial burden

The debt-to-equity ratio rose from 74.7% in 2022 to 103.4% in 2025, with total liabilities of KRW 1.19 trillion at the end of 2025. Operating cash flow fell from KRW 144.6 billion in 2024 to KRW 107.8 billion in 2025, so profit growth did not fully translate into cash generation.

Subsidiary dispersion and ownership-structure risk

Losses at Iljin Hysolus's hydrogen business weighed on consolidated operating profit. If the contract to reduce the stake in Iljin Electric proceeds, the share of profit attributable to the holding company could decline.

10

Risk factors

Financial leverage

The debt-to-equity ratio climbed from 74.7% in 2022 to 103.4% in 2025. Because cash flow from operations declined while borrowing grew, changes in interest rates or funding conditions could strain cash flow before reported profit. A large non-controlling interest share also magnifies swings in profit attributable to the holding company.

Subsidiary business dispersion

Iljin Hysolus recorded losses last year as hydrogen infrastructure expansion slowed. Industrial diamonds and medical devices face different industry conditions, and group results could concentrate on the cycle of a single subsidiary. A slump at one subsidiary flows directly into consolidated operating profit.

Industry cycle and policy variables

Iljin Electric's results depend on demand for power equipment and cables. If capital spending slows, the pace at which orders convert into revenue could slow as well.

The company said U.S. data-center regulation would have limited impact on it, but the direction of such policy needs continued monitoring through disclosures.

Overall view

As a pure holding company, Iljin Holdings' results are essentially driven by its subsidiaries.

Operating profit rose 100.8% to KRW 145.6 billion in 2025, lifting the operating margin to 6.4%, and in the third quarter of 2026 operating profit of KRW 72.7 billion and a return to attributable net income continued the trend.

On the other side, attributable net losses over the last four quarters total KRW 10.6 billion, and the debt-to-equity ratio rose to 103.4%, raising financial burden.

Iljin Electric's order backlog of about KRW 3.05 trillion is a supportive factor, while subsidiary dispersion and plans to reduce the stake remain variables for attributable profit.
