02

Business structure

LS Networks operates three segments: brands, distribution and leasing. On a consolidated basis, the brand segment accounted for 63.0% of revenue in fiscal 2023 (74th term), with distribution at 18.5% and leasing at 18.0%. In fiscal 2024 (75th term) the split was 62.4%, 18.0% and 19.2%.

As of September 30, 2025, brands stood at 53.3%, distribution at 19.0%, leasing at 27.3% and common/other at 0.4%, according to the quarterly report filed with DART. The brand segment sells footwear, apparel and equipment under PRO-SPECS and mont-bell.

Distribution covers global trading and export-import, bicycle sales under biclo, and new-car and motorcycle sales through subsidiaries Best Toyota, KJ Motorrad and Sports Motorcycle Korea, which handle Toyota, BMW and KTM.

Leasing rents offices, retail space and warehouses, centered on the LS Yongsan Tower, where the company reports efforts to fill vacancies and attract new tenants.

Consolidated revenue also includes the results of its financial subsidiary, LS Securities, so the revenue mix is difficult to compare directly with manufacturers or distributors. Management attributed the 2025 revenue increase partly to improved results at LS Securities and stable leasing income.

In global trading, the distribution segment overlaps in business model with LX International, POSCO International and Hyundai Corporation. The brand segment's central challenge is refreshing an aging image to win back younger consumers, and the company has reportedly pursued a full rebrand.

04

Earnings analysis

Annual results show revenue and profit moving in different directions. Revenue rose from KRW 362.9 billion in 2022 to KRW 379.6 billion in 2023, a 4.6% increase, then to KRW 1.93 trillion in 2024, up 409.4%, and to KRW 2.62 trillion in 2025, up 35.2%. Revenue has increased three consecutive years from 2023 to 2025.

Operating profit went from KRW 8.02 billion in 2022 to KRW 11.6 billion in 2023, up 44.9%, and KRW 25.6 billion in 2024, up 120.5%, before falling 13.0% to KRW 22.3 billion in 2025. The operating margin moved from 2.2% in 2022 to 3.1% in 2023, 1.3% in 2024 and 0.9% in 2025.

Net income attributable to controlling shareholders swung from a net loss of KRW 1.75 billion in 2022 to a profit of KRW 10.7 billion in 2023, then to a net loss of KRW 31.9 billion in 2024 and again KRW 31.9 billion in 2025, marking two consecutive loss years.

Including non-controlling interests, the 2025 net loss was KRW 19.1 billion. Management cited improved results at LS Securities and leasing income as drivers of the 2025 revenue increase, while weak brand performance was reported to have pressured profitability.

On a standalone basis, 2025 revenue was KRW 175.9 billion and operating profit KRW 860 million, according to coverage of the approved financial statements. Operating cash flow moved from -KRW 777.2 billion in 2024 to KRW 54.6 billion in 2025.

Quarterly results show revenue of KRW 1.04 trillion and an operating loss of KRW 26.1 billion in the fourth quarter of 2025, which turned into operating profit of KRW 43.3 billion in the first quarter of 2026.

Operating profit rose to KRW 63.1 billion in the second quarter, up 45.7% from the prior quarter, then fell to KRW 8.04 billion in the third quarter, down 87.2% from the second, on revenue of KRW 1.55 trillion. The net loss attributable to controlling shareholders over the last four quarters totaled KRW 1.77 billion.

05

Industry analysis

The brand segment is out of step with domestic consumer trends. Despite reports of a running and hiking boom, PRO-SPECS and mont-bell sales fell from KRW 166.1 billion in 2023 to KRW 150.1 billion in 2024 and KRW 113.2 billion in 2025.

Coverage pointed to an aging brand image that has pushed away younger consumers as the main reason for the weakness. The distribution segment spans new-car and motorcycle sales, bicycle distribution and global trading, and accounted for 19.0% of revenue as of September 30, 2025.

Global trading competes in a model similar to LX International, POSCO International and Hyundai Corporation. Leasing, centered on the LS Yongsan Tower, is working on filling vacancies and attracting new tenants, and its share rose from 18.0% in 2023 to 27.3% as of September 30, 2025.

In financial services, a market aggregator reported that gains on financial and derivative products at LS Securities lifted the first-half 2026 consolidated results sharply. Those valuation and disposal gains can swing with market conditions and add volatility to consolidated operating profit.

Because the three segments move in different directions, a single indicator does not capture where the group stands in its cycle.

Risk factors

Consumer trend and brand risk

Brand revenue has declined even as running and hiking demand grew, so a demand recovery does not automatically translate into earnings. Rebranding may take time to show up in sales, and a slowdown in footwear and apparel demand could raise inventory burdens.

Volatility from the financial subsidiary

Consolidated results include the profit and loss of the financial subsidiary, LS Securities. A decline in valuation and disposal gains on financial and derivative products could sharply affect consolidated operating profit and net income.

Quarterly operating profit in 2026 moved between KRW 43.3 billion, KRW 63.1 billion and KRW 8.04 billion, which illustrates the volatility.

Leverage and cash flow

Liabilities far exceed equity, and operating cash flow has varied widely from year to year, so changes in borrowing terms or working capital could weigh on finances. Consecutive net losses attributable to controlling shareholders in 2024 and 2025 also add pressure on financial capacity.

Overall view
