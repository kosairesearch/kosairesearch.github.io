KNS: 46-pi orders drive quarterly profit recovery; durability still to be tested

KNS posted operating profit in each of the first three quarters of 2026 on orders for 46-pi cylindrical battery equipment, but the 2025 annual loss and the cumulative parent-shareholder net loss over the last four quarters leave the durability of profit as an open test.

  1. 1

    Operating profit was positive in all three quarters of 2026, at KRW 1.34 billion in Q1, KRW 1.62 billion in Q2 and KRW 907 million in Q3.

  2. 2

02

Business structure

KNS focuses on secondary-battery automation equipment and is expanding into unmanned automation and robotics. Its core products are CID and rivet automation systems and assembly and inspection equipment used in cylindrical battery production.

According to the company, battery automation equipment generated 74% of parent-company revenue in the third quarter of 2026. Its customers are concentrated among the three major domestic battery makers, and the 4680 (46-pi) cylindrical battery automation line has been the main growth driver in recent quarters.

Overseas, the Vietnam subsidiary has grown through customer diversification and expanded supply for its robot foundry business.

The company said the subsidiary's cumulative third-quarter revenue rose 87.7% year on year to KRW 14.8 billion, and its cumulative operating margin was 12.9%, up 10.2 percentage points from a year earlier.

On technology, KNS is combining AI-based process optimization with robotic systems to expand its smart-factory solutions and has filed five patents related to physical-AI unmanned automation.

In secondary batteries, it is developing next-generation battery module packs based on its own battery module assembly automation (BMA) technology. As of an April 2026 report, the company targeted a prototype launch in the second half of 2026 and commercialization in 2027.

Because its customers are concentrated among three domestic battery makers, the investment cycle in battery equipment largely determines revenue flow.

04

Earnings analysis

Annual revenue fell from KRW 34.7 billion in 2022 to KRW 30 billion in 2023, a 13.5% decline, and then to KRW 27 billion in 2024, a 10.3% decline. It rose 53.0% to KRW 41.2 billion in 2025, the largest year of 2022 to 2025. The same year, operating results turned to a loss of KRW 1.56 billion.

The operating margin fell from 17.5% in 2022 to 13.2% in 2023, 3.0% in 2024 and -3.8% in 2025.

Net income attributable to parent shareholders declined from KRW 4.83 billion in 2022 to KRW 1.96 billion in 2024, then turned into a net loss of KRW 1.11 billion in 2025, when the total net loss including non-controlling interests was KRW 1.05 billion.

The company said the effect of a large one-time write-down taken last year was reflected in first-quarter 2026 results.

Quarterly operating results swung from a loss of KRW 157 million in Q3 2025 and KRW 3.14 billion in Q4 2025 to operating profit of KRW 1.34 billion in Q1 2026, KRW 1.62 billion in Q2 2026 and KRW 907 million in Q3 2026.

Revenue bottomed at KRW 6.13 billion in Q4 2025 and then rose to KRW 13.3 billion, KRW 17.1 billion and KRW 17.4 billion in the first three quarters of 2026, but growth slowed to 1.3% in Q3.

Parent-shareholder net income in Q3 2026 was KRW 562 million, but the parent-shareholder net loss totals KRW 2.15 billion over the last four quarters.

The company said Q3 net income was affected by valuation losses on investment assets and that cumulative 2026 net income excluding those losses was about KRW 4.2 billion.

At the end of 2025, total equity was KRW 57.1 billion, of which KRW 56.2 billion belonged to parent shareholders, and total liabilities were KRW 33 billion.

05

Industry analysis

KNS operates in secondary-battery manufacturing equipment. In batteries, a slowdown in electric-vehicle demand, often called the chasm, has been cited in broker research alongside weak earnings and share-price pressure. Demand for cylindrical battery equipment has nonetheless continued.

Application of 46-pi cylindrical batteries is widening across vehicle models, and brokers have assessed that KNS, which has moved early into this market, stands to benefit. This is the brokers' assessment, and no public figure for the size of the cylindrical market or KNS's share of it was confirmed.

In robotics and unmanned automation, Rainbow Robotics and Doosan Robotics are listed peers in the same sector. KNS is growing its robotics business in tandem with robot foundry supply from its Vietnam subsidiary.

According to the company, major milestones such as commercialization of robotaxis and mass production of humanoid robots are scheduled for the second half, and supply of related core components is expected to increase. This is the company's own outlook and needs to be checked against external market data.

06

Outlook

According to the company, supply of core components will rise over time as application of 46-pi batteries expands across vehicle models.

The cumulative third-quarter 2026 revenue of KRW 47.8 billion and operating profit of KRW 3.9 billion are preliminary figures from the company's release and have not yet been confirmed in a periodic report.

According to a News1 report, the company is pursuing a capacity increase through a new plant in Anseong, but the timing and size of that investment remain unclear from the reporting.

The company attributed the third-quarter decline in net income to valuation losses on investment assets tied to stock market moves and said net income could improve if markets recover.

In robotics, the expansion of robot foundry supply at the Vietnam subsidiary and the humanoid and robotaxi milestones were cited as reasons for higher supply in the second half.

The battery module-pack business is at the stage of a prototype and a commercialization target, and whether it converts into orders and revenue is the key question. Because third-quarter revenue growth slowed to 1.3%, revenue after the fourth quarter may depend on the timing of equipment orders.

This summary does not treat any annual guidance as a baseline, since no such guidance was confirmed. The 2026 annual report, the next periodic filing, will show audited full-year results and the annual operating margin trend.

08

Bull factors

46-pi equipment supply ramps up and quarterly profit returns

Operating profit turned positive in Q1 2026 at KRW 1.34 billion and stayed positive in Q2 at KRW 1.62 billion and in Q3 at KRW 907 million. The company said full-scale supply of its 46-pi cylindrical battery automation equipment drove the first-quarter profit increase.

Cumulative third-quarter revenue of KRW 47.8 billion and operating profit of KRW 3.9 billion, both preliminary company figures, rose from a year earlier.

Improving profitability at the Vietnam subsidiary

The Vietnam subsidiary's cumulative third-quarter 2026 revenue reached KRW 14.8 billion, up 87.7% from a year earlier. Its cumulative operating margin over the same period was 12.9%, up 10.2 percentage points. The company cited customer diversification and wider supply for its robot foundry business. Overseas operations could partly offset swings in the domestic equipment cycle.

Expansion into robotics, unmanned automation and battery module packs

KNS has filed five patents for physical-AI unmanned automation as it continues developing smart-factory technology. It is also developing next-generation battery module packs using its own battery module assembly automation (BMA) technology.

According to the company, scheduled second-half milestones such as robotaxi commercialization and humanoid mass production could raise demand for related components.

Bear factors

2025 annual losses and cumulative net losses

The 2025 annual operating loss was KRW 1.56 billion and the parent-shareholder net loss was KRW 1.11 billion. The cumulative parent-shareholder net loss over the last four quarters totals KRW 2.15 billion. The operating margin fell from 17.5% in 2022 to -3.8% in 2025. The turn to profit has appeared at the quarterly level, but a recovery in annual profit has not yet been shown.

Volatile quarterly profit and slower revenue growth

Q3 2026 operating profit of KRW 907 million fell 43.8% from the prior quarter. Revenue of KRW 17.4 billion rose only 1.3% from the prior quarter, a much narrower increase. In Q4 2025 the company posted an operating loss of KRW 3.14 billion, so quarterly profit has swung sharply within a short period.

Changes in capital structure and weaker cash flow

The debt ratio rose from 16.2% in 2024 to 57.7% in 2025, and total liabilities stood at KRW 33 billion. Operating cash flow in 2025 was -KRW 10.2 billion. Net income can swing sharply with valuation gains and losses on investment assets, so it should be assessed separately from cash generation.

10

Risk factors

Customer concentration and battery capex cycle

Customers are concentrated among three domestic battery makers, so their capex plans largely determine revenue. If slower electric-vehicle demand weakens the battery sector, equipment orders could be delayed. If the pace of 46-pi model adoption differs from expectations, the timing of equipment supply could also shift.

Valuation gains and losses on investments and net income volatility

Third-quarter 2026 net income was affected by valuation losses on investment assets after stock market declines. The company said these losses involve no cash outflow, but they still flow into parent-shareholder net income. If markets fall, net income can move regardless of operating performance.

Execution risk in overseas operations and new businesses

Growth at the Vietnam subsidiary is supporting a large part of the earnings recovery, so results depend heavily on its customer mix and supply plans. Battery module packs and humanoid and robotaxi-related businesses remain at the prototype and target stage. Expanded capex, including the planned Anseong plant, could add to debt and pressure cash flow.

12

Overall view

KNS posted operating profit in each of the first three quarters of 2026, supported by expanding supply of 46-pi cylindrical battery equipment and growth at its Vietnam subsidiary.

According to the company's preliminary release, cumulative third-quarter 2026 revenue was KRW 47.8 billion and operating profit was KRW 3.9 billion, both up from a year earlier.

However, the 2025 annual operating loss of KRW 1.56 billion, the parent-shareholder net loss of KRW 1.11 billion and the cumulative parent-shareholder net loss of KRW 2.15 billion over the last four quarters show that annual profitability has not yet recovered.

Quarterly profit has been volatile, with operating profit falling 43.8% in Q3 and revenue growth slowing to 1.3%. The rise in the debt ratio to 57.7% in 2025 and the negative operating cash flow are financial points that need to be tracked.

Robotics and battery module packs offer growth options but remain at the target stage. The next checkpoints are the Q4 2026 preliminary results and the 2026 annual report, where the annual operating margin and cash flow will show whether profit durability is developing.
