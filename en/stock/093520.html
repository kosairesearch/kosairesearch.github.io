Report overview

Profit recovery on non-memory chip supply, margin still the open question

Makus, a supplier centered on programmable semiconductors (FPGAs) and other non-memory chips, has seen revenue and operating profit rise together since 2024, while its operating margin has declined every year since 2022.

02

Business structure

Makus was established in 2006 through a spin-off from a predecessor company and re-listed on KOSDAQ in 2007; it specializes in programmable semiconductors (FPGAs).

According to the corporate information aggregator Butler, non-memory semiconductors accounted for 93% of revenue in the second quarter of 2026, storage for 5%, and investment and other businesses for 1% each.

Because most revenue comes from non-memory chip supply, results are effectively tied to the flow of FPGA supply. WiseReport's company profile describes Makus as an official FPGA partner that shortens product development time and supports chip selection.

The investment subsidiary handles investment business, while the storage and server distribution subsidiary handles distribution and maintenance. A May 2025 securities report estimated that supply volume to customer Y expanded sharply.

The same report said demand for FPGAs in defense applications, such as missile systems, has kept rising. As a result, changes in volumes from major customers appear to feed directly into quarterly results.

In competition, official partner status that includes technical support from the design stage is presented as a key strength. Storage accounts for only 5% of revenue, so its effect on overall results is limited.

The investment segment is only 1% of revenue, so the business structure is effectively split between chip supply and storage.

04

Earnings analysis

Annual revenue went from KRW 193.8 billion in 2022 to KRW 175.4 billion in 2023 (down 9.5% year on year), which marked the low point; it then rose to KRW 199.6 billion in 2024 (up 13.8%) and KRW 227.2 billion in 2025 (up 13.9%), gaining for two consecutive years. 2025 was the largest year for revenue in the 2022 to 2025 period.

Operating profit was KRW 31.5 billion in 2022, the largest of the four years, then fell to KRW 26.3 billion in 2023 (down 16.2%) before rising to KRW 26.9 billion in 2024 (up 2.0%) and KRW 29.1 billion in 2025 (up 8.2%), gaining for two consecutive years.

Net income attributable to owners of the parent went from KRW 22.1 billion in 2022 to KRW 20.2 billion in 2023 (down 8.5%) and KRW 20 billion in 2024 (down 1.0%), then recovered to KRW 23.1 billion in 2025 (up 15.0%), the largest year of the four.

Net income including non-controlling interests was KRW 23.1 billion in 2025, the same amount as net income attributable to owners of the parent. Operating margin fell every year, from 16.2% in 2022 to 15.0% in 2023, 13.5% in 2024 and 12.8% in 2025.

In 2025 revenue growth (13.9%) exceeded operating profit growth (8.2%), so margin declined again. Operating cash flow went from KRW 26.5 billion in 2022 to -KRW 51.9 billion in 2023, turned negative, then recovered to KRW 49.4 billion in 2024 and KRW 71 billion in 2025.

The debt ratio (total liabilities to total equity) moved from 206.9% in 2022 and 186.9% in 2023 to 260.9% in 2024, then fell to 86.4% in 2025. At the end of 2025, total equity was KRW 126.1 billion, of which KRW 125.7 billion belonged to owners of the parent, and total liabilities were KRW 108.9 billion.

By quarter, revenue hit its low in the fourth quarter of 2025 at KRW 46 billion (down 6.7% from the prior quarter), then rose for three consecutive quarters to KRW 55.3 billion in the first quarter of 2026, KRW 66.8 billion in the second quarter and KRW 67.7 billion in the third quarter.

Operating profit rose from KRW 6.24 billion in the fourth quarter of 2025 to KRW 9.09 billion in the first quarter of 2026 and KRW 13 billion in the second quarter, then fell 29.2% from the prior quarter to KRW 9.22 billion in the third quarter.

Third-quarter revenue rose 1.3% from the prior quarter while operating profit fell, though operating profit was up 139.4% from a year earlier. LS Securities put the second-quarter 2026 operating margin at 19.5%, up from 14.2% in the same quarter of 2025.

The fall in margin in the third quarter reads as costs growing faster than revenue.

Net income attributable to owners of the parent was KRW 7.27 billion in the first quarter of 2026, KRW 14.1 billion in the second quarter and KRW 8.91 billion in the third quarter, and the sum for the last four quarters (fourth quarter of 2025 to third quarter of 2026) was KRW 35.1 billion.

According to WiseReport's tally, first-half 2026 revenue fell 7.4% year on year while operating profit rose 16.4%.

05

Industry analysis

Non-memory semiconductors refer to logic-type chips other than memory, and Makus's results are concentrated in programmable chip supply within that group.

WiseReport's company profile says that changes in IT industry structure and rising product design complexity are increasing the use of non-memory chips, including FPGAs. In this trend Makus is positioned to receive demand as an official partner that supports shorter development times and chip selection.

In defense, a May 2025 report identified FPGA demand as a field that has kept growing. On the customer side, the expansion of volume from a specific customer lifted results in the first quarter of 2025, which suggests a single customer's demand shift can move quarterly results considerably.

Compared with listed electronics and components peers, Makus's results are tied to chip supply rather than finished products, so its product mix differs. WiseReport explains that non-memory chip revenue fell in the first half of 2026 but profitability improved through cost reductions.

Demand from its end markets is split between IT and defense, so whether one slowdown is partly offset by the other is a variable for how the company copes with industry conditions.

Disclosed quarterly data only starts in the third quarter of 2025, so the period is too short for a long view of the link with industry conditions.

Outlook

On November 5, 2026, the company disclosed a decision to acquire 400,000 common shares for KRW 9.76 billion, with cancellation as the stated purpose. The expected acquisition period runs from November 6, 2026 to February 4, 2027, through direct on-market purchases on KOSDAQ.

According to LS Securities, the company is pursuing a plan to cancel 6 million treasury shares in total, 2 million a year from 2025 to 2027. The same report said 2,261,000 shares were cancelled again in April 2026. Dividend increases are also mentioned as part of shareholder returns.

On the business side, whether non-memory chip supply and defense demand continue is the most important point to check. A May 2025 securities report estimated 2025 revenue at KRW 233 billion, but actual 2025 revenue came in lower at KRW 227.2 billion.

Third-quarter 2026 revenue of KRW 67.7 billion rose for three consecutive quarters, yet operating profit fell to KRW 9.22 billion, so the direction of margin in the next annual report is the key point to check.

Improving financial structure

Bear factors

Third-quarter 2026 operating profit fell 29.2% from the prior quarter to KRW 9.22 billion, a quarter in which revenue growth did not carry through to profit.

Concentration in customers and products

A May 2025 securities report cited expanded supply to customer Y as a main driver of first-quarter 2025 revenue growth. Non-memory semiconductors account for 93% of revenue, which makes the product mix highly concentrated. First-half 2026 revenue fell 7.4% year on year, according to WiseReport's tally.

Volatility in cash flow and balance sheet ratios

Operating cash flow was -KRW 51.9 billion in 2023. The debt ratio jumped to 260.9% in 2024 before falling to 86.4% in 2025, a wide swing. Whether earnings and cash flow are stable together is a question for the next annual report.

Risk factors

Customer concentration risk

Volumes from major customers appear to feed directly into quarterly results. Changes in a customer's order timing or product transitions could move revenue and operating profit together. How shifts in the customer mix show up in disclosures needs to be tracked quarter by quarter.

Product mix and industry risk

Non-memory chips make up 93% of revenue, leaving the company exposed to demand drivers that differ from memory-centered cycles. Analysis links rising IT product design complexity to FPGA demand, but a shift in demand flows could widen swings in results. The changing weight of defense and IT demand should also be watched.

Capital allocation and financial volatility

Continued treasury share cancellations and dividend increases raise the burden of shareholder returns. Operating cash flow was negative at -KRW 51.9 billion in 2023, so the balance between the size of returns and cash generation matters. The debt ratio also moved sharply, from 260.9% in 2024 to 86.4% in 2025.

Overall view

Makus is a company centered on non-memory chip supply such as FPGAs, and in disclosed results for 2022 to 2025, revenue rose for two consecutive years in 2024 and 2025. However, operating margin declined every year over the same period, so revenue growth has not translated into margin expansion.

Quarterly results since 2025 show revenue rising for three consecutive quarters, while operating profit reached KRW 13 billion in the second quarter of 2026 before falling to KRW 9.22 billion in the third quarter.

On the financial side, the debt ratio fell to 86.4% at the end of 2025, and operating cash flow rose to KRW 71 billion. On shareholder returns, the treasury share cancellation plan and the 400,000-share buyback disclosed on November 5, 2026 are continuing.

Strengths include the earnings recovery and improved financial structure, while weaknesses include margin compression and concentration in customers and products.

The next checkpoints are the end of the treasury share acquisition period on February 4, 2027, the filing deadline for the 2026 annual report on March 31, 2027, and the filing deadline for the first-quarter 2027 report on May 15, 2027.
