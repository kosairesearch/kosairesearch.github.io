Report overview

A construction-linked holding company where margin recovery is the test

Hanil Holdings is a building-materials holding company whose cement, ready-mix and mortar subsidiaries depend on a sluggish construction cycle, and whether its operating margin recovers from the 2025 low is the key test.

02

Business structure

Hanil Holdings is a holding company that manages and invests in its subsidiaries, which make and sell cement, mortar and ready-mix concrete (DART semi-annual report, August 2025).

The holding structure was launched in July 2018 through a spin-off, after which operating cement businesses sit under subsidiaries and the holding company handles subsidiary management and investment.

In the first half of 2025, cement accounted for about 35% of sales, mortar about 16%, ready-mix about 21% and other businesses about 28% (DART semi-annual report, August 2025). Key customers are domestic ready-mix producers and construction companies.

The cement operating unit supplies Portland cement alongside ready-mix and mortar, a second subsidiary produces ready-mix and concrete admixtures, and another distributes cement packaged products, bulk cement, mortar and ready-mix (The Power News, June 11, 2026).

The construction arm performs civil, building, housing and landscaping works, while the investment arm is a corporate venture capital unit. Leisure and trading businesses round out the group.

On December 1, 2024, a ready-mix subsidiary was merged into an industrial subsidiary, and in February 2025 the company absorbed another subsidiary through merger (DART semi-annual report; WiseReport). The company profile describes 27 affiliates in total.

Because sales track construction starts and sales of new homes, a slowdown in building activity feeds directly into cement and secondary product demand. As a diversified holding company, however, it has more room to respond than a single operating company (The Power News, June 11, 2026).

04

Earnings analysis

Annual revenue rose from KRW 1.97 trillion in 2022 to a peak of KRW 2.36 trillion in 2023, up 20.2%. It then fell to KRW 2.25 trillion in 2024 and KRW 1.93 trillion in 2025, down 14.2% in the latter year, marking two consecutive years of decline from 2024 through 2025.

Operating profit was largest in 2024 at KRW 279.3 billion, up 6.6%, and smallest in 2025 at KRW 123.2 billion, down 55.9%. The operating margin climbed from 6.7% in 2022 to 12.4% in 2024 before dropping to 6.4% in 2025.

In 2024, margin rose even though sales fell, which suggests cost structure changes were a larger driver of profitability than volume.

Net profit attributable to controlling shareholders fell from KRW 118.8 billion in 2024 to KRW 31.6 billion in 2025, down 73.4%, while total net profit including non-controlling interests was KRW 61.9 billion in 2025.

At year-end 2025, total equity was KRW 2.35 trillion, of which KRW 1.68 trillion belonged to controlling shareholders, and the debt ratio stayed near 59% across the period. Operating cash flow was KRW 271.6 billion in 2023, KRW 200.1 billion in 2024 and KRW 206.4 billion in 2025.

Quarterly, Q4 2025 operating profit fell to KRW 14.2 billion, down 71.2% from the prior quarter, and the company posted a net loss of KRW 5.63 billion attributable to controlling shareholders. Net profit returned to KRW 4.86 billion in Q1 2026.

Q2 2026 operating profit rose to KRW 65.3 billion, up 426.4% from the prior quarter. WiseReport said that in the first half of 2026 cement sales declined but cost reduction lifted operating profit (data as of August 31, 2026).

In Q3 2026, revenue was KRW 475.7 billion and operating profit KRW 26.1 billion, down 60.0% from the prior quarter and 46.8% from a year earlier, while net profit attributable to controlling shareholders was KRW 32.6 billion. Over the last four quarters, net profit attributable to controlling shareholders totaled KRW 65.9 billion.

05

Industry analysis

Demand in this business is tied to construction activity in housing and civil works. Cement, ready-mix and mortar shipments follow construction starts and sales, so the construction cycle is the biggest variable.

In the first half of 2026, weaker housing construction and fewer active sites reduced mortar and ready-mix shipments, and cement sales also declined (WiseReport, data as of August 31, 2026).

A prolonged construction slowdown has put the earnings resilience of building-materials companies to the test (The Power News, June 11, 2026). The same report noted that a delayed industry recovery could limit the pace of margin improvement. Reporting so far has not shown a clear sign of demand recovery.

The holding company's competitive position rests on bundling cement, ready-mix, mortar and distribution into one supply system that covers much of the construction process (The Power News, June 11, 2026).

Profitability at the cement unit depends on cost control and logistics efficiency, so whether the cost reductions seen in Q2 2026 persist will shape how the company's position in the sector is read.

Outlook

In 2026, the company set out value-up goals centered on upgrading its business portfolio, developing future growth engines, strengthening its ESG management system and sustaining shareholder returns through stable cash flow management (The Power News, June 11, 2026).

The same report said the 2026 priority is raising the efficiency of existing businesses rather than expanding volume aggressively. Cost control, logistics efficiency and clearer roles among affiliates were named as key variables for defending earnings.

Q2 2026 operating profit recovered to KRW 65.3 billion, but it fell to KRW 26.1 billion in Q3 2026, so the effect of cost reductions has not been steady quarter to quarter.

A July 8, 2026 disclosure covered a decision to acquire land and buildings in Samseong-dong, Gangnam-gu, Seoul, and the purpose and financing of that acquisition should be confirmed in later filings.

On September 22, 2026, a disclosure reported that an executive bought 1,500 shares on the open market (based on DART disclosure summaries).

The next periodic report is the FY2026 annual report, due by March 31, 2027 under the statutory deadline, and it will be the first disclosure of Q4 2026 results and the annual operating margin. The Q1 2027 quarterly report, due by May 15, 2027, will then show whether construction demand is recovering.

08

Bull factors

Earnings recovery in 2026

Q2 2026 operating profit rose to KRW 65.3 billion, up 426.4% from the prior quarter. WiseReport said cost reductions lifted first-half operating profit even as cement sales fell (data as of August 31, 2026).

Net profit attributable to controlling shareholders has stayed positive for three consecutive quarters since Q1 2026.

Shareholder return plan

In its 2026 value-up plan, the company listed sustained shareholder returns backed by stable cash flow management (The Power News, June 11, 2026). The same report noted that cash generated by subsidiaries feeds into dividends and investment capacity at the holding level. Operating cash flow in 2025 was KRW 206.4 billion, a slight increase from 2024.

Diversified holding portfolio

Beyond cement, ready-mix and mortar, the group includes civil and building construction, venture investment, leisure and trading units. Reporting has said the broader portfolio gives more room to respond to construction cycle swings than a single building-materials company (The Power News, June 11, 2026). Bundling cement through distribution into one supply chain is also cited as a strength.

Bear factors

Weak construction demand

Fewer construction sites and weaker housing construction reduced mortar and ready-mix shipments, and cement sales declined (WiseReport, data as of August 31, 2026). The Power News reported that a delayed industry recovery could limit the pace of profit improvement (June 11, 2026). The same report also noted that Q1 2026 sales and operating profit were slightly below the prior-year quarter.

Earnings setback in 2025

Revenue declined for two consecutive years from 2024 through 2025, and 2025 operating profit fell 55.9% to KRW 123.2 billion. Net profit attributable to controlling shareholders dropped 73.4% to KRW 31.6 billion in 2025, against KRW 118.8 billion in 2024. The 2025 operating margin of 6.4% was the lowest of the 2022–2025 period.

Profit slowed again in Q3

Q3 2026 revenue was KRW 475.7 billion and operating profit KRW 26.1 billion, down 46.8% from a year earlier. The Q2 recovery did not carry into Q3, which is the core of the bearish case. Revenue also fell 5.8% year on year, so a sales recovery is not yet clear.

10

Risk factors

Cost structure execution

Cost control, logistics efficiency and clearer roles among affiliates are the key variables the company has named for defending earnings. If progress on this work slips, cost improvements may not be fully reflected in subsidiary results.

Reorganizing roles among affiliates would reshape the supply system linking the cement operating unit with the ready-mix and mortar subsidiaries, and could create transaction adjustment burdens during execution.

Growth investment execution

The 2026 value-up plan set out portfolio upgrading and the development of future growth engines as its main directions. If capital and management attention shift to new businesses before construction demand recovers, efficiency gains in existing businesses could be delayed.

Because the same plan puts efficiency of existing businesses ahead of volume expansion, the timing of when growth initiatives show up in results remains hard to gauge.

Capital allocation

A July 8, 2026 disclosure covered a decision to acquire land and buildings in Samseong-dong, Gangnam-gu, Seoul (based on DART disclosure summaries). How this acquisition affects cash flow and dividend capacity will show up in later filings.

If subsidiary cash generation weakens, the balance between shareholder returns and investment becomes more important.

12

Overall view

Hanil Holdings is a holding company with building-materials subsidiaries centered on cement, ready-mix and mortar, along with leisure and investment affiliates. Revenue in 2025 was KRW 1.93 trillion and operating profit KRW 123.2 billion, both lower than in 2024, and the operating margin fell to 6.4%.

In 2026, Q2 operating profit of KRW 65.3 billion showed the effect of cost cuts, but Q3 fell to KRW 26.1 billion, so the quarterly pattern is uneven. Weaker construction activity, which reduced cement and mortar and ready-mix shipments, is the most visible negative factor.

On the other side, the value-up plan commits to sustained shareholder returns, and the broad portfolio is cited as room to respond. An open-market share purchase by an executive, disclosed on September 22, 2026, is recorded as a fact, and its interpretation may differ.

The next checkpoints are the FY2026 annual report (due March 31, 2027) and the Q1 2027 quarterly report (due May 15, 2027).
