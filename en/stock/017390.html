02

Business structure

Seoul City GAS is centered on city gas supply. As of revenue for the second quarter of 2026, city gas supply accounted for 99%, real estate leasing for 1%, and overseas resource development for 1%. Competition from district energy and warmer weather limit volume growth in city gas.

The business rests on a regional monopoly with a stable structure, though initial facility investment is heavy. Demand is highly seasonal, with winter months (January to March and November to December) accounting for 69.2% of sales. District heating competes for heat supply, so heating demand drives the revenue base.

Raw gas is sourced through KoreaGas, and the receivables built up as KoreaGas supplies residential gas below cost are a structural issue across the sector. SK GAS, another listed name in the sector, specializes in LPG and has a different business structure. Overseas resource development contributes only about 1% of revenue, so its earnings effect is limited.

04

Earnings analysis

Annual revenue was KRW 1.72 trillion in 2022, KRW 1.72 trillion in 2023 (down 0.4% year on year), and KRW 1.7 trillion in 2024 (down 0.8%), before rising to KRW 1.83 trillion in 2025 (up 7.3%). The largest year in 2022 to 2025 was 2025, and the smallest was 2024.

Operating profit of KRW 3.32 billion in 2022 turned into an operating loss of KRW 3.2 billion in 2023, and the loss widened to KRW 10.3 billion in 2024. In the third quarter of 2024, higher repair costs and various fees increased selling and administrative expenses, which widened the operating loss.

The company attributed the turn to a KRW 17.4 billion operating profit in 2025 to higher gas sales and lower selling and administrative expenses.

Net income attributable to controlling shareholders was KRW 17.9 billion in 2022, KRW 20.5 billion in 2023 (up 14.6%), and KRW 33.9 billion in 2024 (up 65.1%), before falling to KRW 30.9 billion in 2025 (down 8.7%).

The 2025 decline reflected the absence of a one-off gain from a prior-year sale of an affiliate's investment property and lower financial income as rates fell. Operating margin moved from 0.2% in 2022 to -0.2% in 2023, -0.6% in 2024, and 1.0% in 2025.

On a quarterly basis, an operating loss of KRW 13.4 billion in the third quarter of 2025 turned into operating profit of KRW 3.52 billion in the fourth quarter, and then peaked at KRW 22 billion in the first quarter of 2026 (up 527.0% from the prior quarter).

Revenue fell 64.2% in the second quarter of 2026 to KRW 2.755 billion, and the company posted an operating loss of KRW 2.35 billion. Third-quarter 2026 revenue was KRW 227.8 billion, down 17.3% from the prior quarter but up 16.0% year on year, with an operating loss of KRW 9.26 billion.

Net loss attributable to controlling shareholders was KRW 3.83 billion in the third quarter of 2026. Net income attributable to controlling shareholders over the last four quarters totaled KRW 26 billion. Total equity at year-end 2025 was KRW 1.12 trillion, and total liabilities were KRW 676.5 billion.

05

Industry analysis

City gas earnings in Korea depend heavily on winter demand. Seoul City GAS's quarterly results followed the same pattern, falling sharply in the second and third quarters after the first-quarter peak. A sector-wide issue is the raw gas supply structure.

KoreaGas reported in its preliminary third-quarter 2026 results, announced on November 12, 2026, that residential city gas receivables rose to KRW 13.8277 trillion, up KRW 288.6 billion from the first half of the year.

Separately, higher international energy prices after the Middle East conflict improved profitability in KoreaGas's overseas resource development business. That benefit sits in overseas operations and is separate from domestic city gas sales.

In heating, district energy competes with city gas, and Seoul City GAS partly offsets that competition through its regional monopoly. Samchully (Samchully) also runs city gas operations and serves as a comparison point. SK GAS (SK GAS), by contrast, focuses on LPG with a different cost structure.

As the receivables balance grows, the link between supply prices and raw gas costs can affect profits across the sector.

Risk factors

Raw gas cost and receivables risk

KoreaGas's residential city gas receivables rose to KRW 13.8277 trillion in the third quarter of 2026, which keeps cost recovery under pressure. If supply prices fail to track raw gas costs, margins for city gas operators can be affected. The method of clearing these receivables remains a policy variable.

Weather and seasonality risk

With winter months accounting for 69.2% of sales, temperature changes directly drive quarterly results. The second and third quarters fall outside the heating season, when revenue drops sharply. As a result, quarterly swings are large relative to annual totals.

International energy price and interest rate risk

Higher international energy prices after the Middle East conflict improved KoreaGas's overseas resource development profitability. Swings in international prices can also feed through to raw material costs. Lower financial income from rate cuts was reflected in 2025 net income.
