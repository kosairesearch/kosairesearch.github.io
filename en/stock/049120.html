Losses persist as new-business funding expands

02

Business structure

Fine DNC was founded in 1992 and listed on KOSDAQ in 2001 as a precision-mold-based parts maker. Its headquarters and manufacturing sites are in Asan, Chungcheongnam-do, and it runs subsidiaries in Slovakia and Vietnam.

Its core lines are TFT-LCD and display panel module parts, and it produces QD-OLED panel module parts for TVs and monitors using metal stamping. Its products are mainly supplied to Samsung Display and other panel makers. The display business is the company's central revenue source.

The first diversification axis is foldable evacuation stairs, which are approved as an alternative evacuation facility for high-rise buildings such as apartments, and the indoor model was selected as an LH SME growth technology, according to the company.

The second axis is secondary battery parts, including battery cell cases and assemblies; in March 2026 the company secured mass-production orders for cap and current collector assemblies for battery cans from a North American electric vehicle maker and said related sales would expand from the second half.

The third axis is OLED glass etching, for which the board in September 2026 decided to establish a new Vietnamese subsidiary and buy a new site. In 2025 the company discontinued a specific EV-related parts business, which led to impairment of tangible assets and bad-debt charges.

Results depend on the investment schedules of large panel makers. The largest shareholder, Fine M-Tec, raised its stake through a tender offer in June 2026.

04

Earnings analysis

Annual figures below follow the DART periodic reports. Revenue fell from KRW 58.3 billion in 2022 to KRW 43.5 billion in 2023, a 25.4% year-on-year decline, and then rose to KRW 65 billion in 2024 (up 49.5%) and KRW 65.8 billion in 2025 (up 1.2%), increasing for two consecutive years.

Among 2022 to 2025, 2025 was the largest revenue year and 2023 the smallest. Operating losses were KRW 3.97 billion in 2022, KRW 9.62 billion in 2023, KRW 745 million in 2024 and KRW 4.08 billion in 2025, a continuous loss record from 2022 through 2025.

Seoul Economic Daily reported six consecutive years of operating losses, while the DART-based annual series starts in 2022. The operating margin was -6.8% in 2022, -22.1% in 2023, -1.1% in 2024 and -6.2% in 2025, with the smallest loss relative to sales in 2024.

The 2024 narrowing came alongside revenue growth, while the 2025 widening was attributed by the company to impairment of tangible assets tied to discontinuing an EV-related parts business, bad-debt charges and higher bond-related costs.

The loss attributable to controlling shareholders was KRW 25.3 billion in 2022, KRW 10.2 billion in 2023, KRW 2.58 billion in 2024 and KRW 22.2 billion in 2025, and the net loss including non-controlling interests was reported at the same amounts each year.

The debt ratio fell from 295.6% in 2022 to 108.4% in 2025, when total equity was KRW 52 billion and total liabilities KRW 56.4 billion. Operating cash flow was -KRW 8.11 billion in 2022, -KRW 2.58 billion in 2023, -KRW 1.1 billion in 2024 and -KRW 2.06 billion in 2025, negative in all four years.

Quarterly revenue was KRW 11.3 billion in the third quarter of 2025, KRW 10.7 billion in the fourth quarter (down 4.9% quarter on quarter), KRW 13.8 billion in the first quarter of 2026 (up 28.3%), KRW 11 billion in the second quarter (down 20.3%) and KRW 12.1 billion in the third quarter of 2026 (up 10.3% quarter on quarter and 7.3% year on year).

Operating losses over the same five quarters were KRW 548 million, KRW 3.71 billion, KRW 1.02 billion, KRW 1.42 billion and KRW 2.76 billion, and the loss attributable to controlling shareholders reached KRW 4.69 billion in the third quarter of 2026, with the last four quarters totaling KRW 23.3 billion.

In the third quarter of 2026 revenue increased while the operating loss widened, indicating that cost burdens offset the sales gain.

05

Industry analysis

Although this report classifies Fine DNC under semiconductors, its core business is display panel parts. The industry cycle is driven by capital spending from large panel makers and demand for finished display products.

According to an equity information site, display parts revenue improved through the third quarter of 2025 on higher demand for game consoles and computer monitors. The same source notes that Samsung Display has been investing in its Asan 1 campus through 2025 to support the premium display market.

Revenue fell quarter on quarter in the second quarter of 2026, and in the third quarter revenue rose while the operating loss widened. In cycle terms, sales scale has held up while profitability has not yet recovered. On 2025 annual figures, the operating margin compares below the display components industry average.

OLED etching is a new entry area, so the key question is how far the existing mold-making technology carries over. For secondary battery parts, the checkpoint is how much the North American battery can parts orders contribute to 2026 results and later quarterly sales.

06

Outlook

A company official said the OLED etching business would likely take about two years to show revenue or profit improvement, given the need for new subsidiary investment, site purchase and construction (reported in September 2026).

In a disclosure dated September 30, 2026, the company said it would invest about KRW 13.6 billion in its Vietnamese subsidiary VINA DNC, raising its stake to 100%.

Of the KRW 13 billion private convertible bond decided on September 21, 2026, KRW 5 billion is earmarked for facilities from October 2026 through December 2027 to buy and build the OLED etching site.

KRW 8 billion goes to acquiring securities of other companies for the Vietnamese subsidiary, and the difference between the subsidiary investment amount and the bond's use of funds should be checked against the original filing.

In secondary batteries, the next checkpoint is whether the mass-production parts for a North American electric vehicle maker secured in March 2026 lift sales from the second half.

The March 2026 share consolidation was stated to maintain an appropriate number of outstanding shares and stabilize the share price, with total common shares changing from 53,008,319 to 10,601,663.

A proposal to add OLED glass etching to the articles of incorporation was disclosed for an extraordinary shareholders meeting. Outstanding convertible bonds stood at KRW 5.11 billion as of August 14, 2026, and the 13th-series bond is larger than twice that balance.

08

Bull factors

Diversification into batteries and evacuation stairs

In March 2026 the company secured mass-production orders for cap and current collector assemblies for battery cans from a North American electric vehicle maker and presented a plan to expand related sales from the second half.

The company said its indoor foldable evacuation stair was selected as an LH SME growth technology. Adding OLED etching and the Vietnamese subsidiary, several growth axes beyond the core display business are being prepared at once.

A track record of narrowing losses

Operating losses narrowed from KRW 9.62 billion in 2023 to KRW 745 million in 2024, and the operating margin improved from -22.1% to -1.1%. Over the same period revenue rose from KRW 43.5 billion to KRW 65 billion, and 2025 reached KRW 65.8 billion, the largest year in the period. The prior narrowing of losses during a period of revenue recovery is the main basis for the bullish view.

Larger stakes held by the largest shareholder and management

The largest shareholder Fine M-Tec raised its stake through a tender offer in June 2026. CEO Hong Sung-cheon disclosed in March 2026 that he had increased his holdings through open-market purchases.

In September 2026 a third-party allotment rights offering of KRW 6 billion to the largest shareholder was decided, so management-side capital raising continues in the record.

09

Bear factors

Four straight years of operating losses and a recent widening

Operating losses continued from 2022 through 2025, and the 2025 loss widened again to KRW 4.08 billion. The third-quarter 2026 operating loss of KRW 2.76 billion was larger than a year earlier. The loss attributable to controlling shareholders over the last four quarters totals KRW 23.3 billion.

Seoul Economic Daily reported six consecutive years of operating losses, a longer period than the DART-based count.

Convertible bonds and potential dilution

The 13th private convertible bond of KRW 13 billion carries a 0% coupon and a conversion price of KRW 2,990 per share. Full conversion would issue 4,347,826 common shares, equal to 25.52% of total shares outstanding. Ssquare Asset Management reported a 5.01% holding on an as-converted basis. Conversion requests can be made from September 29, 2027, so the dilution burden extends beyond that date.

Cash headroom and short-term repayment pressure

Blotter reported that consolidated cash and cash equivalents at the end of the first half of 2026 were KRW 1.4 billion, less than half of the year-end level, and that the current ratio stood at 40.9%. The same report put short-term borrowings at KRW 27.3 billion.

Since a company official said the OLED business needs about two years to monetize, core business cash generation becomes more important during that period.

Risk factors

Customer concentration and core sales volatility

Products are mainly supplied to Samsung Display and other large panel makers, so revenue depends on their investment and order flow. In 2025 the discontinuation of an EV-related parts business led to impairment of tangible assets and bad-debt charges. Second-quarter 2026 revenue of KRW 11 billion fell 20.3% from the prior quarter.

Delayed monetization and overseas investment

The OLED etching business requires buying and building a new site and establishing a Vietnamese subsidiary, and a company official said monetization could take about two years. Facility spending is scheduled from October 2026 through December 2027. If investment is not converted into sales, the cash burden could persist.

Financing needs and dilution

For the 13th convertible bond, the issuer may exercise call options from September 29, 2027 to February 29, 2028 for up to 30% of the principal each month. The bond also carries early-redemption rights and a conversion price adjustment clause. Combined with the earlier outstanding bonds, shareholder dilution could continue over a long period.

Overall view

Fine DNC recorded operating losses in each year from 2022 through 2025. The loss narrowed in 2024 but widened again in 2025, and in the third quarter of 2026 revenue rose while the operating loss grew.

Bullish points include secondary battery parts orders, diversification into evacuation stairs, and larger stakes held by the largest shareholder and management. Bearish points include continued losses, dilution from a KRW 13 billion convertible bond, and limited cash and short-term borrowing headroom.

The debt ratio fell from 295.6% in 2022 to 108.4% in 2025, but operating cash flow was negative in all four years. The company has said the OLED etching business and the Vietnamese investment could take about two years to produce revenue and profit.

The next checkpoints are the 2026 annual business report due by the end of March 2027 and the start of convertible bond conversion requests from September 29, 2027.
