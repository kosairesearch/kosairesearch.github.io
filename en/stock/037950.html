Elcomtec: Four Quarters of Revenue Growth, Uneven Profits

Elcomtec's quarterly revenue rose for four straight quarters from the fourth quarter of 2025 through the third quarter of 2026, while operating profit and net income attributable to controlling shareholders moved in different directions.

  1. 1

    Annual revenue fell 19.3% to KRW 46.3 billion in 2025, while third-quarter 2026 revenue reached KRW 22 billion, up 86.4% from a year earlier.

  2. 2

    Operating profit jumped 159.9% quarter on quarter to KRW 1.49 billion in the first quarter of 2026, reached KRW 1.81 billion in the second quarter, and eased to KRW 1.71 billion in the third quarter, down 5.6% from the prior quarter.

  3. 3

    Net income attributable to controlling shareholders was KRW 2.47 billion in 2025, down 50.4%, and the four quarters from the fourth quarter of 2025 through the third quarter of 2026 total KRW 5.14 billion.

  4. 4

  5. 5

02

Business structure

Elcomtec was founded in 1991 as an electronic components maker and listed on KOSDAQ in 2000. Its main businesses are camera lens modules for mobile phones, LED lighting, and EMS (electronics manufacturing services).

The company was sold to an investor consortium in 2013, and the largest shareholder group held 57.61% as of September 2025, leaving the free float just above 40%. In camera lenses it is described as a leading domestic supplier, with advantages in technology, sales networks, overseas recognition, and process automation.

Mobile phone parts are presented as the main revenue source, but sources describe the segment mix differently, so segment revenue shares should be checked against the segment information in the business report.

A corporate history entry records a halt to mobile camera lens production in June 2025, while other company profiles still describe lenses as a current business, so the current production setup should be checked against the original filings.

The group has four consolidated subsidiaries, including a China production unit, and it also runs a mining development business in Mongolia. LED lighting sales are being strengthened toward public offices and shopping centers, based on low power consumption and long service life.

Peers in the electronic components sector include Samsung Electro-Mechanics and LG Innotek, which are much larger listed companies. The structure combines handset-linked parts with LED lighting and EMS, so handset set demand feeds into results.

Earnings analysis

Annual revenue rose from KRW 41.6 billion in 2022 to KRW 56.6 billion in 2023 (up 36.1% year on year) and KRW 57.4 billion in 2024 (up 1.3%), then fell 19.3% to KRW 46.3 billion in 2025. Within 2022 to 2025, 2024 was the largest year and 2022 the smallest.

Operating profit dropped from KRW 4.77 billion in 2022 to KRW 2.13 billion in 2023 (down 55.4%), recovered to KRW 3.56 billion in 2024 (up 67.2%), and fell again to KRW 2.22 billion in 2025 (down 37.6%). Operating margin moved from 11.5% in 2022 to 3.8% in 2023, then 6.2% in 2024 and 4.8% in 2025.

Net income attributable to controlling shareholders went from KRW 4.98 billion in 2024 (up 98.9%) back to KRW 2.47 billion in 2025 (down 50.4%). Net income including non-controlling interests is a different figure and should be read separately.

For the first nine months of 2025, revenue fell 25.9% and operating profit fell 47.6% year on year, with lower EMS and handset parts sales and higher cost and expense burdens cited as causes.

The quarterly series starts from the third quarter of 2025, with revenue of KRW 11.8 billion and operating profit of KRW 334 million.

Revenue then reached KRW 12.7 billion in the fourth quarter of 2025, KRW 1.79 billion in the first quarter of 2026 (up 41.4% quarter on quarter), KRW 2.07 billion in the second quarter and KRW 2.2 billion in the third quarter, up 86.4% year on year.

Operating profit rose 159.9% quarter on quarter to KRW 1.49 billion in the first quarter of 2026, reached KRW 1.81 billion in the second quarter, and eased to KRW 1.71 billion in the third quarter.

Net income attributable to controlling shareholders rose to KRW 2 billion in the second quarter of 2026 before falling to KRW 813 million in the third quarter (down 59.3% quarter on quarter), and the last four quarters total KRW 5.14 billion.

Profits did not move in step with revenue, so whether the first-quarter 2026 jump was one-off or structural is a matter for the business report's explanation.

Industry analysis

Elcomtec's demand spans three different markets: smartphone camera lenses, LED lighting, and EMS contract manufacturing. In camera lenses, a 2025 company monitor comment notes that lens applications above 13 megapixels are increasing, which points to rising demand for higher-specification lenses.

Whether this trend contributed to the 2026 revenue recovery needs to be confirmed through product-level disclosure. In the first half of 2025, EMS revenue stayed stable, but weaker other parts and handset parts businesses pushed overall results lower.

This shows that parts earnings can swing sharply when set demand changes. LED lighting sales are being strengthened toward public offices and shopping centers.

In competition, Elcomtec is viewed as a leading domestic camera lens supplier, but Samsung Electro-Mechanics and LG Innotek are much larger listed companies in the same sector. Market share and profitability comparisons with those larger players should be checked against separate industry data.

Smartphone set shipment trends are a key variable for the direction of the company's results, so industry indicators should be read alongside its revenue trend.

Outlook

The company has disclosed three directions. First, it completed a capital reduction in April 2026 that cut its share capital, and it explained the move as a way to secure dividend funding and keep flexibility in medium- to long-term capital policy.

Second, press coverage read the lower capital base as widening the distributable reserves, but any actual dividend requires board and shareholder meeting approvals. Third, the company said that its business activities and growth strategy would continue after the reduction.

In July 2026, three disclosures showed purchases by a controlling shareholder on the open market, at sizes from tens of millions to roughly KRW 100 million per transaction; these are shareholder trades, not company earnings guidance.

Third-quarter 2026 revenue rose for a fourth consecutive quarter, but operating profit eased slightly, so whether revenue growth carries through to profit is the item to watch. The next checkpoints are the fourth-quarter 2026 preliminary results and the 2026 business report.

The business report will show segment revenue, the status of mobile lens production, and whether a dividend is decided after the reduction.
