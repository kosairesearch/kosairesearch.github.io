02

Business structure

Maeil Holdings became a holding company in May 2017 through a spin-off that moved its dairy operations into Maeil Dairies. The company currently holds 12 consolidated subsidiaries including one listed company, with its core asset being a 31.79% stake in Maeil Dairies.

Its food-service subsidiary operates Paul Bassett and Crystal Jade and launched the Sabu Sikdang Sangha brand in 2025. A 2024 press report put food-service sales at under 10% of total holding-company revenue. A food-ingredient distribution subsidiary supplies cafes, bakeries and quick-service restaurants nationwide.

A beverage joint venture with a Japanese partner runs the Sapporo beer-stand business, and the Gochang agri-tourism complex runs a sixth-industry model combining experiences and lodging. Segment revenue figures are limited to those confirmed in filings.

Compared with other holding companies in the sector, the portfolio is heavily weighted toward food subsidiaries.

04

Earnings analysis

Consolidated revenue rose from KRW 1.98 trillion in 2022 to KRW 2.15 trillion in 2023, KRW 2.19 trillion in 2024 and KRW 2.2 trillion in 2025, with growth slowing to 0.3% in 2025. Revenue has risen for three straight years from 2023 through 2025.

Operating profit went from KRW 73.4 billion in 2022 to a peak of KRW 82.2 billion in 2023, then fell to KRW 78.3 billion in 2024 and KRW 64 billion in 2025. Operating margin was 3.7% in 2022, 3.8% in 2023, 3.6% in 2024 and 2.9% in 2025, the lowest of the four years, so revenue growth did not carry through to profit.

A third-party data summary attributes the decline to Maeil Dairies, where dairy operating profit fell from KRW 55.9 billion to KRW 39.6 billion, citing falling birth rates, plant-based beverages and higher raw milk costs.

The same summary reported that food-service operating profit fell from KRW 7.3 billion to KRW 4.4 billion. Profit attributable to controlling shareholders was KRW 6.52 billion in 2022, KRW 49.7 billion in 2023, KRW 24.2 billion in 2024 and KRW 32.5 billion in 2025.

Net income including non-controlling interests was KRW 70.2 billion in 2025, well above the controlling-shareholder figure, which reflects the minority share in subsidiaries such as Maeil Dairies. Operating cash flow fell from KRW 188.1 billion in 2023 to KRW 73.2 billion in 2025.

On a quarterly basis, operating profit bottomed at KRW 14.2 billion in the fourth quarter of 2025 and then rose to KRW 18 billion, KRW 22.7 billion and KRW 25.1 billion through the third quarter of 2026.

Profit attributable to controlling shareholders has been volatile, with KRW 6.63 billion in the first quarter of 2026 and KRW 6.93 billion in the third quarter, and the last four quarters total KRW 35.4 billion.

05

Industry analysis

Maeil Holdings' results depend heavily on dairy demand. A third-party data summary describes the domestic plain milk market as stagnant, pressured by falling birth rates and the rise of plant-based beverages.

Infant formula is seeing early signs of recovery in births, while cheese volumes were hit when food-service channels shrank after the pandemic. Coffee ready-to-drink faces intense OEM and private-label competition.

Maeil Dairies is shifting toward higher-margin segments such as digestive-friendly milk, Greek yogurt and lactose-free products. The food-service market faces margin pressure from global premium coffee brands and competition from franchise and independent stores.

The same summary estimates the domestic B2B food-ingredient market at about KRW 65 trillion, with corporate distributors holding about 15%, but these figures come from a third-party summary and are not treated as confirmed indicators.

Because the holding structure ties earnings to Maeil Dairies, the company's results are most exposed to the dairy cycle and raw milk prices. Relative to other holding companies in the sector, its portfolio is concentrated in food.

06

Outlook

The most concrete near-term item is the treasury share disposal plan for employee compensation. The March 2026 annual meeting approved a plan to dispose of 150,000 treasury shares, to be handled each year in the first quarter over five years, with the final disposal scheduled for March 2031.

At the same meeting, shareholder proposals to cancel treasury shares and raise dividends were voted down, which suggests shareholder-return disagreements may remain on the agenda. The company cancelled 420,000 treasury shares in August 2025.

Strategically, management has described a move away from a dairy-centered structure toward a broader food company. The food-service unit has expanded Sapporo-branded beer stands and set up a dedicated beer company in 2025, while the Sangha farm complex added a workation center and an arboretum.

Whether these investments translate into operating profit is not yet visible in the filed figures. The next periodic report, the 2026 annual report, will show fourth-quarter and full-year 2026 operating margin, and whether it recovers from 2.9% in 2025 is the next checkpoint.

08

Bull factors

Consecutive quarterly operating profit gains

Operating profit rose from KRW 14.2 billion in the fourth quarter of 2025 to KRW 18 billion, KRW 22.7 billion and KRW 25.1 billion in the first three quarters of 2026. Third-quarter operating profit was up 6.9% year on year and revenue rose 4.3%. This quarterly direction contrasts with the two-year annual decline.

Easing leverage

The debt-to-equity ratio fell from 90.0% in 2022 to 66.3% in 2025. Total liabilities at the end of 2025 were KRW 567.5 billion against total equity of KRW 856.1 billion. Lower leverage widens room for dividends and investment at the holding level.

Shareholder-return pressure and treasury share policy

At the March 2026 annual meeting, shareholders proposed a higher dividend and treasury share cancellation. The cancellation proposal was voted down, but a plan to dispose of 150,000 treasury shares for employee compensation over five years was approved. Whether shareholder demands move into dividends or cancellations remains a question for future meetings.

Bear factors

Two straight years of lower annual operating profit

Operating profit fell from KRW 82.2 billion in 2023 to KRW 78.3 billion in 2024 and KRW 64 billion in 2025, with operating margin at 2.9% in 2025. Revenue grew only 0.3% in 2025, so top-line growth did not carry through to profit.

Structural pressure at the core subsidiary

A third-party summary reports that Maeil Dairies' dairy operating profit fell from KRW 55.9 billion to KRW 39.6 billion, citing falling birth rates, plant-based beverages and higher raw milk prices. Food-service operating profit reportedly fell from KRW 7.3 billion to KRW 4.4 billion. Weakness at one or two subsidiaries flows directly into controlling-shareholder earnings.

Volatility in controlling-shareholder earnings

Profit attributable to controlling shareholders fell 51.3% in 2024 before recovering to KRW 32.5 billion in 2025. The first quarter of 2026 fell 49.6% from the prior quarter, and the third quarter was down 25.9% year on year.

Risk factors

Subsidiary demand and raw materials

Demand for dairy products is stagnant due to falling birth rates and the rise of plant-based beverages, while higher raw milk prices pressure margins. Food-service faces margin pressure from premium coffee brands and franchise competition. These factors pass through to holding-level results via the subsidiaries.

Share price volatility and ownership concentration

On May 28, 2026, the exchange designated the stock as a short-term investment-caution issue for one day over concentrated trading in a small number of accounts, with heavy retail buying cited as the cause.

The largest shareholder group, including related parties, reported a 68.4% stake as of April 24, 2026, leaving a relatively small share of the stock in circulation.

Ownership threshold and currency exposure

The holding-company rule requires at least 30% of listed subsidiaries, and the 31.79% stake in Maeil Dairies clears that threshold by just over one percentage point. Overseas subsidiaries in China, Australia and the United States have increased foreign currency exposure.

Overall view

Maeil Holdings is a holding company built around its stake in Maeil Dairies. Annual results peaked in 2023 and fell in both 2024 and 2025. The 2025 operating margin of 2.9% was the lowest since 2022.

Quarterly operating profit, however, has risen for three consecutive quarters from the first quarter of 2026 through the third, with third-quarter revenue up 4.3% and operating profit up 6.9% year on year.

Profit attributable to controlling shareholders remains volatile, falling 25.9% year on year in the third quarter of 2026. The debt-to-equity ratio declined from 90.0% in 2022 to 66.3% in 2025.

Shareholders and the company have taken different positions on dividends, and the treasury share disposal plan runs through 2031. The strengths are the quarterly recovery and lower leverage, while the weaknesses are stagnant subsidiary demand and volatile controlling-shareholder profit.

The next checkpoints are the full-year operating margin in the 2026 annual report and the first-quarter 2027 trend.
