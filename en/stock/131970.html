02

Business structure

Doosan Tesna is a test specialist in system semiconductor back-end processes, centered on wafer test and package test, and it has also run a die preparation (DP) business that sorts tested wafers into individual chips. Its sites are in Pyeongtaek, Ansung and Cheongju.

Founded in 2002 as TESNA, it was acquired by Doosan Group in 2022 and renamed Doosan Tesna. According to a 2025 analysis report on CATCH, most revenue comes from wafer test, which accounts for 95.0% against 5.0% for package test.

Its product families include SoC, CIS, MCU and smart card IC, with CIS, DDI and fingerprint sensors as the core while it expands into automotive power semiconductors.

DP revenue fell from about KRW 10.5 billion in 2024 to about KRW 4.9 billion in 2025 and about KRW 1.8 billion in the first half of 2026, and the board decided in July 2026 to halt the business.

Samsung Electronics foundry is the core customer, and press reports put the single-customer share of first-half 2026 revenue at 94.8%. Press reports also say Doosan Tesna now handles chip testing for big tech companies including NVIDIA and Apple, following its test work on Tesla AI4 chips. In competition, HanaMicron is most often cited as a comparable back-end test company.

04

Earnings analysis

Annual revenue rose from KRW 277.7 billion in 2022 to KRW 338.7 billion in 2023 (up 22.0% year on year) and KRW 373.1 billion in 2024 (up 10.2%), before falling to KRW 303.9 billion in 2025 (down 18.6%). The largest year among 2022 to 2025 was 2024, and the smallest was 2022.

Operating profit went from KRW 67.2 billion in 2022 to KRW 60.8 billion in 2023 (down 9.5%) and KRW 37.9 billion in 2024 (down 37.6%), then swung to an operating loss of KRW 941 million in 2025. Operating margin moved from 24.2% in 2022 to 17.9% in 2023 and 10.2% in 2024, and reached -0.3% in 2025.

Net income attributable to controlling shareholders declined from KRW 52.3 billion in 2022 to KRW 49.1 billion in 2023, KRW 36.8 billion in 2024 and KRW 1.51 billion in 2025, a three-year decline running from 2023 through 2025.

Net income including non-controlling interests moved in the same direction, from KRW 52.3 billion in 2022 to KRW 1.51 billion in 2025.

The company cited weaker demand in downstream markets in the first half of 2025 as the main cause of lower sales and profit, and press reports also pointed to lower utilization in the wafer test business.

The debt ratio fell from 147.6% in 2022 to 61.0% in 2025, and total equity at the end of 2025 was KRW 437.1 billion, all of it attributable to controlling shareholders, against total liabilities of KRW 266.7 billion.

Operating cash flow rose from KRW 168.2 billion in 2022 to KRW 219.2 billion in 2024 before dropping to KRW 147.2 billion in 2025.

On a quarterly basis, operating profit peaked at KRW 15 billion in the fourth quarter of 2025, up 184.3% from the prior quarter, then came in at KRW 5.48 billion in the first quarter of 2026 (down 63.5%), KRW 9.85 billion in the second quarter (up 79.6%) and KRW 1.02 billion in the third quarter (down 89.6% from the prior quarter and down 80.7% from a year earlier).

Third-quarter 2026 revenue of KRW 73.4 billion was down 11.8% from a year earlier. Net income attributable to controlling shareholders rose to KRW 15.9 billion in the fourth quarter of 2025 and KRW 22 billion in the third quarter of 2026, and the last four quarters total KRW 53.4 billion.

Because the gap between third-quarter operating profit and this figure is large, non-operating items and one-off factors need to be checked against the detailed line items of the quarterly report.

05

Industry analysis

The domestic foundry back-end test market is being driven by AI semiconductor demand. One domestic brokerage analyst said AI demand has pushed capacity shortages across the foundry industry (press reports, March 2026).

Analysts also expect test outsourcing volumes to rise as TSMC's capacity constraints shift orders to Korean foundry makers (press reports, April 2026). Brokerages read the industry as turning toward recovery after 2025, when weak demand for IT devices lowered utilization.

Supply shortages across memory are also cited as a factor that could keep foundry order momentum going.

Wafer test expertise is seen as a strength in the company's position, but taking on package test on a turnkey basis could make it a company covering both wafer and package outsourced testing (a late April 2026 report). HanaMicron is named as a competitor among back-end test companies.

Brokerages describe the company as broadening its portfolio from a structure centered on CIS and automotive SoC toward AI accelerator testing. Whether the pace of the foundry recovery and equipment utilization turn into actual revenue is the key question in industry assessments.

06

Outlook

In an April 2026 disclosure, the company resumed investment in a new facility at its second Pyeongtaek plant, with an investment of KRW 230.3 billion and a target completion of November 2027.

The KRW 190.9 billion test equipment, acquired from Teradyne and SEMES, is planned for sequential introduction through the end of 2026. The CIS test equipment investment was revised upward from KRW 171.4 billion to KRW 205.3 billion.

On the customer pipeline, press reports say NVIDIA's Groq 3 LPU testing at Samsung Electronics foundry enters mass production in November 2026, and North American customer CIS testing could start as early as April 2027. Forecasts say LPU test revenue will start contributing from the fourth quarter of 2026.

Analysts expect Apple CIS mass production to run from the second quarter of 2027, with test volumes reflected in earnings. Analysts also see a high likelihood of winning AI5 chip test work, and there is speculation the company will handle wafer testing for next-generation automotive chips.

Brokerage estimates for 2027 include revenue of KRW 494.0 billion and operating profit of KRW 130.1 billion from Meritz Securities, and revenue of KRW 493.2 billion and operating profit of KRW 104.6 billion from iM Securities (press reports, October 2026).

The company said it expects the equipment investments to play an important role in growing revenue and operating profit over the medium to long term. The next checkpoints are whether these volumes show up in quarterly revenue from the fourth quarter of 2026 onward, and whether 2027 CIS volumes lift utilization.

08

Bull factors

Wider AI chip test orders

Press reports say NVIDIA's Groq 3 LPU testing enters mass production in November 2026 and North American CIS testing could start around April 2027. Reports also say the company now handles big tech chip testing including NVIDIA and Apple after Tesla AI4, pointing to customer diversification.

DS Investment & Securities estimated that two large orders could add more than KRW 210 billion in annual revenue (May 2026).

Earnings recovery and the 2027 outlook

Net income attributable to controlling shareholders rose to KRW 15.9 billion in the fourth quarter of 2025 and KRW 22 billion in the third quarter of 2026, totaling KRW 53.4 billion over the last four quarters.

Meritz Securities and iM Securities estimated 2027 operating profit at KRW 130.1 billion and KRW 104.6 billion, respectively (October 2026 reports). If those estimates hold, 2027 operating profit would exceed KRW 67.2 billion, the 2022 figure that was the largest year among 2022 to 2025.

Expanding test infrastructure through capex

Disclosures set out a KRW 190.9 billion test equipment acquisition, a CIS equipment investment raised to KRW 205.3 billion, and a KRW 230.3 billion investment in the second Pyeongtaek plant.

The second plant targets completion in November 2027 and gives the company capacity that can turn into revenue as foundry demand recovers. The company said it expects these investments to play an important role in growing revenue and operating profit over the medium to long term.

09

Bear factors

Customer concentration and foundry dependence

Revenue is heavily concentrated in a single customer. Weakness in the non-memory business at Samsung Electronics, its main customer, was cited as a reason for the 2025 swing to an operating loss.

Because volume swings at one customer flow straight into quarterly revenue, the pace of customer diversification is a key bearish consideration.

Quarterly operating profit volatility

Third-quarter 2026 revenue of KRW 73.4 billion fell 11.8% from a year earlier, and operating profit of KRW 1.02 billion fell 80.7% from a year earlier.

Quarterly operating profit of KRW 5.48 billion in the first quarter of 2026, KRW 9.85 billion in the second and KRW 1.02 billion in the third shows wide swings, which keeps forecasting difficult. Operating margin was -0.3% in 2025.

Business retrenchment and financial burden

DP revenue fell from about KRW 10.5 billion in 2024 to about KRW 4.9 billion in 2025 and about KRW 1.8 billion in the first half of 2026, and the business was set to be halted. Press reports say a sale is being pursued first, with liquidation considered if the sale fails.

If large capex does not translate into higher utilization, fixed costs could weigh heavily, and operating cash flow fell to KRW 147.2 billion in 2025 from KRW 219.2 billion in 2024.

10

Risk factors

Mass production and utilization timing

If LPU mass production or North American CIS test start is delayed, the expected revenue contribution slips. The second Pyeongtaek plant targets November 2027, so any schedule slip would also delay the capacity gain.

Equipment is due to be introduced sequentially through the end of 2026, leaving installation and qualification as variables.

Customer concentration and industry risk

With a single customer accounting for 94.8% of revenue, a drop in Samsung Electronics foundry utilization would hit sales directly. In 2025, weak demand for IT devices lowered utilization and contributed to the swing to an operating loss. If new customers are added slowly, the concentration problem could persist.

Financial and business structure risk

If the DP sale fails, liquidation is under review, which could bring restructuring costs. Total liabilities were KRW 266.7 billion at the end of 2025 with a debt ratio of 61.0%, so further capex could raise financial burden depending on how it is funded.

If quarters with large gaps between operating profit and net income continue, interpretations of earnings quality may diverge.

12

Overall view

Doosan Tesna swung back to profit in recent quarters after its 2025 operating loss, with net income attributable to controlling shareholders totaling KRW 53.4 billion over the last four quarters, but operating profit fell to KRW 1.02 billion in the third quarter of 2026, so the earnings trend is not yet stable.

The declines in operating profit and net income from 2023 through 2025 were attributed to dependence on the Samsung Electronics foundry and lower utilization.

Bullish factors include AI and high-spec test volumes such as Groq 3 LPU and North American CIS, equipment investments of KRW 190.9 billion and KRW 205.3 billion, and brokerage estimates of more than KRW 100 billion in 2027 operating profit.

Bearish factors include a single-customer share of 94.8% of revenue, wide quarterly swings in operating profit, and the financial burden from winding down DP and from capex. The two sets of factors carry comparable weight, and which side prevails depends on points not yet confirmed in reported results.

The next checkpoint is whether AI volumes actually appear in quarterly revenue and operating profit from the fourth quarter of 2026 onward. The annual operating margin in the 2026 business report and first-quarter 2027 results will serve as the benchmarks.
