Report overview

Profit recovery in 2025, controlling-shareholder earnings still a test

02

Business structure

Igloo, founded in 1999, is an AI-based information security company whose offerings include SIEM-based integrated security management, SOAR automation and the hybrid XDR platform. Revenue comes from two streams.

Service revenue, mainly integrated security services, was KRW 111.9 billion in 2025, roughly 78% of the total, while solutions and network/security revenue was KRW 31.4 billion, roughly 22%. Exports were KRW 5.1 billion, a small share, so domestic clients dominate.

Contract backlog at the end of 2025 stood at KRW 34.6 billion, and it includes a public-sector monitoring contract with a delivery deadline in December 2026. In Q4 2025 the company consolidated Pio-link's cloud security business as a subsidiary, widening its scope.

On the product side, the SPiDER ExD hybrid XDR platform received GS certification in March 2026, the AiR security AI agent received an AI+ certification from the Korean Standards Association in July 2026, and a new platform called Plot was launched in July 2026.

R&D spending in 2025 was KRW 10.5 billion, or 7.36% of sales. Competition comes from domestic security software and monitoring providers such as Ahnlab and from the security units of large IT service companies.

04

Earnings analysis

Consolidated revenue rose from KRW 103 billion in 2022 to KRW 107 billion in 2023 (up 3.8%), KRW 111.2 billion in 2024 (up 4.0%) and KRW 143.3 billion in 2025 (up 28.8%).

Operating profit fell from KRW 8.01 billion in 2022 to KRW 5.72 billion in 2023 (down 28.6%), then recovered to KRW 6.07 billion in 2024 (up 6.1%) and KRW 16 billion in 2025 (up 163.8%). The operating margin moved from 7.8% in 2022 and 5.3% in 2023 to 5.5% in 2024 and 11.2% in 2025.

Net profit attributable to controlling shareholders went from KRW 7.56 billion in 2022 to KRW 7.28 billion in 2023 (down 3.7%), KRW 6.24 billion in 2024 (down 14.3%) and KRW 3.63 billion in 2025 (down 41.8%), a three-year decline.

The company attributed the 2025 operating gain to the consolidation of Pio-link, strong sales of new solutions and wider service demand, and said one-time accounting costs from the consolidation weighed on net profit.

On a standalone basis, 2025 revenue was KRW 118.2 billion and operating profit KRW 10.4 billion, up 6.8% and 72.3%. By quarter, Q4 2025 revenue reached KRW 67.2 billion, up 150.9% from the prior quarter, with operating profit of KRW 14.8 billion as the consolidation took effect.

Q1 2026 revenue was KRW 38.9 billion, with an operating loss of KRW 3.18 billion and a net loss attributable to controlling shareholders of KRW 2.56 billion. Q2 2026 turned to an operating profit of KRW 28.47 million on revenue of KRW 45.9 billion.

Q3 2026 revenue was KRW 47.1 billion, up 75.9% year on year, and operating profit was KRW 1.66 billion, up 16.5%. Net profit attributable to controlling shareholders, however, was KRW 929 million in Q3, down 43.4% from the prior quarter and 34.4% from a year earlier, and the last four quarters total KRW 2 billion.

The company said the first-half 2026 consolidated operating loss reflected intangible-asset amortization linked to the Pio-link acquisition.

05

Industry analysis

Igloo operates in security monitoring and security services, a market with demand from both public and private clients. In an August 2026 announcement the company said private-sector security demand is growing quickly.

On the policy side, the national network security framework (N2SF), zero trust and artificial-intelligence transformation are frequently cited together. The company points to AI-based autonomous security operations centers and agentic security operations centers as its key growth drivers.

LS Securities, in a July 2025 report, presented Igloo as ranked first in public-sector zero-trust market share. Kiwoom Securities, in an April 2026 report, covered growth driven by a wider security solutions revenue base.

Competition has two groups: specialist monitoring and security-operations providers on one side, and the security divisions of large IT service companies on the other. Listed peers in the same industry include Ahnlab and Samsung SDS, and product scope and customer base overlap in many areas.

The main industry variables are the size of public procurement and the speed of private investment. Because the public monitoring backlog is large, Igloo's results are exposed to public procurement flows, and expanding the private-sector share is presented as a key task. R&D reached 7.36% of 2025 sales, a rising investment burden needed to keep product competitiveness.

06

Outlook

In its first-half 2026 announcement, the company said it expects steady revenue growth to continue in the second half, citing the full-scale start of synergies with Pio-link and rising private-sector security demand. Q3 2026 revenue of KRW 47.1 billion, up 75.9% year on year, is consistent with that sales momentum.

However, the gap between operating profit and net profit attributable to controlling shareholders is wide, so how far higher sales carry through to earnings still needs confirmation. Net profit attributable to controlling shareholders fell to KRW 929 million in Q3 2026, and that is the central issue for the outlook.

The company announced several product milestones in 2026, including GS certification for SPiDER ExD in March, the AI+ certification for AiR and the launch of Plot in July, and four new patents in August.

Whether these products convert into renewals and new contracts for monitoring services will shape the next revenue trend. Some public-sector monitoring contracts have a delivery deadline in December 2026, so whether follow-on contracts are signed affects the durability of service revenue.

Amortization of intangible assets from the Pio-link acquisition, and whether the consolidated operating margin converges toward the standalone level as that charge fades, are also items to watch.

Since the company decided a cash dividend for 2025, whether a dividend is decided again for 2026 is another point to confirm.

08

Bull factors

Structural improvement in 2025 profitability

Operating profit reached KRW 16 billion in 2025, up 163.8%, and the operating margin rose to 11.2%, the highest in the 2022 to 2025 period. The company attributed the gain to the consolidation of Pio-link and strong sales of new solutions.

Standalone operating profit rose 72.3% to KRW 10.4 billion, and the company presents this as improvement in the core business.

A recurring revenue base centered on monitoring

Service revenue accounted for roughly 78% of 2025 sales, drawn mainly from integrated security services and monitoring contracts. A contract backlog of KRW 34.6 billion at the end of 2025 supports this base.

Because contract-based revenue makes up a large share, sales are less exposed to swings in one-off product purchases.

Expansion of the AI security product lineup

The SPiDER ExD hybrid XDR platform received GS certification in March 2026, and the AiR security AI agent received AI+ certification in July 2026. The Plot platform was launched in July 2026, and in August the company announced four patents for cloud and AI security.

09

Bear factors

Three straight years of lower controlling-shareholder earnings

Net profit attributable to controlling shareholders fell from KRW 7.56 billion in 2022 to KRW 3.63 billion in 2025, a 41.8% drop in the last year alone. Net profit declined even in a year when operating profit rose, which the company links to one-time accounting costs from the consolidation. Whether the decline stays a one-off effect will depend on results in later quarters.

Volatile quarterly earnings in 2026

Q1 2026 brought an operating loss of KRW 3.18 billion and a net loss attributable to controlling shareholders of KRW 2.56 billion. Q2 returned to profit, but operating profit was only KRW 28.47 million.

Q3 net profit attributable to controlling shareholders fell 43.4% from the prior quarter to KRW 929 million, and the last four quarters total KRW 2 billion.

Dependence on public contracts and a small export base

Exports were KRW 5.1 billion in 2025, a small share of sales, so the overseas growth base is limited. A significant part of the KRW 34.6 billion backlog consists of public-sector monitoring contracts, which makes results sensitive to public procurement timing and budgets. The strategy of expanding private-sector revenue has yet to be fully shown in reported results.

10

Risk factors

Consolidation and amortization burden

Since the Pio-link consolidation, amortization of intangible assets has weighed on consolidated operating profit. The company describes the charge as non-cash, but while it continues consolidated profit may remain below the standalone level. How far integration synergies offset this burden is the key question.

Concentration in public contracts

Because the monitoring-centered backlog is weighted toward public clients, delays in procurement or changes in contract terms could affect revenue directly. Part of the KRW 34.6 billion backlog at the end of 2025 is scheduled for delivery in December 2026, so whether follow-on contracts are signed matters.

Rising R&D cost burden

R&D spending in 2025 was KRW 10.5 billion, or 7.36% of sales, up from 5.72% in 2024. As competition in AI security products intensifies, the cost level may stay high. Whether this investment translates into revenue growth and margin gains is a point to track in later results.

Overall view

Igloo restored profitability in 2025, with operating profit and the operating margin rising sharply. Annual revenue grew from KRW 103 billion in 2022 to KRW 143.3 billion in 2025, supported by a monitoring-centered service business.

Net profit attributable to controlling shareholders, however, fell from KRW 7.56 billion in 2022 to KRW 3.63 billion in 2025, a three-year decline that the company linked to one-time accounting costs from a consolidation.

Quarterly results in 2026 alternated between losses and profits, and Q3 revenue rose 75.9% year on year while controlling-shareholder net profit fell to KRW 929 million.

Amortization following the Pio-link acquisition continues to weigh on consolidated earnings, so whether higher sales carry through to controlling-shareholder profit is the central question. On the balance sheet, the debt ratio rose to 28.8% at the end of 2025, while operating cash flow increased to KRW 18.5 billion.
