02

Business structure

Good People, listed on KOSDAQ in November 1997, is a garment manufacturer centered on underwear and apparel manufacturing and sales.

The company says it developed and commercialized IoT-integrated smart innerwear, the first of its kind in the industry, and it is pursuing functional underwear development and production and distribution through a new production base.

Licensed business appears to have held a meaningful share of revenue, and the end of the existing Levi's bodywear license business in 2025 became a main driver of the sales decline.

On the distribution side, the spread of low-priced products and discount stores, outlets and online channels has intensified price competition, which is cited as a background to weaker profitability.

At the regular shareholders meeting on March 30, 2026, the company added online and mobile e-commerce and platform operation to its business purposes, along with intellectual property licensing and investment and M&A.

Business purposes related to landscaping, restaurants and food were removed, leaving the articles centered on underwear, apparel and fashion accessories. Target customers are cited as health- and wellness-conscious consumers and new silver and active senior groups.

Related parties of the largest shareholder hold 44.75 percent, which limits the share of stock that trades freely in the market.

Listed peers in the same textile, fashion and lifestyle sector include Youngone, Hansae, BYC, Handsome and Shinsegae International, which together span production-focused and brand-distribution-focused companies.

04

Earnings analysis

Annual sales rose from KRW 80.5 billion in 2022 to KRW 82.6 billion in 2023, up 2.6 percent year on year, and then to KRW 90.2 billion in 2024, up 9.2 percent, before falling to KRW 72.6 billion in 2025, down 19.5 percent. Among 2022 to 2025, 2024 was the largest year for sales and 2025 the smallest.

The operating result moved from an operating loss of KRW 4.31 billion in 2022 to an operating profit of KRW 1.29 billion in 2023, a swing back into profit, and held at KRW 1.3 billion in 2024, up 0.7 percent.

In 2025 the company swung to an operating loss of KRW 6.08 billion, and it cited the decline in sales from the end of the license business and a heavier fixed-cost burden as the main causes.

The operating margin was -5.4 percent in 2022, 1.6 percent in 2023, 1.4 percent in 2024 and -8.4 percent in 2025, which shows fixed costs were not absorbed as sales shrank.

Net profit attributable to controlling shareholders turned positive at KRW 2.78 billion in 2023, fell to KRW 341 million in 2024, down 87.7 percent, and swung to a net loss of KRW 13.7 billion in 2025.

Quarterly sales went from KRW 19.8 billion in 3Q 2025 to KRW 16.2 billion in 4Q 2025, down 18.2 percent from the prior quarter, and reached a low of KRW 15.3 billion in 1Q 2026, down 5.4 percent.

They then recovered to KRW 19.4 billion in 2Q 2026, up 26.6 percent from the prior quarter, and KRW 20 billion in 3Q 2026, up 2.9 percent from the prior quarter and 0.8 percent from a year earlier.

Operating losses, however, widened from KRW 1.38 billion in 3Q 2025 to KRW 2.39 billion in 3Q 2026, so the sales recovery has not translated into better operating results.

The net loss attributable to controlling shareholders of KRW 8.41 billion in 4Q 2025 was far larger than that quarter's operating loss of KRW 1.49 billion, which suggests non-operating or tax items may have been reflected, although the filed figures alone do not pin down the specific causes.

The net loss attributable to controlling shareholders totals KRW 14.4 billion over the last four quarters. According to a JOBPOST report, cumulative sales for January to September 2026 were KRW 54.7 billion, down 3.1 percent from a year earlier.

Operating cash flow was -KRW 3.5 billion in 2022 and -KRW 5.41 billion in 2023, turned positive at KRW 3.22 billion in 2024, and returned to -KRW 8.2 billion in 2025.

Industry analysis

The domestic underwear market is described as having turned downward as low-priced products and discount stores, outlets and online channels became widespread.

On the demand side, rising interest in health and wellness and a growing population of new silver and active seniors are expected to lift demand for functional products. Analysts also point to an expanding market for climate-related products and a greater premium on trusted brands as consumption polarizes.

The industry backdrop is described as a downturn, which runs in the same direction as the company's 2025 sales decline. Quantitative indicators showing the overall market size and cycle position are not organized in a form that can be compared directly with the filed results.

Within the same textile, fashion and lifestyle sector, production-focused companies such as Youngone and Hansae are listed alongside brand-distribution companies such as Handsome and Shinsegae International.

Good People's point of differentiation is functional innerwear, and maintaining profitability under a distribution structure with intense price competition is seen as the central challenge.

The online and mobile e-commerce and platform operation business added to the articles in March 2026 falls in the same area where distribution structure is changing.

Bull factors

Functional Products and Senior Demand

Rising interest in health and wellness and a growing population of new silver and active seniors are expected to lift demand for functional products. The company says it developed and commercialized IoT-integrated smart innerwear, the first of its kind in the industry. Whether this differentiated product line can ease price competition is the key question.

Legal Groundwork for a Wider Business Scope

Unused business purposes in landscaping, restaurants and food were removed, tightening the focus on the core business.

Bear factors

License Exit and Fixed-Cost Burden

With the Levi's bodywear license business ending in 2025, one pillar of revenue disappeared. Fixed costs became a heavier share of a smaller revenue base, and the operating margin fell to -8.4 percent in 2025. Whether sales can recover to a level that absorbs fixed costs remains the open question.

Five Straight Loss-Making Quarters

The company has posted losses for five consecutive quarters from 3Q 2025 through 3Q 2026. The operating loss in 3Q 2026 was KRW 2.39 billion, wider than a year earlier, and the net loss attributable to controlling shareholders widened to KRW 2.49 billion. The fact that losses did not narrow even as sales rebounded is a bearish factor.

Channel Shifts and Financial Strain

Low-priced products and discount, outlet and online channels have become widespread, and the underwear market is described as having turned downward. The debt ratio rose from 38.0% in 2022 to 48.0% in 2025. Operating cash flow swung back to -KRW 8.2 billion in 2025.

Risk factors

Execution of Online Platform Business

At the March 30, 2026 shareholders meeting, online and mobile e-commerce and platform operation were added to the business purposes. For the purpose addition to translate into sales, channel build-out, staffing and investment must follow, and operating costs could rise along the way.

Whether new channels can earn profit in a distribution structure with intense price competition has not yet been confirmed. Expanding the business scope could weigh on costs more than it improves earnings.

Non-Operating Income Swings

The net loss attributable to controlling shareholders in 4Q 2025 was KRW 8.41 billion, larger than that quarter's operating loss of KRW 1.49 billion. Non-operating or tax items may have been reflected, but the filed figures alone make it hard to pin down the cause.

If such items recur, net income could swing regardless of operating performance. Because the net loss over the last four quarters totals KRW 14.4 billion, the direction of these items could decide the net profit trend.

Share Liquidity and Shareholder Structure

Related parties of the largest shareholder hold 44.75 percent, which limits the share of stock that trades freely. The listed free-float ratio is 55.16 percent, based on data as of December 4, 2025. In a structure with a limited tradable share base, price swings can be larger, which is a risk factor.

Overall view

After the license exit pushed 2025 sales down to KRW 72.6 billion and the company swung to an operating loss of KRW 6.08 billion, sales have risen for two consecutive quarters since 2Q 2026, showing a recovery trend.

However, the operating loss widened to KRW 2.39 billion in 3Q 2026 from a year earlier, and losses have persisted for five consecutive quarters since 3Q 2025.

The net loss attributable to controlling shareholders totals KRW 14.4 billion over the last four quarters, and the debt ratio rose from 38.0 percent in 2022 to 48.0 percent in 2025.

Bullish factors include the sales rebound, demand for functional products and the broader business purposes in the articles, while bearish factors include widening losses, channel shifts and weaker cash flow.

On valuation, the price-to-book ratio below 1 times and the inability to calculate earnings-based multiples frame the picture. The next checkpoints are the operating margin in the 2026 annual report and the size of the loss in the 1Q 2027 quarterly report.
