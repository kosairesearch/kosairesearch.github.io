02

Business structure

DG-ITX (DGI) was founded in February 1998 and develops and sells video recorders (NVR and DVR) and IP cameras used in surveillance systems. CCTV video security equipment remains the core of revenue, and growth in overseas customer sales was one driver of expansion in 2024.

On product design, the company placed a self-developed deep-learning engine on low-cost chipsets rather than high-end GPUs, extending into entry-level NVRs and AI CCTV. Its sales base is set in Europe, Russia, the Middle East, Southeast Asia and Korea.

Segment mix differs across sources: one data provider puts renewable energy at 52% and CCTV video security equipment at 48% of second-quarter 2026 revenue, while another lists DVR/NVR/IP camera at 46.65%, CCTV cameras at 38.71%, R&D income at 12.32% and DVR boards at 2.32%, with no renewable category.

In June 2024 the company signed construction approval and community acceptance services for the Yeonggwang Woawol offshore wind project. Among the same-sector peers, Hanwha Vision is the closest comparison in CCTV equipment.

Earnings analysis

Annual revenue fell from KRW 25.9 billion in 2022 to KRW 13.4 billion in 2023 (down 48.2% year on year), then rose to KRW 21.4 billion in 2024 (up 59.5%) and KRW 25.2 billion in 2025 (up 17.8%), growing for two consecutive years from 2024 to 2025. 2022 was the largest year and 2023 the smallest among 2022 to 2025.

Operating profit of KRW 289 million in 2022 turned into an operating loss of KRW 4.39 billion in 2023, returned to operating profit of KRW 503 million in 2024, and turned into an operating loss of KRW 3.3 billion in 2025. Operating margins were 1.1%, -32.8%, 2.4% and -13.1% over the same years.

Net profit attributable to controlling shareholders was KRW 1.18 billion in 2022, a net loss of KRW 5.09 billion in 2023, a profit of KRW 1.21 billion in 2024 and a net loss of KRW 3.29 billion in 2025.

Net profit including non-controlling interests is reported separately: KRW 1.14 billion, a net loss of KRW 5.03 billion, KRW 1.2 billion and a net loss of KRW 3.3 billion.

The 2024 turnaround was attributed to growth in overseas customer sales and new renewable energy revenue, along with cost improvements and lower selling and administrative expenses.

Operating cash flow turned to KRW -3.41 billion in 2025 from KRW 3.48 billion in 2024, and the debt ratio rose to 28.3% at the end of 2025 from 10.2% in 2024.

At the end of 2025, total equity was KRW 22.9 billion, equity attributable to controlling shareholders was KRW 23 billion and total liabilities were KRW 6.48 billion. Quarterly results are more volatile.

Revenue was KRW 7.65 billion in the third quarter of 2025, KRW 10.4 billion in the fourth quarter (up 35.5% from the prior quarter), KRW 7.37 billion in the first quarter of 2026 (down 28.9%), KRW 9.04 billion in the second quarter (up 22.7%) and KRW 2.53 billion in the third quarter (down 72.1% from the prior quarter and down 67.0% year on year).

Operating results were an operating loss of KRW 712 million, an operating profit of KRW 213 million, an operating loss of KRW 476 million, an operating profit of KRW 1.02 billion and an operating loss of KRW 2.22 billion across the same five quarters.

Second-quarter net profit attributable to controlling shareholders of KRW 1.47 billion exceeded operating profit of KRW 1.02 billion, which appears to reflect non-operating items; the detail should be checked in the periodic filing. Net losses attributable to controlling shareholders over the last four quarters total KRW 1.28 billion.

05

Industry analysis

The CCTV video security equipment market is shifting weight from traditional surveillance hardware built around recorders and cameras toward devices that run AI video analytics on site.

The company's edge AI solutions fit this direction, and its profile presents intelligent video analytics as a potential replacement for conventional CCTV. It cites in-house DSP and ASIC technology as a differentiator.

An NVR built on low-cost chipsets, rather than relying on expensive GPUs, is a cost-structure approach. Demand for surveillance equipment tends to depend on installation and replacement cycles at public and commercial sites, which is one reason the company's quarterly revenue swings widely.

Among same-sector listed companies, Samsung Electro-Mechanics and LG Innotek are component makers focused on camera modules, so their business structures differ from DGI's finished-equipment business.

The renewable energy segment follows a different cycle, with revenue timing tied to permits and construction schedules, and should be analyzed apart from CCTV.

The company's position in its own cycle is best read through its own results: a low in 2023, recovery in 2024 and 2025, and a sharp decline in the third quarter of 2026.

The decision follows a KOSDAQ Committee review on May 11, 2026 that deliberated and resolved to delist the shares, after a two-month improvement period was granted on June 29, 2026, which ended on August 29, 2026.

On the operating side, the third-quarter 2026 revenue and operating loss were sharply weaker, so whether revenue in upcoming filings moves back above that level is the key question. Fourth-quarter 2026 results will be included in the 2026 annual report.

The renewable energy segment is the largest variable: the company signed construction approval and community acceptance services for the Yeonggwang Woawol offshore wind project in June 2024, and the construction schedule and revenue recognition timing will be confirmed in periodic filings.

Overseas customer sales remain a growth lever for CCTV. On governance, two outside directors were re-elected in March 2026, keeping the outside director ratio at 22.2%. The 2026 annual report will show both the audit opinion and whether operating profit is positive for the full year. Given the delisting review history, the audit opinion is as important a checkpoint as earnings.
