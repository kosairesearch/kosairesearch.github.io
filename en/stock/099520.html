DGI: Offshore Wind Orders Meet a Delisting Review

02

Business structure

DGI (KRX: 099520), established in 1998, has long manufactured CCTV video security equipment, mainly network and digital video recorders (NVR/DVR) and IP cameras that convert and store video footage.

The company's 2020 and 2021 annual audit reports received disclaimers of opinion, triggering a formal delisting cause and a trading halt starting in March 2021; it subsequently went through three rounds of corporate rehabilitation proceedings before a pre-approval M&A plan, with an acquirer referred to as HS Innovation, was approved and restructured its governance.

The legacy security equipment business centers on sales of NVR/DVR units and IP cameras and sits in a mature, competitive market contested by large domestic players such as Hanwha Vision and lower-cost overseas suppliers.

Alongside growth in overseas customer sales, DGI began diversifying its revenue base into a new renewable energy segment.

In April 2026, the company disclosed three construction and supply contracts tied to Doosan Enerbility's Yeonggwang Yawol offshore wind farm: a submarine and underground extra-high-voltage (EHV) cable supply contract worth KRW 36.1 billion, an onshore transmission line and substation construction contract worth KRW 11.1 billion, and an equipment logistics contract worth KRW 13.2 billion, for a combined KRW 60.3 billion.

The cable supply contract alone equaled 143.41% of DGI's most recent annual revenue, meaning the new-business contract value already exceeds the company's existing revenue base.

The three contracts run through April 2027, April 2028, and December 2028 respectively, implying revenue will be recognized progressively over several years as construction advances.

The Yawol offshore wind farm itself involves installing thirteen of Doosan Enerbility's 8MW offshore turbines (104MW in total) and targets completion by March 2029, meaning DGI's new-business revenue will track this broader construction timeline.

04

Earnings analysis

DGI's consolidated revenue fell 48.2% from KRW 25.9 billion in 2022 to KRW 13.4 billion in 2023, then rose for two consecutive years to KRW 21.4 billion in 2024 (up 59.5% year on year) and KRW 25.2 billion in 2025 (up 17.8%).

Among 2022-2025, revenue was largest in 2022 (KRW 25.9 billion) and smallest in 2023 (KRW 13.4 billion).

Operating profit followed a different path: KRW 289 million in 2022 turned into an operating loss of KRW 4.39 billion in 2023, swung back to an operating profit of KRW 503 million in 2024, then reverted to an operating loss of KRW 3.3 billion in 2025.

Controlling shareholder net income repeated the same pattern, moving from KRW 1.18 billion in 2022 to a net loss of KRW 5.09 billion in 2023, a net profit of KRW 1.21 billion in 2024, and a net loss of KRW 3.29 billion in 2025.

Operating margin was 1.1% in 2022, -32.8% in 2023, 2.4% in 2024, and -13.1% in 2025, showing swings large relative to the revenue base.

On a quarterly basis, the operating loss narrowed from KRW 1.04 billion in Q2 2025 to KRW 712 million in Q3 2025, turned to an operating profit of KRW 213 million in Q4 2025, reverted to a loss of KRW 476 million in Q1 2026, then swung back to a profit of KRW 1.02 billion in Q2 2026.

Revenue fell 28.9% from KRW 10.4 billion in Q4 2025 to KRW 7.37 billion in Q1 2026, then rose 22.7% to KRW 9.04 billion in Q2 2026, up 105.6% from the year-earlier quarter.

Combined controlling shareholder net income over the last four quarters (Q3 2025 to Q2 2026) was KRW 784 million, a modest net profit despite alternating quarterly losses and gains.

The debt-to-equity ratio rose to 28.3% at the end of 2025 from 10.2% a year earlier, while operating cash flow swung from an inflow of KRW 3.48 billion in 2024 to an outflow of KRW 3.41 billion in 2025.

05

Industry analysis

DGI's legacy CCTV video security equipment business sits in a mature market, contested domestically by large players such as Hanwha Vision and globally by lower-cost Chinese manufacturers.

By contrast, the offshore wind-related equipment and construction segment the company has newly entered in Korea is only at an early commercialization stage.

DGI's key customer, Doosan Enerbility, has built the country's largest offshore wind track record using its own 8MW and 10MW turbines, including a 2025 supply deal for the 100MW Jeju Hallim project, and the 104MW Yeonggwang Yawol project marks the first commercial deployment of its self-developed 8MW turbine.

Doosan Enerbility is also pursuing 10MW turbine supply across additional offshore wind projects, including the 110MW Handong-Pyeongdae, 99MW Dadaepo, and 80MW Aphae sites, suggesting a steadily expanding pipeline at the customer level.

Rising demand from always-on power users such as data centers and semiconductor fabs is cited as a structural tailwind for renewable energy, including offshore wind, over the medium term.

However, DGI does not manufacture turbines or run the overall EPC process; it participates as a subcontractor or supplier of cables, transmission and substation construction, and equipment logistics, which means its revenue is tied to the customer's project execution pace and any additional order wins.

06

Outlook

The nearest checkpoint is DGI's listing eligibility.

The exchange's KOSDAQ Market Committee granted DGI a two-month improvement period on June 29, 2026, and based on the implementation report filed after that period ended, the committee is scheduled to decide on delisting eligibility within 20 business days of filing, by October 22, 2026.

Depending on the outcome, the trading halt in place since March 2021 could either be lifted or lead into delisting procedures.

On the business side, the three Doosan Enerbility-related offshore wind contracts signed in April 2026 run sequentially through April 2027 to December 2028, so revenue recognition tied to construction progress should continue over the coming quarters.

No formal company guidance for full-year 2026 results was identified in available sources. No separate capacity expansion or new product schedule was identified for the legacy security equipment business, leaving the pace of new renewable-energy order wins as the main swing factor for near-term revenue growth.

As Doosan Enerbility pursues turbine supply for additional offshore wind projects such as Handong-Pyeongdae, Dadaepo, and Aphae, whether DGI secures further subcontracting or supply agreements on these projects is also worth monitoring.

Bull factors

Offshore wind contracts adding to top line

The KRW 60.3 billion in contracts signed with Doosan Enerbility in April 2026 exceed DGI's recent annual revenue, creating a revenue stream separate from the legacy security equipment business.

Because the contracts run through 2027 and 2028, revenue could be recognized progressively across multiple quarters as construction advances. This represents a diversification away from a single CCTV-centric revenue structure.

Return to profit in Q2 2026

Q2 2026 operating profit of KRW 1.02 billion and controlling shareholder net income of KRW 1.47 billion both turned positive versus the prior quarter and the year-earlier quarter.

This reversed a Q1 2026 operating loss of KRW 476 million, suggesting new-business revenue recognition may be contributing positively to earnings. The company also posted a smaller profit in Q4 2025, showing earnings can swing positive during active construction periods.

Low leverage provides financial headroom

The debt-to-equity ratio stayed in a low 10-24% range from 2022 to 2024 and remained relatively modest at 28.3% at the end of 2025. Total equity of KRW 22.9 billion at year-end 2025 gives the company some additional leverage capacity for new-business investment or working capital needs. This could act as a financial buffer while executing large construction contracts.

Bear factors

Full-year 2025 swing to losses

Despite revenue growth of 17.8% in 2025, the company swung to an operating loss of KRW 3.3 billion and a controlling shareholder net loss of KRW 3.29 billion. Operating cash flow also reversed from an inflow of KRW 3.48 billion in 2024 to an outflow of KRW 3.41 billion in 2025. Top-line growth did not translate directly into profitability or cash generation.

Delisting uncertainty

DGI's shares have been halted from trading since March 2021 following disclaimers of audit opinion for fiscal years 2020 and 2021.

The KOSDAQ Market Committee granted a two-month improvement period in June 2026, and a decision on delisting eligibility is due by October 22, 2026 after the implementation report was filed, meaning continued listing itself remains uncertain regardless of recent operating improvement.

High quarter-to-quarter earnings volatility

Over the last five quarters, operating profit and controlling shareholder net income flipped direction almost every quarter: a loss in Q2 2025, a loss in Q3 2025, a profit in Q4 2025, a loss in Q1 2026, and a profit in Q2 2026.

This appears to stem from revenue recognized against percentage-of-completion on individual large contracts rather than stable recurring sales. This structure makes it difficult to project quarterly profitability in advance.

Risk factors

Listing eligibility and liquidity risk

DGI has been halted from trading since March 2021 and, after multiple improvement periods, faces a KOSDAQ Market Committee decision on delisting eligibility by October 22, 2026.

If delisting is confirmed, market liquidity for the shares could effectively disappear, and even if the listing is maintained, further review in the future cannot be ruled out.

Customer and project concentration risk

A substantial portion of new revenue is concentrated in a single customer, Doosan Enerbility, and a single project, the Yeonggwang Yawol offshore wind farm.

The contracts include provisions for price adjustments tied to design changes, meaning the timing and scale of revenue recognition could shift if specifications or schedules change during construction. Delays at the customer's project or a failure to win additional orders could slow growth in the new business.

Legacy business profitability volatility risk

The legacy CCTV security equipment business has shown highly volatile profitability, including an operating margin of -13.1% in 2025. Operating margins of 1.1%, -32.8%, 2.4%, and -13.1% from 2022 through 2025 show no clear stabilizing trend, suggesting the segment's structural profitability has not yet settled.

Until the new business represents a larger share of revenue, swings in the legacy segment's earnings could continue to affect overall results.

Overall view

DGI is a KOSDAQ-listed company long centered on CCTV video security equipment that, starting in 2026, has begun diversifying its revenue base through KRW 60.3 billion in new contracts tied to Doosan Enerbility's Yeonggwang Yawol offshore wind project.

For full-year 2025, revenue grew 17.8% to KRW 25.2 billion, yet the company posted an operating loss of KRW 3.3 billion and a controlling shareholder net loss of KRW 3.29 billion, showing growth and profitability did not move in the same direction.

On a quarterly basis, Q2 2026 turned profitable with operating profit of KRW 1.02 billion and controlling shareholder net income of KRW 1.47 billion, but the prior quarter, Q1 2026, was a loss, continuing a pattern of swings.

The company's shares have been halted from trading since March 2021, and based on the implementation report filed in September 2026, a KOSDAQ Market Committee decision on delisting eligibility is due by October 22, 2026, making that decision the most important near-term variable.

The offshore wind-related contracts involve construction running through 2027-2028, so assuming the listing issue is resolved, the pace of revenue and profit recognition over the coming quarters warrants continued monitoring.

The valuation metrics currently displayed reflect a thin earnings base and the unusual circumstance of a trading halt, so the delisting review outcome and the pace of new-business order wins and revenue recognition deserve as much attention as the headline numbers themselves.
