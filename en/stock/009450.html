02

Business structure

Kyung Dong Navien is built around boilers and water heaters, and is widening that core into HVAC (heating, ventilation and air conditioning) and kitchen appliances. Overseas sales make up roughly 70% of revenue, and the overseas share has kept rising since overseas sales first exceeded domestic sales in 2017.

North America accounts for more than 60% of overseas sales, and the company says it holds the No. 1 share in both condensing boilers and water heaters in that market. Its overseas network centers on subsidiaries in the United States, China, Russia and the United Kingdom.

In Central Asia, the company reports the No. 1 share in Kazakhstan and a top-three position in Uzbekistan. In March 2025 it acquired the kitchen appliance unit of SK Magic and launched the Navien Magic brand, adding kitchen equipment as a new business line.

On the heating side, the portfolio is expanding from boilers and water heaters into cooling, heat pumps and air purifiers across HVAC. A 2024 feature reported that the company accounts for about 88% of the industry's total export value.

Domestic sales are exposed to a construction slowdown and weaker replacement demand for boilers. Overseas subsidiaries drive the earnings base, while the domestic business depends heavily on replacement demand.

04

Earnings analysis

Annual revenue rose from KRW 1.16 trillion in 2022 to KRW 1.5 trillion in 2025. Revenue was KRW 1.2 trillion in 2023 (up 3.7% year on year), KRW 1.35 trillion in 2024 (up 12.4%) and KRW 1.5 trillion in 2025 (up 11.0%), so revenue has grown for three consecutive years from 2023 through 2025.

Operating profit climbed from KRW 59.8 billion in 2022 to KRW 132.6 billion in 2024, and reached KRW 143.4 billion in 2025 (up 8.1%). The operating margin rose from 5.1% in 2022 to 8.8% in 2023 and 9.8% in 2024, then eased to 9.5% in 2025.

Net profit attributable to controlling shareholders rose to KRW 83.1 billion in 2023 (up 55.2%) and KRW 124.3 billion in 2024 (up 49.5%), then fell 27.9% to KRW 89.7 billion in 2025.

In 2025 operating profit grew while profit attributable to controlling shareholders fell, so the two measures moved in opposite directions. Quarterly revenue peaked at KRW 438.8 billion in Q4 2025, and then declined for three consecutive quarters through Q3 2026.

Operating profit moved from KRW 7.99 billion in Q3 2025 to KRW 44.7 billion in Q4 2025, KRW 63.8 billion in Q1 2026 and KRW 84.3 billion in Q2 2026, before falling 55.1% to KRW 37.8 billion in Q3 2026.

Press reports say the Q2 2026 figure included a one-time effect from a U.S. tariff refund, and that the base effect weighed on Q3. The Q3 2026 operating margin was 9.9%, compared with 2.6% a year earlier.

The controlling-shareholder net loss of KRW 2.54 billion came below operating profit, and the company said part of the foreign-exchange-related loss from the won's move was reflected in Q3. Nine-month 2026 revenue was KRW 1.196 trillion on a preliminary basis, up 12.5%.

Net profit attributable to controlling shareholders over the last four quarters totaled KRW 141.8 billion. The debt ratio rose from 81.4% in 2023 to 97.6% in 2025, and operating cash flow peaked at KRW 197.4 billion in 2023 before falling to KRW 52.5 billion in 2024 and KRW 71.2 billion in 2025.

05

Industry analysis

Industry analysts describe the domestic boiler market as stalled at roughly 1.2 million to 1.3 million units. Construction weakness and softer replacement demand have narrowed the room for domestic growth.

Central Asia's boiler market is about 300,000 to 350,000 units a year, and government-led infrastructure build-out is expected to lift demand further. In North America, reports say the high-efficiency condensing water heater is proving competitive.

U.S. tariffs weigh on price competitiveness, and a weaker won has partly offset that pressure, according to press coverage. On the demand side, carbon-neutrality policies and electrification are expanding demand for heat pumps and other heating and cooling products.

Shinhan Investment judged that HVAC, which carries higher average selling prices and margins than water heaters, will raise the company's earnings floor over the medium to long term.

In cycle terms, boilers are a replacement-driven mature market, while overseas subsidiaries and HVAC products are shifting the growth center.

The competitive position rests on regional share leadership in condensing boilers and water heaters in North America and Kazakhstan, and on a large share of the industry's exports.

Outlook

In August 2026, Shinhan Investment projected 2026 full-year revenue of KRW 1.6926 trillion and operating profit of KRW 267.9 billion. In the same report, Shinhan Investment projected Q3 2026 revenue of KRW 427 billion and operating profit of KRW 58.7 billion, citing expanding HVAC sales.

Preliminary Q3 2026 results came in at revenue of KRW 382.4 billion and operating profit of KRW 37.8 billion, below that projection. Nine-month 2026 operating profit was KRW 185.9 billion on a preliminary basis, so how much of the full-year projection the fourth quarter adds is the next check.

The company has said it will accelerate its shift from a boiler-centered business to an HVAC platform covering heating, cooling and air conditioning. Reports from August 2026 say the company is strengthening its push in North America with a new high-efficiency gas furnace.

Heat pumps are being developed as a next-generation growth engine. R&D spending in Q1 2026 was KRW 13 billion, or 3.05% of revenue. The company is also pursuing participation in a Jeju district-heating electrification program, which receives KRW 14.448 billion in state funding.

A 2025 report said the company is pursuing a 2028 target, its 50th anniversary, of KRW 300 billion in revenue from its living-appliance unit and KRW 1 trillion in total domestic sales. Whether those targets materialize depends on the pace of overseas growth and any recovery in domestic replacement demand.

Risk factors

Public project participation

Kyung Dong Navien is pursuing participation in a Jeju district-heating electrification program, which receives KRW 14.448 billion in state funding. Whether the company is selected and whether the program converts into actual sales has not yet been determined.

Delays in project schedules or budget execution could push back the expected boost to electrification-related product demand. Public projects can change in scope depending on government budgets and review criteria, which makes the size of the earnings contribution hard to estimate in advance.

North American product launch

Kyung Dong Navien is strengthening its push in North America with a new high-efficiency gas furnace. It is not yet confirmed whether the new product will convert into sales large enough to sustain the company's No. 1 positions in condensing boilers and water heaters.

If sales fall short of expectations, the next growth pillar in North America could be delayed, and the pace of growth in overseas-driven earnings could slow. Competitor responses may also change how quickly the new product gains share.

New-business investment and financial burden

R&D spending in Q1 2026 was KRW 13 billion, or 3.05% of revenue. The debt ratio rose from 81.4% in 2023 to 97.6% at the end of 2025. If new businesses such as Navien Magic and HVAC fail to deliver the expected profitability, the financial burden could grow.

Overall view

Kyung Dong Navien has grown revenue to KRW 1.5 trillion since 2022, supported by overseas sales near 70% of the total and the No. 1 share in North America. Operating profit rose to KRW 143.4 billion in 2025, and the operating margin stayed at a high level, 9.8% in 2024 and 9.5% in 2025.

Bullish factors include North American share, HVAC expansion and Central Asian growth, while bearish factors include a stalled domestic market, currency and tariff exposure, and the 2025 decline in profit attributable to controlling shareholders.
