02

Business structure

Coweaver was established in February 2000 and listed on KOSDAQ in 2001 as a maker of wired and wireless telecommunications equipment. Its revenue is mainly generated from sales of PTN products, with POTN, ROADM, and carrier Ethernet equipment forming the core lineup.

The company is expanding into next-generation products incorporating AI, quantum encryption, and high-speed switching to broaden its market presence from this existing equipment base.

Coweaver is known to be the sole domestic manufacturer that produces ROADM equipment in-house, and it has a track record of carrying out major national infrastructure projects, including the backbone network for KT's national disaster safety communications network, upgrades to Korea Electric Power Corporation's high-speed transmission network, and construction of national defense integrated data centers.

Its core customers are the country's three major telecom carriers, KT, SK Telecom, and LG Uplus, and it holds proprietary technology spanning the full transport spectrum, from ROADM/POTN for backbone networks and PTS/OTN for metro networks to 5G-PON and optical network terminals for access networks.

The company wholly owns subsidiary Weaverbridge, through which it provides integrated communications solutions ranging from terminal equipment to network infrastructure.

Competitively, it faces rivalry from domestic specialized firms such as Woori-Net, Dasan Networks, Ubiquoss, and Telefield, alongside pricing and technology competition from larger overseas equipment makers.

More recently, the company has advanced an integrated quantum-encryption solution that houses QKD blades, PQC server blades, and QENC blades within a single ROADM chassis, combining transport and security functions into one system.

04

Earnings analysis

Annual revenue fell from KRW 89.6 billion in 2022 to KRW 83.4 billion in 2023 (down 7.0% year over year) and KRW 46.8 billion in 2024 (down 43.8%), marking two consecutive years of decline, before rebounding to KRW 62.6 billion in 2025 (up 33.7%).

Within this span, 2022 was the largest revenue year and 2024 the smallest. Operating profit swung from KRW 297 million in 2022 to an operating loss of KRW 2.6 billion in 2023, with the loss widening to KRW 7.63 billion in 2024, before turning positive again at KRW 306 million in 2025.

Net income attributable to controlling shareholders followed a similar pattern, falling from KRW 4.49 billion in 2022 to KRW 799 million in 2023 (down 82.2%), swinging to a net loss of KRW 4.07 billion in 2024, and then turning positive at KRW 3.47 billion in 2025.

On a quarterly basis, revenue fell from KRW 14.2 billion in Q2 2025 to KRW 13.2 billion in Q3 2025 (down 6.9% quarter over quarter), then surged to KRW 26.4 billion in Q4 2025 (up 99.0%), before declining for two straight quarters to KRW 12.4 billion in Q1 2026 (down 52.9%) and KRW 12 billion in Q2 2026 (down 3.5% quarter over quarter, down 15.9% year over year).

Operating profit turned positive in Q4 2025 at KRW 4.59 billion but then swung to losses of KRW 2.19 billion in Q1 2026 and KRW 1.27 billion in Q2 2026, marking two consecutive quarters of operating losses, with the loss narrowing versus the prior quarter but widening versus the year-earlier quarter.

Net income attributable to controlling shareholders similarly moved from a KRW 5.47 billion profit in Q4 2025 to losses of KRW 1.71 billion in Q1 2026 and KRW 422 million in Q2 2026, with the latter narrowing from the prior quarter but reversing from a year-earlier profit.

Over the trailing four quarters from Q3 2025 through Q2 2026, cumulative net income attributable to controlling shareholders totaled KRW 2.81 billion, reflecting a pattern in which order concentration toward year-end amplifies swings in quarterly figures.

05

Industry analysis

Korea's optical transport equipment market is heavily dependent on backbone and metro network investment by the three major telecom carriers and on upgrades to dedicated networks by public institutions.

Domestic makers of transport network equipment are limited to a small group including Coweaver, Woori-Net, Dasan Networks, Ubiquoss, and Telefield, with the market centered on technically capable smaller firms.

More recently, the spread of AI data centers has highlighted the need for high-speed optical transport networks, putting such equipment back in focus as core infrastructure linking data centers.

At the same time, the government's push to adopt quantum-encryption and post-quantum cryptography (PQC) across national core networks is cited as a potential new source of demand for transport equipment makers.

However, as of mid-October 2026, quantum-themed stocks, including Coweaver, saw declines amid short-term profit-taking, and the company also experienced institutional and foreign selling ahead of third-quarter earnings, pointing to a gap between thematic trading flows and underlying results.

On the competitive front, pricing pressure from larger overseas equipment makers and order competition among domestic peers persist, and the industry cycle is heavily tied to the timing of carrier capital expenditure.

Outlook

At the April 2026 World IT Show, the company unveiled a next-generation integrated quantum-encryption solution housing QKD, PQC, and QENC blades within a single ROADM chassis, presenting a path toward combining transport and security functions in one piece of equipment.

This solution is reported to have drawn interest from telecom carriers and public institutions for its potential to lower construction costs and operational complexity.

Tied to the government's push to convert national core networks to quantum security, there is speculation about further orders from existing customers such as the national disaster safety communications network, Korea Electric Power Corporation's transmission network, and defense-related projects.

However, because quantum-related revenue is not separately disclosed in the financial statements, the timing and scale of any actual order conversion remain to be confirmed through future disclosures.

The company has stated its intention to accelerate commercialization by expanding its next-generation product lineup, including AI-based optical line systems.

In the near term, given the back-to-back operating losses in the first and second quarters of 2026, order execution and revenue recognition from the third quarter onward are seen as key variables for whether full-year profitability can be sustained.

Bull factors

Technical Position in the Quantum-Encryption Value Chain

Coweaver is one of the few domestic makers that produces ROADM equipment in-house, and it is pursuing technical differentiation by integrating QKD, PQC, and QENC blades into a single chassis that combines transport and security functions.

Its track record on national core infrastructure projects, including the national disaster safety network, KEPCO's transmission network, and defense integrated data centers, is cited as a factor that could position it as an early contact point for future quantum-security conversion projects.

This remains a forward-looking possibility, however, as quantum-related revenue has not been separately disclosed to date.

Stable Customer Base Spanning All Three Telecom Carriers

The company has secured KT, SK Telecom, and LG Uplus as core customers, reducing excessive reliance on any single carrier. Through subsidiary Weaverbridge, it offers integrated solutions spanning terminal equipment to network infrastructure, broadening its customer relationships.

That said, the tendency for quarterly revenue to swing widely depending on the timing of carrier capital expenditure execution remains unchanged.

Financial Stability From a Low Debt Ratio

The debt-to-equity ratio has steadily declined from 28.2% in 2022 to 16.1% in 2025, and total equity at the end of 2025 stood at KRW 158.5 billion, well above total liabilities of KRW 25.5 billion. This financial cushion can be viewed positively given the operating volatility inherent in the business. That said, financial stability does not by itself reduce the volatility of operating profit.

Bear factors

Renewed Operating Losses in the First Half of 2026

Despite the return to annual profitability in 2025, the company posted operating losses in both the first and second quarters of 2026. Second-quarter revenue also fell 15.9% year over year, suggesting demand recovery has not been clearly established.

Given the order structure in which revenue and profit tend to concentrate in the fourth quarter, the final direction of full-year results could hinge heavily on second-half performance.

High Quarter-to-Quarter Earnings Volatility

Both swings and declines between quarters have been large, with Q4 2025 revenue surging 99.0% quarter over quarter before plunging 52.9% in Q1 2026. Operating profit has also alternated between gains and losses quarter to quarter, making it difficult to judge a trend from any single quarter's results.

This volatility stems from the business's dependence on the ordering and inspection schedules of telecom carriers and public institutions.

Gap Between Thematic Trading Flows and Earnings

Share price movements have been pronounced whenever the quantum-encryption theme gains attention, but because quantum-related revenue is not separately disclosed in the financial statements, it is difficult to gauge the actual profit contribution.

In mid-October 2026, institutional and foreign investors sold ahead of third-quarter earnings, weighing on theme-related stocks broadly. Should thematic trading flows fade without underlying earnings support, volatility could resurface.

Risk factors

Order Concentration and Revenue Volatility

Revenue depends on orders from a small number of telecom carriers and public institutions, and the structure in which orders and inspections concentrate in specific quarters makes quarterly performance difficult to forecast.

If a pattern of surging revenue in one quarter, as seen in Q4 2025, followed by sharp declines in subsequent quarters recurs, full-year earnings volatility could also increase.

Intensifying Competition and Pricing Pressure

The industry structure includes order competition among domestic peers as well as pricing pressure from larger overseas telecom equipment makers. If carrier capital expenditure slows, competition for a limited market could intensify further.

Unconfirmed Quantum Revenue Share and Small-Cap Characteristics

While news of new quantum-encryption products and certifications continues to emerge, the share of related revenue within overall results is not separately broken out in regulatory filings.

Given the company's small market capitalization, the potential for wide share price swings driven by thematic trading flows also warrants consideration.
