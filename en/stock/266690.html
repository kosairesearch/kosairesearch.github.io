Report overview

Three straight quarters of falling sales; mass production is the test

Duksan Navcours lifted annual operating profit to KRW 4.08 billion in 2025, but quarterly revenue has fallen for three straight quarters since the fourth quarter of 2025. The pace at which its large programs move into full production from 2027 is the next key checkpoint.

02

Business structure

Duksan Navcours is a navigation and anti-jamming (electronic warfare countermeasure) specialist founded in 2012, which says it performs design, development, production and testing in-house.

Designated a defense contractor in 2013, it has supplied guided weapons, aircraft and drones, UAVs, launch vehicles and satellites, and the company says it takes part in 82% of the 72 domestic mass-produced weapon systems to which its products can apply.

Its core product is the navigation unit for the Chunmoo guided rocket, and it is widening its scope to anti-jamming functions, fuzes for 155mm shells, RF seeker signal processing for the LAMD air defense system, counter-drone jamming and deception devices, and mid-altitude UAV data links, of which the data link is already in mass production.

According to company investor-relations materials, 2025 revenue came from Hanwha Aerospace (34%), LIG Defense & Aerospace (33%), Hyundai Rotem (15%) and others (18%). Press reports citing the securities filing put the single largest customer share in 2025 at 35.89%, which differs from the investor-relations figure.

The top two customers accounted for 68.47% of revenue in 2025 and 73.31% in the first quarter of 2026. Its products are mounted on platforms such as Chunmoo, the K9 self-propelled howitzer and the K2 tank, so volume moves with the prime contractors' overseas orders.

In space, it supplied satellite navigation receivers for Naro and Nuri launches 1 to 5 and is pursuing low-Earth-orbit satellite programs, and it participates across the satellite, ground and user segments of the Korean Positioning System (KPS). Its parent, DUKSAN HIMETALCO, holds about 51.1%.

04

Earnings analysis

Annual results have recovered from the operating loss of 2023. Revenue fell from KRW 35 billion in 2022 to KRW 31.4 billion in 2023, down 10.4% year on year, then rose to KRW 45.2 billion in 2024 (up 44.1%) and KRW 48.5 billion in 2025 (up 7.2%).

Of the years from 2022 to 2025, 2025 was the largest and 2023 the smallest. Operating profit moved from KRW 1.38 billion in 2022 to an operating loss of KRW 5.83 billion in 2023, returned to a profit of KRW 2 billion in 2024, and reached KRW 4.08 billion in 2025, up 103.6%.

The operating margin was 3.9% in 2022, -18.6% in 2023, 4.4% in 2024 and 8.4% in 2025. Net income attributable to controlling shareholders swung from a net loss of KRW 4.6 billion in 2023 to KRW 1.35 billion in 2024 and KRW 3.67 billion in 2025, up 171.0%.

Net income including non-controlling interests was reported at the same amounts in each year. The debt-to-equity ratio fell from 167.5% in 2023 to 72.6% in 2025. At the end of 2025, total equity was KRW 30.6 billion, all attributable to controlling shareholders, and total liabilities were KRW 22.2 billion.

Operating cash flow rose from KRW 905 million in 2022 to KRW 2.09 billion in 2023 and KRW 7.46 billion in 2024, then turned to -KRW 420 million in 2025. Profits were positive while cash flow was negative, and the company said some negative operating cash flow appears despite a profitable income statement.

Quarterly revenue peaked at KRW 17.4 billion in the fourth quarter of 2025, up 58.2% from the prior quarter, and then declined for three straight quarters to KRW 12 billion in the first quarter of 2026 (down 31.0%), KRW 11.8 billion in the second quarter (down 1.5%) and KRW 10.6 billion in the third quarter (down 9.9%).

Operating profit was KRW 1.95 billion in the fourth quarter of 2025 (up 137.0% from the prior quarter), then KRW 227 million, KRW 524 million and KRW 58.67 million across the first three quarters of 2026, with the third quarter down 92.9% year on year.

Revenue for the first three quarters of 2026 totals KRW 34.4 billion (summed from disclosed quarters), so reaching the 2026 expected revenue of KRW 60.3 billion would require KRW 25.9 billion in the fourth quarter.

The company has said second-half results should improve because defense inspections and deliveries cluster in the second half.

05

Industry analysis

Defense demand is expected to benefit directly from rising spending on defense force improvement within the defense budget. Because the military is the final customer, that demand reaches the company through prime contractors.

Navigation units and data links are gaining importance in modern warfare involving drones and missiles, and press reports note that the company's core product lines are aligned with that area.

The counter-drone market is growing alongside the expanding UAV threat, and the company says it signed a memorandum of understanding with the Korea Drone Industry Association on drone and counter-drone system development.

On exports, products mounted on Chunmoo, K9 and K2 are linked to prime contractors' overseas contracts, and the company says it is currently in direct export talks with three countries. In space, the Korean Positioning System is targeting commercialization in 2035.

The industry is moving from development into production. The company says many large programs enter full production from 2027 onward, and it reflected in its estimates the sector feature that fixed-cost burden falls once development shifts to mass production.

On competition, the company says both evaluating agencies in its technology-special listing assessment gave it an A grade, and it cites NADCAP Gold certification as the first for a Korean PBA manufacturer.

It is classified under semiconductors, but within the Duksan group it is positioned as a defense and aerospace affiliate, separate from the semiconductor materials and components business. Its orders depend on the order flow of prime contractors such as Hanwha Aerospace, its largest customer.

06

Outlook

At its listing investor briefing, the company set out a plan to grow revenue from KRW 48.5 billion in 2025 to KRW 60.3 billion in 2026, KRW 115.4 billion in 2028 and KRW 174.7 billion in 2030. The average annual revenue growth rate presented in its materials is 29.2%.

The estimates in its securities filing show 2028 revenue of KRW 115.37 billion, operating profit of KRW 22.2 billion and net income of KRW 19.0 billion, and the company says it prepared them conservatively with its lead underwriter.

Press reports note that reaching these estimates requires 2.38 times 2025 revenue and 5.18 times 2025 net income. On orders, the total order value at end-March 2026 was KRW 68.9 billion, of which KRW 12 billion was recognized as first-quarter revenue and KRW 56.9 billion remained as backlog.

The backlog stood at KRW 55.7 billion at end-June 2026. Production capacity utilization was 72.00% in 2025 and 72.15% in the first quarter of 2026.

Because defense products need dedicated production lines for each project, press reports noted that the current space may not absorb new volume once development programs move into production.

Of the offering proceeds, KRW 20.3 billion goes to securing a factory and equipment and KRW 16.4 billion to research and development. Rather than building a new plant, the company plans to buy existing facilities to shorten permit and construction periods.

The company has proposed cash dividends from 2027 for five years, funded by 10% of the prior year's standalone operating profit. The next checkpoint is the 2026 annual report, which will show how close annual revenue came to the KRW 60.3 billion target and the fourth-quarter operating margin.

08

Bull factors

Earnings recovery

After an operating loss of KRW 5.83 billion in 2023 and a return to profit in 2024, operating profit rose to KRW 4.08 billion in 2025, an 8.4% margin. Net income attributable to controlling shareholders reached KRW 3.67 billion, up 171.0%. The operating margin has risen from 3.9% in 2022 to 8.4% in 2025.

Backlog and defense demand

How much of the backlog converts into actual revenue is the next point to check. Rising defense force improvement spending is cited as a market expansion factor for its products. Because its products are mounted on prime contractors' platforms, the prime contractors' orders translate into its volume.

Room for expansion

The mid-altitude UAV data link is already in mass production, while Chunmoo anti-jamming, fuzes and counter-drone devices are in development or production. The company says it is in direct export talks with three countries. Offering proceeds of KRW 20.3 billion are planned for factories and equipment to expand capacity.

Bear factors

Slowing quarterly profit

Operating profit fell from KRW 1.95 billion in the fourth quarter of 2025 to KRW 58.67 million in the third quarter of 2026, down 92.9% year on year. Revenue has also declined for three straight quarters, totaling KRW 34.4 billion for the first three quarters of 2026. The second-half recovery the company expected has not yet appeared in the third quarter.

Customer concentration and cash flow

The top two customers accounted for 73.31% of revenue in the first quarter of 2026. Operating cash flow was -KRW 420 million in 2025 and roughly -KRW 1.5 billion in the first quarter of 2026, a wide gap from reported profits. The company says it manages liquidity through advance and interim payment collections.

Gap to targets

Against the 2026 target of KRW 60.3 billion, the first three quarters total KRW 34.4 billion, which would require KRW 25.9 billion in the fourth quarter. Reaching the 2028 estimates requires 2.38 times 2025 revenue and 5.18 times 2025 net income.

Defense products need dedicated lines, so delays in securing the factory could slow the response to mass-production orders.

10

Risk factors

Customer concentration and program delays

Its customers are mainly large defense contractors and government research institutes, so revenue can swing with budgets and project progress. Delays in programs hit reported results directly.

With the top two customers representing a high share, shifts in one customer's order timing show up sharply in quarterly results.

Post-listing share supply

The tradable share ratio is 40.2% on listing day, rising to 47.0% after three months, 48.9% after one year and 100% after three years. Press reports flagged overhang risk right after the listing. The stepwise rise in tradable shares is a key variable for share supply.

Parent control and dual-listing structure

DUKSAN HIMETALCO holds about 51.1% and the company listed as the first case granted an exception to the principle against dual listings. The listing required the parent's general shareholder consent and an in-kind dividend of shares.

The company says it is separated from its parent in business, customers and facilities, and that transactions with the parent equal 0.03% of its revenue.

12

Overall view

Duksan Navcours' disclosed results show a recovery path, from an operating loss in 2023 to a profit in 2024 and KRW 4.08 billion in operating profit in 2025.

Quarterly trends are different: revenue peaked at KRW 17.4 billion in the fourth quarter of 2025 and has declined for three straight quarters through the third quarter of 2026, with operating profit falling to KRW 58.67 million.

According to press reports, the backlog of KRW 55.7 billion at end-June 2026 equals 92.3% of expected 2026 revenue, which the company cites as evidence of visibility.

The business depends on defense prime contractors, and the top two customers accounted for 73.31% of revenue in the first quarter of 2026, so order stability and customer concentration coexist.

Operating cash flow was -KRW 420 million in 2025 and roughly -KRW 1.5 billion in the first quarter of 2026, a wide gap from reported profits. The company's targets of KRW 60.3 billion for 2026 and KRW 115.4 billion for 2028 assume large programs move into production from 2027.

Bullish factors are the earnings recovery, the backlog, defense and space demand, and options such as counter-drone, exports and KPS. Bearish factors are the quarterly slowdown, customer concentration, weak cash flow, the gap to estimated results and post-listing share supply.

The next checkpoints are annual revenue and operating cash flow in the 2026 annual report, the expansion of the tradable share ratio three months after listing, and whether large programs enter production from 2027.
