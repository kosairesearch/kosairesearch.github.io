02

Business structure

Duksan Navcours is a defense and aerospace company that designs, develops, produces and tests special-purpose navigation and anti-jamming products, including satellite navigation receivers, integrated navigation units, antennas and jamming-countermeasure equipment.

After being designated a defense contractor in 2013, it took part in major weapon systems and national space projects. Its products are fitted on Chunmoo, the K9 howitzer and the K2 tank, so exports of those systems lift its own supply.

In space, its chief executive said the company has taken part in every Nuri launch vehicle from the first to the fifth. Customers include domestic defense groups such as Hanwha Aerospace, LIG Defense & Aerospace and Hyundai Rotem, plus government agencies.

In 2025 a single customer accounted for 35.89% of revenue and the top three for 83.43%, and 99.46% of revenue came from the domestic market, so overseas diversification is at an early stage. No confirmed segment revenue split is available; reports describe guided weapons and other defense programs as the revenue core.

For new businesses, it is developing a trajectory-correcting fuze for 155mm shells, an RF seeker signal-processing unit for the long-range artillery interception system, and counter-drone systems, while a datalink for medium-altitude drones is already in mass production.

On competition, integrated GNSS/INS units (EGI) were long dominated by overseas aerospace groups, and Duksan Navcours was the first domestic company to take up localization.

In ownership, the chain runs from Duksan Holdings to DUKSAN HI METALCO to Duksan Navcours, with DUKSAN HI METALCO holding about 61% before the IPO.

04

Earnings analysis

Annual revenue slipped from KRW 35 billion in 2022 to KRW 31.4 billion in 2023 (down 10.4%), then rose to KRW 45.2 billion in 2024 (up 44.1%) and KRW 48.5 billion in 2025 (up 7.2%). That is two straight years of growth from 2024 to 2025, and 2025 is the largest year of 2022-2025.

Operating results swung from an operating loss of KRW 5.83 billion in 2023 to an operating profit of KRW 2 billion in 2024, and reached KRW 4.08 billion in 2025 (up 103.6%). The operating margin improved from -18.6% in 2023 to 4.4% in 2024 and 8.4% in 2025.

Net income attributable to controlling shareholders was KRW 3.67 billion in 2025, up 171.0%, and the debt-to-equity ratio fell from 167.5% in 2023 to 72.6% in 2025. Operating cash flow, however, was -KRW 420 million in 2025, down from KRW 7.46 billion in 2024.

By quarter, revenue peaked at KRW 17.4 billion in Q4 2025, then fell to KRW 12 billion in Q1 2026 and KRW 11.8 billion in Q2 2026, two consecutive quarterly declines.

Q2 2026 revenue was down 5.6% year on year, operating profit of KRW 524 million was down 67.6%, and net income attributable to controlling shareholders of KRW 483 million was down 71.2%.

Q1 2026 operating profit of KRW 227 million was 88.4% below Q4 2025 (KRW 1.95 billion) and rose 130.8% quarter on quarter in Q2, but the absolute level remains small.

Net income attributable to controlling shareholders over the last four quarters (Q3 2025 to Q2 2026) totaled KRW 2.89 billion, below the KRW 3.67 billion for full-year 2025.

The company explained that inspections and deliveries concentrate in the second half in the defense business, and it expects second-half earnings to improve.

05

Industry analysis

The service classifies the company under semiconductors, but Duksan Navcours actually operates in defense and aerospace navigation; the classification reflects its parent, the semiconductor materials company DUKSAN HI METALCO, being in the same group.

Demand therefore depends on defense budgets, weapon-system production schedules and space programs rather than memory or equipment cycles. With GPS jamming and spoofing frequent in recent wars, anti-jamming technology is regarded as a factor that governs guided-weapon accuracy and operational continuity.

Because the military is the end user, one analysis said higher defense-capability improvement spending should be a market-expansion opportunity for the company. It also takes part in the satellite, ground and user segments of the Korean Positioning System, targeted for commercialization in 2035.

On cycle position, the programs of the prime contractors are the variable. The company expects major defense programs to enter mass production one after another from 2027 onward.

That fits a structure in which fixed-cost burdens ease once development turns into production, but a delayed transition could lengthen the revenue gap. Weakness among recent new listings is also relevant, since six of the nine most recent listings were reported to be trading below their IPO prices.

06

Outlook

The company set annual revenue goals of KRW 60.3 billion for 2026, KRW 83.1 billion for 2027, KRW 115.4 billion for 2028, KRW 140.2 billion for 2029 and KRW 174.7 billion for 2030. Its order backlog at the end of June 2026 was KRW 55.7 billion, equal to 92.3% of expected 2026 revenue of KRW 60.3 billion.

However, reported revenue for Q1 and Q2 2026 totals about KRW 23.8 billion, so reaching the goal requires large deliveries and revenue recognition in the second half. Programs expected to expand production include the anti-jamming upgrade for Chunmoo guided rockets and the trajectory-correcting fuze for 155mm shells.

On the unit-price effect of anti-jamming on Chunmoo, the chief executive said the selling price rises more than fourfold versus the existing product.

Overseas, it is in direct export talks with three countries, and the company said its selection for a defense SME export program gives it a foothold for direct overseas exports. In space, it announced on October 7, 2026 that it supplied and installed a satellite navigation receiver and antenna on the fifth Nuri launch.

The IPO proceeds are planned for production capacity expansion and research and development. These targets are company figures, and the specific basis for large programs has not been disclosed for security reasons.

08

Bull factors

Backlog linked to the annual target

The June 2026 backlog of KRW 55.7 billion equals 92.3% of expected 2026 revenue of KRW 60.3 billion. Defense inspections and deliveries cluster in the second half, and in 2025 Q4 was also the largest quarter at KRW 17.4 billion. If deliveries proceed on schedule, the first-half weakness could be made up in the second half.

Stronger balance sheet and IPO proceeds

The debt-to-equity ratio fell from 167.5% in 2023 to 72.6% in 2025, and total equity at the end of 2025 was KRW 30.6 billion. The IPO of 3 million new shares raised KRW 43.8 billion, adding funds for production facilities and R&D from Q3 2026.

Because the business requires building inventory first, the larger capital base increases capacity to absorb working-capital needs.

Production ramp and export-linked demand

The company expects major defense programs to enter mass production one after another from 2027 onward. Its products are fitted on Chunmoo, K9 and K2, so export contracts won by prime contractors feed through to its supply. A higher unit price from anti-jamming content would leave room for operating margin improvement.

Bear factors

Lower first-half 2026 profit

Q2 2026 operating profit of KRW 524 million was down 67.6% year on year, and net income attributable to controlling shareholders of KRW 483 million fell 71.2%. While revenue declined for two straight quarters, profit stayed far below the KRW 1.95 billion operating profit of Q4 2025. If the second-half improvement does not materialize as planned, the gap to the annual target widens.

Customer concentration and low overseas share

In Q1 2026 the top two customers' revenue share rose to 73.31%, higher than in 2025. Customers are mostly large domestic defense groups and government research institutes, so budgets and project delays affect results. With 99.46% of revenue from the domestic market, overseas diversification is at an early stage.

Gap between profit and cash flow

Net income attributable to controlling shareholders was KRW 3.67 billion in 2025, yet operating cash flow was -KRW 420 million. According to the registration statement, the operating cash outflow widened to KRW 1.51 billion in Q1 2026 alone.

This is interpreted as reflecting the need to secure inventory first and collect cash only after delivery and inspection.

Risk factors

Supply and share overhang

The offering had no buyback right and no over-allotment option, and 40.11% of shares were tradable on the first day. The lock-up commitment ratio among institutions was 4.05%. Given the large price swings after listing, the schedule of lock-up expirations is a supply variable.

Program timing and orders

Main customers are large defense groups and government research institutes, and project delays hurt the company's results. Mass production of large programs starts in 2027 or later, so until then revenue depends heavily on delivery schedules of existing programs. The basis for the medium-term revenue targets is undisclosed, which makes outside verification difficult.

Governance and shareholder returns

As the first company granted an exception to the general ban on dual listing, how the parent's shareholder protections are carried out draws attention. DUKSAN HI METALCO plans to distribute 150,000 Duksan Navcours shares as a dividend in kind to general shareholders, at the earliest after late September 2027. Duksan Navcours itself has a dividend yield of 0.0%.

Overall view

In defense and aerospace navigation, Duksan Navcours turned profitable in 2024 after an operating loss of KRW 5.83 billion in 2023, and recovered to an operating profit of KRW 4.08 billion and an operating margin of 8.4% in 2025.

Its debt-to-equity ratio fell to 72.6%, and the KRW 43.8 billion IPO raised its financial capacity. On the other hand, revenue fell for two straight quarters in Q1 and Q2 2026, and Q2 2026 operating profit was 67.6% below a year earlier.

The company cites a June 2026 backlog of KRW 55.7 billion and second-half delivery concentration for its KRW 60.3 billion annual revenue goal, but reaching it requires a large step-up in second-half revenue.

The top-three customer share of 83.43%, a domestic share above 99% and negative operating cash flow belong in any assessment of the bullish case.

The share price has fallen below half the IPO price shortly after listing and the earnings multiple is high relative to earnings, so the market is weighing whether the post-2027 production ramp materializes. The nearest checkpoints are the Q3 report in mid-November 2026 and the annual report in March 2027.
