After a Pneumatic-Led Turn to Profit, Robotics Is the Next Test

02

Business structure

TPC Robotics was founded in 1979 as Danhae Pneumatic Industrial and listed on KOSDAQ in 2001. It adopted its current name in March 2026 and operates two domestic plants along with three plants in China and one in the United States through overseas affiliates.

Its business is organized into a pneumatic division, a robotics (motion) division, a bio and 3D printing division, and a collaborative robot division.

The pneumatic division makes actuators, directional control devices, FRL units and accessories, while the robotics division produces orthogonal robots, linear motors and linear robots. As of December 2025, pneumatic products accounted for about 78% of key product sales and motion products for about 22%.

The company describes itself as the largest pneumatic equipment maker in Korea, and its pneumatic products serve semiconductor, electronics, display, automotive and secondary battery end markets.

On the robotics side, it set up an AI autonomous manufacturing lab and worked with China-based HCFA and HCBOT to present actuators, a core component for humanoid robots. A China subsidiary, Shanghai Danhae Technology, is among its affiliates.

A Korean asset manager's report noted that semiconductor-related sales rose from 4.0% to 10.5% of the total as of the first quarter of 2026.

In terms of competition, the report assessed that the company covers a wider range than domestic rivals with limited mass-production references, extending beyond joint actuators to robot hands and end effectors.

04

Earnings analysis

Annual revenue fell from KRW 94.3 billion in 2022 to KRW 86.2 billion in 2023, a decline of 8.6%, and then held roughly flat at KRW 86.4 billion in 2024 (up 0.3% year on year) and KRW 86.3 billion in 2025 (down 0.1% year on year). Among 2022 to 2025, 2022 was the largest year for revenue and 2023 the smallest.

Operating profit moved from KRW 909 million in 2022 to an operating loss of KRW 5.16 billion in 2023, a swing into losses, and the operating loss then narrowed to KRW 2.48 billion in 2024 and KRW 2.3 billion in 2025.

The operating margin was 1.0% in 2022, -6.0% in 2023, -2.9% in 2024 and -2.7% in 2025, so the loss rate has eased gradually since 2024.

Net profit attributable to controlling shareholders went from KRW 26.45 million in 2022 to a net loss of KRW 7.16 billion in 2023, then narrowed to a net loss of KRW 4.9 billion in 2024 and KRW 2.57 billion in 2025.

Net profit including non-controlling interests, which is a different figure, was a net loss of KRW 2.52 billion in 2025, and the gap comes from non-controlling interest results. The debt-to-equity ratio rose from 164.6% in 2022 to 185.4% in 2025.

At the end of 2025, total equity was KRW 37.4 billion, of which KRW 36.4 billion belonged to controlling shareholders, and total liabilities were KRW 69.3 billion.

Operating cash flow was -KRW 1.6 billion in 2022, KRW 3.26 billion in 2023, -KRW 4.98 billion in 2024 and -KRW 219 million in 2025, with large swings between years.

Quarterly revenue rose from KRW 21.1 billion in the third quarter of 2025 to KRW 24.8 billion in the fourth quarter (up 17.4% from the prior quarter), dipped to KRW 23.9 billion in the first quarter of 2026 (down 3.6%), then grew to KRW 24.9 billion in the second quarter (up 3.9%) and KRW 26.6 billion in the third quarter (up 7.0%), a two-quarter run of increases from the first-quarter low.

Operating profit turned positive at KRW 518 million in the fourth quarter of 2025, then recorded an operating loss of KRW 6.66 million in the first quarter of 2026 and KRW 593 million in the second quarter, before returning to operating profit of KRW 1.69 billion in the third quarter.

The company attributed the second-quarter margin drop to intensifying competition, and said that in the third quarter the effects of price increases and cost reductions took hold.

Net profit attributable to controlling shareholders turned positive at KRW 1.71 billion in the third quarter after a KRW 1.1 billion loss in the second quarter, and the four-quarter total from the fourth quarter of 2025 through the third quarter of 2026 is KRW 1.25 billion.

05

Industry analysis

Pneumatic products are drive components on factory automation lines, and demand moves with capital spending in end markets such as semiconductors, electronics, displays, automotive and secondary batteries.

An online securities information site described the company as the sole domestic maker and said it has won large projects, though that reflects the company's own positioning.

In the robotics segment, TPC Robotics is compared with robot makers in the same machinery and equipment sector, including Rainbow Robotics and Doosan Robotics. A news headline on an online securities outlet paired the launch of seven humanoid actuator models with a target of a second-half earnings rebound.

A thematic market report in early September 2026 cited the expansion of humanoid business as a driver of strength in robot-themed stocks. The asset manager's report said robot equipment utilization rose from 53% in 2025 to around 100% as of the report date.

The report cited the breadth of the company's coverage relative to domestic rivals as a point of difference. On the other hand, the company said intensifying competition weighed on profitability in its core pneumatic business in the second quarter, so pricing pressure remains a variable.

From a cycle perspective, pneumatic components are sensitive to capital spending, so it is worth separating whether earnings recovery reflects an industry upturn or company-specific factors such as pricing, costs and robot utilization.

The rise in semiconductor-related sales from 4.0% to 10.5% of the total also ties into semiconductor equipment investment.

06

Outlook

A Korean asset manager's report assessed that a 2026 earnings turnaround is highly likely. Its basis cited fixed-cost leverage from higher sales, rising robot equipment utilization, growth in semiconductor-related sales and the effect of price increases in pneumatics.

The third-quarter 2026 return to operating profit points in the same direction, but with operating losses in the first and second quarters, whether profitability holds for the full year remains to be confirmed.

The company reportedly aimed to convert its humanoid actuators into its own-brand products within the third quarter and supply them to domestic and overseas customers.

That target point, the third quarter of 2026, has already passed, so whether the conversion happened should be checked in the next periodic report's business review.

Reports also say the company is pursuing supply of actuators for a humanoid mass-production project at a domestic automaker's OEM, but contract size and supply timing remain at the reporting stage.

With HCBOT, it is carrying out a transfer of core technology and joint development and production cooperation, and reports say deliveries to a production base in Vietnam are also under way. Revenue over the latest three quarters moved between KRW 23.9 billion and KRW 26.6 billion.

The points to check in future results are the growth rate of robotics revenue, robot equipment utilization and how far price increases in pneumatics are reflected.

Bull factors

Speed of growth in robotics

According to a Korean asset manager's report, robotics revenue rose 84.0% year on year in the first quarter of 2026, and robot equipment utilization climbed from 53% in 2025 to around 100% by the report date. If robotics revenue grows enough to complement the pneumatic core, the business mix could shift. Still, the robotics division accounted for only about 22% of sales as of December 2025.

Return to profit and cost structure

Third-quarter 2026 operating profit of KRW 1.69 billion swung from a year-earlier operating loss, and quarterly revenue of KRW 26.6 billion rose 7.0% from the prior quarter. The company said the effects of price increases and cost reductions took hold in the third quarter.

Operating profit of KRW 518 million in the fourth quarter of 2025 also suggests the profitability recovery has not been limited to a single quarter.

Semiconductor sales and price increases

The report noted that semiconductor-related sales rose from 4.0% to 10.5% of the total. It argued that fixed-cost leverage, higher utilization, more semiconductor sales and pneumatic price increases together make a 2026 turnaround more likely. The third-quarter 2026 return to profit is consistent with that direction.

Bear factors

Cumulative losses and financial burden

Operating losses ran from 2023 through 2025, and net loss attributable to controlling shareholders was KRW 2.57 billion in 2025. The debt-to-equity ratio rose from 164.6% in 2022 to 185.4% in 2025, enlarging the debt burden relative to assets. Operating cash flow was negative at -KRW 4.98 billion in 2024 and -KRW 219 million in 2025.

Volatility in quarterly results

After an operating loss of KRW 6.66 million in the first quarter of 2026, the second quarter widened to an operating loss of KRW 593 million and a net loss attributable to controlling shareholders of KRW 1.1 billion. The company said intensifying competition temporarily reduced profitability in that quarter.

With the latest four quarters totaling only KRW 1.25 billion, earnings can shift sharply based on a single quarter.

Robotics still at the proof stage

Supply for a humanoid mass-production project is still reported as under pursuit, and the third-quarter 2026 target for converting actuators into own-brand products has already passed. Robotics represents only about 22% of total sales.

Because technology transfer and joint development involve outside partners, the independence and profit structure of this business still need confirmation.

Risk factors

Demand concentration and capex cycle

Pneumatic products account for about 78% of sales, so results could swing considerably with capital spending in end markets such as semiconductors, electronics, displays, automotive and secondary batteries. The company cited intensifying competition as weighing on profitability in the second quarter of 2026. If price competition persists, the benefit of price increases could shrink.

Robotics execution and technology dependence

Mass-production conversion of humanoid actuators and supply to a domestic automaker's OEM program remain at the reporting stage, with uncertain timing and scale. Part of the core technology relies on technology transfer and joint development with China-based HCBOT, so changes in those terms could have an effect. As utilization rises, additional investment needs may also emerge.

Ownership structure, overseas units and trading

As of October 6, 2026, a group led by Eom Ju-seop held 50.14% of shares, which may limit the number of shares freely traded in the market. With three plants in China and one in the United States among its overseas affiliates, the company is exposed to currency movements and local regulatory changes. Short interest stood at 0.87% of market capitalization as of October 29, 2026.

Overall view

TPC Robotics is a factory automation parts maker centered on pneumatic equipment, and it returned to operating profit of KRW 1.69 billion in the third quarter of 2026. Operating losses continued in the first and second quarters of that year, and on an annual basis it recorded operating losses from 2023 through 2025.

From the fourth quarter of 2025 through the third quarter of 2026, it generated operating profit in two of those four quarters. Revenue rose for two consecutive quarters in the second and third quarters of 2026, and the third quarter was up 25.8% from the same period a year earlier.

Strengths cited include higher robot equipment utilization, a larger share of semiconductor-related sales, and the effects of price increases and cost reductions.

Weaknesses include a higher debt-to-equity ratio, wider losses from intensifying competition in the second quarter, and the fact that the move to mass production in robotics is still at the reporting stage.

The next points to check are the full-year operating margin and debt-to-equity ratio in the 2026 annual report, and whether robotics revenue growth continues in the first-quarter 2027 report. This report is provided for information only and does not contain an investment opinion.
