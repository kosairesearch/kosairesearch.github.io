Korea Asset IN Trust: Trust-account loan recovery is the key test

02

Business structure

Korea Asset IN Trust was founded in 2001 as a real estate trust financial company and operates under a trust business license granted by the Financial Services Commission. Land trust accounts for most of its fee income.

The company is widening its scope into non-land trust businesses such as collateral trusts and agency services, as well as REIT AMC and PFV AMC work. Since 2012 it has set up financial subsidiaries covering trust, REIT, lending, investment and asset management.

Trust-account loans, which fund project costs, have become a major income line: their interest share of operating revenue rose from 7.6% in 2022 to 28.2% in 2024, according to a 2025 analysis by NICE Investors Service.

Rising balances also raised recovery risk, which the rating agency judged as lowering the quality of operating revenue. Credit analysts point to the borrowing-type land trust as a competitive area that could help restore market share.

In competition, the company is viewed as top-tier on capital strength, although Korea Ratings noted its net capital ratio is lower than some peers. Reports from 2025 described it as the industry leader in operating margin through 2022, a position that weakened sharply after 2024.

04

Earnings analysis

Annual results peaked in 2023 and turned down. Revenue rose from KRW 233 billion in 2022 to KRW 259.1 billion in 2023 (up 11.2% year on year), then fell to KRW 226.4 billion in 2024 (down 12.6%) and KRW 204.3 billion in 2025 (down 9.8%), a two-year decline from 2024 through 2025.

Operating profit went from KRW 146.9 billion in 2022 to KRW 116.7 billion in 2023 (down 20.5%), KRW 51.8 billion in 2024 (down 55.6%) and KRW 34.2 billion in 2025 (down 33.9%), a three-year decline from 2023 through 2025. 2022 was the largest year in the period and 2025 the smallest.

Operating margin slid from 63.0% in 2022 to 45.0% in 2023, 22.9% in 2024 and 16.8% in 2025, a far steeper fall than the revenue decline. For 2025, the company cited lower operating revenue tied to the property downturn and bad-debt expenses.

Net profit attributable to controlling shareholders was KRW 108.2 billion in 2022, KRW 129.6 billion in 2023 (up 19.8%), KRW 37.4 billion in 2024 (down 71.1%) and KRW 49.4 billion in 2025 (up 32.0%).

The 2025 increase came alongside lower operating profit and was attributed to non-operating income linked to trust contracts. Debt-to-equity rose from 46.3% in 2022 and 43.0% in 2023 to 65.0% in 2024 and 60.5% in 2025.

At end-2025, total equity was KRW 1.08 trillion, with controlling shareholders also holding KRW 1.08 trillion, and total liabilities were KRW 654.9 billion. Operating cash flow was -KRW 5.44 billion in 2022, -KRW 246.6 billion in 2023 and -KRW 325.7 billion in 2024, then KRW 203.3 billion in 2025.

That turn coincides with the period in which the company said it would focus on recovering trust-account loans. Quarterly results are far more volatile.

In 2025 Q3, revenue was KRW 46.8 billion, operating profit KRW 13.8 billion and net profit attributable to controlling shareholders KRW 30.8 billion; 2025 Q4 swung to an operating loss of KRW 5.57 billion and a net loss of KRW 5.22 billion. 2026 Q1 returned to operating profit of KRW 10.8 billion, and 2026 Q2 operating profit reached KRW 31 billion, up 188.2% from the prior quarter.

In 2026 Q3, revenue was KRW 41.4 billion, down 47.5% from the prior quarter and 11.6% from a year earlier, and operating profit was KRW 2.37 billion, down 92.3% from the prior quarter.

05

Industry analysis

The real estate trust industry is summarized by two forces: a property downturn and delays in project financing (PF) sites. Reports from 2025 said that rate hikes and a slowdown in construction and property markets since 2023 led to repeated delays and liquidity stress in PF projects.

NICE Investors Service expected weak profitability to persist while the property slump continues, and judged that rising construction costs and weak sales rates would keep asset quality under pressure (2025 report).

Rising raw material and construction costs have also left some sites with lower completion rates than planned. Competitive positioning is mixed.

Korea Ratings' researcher noted that the company has top-tier capital but a large trust-account loan book and continuing financial burdens outside its trust business (2025 report). A securities research note from early 2025 framed industry restructuring as an opportunity.

Some analysts see the borrowing-type land trust business as a route to regaining market share. Both NICE Investors Service and Korea Ratings kept the A rating but revised the outlook to negative in 2025.

As of 2025 reporting, the property slump was cited as the main drag on earnings recovery, and the cycle appears to remain in a downturn.

06

Outlook

The outlook reads through three facts: recovery of trust-account loans, the project pipeline, and bond repayment. The company said in 2025 that it would focus on recovering trust-account loans, and the pace of recovery on a balance of KRW 819.1 billion at end-2024 is the key variable for earnings quality.

On projects, a 2024 report said the company had won three projects, including the Sangye-dong No. 5 reconstruction in Seoul, and that 15 sites were in progress after being selected as preferred bidders.

A third-party aggregator reported that consolidated revenue, operating profit and net profit all rose year on year in the first half of 2026.

On funding, the KRW 100 billion public bond issued in February 2025 comprised a KRW 40 billion two-year tranche at 5.22%, maturing February 24, 2027, and a KRW 60 billion three-year tranche at 5.62%, maturing February 24, 2028, and was unredeemed as of 2025 Q3 (DART quarterly report).

Debt-to-equity fell to 60.5% in 2025 from 65.0% in 2024, but the negative outlook on the credit rating remains in place. For dividends, shareholders approved a common share cash dividend for fiscal 2025 at the March 2026 annual general meeting, with a record date of March 31, 2026.

The next checkpoints are the operating margin, trust-account loan balance and bad-debt provision trend in the 2026 annual report.

08

Bull factors

Capital base and rating stability

Total equity was KRW 1.08 trillion at end-2025, with controlling shareholders holding the same amount, which supports total liabilities of KRW 654.9 billion. NICE Investors Service and Korea Ratings kept the A rating. Analysts have credited the company with top-tier equity capacity to absorb losses. A common share cash dividend for fiscal 2025 has been approved, continuing the shareholder return flow.

Trust-account loan recovery and cash generation

The company said in 2025 that it would focus on recovering trust-account loans. Operating cash flow turned positive, moving from -KRW 325.7 billion in 2024 to KRW 203.3 billion in 2025.

Operating profit in 2026 Q2 reached KRW 31 billion, up 188.2% from the prior quarter, which offers a basis for expecting recovery to feed into earnings. However, the timing of recovery's effect on quarterly profit has varied from quarter to quarter.

Borrowing-type land trust and project pipeline

Credit analysts have said that the borrowing-type land trust business could help the company gradually regain market share (2025 report). According to a 2024 report, 15 sites were in progress after being selected as preferred bidders, including the Sangye-dong No. 5 reconstruction in Seoul.

Land trust fees make up most of fee income, so a steady pipeline of awards would sustain that fee base. Construction progress and sales pace at the awarded sites are the next points to watch.

09

Bear factors

Three years of falling operating profit and thinner margins

Operating profit fell for three consecutive years from 2023 to 2025, reaching KRW 34.2 billion in 2025, well below the KRW 146.9 billion of 2022. Operating margin dropped from 63.0% in 2022 to 16.8% in 2025.

Operating profit of KRW 2.37 billion in 2026 Q3, down 92.3% from the prior quarter, shows that quarterly profit remains highly volatile.

Trust-account loan growth and bad-debt burden

Trust-account loans rose to KRW 819.1 billion at end-2024, and provisions against them more than doubled to KRW 107.8 billion (NICE Investors Service, 2025 report). Substandard-and-below assets were KRW 836.8 billion at end-2024, about 98% of equity. As a result, both rating agencies revised their outlooks to negative. If recovery slows, provision charges could again weigh on profit.

Property downturn and litigation exposure

The company cited the property downturn for its 2025 earnings change, and credit analysts expected asset quality to keep weakening as construction costs rise and sales rates stay low.

As of 2025 Q3, the company faced 301 lawsuits tied to its trust business, with a litigated amount of KRW 369.1 billion (DART quarterly report). Some sites were also reported to be behind schedule in completion.

10

Risk factors

Recovery and asset quality

As trust-account loans grow, slower recovery or weak sales could feed through to bad-debt expenses. Substandard-and-below assets were reported at about 98% of equity at end-2024.

Slower recovery would limit any rebound in operating margin and could undermine the durability of the positive operating cash flow seen in 2025.

Property cycle and project risk

If the property slump continues, both trust awards and sales progress could slow. Per a 2025 report, seven sites carry responsibility-completion obligations, where trust-account funding needs are heavy and exposure to cost increases is high. Rising construction costs that leave some sites behind their planned completion rates also remain a risk.

Leverage and credit rating

Debt-to-equity was 60.5% in 2025, above 43.0% in 2023. Both NICE Investors Service and Korea Ratings kept the A rating but revised the outlook to negative. The maturity structure of borrowings, including the KRW 40 billion bond due February 2027, could become a burden depending on funding conditions.

12

Overall view

Korea Asset IN Trust is a real estate trust company anchored in land trust fees. Revenue has fallen for two straight years since 2024, and operating profit has declined for three consecutive years from 2023.

Revenue was KRW 204.3 billion in 2025 and operating profit KRW 34.2 billion, with operating margin down from 63.0% in 2022 to 16.8% in 2025.

Net profit attributable to controlling shareholders rose to KRW 49.4 billion in 2025, but operating profit fell while non-operating income contributed, so it is hard to read as a recovery in core business.

Strengths include KRW 1.08 trillion of equity, the maintained A rating, and the positive operating cash flow in 2025.

Weaknesses include the pace of recovery on KRW 819.1 billion of trust-account loans, rising provisions, the negative outlook on the rating, and quarterly volatility, with operating profit of KRW 2.37 billion in 2026 Q3.

The share price sits well below book value per share, but asset value may shift with the outcome of loan recovery. The next checkpoints are the operating margin, trust-account loan balance and provision trend in the 2026 annual report and the 2027 Q1 quarterly report.
