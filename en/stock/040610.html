02

Business structure

SG&G combines automotive seat parts manufacturing with logistics, including third-party logistics (3PL) and international logistics. Its automotive segment makes seat-related parts through KM&I, a wholly owned subsidiary, and supplies them to GM Korea.

Dependence on GM Korea is high, and in the first nine months of 2025 lower demand at that automaker and a weaker passenger car market directly reduced automotive parts sales.

The logistics business took shape after a 2007 acquisition of a small parcel delivery company moved the group away from printing and newspaper publishing toward 3PL and international logistics. Logistics results also weakened on lower cargo volumes.

A 2021 research report described precision press dies and nuclear power parts being produced at the Cheonan plant. A January 2022 technical analysis put related-party sales at 53.6% of total sales, which points to heavy reliance on group orders.

The de facto controlling shareholder Lee Eui-beom and two related parties held 50.54% as reported on April 28, 2026. At the March 27, 2026 shareholder meeting, the company added solar power generation to its business purpose, citing revenue diversification.

A 2022 report citing a Statistics Korea mining and manufacturing survey put the 2019 market for new automotive seat parts at KRW 1.87 trillion. Compared with defense and aviation peers in the same sector category, SG&G's revenue is concentrated in automotive parts and logistics, so its business mix differs clearly.

04

Earnings analysis

Annual revenue fell from KRW 42.7 billion in 2022 to KRW 41.9 billion in 2023 (down 1.9% year on year), rose to KRW 50.8 billion in 2024 (up 21.2%), and then declined to KRW 47.7 billion in 2025 (down 6.0%). 2024 was the largest year in the period and 2023 the smallest.

Operating income climbed from KRW 1.87 billion in 2022 to KRW 2.95 billion in 2023 (up 58.0%) and KRW 6.41 billion in 2024 (up 117.1%), then fell to KRW 3.67 billion in 2025 (down 42.7%). The operating margin was 4.4% in 2022, 7.0% in 2023, 12.6% in 2024 and 7.7% in 2025.

In 2024 margins rose alongside revenue, but in 2025 both revenue and margin declined, and the margin did not return to the 2024 level.

Net income attributable to controlling shareholders fell from KRW 51.6 billion in 2022 to KRW 20 billion in 2023 (down 61.3%) and KRW 16.2 billion in 2024 (down 18.9%), then rebounded to KRW 27.2 billion in 2025 (up 68.1%). 2022 was the largest year for that line and 2024 the smallest.

The gap between 2025 operating income of KRW 3.67 billion and attributable net income of KRW 27.2 billion appears to come from non-operating items, and the specific breakdown should be checked against the notes in the annual report.

Operating cash flow in 2025 was KRW 4.39 billion, which was small relative to net income. Quarterly revenue was KRW 10.6 billion in Q3 2025, KRW 13.1 billion in Q4 2025 (up 23.6% quarter on quarter), KRW 12.5 billion in Q1 2026, KRW 12.8 billion in Q2 2026 and KRW 10.8 billion in Q3 2026 (up 1.4% year on year).

Operating income peaked at KRW 1.93 billion in Q1 2026, then fell for two consecutive quarters to KRW 1.44 billion (down 25.1%) and KRW 696 million (down 51.8%).

Attributable net income of KRW 68.4 billion in Q2 2026 far exceeded that quarter's operating income, which points to a large contribution from non-operating factors. The Q3 2026 attributable net loss of KRW 7.93 billion marks a swing to a loss from a year earlier.

According to fnguide's aggregation, cumulative revenue for the nine months of 2025 fell 7.6% and operating income fell 46.9%, moving in the same direction as the quarterly trend.

05

Industry analysis

SG&G's core markets are automotive seat parts, which track vehicle production, and automotive-related third-party logistics. The fnguide company overview says that for the nine months of 2025, weaker purchasing power amid an economic slowdown and a shift toward small cars weighed on results.

The same source attributes the decline in automotive parts sales to lower demand from GM Korea and a weak vehicle market. It also presents the view that automotive parts demand could recover as the 3PL market grows and the economy enters a recovery phase.

A 2022 report citing a Statistics Korea survey put the market for new automotive seat parts at KRW 9.98 billion in 2016 and KRW 1.87 trillion in 2019, a compound annual growth rate of 23.3%. That figure is from 2019 and does not represent today's market size.

Recent data sufficient to judge where the vehicle demand cycle stands is not in this report. Competitively, SG&G's customer base is concentrated on GM Korea, so its risk profile differs from parts makers with more diversified customers.

Defense and aviation companies in the sector tend to rely on order backlogs and government contracts, whereas SG&G's results depend on vehicle production volumes and cargo throughput. Even within the same sector category, the cycle and demand structure differ.

Outlook

The main changes at SG&G in 2026 concern its capital structure and business purpose. On March 11, 2026, the board approved a share consolidation that raises par value from KRW 500 to KRW 1,000, with the stated aim of stabilizing the share price by maintaining an appropriate free float and enhancing corporate value.

Common shares outstanding fell from 34,087,196 to 17,043,598 after the consolidation, which took effect on April 28, 2026, and the new shares were listed on May 19, 2026. On February 23, 2026, a related party, SGK, bought 341,000 shares on the open market.

The controlling shareholder group's stake was reported at 50.54% as of April 28, 2026, following the consolidation. On the business side, the March 2026 shareholder meeting added solar power generation to the company's business purpose.

The contribution of solar operations to results cannot currently be separated in the quarterly disclosures. The fnguide company overview presents a view that automotive parts demand could recover during an economic recovery phase.

The next checkpoint for earnings is the 2026 annual report, where the revenue trends of the automotive and logistics segments and the nature of non-operating items will be central.

Bull factors

Quarterly operating profit recovery

Operating income of KRW 1.93 billion in Q1 2026 was the highest among the disclosed quarters. The 12.6% operating margin in 2024 compares with 7.7% in 2025, which shows room for margin improvement in operating terms.

Operating income then declined in Q2 and Q3, so whether the Q1 level can be sustained remains the open question.

Balance sheet headroom

Total liabilities at the end of 2025 were KRW 72.1 billion against total equity of KRW 416.7 billion. The debt-to-equity ratio fell from 20.6% in 2022 to 17.3% in 2025. A low borrowing burden can serve as a buffer during periods of weaker industry demand.

Diversification moves

At the March 2026 shareholder meeting, solar power generation was added to the business purpose, and the company cited revenue diversification. The 2026 share consolidation was stated to aim at share price stability and enhanced corporate value. The actual earnings contribution of these moves will need to be confirmed in upcoming periodic reports.

Bear factors

Dependence on GM Korea and softer demand

The automotive segment depends on supply to GM Korea. The fnguide aggregation cites lower demand from GM Korea and a weak vehicle market as causes of the revenue decline for the nine months of 2025. Because customer concentration is high, production changes at a single customer flow directly through to group revenue.

Operating decline and a Q3 swing to loss

Operating income declined for two consecutive quarters, from KRW 1.93 billion in Q1 2026 to KRW 1.44 billion in Q2 and KRW 696 million in Q3. Attributable net income swung to a loss of KRW 7.93 billion in Q3, a switch from a year earlier. The quarterly trend points to weaker operating momentum.

Earnings quality and the cash flow gap

Attributable net income of KRW 27.2 billion in 2025 far exceeded operating income of KRW 3.67 billion, yet operating cash flow was only KRW 4.39 billion. Reliance on non-operating items and weak cash generation raise questions about earnings quality. The composition of the KRW 68.4 billion recorded in Q2 2026 is subject to the same scrutiny.

Risk factors

Customer concentration and demand

Revenue is concentrated on GM Korea, so production cuts or model changes at that customer feed directly into results. A prolonged weakness in vehicle sales could reduce automotive parts volumes.

The logistics segment is also exposed to lower cargo volumes, so a simultaneous slowdown in both businesses is a possible scenario.

Earnings volatility and non-operating items

Attributable net income has often moved in a different direction from operating income, and it jumped to KRW 68.4 billion in Q2 2026. Whether that swing reflects recurring business performance or one-time items changes the reading of results considerably. Without considering operating income alongside it, the direction of earnings could be misread.

Ownership concentration and share float

The de facto controlling shareholder group holds 50.54%, which limits the portion of shares available for trading. The 2026 consolidation halved the share count, which can change price levels and trading patterns. The trading halt and consolidation also changed the basis of per-share indicators.

Overall view

SG&G's disclosed results show revenue falling after its 2024 peak and the operating margin declining from 12.6% in 2024 to 7.7% in 2025.

In the 2026 quarterly series, operating income fell for two consecutive quarters, from KRW 1.93 billion in Q1 to KRW 696 million in Q3, and the company swung to an attributable net loss of KRW 7.93 billion in Q3.

By contrast, attributable net income of KRW 68.4 billion in Q2 2026 and a four-quarter total of KRW 70.2 billion reflect a large share of non-operating factors that operating income cannot explain. On the balance sheet, the debt-to-equity ratio was 17.3% at the end of 2025, and the borrowing burden appears small.

Bullish factors include the Q1 2026 operating recovery, low leverage and the diversification of the business purpose, while bearish factors include dependence on GM Korea, the operating decline and questions about earnings quality.

The 2026 share consolidation changed the basis of share counts and per-share metrics, so comparisons with earlier periods should separate the two. The next checkpoint is the 2026 annual report, where the nature of non-operating items and segment revenue trends for automotive parts and logistics are central.
