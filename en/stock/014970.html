02

Business structure

Samryoong manufactures and sells hygienic carton packaging, a business it has run since its founding in 1980, and also earns leasing income. A January 2021 KOSDAQ technical analysis report ranked Samryoong among the leading carton-pack makers and put its domestic market share at about 30%.

The same report listed four major domestic carton-pack manufacturers including Samryoong. Based on Q2 2026 sales, the mix was milk carton manufacturing 50%, plastic containers 29%, medical 18% and leasing 3%.

Another data aggregator groups milk cartons at 94% and other logistics-related revenue at 6%, so the weight reading depends on the classification used. Plastic containers come from a subsidiary, which accounted for 60% of 2016 revenue of KRW 101.1 billion, according to a 2016 news report.

A 2017 report said the subsidiary's plant mainly produced film lids for ready-meal containers. The same report noted plans to expand investment in the IV bag business, described as relatively more profitable, for export to China.

Demand for food packaging has tracked growth in delivery and takeout markets, which the 2021 report cited as a driver of container use. Leasing is only 3% of sales, so manufacturing drives the earnings trend.

Earnings analysis

On a reported basis, revenue moved from KRW 90.3 billion in 2022 to KRW 86.3 billion in 2023, a 4.4% decline, then rose 9.0% to KRW 94 billion in 2024 and 0.2% to KRW 94.3 billion in 2025. Among 2022 to 2025, 2025 was the largest year and 2023 the smallest. The 0.2% gain in 2025 left top-line expansion limited.

Operating profit went from a KRW 1.17 billion loss in 2022 to a KRW 1.18 billion profit in 2023, the turn to profit noted in the reported figures. It then rose to KRW 5.79 billion in 2024, up 390.9%, and KRW 6.52 billion in 2025, up 12.6%, the two-year increase after the 2023 low.

The operating margin moved from -1.3% in 2022 to 1.4% in 2023, 6.2% in 2024 and 6.9% in 2025, with the largest step-up in 2024. Net profit attributable to owners swung from a KRW 2.61 billion loss in 2022 to a KRW 2.04 billion profit in 2023, a KRW 5.59 billion loss in 2024 and a KRW 6.05 billion profit in 2025.

Net profit including non-controlling interests was KRW 6.04 billion in 2025, close to the owners' figure. The debt ratio fell to 75.7% in 2025 from 90.5% in 2024. Operating cash flow moved from -KRW 2.34 billion in 2022 to KRW 6.96 billion in 2023 and KRW 7.11 billion in 2024, then dropped to KRW 1.33 billion in 2025.

Quarterly operating profit rose from KRW 815 million in Q3 2025 to KRW 3.65 billion in Q4 2025, up 347.9% on the prior quarter. It fell to KRW 678 million in Q1 2026, down 81.4%, the low point of the last five quarters.

Q2 2026 recovered to KRW 2.26 billion, up 233.8%, before Q3 2026 slipped to KRW 1.19 billion, down 47.2%. Net profit attributable to owners rose for two consecutive quarters after Q1 2026, reaching KRW 2.16 billion in Q3 2026, and totaled KRW 6.79 billion over the last four quarters.

05

Industry analysis

The carton-pack market is split among a few domestic producers, and a January 2021 report put Samryoong among the leading players with about 30% share. The same report described growth in food delivery and takeout as lifting the use of plastic and paper containers.

The company has outlined expanding its sterile carton business through equipment upgrades, new lines and better production yields. Plastic containers came through an acquisition of a subsidiary stake and are tied to food-container demand.

Medical contributed 18% of Q2 2026 sales, widening the business beyond food packaging. Demand for food packaging depends on raw-material costs and food consumption trends, a variable common to the sector.

Tighter environmental rules on plastics could bring both compliance costs and substitute demand for plastic containers. Against peers, the combination of a top-tier carton business with plastic containers and medical sales is the distinguishing feature. The market-share figure dates from 2021, so it should be read as a 2021 reference point.
