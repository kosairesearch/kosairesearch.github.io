Report overview

Tariff Reform and Fuel Costs Set the Pace of Earnings Recovery

Korea Electric Power returned to operating profit in 2024 and grew it again in 2025, but third-quarter 2026 operating profit fell 70.9% year on year, so fuel costs and tariff design are setting the pace of recovery.

  3. 3

02

Business structure

Korea Electric Power is the central state-owned utility in South Korea's power supply system, running generation, transmission and distribution, and electricity sales within one structure.

Generation is handled mainly by subsidiary generating companies, and Korea Electric Power buys that power and sells it to household, industrial and general customers through its grid.

Retail prices consist of a base charge, a energy charge, a climate and environment charge and a fuel cost adjustment, and the fuel cost adjustment reflects fuel prices over the previous three months.

The calculated figure is not applied automatically because the government sets the final unit price, so cost increases that are not passed through weigh on results.

By customer type, household tariffs have been frozen for a long period while industrial tariffs saw larger adjustments, and the average industrial unit price was reported at KRW 180 to 185 per kWh.

Nuclear share in the generation mix matters: Hyundai Motor Securities said in a September 2026 report that a falling nuclear share and weak utilization are raising fuel cost burdens. Competition involves electricity trading with private generators and rising private grid investment.

Group-affiliated listed companies KEPCO Engineering & Construction Company and KEPCO Plant Service & Engineering handle engineering, maintenance and services, but the parent's profitability depends on the gap between selling prices and purchase costs.

Overseas nuclear exports and new businesses such as SMRs are being discussed, but the specific order sizes and timing have not yet been confirmed through disclosures.

04

Earnings analysis

Annual revenue rose from KRW 71.3 trillion in 2022 to KRW 88.2 trillion in 2023, up 23.8% year on year, then to KRW 93.4 trillion in 2024, up 5.9%, and KRW 97.4 trillion in 2025, up 4.3%.

Revenue has increased for three consecutive years from 2023 through 2025, with 2025 the largest year and 2022 the smallest among 2022 to 2025. The change in profit structure is more pronounced.

The operating loss narrowed from KRW 32.7 trillion in 2022 to KRW 4.54 trillion in 2023, and the company returned to operating profit of KRW 8.36 trillion in 2024. Operating profit in 2025 was KRW 13.5 trillion, up 61.3% year on year, with an operating margin of 13.8%.

Net profit attributable to controlling shareholders was KRW 8.54 trillion in 2025, up 144.7% year on year, while net profit including non-controlling interests was KRW 8.67 trillion, so the two figures differ. The debt ratio fell from 459.1% in 2022 and 543.3% in 2023 to 416.9% in 2025.

At the end of 2025, total liabilities were KRW 205.6 trillion, total equity was KRW 49.3 trillion, and equity attributable to controlling shareholders was KRW 48.2 trillion. Operating cash flow moved from -KRW 23.5 trillion in 2022 to KRW 20.9 trillion in 2025. Quarterly results were volatile.

After KRW 5.65 trillion of operating profit in the third quarter of 2025, the fourth quarter came in at KRW 1.95 trillion, down 65.5% from the prior quarter, then the first quarter of 2026 rose to KRW 3.78 trillion, up 94.1%, the second quarter fell to KRW 1.13 trillion, down 70.2%, and the third quarter recovered to KRW 1.64 trillion, up 45.7%.

Net profit attributable to controlling shareholders was KRW 267.3 billion in the second quarter of 2026 and KRW 836.7 billion in the third quarter. The four quarters from the fourth quarter of 2025 through the third quarter of 2026 total KRW 4.92 trillion in controlling-shareholder net profit.

Estimates for the fourth quarter of 2026 differ across brokers. Financial News reported a forecast of an operating loss, while Yuanta Securities estimated fourth-quarter operating profit at KRW 304 billion. Both are estimates, not reported results.

05

Industry analysis

Demand is supported by growing power consumption. Yuanta Securities said in a November 2026 report that AI- and semiconductor-driven power demand is expected to expand transmission and substation investment.

Eugene Investment & Securities also referred to expanding private grid investment in a report of the same period. On the cost side, rising oil and LNG prices could lead to fuel cost pressure in the second half, as Hyundai Motor Securities noted in September 2026.

The period thus combines rising fuel costs with tariff reform discussions. On policy, some brokers view regional differentiated tariffs as the starting point for market restructuring.

The Ministry of Climate, Energy and Environment has said it will pursue lower industrial tariffs during daytime solar hours and higher rates during evening and night hours.

Competitively, Korea Electric Power is the central seller of electricity in Korea, and its trading terms with private generators affect profitability. Within the sector, KEPCO Engineering & Construction Company and KEPCO Plant Service & Engineering play roles in engineering and maintenance.

Competition for overseas nuclear orders is intense, and the overseas track record of domestic players has not been confirmed in firm figures.

Outlook

Yuanta Securities projected 2026 operating profit at KRW 6.86 trillion, down 49.1% year on year, and 2027 at KRW 7.87 trillion, up 14.7%. The 2026 annual figures are estimates, since no periodic report has been filed yet.

Recent target prices are lower than before. Hyundai Motor Securities put a target price of KRW 43,000 in a September 18, 2026 report and kept a BUY rating, down from KRW 53,000.

Yuanta Securities set a target of KRW 43,000 in a November 13, 2026 report, keeping BUY, down from KRW 47,000. iM Securities lowered its target to KRW 40,000 in a report reported on November 13, 2026, keeping its BUY rating.

On tariffs, the largest variables are regional differentiated pricing and industrial time-of-use reform. Whether the industrial tariff reform the ministry pledged for the first quarter of 2026 is implemented at what scale is the next point to check.

Because the government sets the fuel cost adjustment every quarter, whether the first-quarter 2027 adjustment reduces the cost burden is another item to verify. Overseas nuclear and SMR orders depend on disclosure timing, so their schedule is not set.

08

Bull factors

Tariff reform and room to pass through costs

The government is reworking the time-of-use structure of industrial tariffs and pursuing regional differentiated pricing and market restructuring. Eugene Investment & Securities linked this to securing funding for AI infrastructure investment in a November 2026 report.

If the tariff structure better reflects costs, the impact of fuel cost swings on results could shrink. The timing and scale of policy decisions are not yet set.

Recovery in the earnings base

After returning to profit in 2024, the operating margin rose to 13.8% in 2025. Operating profit in the first quarter of 2026 was KRW 3.78 trillion, up 94.1% from the prior quarter. The debt ratio also fell from 459.1% in 2022 to 416.9% in 2025. Moving away from losses toward a recovering earnings base is the core bullish argument.

Expanding transmission and grid investment

Yuanta Securities said that AI- and semiconductor-driven power demand is expected to expand transmission and substation investment. It also reported government funding support and diversification of investment sources. Larger grid investment points to a larger business volume. The debt burden that comes with this spending remains a separate challenge.

09

Bear factors

Fuel costs and cost pressure

The sharp drop in third-quarter 2026 operating profit reflects fuel costs and other cost pressure. Eugene Investment & Securities cited higher fuel costs, nuclear restoration provisions and greenhouse gas costs as reasons for falling short of expectations.

Hyundai Motor Securities analyzed that a lower nuclear share and weak utilization raise fuel cost burdens. Rising oil and LNG prices could add pressure in the second half, another bearish factor.

Frozen tariffs and government control

Reports said that although the calculated third-quarter 2026 fuel cost adjustment was -3.4 won, the government kept the +5 won maximum applied. The government has said stable consumer prices are its top priority. Household tariffs have extended a streak of 13 quarters without an increase. A structure in which higher costs do not flow directly into selling prices constrains profit.

Debt burden and fourth-quarter outlook

Total liabilities at the end of 2025 were KRW 205.6 trillion, far above total equity of KRW 49.3 trillion. Yuanta Securities noted that borrowing burdens from large capital spending will persist. Broker forecasts reported by Financial News included a possible operating loss in the fourth quarter of 2026. The combination of investment burdens and possible quarterly losses is the bearish case.

10

Risk factors

Policy and tariff regulation

Tariffs are set finally by the government, so the timing and scale at which cost increases reach selling prices depend on policy. If the government keeps prioritizing price stability, fuel cost adjustments may be limited.

Introducing regional differentiated pricing could change revenue structures by region and customer type. Until policy details emerge, uncertainty around earnings scenarios remains high.

Cost, fuel and nuclear operations

Fuel costs move sharply with oil and LNG prices and nuclear utilization. Hyundai Motor Securities analyzed that a faster recovery in nuclear utilization is needed. Nuclear restoration provisions and greenhouse gas costs are also items that can swing quarterly profit. These costs vary widely by quarter, adding volatility to earnings forecasts.

Balance sheet and investment recovery

Total liabilities at the end of 2025 were KRW 205.6 trillion, and the debt ratio was 416.9% in 2025. The structure carries borrowing burdens from heavy capital spending. The financial burden eases only when conditions for recovering investment costs improve. The pace of tariff normalization and investment recovery is the central issue.

Overall view

Korea Electric Power's earnings structure shifted from an operating loss of KRW 32.7 trillion in 2022 to operating profit of KRW 13.5 trillion in 2025, with the operating margin rising to 13.8%.

However, third-quarter 2026 operating profit fell 70.9% year on year, and fuel costs and cost pressure continue to weigh on results.

The government has kept the fuel cost adjustment at its maximum and frozen tariffs, so the pace of profit recovery depends heavily on policy decisions such as industrial tariff reform and regional differentiated pricing. Brokers have lowered target prices since September 2026 compared with earlier reports.

Broker estimates point to a 2026 operating profit in the high KRW 6 trillion range, but this has not yet been confirmed in a periodic report. The bull case rests on tariff reform and expanding transmission investment, while the bear case rests on cost pressure, price regulation and borrowing burdens.

The next checkpoints are the first-quarter 2027 fuel cost adjustment, details of the industrial tariff reform and the 2026 annual report. This report does not present an investment opinion or target price and is provided for information only. Buy and sell opinions are not included.
