02

Business structure

Hanyang Digitech makes DRAM memory modules and SSDs as a back-end semiconductor manufacturer. The company says it set up mass-production capacity for server and PC memory modules in Vietnam in 2019.

According to company materials, its main product is server-oriented memory modules, and it runs an integrated line from SMT through test and packing with annual capacity of about 25 million units.

In 2025, on a consolidated basis, memory modules accounted for about KRW 425.7 billion (65.5%) of sales and SSDs for about KRW 206.7 billion (31.7%), according to OpenBizData's compilation.

SSD sales rose from KRW 72.1 billion in 2023 to KRW 193.3 billion in 2024 and KRW 206.7 billion in 2025, as Vietnam production scaled up. Production is handled by a Vietnamese subsidiary, and finished products are supplied to Samsung Electronics, which is reported to be the main customer.

Concentration on one customer keeps demand closely tied to that buyer but also carries single-customer dependence risk. The company says it shifted from PC-focused products toward high-performance server DRAM modules. Server high-capacity products take longer to manufacture per unit, which affects output and cost.

Among listed semiconductor peers, HanaMicron and Simmtech also operate in back-end and substrate areas, but product mix and customers differ, so direct comparison is limited.

04

Earnings analysis

Annual sales fell from KRW 484.8 billion in 2022 to KRW 478.8 billion in 2023, down 1.2% year on year, then rose to KRW 595.2 billion in 2024 (up 24.3%) and KRW 651.1 billion in 2025 (up 9.4%).

Operating profit dropped from KRW 52.8 billion in 2022 to KRW 19.1 billion in 2023, down 63.8%, recovered to KRW 35 billion in 2024 (up 83.5%), and fell again to KRW 23.9 billion in 2025 (down 31.9%).

The operating margin was 10.9% in 2022, 4.0% in 2023, 5.9% in 2024 and 3.7% in 2025, so margins in 2025 stayed below 2024 even as sales grew. The company attributed the 2025 profit decline to higher cost of sales, according to a Digital Today report on its annual disclosure dated February 11, 2026.

OpenBizData summarized that a rising share of server and high-capacity products lengthened manufacturing time and reduced output per hour, lifting the cost of sales ratio to 92.8% from 90.5% in 2024.

Net profit attributable to controlling shareholders was KRW 37.2 billion in 2022, KRW 12.6 billion in 2023, KRW 25.5 billion in 2024 and KRW 15 billion in 2025, with 2023 the smallest year. Operating cash flow was KRW 19.7 billion in 2022, KRW 2.99 billion in 2023, KRW 51.8 billion in 2024 and KRW 27.2 billion in 2025.

Quarterly sales bottomed at KRW 168 billion in the fourth quarter of 2025, down 5.8% from the prior quarter, and then rose for three straight quarters to KRW 192 billion, KRW 258 billion and KRW 275.8 billion through the third quarter of 2026.

Operating profit was KRW 9.77 billion in the third quarter of 2025, KRW 9.47 billion in the fourth quarter, KRW 6.76 billion in the first quarter of 2026, KRW 15.4 billion in the second quarter (up 127.4% quarter on quarter), and KRW 1.45 billion in the third quarter (down 90.6% quarter on quarter).

Third-quarter 2026 sales rose 54.6% year on year, while operating profit fell 85.1%. Net profit attributable to controlling shareholders over the last four quarters totaled KRW 27.4 billion.

Because sales growth and profit decline coincided in the same quarter, the third-quarter cost ratio and product mix should be checked against the cost of sales breakdown in the periodic report.

05

Industry analysis

The company's profit recovery leans heavily on server demand. WiseReport's comment on first-half 2026 results (as of September 4, 2026) attributes the improvement to a recovery in server and storage markets and demand for DDR5 server DRAM modules.

High-capacity DDR5 server modules take longer to make per unit, so production efficiency has become more important, according to OpenBizData's summary. Enterprise SSD demand is also a meaningful part of the sales mix.

Downstream demand moves with the production plans and capital spending of memory makers, and Samsung Electronics is both the company's main customer and an indicator of memory demand direction.

The memory market runs on a cycle in which prices and volumes move together, so quarterly order changes feed into results quickly. The company said it has shifted its product mix toward high-capacity server products since building its Vietnam production base in 2019.

Listed semiconductor peers include memory suppliers such as Samsung Electronics and SK hynix, as well as back-end and substrate companies such as HanaMicron and Simmtech. Because the company handles module assembly and testing, its place in the supply chain depends on the investment scale of memory makers.

Where the industry stands in its cycle should be read together with memory price trends and the timing of server investment.

Outlook

Recent reported results show sales expanding while profit moves unevenly. Operating profit recovered to KRW 15.4 billion in the second quarter of 2026 but fell to KRW 1.45 billion in the third quarter, which makes the next quarter's profit trajectory the central uncertainty.

If server DDR5 and enterprise SSD demand holds, sales could stay near the third-quarter 2026 level. The link between sales scale and operating margin depends on product mix and cost ratios, and the third quarter of 2026 showed that sales can rise while profit falls.

The company has said its Vietnam production base strengthens production efficiency and quality competitiveness. Utilization rates and exchange rates at the Vietnam base feed directly into costs.

Net profit attributable to controlling shareholders over the last four quarters totals KRW 27.4 billion, above the KRW 25.5 billion recorded for 2024 as a full year. The next periodic checkpoints are the 2026 annual report and the first-quarter 2027 quarterly report.

The annual report will show fourth-quarter 2026 results and the full-year cost of sales ratio, while the quarterly report will show the cost ratio trend in the first quarter of 2027. Changes in Samsung's memory investment plans and server customer orders would likely move both of these indicators.

08

Bull factors

Recovery in server DDR5 demand

WiseReport cited the recovery in server and storage markets and demand for DDR5 server DRAM modules as the background to first-half 2026 results (as of September 4, 2026). Reported sales have risen for three straight quarters since the fourth-quarter 2025 low, reaching KRW 275.8 billion in the third quarter of 2026.

Memory modules, about 65% of 2025 sales, are the main product, so server demand recovery connects directly to revenue.

Expanding SSD share

SSD sales rose from KRW 72.1 billion in 2023 to KRW 193.3 billion in 2024 and KRW 206.7 billion in 2025, according to OpenBizData's compilation. As Vietnam mass production matured, SSDs grew to about 32% of 2025 sales.

Operating cash flow was KRW 27.2 billion in 2025, well above KRW 2.99 billion in 2023, which can be read as a sign of recovery in cash generation.

Second-quarter profit rebound and cumulative earnings

Operating profit rose 127.4% quarter on quarter to KRW 15.4 billion in the second quarter of 2026, and net profit attributable to controlling shareholders was KRW 11.1 billion. Over the last four quarters, that figure totals KRW 27.4 billion, above the KRW 25.5 billion for 2024 as a full year. On a quarterly basis, the earnings base has thickened.

09

Bear factors

Sharp third-quarter profit drop

Third-quarter 2026 sales rose 6.9% quarter on quarter to KRW 275.8 billion, but operating profit fell 90.6% to KRW 1.45 billion. Year on year, operating profit was down 85.1%. Net profit attributable to controlling shareholders fell 41.2% year on year to KRW 5.13 billion. The most direct bearish factor is a quarter in which higher sales did not carry through to profit.

Customer and product concentration

Memory modules and SSDs together accounted for about 97% of 2025 sales, according to OpenBizData's compilation. Samsung Electronics is reported to be the main customer, so results are sensitive to changes in its orders and production plans.

Because two product lines make up nearly all sales, the pace of diversification is central to earnings stability.

Pressure from higher cost ratios

The cost of sales ratio rose to 92.8% in 2025 from 90.5% in 2024, according to OpenBizData. The 2025 operating margin of 3.7% was well below the 10.9% recorded in 2022. The company attributed its 2025 profit decline to higher cost of sales.

10

Risk factors

Customer concentration and demand swings

Heavy dependence on Samsung Electronics means changes in its production plans and inventory adjustments flow directly into sales. When memory prices and demand shift, quarterly sales swings widen.

Annual operating profit falling from KRW 52.8 billion in 2022 to KRW 19.1 billion in 2023 shows how volatile this structure can be.

Cost, exchange rates and overseas production

Production relies on the Vietnamese subsidiary, so exchange rates and local utilization affect costs. OpenBizData identified longer manufacturing time per product and lower output per hour as causes of the higher 2025 cost of sales ratio.

As the share of high-capacity server products grows, production efficiency management becomes central to earnings.

Management structure changes

After the founder and representative director Kim Hyung-yook died in January 2026, the company moved to a single co-representative structure led by Kim Yoon-sang, according to OpenBizData. A July 24, 2026 disclosure listed Kim Yoon-sang's stake at 37.29%, according to kpinfo.

The effect of this management transition on medium-term strategy will need to be confirmed through future disclosures.

Overall view

Hanyang Digitech is a back-end manufacturer that makes memory modules and SSDs at its Vietnam base and supplies Samsung Electronics. Sales have risen for two straight years since the 2023 low, reaching KRW 651.1 billion in 2025, but operating profit fell to KRW 23.9 billion that year, leaving a 3.7% operating margin.

In 2026, sales rose for three straight quarters, from KRW 192 billion in the first quarter to KRW 275.8 billion in the third. Third-quarter operating profit, however, fell to KRW 1.45 billion, so sales and profit moved in different directions.

Recovering server DDR5 demand and a rising SSD share are bullish factors, while single-customer dependence and cost ratio pressure remain bearish factors.

Net profit attributable to controlling shareholders over the last four quarters totals KRW 27.4 billion, which is above the KRW 25.5 billion recorded for 2024 as a full year. The key point now is whether sales growth turns into a recovery in operating margin. The next checkpoint is the fourth-quarter result and full-year cost of sales ratio in the 2026 annual report.
