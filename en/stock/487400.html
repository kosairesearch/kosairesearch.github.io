02

Business structure

KNS Space and Defence is a communications and broadcasting equipment maker founded on December 26, 2001 and headquartered in Yuseong-gu, Daejeon. Market data classifies it under semiconductors, but its main business is the design, manufacture and sale of maritime and military satellite communication antennas.

The company changed its English name to KNS Space and Defence Inc. in May 2026. Its products span maritime antennas, military antennas for surface ships, submarines and unmanned aerial vehicles, and electronically steered (ESA) flat-panel antennas.

In defense, its exclusive antenna supply to the Navy's MOSCOS-II program and its development and localization of submarine antennas (M4M) are the key references.

The revenue mix is shifting from maritime VSAT toward military products, and The Stock reported the company's expectation that maritime VSAT sales, which had been declining, would hold up while military VSAT sales are recognized.

A company representative explained that military products carry higher unit prices and operating margins than commercial ones, which improves profitability. Specific revenue shares by segment could not be confirmed in this research.

On competition, Intellian Technologies has 500 employees against 76 at KNS Space and Defence, a far larger scale, and the largest shareholder is CEO Jin Byung-wook with a 55% stake.

03

Recent trends

Per the DART filing, cumulative first-half 2026 revenue was KRW 3.8 billion, up 73.0% year on year. Over the same period the operating loss was KRW 1.48 billion and the net loss was KRW 1.54 billion, both wider than a year earlier.

First-quarter consolidated revenue was KRW 3.5 billion with an operating loss of KRW 1.0 billion. Against first-half cumulative revenue of KRW 3.8 billion, the second-quarter revenue contribution appears to have been limited.

Looking at prior years, the company recorded a 2024 operating loss of KRW 1.608 billion, and revenue rose from KRW 13.482 billion to KRW 17.781 billion in 2025. The 2025 operating loss was KRW 1.759 billion and the net loss was KRW 2.226 billion.

On the share price, the company's newsroom stated that the stock traded at KRW 22,900 on its listing day against an IPO price of KRW 11,000. On August 14, 2026 it was designated an investment caution issue after heavy single-account net selling and a decline of 5% or more.

In mid-September 2026 the price was KRW 7,080 against a 52-week high of KRW 24,600. On October 27 the price was KRW 4,220, down 4.95% from the previous session, for a market capitalization of KRW 43.1 billion.

04

Outlook

The first variable for future results is the timing of military VSAT revenue recognition.

The company history lists a contract for the Ulsan-class Batch-III MOSCOS-II build-out with the Defense Acquisition Program Administration in January 2026, followed by a military satellite communication performance-enhancement contract in February.

However, cumulative first-half revenue was only KRW 3.8 billion, so reaching the company's full-year revenue target of KRW 29.166 billion would require a sharp increase in the second half. The second variable is the low-Earth-orbit segment.

On September 10, 2026 the company completed development and delivery of a military LEO manpack antenna prototype co-developed with Hanwha Systems.

Its ESA flat-panel antenna has completed development and is going through OneWeb certification, while FETV reported that the ESA antenna generates first revenue in 2026 but contributes little to profit.

Third, the submarine LEO satellite communication national project is an R&D program running through the end of August 2029, so it may serve more as a medium-to-long-term reference than a near-term earnings driver.

On supply and demand, heavy selling was observed right after listing and turnover is small, so volatility may persist. Quarterly revenue size, any narrowing of operating losses, and new defense order disclosures are the key indicators to track.

05

Bull factors

Defense satellite communication references

Exclusive supply to the Navy's MOSCOS-II program and years of accumulated references show its position in a domestic military market with limited new entry. Its track record in localizing submarine antennas also supports its ability to serve defense customers.

The company says military products earn higher operating margins than commercial ones, so a larger military share could improve its operating metrics.

Expansion into LEO

It delivered a military LEO manpack antenna prototype co-developed with Hanwha Systems, building a record of cooperation with a domestic defense contractor. It was selected as lead institution for the submarine LEO satellite communication national project, which secures R&D funding including government support.

Its ESA flat-panel antenna is going through OneWeb certification, leaving open the possibility of expanding into the commercial LEO market.

Technology ratings and global sales network

It received A and A grades in the technology evaluation for its technology-based special listing. A record of deliveries and exports to roughly 60 countries and a network of about 50 dealers underpin its commercial maritime business. IPO proceeds are planned for next-generation product development and expanded production facilities.

06

Bear factors

Ongoing losses and a gap to targets

The cumulative first-half 2026 operating loss was KRW 1.48 billion and the net loss was KRW 1.54 billion, both wider than a year earlier. Revenue was KRW 3.8 billion. That leaves a large gap to the company's full-year revenue target of KRW 29.166 billion.

Based on first-quarter figures, The Stock pointed out that more than KRW 25 billion would have to be added over the rest of the year.

Trading below the IPO price and valuation debate

The current price of KRW 4,220 is below the IPO price of KRW 11,000. Criticism was raised that the peer group range and multiple used inflated the valuation, though it was described as a minority view.

Because the IPO price was set on projected earnings, a shortfall against targets could weaken the basis of the valuation.

Supply pressure and low liquidity

On August 14, 2026 a single account net sold 310,000 shares, equal to 3.02% of listed shares. The share price continued to fall afterward, and turnover on October 27 was only KRW 300 million. Thin trading means even small volumes can move the price sharply.

07

Risk factors

Macro

With overseas sales, exchange-rate moves can affect earnings. ValueLine named military specification certification and financial risk management, including exchange rates, among the main risks. Changes in defense budgets or procurement schedules could shift when military revenue is recognized.

Business

Revenue depends on specific military programs and military VSAT, so gaps in orders could cause large swings in results. The persistent decline in maritime VSAT sales also weighs on the commercial segment. The LEO ESA market depends on overseas operators' certification timelines and on competing products. A report that the ESA antenna contributes little to profit suggests the new business may take time to monetize.

Financial and trading

If operating losses continue, securing working capital could become an issue. With a small market capitalization of KRW 43.1 billion and thin turnover, short-term supply and demand cause large swings. With the shares below the IPO price, whether market confidence recovers could also affect financing activity.

08

Overall view

KNS Space and Defence is a small company in military and maritime satellite communication antennas that combines defense references with a new low-Earth-orbit business.

Cumulative first-half 2026 revenue rose 73.0% to KRW 3.8 billion, but the operating loss of KRW 1.48 billion and net loss of KRW 1.54 billion show profitability has not yet improved.

The bullish side cites exclusive MOSCOS-II supply, the submarine LEO national project, and the manpack antenna delivery to Hanwha Systems. The bearish side points to the gap between the company's full-year targets and first-half results, a share price below the IPO price, and thin trading.

Market data lists the sector as semiconductors while the actual business is satellite antennas, so simple comparison with sector averages is not appropriate. The scale of second-half revenue recognition, new defense order disclosures and OneWeb certification progress will be the key inputs going forward.
