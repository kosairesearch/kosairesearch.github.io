02

Business structure

KNS Space and Defence is a satellite communication antenna manufacturer founded in December 2001, headquartered in Yuseong-gu, Daejeon. Its core business is an integrated solution covering the design, manufacturing, testing, integrated verification, and maintenance of VSAT (very small aperture terminal) antennas.

The product line falls into three areas. The first is commercial maritime VSAT, the company's origin, which saw sales decline when the commercial VSAT market contracted in 2024.

The second is military VSAT, led by the MOSCOS-II program, the Navy's next-generation maritime operations satellite communication system, under which the company signed a supply contract worth KRW 33.1 billion and which drove the sales recovery in 2025.

The third is electronically steered antenna (ESA) flat-panel antennas for low-orbit satellites, where development is complete and OneWeb certification is underway. The company has said it succeeded in localizing a satellite communication antenna for submarines.

Military antennas require extended reliability verification from development through deployment, which keeps entry barriers high. Maritime VSAT, by contrast, faces competition from several domestic and foreign suppliers.

On September 15, 2026, the company announced a joint development and supply arrangement with Hanwha Systems for military low-orbit man-portable antennas.

03

Recent trends

Based on the DART periodic report for the first half of 2026 on a cumulative basis, revenue was KRW 3.8 billion, up 73.0% from the same period a year earlier. Over the same period the operating loss was KRW 1.48 billion and the net loss was KRW 1.54 billion, with losses widening from the same period a year earlier.

First-quarter 2026 revenue was reported at about KRW 3.5 billion, with an operating loss of about KRW 1.0 billion, and the first-quarter loss narrowed from the year-earlier quarter.

Subtracting the first-quarter operating loss from the half-year figure implies a second-quarter operating loss of roughly KRW 0.5 billion, and second-quarter revenue did not rise much from the first quarter.

For 2025, revenue was KRW 17.78 billion, the operating loss KRW 1.759 billion, and the net loss KRW 2.226 billion, marking a second consecutive year of operating losses.

The 2026 targets of KRW 29.17 billion in revenue and KRW 1.79 billion in operating profit sit far above the first-half run rate, so second-half sales would need to be concentrated.

Since listing on August 13, 2026, the share price stood at KRW 3,310 (down 1.19% from the prior close), with a market capitalization of KRW 34.0 billion. The debt ratio fell from 518.4% at end-2024 to 131.5% at end-2025, and cash and cash equivalents rose 92% to KRW 11.9 billion.

04

Outlook

The key variable for future results is whether the 2026 turnaround target is reached. Meeting the guided 2026 revenue of KRW 29.17 billion and operating profit of KRW 1.79 billion requires second-half results that offset the cumulative first-half loss.

In military VSAT, the question is whether follow-on or new defense contracts beyond MOSCOS-II are disclosed. The 2025 recovery relies heavily on MOSCOS-II-related sales, so a gap in new orders could translate into revenue volatility.

In the low-orbit satellite segment, the next checkpoints are the completion of OneWeb certification and whether ESA flat-panel antenna sales actually begin. Whether the Hanwha Systems man-portable antenna cooperation turns into delivery revenue is also a point to track.

Growing demand for electronically steered antennas tied to expanding low-orbit satellite constellations could act as a business opportunity. Defense revenue, however, can be lumpy by quarter depending on contract timing and certification schedules, so quarterly trends need to be read together.

05

Bull factors

Military VSAT focus and MOSCOS-II track record

The company signed a KRW 33.1 billion supply contract under MOSCOS-II, the Navy's next-generation maritime operations satellite communication system, and that program is its benchmark military win. Its 2025 revenue rose 31.9% year on year as MOSCOS-II-related military VSAT sales were recognized.

Because military antennas require lengthy reliability verification, the segment is hard for new entrants to penetrate, which supports the company's niche position.

Balance sheet repair and higher cash

The sharp drop in the debt ratio by end-2025 is the core of the balance sheet repair. Reports attribute the decline mainly to large repayments of current convertible bonds. Cash and cash equivalents rose 92% to KRW 11.9 billion, and the borrowing dependence ratio stood at 32.4%, close to the 30% benchmark.

Reports also describe the conversion of convertible bonds into common shares before the listing as reducing debt burden.

Low-orbit ESA antennas and partnerships

Reports consistently point to rising demand for electronically steered (ESA) flat-panel antennas as low-orbit constellations such as OneWeb and Starlink expand. The company has completed ESA flat-panel development and is advancing through OneWeb certification.

Whether the cooperation with Hanwha Systems on military low-orbit man-portable antennas leads to actual deliveries is the next point to check. Entry into the low-orbit satellite market could broaden the company's revenue base over the medium term.

06

Bear factors

Persistent operating losses

The operating loss was KRW 1.6 billion in 2024 and KRW 1.759 billion in 2025, marking two consecutive years of losses. The first-quarter 2026 operating loss was about KRW 1.0 billion, and the cumulative first-half operating loss of KRW 1.48 billion extends the pattern. The 2025 net loss widened to KRW 2.226 billion. The widening of the cumulative loss versus the prior year is the most direct weakness.

Rising SG&A and weak profit structure

Selling, general and administrative expenses rose from KRW 5.7 billion to KRW 8.7 billion in 2025, so the revenue gain did not translate into profit. Revenue reached about KRW 17.8 billion, yet the operating loss stayed in the KRW 1.6 billion to KRW 1.8 billion range.

Operating cash flow turned positive, but reports attribute part of that to higher interest receipts. Core profit generation has not yet clearly improved.

Gap between targets and first-half results

Cumulative first-half revenue of KRW 3.8 billion compares with the 2026 revenue target of KRW 29.17 billion. Reaching the KRW 1.79 billion operating profit target requires a sharp swing to profit in the second half. Military revenue can vary widely by quarter depending on contract timing. The quarterly track record to test the targets is still short.

07

Risk factors

Macro

Defense budget allocation and the timing of defense exports and deliveries depend on government approval and policy direction. A rising defense budget is supportive, but slower budget execution could delay revenue recognition.

Overseas low-orbit satellite business is also exposed to exchange rate swings and international partnership terms.

Concentration

Military VSAT sales depend heavily on the MOSCOS-II program, so a gap in follow-on orders could sharply reduce revenue. For the ESA flat-panel business, the timing of OneWeb certification and of first meaningful sales remains uncertain. Both new businesses need long verification cycles, which could delay their reflection in results.

Financial and liquidity

The debt ratio spiked to 518.4% at end-2024, so the balance sheet should be viewed as recently improved rather than structurally settled. With operating losses continuing, cash inflows may rely on temporary items such as interest receipts or asset disposals. How working capital responds to the long-running loss pattern is the central question.

08

Overall view

KNS Space and Defence is widening its revenue base in the niche of military satellite communication antennas, with first-half 2026 revenue of KRW 3.8 billion, up 73.0% from the same period a year earlier.

Over the same period, however, the operating loss of KRW 1.48 billion and the net loss of KRW 1.54 billion widened as losses persisted.

The bullish factors are the MOSCOS-II contract record, the improved balance sheet, and the development of low-orbit ESA antennas, while the bearish factors are persistent operating losses, rising SG&A, and the gap between first-half results and the 2026 turnaround target.

On the business side, the signals to watch are whether operating profit turns positive and whether military revenue becomes steadier from quarter to quarter.

At a share price of KRW 3,310 and a market capitalization of KRW 34.0 billion, market assessment will depend on how it weighs reported results against the company's forward estimates. This summary is provided for information only and sets out facts and the balance of bull and bear factors.
