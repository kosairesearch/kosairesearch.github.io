Profit Target Versus Cumulative Losses

KNS Space and Defence, a maker of military satellite communication antennas, has set a 2026 operating turnaround target, yet cumulative operating and net losses through the third quarter of 2026 keep achieving that target as the central variable.

02

Business structure

KNS Space and Defence was founded on December 26, 2001 and is headquartered in Yuseong-gu, Daejeon. Its core business covers military and maritime satellite communication antennas (VSAT), spanning design, manufacturing, testing, integrated verification and maintenance.

Historically revenue centered on commercial maritime VSAT, but a supply contract signed in October 2024 with KT, the prime contractor for the Republic of Korea Navy's MOSCOS-II program, shifted the mix toward military products.

MOSCOS-II replaces an aging naval communications network, carries an order value of KRW 33.1 billion, and began contributing revenue from the fourth quarter of 2024. The main domestic customer is the Navy, with defense procurement routed through KT.

On the commercial side, the company has supplied maritime satellite communication operators in several countries, and it also provides antenna solutions to global customers including the Korea Coast Guard and the US Navy.

Its product lines fall into three groups: military VSAT for vessels and submarines, commercial maritime VSAT, and ESA flat-panel antennas for low Earth orbit satellites.

The 'Tiny UT' flat-panel antenna for OneWeb has completed development and is undergoing OneWeb certification, and the company plans to grow it as a new growth engine.

Competitively, the company concentrates on defense satellite communications, a segment with high entry barriers, and received A and A grades from two technology evaluation agencies in Korea. Its intellectual property includes 66 patents, 39 design rights and 8 trademarks.

In September 2026, the company was selected as the lead R&D institution for a national project on a submarine satellite communication system and signed the agreement.

03

Recent trends

On the disclosed basis, cumulative revenue through the third quarter of 2026 was KRW 2.85 billion, up 0.5% year on year. The operating loss over the same period was KRW 1.24 billion, still a loss but narrower than a year earlier. The net loss widened to KRW 1.55 billion over the same period.

Operating and net results moved in opposite directions, which indicates that non-operating items behaved differently from core operations.

On an annual basis, the company posted revenue of KRW 173 billion in 2023 with an operating profit of about KRW 960 million, then revenue of KRW 1.348 billion in 2024 with an operating loss of about KRW 1.6 billion.

Revenue recovered to KRW 1.778 billion in 2025, but operating losses of roughly KRW 1.6 to 1.8 billion and a net loss in the low KRW 2.2 billion range persisted, and selling and administrative expenses rose from KRW 5.7 billion to KRW 8.7 billion, which has been cited as a driver of weaker profitability.

Press reports put first-quarter 2026 revenue at KRW 3.5 billion, with an operating loss and a net loss of KRW 1 billion each.

That first-quarter revenue exceeds the third-quarter cumulative figure of KRW 2.85 billion, so whether the two figures use different bases is the starting point for interpreting quarterly results. Before listing, the company's 2026 targets were revenue of KRW 29.166 billion and operating profit of KRW 1.795 billion.

At the time of writing the share price was KRW 2,820 (down 1.05% from the previous close), below the KRW 11,000 offering price, with a market capitalization of KRW 28.9 billion and trading value of about KRW 100 million.

On September 22, 2026, the company signed a national project agreement for a low Earth orbit submarine satellite communication system, under which it receives government funding of KRW 5.5 billion, equal to 42.69% of its equity capital.

04

Outlook

With cumulative revenue of KRW 2.85 billion through the third quarter of 2026, reaching the 2026 revenue target of KRW 29.166 billion requires a large amount of revenue to be recognized in the fourth quarter.

Whether the two first-quarter and third-quarter figures use the same basis is the starting point for reading quarterly results. Turning to an operating profit target of KRW 1.795 billion depends on revenue scale and on controlling selling and administrative expenses at the same time.

The company has presented 2027 targets of revenue of KRW 42.489 billion and operating profit of KRW 6.995 billion. The core basis for these estimates is the expansion of military VSAT revenue, and a company official said the amount reflected in the estimates is not large enough to decide the timing of the turnaround.

Realization of the 2027 targets therefore depends on the size and timing of follow-on or new defense orders beyond MOSCOS-II. Within the low Earth orbit business, the certification of the OneWeb-compatible Tiny UT and any meaningful revenue from ESA flat-panel antennas are the cited milestones.

The government-funded submarine satellite communication project allocates KRW 5.5 billion in government funding and about KRW 1.834 billion in in-kind private contributions to the company as lead institution.

Because the target market capitalization rose sharply from about KRW 45 billion at the 2024 convertible bond stage, the pace at which results validate that valuation remains a central issue.

The next checkpoints are the fourth-quarter 2026 results, the quarterly revenue trend, defense order disclosures and the outcome of OneWeb certification.

05

Bull factors

Defense satellite communication track record

KNS Space and Defence is the exclusive supplier of VSAT antennas for the Navy's MOSCOS-II program. The order value is KRW 33.1 billion, and revenue recognition began in the fourth quarter of 2024.

A defense industry upswing and rising defense budgets are viewed as a favorable backdrop for defense satellite communication equipment demand. Its A and A technology evaluation grades are cited as evidence of capability in a segment with high entry barriers.

Low Earth orbit ESA flat-panel antenna

The 'Tiny UT' ESA flat-panel antenna was developed for OneWeb, and the company intends to grow it as a new growth engine. Development is complete and OneWeb's certification process is underway. The company has stated plans to expand its entry into the low Earth orbit satellite communication market. The timing of certification and the scale of resulting revenue are points to be confirmed in future results.

Balance sheet clean-up

A KRW 10 billion private convertible bond issued in June 2024 was converted into common shares before listing, which addressed overhang concerns in advance. Repayment of a large volume of current convertible bonds reduced current liabilities from KRW 20.6 billion in 2024 to KRW 13.6 billion in 2025.

The lower debt burden is a positive factor for financial stability at a loss-making company. Proceeds from the listing are planned for R&D and staffing to develop next-generation products.

06

Bear factors

Two consecutive years of operating losses

Operating profit of about KRW 960 million in 2023 turned into an operating loss of about KRW 1.6 billion in 2024, and losses continued in 2025. The 2025 operating loss was in the KRW 1.6 to 1.8 billion range, with selling and administrative expenses rising from KRW 5.7 billion to KRW 8.7 billion cited as a main cause.

Press reports put the first-quarter 2026 operating loss and net loss at KRW 1 billion each, so profitability stabilization has not yet been confirmed.

Dependence on one large program

Revenue recovered to about KRW 17.8 billion in 2025, but expansion has been slower than the KRW 16.8 billion and KRW 17.3 billion levels of 2022 and 2023, according to press analysis. The 2025 recovery is mainly attributed to military VSAT revenue from MOSCOS-II.

Commercial maritime VSAT revenue had been declining for some time. Continuity of military orders is viewed as the key to sustaining growth.

IPO valuation and share price pressure

Press reports note that the company was valued at about KRW 45 billion when it raised convertible bond funding in 2024, while the target market capitalization at the offering price of about KRW 112.9 billion was more than double that level. Reports describe differing views on fair value.

Reports also noted that only two of twelve companies listing in 2026 that set their offering prices at the top of the range were holding that price. The share price of KRW 2,820 is currently below the KRW 11,000 offering price.

07

Risk factors

Macro

Demand for defense satellite communication depends heavily on defense budget size and the international security environment. Armed conflicts and a domestic defense industry upswing form the backdrop, but budget changes or delays in large program orders could push back revenue recognition. The company's 2026 earnings targets are sensitive to these policy variables.

Business

Revenue is concentrated in large single programs such as MOSCOS-II, so the absence of follow-on orders could create a revenue gap. The low Earth orbit ESA flat-panel antenna needs OneWeb certification before it can translate into meaningful revenue.

The national project's funding equals 42.69% of equity capital, so delays in execution would also delay its contribution to results.

Market and liquidity

Trading value at the time of writing was about KRW 100 million, indicating shallow liquidity. Press reports describe weak post-listing share price performance among 2026 new listings, which could mean elevated supply and demand volatility.

The small number of companies holding their offering prices after listing may also heighten sensitivity to flows.

08

Overall view

KNS Space and Defence combines a proven track record in military satellite communication antennas, a low Earth orbit ESA antenna and a national project. Against that, cumulative third-quarter 2026 revenue rose only 0.5% and operating and net losses continued, so profitability has not yet been confirmed.

The gap between 2026 revenue and operating profit targets and the third-quarter cumulative figures is large, making fourth-quarter revenue recognition the key variable.

The 2027 targets become meaningful only if defense orders keep converting into revenue, and for now dependence on single programs such as MOSCOS-II is a constraint. The balance sheet has become less burdened after the convertible bond clean-up.

The share price is below the offering price, and thin trading value means interpretations of price moves may diverge.

Overall, strengths in technology and track record coexist with unconfirmed profitability, and the next checkpoints are fourth-quarter 2026 results, follow-on MOSCOS-II order disclosures and completion of OneWeb certification.
