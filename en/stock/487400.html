02

Business structure

KNS Space and Defence was founded in December 2001 and is headquartered in Yuseong-gu, Daejeon, South Korea. The company designs, manufactures, tests and integrates very-small-aperture satellite communication antennas and handles their maintenance in one continuous workflow.

Its product lineup spans commercial maritime antennas, military antennas and next-generation electronically steered (ESA) flat-panel antennas. Revenue used to rest mainly on commercial maritime VSAT products, but military VSAT has grown in weight since the Republic of Korea Navy's MOSCOS-II program.

The company signed a supply contract worth KRW 33.1 billion with the prime contractor in October 2024, and this contract began to be recognized in revenue in the fourth quarter of 2024.

Revenue fell to about KRW 13.5 billion in 2024 mainly because the commercial VSAT market contracted, then recovered to about KRW 17.8 billion in 2025 as military VSAT sales were recognized.

The company also points to its localization of submarine satellite communication antennas as a technical reference in the military segment. Overseas, it operates sales offices in Germany, Malaysia, Russia and China.

For its 2026 IPO pricing, the company applied the average PER of three domestic listed satellite communication and defence companies as its benchmark. Military satellite antennas require long reliability verification in harsh environments, which limits new entrants.

03

Recent trends

Cumulative revenue for the first nine months of 2026 was KRW 2.85 billion, up 0.5% from the same period a year earlier, according to DART filings. Operating loss stood at KRW 1.24 billion, a continued loss but smaller than a year earlier. Net loss, by contrast, widened to KRW 1.55 billion over the same period.

Annual revenue for 2025 reached KRW 17.8 billion, up about 32% year on year, yet the operating loss remained at roughly KRW 1.76 billion. In 2024 revenue had fallen and the operating result had turned into a loss of about KRW 1.61 billion.

Selling and administrative expenses rose from KRW 5.7 billion to KRW 8.7 billion in 2025, which is cited as a reason revenue growth did not translate into profit. The share price moved sharply after the August 13, 2026 listing.

According to the company newsroom, the stock traded at 22,900 won on its first day, and by mid-September 2026 it had fallen to around 7,080 won. The price at the time of writing was 2,965 won, with a market capitalization of KRW 30.4 billion.

On September 22, 2026 the company signed an agreement as lead research institution for a national project on a low-earth-orbit satellite communication system for submarines, with government funding of KRW 5.5 billion and private in-kind contribution of about KRW 1.83 billion.

04

Outlook

The first variable for future results is whether the company reaches its full-year 2026 estimates. The company projected 2026 revenue of KRW 29.2 billion and an operating profit of KRW 1.8 billion, but nine-month cumulative revenue of KRW 2.85 billion means a large amount must be recognized in the remaining period.

Management has said the 2026 return to profit depends mainly on expanding military VSAT sales rather than on ESA flat-panel antennas. The ESA timeline depends on the outcome of OneWeb compatibility certification, and the company targets full-scale mass production in 2027.

Its medium-term estimates for 2027 are revenue of KRW 42.5 billion and an operating profit of KRW 7.0 billion. IPO proceeds of KRW 26.4 billion are earmarked for research and development, expanded production capacity and next-generation military satellite antenna product lines.

On the supply side, institutional lock-up commitments covered only about 0.8% of demand, so a large share of shares can be traded after listing.

Items to track are the scale of revenue recognized in the fourth quarter of 2026, any follow-on military VSAT contract disclosures, and the completion of OneWeb certification and the timing of ESA revenue. Full-year 2026 results will be reported in the annual business report.

05

Bull factors

Defence references in a high-barrier niche

Military satellite antennas require long reliability testing in harsh conditions, which keeps new entrants limited. The company secured a position as the exclusive supplier of satellite antennas for MOSCOS-II through its October 2024 contract with the prime contractor.

Its localization of submarine antennas adds to its technical track record in the military segment. Whether this track record converts into follow-on defence orders will determine how durable its revenue trend proves.

Indirect benefit from higher defence spending

Amid a view that the domestic defence industry has entered an upswing, the company is seen as a direct beneficiary of higher defence budgets. It has said IPO proceeds will go toward expanding military antenna product lines and building mass-production capacity for next-generation flat-panel antennas.

Most institutional bidders in the pre-listing demand forecast priced the shares at or above the top of the range, which the market read as recognition of military antenna growth potential. Whether budget increases translate into individual contract awards depends on future disclosures.

LEO ESA antennas and submarine program

The company has completed development of its ESA flat-panel antenna using in-house technology and is undergoing official compatibility certification with OneWeb. It is pursuing entry into the next-generation flat-panel market with full mass production targeted for 2027.

On September 22, 2026 it signed an agreement as lead research institution for a submarine satellite communication system built on commercial low-earth-orbit satellite service. Government funding for that project is KRW 5.5 billion, which the company receives as the lead organization.

06

Bear factors

Two straight years of operating losses, wider net loss

Operating losses were about KRW 1.61 billion in 2024 and about KRW 1.76 billion in 2025, marking two consecutive years in the red. The first nine months of 2026 again showed an operating loss of KRW 1.24 billion and a net loss of KRW 1.55 billion.

The wider net loss shows that a smaller operating loss did not carry through to the bottom line. Selling and administrative expenses rising from KRW 5.7 billion to KRW 8.7 billion in 2025 is cited as a main cause of the operating deficit.

Flat revenue and slow growth

Revenue was about KRW 16.8 billion in 2022 and KRW 17.3 billion in 2023, then fell to about KRW 13.5 billion in 2024. It recovered to about KRW 17.8 billion in 2025, but commentary notes that expansion has been slow since 2022.

Cumulative revenue for the first nine months of 2026 was KRW 2.85 billion, up only 0.5% year on year. Military sales are offsetting a decline in commercial maritime VSAT sales, so overall top-line growth has not been pronounced.

Post-listing share slump and low lock-up

The IPO price was 11,000 won, and according to the company newsroom the stock traded at 22,900 won on its first day. By mid-September 2026 it had fallen to around 7,080 won, and the price at the time of writing was 2,965 won.

Institutional lock-up commitments covered only about 0.8% of demand, leaving most shares tradable soon after listing. The weak supply structure has added to share price volatility.

07

Risk factors

Earnings estimates

The company estimated 2026 revenue of KRW 29.2 billion and an operating profit of KRW 1.8 billion. With nine-month revenue at KRW 2.85 billion, full-year targets would be missed without a large fourth-quarter recognition.

The 2027 targets of revenue at KRW 42.5 billion and operating profit at KRW 7.0 billion also rest on expanded military VSAT sales. Continued shortfalls would push back the timing of a return to profit.

Certification and production timing

ESA flat-panel antennas can only generate meaningful revenue once official compatibility certification with OneWeb is complete. The company targets full-scale production in 2027, so any certification delay would also postpone the payback on capacity expansion and R&D spending.

The low-earth-orbit submarine program must also pass environmental testing and system development stages, leaving room for schedule changes.

Regulatory and export compliance

Past unlicensed export activity has been flagged, leaving export regulatory compliance as an open issue. Press reports indicate the company set up a dedicated management function in response.

Defence equipment and satellite communications products can be subject to cross-border export controls, so the impact of such rules could grow as overseas expansion into the Middle East, Asia and Latin America proceeds.

08

Overall view

KNS Space and Defence has established credentials in military satellite communication antennas, including the MOSCOS-II supply record and an A-grade technology evaluation obtained for its listing.

Its strengths lie in high entry barriers in defence, a supportive budget backdrop, and completed ESA antenna development with OneWeb certification underway. Against this, operating losses continued in the first nine months of 2026 following losses in 2024 and 2025, and the net loss widened.

Revenue fell from about KRW 16.8 billion in 2022 and KRW 17.3 billion in 2023 to about KRW 13.5 billion in 2024 before recovering to about KRW 17.8 billion in 2025, and nine-month growth was only 0.5%.

The 2026 return-to-profit estimate sits far above the nine-month run rate, so the outcome depends heavily on the remaining period. Post-listing shares trade well below the IPO price, and the low lock-up ratio adds supply pressure.

Accordingly, the scale of revenue recognition and the trend in operating profit and loss remain the key indicators in future disclosures.
