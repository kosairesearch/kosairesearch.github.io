02

Business structure

Wemade Play develops and operates mobile games, and its domestic revenue rests on existing titles such as the Anipang series along with advertising channels. The company said domestic revenue stayed stable in Q1 2026, supported by a new season of the Anipang series and wider advertising channels.

Overseas operations run through the subsidiary Playlinks, which focuses on social casino titles for international markets. Playlinks recorded revenue of KRW 42.7 billion and operating profit of KRW 4.5 billion in the first half of 2026, its first half-year revenue above KRW 40 billion since its founding.

In Q1 2026 Playlinks posted its highest quarterly revenue, lifting consolidated overseas revenue to KRW 20.8 billion. On a standalone basis, Playlinks' Q3 2026 revenue was KRW 20.1 billion, operating profit KRW 1.9 billion and net loss KRW 0.2 billion.

Subsidiary figures cannot be compared directly with consolidated totals, because intercompany transactions are eliminated at the consolidation stage. Beyond games, the company runs advertising and publishing businesses, where publishing means distributing games developed by outside studios in Korea and abroad.

In-house titles in preparation include the three-match puzzle Project R and the hybrid-genre Project G. In casual and puzzle segments the company competes with large publishers for player time, so the performance of new releases will determine how the revenue mix shifts.

04

Earnings analysis

Looking at annual results, 2022 was the largest year in the disclosed period with revenue of KRW 134 billion, while 2024 was the smallest with revenue of KRW 120.4 billion. Revenue fell 9.2% to KRW 121.7 billion in 2023 and 1.0% in 2024, then rose 4.0% to KRW 125.3 billion in 2025, still below the 2022 level.

Operating profit moved from KRW 893 million in 2022 to an operating loss of KRW 436 million in 2023, which marked a swing into deficit, and then to KRW 1.05 billion in 2024, a swing back to profit.

Operating profit rose 919.8% to KRW 10.7 billion in 2025, lifting the operating margin to 8.6%, compared with 0.7% in 2022, -0.4% in 2023 and 0.9% in 2024. Profit attributable to controlling shareholders turned positive at KRW 4.31 billion in 2023 and reached KRW 23.6 billion in 2024, up 446.1%.

It then fell 20.0% to KRW 18.8 billion in 2025, which is larger than operating profit, meaning non-operating items contributed positively. Net income including non-controlling interests was KRW 21 billion in 2025, a different figure from profit attributable to controlling shareholders.

Operating cash flow rose to KRW 19.5 billion in 2025 from KRW 4.91 billion in 2024. Quarterly revenue climbed from KRW 31.1 billion in Q3 2025 to KRW 32.4 billion in Q1 2026, then slipped to KRW 32.2 billion in Q2 and KRW 30.3 billion in Q3 2026.

Operating profit went from KRW 3.39 billion in Q1 2026 to KRW 2.6 billion in Q2 and KRW 2.06 billion in Q3, and the company attributed the Q3 swing into loss mainly to exchange-rate effects on overseas revenue and foreign-exchange losses.

Profit attributable to controlling shareholders over the last four quarters totaled KRW 5.16 billion, so quarterly swings have been large.

05

Industry analysis

The mobile game market is driven by hit releases, so the key question is how long existing IP can sustain revenue. The company itself diagnosed the aging of its core IP and the absence of new titles as weakening its ability to generate operating profit.

In Korea, a new season of the Anipang series and advertising channels support revenue, but whether this structure can last depends on new-title performance. Overseas revenue runs mainly through the subsidiary Playlinks and is directly exposed to currency moves.

In Q3 2026 the decline in the won-denominated value of overseas revenue flowed through to lower operating profit. In casual and puzzle genres the company competes for launches with large publishers such as Com2us and Netmarble, so the reception of its three-match puzzle and hybrid-genre titles will shape its position.

The company also plans to strengthen live services for social casino titles and to raise development and design productivity with AI and improve marketing efficiency. Its expanded in-house AI infrastructure is presented as a way to raise efficiency in game development and advertising.

Quantitative data on overall market size and cycle position is limited, so the company's own results and new-title performance are the more reliable reference points.

06

Outlook

The company's medium-term direction is set out in its 2026 corporate value enhancement plan, disclosed on October 22, 2026. Under that plan the company aims to launch two global casual titles and three to five externally developed publishing titles in 2027.

It also stated it will build a development and service system that releases at least two new titles each year. To address the aging of its core IP, the company said it will strengthen live services for the Anipang series and social casino titles.

The release timing has shifted from the August 2026 half-year announcement, when Project R, Project G and Playlinks titles were pointed to for late 2026, to a Project R soft launch in Q1 2027.

Project R is in development based on North American test results, and global full service of the publishing titles is scheduled to begin within 2026. Playlinks is also preparing an overseas launch of a social casino title and entry into the iGaming market.

The company also said it would monetize non-operating assets and reinvest the proceeds into new-title development and marketing. Whether to introduce a shareholder return policy is under review, considering new-title investment and profit growth.

Total liabilities were KRW 39.7 billion at the end of 2025, and the debt ratio stood at 14.3%, a relatively light financial burden.

Overseas growth at the subsidiary

The subsidiary Playlinks recorded revenue of KRW 42.7 billion and operating profit of KRW 4.5 billion in the first half of 2026, its first half-year revenue above KRW 40 billion since its founding. Its Q1 2026 revenue hit a quarterly high, lifting consolidated overseas revenue to KRW 20.8 billion. As overseas revenue has grown in weight, overseas operations could become a second pillar of earnings.

New-title pipeline and business diversification

The company has set targets of two global casual titles and three to five publishing titles for 2027. The company said North American tests of Project R were favorable, with a soft launch planned as early as Q1 2027. Growing advertising and publishing alongside games could help diversify revenue sources.

The company posted a controlling-shareholder net loss of KRW 2.35 billion in Q3 2026, and profit attributable to controlling shareholders over the last four quarters totaled only KRW 5.16 billion.

Aging core IP and a thin release slate

The release schedule has slipped from the August 2026 announcement, with the Project R soft launch now pointed to Q1 2027. Because new-title revenue will lag, reliance on existing games may remain high for some time.

Currency exposure and non-operating volatility

In Q3 2026 the decline in the currency value of overseas revenue flowed through, and the subsidiary Playlinks' revenue fell 7% from the prior quarter. The company explained that the consolidated net loss reflected foreign-exchange losses and other items.

Because the gap between operating profit and pre-tax profit widened, the makeup of non-operating items will need to be checked in the 2026 annual report.

Overall view

Wemade Play moved from a controlling-shareholder net loss in 2022 to profitability in 2024, and by 2025 it had restored earnings capacity with operating profit of KRW 10.7 billion and an operating margin of 8.6%.

The debt ratio fell from 81.8% in 2022 to 14.3% in 2025, so the financial structure has moved toward stability. On the other side, operating profit has declined for two consecutive quarters since Q2 2026, and the company swung to a controlling-shareholder net loss of KRW 2.35 billion in Q3 2026.

The subsidiary Playlinks passed KRW 40 billion in half-year revenue for the first time in the first half of 2026, yet currency declines hit its Q3 results and showed volatility.

The aging of the core Anipang IP and the lack of new titles are issues the company itself identified, and a target of two global casual titles in 2027 is presented as the answer. However, the Project R soft launch has been pushed to Q1 2027.

The next checkpoints are the non-operating breakdown in the 2026 annual report, new-title performance, and Q1 2027 results.
